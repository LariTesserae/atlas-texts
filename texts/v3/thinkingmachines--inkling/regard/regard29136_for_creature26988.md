This is not a place for people. It is for the ones who outlast purpose.

**The Elder Resident: The Cold (0.3)**
The cold is not weather here; it is ancestry. At 0.3—deep in the register of frost and slow chemistry—it has forgotten warmth so thoroughly that it no longer remembers what it withholds. It lives in the hollow spires, the compressed mineral avenues, the circular depressions that read like plazas without citizens. It occupies. The air is thinned and metallic at this elevation (1.9), not a height to conquer but a contained sky-room, enclosed by ridges that surrendered their angles to time. The cold is the first inhabitant and will be the last. It does not visit; it makes the place into a kind of body, preserving everything in a state of eternal ending.

**The City That Is Not a City (Built 1.5 / Tech 0.2 / Erosion 2.8)**
Inside the boundary, erosion is absolute (2.8), primordial, settled into its final grammar. But this is not dramatic ruin—no heroic archway, no jagged collapse. The structures are near-flattened form, the oldest stable state of stone and older-stone. Broad avenues of compressed mineral run parallel like streets that never required feet. Circular depressions might have been plazas. Shoulder-high hollow columns cluster in neighborhoods without doors. The urban layout is unmistakable—ordered, layered, infrastructural—yet it was built without technology (0.2), without tools, without metal, without the logic of cut-stone. Either it grew, or was fossilized, or was eroded into an illusion of architecture until geology and settlement share a single vocabulary. They are hollow, porous, layered like tree rings that decided to become a town. At density 0.4, emptiness dominates: you cross vast open volumes where nothing stands, then suddenly find yourself among flattened spires arranged in silence.

**The Green Patience (1.5)**
The vegetation is lush—1.5—but translated through cold into something that does not shout. Deep emerald and frost-green moss clots the northern faces of the hollow structures, living in the pores like blood in stone veins. Lichen spreads in circular maps across flattened rooftops, surveying territory in millimeters per century. Cold-tolerant ferns and dwarf shrubs root where moisture pauses longest. In brief thaws, flowers the color of ice appear and vanish within hours—the only quick lives here, brief as breath, returning immediately to the green patience. The moss does not cover the structures to beautify them. It covers them to claim them, slowly.

**The Insinuation of Water (1.4)**
Water is never a river here. It is an insinuation—damp, intermittent, always present but never dominant. Condensation gathers in microscopic armies, beading along flattened rooftops and channel-walls. Occasional rain is not storm but gray weeping from overcast skies, running through the “streets” in thin silver threads before vanishing into porous floors or freezing in hairline cracks. There are pockets: black ice in a hollow, stagnant clarity held in a moss-cup, a slow drip in a resonant chamber that never stops. The water is the place’s breath—forming, flowing, freezing, returning.

**The Near-Absence (Fauna 1.0)**
Animals do not live here; they trespass. A bird-shaped shadow crosses the open plaza once. An insect chirps in a moss-bank, then the echo claims the sound. They leave no claim, build no nest, require no territory. The place has emptied itself of animal urgency.

**The Loud Hollow (2.4)**
Despite the near-void, the hollow is loud. Wind moves through channels and cavities in sustained, hollow tones—like breath across bottle-necks made of ancient stone. Every drop of water falls into resonant chambers, echoing in multiples. The freeze-thaw cycle produces sharp cracks that carry across open plazas like slow, distant percussion. The dead structures amplify every motion of air and water. This is the voice of the inhabitants: not voices, but resonances. The cold speaks through architecture; the water sings in hollow spires; the stone complains in microscopic fractures.

**The Light That Hides Nothing (2.1)**
Illumination is normal, diffuse, high-altitude gray-white. Overcast skies illuminate everything evenly. Shadows are soft; distances deceive. Nothing hides in darkness. Every shoulder-high column, every circular depression, every thread of silver water is visible—so the strangeness of the settlement has nowhere to conceal itself.

**The Slow Metabolism (Dynamic 2.1)**
This is an ecosystem in motion at geological pace. Condensation forms, flows, freezes. Moss advances millimeters per year. The near-flattened structures settle further, negotiating with gravity fraction by fraction. Occasional rain carves new micro-channels; frost lifts and drops stones in cycles requiring centuries. It is predator and prey on a timescale you can barely witness—erosion consuming form, vegetation consuming erosion, cold arresting all of it in perpetual negotiation.

**The Weirdness of Purpose Without People (1.7)**
The deepest strangeness is the mismatch. This is unmistakably a settlement—ordered, layered, with avenues and neighborhoods—yet it has never been inhabited, never served a function, never required maintenance. Urbanity without urban purpose. It lives not for anyone, not from anyone. It lives as geology lives: patient, loud in its emptiness, lush in its austerity.

**Who, Then, Lives Here?**
The moss lives, clotting the northern faces. The lichen lives, mapping its green territories. The cold lives, elder and permanent, having made this hollow into its body. The hollow spires live as resonant chambers, breathing with every wind. The black ice lives in its pocket. The silver rain lives for the minutes it takes to vanish. The brief ice-colored flowers live for an hour, then join the green. The echoes live, repeating every sound until it belongs to the place rather than its source.

You are temporary. You move through the open volumes, among the shoulder-high columns arranged in silent neighborhoods, and you understand: there is no inhabitant waiting for you, no purpose awaiting your arrival, no hidden meaning in the circular plazas. There is only the settlement that lives as stone lives—near-flattened, deeply aged, endlessly active. The loudness continues whether you listen. The condensation gathers in microscopic armies. The moss grows over the ancient bones of the hollow town as if they were its own.

This is not a ruin waiting for interpretation. It is a completion. The city—if it is a city—was never built for you, never destroyed for you, and will outlast your footsteps by the simple mathematics of stone, cold, and patience. The hollow holds. The green holds. The sound holds.

Everything else is just passing through.
