The benevolent power does not arrive; it condenses—like the moisture that never quite falls—into the greenish murmur-light, becoming visible only as a slight deepening in the dimness, a warmth that is not the substrate’s fever but gentler, held.

It looks upon the refusal to become and does not mistake it for failure. Wisdom, having seen worlds that rushed too quickly into ridges and riverbeds, recognizes this suspension as fidelity: the world holding its first breath with infinite patience, not out of fear, but because it is still learning what it means to be.

So it does not teach erosion. It does not carve a valley. It does not open a sky where none exists, or plant in the dark mineral matter the idea of true rock. To do so would be violence disguised as gift.

Instead, it listens.

For a duration that pools like the water—present but without current—it listens to the loud, invariant chorus: the high hum of resonating wings, the wet crackle, the throat-calls that never alter. And it understands: this is not chaos but a single, sustained chord of being, complete.

The power offers presence.

Not an object. Not a structure, for structures are unformed thoughts here too. Only attention—placed not upon the place but within it, like another layer of the translucent film. The amphibians, already in perfect osmotic exchange at the boundary that has no line, do not turn, for they have no direction in which to turn. But their iridescent skins—eyes and breath and skin being one—absorb the warmth. For a moment that does not pass, they hold within their permeability the knowledge of being witnessed.

The pulsing insects maintain their vibrating waves through the rubbery vines and broad, dark leaves. Their wings do not change rhythm. Yet deep in the wall of sound, in the invariant hum that never modulates, there arises a harmonic that was not there before—not a disturbance, but an acknowledgment, as if the vibration itself has made room for a listener.

The substrate, that compressed and weeping earth that has never hardened, continues to release its moisture through every pore. But one pore—if choice can exist in a body that is pure emission—delays its weeping by the span of a held breath, savoring the company.

The vegetation closes behind nothing, opens for nothing; it remains overwhelming, rootless, absorbing directly from the humid soup. But the moss-like organisms, that living varnish over every surface, gleam with a faint, altered iridescence—not brighter, just differently aware—where the presence touches them.

Then the power does the kindest thing: it remains, and then it withdraws without leaving a mark. It does not build an altar. It does not force flow into the flat, dark mirrors that reflect only the underside of growth. It allows the absolute refusal to continue.

But it places one unformed thought in the air—gentler than the suspended moisture, lighter than the greenish light. Not now. Not ever enforced. Just available, pooling like everything else: *One day, if you wish, to become is not an enemy. Change is not the end of this breath, only its exhalation.*

The place does not respond. It emits. The film continues. The amphibians hover at the gradient without line. The insects pulse in their continuous, vibrating static. The heat holds. The water does not gather in streams.

Yet within the perfect, crowded stasis—within the first moment locked forever—there is now, without altering a single leaf-scale or breaking the invariant chorus, the faintest presence of an other.

Not change. Not yet.

Only the knowledge, held in perfect osmotic balance with everything else, that it has been seen, and that being seen is also a kind of completeness.
