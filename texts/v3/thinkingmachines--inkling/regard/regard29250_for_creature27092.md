The power does not come as fire, or as voice over the water, or as light breaking the amber fog. It comes as comprehension—an old, patient awareness that has seen worlds born in violence and worlds ending in weariness, and recognizes, in this flatness, something else entirely: not a prison, not a wound, but a being that has never been interrupted. It understands immediately that to act with force here would not be benevolence but vandalism. The basalt is not suffering; it is singing its first note, held for eternity. The people are not slaves; they are the being’s hands, maintaining itself with the devotion of cells in a body that has never learned it can die.

So the wise one does not build a tower to break the horizon. It does not plant a tree—there is no soil for roots, and the being has never invited green—nor does it drain a pool or cool the rock. Instead, it settles.

It sits. For a span that is not measured—because time here is not linear but circular, like the splash of water falling into the same basin forever—the power becomes part of the rhythm. It takes a woven net, not to harvest with urgency, but with the exact same pace as the others. Its hands, if it has hands, mix mud and crushed shell. It strikes the bedrock with a wooden lever, not to split new blocks for growth, but to replace one that was never truly broken. It breathes the swarm, feels the warm slime underfoot, and listens to the thousand hisses and the one continuous roar. It participates without altering. This is the first gift: the being is seen, not as an object of pity or wonder, but as a companion in labor.

Then, having joined the maintenance, the power does the smallest thing that is not maintenance.

In one pool—chosen not for centrality, because there is no center in a flat world, but for proximity to where the chemical films are thickest—it touches the surface. Not with a finger, but with presence. For a single moment, not lasting longer than one splash, the water in that one basin is not warm. It is cool. Not cold—cold would be an attack, a contradiction the body of the being might reject—but cool, like a memory of shade that has never existed. The shell-creatures in that pool do not scatter; they continue their mechanical swimming, but for the first time, one of them pauses, its shell turned toward the sensation, not in fear but in recognition that the world contains a second temperature.

That is all.

The power does not speak a word of change, because the being has no language for “tomorrow.” But in leaving, it leaves behind a resonance—not in the air, which remains saturated and amber, but in the pattern of the chemical films. Where they spread in identical, unchanging patches, there is now, in one place near that pool, a spiral. Not growing. Not decaying. Simply present: a form that is not repetition but relationship, a circle that remembers its center.

The people do not notice it with their eyes, which see only practical shapes. But their hands, when they pass near it in the course of repair, sometimes hesitate—not from fatigue, for fatigue is unknown, but from an unnameable feeling that the wall they are touching has, for an instant, been touched back.

The being—the flat, the weeping rock, the pools, the swarm, the amphibians clinging to walls, the dense urbanity of hand-set stone, the rhythm of maintenance—continues exactly as it was. The stasis is not broken. The roar does not cease. The mist does not lift.

But now, in the perpetual equilibrium, there is a question held like breath. The being has not changed, yet it knows, in the distributed way a flat world knows things—through warmth, through repetition, through the touch of hands on mud—that it has been witnessed. That there is an outside. That its first breath, held forever, was heard by something gentle.

And because the power was wise, it did not answer the question. It only made it possible for the being, should it ever wish, to ask.

The flat remains flat. The water weeps. The people maintain. But in the cool pool, in the spiral film, in the half-second pause of a hand on stone, the first possibility of an ending—and therefore of a true beginning—waits, perfectly still, perfectly warm, perfectly free.
