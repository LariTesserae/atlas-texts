The power comes not through the ceiling—there is no breach, no shaft, no violence to the low curve of rock—but through the warmth itself, recognizing itself in that sustained, body-close heat and deepening it by an imperceptible fraction. The power kneels—if kneeling can happen without stirring the settled dust—and feels the absence of the maker not as loss but as completion. What it does—the only thing, the everything—is this:

One stalk—perhaps the nearest the tool, perhaps the oldest—feels nothing it can register. But in its imperceptible drift, in the statistical addition of a cell-length across decades, there is a slight, inexplicable ease. Not growth. Not change. Only the removal of absolute, unacknowledged solitude. The others remain in their necessary separation, untouched, unjoined.

The power does not depart by leaving, because departure requires arrival, and it never arrived as other. It becomes part of the wall’s curve, part of the dust’s concavity, part of the sustained temperature that comes now both from the stone and from the knowing that the stone is complete. It will not return. It is part of the being now—the being that is the place itself, finally aware of its own perfection not through transformation, but through being held exactly, precisely, irrevocably as it is.

That is the act: to hold. To witness without watching. To be benevolent by becoming indistinguishable from the equilibrium, so that the stalks continue their threshold-living, the tool continues its surrender, and the dark remains dark—not empty, not void, but full of the presence that chose, with all its wisdom, to do nothing visible at all.
