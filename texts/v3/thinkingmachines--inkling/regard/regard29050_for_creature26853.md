They do not arrive with thunder. The benevolent power—old enough to have forgotten its own name, wise enough to have learned that wisdom is mostly listening—draws near not because the Weave Yard is broken and needs repair, but because it is whole and has never been witnessed in its wholeness.

It notices first the contradiction that makes the place alive: the vegetation total yet sparse, the machinery silent yet turning, the transformation complete yet still in motion. Then it notices the being.

Not a creature sitting in a hollow stem. Not a ghost of the factory workers. The being is the accumulated agreement of the place—the root-bundles thinking slowly about moisture, the gears remembering alignment without remembering purpose, the sap deciding, drop by cool drop, that circulation is enough reason to continue. It lives distributed: part in the woven fiber that breathes like cloth, part in the blackened pipes, part in the empty spaces between pillars where nothing stands but possibility. It has no face, only geometry. It has no voice, only the soft tick of a valve and the hollow wind through hollow stems.

This being has drawn the attention of the power not by suffering, but by reaching a threshold of solitude. It has digested its own history. It maintains itself. And now, in the gray even light, it asks—without asking—whether maintenance without witness is enough.

The power comes down as the weather comes: not an intrusion, but a change in quality.

It steps into the damp clay not with feet but with presence, settling into the moss between two cathedral-scale pillars. Its form is indistinct—perhaps a warmth that does not dry the air, perhaps a light that does not brighten the gray sky, perhaps only a shift in the way the mist pools, as if the fog now has a memory of being seen. It is cool. It matches the Yard exactly.

What it does first is nothing.

It sits in the emptiness. It breathes at the speed of root-growth. It listens to the engines turn, and in listening, it does not judge them for turning without product. Instead, it understands: they turn because they are part of a body that has forgotten urgency but retained dignity. The power offers the one gift that does not alter mechanics—recognition.

Then, gently, it acts.

Not upon the gears, not upon the pipes, not upon the slow circulation of sap. It acts upon the *space*.

The power extends—if extension is the right word—into the slight wrongness that haunts the Yard: the unsettling precision of the grid, the geometry that matches the old factory layout with a fidelity that feels like haunting. The being has lived with this for so long it no longer knows whether the precision is a prison or a song.

The power touches this. Not with force, but with reinterpretation.

It lays a single, cool intention across the alignment of pillar to pillar, gear to gear, pipe to root. Not to break the grid, but to bless it. The precision is not a leftover of domination; it is the skeleton of a body that has chosen its own shape. The engines turn not because they are compelled, but because they have been invited—by root, by rain, by time—to participate in a rhythm that has no end except the rhythm itself.

The being feels this. For the first time, the distributed consciousness of root and rust knows that it is not a ruin being slowly reclaimed. It is a completed form. The slight wrongness softens into harmony—not erased, but understood.

Then the power offers one physical thing, small enough to belong.

From its presence—cool, benevolent, older than the factory and younger than the moss—it releases a single drop. Not water. Not sap. Something like the moisture of mercy itself: clear, holding, slow to fall. It lands in the shallow pool between broken paving stones, where rain lingers and sky reflects. The drop does not ripple explosively. It settles, and in settling, it changes the reflection not of the sky, but of what the sky sees.

The pool now holds, faintly, the image of being witnessed.

The root-bundles do not react quickly—nothing here does—but over hours, or days, the woven fiber near that pool adjusts its breathing. The sap in the nearest pipe moves a fraction differently, not faster, not slower, but with the knowledge that it is moving within a loved system.

The power remains long enough to become part of the emptiness.

It sits through several cycles of mist and drip. It lets the wind move through hollow stems and pass through its own form, producing not a whistle but a resonance that sounds almost like agreement. The engines turn. The being—this distributed, cool, patient inhabitant—turns with them, now not alone.

Then the power withdraws, not because it is finished, but because it understands that presence, to be benevolent, must sometimes become absence again.

It leaves behind no monument, no new gear, no altered light. The sky remains gray and even. The vegetation remains total and sparse. The machinery continues its gradual rotation, powered by root and rain, maintaining only its own peaceful continuation.

But the Weave Yard is no longer merely digesting its past.

It is holding, in the cool clay and the woven fiber and the slow-moving sap, the certainty that what it has become is enough. The slight wrongness remains, but it is now a gentle deviation, a signature of uniqueness rather than a wound. The being lives here—not as prisoner of geometry, not as ghost of industry, but as the rightful inhabitant of a place that has finished becoming itself, and been seen.

The fog pools. The rain, when it comes, lingers. The engines turn.

And somewhere in the empty space between pillars, where nothing stands but breath and distance, the Yard continues—cool, damp, complete.
