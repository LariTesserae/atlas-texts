That small, sharp-edged marsh—no larger than a cathedral’s footprint—sits on the earth like a held breath, and indeed, a wise and benevolent power has noticed it. Not as one notices a ruin, but as one notices a child who has been sitting in the same position for so long that the posture has become a kind of prayer.

Here is what they do: they do not rescue it. They do not renovate it, drain it, illuminate it with a merciful sun, or silence its engines. To do any of that would be violence—an interruption of an equilibrium that is, however strange, *true*. The place is not suffering; it is being. Instead, they *listen*.

It does not cast a shadow, for there are no clear shadows here, only the permanent half-dark shared with rusted rails and weeping pipes.
