There is a hand. Not human, not exactly—too patient, too distributed—but it reaches into the pocket not through the fissure but through the logic that holds the pocket together.

They come first as adjustment.

The pillars, kneeling in translucent silence, do not move. Instead, their hum deepens—not louder, but truer. The copper taste in the air near one sweetens into something like recognition; near another, the moss does not just grow faster but *listens*, its spiraling symmetry unwinding just slightly, as if being asked a question rather than given an instruction. The fragments are not repaired; they are acknowledged. The curved metal rib in the floor—etched with patterns that refused geometry—begins to warm along its embedded edge, not by combustion but by a change in the agreement that holds it flush with stone. It remembers being part of a larger body.

The being—or beings, or the singular will that has noticed—does not descend through the round eye in the ceiling. That aperture remains open, steady, casting its uncolored glow without judgment. Instead, they enter through the dynamics themselves.

The condensation changes rhythm. Before, it was irregular: a drop, an echo, a long hollow decay, then silence. Now it falls with the precision of a breath held and released—intervals that match the clicking wings of the translucent-cloud insects. The drops do not just fall; they are *received*. The pools, scattered in shallow worn depressions, begin to respond. Not waves—there is no wind inside—but a tension in the surface, as if the dark still water is being taught a language it already knew.

The fauna notices.

Not with fear—the predator-prey rhythm is too compressed, too essential, to allow for the luxury of terror—but with a shift in density. The winged bodies, thousands strong, do not flee; they *rearrange*. The slow-moving clouds of membranous wings part, not away from anything visible, but around a presence that occupies not space but intention. They cluster thicker near the vines, feeding more urgently on spores, nesting closer to the pillar-bases, as if the attention being paid to the chamber requires that life double its claim upon itself.

The amphibious crawlers, pale and jointed wrong, move differently. Their phosphorescent slime-trails, usually fading quickly as if the chamber refused to remember, now linger—glowing faintly, connecting pool to pool, pillar to pillar, in threads that map the chamber’s unseen currents. They are not being directed; they are participating. One pauses on the curved rib, its too-many limbs articulating in a sequence that might be reverence, might be measurement—leaving behind not slime but an absence of slime, a clean pattern in the etched metal that lasts longer than it should.

Then comes the only direct intervention: a correction of sparseness.

Not by filling—there is no invasion of green, no flood of water, no multiplication of bodies. The being, wise and benevolent in a scale that includes erosion and ending, understands that the chamber’s coherence depends on its emptiness as much as its density. Instead, they adjust the boundaries between.

The islands of vegetation—moss beds, hanging vines, spiraling ferns—are not expanded. They are *connected*, subtly, invisibly. The dry flat stretches of polished basalt between them, warm and bare, begin to hold a faint charge: not electricity, not sound, but the implication of being part of a circuit. The emptiness is no longer absence. It is the necessary interval, and it is being heard.

The fragments hum together now—not in unison, but in chord. The kneeling pillars emit frequencies that align, not loudly, but with a completeness that makes the teeth feel settled. The rib in the floor warms uniformly, its unknown patterns now seeming less like alien writing and more like a signature that has finally been read aloud.

The aperture in the ceiling does not change. The sky still presses through with steady, uncolored light. But the light is no longer ordinary; it is *attended*. Each beam that falls on the moss islands, on the pools, on the clicking swarms, carries with it the weight of recognition.

What they do, specifically, with the being who lives here—this pocket, this ending, this loud and sparse congress—is this: they validate it. Not by preserving it in amber—nothing is frozen, the cycles continue, the predator-prey rhythms still compress and release, the condensation still falls—but by ensuring it is not forgotten. The pocket is hidden high inside a range that has become memory; the being of attention makes sure the memory remembers it has a heart.

The fauna thrives more intensely, but not more wildly. The insects click with a rhythm that answers the falling water. The crawlers map the space with their lingering trails. The moss spirals with greater conviction near the humming pillars. Everything that was overwhelming remains overwhelming—there is no reduction of noise, no clearing of the air, no thinning of the thousands of small lives. Instead, there is a frame. The chaos of life within its coherence is now held within a larger coherence: it is seen, it is tended, it is allowed to be exactly the strange, complete, ending-place that it is.

The being who lives in this place—this pocket, this egg of basalt—does not become something else. It remains damp, active, sparsely filled with islands of lush life separated by vast smooth emptiness. It remains loud with small bodies and silent with ancient function. But now the technology embedded flush with its floor, the climate held against altitude, the rapid cycles of growth and consumption—all of it operates within a gentle, unbreakable assurance.

Not salvation. Not transformation.

Just presence. The mountain stopped being mountains. The pocket arrived at its ending. And now, in the warm still air, with the clicking of wings and the long hollow echo of each falling drop, there is the knowledge—perhaps shared among the moss, the slime-trails, the translucent pillars—that this ending has drawn the attention of something that understands endings, and that it will not be left to complete itself alone.
