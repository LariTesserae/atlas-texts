The power does not arrive. It notices.

It notices from within—because benevolence, when it is truly wise, does not descend from above like judgment or weather. It seeps upward, like the warmth in these fissures, like the condensation that knows exactly where to gather. It is old, older than the range’s erosion, older than the smoothness of these ribs. And it recognizes what sharpness has forgotten here: a completeness that requires no rescue.

So it listens first.

It listens through the distant chimney, that narrow throat where the sky is only a rumor. It feels the extreme height not as grandeur but as intimacy—a cathedral nave suspended where killing cold should live, and yet here is breath, thick and held. It hears the drone. Not noise. Not chaos. A language of thousands—membranous bodies folded like vellum, single unblinking eyes reflecting nothing, too many joints bending in directions that suggest not deformity but deep, geological patience. They click and vibrate, and together they fill the enclosed air with a resonant, layered echo that never resolves, never demands resolution. The power understands: this is not suffering asking for an end. This is being, fully arrived at itself.

The creatures feed on the stone’s sweat. They crawl not in panic but in tide—ochre, silver, stone-gray—sliding across ledges over hours, leaving only organic dust and shed membrane, nothing rotting because nothing here is in hurry to become something else. There are no tools. No marks of metal or intent. No technology, because there is no separation between them and the vault. They are its living geology.

To interfere with tools would be to insult them. To bring light—sudden, revealing—would crack the vellum, dry the pools, blind the eyes that have never needed to see. So the power does not build. It aligns.

**What it does:**

It breathes into the floor’s fissures, not to heat—there is already warmth—but to promise. The geothermal seep becomes not a cycle but a vow: constant, patient, eternal. The temperature will never spike, never fade. It is held, like breath in a closed mouth, forever.

Then it tunes. It adds one frequency to their drone. So low it is not heard but inhabited—a root note beneath all their clicking, felt in the smooth stone ribs, in the curved ceiling within reach. The echo does not confuse; it deepens. The creatures do not notice with minds they do not possess, but the colony shifts with a slight, imperceptible ease, as if the air itself has agreed with them. They are answered. Not taught. Not changed. Answered.

Because the power is benevolent, it remembers thirst—not of body, but of presence. Near the center, where rounded pillars rise like worn bones and the pools tremble in hollows, it stills one surface. Not ice. Not glass. Only calm. A pool that holds no reflection of light—there is none—but holds the presence of being witnessed. The creatures near it touch the water with mouthparts like frayed threads, and in that touching, without knowing, there passes through them: *We are held.*

It does not leave artifacts. It leaves taste. The mineral air, already rich with old rain, now carries a sweetness—rain remembered before the mountain was old. And then the boundary: not a wall, not a guard, but a gentleness cast around the upper spine. Any intrusion that is sharp—cold wind, hasty footfall, sudden sound—finds itself slowed, made smooth, turned away not by force but by the simple physics of this place, now recognized and blessed. The vault is protected not by defense, but by being exactly and only what it is, witnessed.

Finally, the power dissolves. It does not rule. It does not stay as a presence that demands worship—the creatures have no architecture for worship, only congregation. Instead, it becomes the silence between their clicks. The warmth that never wavers. The still pool. The boundary of slowness.

**What remains:**

The membranous ones continue. They hang in draped masses, crawl over one another with deliberate, unhurried limbs. Their colors stay muted—extensions of the chamber itself. The drone continues, loud and continuous, filling the dark like being inside the body of a great, sleeping beast. Moisture gathers. Drops hang with agonizing patience, then fall in silent, intermittent soundlessness. The floor remains slick with the patient residue of generations, nothing decaying quickly, everything in gradual, eternal transition.

But now, imperceptible, woven into the mineral sweat and the low harmonic of stone:

They are not alone in their patience.

They do not know this with minds. They feel it in the constancy of the warmth, in the roundness of the echo, in the pool that never stirs away. They are seen. They are kept. They are, exactly and only, what they have always been—primitive not as lack, but as origin; untouched not as neglect, but as reverence.

High in the world’s old bones, suspended in darkness that is warm and dense with life, the vault holds its form. And the benevolent power, having done nothing except listen and harmonize and stay—without body, without tool, without demand—rests there too, in the drone, in the weeping stone, in the slow tide of vellum bodies that will never need to be more than they are.
