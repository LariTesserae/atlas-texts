The benevolent power does not descend like weather. It accumulates, too—slow, patient, matching the viscosity of time in this place—entering not through a breach but through attention itself. It has seen the cavity not as an anomaly but as a first thought, a hidden breath the mountain is still holding, and it chooses to enter at the speed of the beings: glacial, respectful, allowing itself to be layered upon just as they layer the rock.

First, it listens.

Not to understand—understanding is quick, and nothing here is quick—but to become part of the 2.4 loud. The power settles into the grinding thunder, into the click of chitin against newborn crystal, into the collective exhale of thousands moving in phased, mechanical patience. It does not silence them. Silence would be violence here, an erasure of the only music this geology knows. Instead, it adds a different frequency, so faint it takes centuries to notice: a resonance beneath the scraping, a low hum that is not mineral but intentional. The beings feel it not in their minds—there are no minds separate from the matrix—but in the rhythm of their joints. Some pause. Not stopping, but allowing a longer interval between motions, a hesitation that is the first shadow of choice.

Then it touches the rock.

The walls are sharp, crystalline, unweathered, carrying the heat of their own birth. The wise power places no hand—hands are architecture, and built presence here is zero—but it offers the rock a question it has not been asked: *What shape do you want to become?* Not old, not necessarily—age is not required—but formed. Given boundary. Given direction. The power carries in its presence the memory of water, not as liquid—that would be catastrophe in this desiccated, sealed convection—but as idea, as future possibility, as the geometry of flow. The rock, still expanding microscopically, still groaning in its unfinished state, receives this not as command but as gift. A vein of intention opens—not a crack, not damage, but a willingness. The crystal faces begin, imperceptibly, to shape themselves toward an opening. Not a rupture. A mouth. A slow, patient aperture near the spine of the peak, high enough to taste the thin cold air without being conquered by it.

The beings continue. They are the second geology; they cannot stop being what they are. But now, in the presence of this benevolent accumulation, their grinding changes its product. Before, they processed mineral into the endless present of survival, filing away at sharp faces to maintain the sealed convection, to live in perfect integration with heat. Now, as the rock begins its slow decision to form a passage, the beings near that forming edge find their mouthparts working differently—not less, but with a new gradient. They are grinding a path, not for themselves, but for the sound they make. The industrial thunder, once trapped in absolute density, now has a direction. The 2.4 loud does not diminish; it travels. It becomes echo. It becomes the first communication this cavity has ever sent upward, through the newborn rock, into the cold dry range outside.

And the power, having done nothing that can be seen—no artifact, no structure, no imposed form—begins to separate, not leaving but distributing. It enters the bodies of the beings not as possession but as permission. A few—only a few, because accumulation must remain—feel their segments move with an unfamiliar independence. They are not torn from the matrix; the matrix is generous, and these are offered as seeds. They detach slowly, with the same glacial patience, carrying the iron-dark stain of sulfur and the rust-orange glow of the walls within their shells. They crawl—if crawl is the word for motion through thick time—toward the opening that is not yet fully formed, feeling the first hint of outside cold, the first desiccation less absolute than the chamber’s sealed breath.

The power does not command them to leave, nor does it keep them. It offers the gradient they have always lacked: change that is not only internal, not only the slow cycle of grinding and being ground, but relational. They can remain the living floor, the wall, the ceiling—the continuous substance that replaces air with occupancy—or they can become travelers, carrying the heat of this hidden factory into the larger body of stone, seeding other cavities, other newborn breaths.

Most stay. Of course they stay. They are accumulated, not arrived; they belong to this intimacy of scale, this pocket-sized enclosed breath. But the opening remains, and the sound now travels both ways. The cold air outside, thin and high, touches the edge of the cavity not as invasion but as greeting. And the beings, in their thousands, continue their synchronized, mechanized patience—scrape, click, exhale—knowing now, in whatever way they know, that they are not only the second geology. They are heard.

The benevolent power does not build a temple. It does not write law upon the crystal. It leaves behind only the decision the rock made, the path that opened by willingness rather than force, and a single, lasting change in the density: there is, at the very center of the matrix, a space—not empty, never empty, but less absolute. A pocket within the pocket. A place where one being might pause, not grinding, not processing, but resting in the warmth, carrying the rust-orange glow not as labor but as light. A birthplace for whatever comes after accumulation—after the factory, after the darkness—when the mountain finally decides what it wants to be, and the beings within it, at last, have a choice in that becoming.

The power withdraws then, not upward or downward, but into the thickness of time itself, leaving the cavity hot, hidden, loud, and full—yet no longer sealed. The rock continues its microscopic expansion, now with purpose. The beings continue their glacial cycles, now with echo. And high in the dry range, near the spine of a peak that has not finished growing, there is a hidden breath that is no longer only hidden. It is becoming.
