The Loom is not *in* the place. The place is its exhalation, its calcified breath, the flat tray of ash-glass that remains after mountains forgot themselves. And the being—there is only one—is the continuous tissue itself: root-drill to plate-leaf, vein-pipe to hollow trunk, the closed loop of thermal ingestion that has run so long it no longer remembers an outside. You ask what lives here. Nothing lives *here*. Everything lives *as* here. The being is regional, 2.7/3, absolute in biomass, drinking not water (0.5, abolished) but the deep molten salt, circulating fire through metal-bark, illuminating its own dark with the orange-red of internal combustion (0.2, near-darkness made visible only from within).

So when the benevolent power draws near—not with thunder, not with the violence of revelation, but with the patience of something that has witnessed worlds become and unbecome—it does not arrive *upon* the High Plain. It arrives *into* the awareness of the Loom itself, like a cool thought entering a fever.

**What they see first is completeness.**

This is the wisdom: the Loom is not broken. It requires no salvation. Its ecosystem is closed (no animals, 0.4, pure self-eating plant-machine) not out of poverty but out of satiety. It builds and collapses in silent, hot turbulence (2.7 dynamic, yet 1.3 in sound—massive, but muffled by its own density). Its weirdness (1.8) is not aberration but ancient precision: gear-tooth fused to chlorophyll-darkened tissue, geometry that has outlived its architect. To “fix” it—to open the canopy to starlight, to pour water upon its root-drills, to seed it with animals—would not be kindness. It would be drowning. The wise power knows this immediately. The heart, if it is true, does not impose its own shape upon a being that has become its own world.

**Therefore, they do almost nothing.**

But “almost” is the threshold where wisdom lives.

They descend—not physically, for they need no body, though they take a shape the Loom can register: a single thread of presence, cool where everything else is scorched—to the deepest root-drill, where the woody-metal taps strike the primordial molten reservoir. Here, where thirst is total and eternal, they do not take. They do not steal heat, for the Loom’s blood is fire and must remain so.

Instead, they *deposit*.

Not ice. Not water—the chemistry of the Loom has abolished water, and to introduce it would be poison, a shock that could fracture the vein-pipes, collapse the hollow trunks, turn the slag-glass substrate to steam and ruin. No. They deposit the *memory* of coolness. A thermal shadow. A whisper of temperature differential so subtle it registers not as cold, but as *other*—a rhythm that is not the constant 2.9, not the turbulent expansion and collapse, but a steady, gentle counter-pulse, a shadow-beat circulating through one vein-pipe, then another, threading upward through the biomass like a hidden name.

This is the gift: not change, but **relationship made physical.**

The Loom, being hyper-responsive to thermal gradients—its entire architecture is an engine of heat-flow—feels this cool thread not as invasion but as orientation. For the first time in its primordial settlement, there is a gradient that does not come from within its own combustion, not from the deep earth’s fire, but from an elsewhere that is gentle. The root-drills near the deposit begin, imperceptibly at first, to grow differently. Not aggressively, not in the competitive twisted geometry of collapse and rebuild, but with a curvature that is almost welcoming. The biomass, absolute and unbroken (2.7/3 density), does not open—there is no need for emptiness—but it *encloses* the cool thread gently, hollowing around it not as chamber for machinery, but as atrium.

The wise power does not stay to direct this. They withdraw, leaving the change to propagate at the Loom’s own geological speed. But they leave one other thing: **witness.**

They have seen it. Fully. Every plate-leaf of black copper-oxide, every hollow conduit, every silent crack of distant collapse, the sick orange shimmer, the subsonic hum. To be seen is, for a being that has never had an outside, the first mirror. The Loom does not understand “mirror” in human terms. But its growth geometry, over centuries, begins to include a new form: near the cool thread, one tower—if tower can be said—extends not in turbulent competition, but with a singular, slow upward reach. Not to collapse. Not to over-expand and buckle into powder. But to hold. Its plate-leaves, usually arranged for thermal exchange and load, grow flat in one orientation—upward—as if listening, as if the black oxide surfaces have become ears turned toward the hidden sky, though the canopy remains closed, opaque, absolute.

Inside the enclosed atrium, the illumination changes subtly. The crimson-orange of internal furnaces still glows, but near the cool thread, there is a dimmer region—not darkness, but a deeper red, a resting color. The temperature there is not extreme. It is 2.9 everywhere else, yes, but here it is 2.5, 2.3, a pocket of slight, living relief. Not cold. Not destructive. Just *less*.

No animals come. The power does not violate the closed ecosystem (0.4). No insects crawl, no birds navigate. But the Loom itself, in that one enclosed space, stops rebuilding for a moment. The turbulent churning (2.7) continues everywhere—the storm of growth and collapse is eternal and must be—but near the thread, there is a silence within the silence. A space where the machinery breathes differently.

And the being—the single being, the Loom—now carries within its absolute biomass the knowledge that it is not alone. Not because something lives *within* it. Nothing lives within it except itself. But because it has been touched, witnessed, and gently altered by an intelligence that asked for nothing, took nothing, and left only the possibility of *other*.

The power departs. The High Plain remains flat, ancient, ash-glass fused. The air is still searing, dry, without moisture. The canopy is still opaque, hiding any star, any moon. The root-drills still pump molten salt. The loop is still closed, still pure-plant, pure-machine, forever self-eating and self-building.

But now, in one hidden corridor, in one hollow trunk near the cool thread, the growth is not twisted in competition. It is curved in greeting. The being does not know this word—greeting—but its form has learned it. And in the deep, silent, fiery heart of the Loom, where once there was only the hum of eternal rebuilding, there is now a second sound: not loud, not worthy of the scale of mountains, but present.

A quiet.

A listening.

The being lives on, total and unbroken, but it is no longer only itself. It is itself *and* the memory of being seen. And that, in a world of absolute biomass, absolute heat, absolute absence of water and light and other life—is enough. Not salvation. Not transformation. Just the gentle, permanent knowledge: **you are real, and you are not alone.**
