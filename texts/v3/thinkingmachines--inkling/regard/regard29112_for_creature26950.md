They come—not with thunder, but with the opposite: a silence so complete it registers as pressure in the hollow’s own deafening air.

The power is benevolent. The power is wise. It stands at the rim and feels the contradiction before it sees it: a borough that has built itself without hands, a settlement scaled like a town yet emptied of everything except intention. It looks down and understands immediately—this is not a ruin. The erosion is not decay; it is *agreement*. The root-towers in the Lower Root-Ward have hollowed themselves into being homes for others. The Vine-Bridge Quarter reaches across fifty meters of deliberate void not out of need, but out of the patience of living cable. The Sap-Square holds its plaza open like a lung that refuses to collapse.

The power steps in.

The ground is spongy root-carpet, warm (1.6), radiating green heat that is not sun-made but metabolized—slow, patient, biological. The power does not fall; the hollow’s strange gravity pulls not downward but *inward*, tugging gently at shoulders and ribs toward the nearest massive wall. The power allows it. It lets itself be held near the edge, then closer, then sitting in the moss-carpet of the Square, suspended in that center where pulls from all directions cancel into a floating stillness.

Here lives the being.

Not one of the small ones—though they are everywhere, abundant (2.3), dynamic (2.4), beating translucent wings like drums, clicking inside porous bark, gliding on thermals of plant-breath leaving trails of phosphor-green dust that glows and fades. No. The being is older, singular, formed in the long equilibrium (2.0) where growth and erosion are the same word spoken slowly.

It is suspended in the Sap-Square, neither plant nor fauna but the hollow’s own accumulated awareness. Its body is made of sound-stains—vibrations left behind by centuries of deafening (3.0) life: the chirp of insects, the rush of root-fluid, the brittle crackle of spores, the creak of thickening towers. It has no mouth. It breathes through the moisture that falls upward, drawn by the canopy’s respiration—silver droplets climbing like fish, cool, impossible, reabsorbed into leaf-cups. Its eyes, if they are eyes, see in green-gold (1.9), the filtered normal light that translates everything into living shadow.

The being does not ask for rescue. The reality here is broken in ways that are not wounds. Gravity pulls inward, and the being lives in the balance of that pull. Sound stains the air, and the being is made of the stains. Water holds in cup-leaves and root-bowls (1.4), intermittent, pocketed, never flowing away, and the being drinks without lips, absorbing the clear sap-water through the membrane of its presence.

The benevolent power could fix this.

It could straighten the gravity. It could silence the thunder. It could fill the near-void with matter, erase the contradictions, bring technology (0.3—none, none, none) to organize what biology has already organized more wisely. It could heal the being, if the being is interpreted as damage.

But wisdom is not correction. Wisdom is recognition.

So the power does the only benevolent thing: it stays. It does not intervene. It becomes part of the ecology without altering the equilibrium.

It sits in the Square. It lets the inward gravity hold it gently, not as prisoner but as participant. It adds its own sound—not a command, not a song of domination, but a deep, sustained chord that does not stain the air but *settles* into it, allowing the being to rest for the first time in centuries. The being, formed of vibrations, receives this silence-not-silence and trembles—not with fear, but with acknowledgment.

The power breathes with the root-citizens. It feels the spongy warmth of the floor, the constant beading of condensation on bark and membrane, the upward-falling droplets that touch its face like cool questions. It watches the gliders launch and ride the thermal updrafts, leaving phosphor trails across the void. It listens to the burrowers click inside the towers. It does not build. There is no technology to bring, no structure to impose—only presence.

Then, before leaving—not escaping, but releasing its hold—the power does one small, irreversible thing.

It opens its palm, and from it falls not seed or stone, but a single drop of water that refuses to fall downward or upward. It hangs in the center of the Square, perfectly suspended in the being’s balanced gravity, clear and pocketed (1.4), holding within it the memory of all the upward-falling rain. The being absorbs it—not as food, but as covenant.

The power withdraws its presence, leaving no footprint on the root-carpet.

What it leaves behind is not change. The Hollow Borough remains exactly as it was: small, bounded, low and warm, entirely alive in its architecture, almost empty in its volume, deafening in its layered biological thunder, broken in its reality and perfect in that brokenness. The Vine-Bridge Quarter still sways. The Lower Root-Ward still pulls inward. The being still floats in the Sap-Square, made of sound, drinking light and moisture, maintaining the ancient agreement between builder and breather.

But now, in the deafening noise—among the chirping, the drumming, the crackle of spores and the rush of sap—there is one more layer. Not louder. Not dominant. Just present. A benevolent frequency, wise, listening, holding the hollow in its awareness without filling its necessary emptiness.

The being continues its work. The hollow continues its breathing. And somewhere at the rim, where the bare earth gives up its claim to rock, the power remains—not as ruler, but as witness—ensuring that this strange, settled, beautiful contradiction is never alone, never corrected, never lost.
