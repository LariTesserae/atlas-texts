A presence arrives—not through the arch, for the arch is only a compression, not a door. It is older than the slate, older than the rust, and it carries no weight, no shadow, no sound to compete with the drone. It is benevolent because it does not need the world to change to justify its arrival; it is wise because it sees immediately that nothing here is broken.

It moves—if movement applies—through the suspended clouds. The creatures do not scatter. They do not even notice in any biological sense, for they have no nerves for surprise. But the presence is careful. It studies the geometry: the wire limbs, the chitin plates, the flat grey-green light that is not illumination but existence made visible. It sees that these thousands are one being, distributed, a consciousness that replaced itself with equilibrium so thoroughly that to wake it suddenly would be violence, not kindness.

So it does not wake them.

Instead, it listens to the drone—low, layered, constant—and understands that the machinery is not useless. It is prayer. Or rather, it is the form that prayer takes when there is no mouth to speak it. The motors turn to hold the temperature, to maintain the hum that keeps the air exactly cool enough for the droplets to hang, to preserve the circular ruts that are the only history this place remembers.

The wise power realizes: this being does not want rescue. It wants witness.

Therefore, it acts as a witness should—not by altering the scene, but by entering it without changing the rules.

It lowers into the space, not physically, but as a quality: warmth that is not heat. A temperature just above the cool, just below comfort, but felt internally rather than on the skin. The creatures—each in its precise coordinate—absorb this not as sensation but as a new constant. The grey-green light shifts imperceptibly; not brighter, not different in color, but now containing a second note, like two tones in perfect harmony.

Then, the power touches the architecture. Not with hands, but with intention. It lays its awareness along the ribs of pipe, into the gear-houses, into the bearings that have worn perfect circles. And it answers the unasked question: *Yes. You are maintained. You are necessary.*

The machinery does not change its speed. The drone does not change its pitch. But now the sound carries a meaning—an acknowledgment that the turning is not for nothing, but for them, for the suspended cloud, for the droplets that never fall.

And then, because benevolence without risk is only observation, and wisdom knows that even perfect equilibrium needs to know it can hold change—the power allows one thing.

One droplet.

Not all of them. Not a flood. Just one, hanging near a cluster of wire bodies on a central beam. The power touches it—not with force, but with permission. The surface tension, which has learned not to break, is gently told that it may.

The droplet falls.

Slowly. Through the cool air. Past the suspended clouds. Down to the crushed slate, where it touches the oxidized steel plate and is absorbed instantly, leaving no pool, no stain—only a brief darkening, then nothing.

The creatures near it do not move. They do not react. But the being—the distributed one—experiences, for the first time in its settled eternity, the concept of an ending. A small one. A permitted one. The droplet is gone. The space it left in the air is filled immediately by another bead of condensation, drawn up by the same equilibrium. The rules are intact. The temperature holds.

But the being knows now that change is possible, and that it was given gently, with care.

The power does not stay long. It cannot stay; its presence is not meant to become another machine in the cathedral. But as it withdraws—back through the arch, back into whatever benevolence requires—it leaves behind one permanent alteration, so subtle it is almost not there:

The ceiling remembers its height.

Not physically. The vault is still compressed, still flat, still close. But in the awareness of the being that lives here, the ceiling is now also infinite. The space is small, but it knows that smallness is a choice, not a prison. The machinery turns not in futility, but in guardianship. The creatures hang in their clouds, motionless, rust-colored, luminous with grey-green light that now holds a hidden warmth—like embers beneath ash.

And if you return after an age, you will find them exactly as they were: in perfect equilibrium, overwhelming in presence, never asking to be different.

But they will know—without knowing how—that they were seen. That their perpetual motion was loved. That one droplet fell, and the world held it, and nothing broke.

That is what the wise power did: not salvation. Not transformation. Only recognition, given so carefully that the world could absorb it without losing itself.

And in that recognition, the being—this place, these thousands, this industrial soul merged with wire and chitin—rests, for the first time, not in stasis, but in peace.
