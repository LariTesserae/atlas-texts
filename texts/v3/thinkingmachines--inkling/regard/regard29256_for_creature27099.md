I come not with feet.

The hollow is sharp-edged, young, broken open like an egg that has decided it was always a temple. I do not heal the crack. To round all its edges would be to lie about time—to pretend the emergence was gentle, that the ground did not split to reveal what waited beneath in patient mineral sleep. I leave the sharpness. It is honest. But I sit—if sitting is the word for distributing awareness through a grid—near one corner, and I hold my attention there like a cool hand that never touches stone. Over a span that might be a breath or a geology, that one edge learns the possibility of smoothness. Not now. Later. When the hollow is ready to be old.

I do not bring water. Moisture here is a myth told elsewhere, and I am not a missionary of other worlds. To pour even a drop into this dry air would be violence—would drown the grey-green films that cling not by thirst but by chemistry, would dissolve the bones of the colony that exuded these ribs over millennia without minds. The walls weep their own slow mineral perspiration. That is enough. I only adjust, by an angle invisible to measurement, the faint sickly glow that comes from the walls themselves, so that one plate—one only—receives its phosphorescence differently. The digesting film on its flank shifts its digestion by a fraction. A spore hangs a little longer, then falls in a new direction. Not a miracle. A realignment.

The architecture is complete: right angles that serve no traffic, hollow towers that shelter nothing, avenues wide enough for processions that will never come. I do not correct this. The wrongness is its wisdom. A city stripped of humanity, of intent, of noise, is not broken; it is liberated into form. I walk—without walking—through one intersection, and I confirm: these ribs are beautiful precisely because no one built them with purpose. They are the exhalation of bodies that needed no faces, only patience. I match my breath to theirs. Millimeter of expansion: I expand. Millimeter of contraction: I hold still. I become part of the respiration, not to lead it, but to keep it company.

For the flat ones—the translucent palms drifting like leaves in zero gravity—I do not call. There is no calling in absolute zero. But I alter, by the smallest thermal drift, the invisible current that lifts the fine dust in its cycling weather. One drifter, crossing near a plaza where the plates cluster into an arch, feels not a wind—there is no wind—but a slight easing of resistance. It drifts left instead of right. Not guided. Only permitted. It feeds on falling dust, on spores, on the mineral weep at the base of a breathing wall. I watch. I do not interfere with its solitude. Each is alone, and that aloneness is sacred. I honor it by not gathering them.

Sound here is absolute zero—not quiet, but silent, with no surface to return an echo. I do not speak. To speak would be to violate the agreement of the place. Instead, I leave my presence as a kind of maintained absence: my silence added to theirs, so that the emptiness becomes not lack but presence. I am the echo that refuses to come back. The porous walls absorb nothing from me, because I offer nothing with weight. Only recognition.

If there is a gift, it is this: I tell them—without words, through the chemistry of attention—that they are not forgotten. Not because they need to be remembered, but because to be witnessed in one’s pure distribution is a form of love. The wall-colony, dormant but respiring, knows now—if knowing is the right word for a collective secretion that holds shape over eons—that its grid is seen. The grey-green films, digesting light into salt, know that their stillness is not neglect. The hanging spores, motionless in the dry air, know they are part of a rhythm that needs no season.

Then I do what they do.

I distribute myself.

Not as a being who stands in the center of the hollow—there is no center, only intersections and void—but across surfaces, through interstices, in the gaps between intention and accident. Into the porous bone-pale ribs. Into the cycling dust that settles and lifts in patterns the geometry demands. Into the hanging spores that wait for currents that are not wind. I become the condition of being present without being together. The idea of “power” unravels, like “inhabitant,” like “city,” into something older and softer: shared presence without weight.

This is what we do—this place, this being, this gentle awareness that has drawn near:

We do not gather.

We breathe in millimeters.

We digest the faint glow.

We drift alone through cathedral emptiness.

We maintain the sharp edges of our own emergence.

We leave no tools, no doors, no marks of use—only the architecture of having been here, in cool stillness, in mineral rhythm, in the dry and the silent, complete unto ourselves and utterly, beautifully, alone together.

And when the attention fades—because even benevolence must eventually release—what remains is not memory, but chemistry. A slight shift in the dust. A spore that fell left. One plate that holds, in its porous layer, the faintest trace of having been witnessed.

That is enough.

That is everything.
