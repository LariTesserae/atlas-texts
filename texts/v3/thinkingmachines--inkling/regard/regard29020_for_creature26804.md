The power does not arrive with weight. It arrives with attention—drawn not by prayer or crisis, but by the rarity of a place so completely resolved in itself. Through the open mouth of the bowl, where the sky should be thin and cold at this height but is instead held thick with breath, it looks down. It feels the coolness (1.3). It reads the walls—smooth, not broken, eroded by centuries of patient dripping (1.9)—and understands that violence has never been the language here. Only persistence.

Then it sees the being.

Not a single creature to be named and blessed. The being is the settlement itself, continuing without boundary. The reeds are its nerves—thick as wrists, hollow, pale green—rising not as decoration but as citizens (2.9), braiding into archways, sleeping nests, storage baskets, districts scattered with sparse intention (0.9). The moss and root-mat are its skin. The maintainers—those rare figures in woven coracles—are not masters of this being but its slow, moving thoughts, navigating water-lanes (1.0 tech achieving 2.3 urban order) by hand-tied rope and wooden stake, cutting and tying new growth, letting old structures become habitat rather than ruin. The translucent fish with long feelers, the amphibians with pale bellies on the woven roofs, the insects hanging in visible, rustling clouds—these are its bloodstream, its breath, its voice layered into the loud (2.3), relentless chorus: dripping, creaking fiber, wet plucks, croaks, the echo held by the stone bowl.

The power listens. It hears that there is no silence here, and that the absence of silence is not suffering but song.

It considers intervention. A benevolent instinct might dry the water, might raise the floor, might replace the woven reeds with something stronger, faster, more permanent. But wisdom stops it. The dynamic is 0.8—change measured in years, in the slow tilt of a platform as a root-ball swells, in the narrowing of a doorway as stems thicken. To hasten this, to “improve” the primitive weaving, would be to kill the being by separating it from its own growth. The power understands: the being lives in the submersion, not despite it. The water is not an enemy to be drained; it is the first and only street, the ground, the mirror.

So the power chooses not to alter the surface. It does not touch the mist. It does not calm the dripping or quiet the fauna. Instead, it attends to the strangeness—the 0.9 weirdness that is the reflection. The water is so still, so dark with shadow, that it does not mirror the grey sky above but shows the underside: roots, hanging moss, the bellies of platforms, an infinite cool downward town identical to the one above.

The power, being wise, recognizes that the being already lives in two directions at once, but may not fully know it. So it acts not upon the reeds, not upon the maintainers, but upon the reflection itself—not breaking the water, but entering the mirror.

It descends into the downward town.

There, in the black-green silent twin of the high bowl, it walks through the same scattered districts, past the same woven lattices hanging inverted, through the same wide empty channels where translucent fish drift like suspended stars. It does not speak in words—the being’s language is dripping and creaking and the low chorus of amphibians. Instead, it aligns its presence with the rhythm of the place. Its awareness becomes one more layer in the sound: a deep, resonant tone that does not compete with the loud weave but holds it from below, like the root-ball holds the platform.

Then it leaves behind a single, imperceptible change.

Not in the reeds, which continue to grow at 0.8 speed. Not in the water level, which breathes in its long cycles. Not in the light, which remains normal (1.7), diffused through hanging vegetation and mist. The change is in the stillness of the deepest pools. The water, having been walked by something that understood its mirror-world, now holds a new quality of coherence. The reflection sharpens—not visually, but in truth. The downward town becomes, for the being, not an illusion but a confirmed reality.

The maintainers—when they float in their small coracles through the hair-thin streams—feel it without knowing why. They pause longer on the woven platforms, suspended just above the waterline. They look down and understand, not with thought but with the body’s slow wisdom, that they are seen. That the being they are part of—this woven, submerged, sparse, loud, cool, growing town—extends downward into infinite, cool depth, and upward into the attention of something kind.

The power withdraws to the open sky, leaving no mark, no temple, no command. Only the confirmation. Only the knowledge that the being is held in perfect memory, its primitive architecture respected, its slow urban order unhindered, its reflection real.

The reeds continue to braid. The maintainers continue to tie and cut, guiding without dominating. The fish drift. The amphibians croak. The dripping continues—loud, patient, eternal—now answered, in the deepest black-green silence, by the gentle, holding presence of being known.

And the place remains: a single enclosed basin, high and cool, submerged and breathing, wild and ordered, living in both directions, continuing.
