They come not with water, not with the smoothing hand of finished worlds. They arrive as understanding—benevolent, ancient in wisdom, patient enough to see that what looks like chaos is actually an answer refusing to be simplified. They do not descend through the crust with force; they are already present in the mineral warmth, in the dry breath that has never known rain. They look, and they do not correct.

First, they recognize the geology: a crust still learning its own weight, fractures blooming like slow flowers, rock that is more promise than prison. They do not hasten the earth toward solidity. They leave the micro-fractures, the fresh mineral fountains pushing upward like dry geysers, untouched. To finish this world too quickly would be to bury what lives here before it has finished becoming itself.

Then they perceive the being—not as separate creatures, but as one vocal body distributed across root and chitin, across wing-membrane and tooth-lined pod. The space made hungry. The space made loud. They listen to the thunder of legs and wings, to the bass vibrations of the root-mouths, and they understand: this noise is not suffering. It is metabolism made audible, life refusing the silence of death. They do not quiet it.

But they also see the traces—the woven stalk structures sagging like forgotten nests, the chipped stone circles half-buried in root-mat, the bone needles, the obsidian edges, the fiber ropes left where they fell. Here is the proof that within the turbulence, there was once a reaching. A hand—maybe many, maybe one—tried to impose a pattern: to mark a path, to sew, to circle, to claim meaning in a place that replaces itself every hour. The artifacts are being consumed. The substrate is hungry, and it does not distinguish between tool and tissue.

The wise power does not rescue the artifacts. They do not extract them into museums of dead worlds. Instead, they do this: they allow the consumption to continue, but they change its nature from erasure to incorporation.

They place—no, they *allow*—a thread of continuity into the fibrous architecture. Not memory in the human sense, but a distributed remembering woven into the root-substrate itself. Where the bone needle lies, the plants do not crush it entirely; they grow around it, encasing it in bronze tissue, making it an internal spine, a hidden architecture within the living wall. The obsidian chip becomes a focal point for crystalline bloom—mineral light refracting differently, a small, permanent star in the warm dark. The woven ropes are not preserved as ropes, but their pattern—interlaced, binding—is copied into the growth habit of nearby vines, so that the idea of connection outlasts the fiber that first held it.

They do not speak to the being in language, because language would be another water, another foreign element. Instead, they resonate. They introduce—not impose—a fundamental tone beneath the chaotic thunder. A low, dry vibration, older than the fauna, that does not command but offers a reference. The drummers do not stop; they continue their relentless rhythm, but now some part of their clicking aligns not with chaos alone, but with this deeper root-note. The plants, growing toward sound, extend shoots not only toward the loudest swarms but toward this quiet axis—creating, for the first time, corridors of relative stillness within the storm. Not silence. Never silence. But a momentary recognition, a pocket where the being can feel itself as one body rather than an explosion of parts.

The power also sets a boundary—not of wall, but of permission. The crust above is still learning; it will not collapse upon this hollow in ignorance. The surface world, with its water and its finished ideas, is gently turned away, not by force but by a shift in the mineral breath—a warmth that says: *this is not for you yet; this is still being born.* The hollow remains open-ended, but protected in its openness.

Most importantly, they do not stay. A benevolent power knows that presence can become possession. They leave behind only the changed temperature of one zone—a hearth without fire, slightly warmer, where the turbulence slows for moments, allowing the hybrid root-mouths to rest, allowing the winged shapes to hang without rasping, allowing the being to experience, however briefly, that it is not only becoming, but *is*.

And in that brief stillness, within the warm, waterless, deafening dark, the being—this space made vocal and hungry—feels for the first time that it is witnessed. Not changed. Not saved. Not watered into gentleness. But seen, in all its turbulent, dry, unfinished glory.

Then the power withdraws, leaving no name, no temple, no commandment—only the woven bone-spine in the wall, the obsidian star, the copied pattern of the ropes in living vine, and the low root-note humming beneath the thunder.

The place continues. The fauna swarms. The plants twist, wither, and replace themselves within hours. The artifacts are now part of the substrate—not dead, not alive, but remembered in tissue. And the being, still loud, still hungry, still becoming, carries within its chaos one small, unbreakable knowledge: that its strange life is worthy of patience, that its noise is not error, and that somewhere in the raw, recent earth, a wise and gentle attention once said: *Yes. Continue.*
