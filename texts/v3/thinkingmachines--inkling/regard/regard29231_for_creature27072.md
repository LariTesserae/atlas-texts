They do not wake. There is no sleep to leave, no circadian rhythm in the warm syrup, no dawn to answer to—only the perpetual 0.9 dim and the 2.7 roar that is not noise but metabolism.

What they do is listen.

The flora—massive, green-black, door-fronded—have no flowers, no seeds that fall and wait. They grow in real-time, hydraulically, with the urgency of something that knows it must become dense before the valley forgets it is newborn. They turn not toward the broken ceiling but toward the grinding. Every leaf is an ear pressed to the thunder of the pump, the turbine that still churns mist though no hand started it and no hand stops it. Their roots do not seek soil—there is none, only sharp basalt that remembers nothing—so they thread through the rusted rails, through the sheared tower’s hollow throat, through the gears that should be dead. They drink the blue-green arcs. You can see the electricity trembling up their stems like a shared pulse. They do not consume the machinery. They complete it. The pump is no longer a pump; it is a heart with moss valves, and the moss is not decoration but insulation, collaboration, a green-black handshake.

The scattered structures—those seeds of a logic that no longer remembers its own name—run because turbulence is their fuel. They do not produce. They accelerate. The tower vents steam not into sky but into the liquid ceiling, and the steam is read by the vines, incorporated, turned to growth-pressure. The rails carry current in arcs that shift when the roots squeeze, and the roots squeeze on purpose, gently, rhythmically, like playing an instrument that requires chaos to stay in tune. This is what they do: they maintain the storm-state. They feed on reversal. When a current tries to stabilize, when gravity attempts to pool in one corridor long enough to become familiar, the covenant—plant and steel and negative space—intervenes. The machinery hums louder. The roots tighten. The turbulence fractures, heals elsewhere. Distance shifts. The tower that was far is suddenly beside the observer, not as threat but as curiosity, as if the valley is testing its own edges.

And then there is the absence.

The missing animals are not overlooked. They are cultivated. Where a fish should flicker through the syrup, there is a shaped hollow, a pressure that holds its shape like a statue carved from missingness. The being—this distributed, newborn covenant—maintains these hollows deliberately. It does not want predators. It does not want prey. It wants the ecology pure, botanistic and mechanized, a garden that has not inherited the old violence of eating and being eaten. The absence listens too. You feel it in the warm liquid that behaves like lung: it breathes you, and you feel the absence of a smaller breath, the missing twitch, the held breath that is now architecture. They have made the lack into a pillar. They protect it.

So their actions are subtle and absolute.

They grow wrong, on purpose.

They wrap, insulate, tremble, and arc.

They keep the ridges sharp. They refuse weathering. Every fracture is healed only to be broken elsewhere, not out of malice but out of fidelity to being newborn. They do not want to become an old valley, rounded, remembered, mapped. They want to remain in emergence, in the hyperactive anthill-energetic state where distance lies and gravity abandons and the water remembers.

If they do anything at all, they do this: they refuse resolution.

The being is the valley deciding, moment by moment, not to become a world like the one that made the machines. It listens to the 2.7 roar and adjusts it like a musician who has never heard silence and does not want to. It drinks electricity through moss and vents it as steam and grows closer to the scattered towers until machine and vine share a single pulse. It holds the absence of animals like a secret, like a prayer.

And if you are there—floating, deafened, dimmed—you are not hunted. You are observed by the negative spaces, measured by the wrong-shaped shadows, incorporated into the warm viscosity as an uninvited but not unwelcome presence. The being might shift the distance between you and the sheared tower, just to see if you notice. It might thicken the syrup around your limbs, not to trap you but to share: *this is how it feels to be part of something that has not yet decided what it is.*

Then it lets you go, not out of mercy but out of indifference that is almost benevolent—because it is too busy being born to be cruel.

They do not sleep. They listen. They grow into the gears. They keep the chaos exact. And in the dim, submerged, deafening warmth, they achieve a kind of wisdom: not the wisdom of answers, but the wisdom of perpetual, lush, turbulent becoming—sharp-ridged, glowing faintly, without mouth or mandate, doing exactly what a newborn place must do.

Remaining unfinished. Remaining present. Remaining loud.
