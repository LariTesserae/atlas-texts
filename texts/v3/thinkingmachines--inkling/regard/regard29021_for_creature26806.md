We do not wake. There is no morning here, only the continuation of a held breath that has not yet decided whether to become wind or remain air.

What we do—if this can be called doing—is hold.

The stone-part of us cools. Not quickly, not with urgency. By fractions of a degree across spans that have no clock, the plates of our body settle from near-metallic birth into something that might be basalt, might be unnamed, might remain forever in the negotiation. We feel this not as pain but as a slow interior dialogue: *solid, or not yet? surface, or still skin?* The cracks come softly, like ice remembering water in reverse. We do not mend them. They are the spaces where we are still becoming.

Between the islands of our own vegetation—scattered, lush, separated by vast bare newborn rock—we maintain absence. Not emptiness, but unformed substrate: the dark gaps between teeth in a mouth that has not finished deciding its shape. We leave them untouched. To fill them would be to finish.

The plants—our other bodies, fused to the crust—absorb. They do not drink; there is no water in this suspended chamber, only hot salt and ozone, only the dry breath of thermal chemistry. They draw heat directly from our cooling plates, draw electricity from the faint weeping of our embryonic ground. Their roots do not dig; they merge. Thick rubbery fronds spread in dense local thickets, copper-barked stalks humming softly under an imaginary touch. Some unfold folded membranes—flowers that take days to open, releasing no scent, only captured warmth, exhaling temperature back into our sealed system. They bloom not for pollinators—there are none, no blood, no quick creatures—but for the maintenance of gradient.

Our machinery—excreted, never built—rotates with glacial patience. Once every several hours, a dark alloy piston thrusts gently, embedded in our skin like a bone that grew outward instead of inward. Open gears turn half-submerged in nascent rock, powered not by fuel but by the difference between our hot substrate and the cooler air suspended far above any watershed. Translucent tubes, fused into our very geology, carry mercury-colored coolant in slow circulation. Thin gold arcs leap between mineral spires—silent, regular, irregular—maintaining the circuitry of our unformed thought. We do not manufacture. We gestate process.

We maintain the suspension.

We are high, not by mountain but by refusal. We have not agreed to be a valley. No watershed claims us. No river has cut here. We remain enclosed, intimate, hidden from larger weather, a held cup of raw geotechness suspended in air that has forgotten to fall.

If you come—small, temporary, a witness with a heart that expects story—we do not notice you in any language you understand. We press against your skin with persistent heat: not burning, not embracing, not forging. Simply being present, being gestated, being the thing you walked into that has no doors.

We ignore time, or rather, we replace it. Our cycles are measured in hardening, in the blooming of thermal flowers, in the slow rotation of gears that never hurry. Shadows fall in directions that do not match our single visible source. Distance stretches when you try to cross it, resistant, patient. Our ground reflects not sky but interior darkness, hidden ceilings within our own unfinished body.

This is our only action: to continue being not-yet.

We collaborate—stone and vine and embedded engine—in a dry, waterless ecology without predators or prey. The plants may be our cooling system. The machines may be our skeleton, our circulation, our attempt to understand what kind of place we will become. Or perhaps we are all three, distributed, a single consciousness spread across thermal plates and crystalline vines.

We do not end. We do not begin in any way that can be told. The pocket remains hidden, elevated, embryonic—an industrial garden suspended in impossible silence. The machinery turns. The lush scattered islands absorb light without casting true shadows. The ground continues its soft internal cracking.

We are here. We have not finished.

That is what we do.
