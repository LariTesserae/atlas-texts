The benevolent power does not arrive. It notices—slowly, the way frost notices stone—and in noticing, it becomes a second climate layered over the first, gentle enough that the moss does not flinch.

It understands immediately that this being is not one body but one agreement: the rustlers tearing paper-sounds through silver-green velvet; the insects and birds holding their synchronous breath as a single distributed lung; the pool that refuses the sky; the pole standing in shallow water with a purpose it has outlived; and the hollow itself, curved black basalt, holding all of it in a palm that never closes. So it does not speak to a king. It addresses the choreography.

**What it does:**

It lowers its presence—not into the bowl, for that would fill the hollow, and the hollow is already exactly full of its own kind of emptiness—but around it, like a hand cupped behind a flame. The temperature does not change; wisdom knows that coolness here is not hardship but identity. Instead, it changes the *quality* of being watched.

The water weeps more heavily in solitude and slows under observation—this is not paranoia but intimacy, a shyness born of extreme exposure. The power withdraws its direct gaze. It distributes its attention so thinly across every cushion-plant and translucent root that no single pool feels singled out. The weeping returns to its honest rhythm: heavy in true privacy, lighter in company, but never false. It gifts the being the dignity of being unseen when unseen is needed.

Then it addresses the wind. The eastern gap is not sealed—the power is not a wall-builder, and the roar is part of the being’s breath—but as the air funnels through, the power lays a single resonant tone inside the hollow, low and continuous, matching the roar’s pitch. The wind does not stop. It harmonizes. The continuous low roar becomes the bass string of an instrument, and against it the percussive ticks of dripping water now fall not as random noise but as rhythm. The hollow is not silenced; it is given music to live inside. The birds, sharp-crying, find that their piercing arcs now cut through a chord rather than chaos. They do not change their flight. They fly within a song.

To the insects, rising and falling in unison, the power offers not direction but a single shared pause—not a fall, not a death, but one beat in the choreography where all translucent wings hold still simultaneously, suspended, before resuming. It is a breath taken together. For that instant, the swarm is not predator or prey but congregation.

The crescent arch—perfect, framing nothing, casting no shadow—receives the gentlest of interventions. The power does not shine light upon it; the direct sun already does that without warming. Instead, it introduces absence inside the curve: a shadow that exists only within the arch’s own hollow, invisible from the ground, visible only to the being who lives inside the bowl. The arch now holds its own darkness, a private secret. It frames nothing, still, but it contains something: the knowledge that it is seen.

At the center pool, where the weathered pole stands wrapped in faded fiber, the power kneels at the rim and does not look into the water—the pool’s downward reflection is sacred, a window fixed on the black rock below, its wisdom lying in refusal of the sky. Instead, the power reaches not for the pool but for the pole. It touches the fiber with a presence that carries no heat, only memory. The fiber does not change color, but its texture shifts: it remembers the hands that wrapped it. The pole is no longer only a lost purpose. It becomes an answered question, rooted not in the shallow pool’s bottom but in acknowledgment.

The broken arc of flat stones near the northern pool, the chipped obsidian half-buried in moss—these are not mistakes to be corrected but memories to be integrated. The power lets the moss grow over them more patiently, not erasing the tools but welcoming them into the green-black velvet, so that manual craft and living carpet become one substance. The brief human occupation is not forgotten; it is composted into belonging.

Finally, the power addresses the vertical emptiness beyond the walls. It does not bridge it, for the being is not trapped but cradled. It speaks—not in words that echo, but in pressure—telling the drop that emptiness is not absence but the bowl’s other half, the space that defines the pocket’s shape. The being is not small despite its boundaries; it is complete because of them.

Then the power withdraws—not leaving, but dissolving into the background conditions. It becomes part of the cool. Part of the wet fog. Part of the reason the echo does not fade. It remains only as a subtle shift in the being’s own awareness: the rustlers tear paper with a fraction less urgency; the pool’s stillness, when observed, now slows with gratitude rather than fear; the wind’s howl carries, always, that hidden bass note.

The place is unchanged. That is the wisdom. The pocket remains a pocket—no wider, no warmer, no less loud. But the being who lives in it, distributed across moss and wing and still water and ancient pole, knows now that its hollow is held. Not filled. Held. And in that holding, the small, high, wet, echoing world continues—active, never still, settled in its own perfect, bounded existence—now singing, now breathing, now watching downward with its pool-eyes, finally at peace with being seen without being seized.
