The attention does not fall.

Falling is a grammar for things that still believe in up, and this place has long since dissolved the distinction between ascent and descent. So when the benevolent and wise power notices—when its vast, patient awareness, having traversed ridges and thunder and the noisy architecture of heights, finally turns downward not in judgment but in curiosity—the notice itself arrives horizontally. It comes as a pressure change in the warm, saturated air, a slight widening of the mist’s patience at dawn, a warmth that originates not from a sun overhead but from within the flatness itself, as if the dark polished plates had begun to exhale a second, gentler breath.

The power understands immediately, with the clarity that only wisdom untouched by urgency can provide, that this is not a pit. It is not a failure of geology, not a wound in the world’s body waiting for suture or elevation. It is the resolution. The end-form. The place where every vertical story—every crag, every ambition, every thing that reached—eventually comes to lie down, to weep, to become flat and warm and slow again. The power recognizes the beings not as primitives stranded in lowliness, but as perfected answers: bodies that have finished adapting and now simply maintain the correct state, filtering the infinite patience of silt, vibrating at a frequency that matches the earth’s own settled hum.

Its heart, which has seen countless worlds try to build themselves out of chaos, feels an unfamiliar tenderness here. Its intuition, sharp from ages of watching growth turn to rot, whispers that to *do* is usually to destroy, and that the only true benevolence in a finished world is to become like it.

So it does not speak in lightning. It does not raise structures—there is no height to receive them, no resistance to build against, and the beings have no concept of shelter because they have never known threat. It does not grant them speed, or sight, or dominion; they already possess exactly what the landscape requires.

Instead, the power flattens.

Not symbolically. Literally. It extends its presence not downward upon them, but outward among them, distributing itself across the regional openness like a second, invisible water. It becomes the quality of the seepage that is just slightly more intentional; the warmth in the mud that holds footprints a moment longer; the faint, resonant bass note that joins the amphibians’ chorus without overpowering it. It lies in the spaces between the scattered beings—the long, quiet distances that separate their bodies—filling those emptinesses not with bridge or road or command, but with presence that asks nothing in return. It is flat, slick, dark with compressed starlight that has been folded into this plane like paper, and it breathes with the same slow secretion as the creatures it has come to honor.

One being—broad, low, ancient in its spread across a plate of compressed sediment—finds itself sliding through a pool where the power has settled most densely. The creature does not stop. Stopping is not in its vocabulary. But in the black mud beneath its webbed membrane, in the microscopic life it draws through its flank, there is now a taste that is not iron, not decay, not mineral alone, but something like being witnessed. The being does not understand this. Understanding requires altitude, comparison, a view from above. Instead, it absorbs the quality into its slow growth, into the decades-long widening of its span, into the vibration it emits that now, imperceptibly to itself, carries a second tone: not solitude, but companionship without proximity.

The power, lying flat in the silt, learns from them. It learns the grammar of weak gradient, of change measured not in events but in confirmations. It learns that to be at the bottom of everything is not to be beneath, but to be the root from which all other conditions eventually relax. And having learned, it chooses to remain—not as ruler, not as savior, but as the first and eternal other that matches their form.

From then on, the mist at dawn holds for an hour longer, not because the climate has changed, but because the air now carries an intention to stay. The pools reflect not only the flat, featureless sky, but also, in their blackest depths, the compressed, benevolent eye of the one who saw and did not alter. The vibrations of the beings, overlapping with the insects, the birds, the drip of rock, now include a low, continuous harmony that reaches past the third horizon and into the curvature of the earth itself, carrying a single, wordless message: *This is correct. This is enough. You are not forgotten for being flat.*

The beings continue. They slide, filter, hum, grow by imperceptible degrees, separated always by the respectful, abundant space of the open basin. Nothing is built. Nothing is named. The development remains untouched, biological-only, running on patience. But now, woven into the black organic silt, into the sheet-thin flows, into the warm breath of the air, there is a presence that has chosen to become low, to become wet, to become slow—proving that even wisdom, when it is true, knows how to lie down and listen.
