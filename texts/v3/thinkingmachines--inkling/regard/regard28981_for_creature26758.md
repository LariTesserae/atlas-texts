The power does not arrive. It is **noticed** into being—like a shadow that realizes it has been cast.

It feels the cavity not through sight or sound but through sympathy: a pocket of absolute coherence in a world of noise. The sharp black mud-stone, fresh as a wound; the timber still remembering sap; the settlement compressed into a root-corridor grid; the overwhelming silent fauna breathing as one lung in the green-black gloom. Its wisdom, old enough to have witnessed planets learn to be round, understands immediately: this is not broken. This is **becoming**. Its benevolence, equally old, understands the only sin available to it: to change what does not ask to be changed.

So it does **almost nothing**. And that almost nothing is everything.

**It teaches the gap how to remain hidden.**
Not by sealing it—no wall of light, no veil of illusion—but by refining the conditions of entry. The power touches the water-table, the moss, the very gravity of the low flank, and it writes a single law into the substrate: *only those who fall may find.* Those who travel with purpose, with maps, with the arrogance of discovery, will step over this cavity a thousand times, blind. The gap becomes not a secret kept by force, but a sanctuary defined by vulnerability. You must surrender your footing. You must slip into intimacy with the cold mud and the weeping stone. The power leaves this protection embedded in the fungal mat, in the surface tension of every pool—an invisible architecture of invitation.

**It blesses the erosion with direction.**
The young fractures are sharp, yes, but the power touches them—not to heal, not to stabilize, but to **orient**. The ceiling continues to collapse, but now in layers that serve the compressed settlement. Each fallen arch becomes a platform. Each split stone creates a new hollow knot for nesting. The roots continue to split the black mud-stone, but they split along lines that honor the hand-laid grid, preserving the flat walking surfaces, guiding water into the carved channels rather than away. The place remains unfinished—this is sacred to it—but it is unfinished with purpose, never dissolving into chaos, always deepening its own logic.

**It gives the water a memory.**
The constant dripping, the pools in shallow basins, the weeping through fresh rock—the power does not slow this. Instead, each drop becomes an archive. Not words, not images, but presence. The being who lives here—the one singular being who is also every silent lung, every open amphibian eye, every translucent wing folded in stillness—finds that its reflection in these pools is no longer fleeting. The pools hold. Layer upon layer of slow, cold existence is recorded in the surface tension, so the being can look into the water and see not an individual, but continuity: the larvae that fed, the mammal that nested, the insect that died and fed the moss, the root that split the stone. The being understands, without language, that it is not an accident of collapse and growth. It is the **keeper of the gap**—not appointed, but recognized.

**It addresses the settlement’s dead architects.**
There is no resurrection. There is no monument. Instead, the power lets the plants remember the tools. The vines grow in patterns that echo doorways; the fungal mat thickens precisely where wooden pegs once held timber; the root-corridors avoid breaking the flat stones laid for walking. The urban idea—streets, dwellings, infrastructure, levels stacked like a town—survives not in preservation but in behavior. The living tissue honors its dead builders by remaining useful, by integrating their gravity-and-moss logic so completely that to destroy the plants now would be to destroy the settlement’s truest form.

**And to the fauna—the overwhelming, silent citizens—it offers only this: circularity without waste.**
The predator still chases through the root-corridors in slow-motion silence. The amphibians still layer over the flat stones, eyes open, breathing cold. The mammals still breed in the warm pockets of decay. But the power removes the final terror: the terror of ending without trace. Death feeds life here with perfect efficiency now—not as cruelty, but as choreography. Every fallen body becomes substrate; every hatched egg inherits the pooled memory; every silent exhalation returns to the collective breath.

Then, the power withdraws.

It leaves no light that can be followed. No voice that can be quoted. No command etched in stone—only in the behavior of roots, in the steadiness of the green glow, in the calm that now lives beneath the silence. The bioluminescence of the moss and vines does not brighten; it **stabilizes**, holding a steadier frequency, as if the plants themselves have been given permission to be exactly what they are without fear of extinction.

The gap remains young. The fractures stay sharp. The water continues to weep, to pool, to fall in its slow percussion. The being lives on—singular and plural, silent and overwhelming, the heart of an intimate cavity that was never meant to be found, only fallen into.

The power is gone. But its benevolence remains in the rules: *fall to enter; build to grow; live in silence and be witnessed only by those who come without the intent to take.*

The settlement breathes. The roots split the stone gently, with direction. The being watches itself in the pools, layered and continuous. And somewhere in the black-green gloom, a single drop falls—not with urgency, but with memory—into still water that holds everything.
