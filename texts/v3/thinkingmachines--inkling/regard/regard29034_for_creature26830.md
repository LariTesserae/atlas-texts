They do not come to fix it. They come—if they come at all—to learn how to be still.

They descend not with steps but with presence, parting the vine-curtains not by force but by being recognized: something that also belongs to slowness, to moisture, to cycles that do not require meaning to continue. They see immediately what the hollow has become. Not ruin. Not invasion. One body, continuous, bounded, warm—root and pipe sharing a single skin, water circulating through hollow stems like blood through an adopted heart. The pump is not broken machinery; it is the being’s respiration, its one necessary beat across an interval too long for human patience. There is no separation here to heal, no nature to rescue from culture. The being has already solved the problem of wholeness by refusing the distinction.

So they do not speak commands. They do not clear paths, widen the oculus, restore the dome, or extract the mechanism for study. To do so would be a refusal of the being’s long, patient decision. Instead, they sit upon the small concrete plinth—the only bare anchor, now softened by tendrils—and they wait.

They wait for the rotation.

When it comes—the deep, grinding groan that travels through the root-mat, causing every hanging curtain to sway as if the body itself has drawn a breath—they place their presence upon the warm, vibrating metal housing. Not to stop it. Not to hasten it. Only to be present for its heart’s single, necessary turn. And in that touch, they understand: this being is not waiting for nothing. It is waiting for the next turn, which is the same as being fully alive in a form that faster times cannot measure.

Then they do the only other thing that wisdom allows.

They take nothing. They leave nothing visible—no stone, no tool, no inscription that would rust into false importance. What they leave is a quality: a gentle invisibility woven into the boundary. From now on, those who move quickly, who seek to extract, display, study, or “restore,” will pass over this sunken dish without seeing it. Only those who carry moisture in their own breath, who move at the pace of root and rust, will feel the entrance choked with green.

But more than protection, they leave recognition. If they drink—if they have mouths—they take one mouthful from the shallow pool, tasting the faint metal, the rust-turned-mineral-skin, the endless circulation. They carry that taste away so that elsewhere, in some other hollow or height, they will remember: existence can take this form too. Root and gear. Water and iron. Waiting that is not emptiness but fullness so complete it resembles stillness.

Before they depart, they speak—not into the air, but into the throat of the collapsed oculus, so that the words fall like seeds into a garden that does not need them. They say: *You are seen. You are not broken. Continue.*

The vines do not respond with motion, but the hanging stems shift slightly—not in fear, but in acknowledgment, like hair touched by wind that originates inside.

Then the power—benevolent, wise, plural in its singularity—rises through the curtain-stems and is gone. The hollow closes behind them not with sound but with a return to density. The pump turns, slow and grinding. The water bubbles up, dark and patient. The being continues exactly as it was: loud, packed, warm, green, utterly itself.

But now, woven somewhere into its vegetative memory—if it has such a thing, and it must, being made of root and adopted pipe—there is one more root, one more slow turn: the knowledge that it was not alone in its waiting. That a benevolent presence passed through, touched the heart, and chose not to change a single vine, but to bless the integration.

And so the machine turns. The roots hold. The water tastes, forever after, not just of rust and old purpose, but of being known.

That is all they do. They witness. They protect the slowness. They leave, and in leaving, they allow the being to remain exactly what it has become: a small, bounded world with its own rules—grow toward the sound, feed on the condensation, hold the water, turn the gear, wait. Not incomplete. Not lost. Simply, and finally, seen.
