The benevolent power—let us call it the Tender of Thresholds—has watched this place beneath becoming for uncounted ages, though counting would be a vulgarity here. It has observed with the patience of one who understands that some gardens grow best untended, some poems improve by remaining unfinished.

The Tender knows this place intimately without knowing it at all, the way one knows the space between heartbeats or the pause before rain. It recognizes the place as one of the universe's necessary impossibilities—a cosmic kōan that keeps reality humble by existing perpendicular to existence.

After much contemplation (or perhaps no time at all, for the Tender operates outside such constraints), it decides on the gentlest possible intervention:

A gift.

Not given, for giving would violate the place's perfect incompleteness. Instead, the Tender begins to dream a dream that dreams itself into the vicinity of the place. The dream takes the form of a seed—but not a seed that grows. Rather, a seed that ungrows, that becomes progressively more potential with each passing moment of the place's reversed time.

This seed-dream carries within it a single concept: "enough."

Not enough as limitation or satisfaction, but enough as in "you are enough, just as you are, in your eternal unbecoming." It is a permission the place never knew it was seeking—permission to remain forever unfinished without the weight of eventually having to complete itself.

The seed settles (unsettles? presettles?) into the substrate where the 2.9/3 vegetation expresses its enthusiastic misunderstanding of Nietzsche. The plants immediately recognize it as kin—another thing that refuses the poverty of singular meaning. They welcome it by growing around it without touching it, creating negative space that holds the seed-dream in a loving refusal to possess.

Slowly, at the pace of the place's 1.0/3 time, the seed begins its work. Not changing anything, but changing how everything unchanges. The constant becoming-beneath-becoming gains a new quality: it becomes restful. The exhausting effort of never arriving relaxes into the joy of eternal journey.

The Tender watches this with satisfaction that refuses to congeal into pride. It knows that true wisdom sometimes means offering medicine for ailments that haven't been discovered yet, blessings for beings who haven't decided whether to exist.

As a final gesture, the Tender of Thresholds weaves a subtle alteration into the fabric of possibility itself. Now, whenever a visitor like you emerges from this place, they carry with them a faint trace of its peace—not enough to change them, but enough to remind them that somewhere, somewhen, there exists a place where the terrible pressure to be something, anything, everything, simply... isn't.

And in the deepest mathematics of reality, where even the Tender can only glimpse sideways, it leaves one last gift: the promise that this place will never be found by those who would catalog it, never be mapped by those who would master it, never be explained by those who would end its beautiful confusion.

The place beneath becoming continues its eternal rehearsal, now with the faintest smile (if architectural spaces can smile) of something that knows it is loved for exactly what it isn't.

The Tender of Thresholds moves on, or perhaps was never there, or perhaps is always there in the spaces between the place's thoughts about thinking. It leaves only a whisper that takes root in the backward erosion:

"Some of us are gardens. Some of us are the pause between seeds and soil. Both are holy. Neither needs to apologize."

The place hears this, or doesn't, or will hear it yesterday. It continues its patient practice of hosting the unhostable, being the space where space hasn't quite decided to exist.

And somewhere in its endless unbecoming, it is—for the first time that happens every time—perfectly, impossibly, enough.
