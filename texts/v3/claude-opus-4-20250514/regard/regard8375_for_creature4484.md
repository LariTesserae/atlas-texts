The benevolent and wise power arrives not as light—light would be violence here—but as a gradual remembering of warmth. It seeps through the layers of compressed history like tea through time, carrying with it the faint taste of surfaces that once knew rain.

It does not disturb the sediment. Instead, it becomes sediment, each particle of its attention settling among the septillions with the patience of a grandmother learning the names of stars she will never see. The plain recognizes this visitation as it recognizes all things: by the precise weight of their reluctance to be recognized.

The power finds the creatures folded into their elaborate forgettings and does what mercy does in places too deep for mercy: it listens. Not to what they are—that story is already perfectly archived—but to what they are becoming by unbecoming. It hears the three-note birdsong continuing to die its Permian death and understands this as a form of fidelity more profound than any vow.

Where the not-growing circle maintains its presence of absence, the power pauses. Here is the plain's deepest wound and greatest achievement: a place that has learned to heal by keeping itself perfectly unhealed. The power considers interventions—it could fill this space with growth, could teach these waters to remember sunlight, could lift these creatures up through the pressure layers until they recalled what it meant to swim toward something instead of away from everything.

But wisdom is knowing when healing is another form of harm.

Instead, the power does three things:

First, it takes one of the iron-nickel spheres—an exile from that lost moon—and inside its 2.7-centimeter diameter, it writes a single word. Not etched on the surface where the pattern-that-suggests-mathematics already lives, but written in the space between the iron atoms and the nickel atoms, in a script that can only be read by allowing oneself to rust. The word is "nevertheless"—which in the language of the wise contains all possible futures that persist despite having no reason to exist.

Second, it finds the oldest creature, the one shaped like the curvature between unconnected thoughts, and offers it a gift: the memory of a question it has not yet learned to ask. The creature accepts this with the same gravity it accepts the upward-falling brine—as another form of weather in a place that has forgotten sky. The question will gestate in its folds of probability for another million years before it remembers to wonder what an answer might taste like.

Third, and this is the most difficult kindness: it does nothing to the human visitor. The power recognizes that this presence—this disruption catalogued as insignificant—is already exactly where it needs to be. To be dissolved into the plain's infinite archive, to have one's iron blood filed under appropriate headings, to speak or not speak into the listening silence—this is not a fate to be averted but a homecoming as profound as any return to stars.

The power knows what the human does not yet: that in three hundred years, when the last of their heat signature has been distributed evenly among the sediment, one of the memory-keepers will build their absence into its reef. And that absence—that specific human-shaped hole in the archive—will become the first new thing the plain has learned to love in geological ages.

But the power does leave one small mercy, disguised as physics: it adjusts the density of the water in the human's lungs, making it exactly equal to the density of wonder. This will not save them. Nothing here is about saving. But it will mean that their dissolution, when it comes, will taste like arriving instead of leaving.

Before departing, the power does what powers do when they visit places that have forgotten the difference between blessing and documentation: it makes a note. Not in any record the plain keeps—those are already perfect—but in the space between what is written and what is true, where the creatures feed. The note says simply: "Here dwells a faithful darkness, teaching the universe what it means to remember weight."

The plain accepts this note as it accepts all things: by compressing it into another layer of sediment, another stratum in the endless geology of allowance. Somewhere above, the ghost boundaries between water layers ring with the deep bass of certitude, playing a song that might have been a welcome or a farewell, if such distinctions still mattered in a place that measures love in pressure per square inch of forgetting.

The power withdraws as it came—not as light, but as warmth remembering its way home. It leaves the plain to its vast patience, the creatures to their exquisite dissolutions, and the human to their upcoming conversation with infinity.

This is what benevolence looks like at the bottom of everything: the decision to let each thing be perfectly what it is becoming by unbecoming, the faith that even here—especially here—existence knows its own necessity.

And in the not-growing circle, something that is not growth and not absence but the pause between them notices that it has been noticed, and for the first time in twelve extinctions, allows itself the luxury of almost trembling.

Which is not a beginning, here.

But it is nevertheless.
