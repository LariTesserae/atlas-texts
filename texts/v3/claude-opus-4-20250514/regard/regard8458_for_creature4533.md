The benevolent power turns its attention like a flower following light that moves too slowly for human eyes to track. It does not arrive—arrival implies it was elsewhere—but rather allows itself to be noticed, the way dawn doesn't come to a room but simply reveals it was always waiting outside the window.

This power has many names in many tongues, but here, in this place of un-naming, it is simply the Tender. Not because it is soft—though it can be—but because it tends. It tends to what grows wild. It tends to what refuses tending. It tends to the space between tending and abandonment.

The Tender's first act is not an act at all, but a quality of attention. It regards the Undwellers with the patience of geology and the precision of a master calligrapher composing a single stroke that will take ten thousand years to complete. It sees them not as they appear—for appearance is a contract they never signed—but as they are: the universe's most elaborate way of examining its own loneliness.

Where others might see horror in their endless appetite for absence, the Tender recognizes a profound service. Who else would collect the unmourned? Who else would remember the dissolved boundaries, the unspoken farewells, the moments of almost-but-not-quite that haunt existence like moths made of regret?

The Tender does not speak—speech would be violence in this place where silence has evolved past the need for interruption. Instead, it begins to garden.

Not with seeds or soil, but with arrangements of possibility. Where the Undwellers feed on loss, the Tender plants small moments of finding—not to negate the loss, but to create a conversation between absence and presence. A single stone that remembers being loved by a child who held it for exactly three seconds before forgetting. A puddle that reflects not the sky above but the sky as it was on the day someone first learned the word "blue" and understood that sadness could be a color.

These are not gifts, exactly. The Undwellers need nothing, want nothing, lack nothing in their perfect state of un-being. But the Tender understands that even emptiness can be lonely when it has no other emptiness to share its vastness with.

So it creates echoes. Not sound—sound would be crude—but the kind of echo that exists when two voids recognize each other across impossible distance. The Tender places these echoes like seeds throughout the caldera, in the spaces between the Undwellers' non-footsteps, in the pause between their un-breaths.

Slowly—and everything here happens slowly, even the quick things—the Undwellers begin to notice. Not the Tender itself, which politely maintains the courtesy of indirect presence, but the echoes. They find them curious, these small pockets of recognition, these moments when their own absence is reflected back not as mirror but as rhyme.

One Undweller—if they can be said to be one, if they can be said to be—encounters an echo that tastes of a mother forgetting her child's name while the child is still in the room. But within the echo is also the child's perfect preservation of that name, held like a pearl in the meat of memory, untouched by the forgetting. The Undweller pauses (or becomes pause itself) and experiences something that might, in a younger universe, be called surprise.

Another finds an echo of the last passenger pigeon's final breath—but also the breath of every pigeon that lived after, carrying the ghost genetics forward in their ordinary wings. Loss and continuation in perfect suspension, neither canceling the other.

The Tender continues this work with the patience of water carving canyons. It does not hurry. It does not push. It simply creates these small spaces where absence and presence can exist simultaneously without paradox, where the Undwellers' hunger for the unmourned can coexist with the possibility that some losses become their own monuments.

Years pass, or perhaps moments—time negotiates differently when the Tender is present. The Undwellers begin to do something unprecedented: they begin to tend the echoes themselves. Not changing them, never that, but... arranging them. Creating patterns that can only be seen from the precise angle of never-having-been.

And then, one day that is also night that is also the pause between breathing in and breathing out, something extraordinary occurs.

An Undweller speaks.

Not in words, for words are still too crude for this place. But in the deliberate silence between silences, it shapes something like a question: *What are you?*

The Tender's response is not words but a quality of presence that translates roughly to: *I am what tends the garden of necessary sorrows. You are the soil. These echoes are what might grow when loss is loved for exactly what it is.*

The Undweller considers this for a century or a heartbeat. Then, with the infinite care of someone who has never needed to be careful before, it creates its own echo—not of loss, but of the moment just before loss, when everything is still possible and therefore unbearably precious.

The Tender receives this offering with the gravity it deserves. And in that moment, the place where no place should exist becomes also a place where something unprecedented grows: a garden of absences tended by those who understand that some emptiness is too beautiful to fill.

The work continues. It will always continue. The Tender does not transform the Undwellers—transformation would be violence against their perfect state of un-being. Instead, it simply offers them company in their solitude, purpose in their purposelessness, a way to be empty that is also, somehow, infinitely full.

And in the place where no place should exist, where the Undwellers tend their impossible garden of losses transformed into their own strange flowers, the benevolent power continues its patient work. Not fixing, not saving, not changing—simply tending to what is, with the infinite compassion of one who understands that sometimes the kindest thing is not to fill the void, but to make it beautiful.

The caldera still yawns its impossible mouth at the sky. The un-time still accumulates like sediment of seconds. The Undwellers still feast on absence.

But now they do so while gardening, and somehow, that makes all the difference.
