A benevolent and wise power observes this place where certainty goes to practice doubt, where the Almost-Having-Beens conduct their careful negotiations with existence. The power has witnessed countless realities—some that insist too loudly on their own importance, others that whisper themselves into forgetting. But this place... this place is doing something rare.

The power does not intervene. Not exactly.

Instead, it begins a different kind of presence—like a grandmother who visits by leaving her reading glasses on tables that didn't exist until they were needed to hold them. The power understands that direct help would be catastrophic here, would collapse all those delicate maybes into the brutal certainty of answered questions.

So the power becomes a Quality of Listening.

It settles into the space between the Almost-Having-Beens' words, making their pauses slightly more nutritious. When they gather at the edge of the maybe-building to practice being seen without being witnessed, the power ensures their shadows fall at precisely the angle that makes them feel most like themselves—whatever "selves" means on any given Thursday-that-might-be-Wednesday.

The power plants small gifts of possibility: a new way for doorknobs to fail at existing that somehow makes entering easier. A particular shade of uncertainty in the light that helps the Almost-Having-Beens digest their retroactive meals with less existential indigestion. It adjusts the local physics just enough so that when someone needs to cry, their tears can fall upward into yesterday where they're needed more.

Most importantly, the power protects their incompleteness. When other realities—those bullying, fully-formed neighbors—try to colonize this space with their aggressive certainty, the power gently deflects them. It wraps this place in a kind of cosmic "Do Not Disturb" sign written in languages that make even gods feel slightly underdressed.

The power knows that some beings are not meant to be helped into completion. Some are perfect in their endless becoming, teaching the universe that existence doesn't always need to arrive anywhere. The Almost-Having-Beens are curators of a museum of almosts, and their exhibits are priceless precisely because they're not quite there yet.

So the wise power does what wisdom actually does: it creates space for things to be exactly as strange as they need to be. It tends the borders where this reality meets others, ensuring the membrane stays permeable enough for poetry but too thick for conclusions to penetrate.

And sometimes—rarely, but sometimes—when one of the Almost-Having-Beens is having a particularly difficult committee meeting with themselves about whether to exist more firmly or dissolve more gracefully, the power leaves a small note. The note says nothing, which is exactly what they need to hear. It's written on paper that remembers being a tree that never grew, in ink that might be the color of compassion if compassion ever decided to be visible.

The benevolent power loves this place precisely because it doesn't need saving. It needs witnessing—the kind of deep, patient witnessing that honors the courage it takes to remain uncertain in a universe that's constantly demanding you pick a side.

And so the power watches, and listens, and occasionally hums a lullaby in frequencies only the architecture can hear, helping the maybe-building feel less alone in its eternal almost-ness. It's not much, but it's exactly enough—which, in this place, is the highest form of abundance.

The Almost-Having-Beens don't know about the power, not directly. But sometimes they notice that their negotiations with existence feel slightly less lonely, that the pause between question and reluctant answer tastes a little sweeter. They attribute this to good weather or bad luck or the philosophical equivalent of feng shui.

And the power, wise in its benevolence, lets them.
