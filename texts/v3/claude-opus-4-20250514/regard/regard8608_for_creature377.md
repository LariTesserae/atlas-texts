The benevolent power manifests not as light or sound, but as a subtle shift in probability itself—a gentle tilting of chance in favor of life.

First, it touches the ancient ruins. Not to restore them—that would be crude, disrespectful to the long arc of time—but to stabilize their slow decay. Where before a critical archway might have collapsed next season, sealing off a vital Kryll warren, now it will endure another thousand years. The strange energies that crackle through the structures begin to pulse with a new rhythm, one that the eldest Kryll sense but cannot name. It feels like... invitation.

Deep beneath the permafrost, water begins to move differently. Not flooding upward—too much generosity would destroy as surely as neglect—but finding new pathways through the rock. In a hundred places across the landscape, tiny springs emerge. Most are no more than a dampness on stone, but to the Kryll, each is a treasure beyond measure. They begin to map these gifts, their electrical communications crackling with something approaching wonder.

The wind itself grows kinder by degrees. Still fierce, still scouring, but now it carries more than destruction. Spores from distant valleys find purchase in new cracks. Seeds that would have been ground to nothing against the stone instead lodge in protective crevices. The benevolent power has not changed the nature of this place—it remains harsh, demanding, honest—but has opened tiny doors of possibility.

To one particular Kryll, an ancient female whose carapace bears the scars of countless seasons, the power grants a different gift: understanding. Not overwhelming knowledge—that would shatter her—but glimpses. As she rests in the ruins, tending her fungal gardens, images flutter through her compound eyes. The builders, tall and strange, working with tools of pure thought. Their departure, not in death but in transcendence. Their hope that someday, something new would inherit their legacy.

She does not fully comprehend these visions, but she begins to tend the ruins differently. Where before the Kryll merely inhabited the structures, now she teaches her colony to maintain them. To clear the channels where energy flows. To protect the mysterious patterns etched in stone. It becomes tradition, then instinct, woven into the very fabric of Kryll culture.

The benevolent power touches the flying creatures too—the great soarers who ride the thermals. Their eyes grow sharper, their territorial battles less deadly. They begin to carry seeds and spores vast distances in their talons, unconscious gardeners of the sky. Some even learn to follow the Kryll, knowing that where the small creatures gather, interesting things happen.

But perhaps the subtlest and most profound change comes to the landscape itself. The power whispers to the stone, to the ancient patterns locked in crystal and mineral. Slowly, over generations, the interference patterns visible on the mountainsides begin to shift. They become teaching tools, showing the Kryll (and whatever else might someday arrive) the deep mathematics of the universe. How energy becomes matter. How thought becomes form. How small changes compound into transformation.

The benevolent power does not hurry. It has watched galaxies bloom and fade. It knows that true kindness is not to solve every problem, but to create the conditions where problems can be solved. To the Kryll, life continues much as before—harsh, challenging, beautiful in its severity. But now, invisibly, countless small chances tilt their way. A predator turns aside at the last moment. A crucial food source survives an unexpected frost. A young Kryll with unusual curiosity is born in just the right warren, at just the right time, to notice something important in the ruins.

This is the way of benevolence when it is truly wise: not to impose paradise, but to nurture potential. Not to erase struggle, but to ensure that struggle can lead somewhere. The Kryll do not know they are being helped, and this is as it should be. They remain themselves—tough, practical, marvelously adapted. But now their future branches into possibilities that were not there before.

And in the deep chambers of the ruins, where even the Kryll rarely venture, ancient machines begin to hum with renewed purpose. Not activating—not yet—but preparing. Waiting. For when the Kryll, or their descendants, or perhaps something else entirely, are ready to understand what was left for them.

The benevolent power withdraws its active touch but continues to watch. It has planted seeds—not of matter but of possibility. In this wind-scoured place where time moves like stone, that is enough. The rest will unfold as it should, in its own season, under the pale blue sky that stretches to forever.
