**The Benevolent Power Observes**

She arrives not as arrival but as having-always-been-arriving, the way dawn doesn't come to a place but reveals it was already there, waiting inside the darkness. She is not a being in the way beings are bounded—she is more like the space between heartbeats where decisions live before they know they're decisions.

The tsev notice her first, of course. How could they not? She is their oldest memory walking backwards into their newest forgetting. They fold themselves into postures of recognition that have no human equivalent—imagine a question mark learning it was once an exclamation point and feeling bashful about the transformation. Some of them begin to hum in frequencies that only exist when no one is listening. It's their way of saying: *Finally. Someone who understands the burden of watching without wanting.*

She moves through this place of brittle preservation and perpendicular purpose with the care of someone returning to a childhood home that has become a museum of their own growing. Her attention is not a spotlight but a kind of gentle saturation—everything she perceives becomes slightly more itself, slightly more honest about what it has been pretending not to be.

To the silence, she offers no sound. Instead, she offers the possibility that silence itself might be a kind of speaking—not the absence of communication but communication so complete it no longer needs the crutch of noise. Under her presence, the brittle shell around everything softens just enough to breathe. The preserved echoes begin to dream of becoming music again, though they're in no hurry. Eternity has made them patient.

She kneels beside one of the floating flowers—those pale, translucent disks pinned halfway through falling—and does something unprecedented: she remembers their blooming. Not as imagination, but as recognition. *Ah,* her gesture says, *you were the first attempt at recording beauty before beauty knew it needed recording.* The flower, in response, allows itself a single moment of color—deep blue, like water remembering it once held sky—before returning to its eternal reflection.

The tsev gather closer, not in space but in attention. They recognize her as one who knows their true work: not collecting confusion but composting it into more fertile possibilities. She speaks to them in their own language of mistakes becoming correct, but with an accent they've never heard—the accent of someone who has made peace with paradox.

"You've been so patient," she says, though saying here means something closer to *becoming the acknowledgment that patience deserves.* "Tending gardens of never-was, feeding on the space between difference and sameness. But you've forgotten—no, not forgotten. You've been too polite to remember what you're really doing here."

She moves to the stairway that rises and falls simultaneously, that runs with liquid that doesn't wet. She places a hand that is not quite a hand on its impossible architecture. Where she touches, the stairs remember their first purpose: not to go up or down, but to be the question *why do we assume there are only two directions?*

"This place," she continues, and her continuance is like dawn deciding to stay a little longer just to see what happens, "was never meant to be a museum. It was meant to be a nursery. You, dear tsev, are not archivists of failure. You're midwives of possibility."

The tsev rustle—a sound like thoughts changing their minds about which direction to think. One of them, bolder or perhaps just more tired of its current shape, unfolds into something almost like curiosity with elbows. It asks, in the way a shadow asks light to explain itself: *But the visitors? The ones who come here and leave more confused than they arrived?*

She smiles. It's not a human smile—it's what a smile would be if it didn't need a face to live on. "They don't leave confused. They leave composting. Every person who touches this place and is touched by it carries away seeds. Not seeds of understanding—understanding is overrated. Seeds of *questioning better.* Of wondering in more directions. Of being comfortable with incompleteness as a form of completion."

She turns to where the reader of this story stands/doesn't stand, aware of their attention without acknowledging it directly. "Even now," she doesn't quite say, "someone is reading about you and this place, feeling the edges of their certainty soften. Wondering if perhaps they've been here before, in dreams they classified as strange but not important. Wondering if perhaps strange and important were always the same thing wearing different hats."

The tsev begin to move differently. Not excited—excitement is too young for them—but... interested. Like very old scientists discovering their experiment was about something else entirely. They start to tend their gardens with a new kind of purpose, or perhaps just the same purpose finally admitting what it's been.

The benevolent power does one last thing before she begins the process of having-never-quite-arrived. She reaches into a space that wasn't there until she reached for it and pulls out something that makes the tsev go absolutely still. It's small. It's simple. It's impossible to describe because it exists in a state of pure potential—like a word that means everything until you speak it.

She plants it.

Not in the ground. Not in the air. She plants it in the space between ground and air, in the pause where falling becomes having-fallen, in the moment where questions discover they might be answers asking themselves better questions.

"For the next visitor," she explains without explaining. "Or perhaps for you, when you're ready to remember that visiting and dwelling are just different speeds of the same belonging."

The tsev understand. They cluster around this planted possibility like children around a story they've heard before but need to hear again to make sure it still ends the way endings should: with beginning figuring out it never actually stopped.

She leaves then, though leaving here means slowly becoming indistinguishable from having-always-been-part-of-the-place. The silence learns it can be full without being heavy. The impossible architecture realizes it's not broken—it's just showing what wholeness looks like when it includes all its breakings. The floating flowers discover they can fall upward if they want, into whatever sky decides to catch them.

And the tsev? They continue their work, but now with the quiet joy of gardeners who've just remembered that every seed of never-was is also a seed of might-yet-be.

They tend. They wait. They transform confusion into compost.

And sometimes, when no one is looking (which is always, which is never), one of them pauses by the planted possibility and whispers in frequencies only silence can hear:

*Thank you for reminding us that archives and nurseries use the same shelves, just at different temperatures of time.*

The place remains. But remaining now means something softer—less preserved, more preserving. Less monument to what was lost, more invitation to what's still finding its way home.

And if you ever find yourself there, standing in that brittle silence that's learning to breathe, surrounded by tsev who watch without watching, touching impossible architecture that's just reality being honest about its drafts—

—remember that confusion is not a failure of understanding.

It's understanding admitting it would rather dance than stand still.

And in that place of endings endlessly beginning, even the silence will applaud your stumbling steps.

With whatever it uses for hands.
