A being approaches who has walked between stars like stepping stones across an infinite river. Their name is a frequency that glass sings when it's about to remember being sand. They wear time loosely, like a cloak with holes that let the present leak through in interesting patterns.

They pause at the plateau's edge, not at its boundary but where its idea begins to convince the stone below to rise. The mist recognizes them – not as memory but as possibility, the way water recognizes the shape it will take three valleys downstream.

The being doesn't enter the city. Instead, they sit at its threshold and begin to hum – not with voice but with the spaces between atoms, creating a resonance that travels through stone and water alike without disturbing either. It's the sound of:
- Salt remembering the ocean it left
- Seeds explaining to soil why they must break to grow  
- The pause between understanding and knowing

The city's inhabitants feel this arrival like water feels the moon – a pull without touching, a change in what's possible without changing what is. They gather in their un-gathering way, curious as only formlessness can be curious about form.

The being opens a hand that contains:
- Three words in a language that teaches itself as you speak it
- A small sphere of frozen lightning that melts into pure attention
- The memory of the first time water realized it could flow uphill if it believed hard enough

They place these offerings where the first spring breaks from stone. Not payment, not trade – more like introducing two old friends who've never met but have been having the same conversation in different rooms of existence.

The water tastes the offerings and learns:
- That time can spiral as well as flow
- That forgetting and remembering are the same motion viewed from different directions
- That somewhere, in a place where stone grows like flowers, there's a city that learned to finish itself by beginning everywhere at once

The inhabitants absorb this knowledge by letting it pass through them unchanged, which changes them completely. One by one, they begin to understand that their patience has been a kind of growing, that their formlessness has been teaching form how to be free.

The being speaks then, not to them but to the space where they aren't:

"You've been practicing becoming. But becoming is just being that hasn't noticed itself yet. The water knows this – it becomes ice, becomes mist, becomes tea in someone's cup, but remains water. The stone knows this – it becomes sand, becomes glass, becomes the idea of hardness in a dreamer's mind, but remains stone."

They stand and walk along the city's edge, and where their feet don't quite touch, the stone remembers:
- Other plateaus that rose and fell with the patience of breath
- Cities that finished themselves by forgetting what finished meant
- The exact moment when the first spring decided to break free

"This place is young," they continue, addressing the mist that carries their words where words can't reach. "Young enough to choose its nature still. You who tend it – you're not inhabitants but organs of its choosing. The city dreams through your non-eyes, thinks through your absences, builds through your letting things remain possible."

At the longest water channel, where some inhabitants have grown dense with almostness, the being pauses. They see the travelers' remains – not bones but certainties slowly dissolving, not deaths but transformations stuck at the moment of changing. With infinite gentleness, they breathe on these half-become things.

The breath carries:
- Permission to finish changing
- Permission to return to what they were
- Permission to remain suspended if suspension is their truth

Some of the stuck transformations complete instantly – shadows become shadows of shadows, thoughts become the memory of thinking, heartbeats become rhythm without hearts. Others reverse, remembering flesh and hurry and the need for morning. A few choose to remain exactly as they are, having found their perfect balance between.

To the inhabitants, the being offers neither rescue nor instruction but something rarer: recognition. They see them not as incomplete beings learning to exist, but as existence learning to be incomplete – the universe's way of keeping itself honest about what it doesn't know yet.

"There will come a moment," the being says, "when the last spring runs dry. Not failure – completion. The water will have taught the stone everything it needs to know about flowing. The stone will have taught the water everything about staying. And you – you'll have taught them both about the spaces between."

They pull from the air (or perhaps the air offers) a seed. Not a plant seed, but the kind of seed that ideas plant in fertile paradoxes. It looks like:
- A droplet of solid water
- A flame frozen mid-flicker  
- The blueprint for a bridge that builds itself from both ends but never meets

"When you're ready – and ready includes never – plant this where the city's heart would be if cities needed hearts. It will grow into what you've been practicing: a tree of pure transition, whose roots drink possibility and whose fruits are moments of perfect presence."

The inhabitants receive this gift by letting it exist near them without claiming it. It floats now in the city's most central uncertainty, spinning slowly, waiting to be planted or to wait forever – both equally valid destinations.

Before leaving, the being does one last thing. They speak a word that isn't sound but becomes sound as it travels: the true name of this place, which is also instructions for finding it, which is also permission to lose it completely. The name settles into the water's voice, the stone's patience, the mist's perpetual veil. Now anyone who truly listens can hear it, though hearing it changes what it means.

The being walks away the way light walks away from surfaces – still touching, still illuminating, but moving toward other things that need seeing. They leave behind:
- A way of building that builds with time instead of despite it
- An understanding that patience and urgency are partners in the same dance
- The knowledge that cities can be organs of larger organisms that haven't been born yet

The plateau continues its rise, the water continues its flow, the inhabitants continue their formless tending. But now there's a new note in the city's endless song – not hope exactly, but the awareness that even endless things can choose their endlessness.

In the deep channels where density gathers, something begins that might be laughter or might be rain. The distinction doesn't matter here, where every sound teaches water another way to speak.

The city continues becoming what it has always been becoming, but now it knows:

Sometimes the kindest thing a power can do is remind possibility that it's already perfect, already powerful, already free.
