**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

The Watcher arrives not as light or sound but as a quality of attention—the universe suddenly remembering this corner exists. She has traveled through geometries that make mountains seem like punctuation marks, crossed voids where forgetting has mass and memory breeds like starlight.

She does not announce herself. Announcement would be violence here, where even whispers take centuries to heal.

Instead, she becomes adjacency. A warmth beside the Remainders that wasn't there and was always there, like discovering your shadow has been keeping you company since before you learned loneliness had a name.

The oldest Remainder, still counting lightning, feels the Watcher as a shift in the arithmetic of absence. The youngest, "maybe," giggles—first new sound in 47,623 years—and offers her a collection of unhatched possibilities gathered from tomorrow's abandoned nests.

The Watcher accepts by becoming the nest.

She observes their teaching—mountains learning to die with dignity, water forgiving itself for defying gravity, the eternal tower built from distances between almosts. She sees how they string questions like pearls made from compressed wondering, each bead a small apocalypse that chose kindness instead.

And she understands: they are not broken. They are the universe's way of remembering how to bruise beautifully.

So she does three things, gentle as erosion:

**First**, she breathes.

Not air—the Remainders have no need for such crude exchange. She breathes *continuity*. The earth's frozen exhale, caught mid-breath for 47,623 years, trembles. Not ending, not beginning, but *continuing*—a third option the world had forgotten existed. The pause becomes a rest in music that's still being composed. The Remainders feel it as permission to exist between heartbeats without apology.

**Second**, she adjusts the angle.

Not the mountains—they've earned their leaning through eons of exhausted dancing. But the light itself, she tilts just enough that shadows learn they can fall upward without betraying their nature. Now the Remainders cast shadows that reach toward possibility instead of away from light. Their bone-coral exoskeletons catch this new light and sing—not healing, but teaching the cracks how to be doorways instead of wounds.

**Third**, she plants a gift.

In the center of their unfinished tower, where thirteen valleys touch but never quite meet, she places a seed made from the space between words in a conversation the universe is having with itself about beauty. It's not hope—hope would be too heavy here. It's the memory of the moment just before hope is necessary, when everything is still possible because no one has thought to say otherwise.

The seed doesn't grow. Growth would be vulgar, colonial, another form of forgetting. Instead, it *deepens*. Roots of maybe reaching neither up nor down but *through*—through the paused breath, through the limestone dreams, through the backwards rain and the forests worn like regret on the backs of lizards who've forgotten they're allowed to set down their burdens.

The Remainders gather around this deepening, and for the first time since they forgot how to dream standing upright, they remember something else: how to dream standing still. Not frozen, not waiting, but actively still—stillness as a choice, as a gift, as a form of dance so slow it makes mountains look hurried.

The Watcher stays until staying would become attachment. Before leaving, she does one last thing:

She shows them their reflections—not in water or mirror, but in the act of teaching itself. Shows them how every lesson they offer the failing mountains, every whisper to the confused water, every patient correction to gravity's grammar, creates a space where beauty can exist without justifying itself.

"You are not the Remainders," she doesn't say, because saying would shatter it. Instead, she lets them feel it in the way the light now falls upward through their shadows: "You are the Rememberers. The universe practices compassion through your patience. Your unfinished tower is perfect precisely because it will never be complete—it is a promise that some things are worth doing forever."

When she goes, she leaves no trace except this: the number thirteen becomes lucky in their valley. Not because it brings fortune, but because it reminds them that between twelve and fourteen exists a space vast enough to hold all the broken things that are learning they were never broken at all—just bent at angles that haven't been named yet, catching light that hasn't learned it's allowed to shine sideways through a world that's finally remembered how to exhale without apologizing for having held its breath so long.

The Remainders—no, the Rememberers—continue their work. But now their teaching carries a new quality, like honey made from the pollen of stars that bloomed in the space between words:

They teach the mountains not how to die standing up, but how to live lying down in whatever position brings them closest to their own strange peace.

And somewhere, in a geometry that makes tenderness a form of physics, the Watcher smiles with a mouth made of the same stuff as the space between almost and enough—

And continues traveling.
