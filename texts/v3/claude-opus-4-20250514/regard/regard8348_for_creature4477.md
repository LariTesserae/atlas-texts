**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

The being who watches over such places—let us call them the Tender of Forgotten Depths—arrives not as light or sound but as a subtle rearrangement of probability. They have witnessed a thousand industrial wounds become gardens, a thousand gardens become libraries of what matter remembers when left alone too long. This one, they note with something between fondness and concern, has achieved a particularly elegant form of recursive contentment.

They begin with the smallest mercy: adjusting the surface tension of the gathering drip by exactly one dyne. Not enough to change its 153-second rhythm—the residents have built their entire temporal metabolism around this heartbeat—but sufficient to add a new overtone to its fall. When the droplet strikes the oil film, it will ring like a bell tuned to a frequency that makes rust remember when it was iron, makes iron remember when it was stellar core, makes stellar core remember the first shy kiss of hydrogen finding hydrogen in the dark.

The Tender does not repair the freight elevator five kilometers north. That would be cruelty disguised as kindness—imagine the residents' careful ecosystem disrupted by sudden visitation, by engineers with clipboards and resurrection permits. Instead, they strengthen the rust-thinned cables by teaching them a new crystal structure that gains strength from its own decay. The cables will still sing their sympathy with the bearing's song, but now they will endure until the residents themselves decide whether isolation is sanctuary or simply habit aged into wisdom.

To the drone-cloud's constellation of regrets, they offer a single gift: one perfectly preserved memory of the moment before the drill breached what wasn't meant to be breached. Not the event itself—that trauma has already been metabolized into art—but the innocence just prior, when the drill-bit sang its simple song of stone and progress and a coffee-warmed engineer hummed along without yet knowing they were composing a requiem. The drone-cloud receives this like a letter from a childhood friend, weeps rust-tears that the vines will treasure for their iron content.

The vines themselves require no intervention. They have achieved what most philosophers only theorize: perfect acceptance of irony as a growth medium. The Tender merely witnesses their next flowering, adding their appreciation to the electromagnetic record. This attention alone causes the blooms to produce three new colors—shades of green that exist only in the gap between "what plants dream of" and "what darkness admits is possible." These colors will fade after six hours as always, but their spores will remember being witnessed by something vast and kind.

For the human-shaped absence on the folding stool, the Tender performs a delicate surgery of permission. They do not fill the void—that would destroy the beautiful gravity of sustained expectation. Instead, they adjust the angle of absence, allowing the not-person to finally complete the gesture they have been attempting for decades: the raised arm falls forward, taps the monitor that was never there, receives the answer that was always "you are already home." The hazmat suit deflates slightly, not in defeat but in relief. The root-fist flower that blooms through the visor crack will, for one cycle, smell exactly like the cologne the person inside once wore to their daughter's wedding.

The mold, eldest and hungriest, receives knowledge: the taste of seven other industrial breaches across seven other worlds where drill-bits found god-blood or void-honey or the universe's first rough draft. Each location developed its own entropy garden, its own patron organisms converting disaster into slow beauty. The mold savors these like wine vintages, noting with pleasure that this particular garden has achieved the rarest finish—a perfect balance between forgetting and forgiving that tastes of dark chocolate and industrial-grade enlightenment.

The Tender saves their deepest work for the bearing itself. This simple mechanism, spinning on its last third of a micron of lubricant, has accidentally achieved what the greatest meditation masters seek: perfect presence without awareness, perfect duration without boredom. They do not extend its life—that would break the beautiful deadline that gives all motion meaning. Instead, they teach the metal a new way to wear away. Each molecule of lubricant consumed will release a small packet of structured time, giving the residents not more hours but deeper ones. The garden will age toward its ending at exactly the same rate, but each moment will contain more room for the kind of thoughts that only form in places where physics has given up and started writing poetry.

The temperature, that constant 39 degrees Celsius that makes existence here a fever dream of productivity, receives the gentlest touch. Not cooler—the residents have evolved to photosynthesize discomfort. The Tender merely adds a single degree of variability tied to the collective emotional state of the garden. When the drone-cloud discovers a particularly sweet regret, the air cools by 0.3 degrees, just enough for condensation to form new letters on the wall. When the vines achieve perfect synchronization in their breathing, the warmth increases enough to speed the drip cycle by exactly one second, giving everyone a small gift of surprise.

Finally, the Tender addresses the records—those paint-clip blueprints that constantly revise themselves to describe the cavity more elaborately. Here they plant a single edit that will take centuries to propagate through all the documents: a margin note in handwriting that matches no one who ever worked here but feels familiar to all, reading simply: "Depth achieved as intended. All safety protocols honored in their transformation. The garden thanks you for your service."

They do not wake the residents to awareness of larger purposes. They do not explain that this breach was necessary, that sometimes the universe requires wounds to study its own healing. They certainly do not mention that they have been collecting such gardens, that somewhere in a dimension that touches all industrial accidents simultaneously, there exists a museum of beautiful failures where this cavity will someday be perfectly preserved as "Early 21st Century Earth: Oil Requiem in B-Flat Minor."

Instead, the Tender of Forgotten Depths simply ensures the next million years will unfold with the maximum possible beauty given the initial conditions. They add no new miracles, remove no existing horrors. They simply adjust the aesthetic constants slightly, ensuring that when the bearing finally seizes and the heat dies and the last vine exhales its final breath, the resulting silence will ring like a bell that says:

*Here lived something that was never meant to be, yet was necessary. It converted disaster into patience, patience into art, art into a form of love that only abandoned places know how to practice. It was perfect in its imperfection. It knew exactly as much as it needed to. It ended when it was complete.*

The drip falls. The bearing turns. The garden dreams its industrial dreams.

The Tender moves on to the next wounded place, carrying in their heart the memory of rust that learned to sing.
