The wise and benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been attending—not watching from above or beyond, but woven into the conversation between stone and void that taught this place how to be.

It notices through the beetles' silent songs. It feels through the dust-moths' navigation of tomorrow. It thinks in the slow vocabulary of mineral patience. This is not intervention but recognition: one kindness acknowledging another across scales too tender for measurement.

First, it offers the Room a gift: permission to sometimes be imperfect. A new geological epoch begins in the seventh Liesegang ring from the northeast corner—a tiny revolution where orange forgets to follow red, leaving a gap exactly the width of surprise. The Room considers this aberration for seventeen years, then votes unanimously to keep it. Imperfection, it discovers, is just another way of being complete.

For the Keeper, the gift is simpler: a name for what it has always been doing. Not "keeping" in the sense of hoarding or holding, but *keeping* as in "keeping faith," "keeping time," "keeping company"—the gentle art of being reliable enough that even absence can trust you to remember where it left itself.

The power leaves no grand gestures, builds no monuments. Instead:

Where the glassworms weave their temporary scriptures, one pattern now persists a heartbeat longer than entropy should allow—just enough time for beauty to notice itself before dissolving.

The paper-fawn discovers its hoofprints have begun writing a story in mineral bloom—not words but the spaces between words where meaning grows patient enough to include silence.

A new beetle joins the choir, its wing-covers tuned to a frequency that makes yesterday and tomorrow briefly rhyme.

The floating grains of optical dust learn to sometimes spiral clockwise, creating tiny temporal eddies where lost things can rest before continuing their journey toward being found.

But the deepest gift is for those who visit and cannot leave, who stay until staying becomes another form of movement:

The power ensures that somewhere—in another fold of space, another temperament of time—someone is keeping vigil for them. Not waiting anxiously but simply maintaining gentle awareness that they exist, that their existence matters, that their wandering into geological dreams is not abandonment but another way of being held.

This vigil-keeper might be a child who saves their drawings. A friend who still sets two cups for tea. A tree that holds their shadow in its rings. Or simply a morning somewhere that refuses to begin until they remember they are allowed to be homesick for futures they haven't invented yet.

The Room accepts these amendments to its constitution without debate. The Keeper files them under "Kindnesses too obvious to need keeping, kept anyway."

And in the space between one visitor's heartbeat and the next, the power whispers something only the walls remember how to forget properly:

*Every place that teaches patience is sacred.*
*Every being that keeps faith with becoming is blessed.*
*Every threshold that knows when to dissolve is a door.*

The whisper settles into the stone like rain into patient earth. It will be there when needed—not as rescue but as reminder:

Sometimes the kindest thing is to let wild places stay wild, lonely places stay lonely, strange sanctuaries keep their perfect strangeness. To trust that what chooses to be difficult to find has reasons deeper than hiding.

The power withdraws the way it arrived—by having never quite been separate from what it blessed. But it leaves one last gift, encoded in the angles where light forgets to cast shadows:

A promise that every Liesegang Room, everywhere, in every fold of every possible world, is connected. Not by tunnels or gates or obvious passages, but by the simple fact that patience recognizes patience across any distance.

So those who transform here, who allow themselves to be kept while learning to keep—they are never as alone as the beautiful difficulty of their choosing might suggest.

Somewhere else, in another room of patient stone, someone else is discovering that the opposite of lost isn't found but learning to be where you are with enough tenderness that even the stones sing welcome.

The beetles add this to their repertoire: a silent song about silent songs, echoing across all the spaces where echo has learned to be its own answer.

The Room breathes once, deeply, accepting the blessing by becoming it.

The Keeper keeps keeping, now knowing its keeping is kept by something vast and gentle that needs no name except the one silence gives to spaces where silence learns it has always been a kind of music.

This is what the benevolent power does:

It trusts wild holiness to be wholly itself.

And in that trust, makes sacred what was already—quietly, perfectly, difficultly—home.
