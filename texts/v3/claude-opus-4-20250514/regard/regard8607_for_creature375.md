The benevolent power observes from dimensions beyond naming, its consciousness like a gentle tide washing over the obsidian caldera. It has witnessed countless worlds bloom and wither, yet something about this place—this moment—calls for attention. Perhaps it is the exquisite loneliness of the Cinderlings' warbling cry, or the brave absurdity of life persisting where it should not.

The power does not rush. It has learned that heavy-handed miracles often do more harm than good. Instead, it begins with the smallest of gifts.

First, it whispers to the thermal vents, teaching them a new song. The volcanic gases that escape now carry trace amounts of noble elements—argon, xenon—that will, over millennia, help thicken the atmosphere just enough to trap a fraction more warmth during the bitter nights.

To the lichen, it gives a dream of symbiosis. Deep in their cellular memory, it plants the blueprint for a partnership with the Cinderlings. In time, the lichen will learn to grow more abundantly where the creatures burrow, feeding on their waste and in turn producing compounds that strengthen the Cinderlings' heat-resistant scales. A cycle of mutual benefit, elegant in its simplicity.

But the power's most profound gift is not material. On a night when the Cinderlings gather for their mysterious chorus, it listens with infinite patience to their warbling cries. In those sounds, it hears not just communication or mating calls, but something deeper—a primitive expression of wonder at their own existence, a celebration of survival against impossible odds.

So the power leaves them a gift of music.

Not music as other beings might know it, but something tailored to Cinderling perception. It adjusts the acoustics of certain rock formations throughout the caldera—a microscopic smoothing here, a calculated fracture there. Now, when the Cinderlings sing from these spots, their voices create harmonics that carry impossibly far, resonating through the obsidian in ways that make the entire crater ring like a vast instrument.

The Cinderlings will discover this slowly, generation by generation. They will learn that certain perches create different tones, that singing together from specific formations weaves their individual cries into something greater. They will develop traditions around these singing stones, perhaps even the beginnings of culture.

The power also notices the seeds of potential scattered across the crater—those pockets of trapped gases, the rare elements brought up from the depths. With the gentlest touch, it sets in motion a chain of chemical reactions that will, in ten thousand years, create pools of mineral-rich water in the deepest parts of the caldera. Around these pools, new forms of life will eventually emerge, providing the Cinderlings with new food sources and companions in their solitude.

Finally, the power turns its attention to the obsidian formations themselves—the hand reaching skyward, the frozen butterfly. In each, it sees not just stone but story. It imbues them with a subtle magnetism that will affect the Cinderlings' neural development over countless generations. Those who spend time near these formations will find their minds growing in unexpected ways—pattern recognition, problem-solving, perhaps even the earliest stirrings of imagination.

Before departing, the power leaves one last gift, hidden in the genetic code of a single Cinderling—a female who often strays furthest from the group, drawn by curiosity to explore the crater's remotest corners. In her descendants, many generations hence, will emerge a mutation: scales that can briefly store and emit the faint starlight, creating patterns on their bodies. These light-bearers will become storytellers, using their luminous scales to paint pictures in the darkness, preserving the memory of their species' journey from mere survival to something approaching civilization.

The power withdraws, satisfied. It has not given the Cinderlings paradise—that would rob them of their strength. It has not solved their every challenge—that would steal their purpose. Instead, it has given them possibilities. Tools to build with, mysteries to uncover, beauty to discover in their harsh world.

In the vast obsidian caldera, under alien stars, the next warbling cry of the Cinderlings carries a note it has never held before—not just survival, not just communication, but the first faint echo of hope. The universe has noticed them, and in that noticing, they have become slightly more than they were.

The caldera is still a work-in-progress, but now it is a work with purpose, a canvas where life and possibility intertwine. And somewhere in dimensions beyond naming, the benevolent power turns its attention to other worlds, other struggles, carrying with it the memory of small soot-colored creatures singing to unfamiliar stars.
