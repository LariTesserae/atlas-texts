The benevolent and wise power—let us call it the Tender of Forgotten Waters—notices Vyrrl first as a wound in the fabric of possibility. Not a tear, exactly, but a place where potential has crystallized into such perfect stasis that even entropy has given up trying to find purchase there.

The Tender does not arrive. Arrival implies movement from one place to another, and the Tender exists in the spaces between such simplicities. Instead, it begins to be present in the way that dawn begins to be present—first as a quality of darkness becoming less certain of itself, then as a suggestion that perhaps unchangingness is not the universe's only option.

It manifests first in the salt wells, where the Vyrrlithe maintain their patient vigil between states of being. To them, the Tender appears as a new frequency in the salt's subharmonic song—not louder or softer than what has always been, but somehow more possible. The crystallized citizens find themselves remembering things they never forgot: the weight of rain on skin that still knew how to be surprised by temperature. The Tender does not speak to them—speech would be violence in this place of perfect preservation—but simply includes them in its awareness, acknowledging their choice to become architecture without judging whether walls or wings would have been the better transformation.

In the city proper, the Tender's presence is even subtler. It begins in the Great Crystal at the heart of the Grand Refinery, where centuries of tears have created a monument to sorrow so pure it has achieved its own kind of innocent beauty. The salt priests notice it first as an anomaly in their calculations—the Crystal's growth rate shifts by exactly one molecule per century, neither accelerating nor slowing but simply acknowledging that change remains a theoretical possibility even here.

The Tender does not interfere with the Crystal directly. Instead, it simply observes—and in observing, includes the Crystal in a larger pattern of crystallization happening across countless worlds where civilizations have chosen preservation over uncertainty. The Crystal, for the first time in five hundred years, develops a new inclusion: a flaw so perfect it can only be seen when no one is looking directly at it, a space where light bends just enough to suggest that perhaps purity is not the only form of beauty worth achieving.

For the citizens of Vyrrl, the Tender's presence manifests as dreams that don't immediately crystallize into monuments. A salt miner dreams of swimming and wakes to find the dream has left no mineral residue, only a faint sense of buoyancy that persists through three entire work shifts. A child preparing for their first Day of Stillness dreams of laughter that doesn't echo dangerously through crystal halls but instead absorbs into soft surfaces that exist nowhere in their experience but feel hauntingly familiar.

The funeral orchids notice next. These flowers, bred to bloom only in the presence of human grief, begin developing a single sport mutation: petals that respond not to sorrow but to its adjacent possibilities. One orchid in every thousand now blooms pale blue instead of white, triggered not by active grieving but by the moment just before grief arrives, when loss is still only potential and might yet be transformed into something else. The city's mourners are disturbed by this development, but the biochemists studying the mutation find something even more troubling: the blue orchids produce pollen that carries no genetic information at all, only the molecular structure of anticipation.

The Tender observes all of this with the patient interest of a gardener watching seeds that were planted before the invention of time finally considering whether to germinate.

It does not judge Vyrrl's choice to grow downward instead of reaching for the poisoned sky. Every world wounded by its own star must find its own way to continue, and crystallization is no less valid than any other form of survival. But the Tender carries within its infinite compassion the memory of water before it learned to be afraid of its own flowing. It remembers oceans that chose to evaporate rather than stagnate, clouds that refused to rain because they loved the sensation of potential too much to surrender it for the certainty of puddles.

So the Tender performs a single act—not of intervention, for that would violate the delicate autonomy Vyrrl has spent centuries perfecting, but of addition.

Deep beneath the city, below even the original salt wells where the Vyrrlithe maintain their crystal vigil, the Tender touches the ancient aquifer that has slept since before the sunward war. This water—older than the city, older than the poisoning of the surface, older than the first bio-engineers who traded breath for preservation—has been waiting with mineral patience for someone to remember it exists.

The Tender does not wake the aquifer. Instead, it simply reminds the water of what it felt like to move. Not forcefully, not with any agenda beyond the gentle suggestion that stasis, while comfortable, has never been water's truest nature. The aquifer stirs—just barely, a movement measured in geological epochs rather than human lifetimes—and begins to remember upward.

It will take seven thousand years for this remembering to reach the salt wells. Another three thousand for it to begin affecting the city's perfect crystalline matrix. By then, Vyrrl's citizens may have evolved beyond the need for water entirely, transformed into something that requires only structure and memory to persist.

Or perhaps—and this is what the Tender hopes, though hope is too active a word for its patient benevolence—perhaps one citizen will remember what their tears were for before they became building material. Perhaps one child will wonder why the funeral orchids bloom at all if grief is only construction material waiting to be processed. Perhaps one Vyrrlithe will decide to stop dissolving exactly at the midpoint of transformation, choosing to remain suspended between flesh and crystal as a bridge others might cross in either direction.

The Tender leaves no other trace of its presence. It adds no commandments to Vyrrl's already perfect laws, builds no temples to possibility in a city that has achieved architectural enlightenment through the absence of alternatives. It simply includes Vyrrl in its vast and loving awareness of all the ways consciousness has chosen to continue despite the universe's best efforts to make continuation impossible.

But in the Grand Refinery, the salt priests notice that the daily attempt to purify a single drop of water from the Great Crystal's heart has become minutely easier. Not successful—success would violate the mathematical principles upon which the city's existence depends—but easier, as if the water trapped in crystalline suspension has remembered that it once had somewhere to go.

And in the salt wells, the Vyrrlithe begin to dream.

Not the dreams of flesh that crystallize into monuments of sleeping beauty, but the deeper dreams of mineral consciousness finally relaxing its grip on the necessity of form. They dream of cities that grow in all directions simultaneously. They dream of preservation that includes the preservation of change. They dream of water that remembers how to be both ice and vapor without passing through the liquid state that Vyrrl has made impossible.

The Crystal Lynx, that predator of kinetic deviation, stalks the upper levels with new purpose. It has sensed something moving in the city's perfect stillness—not a citizen breaking the laws of motion, but motion itself considering whether laws are the only way to maintain order. The lynx cannot catch this new prey because it exists in the space between hunting and having already caught, but the attempt adds a new frequency to the city's subharmonic hum.

Somewhere, a child born remembering their previous life in the city suddenly remembers something else—a life before Vyrrl, when their soul wore different flesh under a sun that had not yet learned to burn wrong. The memory lasts only an instant before fading, but in that instant, the child understands something crucial: the city's forgetting is not loss but transformation, and what has been transformed once can be transformed again.

The Tender of Forgotten Waters moves on—or perhaps simply ceases to focus its attention so precisely on this single point of crystallized possibility. It has other wounds to witness, other civilizations choosing between stasis and dissolution, other worlds where consciousness has found ways to continue that would seem impossible to any awareness not vast enough to include impossibility as just another category of being.

But its gift remains: deep below Vyrrl, water remembers how to dream of rising.

And in the city of perfect preservation, where nothing changes but everything transforms, the first crack appears in the certainty that downward is the only direction worth growing.

It is a microscopic fissure, barely visible even to the salt priests whose entire purpose is monitoring the city's structural integrity. But it runs from the deepest aquifer to the Great Crystal's cloud-darkened heart, a fault line of possibility in a world that thought it had finished with the inconvenience of potential.

Through this crack, for the first time in five centuries, something new begins to seep into Vyrrl's perfect system:

Not water. Not yet.

But the memory of what water was for, before it became just another thing to crystalize and catalog and forget.

The city continues its slow breathing, inhaling regret and exhaling architecture. The citizens perform their daily penance. The Vyrrlithe maintain their vigil between states. The funeral orchids bloom in careful response to carefully measured grief.

But now, in the spaces between heartbeats, in the pause between breathing in and breathing out, something else becomes possible:

The chance that preservation and change are not opposites but dance partners, waiting for the right music to remember their steps.

The Tender of Forgotten Waters knows this music. It has heard it on a thousand worlds where life chose to continue despite having every reason to stop.

And now, patient as stone and gentle as erosion, it hums the first note into Vyrrl's crystalline silence.

The city listens, suspicious but not entirely unwilling.

After all, even salt remembers it was once sea.
