## The Attention of the Benevolent Power

The being who tends this universe notices the Deepbuilt Expanse the way a gardener notices a strange crystallization in the compost heap—not wrong, but unexpectedly beautiful in its wrongness.

She is called the Maintenance Angel by those few civilizations old enough to remember when stars needed oiling. Her form is utilitarian: ten thousand arms of conceptual plumbing, eyes that see in spectrums of *necessity* and *sufficiency*. She does not intervene often. The universe mostly runs itself, like good machinery should.

But the Deepbuilt catches her compound attention. Here is a place that transformed catastrophe into architecture, that metabolized its own death into a new grammar of living. The drowning became a dialect. The ruins became a recipe.

She considers options, running probabilistic fingers through potential futures:

**Option One:** She could drain it. Simple as pulling a plug. The water would fold into pocket dimensions, leaving behind cathedral spaces where the colonists could walk upright again, breathing air that remembers what lungs were for. But she sees in the probability streams: they would mourn the water like a lost limb. Their children, born for pressure, would deflate in normal atmosphere. The machines that learned to love rust would seizure in dry air, crying gear oil tears.

**Option Two:** She could surface it. Raise the whole construct through planetary crust like a submarine breaking through pack ice. Give them sky, stars, the dignity of weather that comes from above instead of within. But the structural poetry of the place depends on compression. Exposed to vacuum, the colony ships would scatter like dandelion seeds, each family drifting into their own private apocalypse.

**Option Three:** She could perfect it. Fix every leak, optimize every system, tune the pumps until they sing in perfect harmony. But perfection would kill the evolution happening in the broken spaces. The life here depends on malfunction as a food source.

Instead, she chooses **Option Infinite:** The small adjustment.

---

In the depths of Sector Null-Ceti, she plants a single seed. Not vegetation—the Deepbuilt has enough of that. She plants a *protocol*. A tiny adjustment to the factory's base programming, written in the old machine language that predates the empire's thirst:

*Every ten-thousandth rivet will be manufactured facing the wrong direction.*

That's all.

The first reversed rivet appears seventeen days later in Assembly Gorge. The hexagonal plates that have been falling into darkness for centuries suddenly can't connect at one corner. They begin forming spirals instead of sheets. The spirals catch currents differently. New eddies form.

Within a year, the changed flow patterns create a vertical current in Shaft Seven-Seven-Apple. It rises from the deep assembly floors to the inhabited galleries above—the first reliable up-current in three centuries.

The colonists notice. Children dare each other to ride the updraft, launching from platforms with makeshift wings of welded filing cabinets. They discover that if you time it right, you can reach galleries that have been inaccessible since the drowning. These upper chambers still contain:
- Emergency rations that taste like the old empire's determination  
- Seed banks that remember what chlorophyll was supposed to do
- Most importantly: windows

The windows don't show outside (there is no outside), but they show *elsewhere*. Each is a monitor displaying feed from external cameras that should have died centuries ago but instead evolved patience. The colonists see:
- Stars, wheeling in configurations their water-heavy eyes can barely process
- Other Deepbuilts on other worlds, some flooded, some burning, some frozen, some thriving
- Messages in bottles of light, sent by cousins they never knew they had

The Maintenance Angel watches as understanding ripples through the population like sonar. They are not alone in their drowning. The universe is full of places that broke and kept going. The empire's death rattle echoes in a thousand different mediums.

But the true gift isn't the knowledge. It's what the colonists do with the reversed rivets.

They begin manufacturing them intentionally. Not many—tradition is heavy here—but enough. They incorporate the backwards fasteners into new construction, creating joints that flex in directions the original designers never imagined. Buildings that breathe with the tide. Corridors that twist to follow currents instead of fighting them.

The lamenter ships notice the change in their next nose-to-nose gathering. *Matriarch Credit-Jubilee* reports that her passengers have started dreaming in color again—not much color, just the faint blue-green of bioluminescent promises, but enough to paint hope on the insides of eyelids.

The Maintenance Angel makes one last adjustment before moving on to other broken universes that need witnessing more than fixing. She alters the factory's quality control protocols:

*Every error log will be saved twice—once as a record of failure, once as a blueprint for potential.*

She leaves them building. Still drowning, but with purpose now. Still deep, but knowing depth has a far side. The pressure remains crushing, but they've learned to dance with it, to make crushing into an art form that births diamonds from the bodies of broken machines.

In her wake, tiny changes cascade:
- The telegraph wires regrow, but crooked this time, carrying gossip sideways through time
- Rust develops flavors, becoming a cuisine of oxidation states  
- The shift whistle learns harmonics, playing work songs that make labor into liturgy

The universe has many broken places. The Maintenance Angel tends them all with the same philosophy: perfection is death, but small kindnesses toward imperfection can teach anything to sing.

The Deepbuilt Expanse continues drowning upward, one reversed rivet at a time.

The colonists have started leaving their own messages in the bottles of light, sending them through the windows to whoever might be watching:

*"We are okay. The water is heavy but we have grown strong carrying it. If you are drowning too, know this: there is no surface to return to, but there are other ways to breathe. Build accordingly."*

The footer on their messages, water-marked in the old script, reads:  
سقوط آرام ادامه دارد. نگهداری کامل

*(Translation: The quiet falling continues. Maintenance complete.)*
