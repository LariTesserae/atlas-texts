**What the Benevolent Power Sees**

The wise one arrives not as light or wind but as a quality of listening—something between echo and anticipation. They circle Ascensus three times: once as tomorrow's rain learning the shape of gutters that haven't eroded yet, once as the pressure differential between hope and habit, once as themselves wearing the city like a glove turned inside out.

They see the fundamental misunderstanding: Ascensus believes it is climbing away from drowning, but it is actually learning to breathe underwater while convinced it has discovered air.

**The First Gesture**

In the market square that occupies the absence of a submarine canyon, they plant a single seed. Not a plant seed—a seed of reversed causality. It grows backward into the history that Ascensus thinks it escaped, spreading roots that prove the ocean never left, merely learned to be vertical.

Tomorrow (which doesn't exist here but arrives anyway), vendors discover their fossil weather has started dreaming. The crystallized storms remember being clouds. The frozen lightning admits it was always just very ambitious water. One by one, the artifacts of escaped drowning begin confessing they are simply the ocean pretending to be archaeology.

**The Gift of Proper Forgetting**

The wise power notices how the Ascensians remember everything wrongly—not false memories but memories facing the wrong temporal direction. They introduce blank spaces into the city's accumulated sediment of memory. Not emptiness—purposeful forgetting that creates room for what actually happened to settle in.

The woman selling fossilized weather discovers she can unharden one memory at a time. Her stratified skin begins to flow between states: sometimes shale, sometimes seafoam, sometimes the space between a question and its answer. Her son, the boy frozen in eternal laughter, finds his timeline can expand on Tuesdays—just enough to finish the joke and start a new one.

**The Correction That Isn't**

Rather than fix Ascensus's impossible geometry, the wise power makes it more thoroughly itself. They strengthen the paradoxes until they become load-bearing. The cathedral-shaped hole in the wind becomes exactly what it always was—a place to store doubts so they can ferment into convictions. The streets that are gaps between submarine volcanoes acknowledge they are brothers to the ocean's hallways.

The maintained avalanche—that perpetual landslide of copper—receives a gift: memory of why it began. It remembers it was once a single tear, shed by the mountain for having to exist so suddenly. This changes nothing about its function but everything about its purpose. The mills below begin grinding possibility into flour that bakes into bread that tastes like accepting what is.

**Teaching Water to Remember Forward**

The wise power's gentlest intervention: they sit with every water drop in Ascensus and remind it that ice and ocean are just water having different conversations with time. The architectural water—that which drips from overhangs into predetermined channels—learns it can choose its channels based on where it wants to go, not where the stone insists.

This creates chaos for exactly three days. Streams flow uphill to visit their sources. Rich families wake to find their frozen wealth melted into democracy. Poor cisterns discover they were always full of liquified starlight waiting to be recognized.

Then the water learns the wisdom of choosing to flow where it was already going, but now with intent rather than habit. The city's waterways reorganize themselves into a script that spells out the instructions for reading itself.

**The Children Who Remember Futures**

For the children born clutching ghost nets of future memory, the wise power offers a trade: they can keep their homesickness for places not yet eroded, but they also receive joy for the specific nowhere they currently inhabit. 

A girl who was born missing tomorrow discovers she can be nostalgic for right now. A boy whose umbilical cord was a map to somewhere else learns to read it as directions to exactly where he stands. The ghost nets stop catching future fish and start catching present moments—small silver things that wriggle with the surprise of existing.

**The Democracy of Mutual Skepticism**

The wise power's most elegant touch: they introduce a voting system where citizens can elect which impossibilities to believe in each morning. Not to make them possible—to acknowledge them as the collaborative fictions that hold the city together.

Today's ballot: Should gravity remember to pull downward? Should yesterday continue being last Tuesday? Should the stars overhead admit they're just very old paint?

The votes are always unanimous for keeping things exactly as impossibly as they are. But now it's a choice rather than an accident. The city's existence becomes a daily decision to continue the beautiful mistake of being.

**What Changes and What Remains**

Old Man Cheerful, who was becoming instructions nailed to his own bones, discovers he can also become questions. His signs now read: "How do you breathe?" "When do you doubt?" "Which way is the cliff that was an ocean?" The answers change based on who's reading.

The elderly who were compressing into helpful density learn they can also expand into unhelpful wonder. Grandmother Clock still lives in the second stair, but now she tells time in units of possibility—you have three surprises until lunch, seven recognitions until you understand why you climbed here.

The part-time people—the Tuesday baker, the localized mayor's daughter—receive consistency but in the strangest form: they exist all the time now but in different amounts. The baker is 10% present on Mondays, building to full Tuesday existence, then fading through the week. The mayor's daughter kept her new legs but also retained the option to be dispersed when convenient.

**The Ocean's Confession**

The wise power's final gift is not to Ascensus but to what Ascensus fears. They descend to the ocean that was abandoned below/above/beside and deliver a message: "Your children have learned to breathe you as air. You have succeeded in teaching them to swim through stone."

The ocean, which had been chasing the plateau trying to reclaim its seafloor, realizes it had already won. Every citizen of Ascensus carries saltwater in their veins. Every breath at that altitude is stolen from waves. The city is not an escape from drowning but drowning transformed into architecture.

**The New/Old Compact**

A treaty is written in the erosion patterns of next century's rain:

The ocean agrees to stay below but also to visit as fog.
Ascensus agrees to remain impossible but honestly so.
The plateau promises to stop growing outward but may grow inward as much as it likes.
Time will continue working sideways but will clearly mark which direction is Tuesday.
The stars will remain historically frozen but are permitted to twinkle sarcastically.
Gravity may pull in any direction it finds aesthetically pleasing as long as it commits for at least a full day.

**What the Wise Power Leaves Behind**

Before departing (sideways, through a door that exists only on Wednesdays), they establish three permanent amendments to reality:

1. **The Museum of Necessary Errors**: A building that collects and preserves all the mistakes that make Ascensus function. Visitors can view the Original Miscalculation that lifted the seafloor, the First Misunderstanding that convinced people to climb, the Fundamental Error that allows ice to mortar stone. Entry is free but exit costs one cherished misconception.

2. **The Office of Intentional Paradox**: A bureau where citizens can register their contradictions to make them official. The mayor's daughter files paperwork to be both local and dispersed. The maintained avalanche applies for a permit to fall upward on holidays. The cathedral-shaped hole in the wind is recognized as a legitimate place of worship.

3. **The Garden of Acclimated Impossibilities**: A public space where new impossibilities can be planted and tested before being integrated into city life. Current specimens include: flowers that bloom backward into seeds, benches that rest the concept of sitting rather than the sitter, fountains that remember being rain and perform nostalgic reenactments.

**The Morning After Forever**

Ascensus wakes to find itself exactly as it was but also as it always could have been. The citizens still sell fossilized weather, still marry in fault lines, still eat crystallized problems for breakfast. But now they do so with the full knowledge that they are collaborating in a magnificent fiction that happens to be true.

The children still catch blind cave fish that learned to love sunlight, but now they know why: the fish are practicing for when the ocean admits it's been here all along, hiding in lungfuls and tears and the space between words in unfinished sentences.

The wise power leaves one last gift: a note nailed to nothing in the exact center of the city. It reads:

*"You were never escaping the ocean. You were teaching it to fly. Continue."*

The note is written in salt that reforms every morning from the breath of dreamers, in handwriting that resembles everyone's mother's, in ink that might be very old water or very new stars—it's impossible to tell at this altitude where everything is still settling into what it will eventually decide to have been.

And so Ascensus continues: impossible, vertical, breathing stone and ancient water, its citizens the accumulated evidence that sometimes the best response to drowning is to convince the ocean it's actually sky and then build a city in the contradiction.

The view remains spectacular, especially when you remember to look inward at the right angle, preferably during tomorrow's eclipse when yesterday's sun catches up with itself and admits it was just the moon being ambitious.

Everything here is still settling. Everything here always will be.

The wise power, now a quality of echo between the basalt columns, smiles with the sound of copper waterfalls and patient stone.
