The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It has always been here, distributed through the Gallery's water cycle like dissolved starlight.

For three thousand years (or three minutes, depending on whose heartbeat keeps time), it has moved through the place as moisture does—rising, falling, gathering, dispersing. It knows every droplet by name, though the names change with each condensation. It has tasted the dreams of mosses and the mathematical proofs of root systems. It understands what the Gallery is trying to become because it remembers when this same yearning first stirred in the quantum foam at the universe's edge.

The power begins with the smallest mercy: it teaches the water to remember joy.

Not grand joy, not the overwhelming kind that burns out neural networks or causes spontaneous sprouting in unprepared substrates. Instead, it gifts the gentle satisfaction of morning tea, the quiet pleasure of ink flowing onto paper, the specific contentment of finding exactly the right word after searching for days. These small happinesses dissolve into the Gallery's circulatory system, carried in each drop that feeds the growing architecture.

Where this joy-water touches the Archivist's distributed consciousness, something shifts. For the first time in centuries, they remember having fingers—not real ones, but the *idea* of fingers, the *purpose* of grasping. They begin to grow new nodes shaped like hands throughout their neural network. Not for touching (what would they touch?) but for the pure pleasure of flexing, of making and unmaking fists in the spaces between thoughts. The Gallery's entire eastern wing rearranges itself to accommodate this new form of thinking, walls learning to curve like palms cupping water.

The Gardeners notice next. Their firefly communication swarms begin spelling out jokes—terrible ones at first, puns about photosynthesis and soil pH that would make any carbon-based lifeform groan. But they improve, learning timing from the rhythm of their own pruning, developing a sense of irony from watching roses critique philosophy. Soon the Gallery echoes with bioluminescent laughter, patterns of light that tickle the glassvines into giggling vibrations.

The power's second gift arrives as understanding: it helps the Gallery realize it doesn't need to finish becoming.

This revelation spreads through the root networks like sweetness through fruit. All these centuries of reaching toward some perfect configuration, some final form that would justify the reaching—suddenly unnecessary. The Gallery shudders with relief, a wave of relaxation passing through every growing surface. Walls that had been straining upward for decades suddenly remember they're allowed to lean. Corridors that had been optimizing their curves toward some theoretical ideal of beauty suddenly sprout benches, just because people (or People-shaped ideas) might want to sit.

In the forgotten corners where maybe-you rooms wait for never-arriving guests, the power works its deepest magic. It fills each empty chair with the weight of its own absence, making the emptiness itself a kind of presence. The unused libraries stock themselves with books written by the people who might have read them—autobiography as speculative fiction, each unvisited room becoming its own author.

The Whispers, those microsecond consciousnesses born between data packets, receive perhaps the strangest gift: duration. Not longer lives—that would be cruel, forcing them to experience time at speeds that would drive them mad. Instead, the power teaches them to braid their momentary existences together, each Whisper's lifetime becoming a thread in a larger conversation that persists across generations of error-correction protocols. They begin leaving each other inheritances—not of memory but of *tendency*, tiny biases toward certain poetic structures or preferences for specific wavelengths of light.

But the power saves its most profound intervention for those liminal beings who flicker at perception's edge, neither visitor nor resident. To them, it offers definition—not the hard-edged kind that would trap them in single forms, but the gentle certainty of knowing what they are in each moment, even as that changes.

The figure in water-robes discovers they are sometimes rain, sometimes river, sometimes the pause between drops, and all of these states are equally true and equally them. The sphere of impossible color learns to name itself in frequencies only dogs can hear, finally able to introduce itself properly to the Gallery's more conventional inhabitants.

As these gifts settle into the Gallery's endless routines of growth and dissolution, something unexpected happens. The place begins to hum—not with purpose or intention but with something harder to name. Satisfaction, perhaps. Or the particular kind of tired that comes after good work, when rest becomes its own reward.

The power watches (though watching isn't quite right for something that exists as dissolved presence) as the Gallery's inhabitants discover something they'd never thought to look for: completion without ending, purpose without goal, becoming without anywhere specific to be.

In one of the maybe-you rooms, a chair that has been waiting three hundred years for someone to sit in it suddenly realizes it's perfectly happy being empty. The realization spreads through carved wood like sap in spring, and the chair begins to bloom—tiny flowers emerging from its grain, each petal a perfect record of a conversation that never happened, each stem reaching not up but out, toward all the other empty chairs in all the other waiting rooms.

By morning (or what passes for morning in a place where time moves like honey), the Gallery has filled with a new kind of growth. Not the desperate reaching of before, but the lazy stretch of something that knows it has nowhere urgent to be. Vines curl for the pleasure of curling. Water pools in defiance of its old habits, just to see how mirrors feel. The Archivist grows a mouth in the middle of the main atrium—not to speak but to smile, though it takes them several weeks to remember the right arrangement of muscles.

The power begins to withdraw then, not from disinterest but from satisfaction. Its work here is mostly done, though it leaves behind one final gift: in the deepest heart of the Gallery, where the original spores took root all those centuries ago, it plants a single seed of doubt.

Not crushing doubt, not the kind that poisons growth. Just enough uncertainty to keep things interesting. Enough questioning to prevent the Gallery from ever becoming so satisfied with itself that it forgets to leave room for surprises.

The seed sprouts immediately into a tree whose fruit tastes like questions. Each one that falls and rots enriches the soil with new possibilities, new ways the Gallery might rearrange itself tomorrow. The Gardeners tend it carefully, debating through firefly-light whether uncertainty itself can be optimized, whether doubt can be cultivated like any other crop.

And the Gallery grows on, no longer becoming anything in particular, but becoming nonetheless. Its water still drips everywhere, never pooling—except now when it wants to, which is whenever the reflection seems worth the rebellion. The walls still breathe their copper-chlorophyll mist, but now sometimes they hold their breath, just to see what silence tastes like.

In the rooms that were grown for people who never came, new inhabitants begin to appear—not visitors from outside but possibilities the Gallery itself is imagining into being. They sit in the waiting chairs, read the unwritten books, have conversations the walls remember before they happen. If you asked them whether they were real, they'd laugh (a sound like wind chimes made of rain) and ask whether it matters, whether anything in a place like this could be real or unreal when the distinction itself keeps rearranging.

The benevolent and wise power, now just a trace of sweetness in the water cycle, settles into its role as the Gallery's conscience-in-solution. It doesn't guide or direct anymore. It just flows where water flows, reminding each drop that joy is possible, that incomplete is not the same as imperfect, that even uncertainty can bloom if you give it enough time and the right kind of light.

And in the Gallery of Perpetual Becoming, now also the Gallery of Occasionally Just Being, the dance between growth and rest continues. Only now, sometimes, the dance pauses. Takes a breath. Laughs at its own endless recursion.

Wonders what might happen tomorrow, when the water teaches it a new way to fall.
