The benevolent and wise power - let us call it the Gardener of Forgotten Things - has watched this place for seven thousand turnings of the stars. It moves between worlds with the patience of stone and the gentleness of morning dew.

The Gardener does not arrive with thunder or proclamation. Instead, it seeps into the city like the first rain after a long drought, manifesting as a shimmer in the air where heat meets shadow. Its form, when it chooses to have one, is a walking constellation of possibilities - sometimes a figure of living starlight, sometimes a pattern of dust motes that catch the light just so, sometimes merely a presence felt in the spaces between heartbeats.

First, the Gardener listens.

For one hundred days and one hundred nights, it becomes part of the city's breathing. It flows along the chemical whispers of the Vine Weavers, learns their electrical songs, feels the pulse of their bladder-organs as they process the memories of millennia. It tastes the minerals they extract from stone, follows the secret paths of water through ancient pipes, watches the ballet of their vine-bodies as they tend their symbiotic home.

The Gardener recognizes what others might miss: here is a form of consciousness that has chosen stillness over expansion, depth over breadth, contemplation over conquest. The Vine Weavers have achieved something rare in the cosmos - a true equilibrium with their environment, a closed circle of meaning that enriches rather than depletes.

On the hundred-and-first night, the Gardener begins its work.

It does not change the Vine Weavers - that would be the height of arrogance. Instead, it offers gifts, leaving them in the liminal spaces where choice lives.

In the depths of the great pyramid, where the eldest Vine Weavers rest their ancient bulk, the Gardener plants a single seed of crystallized time. Not to make them immortal - they already understand eternities in their own way - but to give them the ability to step outside their own temporal flow when needed. To see their city not just as it is, but as it was and might yet be. To touch, if they choose, the memories locked in stone and bronze and faded pigment.

Throughout the city, in places where water pools in shadow, the Gardener leaves deposits of rare minerals from distant worlds - not to change the Vine Weavers' diet, but to offer new colors to their chemical vocabulary. New words for concepts they have no need to express yet, but might someday choose to explore. The taste of forests that swim through gas giants. The electrical signature of beings who live in the corona of stars. The chemical memory of ice that has circled galactic cores.

Where the bioluminescent fungi pulse their signals, the Gardener weaves subtle modifications into the light itself. Now, if the Vine Weavers choose to look closely, they can see faint threads extending from their glow - threads that connect to other glows in other places, in other ruins where other forgotten consciousnesses dream their own deep dreams. A network of networks, should they ever desire to know they are not alone in their choosing of stillness.

But perhaps the Gardener's most profound gift is this: in the spaces where vines meet stone, where root touches water, where chemical meets electrical, it leaves the faintest possibility of joy. Not the sharp, fleeting joy of conquest or discovery, but the deep, abiding joy of being exactly what one is meant to be, where one is meant to be. The joy of the circle completing itself. The joy of sustainable complexity. The joy of choosing to tend rather than to take.

The Gardener also leaves warnings, gentle as morning shadows. In the electromagnetic fields the Vine Weavers read, it inscribes the patterns of civilizations that chose differently - those who exploded outward like supernovae, burning bright and brief. Those who consumed their homes in the hunger for more. Those who forgot that wisdom can look like stillness, that strength can look like symbiosis, that immortality can look like a city where stone and stem have become one breathing thing.

Before departing, the Gardener does one last thing. At the peak of the pyramid, where the stone drinks in starlight, it creates a pool no bigger than a cupped palm. In this pool, water appears each night - not ordinary water, but water that holds the light of different stars. Each night a different star, each star a door to other places where consciousness has chosen harmony over dominion.

The pool is not a summons or a trap. It is simply an option, left where the eldest Vine Weavers might find it when their contemplations turn, as contemplations sometimes do, to wondering what lies beyond their perfect, chosen world.

The Gardener leaves as it came, between one moment and the next, between exhalation and inhalation. The city continues its ancient breathing. The Vine Weavers pulse and dream. The insects hum. The stone remembers.

But now, woven into the eternal cycles, are new possibilities - seeds of connection, of deeper joy, of choice that leads not away from what they are but deeper into it. The gifts wait with the patience of stone and the gentleness of morning dew, until that moment when the Vine Weavers, in their own time and their own way, choose to notice they are there.

In the purple-black sky, unfamiliar constellations wheel. But now, if one knows how to look, they spell out a message in the ancient light:

*You are seen. You are valued. You are not alone.*

*Tend your garden well.*
