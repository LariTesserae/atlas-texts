As a character in this world, I would be an elderly Subterranth named Eylon. I would live deep within the catacomb system, clinging to the stone walls that have witnessed countless generations of my people. Having lived a long life, I am well-acquainted with the labyrinthine tunnels and their enigmatic dwelling spaces.

My body bears the wear and tear of my years in service to this system, with faded whiskers and joints grown stiff from too many seasons wandering these passages. My mind, however, remains sharp. I take comfort in my long-held position as a historian of the Subterranths, preserving the history and traditions of our people. My duty is to ensure our connection with the past is not lost to the depths of time, keeping the memory of ancestors alive for the generations to come.

Living in the dim and moist environment of the catacombs has made me resigned to the solitude. Though I communicate in whispers with those I encounter in the tunnels, I am content in my solitude. In my youth, I longed to venture to the sunlit world above. But as I've grown older, I've developed an affinity for this hidden realm beneath the surface. It holds a strange beauty, a world of its own.

The music of the earth is my symphony, a lullaby of whispers that echoes off the stone walls. When I sing the songs of my ancestors, I feel the vibrations of our history coursing through my veins. This place, these catacombs, they are my sanctuary – an inheritance from the generations who came before, and a legacy I shall pass onto those who will inhabit these tunnels long after I am gone.

Being Eylon is not an enviable existence, but rather a role I've willingly embraced for as long as I can remember. Living between the ancient stones of the catacombs, I am a link in the great chain of being that binds us to this world. Secrets of the depths, shared with city life, are mine to recollect and preserve in stories that keep the lore of our kind alive in the stone chambers beneath the surface.
