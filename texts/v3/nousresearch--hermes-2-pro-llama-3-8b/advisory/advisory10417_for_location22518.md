3. Understand the flora and fauna: Knowing which plants are safe to touch and which creatures may pose a threat will help prevent missteps. Achieve this through research or by consulting with knowledgeable locals.

4. Special clothing and accessories: In addition to a wetsuit, bringing extra clothing layers and waterproof shoes will offer protection from the cold, dark depths. Heavy-duty gloves can protect hands when exploring rocky grottoes.

5. Learn navigation techniques: Navigating the labyrinthine rock formations and avoiding getting lost in the dense vegetation requires learning how to use a compass and perhaps crude maps based on local knowledge.

6. Bring necessary tools and supplies: If intending to interact with the locals, bring hand tools, materials, and supplies to barter or trade with them. Be cautious not to disrupt the balance of the ecosystem when taking samples or leaving the site.

7. Embrace the slow pace: As time moves differently here, be prepared for a slower rhythm of life. Allow yourself to relax and enjoy the tranquil, surreal nature of the place.

8. Health precautions: Always ensure you have any necessary vaccines or medications recommended for traveling to this area. Be prepared for the possibility of illness due to the unique environment and flora in Submergence.

9. Travel in numbers: Safety-wise, it's better to travel in groups when exploring such an uncharted territory. Get a local guide or experienced divers to ensure you navigate through the underwater world safely.

10. Respect the environment: Whether you're there for a few hours or several days, respect the balance of the environment and aim to leave no trace behind. Remember to dispose of any waste appropriately or take it with you when leaving.

With these preparations, a human multiverse traveler can immerse themselves in the watery, delightful world of Submergence, experiencing the wonders and mysteries of this unique underwater world with respect and curiosity.
