The landscape lies beneath the surface, forming a vast network of interconnected chambers, tunnels, and caverns. These are the lairs of the industrialized, constructed world, which while not fully detached from nature, is predominately made with little natural substrate to be seen.

The temperature here simulates conditions of a hot alien world, a phenomenon the builders have harnessed, maintaining a comfortable temperature for their operations. Despite this heat, you'll find a chilly dampness in some of the workings. Riverine water courses and pockets of liquid amid larger pockets of dryness refresh the air and nurture limited but industrious flora - only because, in the confines of the caverns, water finds a way.

The environment's density is high, cramped even. The artificially constructed world inhabits every available crevice, leaving little room for the natural environment. Still, traces of the original world persist amidst the machinery and the hard-pressed plant life.

The landscape suffers from a chronic lack of light. Illumination largely comes from the artificial sources built specifically to combat the darkness. This means electric light glowing off machinery and the dim lights of the riverine networks.

While the environment has limited fauna, every life form contributes to the strange balance of this sealed world. "Rare" is not a hindrance here; it is a necessity. Surviving is a testament to resourcefulness, as every element, plant, and animal external to the built environment plays its part.

Lastly, an unusual quality pervades the subterranean world: the convergence of dampness, heat, and slow running processes, imbuing the place with a unique, lived in character. This is an environment far removed from the surface world, embodying a strangeness and charm all its own.
