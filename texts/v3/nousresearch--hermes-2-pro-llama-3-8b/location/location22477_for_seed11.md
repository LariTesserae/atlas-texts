In the high, rugged valley of Escalantura, the landscape is a testament to years of turbulent change. The air hangs heavy with the dampness that seeps from misty mornings and infrequent rains, whispering stories of the ancient rivers that once flowed through this land, leaving behind swirling marks in the primordial earth.

Lush and verdant, the vegetation greens the slopes of Escalantuna, thriving in pockets of water amidst the large expanses of dry, weary earth. The water, often sporadic, can create a sudden oasis, but quickly disappears if not nurtured by the rare, intermittent rains. The ground, marshy near areas of water, gives way to solid earth leading up to rocky cliffs and craggy summits.

Although the valley stretches across town-or-valley-size dimensions, the deceptive serenity belies the extreme elevation that characterizes Escalantuna. With peaks reaching toward the sky and barycentric depressions, the landscapes varies from near-cultivated-flatness in patches of fertile earth to sheer slopes that require the capabilities of primordial-tech tools. Despite the artificial light, the strangeness of Escalantuna comes from its geology, weather, and deep history.

The scattered structures, faint reminders of occupation in the past, punctuate the landscape with the occasional half-fallen wall or partially buried foundation. Artifacts speak of struggles to thrive in this harsh place, while the mystery of the rare fauna makes for a largely empty world.

In this landscape, powerful sounds shatter the quiet. The deafening cacophony is often fractured by wind whistling through hollows and across the high slopes. Storms can be sudden, with pressures equal to space itself, delving into the turmoil that has always surrounded Escalantuna.

As Escalantuna residents learn, time and history are impermanent in this turbulent world. With a turbulent force that could rival a tempest's eye, this landscape reshapes itself daily, revealing this as the oldest-stable-state of a world always in motion and in flux.

Ecology, erosion, and elevation collide in this strange and beautiful landscape.
