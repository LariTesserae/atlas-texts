In the secretive crevices of a low, barren land held in the grip of an unyielding cool, squirrels and eggs reside. Quivering under the ferocious glare of the perpetual brilliance bestowed by the alien zenith, this hidden refuge withers forlornly.

The ashen desolation brims with unusual motifs of erosion, for time here has established order through great patience and persistence. Every facet holds a story told in fractured lines of pockmarked minerals — sacred and profaned in turn. The spirits of ancient lives linger on these exposed memories, their weary wails a mournful dirge for the transience of all things.

The atmosphere presents a practiced equilibrium, eschewing any and all variation. Encased in this desiccated void, every element maintains a tightly keyed dance, never allowing deviation or exchange, no variance in the vaporous ballet that circulates from every pore. This air is far from corporeal, more akin to a thinned and transparent broth, providing little medium for any life beyond that which has developed fortitude akin to the land that births them.

The dismal burial site of all sentient development, only the unassuming floral sparse fragment persists. These insubstantial plants, long acclimatized to the rigorous lack of water and constant light, have locked away their sustenance in resilient spores which may endure even in the austere opposition. Each becomes a vessel for the bewildering ascent to life, waiting for eons to translate themselves into fruition whenever the stars are willing to offer their scarce resources.

An eerie graveyard, this sunlit desert levitates in time, hovering in that liminal space between ever-changing and never-altered. Timelessly absorbing the ivy scaffold of all that dares to violate its borders, it mocks the delusion of permanence. For in this frigid, spike-filled domain of moon-colored stones and rock-cold starlight, the thrill of invention finds only a bleak solace or forced, furtive permanence. In this perpetual crystal, the only anomaly is the notion of being still — the everything and nothing of this world.
