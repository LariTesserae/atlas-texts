The landscape was an ever-shifting realm of frozen rivers and rivers of ice. It lay on the edge of a world that could hardly be described, rooted in raw geological tissue between unstable fault lines of colossal scale.

Vegetation flourished here in fits and starts, born of youthful earth that sparked from its surface. Sometimes it seemed as if water welled up and burst forth, anonymous and policy-free, only to freeze again as a rash of white threads against a pale gray canvas. These small infantile hands of nature struggled to ornate the realm, their gaze suddenly shy when winter took its leave.

Towns had sprung up sporadically amidst the tatters of ice and snow, fractured villages pinned like specimens in the cracks of a mammoth mosaic. The fronds of frozen carp were as thick as algae, bearing the weight of crowns and lake-dwelling machinations. The fertile and infertile landscapes rubbed elbows in this expanse, leaning together like conspirators in the dark while squatters remained engrossed in the stubborn dance of brutal numbers.

A river claimed the landscape, its meandering path eternally victim to the ceaseless erosive force of tumultuous extremes. Amid a cacophony of voices—the salivary splatter of icy chunks and the shrill incisor-work of ceaseless cold—the river kept a dogged indiscreet pace, departing as fast as it came. This watery enigma provided much-required relief and sustenance to many of the towns, unjustly splintering the realm depending on their size, guile, and tenacity.

The towns and cities themselves could only be likened to a profusion of industrial monoliths that heaved and tolled in paradox. Such was the city of Pissaquion, a pulsing organ nestled in the glacier's gory upper workings. Cities there were that kept pace with the sibilant round of oscillations, always shifting, always changing, nearly frozen in their constant metamorphosis. Factories wheezed and groaned, strings of ruined tenements that stretched horribly up the jagged terrain in accidental solidarity.

The realm danced to the rhythms of light, which pulsed through the aorta of its wedge-bearing cities with a genesis of unpredictable bass, accompanied by celestial keys. Adrenaline scudded around more tender structures, befitting a fitful advent of electric seconds echoing with some fabricated haste, creating a mildly discordant fluke of multiplex alignments.

A tumultuous unison surfaced as animals darted through the landscape, the underlying harmony rending their actions. Songbirds suddenly burst forth in delicious, violaceous tones that interlocked and bound more languid players to the fray. As the weather waxed and waned, rosetta stones were scattered about, giving birth to strange and illicit charms between the otherwise adverse tastes of the realm.

The tumult of streets shivered and jarred, retaliating against stiff freeways in a dynamic juxtaposition of temporary sound that broadcasted the power of temporary subjugation. The "percussive" sound of the realm broke through in spurts between the orchestrated hums and hiss of laden water, feral animals, and languid railroading, often out of a slow-cogging confection of stone.

The several-threads-of-light realm buzzed and stuttered before the blinding pallor of a hyperactive factory-city, cultivating a sulfuric haze above its path. The landscape rashly pushed through the crimson iron of a particular quarry set deep in natural apostasy, furtively spending in rapt economy.

Unspoken anxieties of unbound metal undermined this landscape, whispering that it was the last grasp of a perpetually decaying civilization. Tender moments of grime and life fought to inhabit these bony buildings, the clandestine hum of infrastructure lingering in the surrounding underworld. But here the world pruned itself, the realm's deliquescence a thin, ever-thinning stretch of holder-realm-scape, steadily surrendering to the monkish humdrums of bloated industry.

Pivoting through the possible lived experiences of this realm took great effort, the atmosphere carrying the vestiges of undocumented and hidden histories—a landscape clothed in symbols that bore the enigma of a lost civilization, its facades crisscrossed with illegible patterns of intimate stories left behind. With this, the realm itself was a communal living relic, held in a paradox of internal decay and intestinal fortitude that attributed something to a population of pillagers and plunderers. In the end, it remained a constant riddle—beautiful, strange, and lost.
