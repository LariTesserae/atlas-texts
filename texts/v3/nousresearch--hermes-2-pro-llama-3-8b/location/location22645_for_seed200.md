The landscape described is a region of Scrith, where geometric seas contend in an eternal ballet, and angular strewnfields  sands of possibility  shift and exchange with the shape of the land. It is a post-rain place, vibrant with lonely promise after the washing has retreated to the pebble-bunched shores.

Here, the humid soak of ground lies dense underfoot, carrying water like the deck of a ship, so the landscape breathes with an earthy sibilance as each deluge seeps deeper into the lying clays of the terrain, each drop threading through unseen channels. You can hear it if you listen closely enough.

Towering over this is an ecology that comes in waves: verdant lianas coil upwards in a fight against time; leathery ferns that clutch to boulders, extending their roots through cracks invisible to the eye; bushes cede to thicker groves, which bear the heavier demands of the canopy. It is a thicket of botanical intricacy; there is no rail of brown that doesn't hide some scrambling story of green reclamation.

Though the temperature burns at the hottest, it holds no trace of engineered technology to abide within, no wires or metals nor shades of human-built construction distort this region. This is a place where only biological life thrives: crickets pierce the wild air with their mean chirping, pheasant beetle flights blanket the skies with interference patterns, a tumult of colour that a human would struggle to avoid collision with.

Underneath it all hovers a dim light, an unequal struggle from the frosted sun that hooks over the far-off horizon line, shut out by the hanging lianas and the high swaying branches of crowned trees, where the gloom of shade holds its own. Its luminescence is rare, tardy.

Amidst this are the creatures born of the strange and the grasping, those wonderful riddles of what happens when no human lays a hand to design them. The sibilant tongue of a blind killifish, dappled in mud and the heat haze; balloon-like mammals whose limits exist only to prove elegance; graze-nodders, constantly questioning the upright walk: all coexist within this angular and primordial region.

There is nothing static in this place. There is nowhere to stand still, for the land itself ebbs and flows like the geometric seas above it, an ever-modifying sprawl of geological conundrums. No gradient holds its form for long; relentless processes reshape everything according to the whim of the land, the heat of its vitals.

In the midst of it all, there is a cunning and strange interplay between every element mentioned. One can only scratch the surface in an attempt to describe it all, and within a blink of an eye, some unseen force creates something wholly new and unexpected. It is a place of wild, unexplored beauty, defiant of the plausible system.
