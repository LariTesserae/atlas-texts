In the depths of Eremia, an unseen civilization thrives. Known as the Urks, these beings are the original inhabitants of this ancient realm. Heightened senses allow them to navigate the labyrinthine tunnels with ease, while their forms have evolved to withstand the damp, cool conditions. Their opalescent skin reflects the precious light that penetrates the depths, permitting them to absorb nutrients from the minerals in the stone. This adaptation allows them to subsist without the need for food or water, as the Urks have long since lost the biological need for sustenance. Instead, they spend their time examining the patterns and shapes that etch their way of life into the stone.

Art and mathematics are paramount in Urk culture, and the beauty of the intricate designs is a source of pride for all of Eremia. The structures built by the Urks reflect their deep understanding of the environment's unique characteristics and the specific chisel-workings of each layer of stone. Architecture both honors and harnesses the caverns' existing geological frameworks, layered to suit the needs of the inhabitants who dwell in harmony with the landscape itself.

Beyond the immediate settlements, great halls stand as monuments to the Urk's artistic and intellectual pursuits. Built within the network of accessible caves, these grand edifices are used to conduct ceremonies, share knowledge, and explore the mental boundaries of the Urk's ancient and inscrutable culture.

The touch of Urk society is as urbane as their world is rustic, with ordinary conversations focused on the sharing of Enlightenment; the high, guttural language is punctuated by sound echoes, their unique communication system that leverages the exact acoustics of their underground realm. The ever-changing and tumultuous environment of Eremia has thrust the Urks into a world of constant unpredictability. Theirs is a society built around flexibility, the ability to change and adapt to an ever-eroding landscape. Lifetime employment is rare in Urk culture, with individuals frequently shifting roles according to the demands of the day and the needs of their people.

Despite the Urk's physical and cultural uniqueness, many argue that they are far from alien; rather than an entirely new lifeform, the Urks might be seen as analogous to the peculiar clusters of insects with whom we share our own world. Their existence, like that of the insects, serves to illustrate and magnify the adaptability of life in the face of adversity. Embodying a triumph over extinction, the Urks are the ultimate symbol of life's indomitable will.

In the end, Eremia stands as a testament to the strength of life's will to survive in the most unlikely of places. With all of the otherworldliness and peculiarity it embodies, it reminds us to never underestimate the tenacity of life, as even in the unlikeliest of places, shadows of the unknown hold the most fascinating of realms.
