The inhabitants of this place are a diverse array of beings, each uniquely adapted to the harsh and peculiar environment. At the base of the food chain are the microorganisms that thrive in the damp, nutrient-rich soil, breaking down organic matter and contributing to the rich urban ecosystem. They are joined by molds, fungi, and small insects that find sustenance in the detritus of the town's industry and agriculture.

As the visitor moves up the ladder of life, they might spy industrious creatures mechanized for survival in these unnatural constructions. Contractions of their original form, these metallic beings, a meld of metal and machine, have taken to the cityscape as their domain. The clockwork rats, with wheels replacing legs, navigate the labyrinth of pipes and ducts, their keen senses adapted for tracking and hunting. Hovering above the treetops are the sky birds, wings replaced with built-up kites, learning from the heavy clouds above to soar and drift on the currents.

There are few animals left in their natural state, having long been domesticated or artificially engineered to serve the needs of the urbanites in elegant, mechanical ways. Electric eels glide through the murky waters, providing the electricity to power the machines, while modified rabbits and foxes move silently through the fog, sniffing out stops in pipes and detecting any trace of gas leaks.

The human population has adapted in their own ways. The town's inhabitants, a skein of engineers and innovators, live harmoniously with the industrial landscape, embedded deep into the sinews of their constructions. They maintain the labyrinth of pipes and wires, overseeing the ceaseless hum of energy, and have made peace with their surroundings. Nvidia of their work, the humans now play a role, not just in casing and maintaining the tools of the industry, but in harmonizing the the town's advanced ecosystem. They do this by nurturing the growth of the once-barren landscape, developing plants that sustain and feed the strange and wonderful residents living above the lowlands.

These beings, all life adapted to this unique urban wilderness, coexist in a beautiful, uncanny chorus of metal and green, of hum and dampened whispers. Day and night shift in tandem with the fluctuations of the industry, giving way to a unique biosphere, a proud creature of the town and its industrious way of life. The landscape, a reflection of the mixture of natural and built, is alive and growing, and yet another proof of the constant, creative, and eroding forces of time.
