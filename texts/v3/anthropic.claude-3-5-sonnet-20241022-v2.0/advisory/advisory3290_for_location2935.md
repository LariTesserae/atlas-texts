Preparation Guide for Visiting the Garden Cities

Essential Equipment:
- Lightweight, breathable clothing that can handle humidity
- Non-slip, soft-soled boots that allow you to feel surface changes
- Waterproof bags for electronics and documents
- Multiple small LED lights with red filter options (to minimize disruption to bioluminescent ecosystems)
- Climbing harness and basic safety gear
- Moisture-wicking undergarments
- Small towels for constant dampness
- Antihistamines and basic first aid
- Water purification system (though city water may be safe, better cautious)

Physical Preparation:
- Practice climbing and balance exercises
- Develop comfort with heights and swaying motions
- Work on night vision adaptation
- Build flexibility for navigating organic tunnels and passages
- Learn basic breathing techniques for high humidity environments

Mental Preparation:
- Meditation practice to cope with constant movement
- Exposure therapy to dark, enclosed spaces
- Study of basic botanical concepts
- Practice moving slowly and deliberately
- Development of spatial awareness without visual cues

Special Considerations:
1. Bio-respect protocols:
- No cutting or damaging living surfaces
- Minimize impact on bioluminescent organisms
- Learn to read growth patterns before touching surfaces
- Respect the "breathing" cycles of the structure

2. Navigation:
- Learn to identify pattern variations in the bioluminescent moss
- Understand basic growth geometries
- Practice using sound echoes for orientation
- Develop system for marking position without damaging surfaces

3. Temporal awareness:
- Track growth-decomposition cycles
- Learn to identify unsafe areas preparing for decomposition
- Understand bridge movement patterns
- Monitor structural sounds for safety

4. Communication:
- Establish clear signals for group communication in dark
- Learn to distinguish between normal and warning sounds
- Develop touch-based communication for zero visibility situations

Safety Protocols:
- Never travel alone
- Maintain constant contact with stable surfaces
- Regular hydration checks
- Establish safe retreat paths
- Monitor for allergic reactions to novel organisms
- Keep distance from actively growing or decomposing areas

Cultural Sensitivity:
- Consider the cities as living entities
- Move at the cities' pace, not yours
- Minimize disruption to natural cycles
- Respect any remaining gardener traditions

Duration Management:
- Plan shorter initial visits to acclimate
- Schedule rest periods to process the unusual environment
- Allow time for dark adaptation
- Build in flexibility for city's organic schedule

Personal Comfort:
- Bring calming aids (familiar scents, textures)
- Pack light but prepare for constant moisture
- Consider using natural cordage instead of synthetic
- Wear minimal jewelry that could catch on growth

Most importantly, approach with patience and humility. The Garden Cities operate on their own timescale and patterns - success comes from adaptation to their rhythm rather than imposing human expectations. Visitors should prepare to be changed by the experience rather than simply observing it.
