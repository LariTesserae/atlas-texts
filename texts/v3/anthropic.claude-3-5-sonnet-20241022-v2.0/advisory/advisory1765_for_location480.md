Preparation Guide for Human Visitors to the Migrant City

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Respiratory equipment: Bring a high-quality filter mask with moisture management and replaceable filters. The air is thick with mineral particulates and ozone.
- Protective eyewear: Sealed goggles with anti-fog coating to protect from mineral-rich moisture and occasional acidic condensation
- Full-body weatherproof suit: Must be acid-resistant and breathable. Multiple layers recommended.
- Specialized footwear: Non-slip boots with excellent grip for wet metal surfaces
- Electromagnetic field protection: Shielded equipment cases and personal EMF protection due to the Migrants' bioelectric fields
- Portable water purification system: Despite abundant water, it's highly mineralized and needs treatment

NAVIGATION & SAFETY:
- Altimeter and pressure gauge: Essential for tracking your position in the vertical city
- Bio-signal broadcaster: Helps Migrants detect and avoid you
- Climbing gear: Including anchors suitable for metal surfaces
- Emergency shelter pod: In case you need to wait out a Migration event
- Powerful light source: For the darker regions, preferably red-spectrum to minimize disruption to bioluminescent organisms
- Thermal imaging equipment: Helps navigate the complex temperature gradients

ENVIRONMENTAL ADAPTATION:
- Acclimatization period: Spend 2-3 days at base level before ascending
- Time management: Plan around known Migration patterns (consult local guides)
- Scale reference tools: Bring laser measuring devices to help navigate human-safe paths through oversized spaces

HEALTH CONSIDERATIONS:
- Pre-trip medical screening: Check for respiratory sensitivities and inner ear issues
- Electrolyte supplements: The mineral-rich environment can affect body chemistry
- Anti-vertigo medication: The vast spaces and constant motion can be disorienting
- Skin protection: Barrier cream against mineral-rich moisture

CULTURAL/SCIENTIFIC PROTOCOLS:
- Non-invasive observation equipment: Sound and electromagnetic field recorders
- Maintain distance from maintenance Migrants: Their work is crucial to city function
- Follow established human pathways: Don't disturb accumulated growth patterns
- Documentation tools: Moisture-resistant notebooks/devices

TIMING CONSIDERATIONS:
- Visit during minor social seasons to avoid major Migration events
- Plan for longer stays: The city's rhythms don't align with human timescales
- Build in buffer days: Weather patterns can delay movement between levels

COMMUNICATION:
- Low-frequency radio equipment: Works better in the mineral-rich atmosphere
- Visual signaling devices: For emergency communication
- Translation algorithms: To interpret basic Migrant movement patterns

PSYCHOLOGICAL PREPARATION:
- Meditation/centering practices: The constant motion and sound can be overwhelming
- Scale adaptation exercises: Helps manage spatial disorientation
- Patience training: The city operates on geological timeframes
- Sensory adjustment period: Allow time to adapt to the constant background noise

LOCAL SUPPORT:
- Connect with established human research stations
- Hire experienced guides familiar with safe passages
- Establish emergency extraction protocols
- Register your visit with monitoring stations

Remember: You are visiting a living system that operates on its own terms. Success depends on adaptation rather than attempting to impose human patterns on this unique environment. Flexibility and respect for the city's rhythms are essential.
