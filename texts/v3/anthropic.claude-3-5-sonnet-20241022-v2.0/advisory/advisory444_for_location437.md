Preparation Guide for Visiting the Ice-Gate Domain

Essential Gear:
- Multiple layers of advanced cold weather clothing, including:
  * Moisture-wicking base layer
  * Thick insulating middle layer
  * Waterproof, breathable outer shell
  * Insulated, waterproof boots rated for extreme cold
  * Multiple pairs of wool socks
  * Heated gloves with waterproof shells
  * Face protection (balaclava + goggles)
- Specialized equipment:
  * Crampons or ice cleats for walking on frozen surfaces
  * Collapsible hiking poles with snow baskets
  * Emergency shelter (lightweight tent or bivy sack)
  * Chemical hand/body warmers (multiple)
  * Specialized anti-fog coating for goggles and cameras
  * UV-protective sunglasses (the reflection from ice can be blinding)

Physical Preparation:
- Acclimatize to high altitude before visiting (location appears to be in high mountains)
- Practice cold weather survival techniques
- Build endurance for walking on unstable surfaces
- Learn basic ice climbing and glacier traversal skills
- Practice controlled breathing in cold conditions

Safety Considerations:
- Never travel alone; always use buddy system
- Bring emergency communication device (though signals may be unreliable)
- Carry basic first aid kit plus hypothermia-specific supplies
- Bring emergency food and water (insulated containers)
- Learn symptoms of hypothermia and altitude sickness
- Map your route on the wooden walkways beforehand
- Avoid touching any pools directly
- Maintain distance from glass-wings (unknown if hostile)

Scientific/Documentation Equipment:
- Cold-weather rated camera with multiple batteries
- Waterproof notebook and special cold-weather pens
- Small spectrometer if possible
- Temperature and humidity sensors
- Sample collection containers (if permitted)

Spiritual/Mental Preparation:
- Meditation practice to maintain calm in disorienting conditions
- Study any available ice-priest protocols or rituals
- Prepare for possible reality-bending experiences
- Practice maintaining orientation in low-visibility conditions
- Research known glass-wing behavior patterns

Cultural Considerations:
- Seek permission from ice-priests before visiting
- Learn their basic customs and taboos
- Bring appropriate offerings if required
- Learn any restricted areas or pools
- Understand proper behavior near sacred sites

Time Management:
- Plan visit during optimal light conditions
- Account for slower movement on ice
- Build in extra time for disorientation
- Schedule regular warming breaks
- Plan shorter initial visits to assess tolerance

Special Notes:
- Bring no metal if possible (may affect local energies)
- Consider bringing ice-priest approved offerings
- Prepare for visual distortions and unusual phenomena
- Be ready to abort visit if conditions become too intense
- Document any unusual patterns or glass-wing behaviors
- Avoid making loud noises or sudden movements
- Respect the delicate ecosystem of each pool

Remember:
This is not just a physical journey but a venture into a space where normal rules may not fully apply. Maintain flexibility in your preparations and be ready to adapt to unexpected conditions. Mental preparation may be as important as physical gear.

Final Advice:
Start with shorter visits to acclimate to the unusual conditions. Pay attention to your body's reactions and any unusual mental effects. Consider multiple visits rather than one long exposure. Always defer to ice-priest guidance and local protocols.
