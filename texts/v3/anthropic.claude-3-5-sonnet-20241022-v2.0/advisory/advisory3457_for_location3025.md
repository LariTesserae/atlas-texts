Preparation Guidelines for Visiting the Living Caverns

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Full-body sealed environmental suit rated for biological containment
- Minimum 12-hour independent air supply with biological/chemical filters
- Backup air system and emergency purification tablets
- Anti-fog coating for suit viewplates
- Chemical-resistant boots with excellent grip (surfaces will be slick)
- Structural reinforcement in suit joints (high-pressure environment)
- Multiple light sources (minimum 3) with backup power
- Recording equipment in waterproof/chemical-resistant housing

SAFETY EQUIPMENT:
- Emergency beacon with satellite uplink capability
- Chemical analysis kit to monitor atmospheric composition
- Portable airlock/decontamination chamber for exit procedures
- Rescue line with automatic retraction (minimum 100m)
- Cutting tools for emergency egress if trapped by growth
- Chemical neutralizing agents and basic first aid
- Emergency shelter pod (in case of becoming trapped)

MONITORING GEAR:
- Real-time biometric monitoring system
- Structural integrity sensors for suit breach detection
- Chemical exposure badges
- Motion tracking system (cavern layouts change rapidly)
- 3D mapping device with breadcrumb tracking

PROTOCOLS:
1. Never enter without a surface team monitoring your position
2. Maintain constant communication with surface team
3. Set maximum exploration time of 4 hours per visit
4. Use buddy system - never explore alone
5. Mark exit routes with both physical and digital markers
6. Establish safe rooms every 50 meters with emergency supplies
7. Regular suit integrity checks every 15 minutes

MEDICAL PREPARATION:
- Full physical examination before entry
- Updated vaccinations including experimental anti-fungal treatments
- Blood work to establish baseline for post-visit comparison
- Implanted medical monitor for real-time vitals tracking
- Anti-nausea medication (movement of walls can cause severe disorientation)
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics on hand

TRAINING REQUIREMENTS:
- Advanced confined space certification
- Biological hazard handling certification
- Emergency decontamination procedures
- High-pressure environment training
- Orientation in zero-visibility conditions
- Emergency equipment deployment
- Psychological preparation for biological-mechanical hybrid environments

RESEARCH PREPARATION:
- Study all previous expedition logs
- Review current theories on colony behavior patterns
- Learn recognition patterns for dangerous growth formations
- Understand basic chemical signals and their meanings
- Familiarize yourself with known safe zones and danger areas

POST-VISIT PROCEDURES:
1. Minimum 48-hour quarantine
2. Full decontamination sequence (minimum 3 stages)
3. Medical examination and blood work
4. Equipment sterilization and disposal of disposables
5. Detailed debriefing and psychological evaluation

PSYCHOLOGICAL CONSIDERATIONS:
- Prepare for severe disorientation
- Expect strong feelings of being "watched" or "hunted"
- May experience temporary synesthesia due to chemical exposure
- High risk of claustrophobia and panic attacks
- Possibility of temporary psychiatric effects from biochemical exposure

TIME LIMITATIONS:
- Maximum single visit: 4 hours
- Minimum rest between visits: 72 hours
- Maximum visits per month: 4
- Lifetime exposure limit: 100 hours

The key to survival in this environment is maintaining absolute isolation from the biological systems while still being able to observe and study them. Your suit is your lifeline - treat it as such. Remember that everything in this environment is dynamic and potentially hostile. Always maintain multiple exit strategies and never assume that what was safe an hour ago is still safe now.
