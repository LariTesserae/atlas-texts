Preparation Guide for Visiting the Geometric Gardens

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Anti-vertigo medication (prescription strength recommended)
- Motion sickness patches
- Non-polarized wraparound goggles with adjustable tint
- Moisture-wicking, breathable clothing in neutral colors
- Boots with excellent grip and ankle support
- Emergency beacon/dimensional anchor device
- Basic first aid kit with additional mental health supplies
- Water purification tablets (despite the pure-looking pools, better safe than sorry)
- High-calorie emergency rations
- Multiple light sources (the ambient light can be unreliable)

PHYSICAL PREPARATION (Begin 2-3 months prior):
- Balance training exercises
- Meditation practice focusing on spatial awareness
- Exposure therapy to heights and unusual perspectives
- Basic rock climbing or bouldering experience
- Flexibility training (you may need to move in unusual ways)
- Cardio conditioning for high altitude

MENTAL PREPARATION:
- Study of non-Euclidean geometry basics
- Practice with optical illusions and impossible objects
- Mindfulness techniques for managing spatial disorientation
- Development of strong mental anchoring practices
- Familiarization with tesseract and hypercube concepts

SAFETY PROTOCOLS:
1. Never travel alone - use the buddy system
2. Maintain physical contact with solid surfaces when possible
3. Use guide ropes in areas where paths seem to loop or intersect
4. Set a maximum exposure time of 4 hours for first visit
5. Take regular breaks in areas with more normal geometry
6. Document your path (though be aware maps may be temporary)
7. Establish regular check-ins with a base camp outside the garden

BEHAVIORAL GUIDELINES:
- Move slowly and deliberately
- Don't attempt to "solve" or predict the garden's movements
- Avoid looking directly at edge areas for extended periods
- Rest immediately if you experience reality vertigo
- Don't chase or attempt to interact with the garden's creatures
- Stay on clearly defined paths, even if they seem impossible
- Resist the urge to test gravitational anomalies

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS:
- Bring a grounding object (something simple and geometrically regular)
- Consider wearing a personal gravity anchor
- Pack emergency spatial stabilizers
- Carry chalk or markers to temporarily mark your path
- Bring a simple geometric puzzle as a cognitive reset tool

DOCUMENTATION:
- Non-digital cameras recommended (quantum effects may interfere with electronics)
- Sketch pad and pencils for manual recording
- Audio recorder for verbal notes
- Sample containers (if permitted by garden authorities)

RECOVERY PLAN:
- Schedule decompression time after visits
- Plan for possible spatial readjustment period
- Arrange for psychological support if needed
- Keep detailed journals of experiences
- Practice regular reality checks during and after visits

CONTRAINDICATIONS:
- Those with severe motion sickness should not visit
- Individuals with spatial processing disorders should consult specialists before visiting
- People prone to existential crises may want to limit exposure
- Those with vestibular disorders should avoid the more complex areas

IMPORTANT: Remember that human perception is limited by our evolution in standard three-dimensional space. Accept that you won't fully comprehend everything you see, and focus on maintaining your psychological and physical well-being while exploring. The garden is not hostile, but it is fundamentally alien to human experience.

Maintain humility and respect for the space - you are a visitor in an environment that operates on principles far beyond ordinary human understanding. When in doubt, retreat to simpler areas and allow yourself time to process the experience.
