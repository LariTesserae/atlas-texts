Here's how to prepare for visiting the Mountain City:

Essential Gear:
- Layered, moisture-wicking clothing (the constant steam means you'll need to manage both warmth and dampness)
- Sturdy, non-slip boots with excellent traction (streets can be slick and steep)
- Waterproof outer layer (lightweight but effective against persistent mist)
- Small dehumidifier bags for electronics and valuable documents
- Altitude sickness medication (consult doctor before travel)
- Portable oxygen supplement for upper levels
- UV-protective eyewear (high altitude means intense sun above the mist)
- Compact umbrella or rain hat (sudden moisture is common)
- Strong backpack with water-resistant coating

Physical Preparation:
- Begin altitude conditioning 2-3 months before visit
- Practice stair climbing and hill walking
- Build leg and core strength for navigating steep terrain
- Work on balance exercises (for narrow bridges and walkways)
- Consider vestibular training if prone to motion sickness

Practical Arrangements:
- Book accommodations by level (moving between levels is energy-intensive)
- Plan your daily activities within similar elevation zones
- Purchase a multi-day pass for mechanical lifts
- Schedule rest days every 2-3 days for altitude adjustment
- Make reservations for public baths in advance (popular among locals)

Cultural Considerations:
- Learn basic water conservation practices (it's a social responsibility)
- Understand level-based time schedules (different elevations operate on different rhythms)
- Bring small gifts if visiting locals (practical items related to moisture management are appreciated)
- Learn vertical navigation references (locals navigate by levels rather than cardinal directions)

Health Precautions:
- Get a medical check-up focused on altitude tolerance
- Bring electrolyte supplements
- Pack anti-fungal cream (constant moisture can cause issues)
- Bring respiratory masks for misty days
- Consider ear pressure equalization plugs for rapid elevation changes

Navigation Tools:
- Download offline 3D maps (standard 2D maps aren't sufficient)
- Bring a barometric altimeter
- Get a local level-guide (similar to a subway map but vertical)
- Consider hiring a local guide for initial orientation

Documentation:
- Weather-proof case for documents
- Multiple copies of important papers (moisture can damage them)
- Local elevation permits if visiting highest levels
- Insurance that covers altitude-related medical issues

Additional Considerations:
- Camera with good low-light capability (for misty conditions)
- Portable battery packs (humidity can affect battery life)
- Small towel for wiping condensation
- Collapsible walking stick for steep sections
- Local communication device (standard phones may struggle with reception)

Time Management:
- Plan for slower travel between levels
- Allow extra time for altitude adjustment
- Schedule indoor activities during peak mist times
- Consider seasonal timing (some levels are more accessible in certain seasons)

Mental Preparation:
- Accept that plans may change with weather conditions
- Be prepared for constant background noise (steam, machinery)
- Develop comfort with heights and exposure
- Practice mindfulness for dealing with spatial disorientation

Remember: The Mountain City rewards patient, prepared visitors. It's not a place for rushing or improvising. Take time to understand its rhythms and respect its unique environmental challenges.
