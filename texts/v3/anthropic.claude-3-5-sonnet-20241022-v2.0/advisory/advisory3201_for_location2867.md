Preparation Guide for Visiting the Living City

Essential Equipment:
- Waterproof everything. Multiple sets of quick-drying, moisture-wicking clothing
- Sealed, waterproof bags for electronics and sensitive items
- Anti-fungal treatments for skin and equipment
- Respirator with fine-particle filters (for spores)
- Goggles with side protection
- High-grip, waterproof boots that can be easily cleaned
- Portable water purification system (the golden water needs processing for human consumption)
- Dehumidifiers for sleeping quarters
- UV-sterilization wand
- Anti-condensation coatings for any optical equipment

Medical Preparations:
- Full spectrum of anti-fungal medications
- Allergy medications
- Immune system boosters
- Skin barrier creams
- Eye drops for moisture regulation
- Recent medical check to ensure no underlying respiratory issues
- Vaccination record check and updates

Physical Training:
- Practice walking to irregular rhythms (to sync with the living streets)
- Balance training (surfaces are constantly moving)
- Cardiovascular conditioning (hot, humid environment)
- Flexibility training (adaptation to organic architecture)

Cultural/Technical Preparation:
- Study of mycological basics
- Learning local bio-harmonic communication methods
- Understanding of organic architecture principles
- Basic navigation in non-grid environments
- Training in reading bioluminescent patterns
- Learning local customs regarding interaction with living structures

Safety Protocols:
- Never touch unknown organisms directly
- Establish regular decontamination routines
- Monitor for allergic reactions
- Keep emergency beacon active
- Maintain contact with local guides
- Learn emergency exit routes (they change, but patterns exist)
- Understand which market goods are safe for humans

Accommodations:
- Book stabilized living quarters (less movement)
- Ensure quarters have dehumidification systems
- Verify air filtration capabilities
- Confirm waste management compatibility
- Check structural stability cycles

Documentation Equipment:
- Humidity-sealed cameras
- Organic-compatible lighting (some organisms react poorly to artificial light)
- Bio-safe recording devices
- Moisture-resistant notebooks
- Non-toxic marking tools

Mental Preparation:
- Acceptance of constant movement in environment
- Comfort with biological processes at macro scale
- Tolerance for constant ambient sounds
- Adaptation to perpetual twilight
- Understanding of fluid architectural concepts

Time Management:
- Learn local biological time-keeping methods
- Understand activity cycles of the city
- Plan around structural change patterns
- Account for slower travel times due to organic transit systems

Emergency Preparations:
- Multiple evacuation routes
- Local guide contacts
- Emergency shelter options
- Medical evacuation plans
- Communication backups
- Biological contamination protocols

This environment requires a fundamental shift in how we think about urban spaces and our relationship with our surroundings. Success depends on treating the city as a living entity that must be collaborated with rather than merely inhabited.
