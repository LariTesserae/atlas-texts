Preparation Guide for Visiting New Chloros Hanging Gardens

Essential Equipment:
- Lightweight, moisture-wicking clothing (the humidity is constant)
- A light jacket for temperature variations between tiers
- Non-slip, sturdy walking shoes (surfaces can be wet from condensation)
- Anti-fog goggles or glasses (necessary in some of the more humid sections)
- Water bottle with built-in purification system (though the gardens purify water, it's best to be self-sufficient)
- Personal humidity meter (to monitor conditions in different sections)
- UV protective clothing/sunscreen (the perpetual golden hour can cause unexpected sun exposure)
- Small towel for wiping condensation
- Camera with anti-glare filter (to handle the eternal sunset light)

Health Preparations:
- Altitude acclimation schedule (start 2 weeks before visit)
- Motion sickness medication (for cable car transport)
- Consultation with doctor about potential allergies to engineered plant species
- Updated vaccinations (research facilities have strict health protocols)
- Inner ear pressure equalization devices for elevation changes

Documentation Required:
- Standard multiverse travel permits
- Research facility visitor clearance (apply 3 months in advance)
- Botanical contamination waiver (to prevent cross-universe plant contamination)
- Health insurance with coverage for engineered biology exposure

Psychological Preparation:
- Meditation practice (the silence can be unsettling for urban visitors)
- Temporal adjustment exercises (the unchanging light can disrupt circadian rhythms)
- Geological anxiety management (the mountain's movements and sounds can be disturbing)

Scheduling Considerations:
- Plan visits to different tiers based on plant activity cycles
- Allow time for decontamination procedures between garden sections
- Schedule rest periods (the combination of altitude and humidity can be taxing)
- Book accommodations in the less geologically active areas

Cultural Notes:
- Observe the local custom of quiet movement and speech
- Respect research protocols and restricted areas
- Learn basic hand signals used by garden staff
- Understand emergency procedures for geological events

What Not to Bring:
- No outside plant material
- No conventional timepieces (they can be psychologically disorienting in the eternal golden hour)
- No scented products (can interfere with plant processes)
- No bright or reflective clothing (can disrupt light-sensitive plants)

Recommended Skills:
- Basic rock climbing
- Cable car safety procedures
- Humidity survival techniques
- Basic botanical contamination protocols
- Emergency descent procedures

Special Considerations:
- Maintain regular sleep schedule despite unchanging light
- Stay hydrated but don't over-hydrate (altitude effects)
- Pay attention to geological warning systems
- Keep electronic devices in humidity-proof cases
- Be prepared for communication delays due to infrastructure spacing

This destination requires careful preparation but offers unique insights into bioengineering and geological processes. The key is to approach it with patience and respect for both the natural and engineered systems at work.
