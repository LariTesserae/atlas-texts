Here's how I'd recommend preparing for a visit to this underwater filtration chamber:

Essential Equipment:
- Basic scuba gear including tank, regulator, and BCD
- Tropical wetsuit (3mm should suffice given the warm water)
- Underwater camera with good low-light capabilities to capture the light play
- Waterproof notepad for observations
- Small LED light for examining filter details
- Dive watch with depth gauge
- Surface marker buoy for safety

Physical Preparation:
- Get comfortable with basic scuba skills
- Practice controlled breathing to minimize air consumption
- Work on maintaining neutral buoyancy to avoid touching the structure
- Build up core strength for staying stable in water currents
- Consider ear equalization exercises as you'll be hanging around one depth

Mental Preparation:
- Study basic water filtration principles to understand what you're seeing
- Practice meditation or similar techniques to cope with the constant noise
- Prepare for potential claustrophobia in the confined space
- Research local marine life to understand the ecosystem context
- Learn about glass architecture and its behavior underwater

Safety Considerations:
- Bring a dive buddy - never examine structures alone
- Plan for multiple short visits rather than one long one
- Check weather and water conditions carefully
- Maintain safe distance from intake/outflow areas
- Have emergency ascent plan ready
- Know local dive emergency resources

Special Notes:
- Bring a good camera housing - the light effects are worth documenting
- Consider visiting at different times of day to see varied light patterns
- Wear gloves to avoid leaving fingerprints on the glass
- Plan shots/observations in advance as the noise will make communication difficult
- Bring something for scale in photographs
- Consider bringing color correction filters for your camera

Cultural/Scientific Context:
- Research local water management practices
- Learn about traditional filtration methods
- Study basic oceanography principles
- Understand local attitudes toward marine construction
- Consider environmental impact assessment methods

Time Management:
- Plan multiple visits if possible
- Schedule around optimal sunlight conditions
- Allow time for eyes to adjust to changing light
- Build in observation time for both technical and aesthetic aspects
- Include surface intervals between dives

Post-Visit Processing:
- Plan how you'll organize photos/notes
- Consider methods for sound recording if possible
- Prepare questions for local experts
- Have system for cataloging observations
- Plan how to share/present findings

Remember that this is both a technical installation and an aesthetic experience - try to appreciate both aspects. The combination of mechanical process and natural elements creates a unique environment worth documenting thoroughly.
