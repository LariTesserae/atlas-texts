Preparation Guide for Visiting the Rust Plains

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Respirator with fine particle filters (the rust dust is likely toxic if inhaled)
- Heavy-duty boots with metal-resistant soles (the rust plain may be corrosive)
- Protective clothing covering all skin:
  * Long-sleeved, tightly-woven synthetics treated with anti-corrosive coating
  * Gloves with similar protection
  * Wide-brimmed hat for radiation protection under the hazy sky
- Goggles/eye protection (against dust and glare)

MEDICAL/SAFETY:
- Up-to-date tetanus vaccination
- Blood iron level check before visiting (the metallic air may affect iron absorption)
- First aid kit including:
  * Chelation tablets (in case of metal toxicity)
  * Anti-inflammatory medication
  * Sterile bandages (any cuts must be promptly cleaned and covered)
- Personal radiation detector
- Emergency beacon/communication device

SCIENTIFIC/DOCUMENTATION:
- Non-magnetic storage devices (magnetic fields may be unpredictable)
- Ceramic or polymer-based tools (metal tools may react with environment)
- Sample collection containers (non-metallic)
- Multi-spectrum camera (for documenting bio-metallic life forms)

NAVIGATION/TIMING:
- GPS unit in radiation-shielded housing
- Compass alternative (traditional magnetic compasses may be unreliable)
- Plan visits during cooler periods (plate movement/groaning minimal)
- Allow extra time for travel (walking on plates requires careful footwork)

SURVIVAL:
- Minimum 3-day water supply (assume local water contaminated with metals)
- Sealed food supplies
- Portable shelter with insulation (temperature fluctuations may be extreme)
- Chemical light sources (electrical equipment may be affected by environment)

RESEARCH RECOMMENDATIONS:
- Study plate movement patterns before visiting
- Map active vs. inactive towers in your area of exploration
- Document known bloom field locations (avoid disturbing these slow-growing formations)
- Bring measuring tools for plate thickness/composition

BEHAVIORAL GUIDELINES:
- Walk only on plate centers (edges may be unstable)
- Maintain distance from active towers (possible radiation/electromagnetic fields)
- No sample collection from bloom fields without proper permits
- Regular suit integrity checks (watch for rust damage to gear)
- Decontamination procedure after each excursion

CULTURAL CONSIDERATIONS:
- If research station is manned, bring tradeable goods (supplies may be scarce)
- Document any encountered artificial structures (archaeological significance)
- Respect any warning markers/restricted areas

This environment requires constant awareness of the interaction between your gear and the corrosive surroundings. Plan for shorter exploration periods with regular gear maintenance breaks. Consider bringing a partner for safety, but maintain distance between explorers to distribute weight load on plates.
