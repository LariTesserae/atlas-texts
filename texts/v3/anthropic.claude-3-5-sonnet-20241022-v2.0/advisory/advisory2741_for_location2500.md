For visiting the Domain of the Root-Shapers, here's how a multiverse traveler should prepare:

Essential Equipment:
- Waterproof clothing and bags (moisture is constant)
- Non-slip boots with good ankle support
- Multiple light sources (headlamp + backup)
- Water filtration system (the ambient water may contain unknown fungal spores)
- Breathable hazmat suit or respiratory protection (fungal spores could be hazardous)
- Basic climbing gear for navigating overgrown passages
- Documentation equipment (low-light cameras, moisture-resistant notebooks)
- Emergency position markers (the layout is complex and changing)

Health Preparations:
- Get a full medical check-up, focusing on respiratory health
- Ensure all vaccinations are current
- Undergo allergy testing for various fungi
- Build up immunity to humid conditions
- Consider taking anti-fungal supplements preventatively

Scientific Equipment:
- Air quality monitor
- Spore detector/counter
- pH testing equipment
- Temperature and humidity sensors
- Sample collection containers (sterile)
- UV light source (for studying bioluminescence patterns)

Navigation:
- Study any existing maps thoroughly
- Bring multiple compasses (magnetic fields may be disturbed)
- Consider bringing guide ropes
- Plan to track and follow moth colonies
- Learn to recognize major fungal species for orientation

Time Management:
- Plan visits around known blooming cycles
- Allow extra time for passage navigation
- Schedule rest periods (humidity is physically taxing)
- Factor in time for eyes to adjust to low light conditions

Safety Protocols:
- Never travel alone
- Maintain constant communication with surface team
- Establish clear evacuation procedures
- Set up decontamination stations at entry/exit points
- Define clear "turn back" conditions

Cultural Considerations:
- While the Root-Shapers are gone, treat the space as a living archive
- Minimize impact on moth colonies and other fauna
- Document but don't disturb apparent pattern arrangements
- Respect the slow rhythms of the environment

Research Preparation:
- Study mycology basics
- Learn about bioluminescent species
- Understand basic cave ecology
- Review any available historical records
- Learn to identify signs of structural instability

Physical Conditioning:
- Build stamina for humid conditions
- Practice moving in confined spaces
- Develop balance for uneven surfaces
- Train in low-light conditions
- Build core strength for careful movement

Mental Preparation:
- Develop comfort with enclosed spaces
- Practice patience (everything moves slowly here)
- Learn to observe subtle patterns
- Prepare for sensory adjustment
- Accept uncertainty and changing conditions

Remember:
- The environment is both fragile and dynamic
- Your presence will impact the ecosystem
- Time moves differently in this space
- Always maintain awareness of exit routes
- Be prepared to abort if conditions change

This is not a space for casual exploration. Success depends on thorough preparation, respect for the environment, and the ability to move carefully through a living system that operates on its own ancient rhythms.
