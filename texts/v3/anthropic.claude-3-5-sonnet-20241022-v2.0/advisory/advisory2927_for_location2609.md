Here's how I'd recommend preparing for a visit to the City of Wells:

Essential Gear:
- High-altitude mountaineering equipment (the thin air demands acclimatization)
- Multiple layers of warm, moisture-wicking clothing
- Sturdy boots with excellent grip for worn stone surfaces
- Headlamp plus multiple backup light sources (batteries perform poorly in cold)
- Emergency thermal blankets and high-calorie rations
- Water purification methods (despite the springs, assume nothing about potability)
- Basic climbing gear for navigating steep sections
- Small rope or cord for guidance in maze-like passages
- Notebook with waterproof pages (regular paper will deteriorate in the humidity)

Physical Preparation:
- High-altitude training several months in advance
- Flexibility training for navigating tight passages
- Core strength building for irregular terrain
- Practice moving in low-light conditions
- Cave navigation experience highly recommended

Health Considerations:
- Full medical check-up focusing on respiratory health
- Altitude sickness medication
- Anti-fungal treatments (the omnipresent fungi may cause reactions)
- Eye drops (the dry, cold air will be irritating)
- Consider wearing a filtration mask for fungal spores

Cultural/Social Preparation:
- Study geological formations and basic speleology
- Learn to recognize different types of cave-dwelling creatures
- Research thermal spring etiquette (crucial for not offending locals)
- Practice non-verbal communication methods
- Learn basic cave acoustics to understand storm-songs
- Study architectural history focusing on organic forms

Practical Tips:
- Move slowly and deliberately to preserve energy
- Touch walls minimally - some surfaces may be culturally significant
- Allow eyes to adjust naturally to different light levels
- Listen carefully - sound carries important information
- Mark your path (removably) - getting lost is a serious risk
- Respect the natural pace of the city - rushing is both dangerous and culturally inappropriate

Special Considerations:
- Bring offerings for the springs (research appropriate types)
- Pack equipment that won't degrade in constant humidity
- Consider bringing recording equipment for storm-songs
- Prepare for long periods of darkness during storms
- Plan extended stays - the city operates on geological time

Mental Preparation:
- Practice meditation or mindfulness to adjust to the city's tempo
- Develop comfort with enclosed spaces
- Work on night vision adaptation
- Learn to navigate by touch and sound
- Build tolerance for constant background noise

Documentation:
- Waterproof camera with low-light capabilities
- Audio recording equipment for storm-songs
- Sketching materials for documenting carved reliefs
- Small measuring tools for architectural documentation
- Journal for recording observations

Remember:
- The city operates on its own timescale - adjust your expectations
- What looks like decay may be intentional aging
- Respect the complex social hierarchies around springs
- Be prepared for significant sensory adjustment
- Consider yourself a temporary part of an ancient living system

This is a place that demands patience and respect. Success here isn't measured in distance covered or sights seen, but in how well you adapt to its rhythms and patterns. The city has existed for millennia - your visit should honor that permanence rather than try to rush through it.
