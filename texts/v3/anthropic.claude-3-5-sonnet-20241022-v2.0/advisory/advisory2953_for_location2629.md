Preparation Guide for Visiting the Garden of Mechanisms

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Heat-resistant, moisture-wicking clothing (multiple layers recommended)
- Sturdy boots with non-conductive soles
- Protective goggles with anti-fog coating
- Breathable face mask with oil mist filtration
- Water-resistant pack for supplies
- Multiple light sources (mechanical preferred over electronic)
- Analog compass (electronic navigation systems may be unreliable)
- Mechanical watch (to track subjective time)
- Second watch synchronized to external time (kept sealed until exit)
- Chalk or markers for navigation
- Thick gloves resistant to both oil and sharp edges
- Basic mechanical tools (wrenches, pliers)
- Clean cloths for wiping condensation from equipment
- Container for collecting drinking water (from designated safe basins only)
- Emergency rations for 3x your planned stay duration
- Brass or copper tokens (some areas respond to metallic offerings)

PHYSICAL PREPARATION:
- Acclimatize to high humidity environments beforehand
- Practice moving carefully around delicate machinery
- Build heat tolerance through sauna training
- Learn basic mechanical principles and plant identification
- Develop strong spatial awareness
- Practice navigation by sound
- Work on balance and careful footwork

MENTAL PREPARATION:
- Study mechanical-biological hybrid systems
- Meditate to prepare for temporal distortions
- Practice maintaining focus in complex sensory environments
- Learn to distinguish safe vs. unsafe mechanical sounds
- Research basic clockwork principles
- Accept that not everything will make logical sense
- Prepare for possible mild hallucinations from oil mist exposure

SAFETY PROTOCOLS:
- Never stay longer than 4 hours subjective time on first visit
- Always maintain physical contact with your group
- Avoid touching any moving parts
- Don't attempt to collect specimens without proper tools
- Keep track of entrance location
- Document any temporal inconsistencies
- Watch for signs of mechanical integration with your gear
- If disoriented, follow water flow to major channels
- Maintain distance from larger autonomous entities
- Don't enter chambers that feel temporally "wrong"

DOCUMENTATION:
- Bring mechanical sketching tools (electronics may malfunction)
- Use waterproof notebooks
- Document temporal shifts between chambers
- Record unusual sounds or patterns
- Note any interactions between your gear and the environment

ETIQUETTE:
- Move slowly and deliberately
- Avoid disrupting mechanical-organic processes
- Respect the garden's self-maintaining nature
- Don't attempt repairs unless specifically trained
- Maintain quiet in echo-sensitive chambers
- If offered mechanical "gifts," accept graciously
- Follow established pathways where visible

RECOVERY:
- Plan for extended rest after visit
- Watch for residual temporal effects
- Clean all equipment thoroughly
- Document any unusual dreams or memories
- Check for subtle changes in your mechanical devices
- Consider quarantine for items exposed to garden systems

WARNING SIGNS TO EXIT IMMEDIATELY:
- Loss of temporal awareness
- Mechanical sounds synchronizing with your heartbeat
- Attraction to or merger with garden components
- Your gear beginning to sprout or change
- Difficulty distinguishing organic from mechanical matter
- Strong urge to "join" garden processes
- Unusual resonance with bell tones
- Loss of boundary between self and environment

Remember: The Garden of Mechanisms operates on its own logic. Your role is to observe and survive, not to understand everything. Maintain your separation from the system while respecting its complexity. When in doubt, err on the side of caution and exit to regroup.
