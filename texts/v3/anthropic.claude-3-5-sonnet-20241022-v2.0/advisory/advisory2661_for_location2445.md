Preparation Guide for Visiting the Glass Mountains

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Specialized footwear: Waterproof boots with excellent grip, preferably with crystalline-resistant soles. The permanent ankle-deep water and obsidian surface require both water protection and sure footing.
- Light-filtering goggles with adjustable settings: Critical for managing the three-sun brightness and constant reflections. Should include anti-glare coating and prismatic filters.
- Full-spectrum UV protection clothing: The intense light from multiple sources demands comprehensive skin coverage.
- Non-metallic equipment: The complex crystalline resonances may interact unpredictably with metal objects.
- Sound-dampening earplugs with adjustable filtering: To manage the constant crystalline frequencies while still allowing awareness of surroundings.

ENVIRONMENTAL PREPARATIONS:
- Equilibrium training: Practice moving in uniform shallow water while maintaining balance on slick surfaces.
- Vision adaptation exercises: Spend time training your eyes to function in environments with minimal shadows and multiple light sources.
- Thermal regulation preparation: The blood-warm water will affect body temperature regulation; build heat tolerance.

SAFETY PROTOCOLS:
- Maintain minimum 500-meter distance from active growth zones where new spires emerge.
- Never attempt to climb the crystal formations - they're likely sharper than they appear and may have unexpected resonant properties.
- Avoid disrupting Swimmer patterns - their movements may be crucial to local ecosystem stability.
- Keep constant awareness of Crystal Weaver activity above; their dissolving structures could create falling silica debris.

RESEARCH EQUIPMENT:
- Non-contact temperature sensors
- Crystalline frequency analyzers (acoustic and sub-acoustic)
- High-speed cameras with anti-glare capabilities
- Water composition testing kit (non-reactive materials only)
- Holographic mapping equipment for documenting Swimmer patterns

PSYCHOLOGICAL PREPARATION:
- Meditation training to manage sensory overload
- Depth perception exercises for functioning in shadowless environments
- Mental conditioning for extended exposure to mathematical patterns
- Awareness training for maintaining orientation in visually complex environments

EMERGENCY PROVISIONS:
- Polarized emergency shelter (for visual relief)
- Sound isolation chamber (in case of harmful resonance exposure)
- Water purification systems (though the water appears clean, its properties are unknown)
- First aid kit specifically prepared for potential crystalline-related injuries

TIMING CONSIDERATIONS:
- Plan visits around periods of lowest solar activity (if such patterns exist)
- Allow for extended acclimatization period
- Limit initial exposure to 4-hour intervals

CULTURAL/SCIENTIFIC PROTOCOLS:
- Document any changes in Swimmer patterns
- Record new spire formations (from safe distance)
- Map any variations in the ruins' submersion levels
- Avoid disturbing any artifacts in the abandoned structures
- Maintain detailed logs of any perceivable patterns in Crystal Weaver activity

HEALTH MONITORING:
- Regular inner ear checks (due to constant crystalline frequencies)
- Eye strain monitoring
- Body temperature regulation
- Psychological state assessment

RESEARCH SUGGESTIONS:
- Focus on non-invasive observation methods
- Prioritize pattern documentation over specimen collection
- Maintain multiple backup recording systems
- Establish safe observation points for long-term monitoring

LOCAL INTERACTION GUIDELINES:
- Minimize disruption of water surface
- Avoid touching crystal formations
- Maintain distance from Swimmer congregations
- Do not attempt to interact with Crystal Weavers
- Document but do not disturb ruins or artifacts

Remember: This environment operates on principles that may not align with standard Earth physics. Always prioritize cautious observation over direct interaction, and be prepared to evacuate if any unexpected phenomena occur.
