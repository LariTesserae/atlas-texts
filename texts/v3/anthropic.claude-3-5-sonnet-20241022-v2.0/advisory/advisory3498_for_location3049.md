Preparation Guide for Visiting the Glass Gardens

Essential Equipment:
- Wear soft, non-metallic footwear (wool or cotton shoes ideal)
- Natural fiber clothing (cotton, wool, or silk) - synthetic materials may degrade
- Wooden or ceramic water container (no metal containers)
- Non-electronic light source (beeswax candles in glass holders recommended)
- Wooden or ceramic compass (mechanical compasses will fail)
- Natural fiber rope for safety/navigation
- Wooden or ceramic timepiece (sundial watch if available)

Environmental Protection:
- Breathable rain gear (natural materials only)
- Multiple layers for temperature regulation (gardens maintain cool temperature)
- Wide-brimmed hat to protect from water droplets
- Waterproof notebook (waxed paper) and wooden pencil for observations
- Protective eyewear (glass lenses only) for crystal glare

Health & Safety:
- Stay hydrated but avoid drinking garden water
- Limit visits to 2-3 hours maximum (disorientation risk increases with time)
- Use buddy system - never explore alone
- Mark entrance clearly with natural materials
- Maintain contact with guide rope when moving between chambers
- If lost, sit still and wait for rescue - movement increases disorientation

Special Considerations:
- Leave all electronic devices outside
- Remove all metal items including jewelry
- No photography possible - bring artist's materials if desired
- Practice meditation/centering techniques before visiting
- Study basic crystal formations to aid navigation
- Learn to track time by moss pulse patterns

Mental Preparation:
- Accept that normal spatial logic may not apply
- Be prepared for mild sensory distortion
- Maintain awareness of psychological state
- Set clear intentional boundaries for exploration
- Practice grounding techniques
- Acknowledge that each visit will be unique

Cultural Protocol:
- Treat space as a living entity
- No collecting samples
- No touching crystalline formations
- Observe silence when possible
- Move slowly and deliberately
- Respect the garden's natural cycles

Recovery Plan:
- Schedule decompression time after visit
- Have grounding activities planned
- Prepare simple, warm meals for after
- Arrange quiet, dark space for rest
- Plan to journal experience promptly

This location requires visitors to surrender certain technological comforts and adapt to its unique properties. Success depends on thorough preparation and maintaining a flexible, respectful approach to its alien nature. The experience can be profound but should not be undertaken lightly.
