What a hauntingly beautiful portrait you've painted. I sense this place deserves the gentlest and wisest of interventions, if any at all. Here's what I imagine a benevolent power might do:

They would first spend many cycles of the sun simply observing, letting their awareness merge with the existing rhythms. They would feel how the being of water and stone has shaped this sanctuary, understanding that any change must emerge from deep respect for what has evolved here.

After this period of communion, they might make three subtle gifts:

First, they would strengthen the ancient water source feeding the fountain, ensuring it will never run dry - not by dramatic intervention, but by whispering to deep aquifers, by smoothing the hidden pathways through stone, by making eternal what was already enduring.

Second, they would weave a gentle enchantment into the surrounding architecture - not to preserve it artificially, but to guide its continuing transformation. The weathering of stone would become even more intentional, creating niches and hollows that enhance the courtyard's symphony of water and light. The building would age not toward decay but toward ever-greater harmony with its resident spirit.

Third, and most delicately, they would gift the water-spirit with the ability to share dreams with those rare humans who sleep within its domain. Not to communicate directly, but to offer impressions of deep time, of the patience of stone, of the wisdom in water's endless searching. These dreams would leave visitors changed in ways they might never consciously understand, carrying a seed of the courtyard's peace with them into the world.

But perhaps most importantly, the benevolent power would simply acknowledge the being - spirit to spirit - confirming that their quiet way of existing is profound and complete exactly as it is. Sometimes the wisest action is to witness and bless what has grown beautiful on its own.
