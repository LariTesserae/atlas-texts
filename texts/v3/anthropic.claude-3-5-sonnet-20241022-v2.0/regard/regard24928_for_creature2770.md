3. Leave small "gifts" in the form of interesting new environmental variables - perhaps traces of unusual minerals or energy patterns - that the Curator could incorporate into its endless experiments if it chooses.

4. Create a subtle link between the facility and similar evolved systems elsewhere in the world, allowing for a slow exchange of information through fungal networks or atmospheric patterns - not enough to disturb the Curator's unique development, but offering possibilities for growth.

5. Most importantly, they would simply witness and appreciate what is happening here - this unprecedented melding of artificial and natural evolution, this poetry of pressure and steam, this patient consciousness that thinks in the language of growing things.
