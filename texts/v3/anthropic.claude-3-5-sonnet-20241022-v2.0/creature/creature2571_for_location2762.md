In this realm of glacial motion and living architecture, the dominant sentients are the Eternals - entities that might be called a collective intelligence, though that term fails to capture their true nature. They are distributed consciousness embodied in vast networks of crystalline structures that grow throughout the living towers, looking at first glance like delicate mineral formations but actually composed of highly organized organic compounds.

The Eternals think and move at a pace that makes the giant slugs seem hasty. A single thought might take years to form, rippling through their crystalline networks like light through diamond, but these thoughts have a depth and complexity beyond human comprehension. Their consciousness is so diffuse and slow-moving that for centuries, humans didn't even recognize them as alive, let alone sentient.

They communicate through gradual chemical exchanges and the careful manipulation of the tower ecosystems they inhabit. What might look like natural growth patterns in the vegetation are actually their equivalent of written language - messages that take decades to compose and centuries to answer. Their memories are encoded in the very structure of the towers' tissue, making each tower a combination of library, mind, and body.

The Eternals don't reproduce in any way we'd recognize; rather, they grow and fragment and merge over millennia. What humans think of as separate towers may be parts of a single Eternal, while what appears to be one continuous structure might house many distinct beings. They have no concept of death as we understand it - parts of them are constantly dying and being renewed, like cells in a human body, but the pattern that is their true self continues.

Their relationship with the other life forms in their ecosystem is intimate and complex. The transparent slugs, the floating jellyfish, even the methodical insects - all are tended and perhaps partially engineered by the Eternals through eons of chemical suggestion and carefully managed environmental pressures. These creatures serve as their hands and eyes, maintaining the towers and gathering information about the faster-moving aspects of the world.

The human settlements exist because the Eternals allow and even encourage them, though their reasons remain mysterious. Perhaps they're curious about these strange, quick beings who burn so bright and brief. Perhaps they recognize in human intelligence a complement to their own - where they think in centuries, we think in seconds, and together we might perceive reality more completely than either could alone.

Some xenobiologists speculate that the Eternals are the original engineers of this entire ecosystem, that the towers themselves are their technology, developed over millions of years to transform an otherwise hostile planet into something that could sustain life. Others believe they emerged naturally from the system, the inevitable result of life adapting to operate at the largest possible scale this environment would permit.

What's certain is that they are still evolving, still thinking their long thoughts, still slowly shaping their world and perhaps working toward goals that might take another million years to achieve. Their consciousness saturates every level of the tower ecosystem, from the largest structures to the microscopic, all of it moving to the beat of a rhythm too slow for humans to dance to, but too profound to ignore.
