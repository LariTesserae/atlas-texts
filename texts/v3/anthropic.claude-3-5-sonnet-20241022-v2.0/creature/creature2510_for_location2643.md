The Tenders inhabit this living city, beings whose very essence bridges the gap between moth and bird, though they are truly neither. Their bodies are covered in dense, velvety down that absorbs both sound and moisture, allowing them to move in perfect silence through their hushed realm. Their wings - four to each being - have evolved beyond flight into sensory organs and manipulation tools, their surfaces richly innervated and capable of detecting the subtle biochemical signals that pulse through their fungal home.

They stand as tall as humans but move with an otherworldly lightness, their three-jointed legs allowing them to navigate vertical surfaces with ease. Their heads bear elaborate sensory appendages - not quite antennae, not quite feathers - that wave gently in the warm air currents, reading chemical signatures and electromagnetic patterns. Their eyes are large but simple, adapted to the eternal twilight, seeing more in patterns of bioluminescence than in what we would call light.

The Tenders live in small family groups, though "family" means something different here - they form fluid bonds based on resonant biochemistries and complementary tending styles. Their young emerge from carefully tended spore-pods, already knowing some of what they need through inherited memories carried in the fungal networks that sustained their gestation.

Their language is a symphony of subtle movements, pheromone releases, and low vibrations conducted through the living architecture of their home. They can communicate across great distances by tapping into the mycelial networks that permeate their city, sending chemical signals that translate complex concepts and emotions. Their art forms are temporal - elaborate patterns of bioluminescence encouraged through careful cultivation, harmonics of dripping water shaped by generations of architectural evolution, sculptures of living tissue that grow and change over decades.

The Tenders take their name from their primary purpose - they see themselves not as rulers or owners of their city, but as caretakers in an endless dance of mutual cultivation. They understand implicitly that they are part of the organism that is their home, neither controlling nor controlled, but engaged in a perpetual collaboration of growth and adaptation.

They mark time not by days or years, but by growth cycles of the great fungal trunks, by the rhythms of spore releases, by the slow pulse of water through the living cities' veins. Their histories are recorded in the very architecture they inhabit - patterns of growth and pruning that carry meaning legible to those who know how to read the language of shaped life.

Their society has no hierarchy as we would understand it, but rather a complex web of responsibilities and relationships, each Tender attuned to particular aspects of their home's needs. Some focus on encouraging new growth, others on maintaining crucial pathways, still others on harmonizing the biochemical signals that keep the city's various systems in balance.

In their deepest essence, the Tenders are embodiments of patient cultivation - beings who understand that the greatest works are grown, not built, and that wisdom lies in learning to dance with the rhythms of living things rather than imposing order upon them.
