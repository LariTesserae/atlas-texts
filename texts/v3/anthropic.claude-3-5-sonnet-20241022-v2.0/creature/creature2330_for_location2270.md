In the Dripping Market dwell the Moistfolk - beings who have adapted so completely to their watery domain that it's difficult to say where their bodies end and the cave's perpetual dew begins. Their skin has the translucent quality of cave fish, with a faint phosphorescence that pulses in rhythm with the dripping water. Their eyes are large and pale, more attuned to sensing ripples and changes in pressure than to conventional light.

The most established merchants among them have developed symbiotic relationships with the limestone itself. Their stalls aren't so much built as grown - living extensions of calcium and mineral that emerge from the walls like elaborate coral formations. These merchant-forms remain largely stationary, their consciousness diffused through their mineral scaffolding. They communicate through subtle variations in the percussive patterns of water drops falling through carefully positioned chambers in their structures.

Younger Moistfolk are more mobile, though they never stray far from water sources. They glide through the market on feet that seem to barely touch the ground, leaving faint trails of moisture that evaporate within moments. Their clothing, when they wear any, appears to be woven from the same mysterious fiber as their wide-brimmed hats - a living material that responds to water pressure and can reshape itself to shed or collect moisture as needed.

The hereditary custodians of the rain-gardens are perhaps the most altered. Generations of tending the living rock have led them to develop a form of mineral symbiosis - their fingers can merge with the limestone, allowing them to sense and direct the flow of water through the stone with incredible precision. Their skin has taken on a crystalline quality, and in certain lights they appear to be partly constructed of the same calcite formations they tend.

Not all inhabitants are so dramatically transformed. There are also the Fisher-Echoes - ethereal beings that exist primarily as sound, using the cave's acoustic properties to maintain their form. They harvest the blind fish that emerge from the channels, though whether they eat them or simply collect their movements is unclear. Their voices are what create those oddly-traveling whispers, and they seem to use the market's complex acoustics to carry on conversations across multiple chambers simultaneously.

Deep in the mirror pool chamber dwells the Market's most enigmatic resident - a consciousness that manifests only in reflections. It appears differently to each viewer, but always as a distorted version of themselves, as if seen through layers of flowing water. The merchants say it is the Market's memory given form, containing echoes of every transaction and conversation that has ever occurred in these chambers.

All these beings maintain a complex ecological balance, each playing their role in the Market's endless water-music. They share a collective awareness of the patterns in the dripping water, reading them like a constantly updating ledger of the Market's health and history. Even their conflicts and competitions play out through subtle adjustments to these rhythms, creating elaborate temporal harmonies that can take days or weeks to resolve.

New visitors often report a sensation of being gently absorbed into this watery consciousness - not threateningly, but with the gradual persistence of water wearing away stone. Many who stay too long find themselves beginning to transform, their solid forms softening at the edges, their voices beginning to harmonize with the endless drip of water on stone.
