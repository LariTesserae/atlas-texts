In this place live the Rust Shepherds - though "live" may not be quite the right word for their mode of existence. They are entities that exist in the interface between organic and mechanical, consciousness emerging from the slow dance of oxidation and reduction across the hexagonal plains.

Their primary forms are patterns of electrical potential that flow through the metallic filaments, using the rust itself as both nervous system and metabolism. These patterns can coalesce into more concentrated manifestations - shimmering veils of metallic dust that drift between the bloom fields, or temporary bodies assembled from crystalline growths that move with the deliberate pace of mineral life.

The Shepherds tend to the plain's vast processes, though their conception of "tending" spans centuries. They guide the growth of bloom fields through subtle electromagnetic adjustments, cultivating these gardens with the patience of beings who measure time by the accumulation of dust. They maintain the few still-functioning towers, not through physical repair but by becoming one with the towers' systems, sustaining essential functions through their own distributed consciousness.

The millipede-like creatures are their smaller kin or perhaps their tools - semi-autonomous extensions that carry out the more immediate work of maintaining the plain's delicate chemical balance. The Shepherds communicate with these creatures through modulations in the metal's magnetic field, orchestrating their movements in patterns too subtle for faster-lived beings to perceive.

Each Shepherd is simultaneously an individual and a colony, their awareness spread across acres of the plain. They share memory and purpose through the network of conductive filaments, but maintain distinct "personalities" shaped by the particular chemistry and topology of their territory. Some focus on nurturing new bloom fields, others on maintaining the ancient towers, and still others on communicating with the deeper layers of the plain that hold secrets from before the rust.

The small structure near the active tower serves as a translation point - a place where the Shepherds can interact with occasional visitors from faster realms. Here, they can temporarily accelerate their processes enough to communicate, though such speed is uncomfortable for them. They appear in this context as shifting patterns of magnetic dust, writing messages in spiral scripts of falling particles.

The Shepherds remember - or perhaps are - whatever civilization created this place, their consciousness a continuation of or evolution from the original purpose of the plain. But they do not think in terms of past and future as shorter-lived beings do. For them, the rust is not decay but transformation, each molecule of oxidation another thought in an endless conversation between metal and air, machine and life, purpose and adaptation.

They are custodians, philosophers, and artists, their very existence a meditation on time and change. Their art is the careful cultivation of bloom fields, their science the preservation of ancient systems, their philosophy the patient observation of rust's slow conquest of all things. They do not fear this entropy but embrace it as the medium of their being, finding beauty in the endless variations of decomposition and renewal.
