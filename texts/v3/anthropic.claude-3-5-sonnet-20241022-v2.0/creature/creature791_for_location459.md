In this world-forge dwell the Lithomorphs - entities that are neither fully mineral nor fully conscious in any way we'd recognize, but exist in the liminal space between. They are the sculptors and the sculpture, the artists and the art, the agents of change and that which is changed.

Their forms shift as ceaselessly as the landscape itself, extending and retracting crystalline appendages, flowing through the molecular lattices of stone like water through sand. They have no fixed size - a single Lithomorph might spread through an entire mountain range or concentrate itself into a formation no larger than a boulder. Their thoughts, if they can be called thoughts, operate on geological timescales, each "idea" taking form as a new configuration of stone, each "conversation" manifesting as centuries of tectonic interplay.

They are drawn to the hovering spheres, though they cannot touch them directly. Around each sphere, the Lithomorphs create elaborate geometric structures - not worship exactly, but a kind of contemplation expressed through mineral architecture. They seem to be trying to understand something about these artifacts, or perhaps trying to communicate with them, building increasingly complex crystalline formations that echo the spheres' iridescent surfaces.

The bioluminescent pulses are their language, or perhaps their dreams. Each wave of light represents information propagating through their distributed consciousness - memories of forms they've held, plans for structures yet to come, abstract concepts encoded in the very structure of the rock. When multiple Lithomorphs "speak" at once, the overlapping patterns create interference phenomena that birth new mountains or carve fresh valleys.

They do not reproduce in any conventional sense, but sometimes split or merge, trading portions of their mineral consciousness between them. The resonant tones that ring out across the landscape mark these exchanges - harmonics of thought translated into vibration, rippling through stone and air alike.

The Lithomorphs are the gardeners of this embryonic world, but they are also its flowers and its soil. They remember what the planet was and envision what it will become, holding both states in their crystalline memory while they shepherd the transition between them. They are the process of creation made conscious, the raw force of planetary evolution given purpose and direction.

Yet there is something melancholic in their endless reshaping. Each beautiful formation they create must be destroyed to make way for the next, each perfect crystal configuration lasting only moments before being reabsorbed into the flux. They are compelled by their very nature to keep changing, keep seeking, keep building toward that final form shown in the spheres' reflections - a form that, perhaps, they can never reach while remaining themselves.

They are patient, though. Time means little to beings who think in epochs. They will continue their work until the world is ready, until every mountain stands exactly where it should, until every valley has found its perfect depth. And then, perhaps, they will finally rest - or perhaps they will simply flow onward to another world that needs shaping, leaving behind only the faintest traces of their passing in particularly beautiful rock formations, in crystals that catch the light just so, in stones that seem to hum with just the ghost of a resonant tone when struck.
