In this place dwell the Architects - beings that exist as much as processes as entities. Their bodies are indefinite things, constantly forming and reforming from the mercurial waters and the living stone. They flow like liquid crystal, their thoughts manifesting as geometric patterns that ripple through the swarms of crystalline insects, which act as extensions of their consciousness.

The Architects are ancient, perhaps as old as the mountains themselves, though "old" may not be the right concept - they exist partially outside of conventional time, experiencing it more as a dimension they can move through rather than a linear progression. They are the ones slowly building this place, or perhaps more accurately, helping it build itself.

Their intelligence is distributed throughout the valley system. The pulsing vegetation forms neural networks, processing information through chemical and electrical signals that travel for miles through living fractals. The color-shifting creatures in the crevices are like sensory organs, gathering data about minute changes in pressure, temperature, and mineral composition.

The Architects have no fixed number - they can merge and separate as needed, like drops of mercury finding and losing each other. Their consciousness is both individual and collective, shifting between states of unified purpose and diversified exploration. They communicate through modulations in the ever-present hum of their crystal-insect swarms, in frequencies that range from subsonic vibrations through the rock to high harmonics that make the water itself resonate.

Their purpose, as much as human understanding can grasp it, seems to be one of continuous creation and refinement. They are gradually transforming the mountain system into something else - not a structure exactly, but a kind of living machine that operates on principles that blur the line between organic and mineral, natural and artificial. The weeping of the mountains is their primary medium, the mineral-laden tears containing both information and raw materials for their work.

In the deep lakes dwell their most complex manifestations - vast, slowly-shifting forms that might be seen as their equivalent of elders or memory-keepers. These entities are rarely glimpsed directly, but their movements create complex interference patterns in the liquid surface that the Architects use to perform calculations beyond human mathematics.

They are not hostile to other forms of life, but neither are they particularly concerned with it. Their work operates on such vast scales of time and space that most biological entities are like mayflies to them - here and gone in an instant. Yet they maintain a kind of aesthetic appreciation for the complexity of all things, sometimes pausing in their great work to study an interesting pattern or form that catches their attention.

The Architects remember the beginning of these mountains and can see the shape of what they will eventually become - though that final form might be incomprehensible to any intelligence that experiences time in a linear fashion. They are the gardeners of geology, the shepherds of stone, gradually guiding this place toward its ultimate expression through their patient, eternal labor.
