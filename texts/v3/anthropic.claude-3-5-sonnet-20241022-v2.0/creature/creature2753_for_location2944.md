Here lives the Tending Choir - not a single species but a complex symbiosis of creatures that have evolved to maintain and expand the fungal city. They are the caretakers, the gardeners, the living tools of the colony's growth, though "tool" implies too much passivity. They are more like specialized organs that have developed the ability to move independently.

Most numerous are the Glaze-slugs, translucent creatures whose metabolisms are perfectly tuned to the fungal chemistry. They slide along the mycelial pathways leaving behind trails of protective mucus that helps regulate moisture and prevents bacterial infection. Their bodies are studded with chemosensitive nodules that can detect imbalances in the fungal tissue. When they find damaged or diseased areas, they secrete precisely calibrated enzymes that help the colony heal or excise the compromised sections.

The Spore-moths are more delicate, their wings having evolved away into fine sensory filaments that detect air currents and chemical gradients. They navigate the city's airways with uncanny precision, following invisible chemical trails to harvest mature spores from the reproductive chambers. Their larvae develop inside specialized fungal pouches, emerging as adults that are perfectly adapted to their role as pollinator-analogues for the colony.

Deeper in the structure live the Shapers - blind, segmented creatures that might once have been millipedes or worms. They carve new passages through the fungal mass, their movements guided by the colony's chemical signals. But they don't just excavate - their digestive systems process and transform the fungal tissue they consume, and their excretions contain compounds that stimulate new growth patterns. They are living architects, unconsciously sculpting the city's expansion.

Most mysterious are the Resonators - creatures that look like delicate crystal formations but are very much alive. They anchor themselves at key points throughout the colony, their bodies vibrating in response to the fungal city's metabolic rhythms. These vibrations seem to help coordinate growth and resource distribution across the vast structure, like a distributed nervous system. The Resonators pulse with subtle light patterns, suggesting some form of communication, though its nature remains unclear.

These beings exist in such perfect harmony with the fungal colony that it's impossible to say where their influence ends and the fungal architecture's natural growth begins. They may have begun as independent organisms that adapted to the fungal environment, or they might have been deliberately evolved by the colony itself. Their consciousness, if they possess it, operates on a different wavelength than ours - slower, more diffuse, more chemically-mediated.

Together, they form something like an immune system, a maintenance crew, and a planning committee all in one - organic robots programmed by evolution to serve the colony's needs while having their own needs met in return. They are the living machinery that keeps the fungal city alive and growing, each species precisely adapted to its niche in this strange ecosystem.
