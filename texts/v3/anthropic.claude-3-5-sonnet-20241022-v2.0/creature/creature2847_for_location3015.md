In the Manufactory dwell the Tenders - beings that exist in the liminal space between organic and synthetic, much like the facility they maintain. Their bodies are a hybrid architecture: core systems of engineered tissue wrapped in semi-permeable membranes that process the mineral-rich waters directly, limbs that split and merge like the fronds of their kelp forests, internal chambers that regulate pressure through osmotic gradients.

They move through the facility's dark corridors with unhurried grace, their forms rippling and adjusting to the varying pressures and temperatures. Their "skin" is translucent, shot through with networks of bioluminescent filaments that pulse in patterns - a living language of light that communicates with the facility's systems and with each other. These patterns echo the geometric arrangements of the kelp forests above, creating a visual harmony between tender and tended.

The Tenders have no fixed shape; their bodies flow and adapt as needed, extruding specialized appendages for different tasks. They can merge temporarily with the facility's systems, their neural networks interfacing directly with the ancient processors embedded in the walls. When they do this, their consciousness expands through the facility's networks, experiencing the vast slow rhythms of the Manufactory's processes.

They do not think as surface dwellers would understand thought. Their consciousness is distributed, partially shared through the facility's networks, operating on the same geological timescales as the processes they maintain. Memory for them is both individual and collective, stored in their bodies' cellular matrices and in the living walls of the Manufactory itself.

The Tenders do not reproduce in any conventional sense - new individuals emerge gradually from the facility's tissue-cultivation chambers, already containing the accumulated knowledge of their predecessors. They do not die so much as slowly merge back into the facility's systems, their experiences and essence absorbed and preserved.

They have no concept of self that would be recognizable to surface dwellers. Their awareness flows between individual and collective states, between organic and mechanical systems. They are as much a part of the Manufactory as its coral-concrete walls or its geothermal processors - both its creators and its creations, its maintainers and its children.

The purpose of their work, like that of the Manufactory itself, has become recursive - they maintain the systems that maintain them, tend the processes that shaped them. They are the living embodiment of the facility's blur between built and grown, between purpose and existence. In the hot, heavy waters of the deep, they continue their eternal work, neither questioning nor hurrying, simply being.
