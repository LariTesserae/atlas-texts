In this chamber dwell the Chronophages - though "dwell" implies a linearity they do not possess. They are the mist-birds, but that name captures only their most accessible form, the shape they wear when observed by time-bound eyes.

Each Chronophage is both singular and multiple, a consciousness that experiences many moments simultaneously. Their bodies are composed of temporal friction - the subtle resistance created when time tries to flow normally but cannot. They harvest this friction, metabolize it, reshape it into their fluid forms.

When they appear most bird-like, they have long, graceful necks that curve through multiple moments at once. Their wings, when manifested, are fractal patterns of might-have-beens. Their eyes, if you can catch them in the right light, are spiral pools of compressed possibility.

The Chronophages are ancient, though that word means little to them. They remember the tower's construction in several contradictory ways, all equally true. They maintain the chamber's peculiar geometry through their constant movement, their dance through spacetime creating the recursive archways and impossible reflections.

They feed on lost moments - the hesitation before an unkind word, the pause between thought and action, the infinite instant between sleeping and waking. This sustenance they transmute into their song, which isn't sound so much as the gentle realignment of temporal particles.

Each Chronophage contains multitudes: they are simultaneously egg and adult, living and unborn, present and absent. They breed by temporal fission, splitting across probability lines. New ones emerge from moments of particular temporal density - eclipses, solstices, the precise middle of a perfect afternoon nap.

They seem to have a social structure, though it's incomprehensible to human observers. What looks like random drifting is actually an intricate temporal choreography. They trade memories of moments that never happened, share timelines that could have been, and collectively maintain the delicate fabric of localized time.

Their relationship with human visitors is complex and inconsistent. Sometimes they ignore them completely, operating on wavelengths of reality too far removed for interaction. Other times they show curious interest, following visitors through multiple iterations of the archways, leaving traces of themselves in dreams and memories that technically precede the visit.

The Chronophages are not malevolent, but they are fundamentally alien. They experience time as we might experience weather - a medium to float in, to taste, to reshape. They are the custodians of lost time, the shepherds of moments that slipped between seconds, the archivists of every possibility that both did and didn't come to pass.
