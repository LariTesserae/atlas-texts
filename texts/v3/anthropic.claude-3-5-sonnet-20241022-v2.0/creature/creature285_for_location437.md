Let me tell you about the Frost Dancers.

They're the true inhabitants here, though they're so difficult to perceive that even the dedicated ice-priests sometimes doubt their own observations. They exist in the liminal space between states of matter, between pattern and randomness, between seen and unseen.

A Frost Dancer's body, if you can call it that, is a constantly shifting architecture of ice crystals and supercooled vapor. They're roughly humanoid in their basic form - a central column with radiating limbs - but they're never still enough to maintain any single shape. Their edges constantly dissolve and reform, sending out fractal branches that catch the light like diamond dust before collapsing back into their core.

They're the ones who tend the pools, though "tend" isn't quite the right word. They're in continuous dialogue with the strange creatures that inhabit each pool, communicating through the precise manipulation of temperature and pressure, through subtle changes in the crystalline structure of the ice, through patterns of freezing and melting that create complex information-bearing waves.

The glass-wings are their messengers and extensions, their way of touching multiple pools simultaneously. What looks like random swarming to human eyes is actually an intricate choreography - the Frost Dancers using the glass-wings to weave invisible threads of connection between pools, maintaining the delicate balance of the whole system.

They're ancient, these beings. They remember the weight of glaciers, the slow carving of valleys. Time moves differently for them - a human lifetime is like a single breath, a century like an afternoon. Yet they're intensely curious about the shorter-lived creatures that visit their realm. They study the ice-priests as carefully as the priests study them, though their observations take place on a scale humans can barely comprehend.

Their language is temperature and phase change, crystallization and sublimation. When they speak to each other, it's through precisely controlled patterns of freezing that spread across the surfaces of their pools. The more sensitive ice-priests have learned to recognize some of these patterns, though true communication remains elusive.

The Frost Dancers don't reproduce in any way we'd recognize - they simply are, and have been, and will be as long as there is cold and moisture and the possibility of crystalline order emerging from chaos. But they do change and grow, slowly incorporating new patterns and possibilities into their being. Each interaction with the world outside their realm adds to their complexity, like new facets growing on an endless crystal.

They are the guardians of this place, the keepers of knowledge written in ice, the shepherds of whatever strange evolution is taking place in their pools. Patient, curious, utterly alien yet deeply connected to the fundamental forces that shape our world. They dance their slow dance of frost and time, maintaining their intricate balance between order and entropy, watching and learning and slowly, ever so slowly, changing.
