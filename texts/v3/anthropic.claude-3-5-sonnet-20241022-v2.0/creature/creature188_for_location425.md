In the Whistling Plain, three distinct forms of life have evolved into a complex ecological harmony, each shaped by and contributing to the eternal song of the landscape.

The Resonators are the most numerous - colonial organisms that superficially resemble blind, translucent arthropods but function more like living tuning forks. Their segmented bodies, ranging from thumbnail to hand-sized, are precisely shaped to amplify or dampen specific frequencies. They cluster in the canyon depths by the thousands, their combined vibrations creating complex interference patterns that they use for everything from communication to hunting to reproduction. Each colony develops its own unique "signature frequency" that helps maintain territory boundaries. Their crystalline exoskeletons accumulate sonic energy over their lifespans, and when they die, this energy helps form the resonant crystals the hermits prize.

Higher up, in the mid-levels of the canyons, dwell the Weavers - amorphous creatures that manipulate the standing waves to construct elaborate "sound webs." These nearly invisible structures trap the vibrating air itself, which the Weavers use to snare the small flying creatures that occasionally fall into the canyons. The Weavers themselves are difficult to observe directly, as their bodies seem to exist partially out of phase with normal matter, vibrating between states of solidity. They appear as shimming distortions in the air, their true form only visible in the patterns of their webs.

Most enigmatic are the Chord Sages - massive, extremely slow-moving beings that anchor themselves to the canyon walls near the surface. Their bodies resemble elaborate pipe organs made of living tissue, with multiple appendages that they can extend or retract to alter the local acoustics. They appear to be highly intelligent, though their consciousness operates on a time scale so slow that even the hermits have trouble communicating with them. The Sages seem to be studying the plateau's song, using their bodies to create complex harmonic analyses that play out over decades. The silence gardens often grow around them, the plants responding to the Sages' careful acoustic modifications.

These three species have developed a symbiotic relationship. The Resonators' activities help shape the sonic environment that the Weavers need for their webs, while the Weavers' structures help protect colonies of Resonators from harmful frequencies. The Chord Sages appear to orchestrate this relationship, their slow manipulations creating stable acoustic ecosystems that benefit all.

The hermits believe that these creatures are not native to the plateau but were either created by or attracted to its song. They point to the way each species seems to be slowly evolving toward greater harmony with the plateau's frequencies, as if working toward some collective purpose. Some hermits claim to have witnessed occasions when all three species synchronized their activities, creating moments of profound acoustic convergence that briefly revealed patterns in the air that looked like writing in an unknown script.

Most disturbing are the recent observations suggesting these creatures are beginning to venture beyond the plateau's borders, following the spread of the sound into neighboring regions. Whether they are fleeing something or preparing for something remains unclear.
