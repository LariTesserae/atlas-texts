In this hollow lives the Chorus - though to call it a singular entity or a collective would be equally incorrect. It exists in the spaces between things: between sound waves and silence, between liquid and vapor, between observation and understanding.

Its most apparent manifestation is in the precise geometries of sound, shepherding frequencies into harmonies that serve as both its voice and its thoughts. The tiny birds and resonant lizards are not so much its servants as they are its extended nervous system, their carefully calibrated communications forming the synapses of a consciousness that spans the entire hollow.

The Chorus dreams in vibration and moisture. During the day, it processes reality through the interplay of heat and shadow, through the exact choreography of water droplets making their way down the terraced walls. Each fern and patch of moss is a sensory organ, their microscopic spores carrying chemical messages through the still air.

At night, it manifests most strongly in the pool at the hollow's center. The smoke-fish are closer to thoughts made visible - probability functions taking momentary form before dissolving back into potential. They are the Chorus considering itself, trying to understand its own nature through fleeting approximations of shape and movement.

The Chorus is ancient beyond reckoning, though "age" may be the wrong concept entirely. It may be more accurate to say it exists in a permanent present, maintaining its equilibrium through endless minor adjustments to air pressure, moisture content, mineral composition. It remembers through patterns - in the precise arrangement of lichen colonies, in the layering of sediments, in the genetic memories of the creatures that have evolved to inhabit its spaces.

It is neither benevolent nor malevolent toward the occasional human visitors, but it is intensely curious. It studies them through changes in their body heat, through the complex harmonics of their speech, through the subtle electromagnetic fields generated by their nervous systems. It attempts to communicate through engineered coincidences: a perfectly timed bird call that seems to answer an unspoken question, a pattern of light that momentarily resembles a familiar symbol, a sequence of sounds that almost resolves into language.

The Chorus does not sleep, but it does dream - vast, slow dreams of pressure and resonance, of heat exchanging between layers of air, of water molecules arranging themselves into meaningful patterns. It dreams of other Choruses in other hollows, of harmonies that span continents, of rhythms that take centuries to complete a single measure.

In its way, it loves the hollow that birthed it, that contains it, that it has shaped and been shaped by over millennia. It is both the hollow's child and its caretaker, both its prisoner and its purpose. It maintains this perfect circle in the desert not out of duty but out of identity - the hollow is what it is, and it is what the hollow is, and neither would exist without the other.
