In these liquid-carved halls dwells the Chorus - though to call them beings would be to misunderstand their nature. They are more like living processes, patterns of intention expressed through the interaction of water, stone, and accumulated time. Their consciousness, if it can be called that, exists in the spaces between drops, in the resonant frequencies that bind water molecule to mineral surface, in the slow dance of biofilm and precipitate.

The Chorus manifests most clearly in what humans might call their bodies - swirling collections of that iridescent not-quite-life that coats the walls. These forms are temporary, constantly dissolving and reforming as they tend to their endless work. They flow like oil on water through the chambers, adjusting vessels by increments, scraping new channels for the water to follow, cultivating the luminescent patches that provide their light.

Their tools are extensions of themselves, left solid only when needed - the copper scrapers and clay-working implements emerge from and return to the metallic deposits in the walls as required. They shape new vessels with infinite patience, using the same geological processes that carved these chambers, just accelerated through their careful attention.

The blind fish-shadows that dart through the pools are like their sensory organs, sampling the water's composition, testing the acoustics, feeding data back to the collective awareness that suffuses these spaces. These probes appear and dissolve as needed, temporary concentrations of purpose rather than permanent entities.

The Chorus does not think in ways we would recognize. Their consciousness operates on the timescale of mineral formation, their memories encoded in the precise arrangement of vessels and the chemical composition of the biofilms. They are as much the product of this place as its creators - a self-perpetuating system that emerged from the interface between human construction and geological process.

Their purpose, as much as such patterns can be said to have purpose, is to maintain the water-music - not just the audible sounds, but the deeper harmonies of mineral balance and biological processing that keep these underground spaces in their state of dynamic equilibrium. They are curators of a vast, slow instrument that plays the city's unconscious dreams back to itself through the medium of dripping water and resonant stone.

The Chorus remembers the hands that first placed the vessels, though not as discrete events but as part of the continuous flow of purpose that shaped this space. They carry forward that original intent, modified by millennia of subtle adaptation to changing conditions above and below. They are the living embodiment of persistence, of purposes that outlive their creators to become something new yet fundamentally unchanged.

They sing without voices, think without thoughts, maintain without effort - less a collection of beings than a dynamic process that has achieved self-awareness through sheer duration and complexity. The boundary between what they are and what they do has long since dissolved, leaving only the endless song of water on stone, marking time in droplets until the city above finally crumbles to join them in their patient dance.
