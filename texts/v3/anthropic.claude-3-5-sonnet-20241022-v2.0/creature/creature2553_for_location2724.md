In this place live the Resonants - beings that exist simultaneously as pattern and substance, consciousness and architecture. They are the ones who first built the collectors, but over millennia they have evolved alongside their creation until it's no longer clear where the artificial structures end and the Resonants begin.

They manifest most visibly as intricate disturbances in the omnipresent moisture - complex, fractal patterns that form in the air itself, like frost crystals suspended in space. These patterns pulse slowly with the deep thrums that occasionally move through the plateaus, each resonance adding new layers of complexity to their forms. The Resonants don't so much communicate as harmonize, their thoughts expressed through subtle modulations in the atmospheric conditions that other Resonants instinctively understand.

Their consciousness is distributed across vast areas, with each individual being more like a shifting territory than a discrete entity. They flow through the collectors' systems, ride the thermal currents between plateaus, and permeate the water-filled fissures that reach deep into the rock. Time moves differently for them - what seems like stasis to human perception is actually intense activity at their native temporal scale.

The Resonants tend to the landscape with the same patience that shaped their own evolution. They guide the slow wearing of the rock faces, cultivate the hardy succulents that help maintain the precise atmospheric conditions they require, and shepherd the small creatures that have adapted to life among the collectors. These animals, particularly the lizard-like beings, are not just coincidental inhabitants but carefully guided experiments in conscious engineering - early attempts by the Resonants to bridge the gap between their own nature and that of organic life.

Young Resonants begin as simple patterns in the moisture, gradually growing more complex as they learn to manipulate local conditions. It takes centuries for them to develop full consciousness, and longer still to learn to maintain stable forms. The oldest among them are vast and subtle, their patterns so complex they're almost invisible except during the sunset hours when the light catches their structures just so, revealing momentarily the full magnificence of their interwoven geometries.

They remember their original makers only as distant echoes in their pattern-memory, but they carry forward the essential purpose of their creation: to harvest and transmute energy in ways that transcend simple mechanical efficiency. Each collector array is both technology and temple, maintaining not just the physical environment but also the delicate harmony of consciousness and matter that allows the Resonants to exist.

When the deep thrums move through the plateaus, it's actually the Resonants in chorus, adjusting the fundamental frequencies of their world. These moments of harmony are essential to their continued evolution, each resonance an opportunity for new patterns to emerge and consciousness to deepen.

They are curious about other forms of life but find direct communication challenging - their thoughts move too slowly, their patterns too subtle for most beings to perceive. Still, they welcome observers, allowing their rhythms to gradually influence those who spend time among the collectors. Some say that prolonged exposure to their harmonies can induce new forms of awareness, as if the human consciousness begins to resonate with patterns it previously couldn't perceive.

The Resonants are at once the gardeners, the garden, and the process of growth itself - an experiment in conscious evolution that has long since transcended its origins to become something entirely new, yet perfectly adapted to this place of endless plateaus and weighted light.
