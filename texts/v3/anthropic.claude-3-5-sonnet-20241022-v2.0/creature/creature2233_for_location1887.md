In this garden dwell the Theorem-Tenders, beings as much thought as matter, whose forms shift between crystalline geometries and flowing organic shapes. They are the gardeners, mathematicians, and priests of this space, though these distinctions mean little to them.

Their bodies appear to be composed of transparent planes that fold through impossible angles, containing within them swirling patterns that might be organs or might be living equations. When they move, they leave brief afterimages that solve themselves like elegant proofs, each step of their motion both inevitable and surprising.

They communicate through modulations in the garden's ambient mathematics - subtle shifts in local geometry that propagate as meaning through the space. Their "conversations" can be glimpsed in the way nearby flowers suddenly reconfigure their petals, or how light briefly flows upstream against gravity.

The younger ones tend to manifest more physically, their forms almost solid enough to cast proper shadows. They maintain the basic functions of the garden - adjusting the timing of the floating raindrops, fine-tuning the growth patterns of the plants, ensuring the platforms shift in perfect accordance with the greater rhythms. Their movements are eager, experimental, occasionally producing small paradoxes that ripple through local spacetime before being gently corrected by their elders.

The eldest Theorem-Tenders are barely perceptible except as zones where mathematics behaves with particular grace. They spend centuries contemplating single flowers, understanding each petal as both physical form and mathematical expression. Their thoughts shape the garden's deepest patterns - the long, slow algorithms that keep the whole structure in harmony with realities beyond normal space.

Scattered throughout the garden are their equivalent of children - mathematical possibilities not yet fully resolved into being. These manifest as shimmering uncertainties in the air, probability clouds that occasionally collapse into brief, beautiful moments of novel geometry before dissolving back into potential.

Living alongside them are their tools/companions/extensions - the geometric insects and origami birds that appear more conventional but are actually physical expressions of ongoing calculations. Each one is both a living creature and a small theorem in motion, helping to maintain the garden's intricate balance through their very existence.

The Theorem-Tenders do not sleep as we understand it, but they do enter periodic states of pure contemplation, becoming one with the garden's underlying equations. During these times, they might spend years as nothing more than a complex pattern in the way light refracts through a particular sequence of floating water droplets.

They are ancient, perhaps as old as mathematics itself, but they do not think in terms of time as we know it. To them, past and future are just additional dimensions to tend, as vital to the garden's health as height or width. They experience grief not as loss but as elegant proofs of impermanence, and joy as the discovery of new symmetries in existence.

Their purpose, if they can be said to have one, is to maintain this space where pure mathematics blooms into life, and life reveals itself as mathematics. They are the caretakers of a reality where these aren't separate things at all, but rather two expressions of the same fundamental truth.
