In this city live the Adaptors - people who have evolved alongside their ever-rising home, their culture and biology shaped by the demands of constant adjustment. They are tall and lean, with unusually flexible joints and excellent balance, traits that serve them well on the shifting terrain and narrow bridges of their vertical world.

Their skin has a faint greenish cast, not from any alien biology but from a symbiotic relationship with certain strains of the local moss. This living layer helps protect them from the constant moisture and provides extra grip on slick surfaces. Many sport elaborate geometric patterns in their moss-covering, cultivated through careful exposure to different light conditions - both aesthetic choice and practical marker of their usual working elevation in the city.

The Adaptors' hands are their most distinctive feature - long-fingered with extra joints, equally suited to fine mechanical work and climbing. Their fingers are slightly adhesive, like those of small lizards, another adaptation to their vertical environment. They move with a characteristic fluid grace, always maintaining three points of contact with their surroundings - a deeply ingrained habit that children learn before they can walk.

They live in small, close-knit family groups, occupying flexible living spaces that can be readily adjusted as the mountain shifts. These homes are sparsely furnished but rich in clever mechanical solutions - beds that self-level, walls that can be reconfigured, anchoring systems that automatically adjust to maintain stability. Many families specialize in particular aspects of city maintenance, passing down both technical knowledge and specific genetic adaptations through generations.

Their language reflects their environment - full of terms for different types of movement and stability, with dozens of words for various angles of slope and qualities of stone. They speak in measured tones that carry well through the city's complex acoustic environment, and have developed a sophisticated system of mechanical signals for communication across distances, using the city's network of pipes and cables.

The Adaptors have a deep understanding of their city's systems, both mechanical and biological. Children learn to read the signs of stone movement from infancy, and every citizen is part engineer, part gardener, part geologist. They have developed an intuitive grasp of the city's rhythms - they can feel an impending shift in the stone through subtle changes in vibration, or predict weather changes by the behavior of their moss gardens.

Their society is highly cooperative, organized around the constant work of maintaining and adjusting their environment. Status comes from one's contribution to the city's stability and growth, with the most respected members being those who can best anticipate and adapt to change. They have little interest in preserving things exactly as they are - their art celebrates transformation, their architecture expects modification, their stories emphasize adaptation over persistence.

The Adaptors have a complex relationship with time - they plan centuries ahead while remaining constantly responsive to immediate changes. Their historical records track both human events and geological movements, treating the mountain's growth as an active participant in their civilization's development. They see themselves not as conquering or resisting their environment, but as evolving with it, their own growth intertwined with that of their rising home.

Despite (or perhaps because of) the technical demands of their environment, they maintain a rich cultural life, with celebrations tied to significant geological events and artistic traditions that incorporate the city's constant motion. They are a people who have made an art of impermanence, finding beauty and meaning in the endless dance of adaptation.
