In these liquid forests dwell the Drift-Weavers - entities that exist as much as patterns of movement as discrete organisms. Their bodies are translucent and mutable, able to extend into gossamer-thin membranes or contract into more solid forms as needed. They range from the size of a human hand to that of a small whale, though their boundaries are often difficult to define.

The Drift-Weavers' primary form resembles a loose constellation of interconnected nodes, each one a concentration of neural tissue and specialized organelles, connected by living fibers that can stretch for meters. These fibers aren't just structural - they're sensory organs that detect minute changes in the living water's chemical composition, current patterns, and the deep songs of the kelp-towers.

They create their homes by cultivating relationships with the ecosystem rather than building fixed structures. A Drift-Weaver might spend decades nurturing a particular junction between kelp-towers, encouraging the growth of certain symbiotic organisms while pruning others, until they've shaped a living space that provides both shelter and sustenance. These spaces often become shared habitats, with multiple Drift-Weavers coexisting in loose, shifting communities.

Their intelligence is distributed and highly adapted to their environment. They think in patterns of current and chemical gradients, in the slow rhythms of the kelp-towers' songs, in the intricate dance of symbiosis and flow. Their memories are stored not just in their neural nodes but in the very patterns they weave in the living water - modifications to the local chemistry that can persist for years, forming a kind of external memory that other Drift-Weavers can read and contribute to.

They reproduce through a process that blurs the line between growth and reproduction - occasionally, a complex pattern of movement and chemical exchange will cause portions of several Drift-Weavers to merge and then separate, forming new individuals that carry aspects of multiple parents. These new beings might maintain loose connections to their progenitors for years before fully establishing their own patterns.

The Drift-Weavers appear to have evolved from one of the original terraforming organisms, but they've developed far beyond their initial programming. They've become caretakers of sorts, though not in any planned way - their activities help maintain the health of the Living Sea simply because their own existence is so thoroughly interwoven with it. They drift through the liquid forest like living dreams, shaping and being shaped by the endless dance of life in this strange and beautiful world.
