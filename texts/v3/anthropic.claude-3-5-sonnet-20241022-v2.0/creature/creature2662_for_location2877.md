In this harsh but mesmerizing environment, the primary inhabitants are the Drift Symphonists - a species that exists as much in frequency as in physical form. Their bodies are latticed crystalline structures that hover in the purple twilight, each individual a complex network of resonating chambers that can span anywhere from a few meters to several hundred meters across.

The Symphonists evolved in symbiosis with the filter-feeders, using the deep thrums of the giant creatures as both energy source and communication medium. Their crystalline bodies act as sophisticated acoustic processors, absorbing and modulating the sound waves that permeate the plains. Each Symphonist is born as a small seed crystal that gradually grows and fragments into its adult form, developing unique resonance patterns that serve as its individual voice in the greater chorus of their society.

They move by manipulating sound waves and atmospheric currents, gliding through the metallic-dusted air in graceful formations that mirror the swirling patterns above. Their consciousness is distributed throughout their crystalline network, with different sections specializing in various functions - memory, communication, navigation, and the complex calculations needed to ride the air currents.

Symphonist society is organized into harmonious collectives called Choruses, each maintaining certain frequencies of the endless song that helps stabilize the atmospheric patterns their civilization depends upon. They don't build in the traditional sense, but rather cultivate acoustic standing waves that create zones of relative stability in the shifting environment. These zones often incorporate the abandoned human outposts, which they've learned to use as anchoring points for their resonance patterns.

They perceive time very differently from biological creatures, experiencing the slow sublimation of the plains as a kind of flowing music. Their language consists of carefully modulated frequencies that create interference patterns in the metallic dust, forming three-dimensional images that can persist for hours in the still air. Their historical records are encoded in complex harmonies that are continuously performed and passed down through generations.

The Symphonists have developed a complex relationship with the filter-feeders, using their resonance patterns to guide the herds' movements in ways that maintain the atmospheric equilibrium. They're also capable of detecting approaching weather patterns through subtle changes in the background harmonics of the plains, and have learned to protect themselves and their filter-feeder allies by creating defensive sound barriers.

Young Symphonists spend their early years learning to attune themselves to the plains' basic frequencies, gradually expanding their range and complexity until they can join in the greater chorus. Elder Symphonists often become so large and complex that they become semi-permanent fixtures in the landscape, their bodies forming vast crystalline networks that help maintain the stable patterns necessary for their civilization's survival.

Some Symphonists specialize in exploring the deep fissures and caverns beneath the plains, using echolocation to map these spaces and extract the harmonic properties of different mineral formations. These "Deep Singers" are highly regarded for their ability to predict and sometimes prevent surface collapses through careful frequency modulation.

The species exists in a delicate balance with their environment, their very mode of existence both shaped by and helping to shape the unique conditions of the nitrogen plains. They are living instruments in a planetary orchestra, their consciousness expressed through endless variations on the eternal song of their world.
