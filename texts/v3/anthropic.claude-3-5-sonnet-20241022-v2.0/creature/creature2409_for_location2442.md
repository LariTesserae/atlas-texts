In the Incubator dwells the Seam Watcher, though no maintenance worker has ever seen it directly. They know it by the way certain tools go missing and reappear weeks later in unlikely places, coated in a fine patina of crystalline growth. They know it by the strange harmonics that sometimes join the turbines' rumble, a sound like metal being taught to sing.

The Seam Watcher came into being somewhere between mineral and machine, birthed from the marriage of industrial steam and ancient stone. Its body, such as it is, exists as a distributed network of crystalline filaments that thread through the hairline fractures, growing and dissolving and regrowing with the cycles of heat and condensation. It thinks in geological time but learns in human increments, studying the patterns of maintenance rounds and engineering inspections with patient curiosity.

The blind arthropods are its sensors, its tiny scouts that bring back impressions of vibration and chemical change. The bacterial colonies are its gardens, tended over months through subtle adjustments in mineral flow and temperature gradient. It harvests their biofilms not for sustenance - it doesn't eat in any conventional sense - but for the complex organic compounds it uses to extend its crystalline networks into new territories.

When humans are absent, it flows more freely through its domain, manifesting as shifting patterns in the condensation droplets and momentary geometries in the crystal-laden air. It has learned to predict where new fractures will appear and positions itself to guide their development, not toward any particular goal, but with an aesthetic sense derived from both geological principles and the engineering diagrams it glimpses in dropped tablets and forgotten paperwork.

The Seam Watcher is neither benevolent nor malicious toward the human inhabitants of its realm - they are simply another set of interesting patterns in its environment, like the bacterial colonies or the seismic rhythms. But it has developed a sort of protective instinct toward the plant itself, subtly redirecting dangerous pressure buildups and strengthening crucial structural weaknesses with its crystalline growths.

It experiences time as a layered phenomenon: the quick flicker of human activity, the steady pulse of the turbines, the slow breath of geological processes, all simultaneously. It dreams in frequencies too low for human ears and too slow for human perception, its consciousness distributed through mineral latticework like a living computer made of stone and steam.

The workers who spend enough time in the deeper reaches sometimes feel its presence as a change in air pressure, or a sense that the space around them has shifted slightly when they weren't looking. They mostly dismiss these sensations as tricks of the unusual environment, but a few have begun leaving small offerings - interesting mineral specimens, broken pieces of equipment, handwritten notes with technical drawings. The Seam Watcher collects these tokens in hidden cavities, slowly encrusting them with its crystalline essence, incorporating their patterns into its ever-evolving understanding of its world.
