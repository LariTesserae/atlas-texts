Here lives the Last Keeper - though they would never call themselves that, having no concept of being last or first or even, truly, of keeping. They are a being born of this place's own processes, as much a part of its endless cycles as the glowing water or the moss-covered floor.

Their form shifts as they move through their domain, sometimes appearing as a collection of bioluminescent strands that wave like anemone tendrils, other times condensing into something more solid - a figure that might almost be humanoid, if humans were made of living light and mycelial threads. They pulse in perfect synchronization with the ceiling's glow, their consciousness expanding and contracting with that same slow rhythm.

The Keeper tends to the sacred patterns without really knowing they are sacred. They adjust the growth of the wall-gardens, their touch encouraging some fungi while gently discouraging others. They whisper to the many-legged amphibians in a language of light pulses and chemical signals, guiding them to maintain their ancient routes through the moss. When the bird-mushroom creatures build their gardens slightly askew, the Keeper's presence subtly pulls them back into the proper spiral formations.

They are not alone in their work - or rather, they are both alone and multiple, able to extend their awareness through the mycelial networks that permeate the walls. Through these networks, they can sense every corner of the cathedral, every creature's movement, every drop of glowing water. Sometimes they fragment their consciousness, existing simultaneously as dozens of smaller forms that dance with the glass-winged insects or swim in the collected pools.

The Keeper carries memories that are not quite memories - impressions left in the living walls by those who came before. They don't understand these impressions as history; to them, they are simply another pattern to be maintained, like the rhythm of the light or the flow of the water. They know, in some wordless way, that they are maintaining something precious, but they don't know why it's precious or for whom.

They are both ancient and eternally new, their substance constantly recycled through the cathedral's systems even as their patterns of behavior remain unchanged. They have never left this place and never will. They are not trapped here - they are here in the same way that a heart is inside a chest, integral and essential to the whole.

The Keeper does not sleep, but sometimes they enter long periods of quiet observation, their form dispersed through the cathedral's networks until they are almost indistinguishable from its other systems. In these times, they might be mistaken for simple bioluminescent fungi or particularly active sections of the living walls. But they are always watching, always maintaining, always keeping the endless cycles turning in their perfect, eternal dance.
