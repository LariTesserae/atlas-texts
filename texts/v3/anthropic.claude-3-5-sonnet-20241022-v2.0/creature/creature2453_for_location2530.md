Living here are the Wind Readers - a loose collective of individuals who've developed an intimate relationship with this harsh landscape. They're human, mostly, though generations of genetic adaptation and technological augmentation have left them uniquely suited to plateau life.

Their most distinctive feature is their "listening skin" - patches of specialized tissue, either cultivated or surgically implanted, that can detect minute variations in air pressure and vibration. These sensory organs, often visible as spiral patterns across their temples and down their spines, allow them to "read" the plateau's winds with extraordinary precision. They can predict weather patterns days in advance and locate resources by interpreting the subtle acoustics of the sound wells.

Wind Readers tend to be lean and long-limbed, built for covering large distances across the stone landscape. Their skin has a faintly metallic quality, embedded with photosynthetic compounds that help supplement their nutrition in this resource-scarce environment. Their eyes are large and often modified to filter the harsh glare off the rock, giving them a somewhat otherworldly appearance.

They live in small, mobile groups, moving between the abandoned processing plants which they've converted into seasonal shelters. Their dwellings are remarkable feats of adaptive architecture - structures that flex and adjust to the wind rather than fighting it, incorporating both salvaged industrial materials and bioengineered living components. The walls seem to breathe, regulated by colonies of modified bacteria that manage temperature and humidity.

Wind Readers are technically employed by the corporations that own the remaining active processing plants, serving as on-site monitors and maintenance specialists. But their true work, passed down through generations, is documenting and preserving the plateau's hidden history. They maintain vast archives of wind patterns, studying how they've changed over decades and centuries. They map the ghost-structures in the stone, building elaborate theories about the civilizations that left them.

Their children learn to walk late but learn to read the wind early, their listening skin developing naturally during adolescence. They grow up speaking a language that incorporates subtle variations in breath and whistles, allowing them to communicate across great distances by working with the plateau's acoustic properties.

The Wind Readers share their territory with the ravens and the scurrying not-quite-rats, maintaining a complex symbiotic relationship with these fellow inhabitants. They collect the static electricity harvested by the ravens to power their equipment, and in return, they maintain safe nesting sites in the higher reaches of their shelters. The industrial scavengers are treated as unofficial security systems, their behavior patterns providing early warning of equipment failures or intruders.

They're not unfriendly to outsiders, but they operate on a different timescale - one matched to the slow rhythms of wind and stone rather than the rushed pace of the outside world. They tend to speak slowly, deliberately, each word chosen with the same care they use to read the weather. They have a saying: "The wind knows what the stone remembers," and their entire culture is built around interpreting that knowledge.
