In these waters dwell the Threadsingers - beings as hybrid as their home, neither fully human nor wholly other. Their bodies are long and sinuous, skin translucent in places to reveal the bioluminescent symbionts that curl through their lymphatic systems like living jewelry. Their fingers are impossibly delicate, extending into gossamer filaments perfect for tending the floating algae gardens or conducting the city's submarine symphonies.

They move through water with liquid grace, their bodies adapted over generations to the pressure and perpetual immersion. Their eyes are large and dark, equally suited to the twilight depths or the harsh glare of noon filtered through hundreds of meters of water. Fine sensory tendrils extend from their temples and along their spines, reading the complex patterns of current and vibration that make up the city's acoustic landscape.

The Threadsingers live in small family groups, occupying the bubble-gardens where they maintain precious collections of air-breathing plants and practice their arts. They are caretakers and composers, understanding that in this place music and maintenance are the same thing. Their songs, produced through a combination of modified vocal cords and the deliberate manipulation of water pressure, help direct the growth of the coral-stone and maintain the health of the vast algae sheets that feed the city.

They breed rarely and live long, their metabolism slowed by the constant pressure and the specialized nutrients they derive from their symbiotic relationships with the city's organic components. Young Threadsingers spend decades learning to read the subtle signs of growth and decay in the structures around them, to understand the complex interplay of currents and chemicals that keeps the bubble-gardens stable.

The eldest among them remember when the city first began to rise, though they speak of this time only in songs too deep for human ears to perceive. Their memories are stored not just in minds but in the very tissues of the coral-stone, accessed through careful manipulation of the resonance chambers scattered throughout the city.

They share their domain with schools of engineered fish that help maintain the delicate ecological balance, and with colonies of luminous creatures that may once have been human themselves, though they've transformed far more radically than the Threadsingers. These beings nest in the deeper recesses of the city, emerging only rarely to exchange incomprehensible signals with their more humanoid cousins.

Most fascinating are the Threadsingers' hands - their extra joints and partially webbed fingers allow them to literally play the city like an instrument, drawing music from the movement of water through specially shaped channels and chambers. This is not mere artistry; the vibrations help maintain the structural integrity of the coral-stone and influence the growth patterns of the living architecture.

They are patient creatures, operating on the same geological timescale as their home. A single musical composition might take years to complete, its notes played out in the slow pulse of tidal rhythms and the gradual accumulation of mineral deposits. They understand that they too are part of the city's ongoing transformation - neither its masters nor its servants, but another element in its endless process of becoming.
