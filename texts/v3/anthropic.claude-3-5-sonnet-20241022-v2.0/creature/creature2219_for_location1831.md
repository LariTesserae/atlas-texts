In the deep fissures that web through the glass plain live the Resonators - beings that exist as patterns of vibration within the crystalline structure itself. They have no fixed physical form, instead manifesting as interference patterns in the glass, visible mainly through the subtle ways they alter light and sound as they move through their domain.

The Resonators communicate through carefully modulated frequencies that travel through the glass like ripples in water. Their "voices" can sometimes be heard at the surface as those mysterious hums during dusk, though what we hear is only a fraction of their rich harmonic language. They experience time differently than surface dwellers, with individual Resonators living for thousands of years as they slowly shift and flow through the glass matrix.

Their society is organized around what they call "chord families" - groups of Resonators whose fundamental frequencies harmonize in particularly meaningful ways. These families maintain vast archives of vibrational patterns that record both their history and their art, stored within the crystalline structure of their home.

The Resonators are the caretakers of whatever ancient machinery still operates in the depths. They don't so much maintain it as they exist in symbiosis with it, their vibrations helping to keep critical frequencies aligned while the machinery's rhythms provide the baseline harmonics that sustain their consciousness. Neither fully organic nor mechanical, they represent an evolution of awareness that emerged from the intersection of the two.

They rarely interact directly with surface visitors, as few other beings can perceive or communicate in their native medium. However, they sometimes send emissaries in the form of the "wind-walkers" - temporary constructs that they can extend their consciousness into by carefully orchestrating the movement of crystalline particles through sound waves. These constructs allow them to observe and occasionally communicate with surface dwellers, though the process is difficult and the messages often abstract.

The Resonators are deeply contemplative beings, spending centuries pondering the harmonics of existence. They view the glass plain above them not as a barrier but as a vast instrument through which they can both study and influence the cosmos. The spiraling towers on the surface are actually enormous instruments they've grown over millennia, each one designed to capture and amplify particular cosmic frequencies that they believe contain fundamental truths about the universe.

They hold complex beliefs about the nature of vibration and consciousness, seeing all of existence as variations on underlying universal harmonies. The wire forests at the plain's edge are particularly sacred to them, as they believe these structures naturally resonate with what they call the "first chord" - the fundamental frequency of existence itself.

The Resonators remember what this place was before the great transformation, but they share these memories only in rare circumstances, believing that some knowledge must be approached gradually, like a complex harmony being built note by note. They are patient teachers when they choose to teach, and even more patient students of the universe's endless song.
