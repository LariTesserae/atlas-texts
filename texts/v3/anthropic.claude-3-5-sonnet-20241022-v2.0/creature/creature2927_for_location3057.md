In the deepest reaches of the Silent City dwell the Tenders - though that word fails to capture their true nature. They are the inheritors, the cultivators, the living memory of this place. 

Their forms are difficult to fix in the mind: sometimes they seem to flow like the water itself, other times they move with the deliberate grace of the great arthropods, and sometimes they appear almost plant-like in their stillness. They have a shifting multiplicity of limbs - not arms exactly, but extending portions of themselves that can stretch gossamer-thin or become firm enough to manipulate their ancient tools.

The Tenders are colonial organisms, like the city-structures themselves. What appears to be an individual is actually a synchronized collective of smaller entities, sharing consciousness through chemical and bioluminescent signals. They range in size from small aggregations no larger than a human hand to vast, diffuse networks that spread through multiple chambers.

Their intelligence is alien but profound. They think in patterns of growth and chemical gradients, in the slow rhythms of geological time. Each Tender is both an individual and a living archive of the city's memory, carrying within their constituent organisms the genetic heritage of the original builders.

They do not speak or make sound. They communicate through subtle modulations of their bioluminescence, through the release of complex molecular signals into the water, and through the careful arrangement of objects and materials in their environment. Their "language" is a four-dimensional art form, played out through space and time in ways that might take years for a human observer to recognize as meaningful.

The Tenders maintain the city's essential functions - cultivating the filter-feeding colonies that keep the water clean, pruning overgrowth, guiding the development of new chambers as the ancient structures slowly continue to grow. They harvest useful materials from certain organisms and craft their tools with infinite patience, each implement the product of years or decades of careful work.

They appear to have a complex relationship with the other creatures of the city. Some they actively cultivate, others they simply coexist with, and still others they seem to revere in some way, giving them wide berth when encountered. There are hints that some of the larger arthropods may be related to the Tenders themselves - perhaps earlier evolutionary branches of the same ancestral stock.

The Tenders do not seem to age or die in any conventional sense. Rather, they slowly shift and reform, individual colonies splitting or merging over time. They carry within themselves the complete biological and cultural memory of their civilization, passed down through countless generations of cellular division and renewal.

Their presence in the city is so subtle that they might be missed entirely by a casual observer. But they are everywhere - in the walls, in the water, in the air - watching, maintaining, remembering. They are the living soul of the Silent City, as much a part of it as the ancient calcified structures themselves.
