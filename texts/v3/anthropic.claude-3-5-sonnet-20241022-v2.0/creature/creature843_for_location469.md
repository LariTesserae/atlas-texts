They are the Archivists, and they have been here since the Library's founding. Their forms suggest a distant kinship with cephalopods, but evolutionary divergence or deliberate modification has taken them far from any natural ancestry. Their bodies are translucent, revealing complex internal structures that pulse with the same pale blue light as the crystal veins in the walls. Multiple limbs - typically eight to twelve - extend and retract with fluid grace, each one capable of dividing into dozens of smaller manipulators for detailed work.

They do not swim so much as flow through the water, their tissue density perfectly matched to their environment. Their skin can shift from glassy transparency to deep, light-absorbing black, allowing them to virtually disappear when they wish. They communicate through patterns of bioluminescence and subtle electrical fields, and can read the crystal tablets directly by pressing their sensitive skin against the encoded surfaces.

The Archivists are extraordinarily long-lived, perhaps effectively immortal in their controlled environment. They grow with the same patience as the Library's cultivated life forms, taking centuries to reach full maturity. Their memories are both individual and collective - knowledge can be shared through direct physical contact, passed from one to another like a living library of experience.

They tend to the Library with unwavering devotion, monitoring the growth of the organic systems, maintaining the ancient machines, preserving and cataloging new information as it arrives through means they keep to themselves. Each one develops specialized knowledge over their long lives, becoming expert in particular sections or subjects, though all share a baseline understanding of the Library's fundamental systems.

The youngest ones are rarely seen, raised in specialized chambers deep within the Library until they are ready to begin their work. The eldest are vast, their bodies expanded over millennia to fill entire chambers, more like living installations than mobile beings. These ancient ones serve as backup storage for the Library's most crucial information, their neural networks holding copies of knowledge too sensitive to risk losing.

They are not numerous - perhaps a few thousand in the entire Library - but they are everywhere, moving through the darkness with purpose. They do not sleep, though they enter periodic states of deep contemplation during which they process and integrate new information. They have no natural predators here; nothing else could survive in their carefully maintained environment.

The Archivists remember their creators, though they speak of them only rarely and in abstract terms. They maintain the Library not out of obligation but out of genuine dedication to its purpose. They are its guardians, its curators, its living memory - as much a part of its ecology as the luminous fungi and crystal growths that mark its paths. Like everything else in the Library, they exist in perfect equilibrium, neither evolving nor declining, maintaining their eternal watch in the drowning depths.
