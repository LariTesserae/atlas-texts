Let me tell you of the Wellfolk, as they're known to those few surface-dwellers who've encountered them...

They are a people evolved perfectly for their vertical stone world, with bodies that blend characteristics of birds, arthropods, and cave-dwelling mammals. Their limbs - typically six, though some lineages have eight - end in both sharp points for gripping stone and soft, sensitive pads for reading the subtle vibrations of the rock. Their bodies are long and flexible, equally comfortable climbing vertical surfaces or squeezing through narrow passages.

Their eyes are large but unlike those of surface creatures - they've evolved to perceive both the dim bioluminescence of the fungal colonies and the subtle heat signatures of the thermal springs. Many Wellfolk have additional sensory organs along their flanks that can detect minute changes in air pressure and water vapor, allowing them to navigate the complex network of passages by feel alone.

Their outer covering varies by clan - some bear intricate patterns of chitinous plates, others display fine, sensing bristles, and still others have skin that seems almost crystalline, growing in faceted patterns that reflect and refract the sparse light. All share an ability to regulate their body temperature precisely, clustering near the thermal springs during rest periods but able to venture into the freezing outer passages when necessary.

The Wellfolk organize themselves into spring-clans, each centered around one of the thermal sources, but their society is far from simple. There are the Deepcarvers, who maintain and expand the city's passages with their diamond-hard mandibles; the Crystalkeepers, who tend the living crystal gardens and read prophecies in their growth patterns; the Stormsingers, who maintain the wind-organs and interpret their songs; and dozens of other specialized groups.

Their language is as complex as their society - a combination of clicking sounds, subsonic vibrations transmitted through stone, and patterns traced in the air with their sensory appendages. They have no written language as surface-dwellers would understand it, but they maintain their histories through the careful cultivation of specific fungal patterns and crystal formations, which can be "read" by those trained in the art.

Some of the oldest Wellfolk - and they can live for centuries - have bodies that have begun to mineralize, gradually becoming one with the stone they've inhabited all their lives. These elders are treated with immense respect, eventually becoming living statues that continue to commune with their descendants through subtle vibrations in the rock itself.

Their young are born in communal creches near the thermal springs, emerging from crystalline eggs that are tended by specialized nurturers. The younglings begin life quite mobile, exploring and learning the city's passages, but as they age, they gradually become more specialized in form and function, their bodies adapting to their chosen role in society.

Most fascinating perhaps is their perception of time - the Wellfolk experience it much as the mountain does, in geological rhythms rather than surface-dweller days and years. A single conversation might last what we would call weeks, ideas and responses flowing as slowly and inevitably as the growth of stalactites.

They are not numerous, but they are ancient and wise in ways that surface-dwellers can barely comprehend, these patient shapers of stone who have made their peace with time itself.
