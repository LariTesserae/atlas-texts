In this eternal twilight dwell the Umbral Folk - a people as much shaped by their environment as they have shaped it. Their most striking feature is their eyes: large, liquid-dark orbs that reflect the bioluminescent light like mirrors, with pupils that expand and contract in subtle gradations rather than simple dilation. These eyes can detect minute variations in the perpetual dimness, reading the shifting purple-slate darkness as clearly as surface-dwellers read full daylight.

Their skin is remarkably pale but has a peculiar translucent quality, with faint patterns that echo the veining in marble. These patterns shift subtly with their moods and health, serving as a form of non-verbal communication among them. Their bodies are lean and tall, with long fingers adapted for precise work in low light conditions. Their movements are deliberate and graceful, having evolved in a vertical environment where a misstep could be fatal.

The Umbral Folk have developed a complex society structured around the management of water and light. Their highest social class consists of the Moss-Tenders, who cultivate and maintain the bioluminescent patterns that give life and direction to the city. These practitioners combine botanical knowledge with artistic vision, creating living light sculptures that serve both practical and aesthetic purposes.

They speak in soft, melodic tones that carry well through the moisture-laden air without disrupting the city's natural acoustics. Their language includes subtle variations in breath and whistle that mimic the omnipresent sounds of water and wings. Much of their communication is non-verbal, expressed through those shifting skin patterns and precise gestures that catch and reflect the moss-light.

Their technology focuses on working with rather than against their environment. They are master hydroengineers, understanding intimately how water moves through stone. Their steam engines are not the clattering, aggressive machines of the surface world, but quiet devices that capture and redirect the mountain's natural heat and moisture. They forge metals using geothermal heat, creating tools and machines that seem to grow from the rock itself.

The Umbral Folk maintain a complex relationship with the city's other inhabitants. They leave offerings for the cave swallows, whose movements help predict subtle changes in air pressure and temperature. They cultivate the blind salamanders in specially designed pools, reading the creatures' behavior to monitor water quality. The moth-folk, as they call the giant moths, are considered sacred messengers, their wing patterns studied for omens.

Their children are born with eyes closed, opening them gradually over their first month of life as they adjust to the eternal twilight. They learn to climb before they walk, developing an innate understanding of their vertical world. From early age, they are taught to read the subtle languages of their environment - the whispers of water, the patterns of moss-light, the movements of air through the city's vast spaces.

They mark time not by days but by the subtle shifts in the quality of darkness, and by the behaviors of the various creatures that share their realm. Their calendar follows the breeding cycles of the cave swallows and the blooming patterns of the leather-leafed vines.

The Umbral Folk are not numerous, but they are long-lived and deeply connected to their environment. They view themselves not as the city's rulers but as its caretakers, maintaining the delicate balance that allows this unique ecosystem to persist. They have developed a philosophy that sees darkness not as the absence of light, but as a presence in itself - something to be understood, respected, and worked with rather than against.

In their minds, they are not people who live in darkness, but people who live in harmony with it. The eternal twilight is not a limitation but their natural state, and they view the harsh light of the surface world with a mixture of fascination and mild horror. They are the custodians of this twilight realm, the keepers of its rhythms, the readers of its shadows - and they would have it no other way.
