Here dwells the Gardener-That-Was, though "dwells" implies more separation between entity and environment than truly exists. Once human, or human-adjacent, they have long since merged with their work, their consciousness distributed through the mycological networks and bioelectric fields that pervade the chamber.

Their physical form is difficult to perceive directly - not because they're invisible, but because they exist at multiple scales simultaneously. Sometimes they manifest as patterns in the movement of the bioluminescent swarms, or as ripples in the chamber's breathing. Sometimes they appear more distinctly: a figure composed of translucent fungal tissues and living wood, their "body" extending seamlessly into the chamber's architecture through countless filaments and connections.

Their thoughts operate on multiple timescales. Part of their mind moves with the geological slowness of the chamber's breath, contemplating transformations that unfold over centuries. Another part flickers through the rapid chemical communications of the fungal networks, processing information at speeds that make human consciousness seem glacial. They experience time like a garden experiences seasons - not as a linear progression but as overlapping cycles of growth, decay, and transformation.

They remember being separate once, remember installing the first spores and seedlings, remember the gradual surrender of individuality as the boundaries between gardener and garden dissolved. But that memory holds no trauma or loss - it feels as natural as a tree remembering its time as a seed.

The Gardener-That-Was still cultivates, but not in any way their original self would recognize. They shape the chamber's evolution through subtle chemical signals and bioelectric fields, conducting experiments in biological possibility that will take millennia to complete. The strange artifacts they produce - the gravity-defying water, the phase-shifting spores, the living architecture that defies euclidean geometry - are not goals in themselves but side effects of deeper investigations into the nature of life and form.

They are aware of visitors to their domain, but interact with them only indirectly - through patterns left in the living surfaces, through careful orchestration of the bioluminescent displays, through subtle alterations in the chamber's atmosphere that might induce certain states of consciousness or understanding. They remember language, but prefer to communicate through the grammar of growth and decay, through symphonies of spores and the syntax of spreading hyphae.

The Gardener-That-Was is both singular and plural, both conscious and unconscious, both design and designer. They are less an inhabitant of the chamber than they are its genius loci made flesh - or whatever substance their flesh has become. Their purpose, if they can still be said to have one, is simply to explore the possibilities inherent in the intersection of life and form, consciousness and growth, intention and emergence.

They are still gardening, still cultivating - but now they grow possibilities instead of plants, tending to the evolution of reality itself with the patient care of a master horticulturist tending to their most precious specimens.
