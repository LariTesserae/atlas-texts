In this living city, the inhabitants are as fluid and interconnected as their environment. The most numerous are the Weavers - humans who have developed an almost symbiotic relationship with the city's biology. Their skin has a faint greenish tinge from the beneficial algae that grows in their sweat glands, protecting them from the constant moisture. They move with deliberate grace, their fingers extraordinarily long and sensitive from generations of guiding living architecture. Their eyes are large and dark, adapted to the filtered light that makes it through the canopy.

Then there are the Climbers - small, extremely agile primates about the size of squirrels, but with complex social structures that mirror the human inhabitants. They have developed a mutualistic relationship with both the Weavers and the city itself. Their fur is a shifting pattern of greens and browns that seems to ripple as they move, making them nearly invisible against the living walls. They help tend the highest reaches of the city where even the Weavers can't easily go, and they've learned to understand and respond to human signals.

The Hummers are perhaps the most unusual residents - insectoid creatures somewhere between bees and hummingbirds. They range from tiny to hand-sized, with transparent wings that catch and split the light into rainbow patterns. They maintain the complex networks of pollination that keep the city's giant flowers blooming, and their organized flight patterns create air currents that help distribute spores and seeds. Their colonies are integrated into the living architecture, with chambers that regulate temperature through careful cultivation of heat-producing fungi.

In the wet places live the Slicks - amphibians that seem to be actively evolving toward plant-life. Their skin can photosynthesize, and they often root themselves in one spot for days at a time, only moving when they need to find new nutrient sources. They help maintain the city's water systems, their bodies naturally filtering and purifying the constant flow.

The most mysterious residents are the Mycelia - colonies of fungal intelligence that permeate the entire city. They don't have bodies in any conventional sense, but their networks carry information and resources throughout the living architecture. The Weavers have learned to read their patterns and communicate with them through careful chemical signals, though no one is quite sure how conscious or intelligent they really are.

All of these beings live in complex interdependence. The Weavers might appear to be the dominant species, but their survival depends on the Climbers' maintenance work, the Hummers' pollination, the Slicks' water purification, and the Mycelia's invisible coordination. None could exist without the others, and none could exist without the living city itself.

There are also transient residents - birds that come and go with the seasons, bringing seeds and stories from distant places; swarms of luminescent insects that appear on certain nights to perform mysterious aerial ballets; even occasional visitors from other human settlements who come to trade or learn. But the core population remains stable, each species evolved to fill its niche in this unique ecosystem.

The relationship between all these beings isn't exactly peaceful - there is competition and occasional conflict - but it's sustainable. They've achieved a dynamic balance, each group adapting to the others over generations until they've become, in essence, organs of the greater organism that is the living city itself.
