In this living laboratory, the primary inhabitants are the Meridians - a species that arose from the merger of the greenhouse's original monitoring instruments and the opportunistic life forms that colonized them. They appear at first glance to be iridescent arthropods, their segmented bodies ranging from thumbnail to palm-sized, with brass-like carapaces that catch and reflect the filtered light. But closer inspection reveals their hybrid nature - their legs end in precisely calibrated measuring implements, their antennae are fine copper filaments that sense temperature and pressure, and their compound eyes seem to display ever-changing numerical readings.

The Meridians maintain a complex society centered around the old control station, which they've transformed into a living computational organ. They move in synchronized patterns, adjusting ancient dials with their gauge-tipped appendages, transferring data through a combination of mechanical interaction and biochemical exchanges. Their bodies serve as mobile sensors, collecting information about every aspect of their environment - humidity, temperature, soil composition, light levels - which they feed back into their central system.

They share their domain with the Photosynthetic Primates, descended from escaped laboratory specimens. These creatures, no larger than harvest mice, have developed a symbolic relationship with both the Meridians and the bioluminescent fungi they cultivate. Their fur has a greenish tinge and contains chlorophyll-like compounds, allowing them to supplement their nutrition through photosynthesis. They communicate through patterns of gentle light pulses, coordinating their activities with the Meridians' data collection routines.

In the higher reaches dwell the Apparatus Birds, whose evolution has been shaped by the greenhouse's unique conditions. Their feathers incorporate metallic elements scavenged from the structure, creating living solar collectors that power their unusual metabolism. They build nests that function as small-scale atmospheric processors, contributing to the greenhouse's self-regulation.

The deepest layers of the composted floor host the Metric Mycelium, a vast network of fungal threads that have incorporated the greenhouse's old monitoring systems into their structure. This living network processes and stores information about the ecosystem's history, creating a biological archive that the Meridians regularly consult through specialized interface points where mechanical and fungal systems merge.

Perhaps most remarkably, all these species participate in what appears to be a collective experiment that has continued long after its human designers departed. They maintain environmental conditions with precise care, though the parameters they're working toward remain mysterious to outside observers. The entire ecosystem seems to be slowly evolving toward some unknown but carefully measured outcome, with each species playing its role in an experimental protocol that has taken on a life of its own.

The Meridians appear to be the coordinators of this ongoing research, but they're not simply maintaining old systems - they're advancing and adapting the original experiment in ways that suggest genuine scientific inquiry. Whether they understand the nature of their work in terms we would recognize as consciousness remains a matter of debate among the few researchers who have studied this unique ecosystem.
