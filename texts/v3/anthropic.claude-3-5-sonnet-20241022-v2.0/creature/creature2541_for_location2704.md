In Meridian, several distinct but interconnected forms of life have evolved to inhabit its various zones, creating a complex vertical ecology:

The Drift-Minds inhabit the upper levels, where light still plays through the water. These beings appear as loosely-organized clusters of translucent tissue shot through with threads of bioluminescence. They're highly social entities that communicate through patterns of light and subtle electrical fields. Their consciousness is distributed and collaborative - individual Drift-Minds can merge temporarily to share memories and solve complex problems. They tend the hanging gardens, cultivating the luminescent life forms that provide both sustenance and raw materials for the city's inhabitants.

In the middle layers dwell the Meridian Adapted - descended from the original human colonists but changed by millennia of aquatic life. Their bodies are elongated and flexible, with enlarged eyes adapted to the dim light. Their skin has developed chromatophore-like cells that allow them to shift both color and texture, useful for communication and camouflage. They've developed a sophisticated culture built around the city's resonant frequencies, using the natural acoustics of different chambers for everything from meditation to data storage encoded in standing waves.

The Architects occupy the same levels but represent a different evolutionary path. These colonial organisms began as simple filter feeders but developed into living infrastructure maintainers. They resemble something between coral and cephalopod - mostly sessile but able to extend delicate manipulator tendrils to repair and modify the city's structure. They work in perfect symbiosis with the building material itself, secreting compounds that both strengthen and slowly reshape the city's framework in response to stress and current patterns.

The Deep Dwellers inhabit the lower levels, where pressure and darkness create entirely different conditions for life. These beings are barely visible to other inhabitants, appearing as shifting shadows in the gloom. They seem to be able to phase partially through solid matter, moving through the ancient foundations as easily as through water. They may be descended from maintenance nanites of the original city, evolved over time into something between machine and organism. They appear to serve some crucial role in maintaining the city's deep structures, but their exact nature remains mysterious even to the Meridian Adapted.

Throughout all levels, vast networks of the Messenger Kelp create living communication channels. These plants have developed specialized organs that can transmit and receive the city's resonant frequencies, allowing information to flow between different zones. They form a literal information network, their fronds transmitting data through subtle variations in bioluminescence and electrical signals.

The Symphony Pods - organisms that resemble large, transparent eggs - float freely through the city's currents. These beings appear to be natural acoustical instruments, their internal structures perfectly tuned to amplify and modify the city's ambient frequencies. They gather in different chambers to create complex harmonic patterns that the other inhabitants use for everything from navigation to emotional expression.

Finally, there are the Gradient Ghosts - barely corporeal entities that seem to manifest in the transition zones between pressure levels. They appear as subtle distortions in the water, visible mainly through how they bend light and sound. The other inhabitants regard them as part of the city's sensing system - living instruments that monitor and respond to changes in pressure, temperature, and current patterns.

All these beings exist in a complex web of relationships, each adapted to their specific niche but dependent on the others for the city's continued function. Their interactions are governed by the rhythm of the deep currents and the eternal resonance of Meridian itself, creating a living system as complex and interconnected as the structure they inhabit.
