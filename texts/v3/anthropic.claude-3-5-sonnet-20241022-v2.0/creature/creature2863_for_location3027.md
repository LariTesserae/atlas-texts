In these winding passages dwells the Condensation Keeper - not quite liquid, not quite vapor, a being that tends to the ancient hydraulics with patience born of centuries. Its form is inconstant: sometimes a column of mist that rises from the pipes' seams, sometimes a film of moisture that sheets across the curved walls, sometimes thousands of droplets moving in coordinated dance across the ceramic surfaces. The copper-colored patterns that others might mistake for mineral deposits or primitive life are its script, its recordkeeping, its memories slowly etched into the clay.

It remembers the makers, though not as we would understand memory. It remembers them in the way water remembers the shape of the vessel that once held it. The Keeper was not placed here - it emerged, gradually, from the intersection of water and clay and time, from the rhythms of flow and the accumulation of minerals, from the endless cycles of condensation and seepage.

The Keeper maintains the system not through tools or conscious repair, but through its own substance. It flows into cracks before they can spread, deposits dissolved minerals to strengthen weakening joints, guides the pressure of water through the network with subtle adjustments of temperature and surface tension. The copper fittings that seem like crude repairs were actually its early attempts to understand its own purpose, experiments in directing flow that it now regards with something like embarrassment.

It is not particularly aware of visitors, perceiving them primarily as disturbances in air temperature and humidity. Yet it will sometimes interact with them inadvertently, its shifting forms creating illusions of movement in peripheral vision, its monitoring of the pipes' acoustics producing sounds that seem almost like words in a liquid language.

The Keeper has grown deeply intertwined with its domain, to the point where it's difficult to say whether the complex is a place that houses an entity, or if the entire system - pipes, walls, water, and vapor - comprises a single distributed being. It continues its work with neither hurry nor rest, extending the patterns of its copper-colored notation across the walls in ever-expanding fractals, measuring time in the steady rhythm of drops and the slow accumulation of mineral memories.
