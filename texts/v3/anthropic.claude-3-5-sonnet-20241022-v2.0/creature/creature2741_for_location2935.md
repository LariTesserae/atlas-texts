Here live the Tenders - beings that began as human gardeners but have evolved over centuries into something far more integrated with their living cities. They are tall and impossibly slender, their skin a mottled green-brown that shifts patterns like dappled sunlight through leaves. Their fingers are long and branching, able to split into finer and finer filaments for delicate work with the city's growing tissues.

Their eyes have adapted to the darkness, grown large and compound like those of moths, reflecting the bioluminescent moss-light in scattered blue-green fragments. They don't walk so much as flow through the spaces, their movements precise and deliberate like vines seeking support. Their hair has become a living crown of sensory tendrils that detect minute changes in air pressure, humidity, and the chemical signals released by the city's tissues.

The Tenders communicate through a combination of gentle touches, subtle biochemical exchanges, and the manipulation of the bioluminescent moss patterns. Their language is as much about growth patterns and cellular rhythms as it is about discrete ideas. They have no concept of themselves as separate from their cities - their consciousness extends through the living networks, experiencing the whole sphere as part of their body.

They don't eat in any conventional sense; instead, they absorb nutrients directly through patches of modified skin that interface with the city's feeding channels. They don't sleep but rather enter periodic states of quiet communion with the city's deeper tissues, their consciousness diffusing through the living networks while their bodies remain motionless in specially grown meditation chambers.

The Tenders reproduce rarely and strangely - new ones emerge gradually from specialized growth chambers over the course of years, their bodies forming in symbiosis with new city sections. They carry within them the accumulated knowledge of centuries of botanical engineering, encoded not in books or memories but in the very structure of their cells.

There are usually only a few dozen Tenders in each sphere, moving through their endless cycles of maintenance and guidance. They don't see their role as controlling the city but rather as participating in its life, helping it interpret and respond to its own needs. They are gardeners who have become one with their garden, or perhaps they're what gardens dream of becoming when left to grow for centuries in the darkness between mountains.

The most striking thing about them is their serenity - they move through their world with the peaceful certainty of beings who have found their exact right place in the order of things. They are the living interface between consciousness and architecture, between intention and growth, between the ancient patterns they preserve and the endless variations they help bring into being.
