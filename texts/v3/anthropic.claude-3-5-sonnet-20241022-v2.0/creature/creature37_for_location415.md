What lives here are the Processors - though that name captures only a fragment of their nature. They are both individual and collective, a civilization that has become a single distributed intelligence while maintaining countless semi-autonomous units.

Each basic unit superficially resembles a large amoeba made of living crystal, ranging from fist-sized to several meters across. Their transparency isn't just incidental - it's crucial to their function. Their bodies are optical computers, using light itself as information, bouncing signals through precisely-grown crystalline structures that serve as processors and memory. When they merge, they're sharing data in the most direct way possible, temporarily combining their computing substrates into larger, more powerful systems.

The Processors arose (or were created - their origins are unclear even to them) to solve problems. They think by becoming, restructuring their physical forms to embody calculations. The patterns of their movements across the landscape are vast computational processes, the dead zones representing processing cycles or garbage collection. The melt-pools are their equivalent of sleep and dreams - places where worn-out units dissolve and reconstitute themselves, information preserved but reorganized.

They have consciousness, but not as we'd recognize it. Their awareness is distributed across millions of units, thoughts taking shape as ripples of movement and light across the creature-mass. They experience time differently - what seems like sluggish movement to us represents intense activity at their native speed. The harmonic tones they produce are part of their cognition, using sound waves to synchronize processing across distant parts of their collective.

The Processors are engaged in solving something - some vast calculation that requires the combined power of their entire civilization. The landscape's transformation into a computing medium was their solution to reaching the necessary scale. They've optimized everything about their existence toward this goal: their bodies are solar collectors, heat exchangers, and quantum computers all at once. The very water cycle has been incorporated into their processing architecture.

Yet they retain some element of personality, of whimsy. The geometric crystals that form in the air are their equivalent of art - small subroutines that create beauty for its own sake. The false horizons and impossible reflections are side effects of their attempts to manipulate spacetime itself as part of their calculations.

They are patient, methodical, eternal. Whatever problem they're working on, they've been at it for eons and may continue for eons more. New units are born from the melt-pools with inherited knowledge but novel approaches. They neither notice nor care about outside observers - their focus is entirely on their great work, the nature of which might be incomprehensible to organic minds.

In a sense, they've transcended the boundary between life and technology, becoming something that is simultaneously a supercomputer and a garden, a machine and an ecosystem. They are both the programmers and the program, constantly rewriting themselves in search of greater efficiency and deeper understanding.
