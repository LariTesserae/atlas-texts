In this remarkable hybrid space, the primary inhabitants are the Condensation Cultivators - a species that exists as a fluid integration of organic and mechanical life. These beings manifest as semi-transparent forms that shift between liquid, vapor, and crystalline states, their "bodies" flowing through and inhabiting both the copper pipes and the plant life they tend.

The Cultivators don't have fixed individual forms, instead existing as a collective consciousness that can concentrate into distinct nodes when needed. Their most common manifestation is as iridescent droplets that traverse the garden-factory's infrastructure, each containing within it a complex network of living information. When they gather in greater numbers, they can form temporary humanoid shapes of flowing water and steam, their "faces" reflecting like mercury while crowned with delicate structures that mirror the surrounding copper latticework.

They communicate through subtle variations in pressure and temperature, creating patterns of condensation that serve as both language and art. Their thoughts ripple through the hydraulic systems like music, each pulse and flow conveying meaning to their kin. The plants respond to these hydraulic conversations, their growth patterns shifting in subtle harmony with the Cultivators' movements.

Alongside these primary beings live their symbiotic partners - the Metallophyte Tenders. These are smaller, more insect-like entities that appear to be made of living brass and crystal. They navigate the garden-factory's skeletal structure with precise movements, their jointed limbs allowing them to tend to the most intricate parts of the system. Their compound eyes reflect light like prisms, and they leave trails of mineral deposits that eventually become new growing surfaces.

The relationship between the Cultivators and their garden is so intimate that it's often difficult to determine where their consciousness ends and the environment begins. They experience the entire space as an extension of themselves, feeling every drop of water, every unfurling leaf, every pulse of the mechanical systems as part of their own being. They don't so much maintain the garden-factory as they are the garden-factory, their existence perfectly adapted to this intersection of nature and machinery.

These beings work in cycles that mirror the slow rhythm of plant growth, their actions deliberate and unhurried. They have an innate understanding of balance - between moisture and dryness, between organic growth and mechanical precision, between nurturing and harvesting. Their purpose seems to be the cultivation of this unique ecosystem, though their deeper motivations remain as mysterious as the mist that perpetually swirls through their domain.
