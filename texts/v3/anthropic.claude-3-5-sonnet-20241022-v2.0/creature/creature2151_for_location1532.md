In this impossible city live the Glass Dancers - humans who have adapted so completely to their volatile environment that they've become something subtly other. Their skin has a faint translucent quality, as if generations of exposure to obsidian dust has worked its way into their very cells. They move with an unconscious grace, their bodies constantly making micro-adjustments to the endless tremors and shifts.

Their eyes are their most striking feature - black as the glass they live among, with a subtle internal fractal pattern that seems to shift when caught in certain lights. These eyes can read the stress patterns in the obsidian towers, seeing the flow of geological forces as clearly as we might see water moving in a stream. The oldest Glass Dancers have eyes that seem to contain entire landscapes.

They live in small, tight-knit family groups, each maintaining their own section of the safety-rope network that makes navigation possible. Children learn to read and tie the knot-language before they learn to speak, their small fingers dancing through complex patterns that describe safe passages, growing zones, and the territories of various Lithophage species. They can feel the larger stone-eaters moving through their territory by pressing their foreheads against the glass surfaces - a practice that leaves permanent mark-points on their skin, like living maps.

The Glass Dancers have developed a complex symbiosis with the smaller Lithophages, particularly the tunnel-boring species. They cultivate certain varieties in their homes, directing their feeding patterns to create living spaces within the towers. Their relationship with the smoke-flowers is equally intricate - they wear the blooms in their hair and weave the metal-leaves into their clothing, using the plants' sensitivity to geological energies as an early warning system.

Their culture is built around adaptation rather than permanence. They have no written language because nothing stays still long enough to write on. Instead, they maintain their history and knowledge through intricate dances that mirror the movement of the city itself. These performances are both art and survival manual, teaching new generations how to read and move with the endless changes in their environment.

The Glass Dancers practice a form of meditation that involves synchronizing their breathing with the deep frequencies that pulse through the plateau. They claim this allows them to communicate with the city itself, though whether this is literal or metaphorical is hard to determine. What's certain is their uncanny ability to predict and flow with the city's transformations.

They are not unfriendly to outsiders, but their way of thinking can be difficult for others to grasp. They view time as a solid thing, like their glass mountains - something that can be shaped and flowed around. Their language has no future or past tense, only various words for different rates of change. They find the concept of "permanent" buildings amusing, and the idea of trying to prevent change rather than adapt to it somewhat horrifying.

The oldest among them sometimes speak of the plateau itself as a living thing, and themselves as something between parasites and symbiotes - not controlling their environment, but participating in its endless dance of transformation. Some show signs of further physical adaptation: hair that grows in crystalline patterns, skin that reflects light like polished obsidian, voices that resonate perfectly with the city's deep harmonics.

They are becoming something new, these people, just as their city is eternally becoming something new. Each generation is a little further from their original human stock, a little more attuned to their impossible home. They don't seem to mind this gradual transformation. As they say in their fluid language: change is the only constant, and to resist it is to choose death over dance.
