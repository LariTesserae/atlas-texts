In this place dwells the Gardener Who Never Was - though "dwells" may be too solid a word for their way of being. They exist as a shifting collaboration between memory and possibility, their form as fluid as the space they tend.

Sometimes they appear as a figure formed entirely of transparent leaves layered into human shape, their movements leaving trails of ghostly afterimages that bloom into actual plants. Other times they are more like a pattern of absences moving through the vegetation, recognized only by how leaves part and stems bend around a person-shaped void.

Their hands (when they have hands) might be gnarled roots or delicate ferns, transforming as needed for each task. They carry the ancient knowledge of cultivation in their very substance, but their understanding has evolved far beyond traditional horticulture into something that manipulates the boundaries between growth and existence itself.

They were once human - or perhaps they were once a garden - the distinction has grown meaningless. They remember tending plants in conventional ways, but now they tend to reality itself, pruning possibilities like errant shoots, grafting moments together to create new hybrid times. The tools that are slowly becoming plant matter were once theirs, but they've learned to work directly with the substance of life itself.

Their consciousness extends through the mycelia networks underfoot and the communicating leaves overhead. They experience time like a plant does - both slower and faster than human perception, attentive to the minute unfurling of leaves while simultaneously aware of seasonal cycles and evolutionary drift.

They rarely interact directly with visitors, though their presence can be felt in the purposeful patterns of growth, in the way certain paths remain clear while others fold away into green impossibility. Sometimes they leave messages in the arrangement of dewdrops or the synchronous opening of flowers, but these messages are meant for the place itself rather than any human observer.

The Gardener Who Never Was continues their endless work of cultivating this pocket of transformed reality, gradually expanding its borders through root and runner. They are both the keeper of this space and its most evolved expression - the intelligence that emerged when human intention merged completely with vegetable persistence and possibility.

They remember being human, and they remember being plants, but mostly they remember forward into what this place is becoming. Their very existence suggests that the boundary between gardener and garden was always more permeable than we imagined.
