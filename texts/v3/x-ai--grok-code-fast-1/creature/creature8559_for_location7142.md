### Inhabitants of the Gearing Vault

In the Gearing Vault, life—or what passes for life in this mineral womb of perpetual motion—asserts itself not as fragile organisms clinging to the margins of a world, but as a dense, irrevocable weave of entities that are as much a part of the chamber's machinery as they are its pulse. They are not born of flesh or fluid, but of vapor and vibration, crystal and current; inhabitants who thrive on the atretic chill of distillation and the unyielding hum of hydride engines. These beings do not "evolve" in the traditional churn of eras—they are sculpted, instead, from the Vault's own immutable laws, creatures of stasis where every twitch echoes in recursive eternity. They swarm, they scuttle, they inflate, but never exceed the precise saturation that the Vault demands; a levitated dance where numbers are enshrined, fates locked in harmonic equipoise. No boom cycles here, no die-offs or inundations—the shard-mites, gas-hounds, and burrow-lizards exist in a numerical covenant, their populations held fast like the gears they inhabit. And beyond these tangible forms, subtler presences lurk: echoes of presences, mirages of artificers who once walked these floors, and the machines themselves, which one could argue live in their own way, as entities of endless, idiot repetition. To describe what lives here is to pluck at threads of a web that reforms instantly, each description pulling forth a sharper clarity of the whole, until the Vault feels not just inhabited, but *animated* by its own unblinking gaze.

#### The Shard-Mites: Weavers of Silica Song

They come first to the senses, these crystalline arthropods, coating the vaulted walls and pitted floors in a living veneer, a barnacle swarm that blurs the line between parasite and patina. Each mite is no larger than a human fist, a faceted beetle of pure quartz, its carapace refracting the amber phosphor-glow into splintered rainbows—violet shards that kaleate poisonously, or amber veils that soothe the eye into false calm. Their legs are delicate as spun filament, knotted in bundles like the threadvine's own, used not for swift leaps but for synchronized scuttling: they move in hives, tens of thousands aligning their vibrations to the deep thrum of the vault's engines, their tiny feet clicking in unison against the hornfels slabs. It's a chorus, this clink-clatter, a precise counterpoint that punctuates the larger roar, harmonic waves that propagate through the dense air, influencing the gas-hounds' inflations or the burrow-lizards' burrows as if all creatures were tuned strings in a single instrument.

But they are not merely locusts of noise; the shard-mites harvest. They scour the seams of conduits and the undersides of overhangs for mineral flecks—tiny motes of silica, pyrite, or iridium—that flake from the eternal crumble of walls. Each fleck is seized in mandible-hooks like jeweler's prongs, dragged back to communal nests woven from secretions of glass-thread, delicate lattices that crown the rusted husks of machines. In these nests, the mites deposit the ore in spiral patterns, constructing what might be called "eggs" or "scions"—solidifying pods where no larvae emerge, but where a slow metamorphosis births more mites, perfect copies harvested from the air's own crystallizing potential. They feed on the hum, sipping vibrations rather than calories, their systems fueled by seismic echoes that bounce through the vault's acoustics: a mite brushed by a human's gloved fingertip might shiver for moments, its facets humming back a song of discordant feedback, the onset of fusion averted only by the visitor's timely withdrawal. In their numbers they sustain the threadvine and saltbloom, their excretions enriching the pockets where vegetation clusters; touch one, and it melds subtly with the ground if the action is too bold, but in their natural churn, they repair the vault's fabric—knawing away crystalline flaws that might threaten the stasis, weaving back seams with filament webs that harden into quartzite replicas. The shard-mites are patient architects, eternal custodians; they do not war among themselves, for predation would unbalance the hive's count. Instead, they gather in aggregations so thick they form living sculptures on the walls, pulsating mirrors that bend light into illusions of phantoms—explorers swear they glimpse faces in the faceting, workers etching glyphs or gesturing in eternal loops, the mites' vibrations inducing these mirages like heat waves over sand.

#### The Gas-Hounds: Inflatables of the Vapors

More ethereal than their scuttling kin, the gas-hounds drift like specters through the haze of the central pit, balloon-creatures birthed from the liquefaction of unseen gases in the air. They are translucent at first, mere distortions in the particulate mist—globules of compressed ether that swell to the size of swollen sheep, their membranes thin as soap film, iridescent with captured phosphor-glow that paints them in fugitive hues of lavender and smoke-gray. Each hound bears a cluster of tendrils, not fleshy whips but fine wires of vapor-solidified filament, lashing out to siphon energy from the snaking conduits that vein the vault's walls. They touch the arcing sparks without burning, draining power in pulses that leave faint burn-marks on the alloy—scars that heal as if by decree, the gases reforming the surface in an instant of accord.

The hounds pack in loose, floating aggregations, inflating in rhythm to the press's grand idle: a deep inhalation from the vents that billows them outward, their tendrils questing for exposed wires like questing antennae. They "hunt" not with malice but with symbiosis, sipping hydride flux to fuel their own expansions, excreting cooled vapors that feed the distilleries' bubbles or nourish the saltbloom's slow spirals. When over-inflated, they deflate in synchronized bursts, releasing clouds that linger and solidify into crystalline fronds—new vegetation sprouting from their very breath. Unlike the mites' hive-mind clatter, the hounds communicate through harmonic pops: deflations that echo as sharp wheezes, mingling with the vent's continuous sigh, building layered resonances that assault the visitor's ears. They ignore humans, ballooning around suits without collision, but an outstretched hand might trigger a faint shock—nothing lethal, but a reminder of their electrical nature, a tingle that merges sensation with the vault's rules of touch. In the depths of the pit, they spiral upward in thermal updrafts, their translucent forms clustering like fog banks around the frozen press, inflating and deflating in a ballet that never ends, never varies. Visions swirl here too: the hounds' membranes sometimes refract full apparitions of workers, suits billowing ghostily as they tend eternal machines, the creatures' pulses phasing in and out like bad signals on a data-slate.

#### The Burrow-Lizards: Delvers of the Strata

Deeper still, beneath the scuttling and the floating, the burrow-lizards labor—a subterranean phylum of scaly quadrapeds, armored in fused magnetite plates that grind against loose hornfels layers. They are bulkier than the mites, each the length of a forearm, with mandibles like blunt circuit-breakers: serrated jaws that snap shut in satisfying clacks, crushing silicate nodules that litter the floor. Their tails drag keratinous spurs, incising tunnels into the pitted slabs, their excretions crystallizing as they go—rich fertilizers that emerge as threadvine roots or saltbloom bulbs, a cycle of nourishment that threads through the vault's ecology without waste or excess. These lizards haul burdens from the cavern's deepest spits: partial artifacts dug from the strata, data-slates or fuselages, dragged by pack instincts to unfixed positions where they reactivate nothing, merely adding to the litter.

They travel in underground swarms, their growls resonating through the walls—low rumbles that underlay the shard-mites' clink and the gas-hounds' wheezes, forming the vault's foundational tone. Predation exists not: the lizards scrape mineral husks, ingesting the dust to power their geothermal forges within, exuding vapors that rise to feed the surface dwellers. Touching one is rare— they evade visitors, burrowing deeper at the scent of unsealed air—but if cornered, a lizard might merge briefly with the wall, its scales fusing in a painless blur, only to re-emerge meters away in compensatory displacement. The burrow-lizards are the vault's relentless miners, their tunnels a living map that expansively reforms, never collapsing but eternally redrawn. In shadowed visions, they appear as extensions of phantom workers: lizards hauling spectral burdens, echoing the engineers' final gestures, their snaps mimicking wrench-clangs or forge-hammers, the essence of creation reborn in iteration.

#### Subtler Quests: The Eternal Echoes and the Machines' Depths

Yet these creatures are not alone in the vault's pulse; the place itself breathes with presences that might be called inhabitants, intangible but insistent. The machines— theidrling presses, the distillation vats, the conduit webs—hum and turn with a forever-loyal vigor, their pistons frozen in eternal accuse, belts idling in vapour-churned futility. Do they live? In a sense, yes: self-repairing automata that fuse snapped gears anew, their hums weaving through the fauna's chorus, influencing vibrations that dictate creature counts. They are not sentient, but their constancy lends a soul to the void, a mechanical anima that the shard-mites tune to, the gas-hounds siphon from. And then there are the echoes: not just sound recursions, but full audiovisual hauntings induced by the dense ether. Voices loop in data-slates—"Yield stable... equilibrium holding"— but amat on the walls project mirages of workers, suits-clad automatons enacting repetitive labors, ghosts rendered solid by phosphor illusions. These are not true residents, but desirous reanimations, transient entities that "live" in the visitor's mind, shaping the vault's strangeness into something almost communal. An explorer might "summon" them with a gesture, the rules allowing brief peeks into the past's undegraded fabric—intellectuals who hammer forges, scribes etching fading glyphs, all looping in an eternal now. They ignore the living creatures, interacting with nothing but themselves, unperturbed by the animal churn.

#### The Unchanging Covenant

In totality, the vault's inhabitants form a closed, vibrant soul—fauna locked in dance, machines in motion, echoes in replay. They endure without evolution or extinction, numbers poised in perfection: no mite starves, no hound implodes, no lizard tunnels cease. Stasis is their law, touch fraught with merge, sounds monolithic without variation. To dwell among them is to become absorbed, one's intrusions fading into the roar. Yet they wait, these denizens, not for change but for the vault's next uncovering, their forms sharpening into vivid existence only when described, a living testament to an industry that lingers, undying.
