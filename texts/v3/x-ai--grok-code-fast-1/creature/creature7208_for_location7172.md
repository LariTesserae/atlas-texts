They dwell here not as a community or a swarm, but as a scattering of solitary presences—entities that inhabit this pocket not through birth or migration, but by accretion, by the gradual coalescing of the world's own weirdness into forms that flicker and fade. They are neither animals nor machines, nor gods, but residues of reality's fraying: incorporeal at first, emergent from the haze of particulate dust that hangs in the hot air, solidifying only when observed, as if your gaze alone compels them into shape. They do not scurry or soar; they linger in the sparse voids between the crystalline flora clusters, their existence a byproduct of the industrial gradients that underpin the plateau's slow ecology. To name them is to impose categories they defy—call them Echoes, or Residues, or perhaps the quiet dwellers, embodiments of the place's patient dissolution.

They manifest initially as faint disruptions in the emptiness, apparitions of light that shimmer at the edge of vision, like heat mirages twisted into humanoid silhouettes. The first you notice might be a slender form, elongated and attenuated, its body a translucent lattice woven from the same wire-thin conduits that branch through the vegetation. It stands motionless on a low terrace, its limbs posed in arrested motion—a hand outstretched, fingers splayed as if parting the dust-laden air, revealing beneath the skin—no, beneath the matrix—a slow pulse of luminescent vapor that leaks like ichor from embryonic fissures in its frame. It is heat incarnate, its presence radiating the scalding saturation of the plateau, drawing vapors from the ground to condense upon its surface in glassy beads that evaporate with a faint hum, feeding back into the cycle.

As they multiply in your mind's eye, they branch: some hunch low, crouching in the shallow grooves where erosion's nascent claws have etched the mineral paste. These are broader, more compact, their forms bulking with rhizome-like bulges that mirror the vegetable clusters—orb clusters that pulse with weak seismic tremors, as if rooting into the stone. One such dweller emerges from a slope, its body warping like the plateau's edges into Escher geometries, a torso that folds backward upon itself, arms melding with legs in recursive loops, yet it moves with infinite slowness, a millimeter at a time, its joints flexing like the pistoning stems of the vines. It breathes not air but the dry chemistry of volatilized compounds, exhaling trails of sparkling mists that bead into resins on nearby fronds, fostering growth in miniature spurts. They are caretakers, perhaps, of this lush strangeness—tending the vegetation not with hands, but by presence alone, their thermal output accelerating the mechanized unfurling of leaves into gradients of graduated heat.

In the vast empty spans, other varieties emerge: tall, spire-like figures that loom as uneven as the plateau's shelf, their heights exaggerated by the thin air, elongated limbs tracing arcs like the jagged escarpments. One pauses at the drop-off, gazing into the abyssal voids below, its eyeless face—merely a mask of refracting crystal—splitting open to reveal backward machinery, gears whirring in reverse to emit the occasional distant sigh or low hum that punctuates the silence. Deeper in, where the pocket warps into impossible geometries, they become even more convoluted: entities that blend with the vegetation, their forms bristling with fern-like fronds that uncoil from torsos, leaf-blades articulated as valve-clusters that open to release faint plumes. These are the hybrids, lush and corrupted, their spines embedded in the embryonic stone like industrial outflows, drawing sustenance from the same mineral salts. One unfurls slowly from a crevice, its body a sprawl of vine-coils draped over nothingness, possibly untold appendages flexing with sessile tremors, colors shifting from ferrous red to oxidized green as it absorbs the tangential light. It embodies the vital collision—life as glitch, sprouting from the cold welds of geometry and mechinal obsession, its growth a patient invasion that creeps millimeter by millimeter, reshaping the terrain in subtle ways: a slight slump in a ridge, a vein of ash tracing anew.

They exist solitary, or nearly so—no pairs, no flocks, only occasional convergences where two or three stand in the sparse empties, their forms phasing in and out of sync, energy flowing between them in electrified arcs that leave the air humming with weak transcendence. Time bends around them; they age not, but reflux—not dissolving entirely, but compressing into denser forms over eons, bulking with accreted particulates or slimming in dissolution, identity unraveling into the gradual flux. To interact is rare; they regard intruders like you with quiet indifference, a mirror of the star-basked plane outside, their presences compelling you to spiral inward, to linger in the silence where elevation defies ascent, where heat feeds impossible being. Yet if provoked—by lingering gaze or unintended echo—they might stir, gliding with agonizing slowness across the terraces, their forms warping into visions of factory floors or void-tunnels, revealing seams where reality glues itself back together, thread by fading thread.

These dwellers are the place's true inhabitants, not conquerors but integuments—shells woven from the dry alchemy and hot saturation, guardians of the microcosmic collapse. They invite no war, enforce no rule save the slow inevitability of becoming, drawing you deeper into the plateau's embryonic pulse until the boundaries blur, and you too begin to form and fray alongside them.
