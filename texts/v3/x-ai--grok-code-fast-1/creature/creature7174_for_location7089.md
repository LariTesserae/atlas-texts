In this submerged realm, the beings that live here are not creatures of quick action or conquest, but essences woven from the slow accretion of stone and water—half-formed from the geology itself, as if the plateaus exhaled them into existence over epochs. They are the inhabitants of the blooming cities, the custodians of the emergent formations, and they drift through the dim currents like living shadows, patient and undefined at the edges, emerging only in the twilight haze of luminescence. To call them "people" would be to impose human shape on something more fluid, more symbiotic; they are the place itself made ambulatory, breaths of the vents and whispers of the fronds. Yet as one lingers, they resolve into forms that are recognizably alive: elongated, sinuous figures with bodies that blur the line between flesh and the porous stone around them, as if they were carved from the plateaus yet swimming free.

Physically, they are slender and supple, their torsos and limbs tapering into tendrils that fork and curl, resembling the fleshy fronds of the vegetation but animated—populations of fine, whip-like appendages that allow them to navigate the currents with precision, probing crevices or weaving through thickets without effort. Their skin is not a membrane but a porous texture, etched with fractal patterns that mirror the stone’s swirlings, absorbing the faint glow of the plateaus like living bioluminescence. Colors shift subtly: a muted palette of deep blues and greens near the cooler sinks, where icy veins outline their forms, and warmer hues of iridescent purple-red in vented eddies, where mineral seepages stain their bodies with transient flecks that glitter like embedded jewels. They have no eyes as humans know them—senses attuned instead to vibrations, to the pulse of the water and the heartbeat glow of the rock. Perception comes through the tendrils, which quiver in response to disturbances, or through the spores exhaled by the buildings, which settle and illuminate fleeting visions in their minds. No mouths for speech; communication is a low hum transmitted through bone-deep vibrations, or through touch—tendrils entwining in gestures that convey stories of drifting memories, or the communal will that shifts corridors overnight.

Their size varies, but they are generally modest, perhaps twice the length of a human stretched out, folding compactly when at rest in the nooks of stone. Some are bulkier, with broader torsos that mimic boulders, allowing them to anchor themselves against strong currents; others are more ethereal, almost translucent, blending into the dimness until their luminescent veins pulse to life. Reproduction is not a hurried affair but a gradual sharing, akin to mineralization—pairs or small pods emitting spores that drift and settle on plateaus, germinating into outgrowths that mature over seasons, tendrils unfurling slowly as they absorb the warmth of vents. Age is measured not in years but in accretions: older beings bear thicker layers of porous stone-skin, their forms melding further with the geology, until they become almost immobile statues themselves, integrated into the city-scapes, exhaling spores of wisdom or memory to the young.

Behavior is governed by the realm's lethargy; they move with the patience of currents, a single traversal of a plateau taking days or weeks, guided by eddies rather than intent. They are not hunters but gatherers of the rare fauna—those eel-like shapes or winged fish that flutter by are not pursued aggressively but lured with emanations of luminescence or guided into woven vessels made of fibrous tendrils from the vegetation. Predation, when it occurs, unfolds glacially: a being might shadow another for months, closing in with tendrils extended like a slow embrace, absorbing them in a melding that blurs individual identity into communal form. Among their own kind, conflicts are rare and resolved through accretion—rival bands drift together, boundaries eroding over time into shared pods, their stories merging in vibrations that echo through the structures.

Society manifests in the primitive cities, where they form loose collectives, not governed by leaders but by the emergent will of the place. They dwell in the carved chambers, tendrils coiled around pillars or draping from platforms, gathering in eddies for exchanges of mineral-beaded pods—goods floated between terraces via guided currents, where value is intrinsic, tied to the luminescence or the stories embedded in each item. Rituals involve syncing with the structures: during bioluminescent pulses, they align themselves, bodies echoing the algae's glow, fostering cohesion without hierarchy. In times of geological upheaval—when a new plateau thrusts upward—they migrate patiently, spore clouds signaling the shift, rebuilding not with tools but through willed emergence, stones stacking themselves under their influence, vegetation reinforcing cracks.

Interactions with the environment are seamless, symbiotic; they are not apart from it but extensions of it. They breathe the warm, mineral-infused water through their porous skin, exhaling as the buildings do—condensing elements into spores or gelled fluids that bridge chasms for a time. In warmer pockets, they cultivate outgrowths on their own forms, branching tendrils that mimic flora for camouflage or sustenance. Fauna integrates with them too: some beings form bonds with the elongated flutterers, guiding them as mounts or messengers, their luminous wings pulsing in harmony with communal signals. Predators among them or the wild creatures are elusive, haunting the dimmer depths, but they too are of the place—perhaps evolved guardians, ensuring balance in the slow ecology.

As the place births more of itself, these inhabitants evolve alongside, their forms adapting to emergent geology: a new vein of stone might inspire a generation of thicker-skinned dwellers, or a spreading moss-frond could elongate their tendrils further. They carry the realm's identity—emerging from oblivion, not sinking into it, their presence a drift of becoming. To encounter them is to be enveloped, slowly, in the hush of their world: a tendril brushes against you, vibrating with stories of layered depths, plates rising, currents shifting, until you too feel the pulse of something new forming within.
