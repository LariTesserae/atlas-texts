They call themselves the Veinfolk, though the term is an approximation, muttered in the rust-shudders of fern-fronds or exhaled as spore-clouds that linger like memories in the air. No one invented the name; it emerged, perhaps, from the mural chambers where living spores paint and repaint the walls in ever-evolving histories, depicting figures that shift between bipedal forms and creeping vines. To an outsider, stumbling through the rift with chilled skin and the drip of mist in their wake, the Veinfolk might first appear as silhouettes—hunched, mottled shapes swaying in the gloom, indistinguishable from the foliage until they move with deliberate, sap-slow grace. But linger, and the pocket reveals them not as visitors but as its born inhabitants, woven into the very warp of the place, where flesh and frond intermingle so seamlessly that drawing lines between them feels like severing a root.

At the core are the clan-nests, those clustered nodes of habitations where the Veinfolk dwell—not in fixed homes, but in mutable pods of lichen and hollowed stalks that bloom and wither with the cycles. A single nest might harbor a family of twelve, their bodies coiled in communal heaps around a tuber-hearth, limbs entwined like vines for shared warmth against the bone-deep chill. They are bipedal, for the most part, standing at heights that match the low ceilings—elders stooping to rub scarred scalps against the stratum roof, their flesh ridged and veined like old bark, while youths stretch upwards, fingertips brushing drips that fall like benedictions. Skin tones vary: some pale as frost-rime, others mottled in greens and grays from symbiotic algae that cling to folds and creases, photosynthesizing weakly in the scant light. These algae aren't parasites; they are partnerships, gauging the body's temperature and pulsing faint glows when frost creeps near, turning kin into living lanterns in the darkest niches. Eyes are large, adapted to the shadows—wide orbs that catch geode-slime light like pools of ink, iris-less and reflective, with lids that flutter like leaf-edges in the vapor-thick air.

Physiologically, they've grafted themselves to the pocket's weave. The eldest, those wrinkled forms who claim to have "greened" their limbs, speak in creaks and rustles as if their very bones have stolened roots, extending tendrils that graft onto the walls for nutrient sips. A hand might palm a frond, and over days, sap-vessels fuse, allowing a Veinfolk to draw strength from the earth without eating—though they still partake of fungal mats and hunted fauna, gnawing bones or dissolving prey slugs in enzymatic puddles. Reproduction is tied to the blooms: during accelerated seasons, when towering flowers erupt from walls and draw in comet-like pollinators, Veinfolk pair in ritual circles, their bodies entwining with petals that encapsulate spawn, birthing pods that hatch as wriggling, vine-threaded infants. These young emerge with flexible forms, spines like twigs that harden over winters, and from the start, they mimic the fauna—blob-like cilia sprouting from skin to wave in the damp, attracting symbionts that teach the chill's dangers.

Their clothing, if it can be called that, is the pocket itself: shawls spun from dried kelp-fronds, pinned with chert shards, or cloaks of sludge-moss that absorb condensation and rewet themselves. Tools are part of them too—sharpened shale knives holstered in fleshy sheaths that grow from ribs, bone-gouged by hands that bleed sap when cut. They trade with outsiders who squeeze through the rift, offering nets woven from arachnid silk or tubers glowing with heat, but such dealings are rare; the pocket narrows to those who stay, inviting grafts that root the visitor's feet. Society isn't hierarchical like some surface tribes; it's a collective pulse, dictated by the plants' whispers. Family units form ad hoc councils, huddling in blossom-barrow hearths where petals quiver forecasts— "Frost-rise tomorrow; cluster near the boiler-tubers" or "Prey-swarm comes; weave nets from vein-roots." Decisions ripple like sap: a severed vine without replacement causes colic shudders through the whole nest, teaching harmony. Rituals involve huddling, sharing warmth in typhoon-like clusters where breaths intermingle, or tracing mural spores with fingers to alter histories—erasing hunts that went ill, or introducing cycles of alliance with spectral fauna that phase in and out of tangibility.

The Veinfolk aren't numerous; the sparse density means families space out, communicating across voids via elongated moth-wing flyers they raise as companions, bodies affixed with command-knots that coil in air currents like messages. Solitude is common, a quiet negotiation with shadows, where one might wander alleys of brittle kelp, brushing fronds that rustle responses. But when pressed, as in chases from predatory slugs, they form flocks, leaping pools in arcs that mirror the amphibians, skins flashing symbiotic glows. Elders recount origins in threaded stories: perhaps once like the outsiders, they retreated here from a drowning vastness, shrugging off skin to become the pocket's dwellers. Some claim divine parentage from the upheaval itself, born from the earth's wound. Food webs entwine them—harvesting condensation-drapers for puddles that fertilize their vines, or dissolving prey into enzymatic lacquers that vine-digest and recycle. Waste flows back to the substrate; cleanliness is biological obligation, where decay births new life in the cold's lull.

Fauna allies dot their lives, not as pets but as extensions. A blob-entity might cling to an arm, cilia waving hypnotic feeding patterns that soothe cold-numbed senses. Arachnids repair huts, spinning silken bridges stronger when "fed" with warm exhalations. Predatory cycles engage them directly: youths train in open voids, stalking gelatinous ooze-prey with twig-leg legs that mimic arachnid skitters, learning predator's patience. Blooms bring feasts—pollen clouds offering bursts of rare sweetness, where swarms are caught in flaxen nets and shared communally. Yet weirdness lingers; some Veinfolk claim brief symbiote-alliances, merging with phase-fauna that whisper marsh-secrets, rendering them spectral for glooms, able to navigate shadows unseen. Mural chambers serve as libraries and temples, walls rewriting truths: one spore-mural depicts an eternal cycle where Veinfolk hunt "false-veins"—outsiders who threaten harmony with haste, inviting frostbite or severing vines wrongly.

As days pass, a visitor might notice how the Veinfolk deepen the pocket's strangeness—they gesticulate, inviting grafts, coiling vines around limbs like invitations, transforming guests into kin. Leaders emerge as well-rooted elders, their veins pulsing bioluminescent maps of the bog's hidden paths. Children play in alcoves, mimicking moth-streamers or digging boiler-tubers, their laughter a soft chirp like distant echoes. The living tableau pulls inward; none leave unchanged, for rules solidify in adaptation—never rush, listen to petal-quivers, share the weave. The Veinfolk are this place's heart, pumping through roots and rites, inviting eternity in the frost-marred pulse, where to live here is to become the pocket's verdant, whispering self.
