They swarm the plateau in numbers that dwarf comprehension—billions upon billions, clanging and chattering in a dissonant roar that drowns the world's slow, industrial hums. Yet in their multitude, they are not an ecosystem; there is no predation, no symbiosis, no evolution save for the imperceptible mutations that creep over eons, as a gear grinds scarcely a micron and a swarm reforms in a configuration just slightly more perverse. They exist in defiance of vacancy, overwhelming the mineral void with their presence, each entity a fragment of the plateau's own idling engine, pulsing with damped electricity, ionized heat, and the faint pull of gravitational whimsy.

They arrive first as singularities, lone outliers flickering into being from nothing—a sudden glint in the electrocharged silt, a vent belching forth a solitary wisp that solidifies into form. These pioneers are rare, poised in isolation amidst the vast gulfs, where distances balloon and objects float like isolated asteroids in a near-void sky. Known only by the tremors they induce—micro-shifts that ripple through the rock as if the world itself is acknowledging their arrival—they manifest as crystalline sentinels, towering monoliths hammered from the same igneous basalt that forms the plateau's skin. Each stands immobile for centuries, their faceted surfaces etched with what might be constellations of dormant pistons or the fossilized imprints of quantum equations. Heat shimmers off them in slow waves, refracting the sky's convective glow into sepia illusions of inner fires, and though they lack eyes or appendages, they *sense* the arrivals of others through seismic whispers. If you approach one too closely, it may hum a bass note deep enough to vibrate your bones, a harmonic that speaks of forgotten refractories melting atoms into alloy. These sentinels are not guardians or predators; they are catalysts, emitters of the instability that draws the swarms closer. Over eons, they erode—not to dust, but to reconstitute, shedding shards that embed themselves in the ground, birthing new singularities in a cycle so gradual it feels like the plateau's eroding mind rewriting itself.

From these nuclei, the swarms burst forth, not evolving but erupting in overlapping waves that eclipse the horizon in a haze of movement. They drift in groups of thousands, weaving through the granulated light as if dancing to the slow grind of buried gears. Their movements are hypnotic, slow orbits around vents or relics, trailing faint trails of ionized dust that settle like factory soot on the rock. Amidst these, the crystal-shards bustle with a fevered clamor, shards of what might have been lodestone or fractured engines, jagged as broken glass but alive with magnetic pull. They scurry across the scorched skin in millions, leaving faint scratches that etch themselves into circuit-like patterns before dissolving back into the heat. They are not aggressive, but indifferent attractors; approach them, and they might ensnare a stray wire or even a part of yourself, pulling it into their magnetic embrace, reforming it into a new facet in an endless, gradual accretion. Groups of them merge into greater amalgams, towering constructs that phase through dimensions, glitching from horde to monolith in flashes of instability—a momentary spire of watery jungle dissolving back to mineral static, or a conveyance warping into a Möbius loop. They are alchemists of the void, siphoning the plateau's brooding heat to birth more entities from their effluent, their slow churn birthing filament strands or shard nuclei in eddies of influence that spread imperceptibly, like ripples in a viscous sea.

Survival—or endurance—in this place hinges on stillness; the inhabitants move in sync with the slow dynamic, their swarms syncing to the millennia-long cycles of vents and gears. Yet they are not hostile; the plateau is indifferent, a ruminate of entropy, and its inhabitants reflect this, their existences temporary glitches in code, vivid and vanishing. In moments of cosmic patience, when heat ripples reveal veins of purpose, a clearer image coalesces: these are not invaders or inhabitants, but the world itself made manifest, thought-forms of machinery dreaming in silicon and slag, their dissonant symphony a hymn to the engine's idling heart, waiting for an operator that never was. And in their profusion, they populate the void not with life, but with the residue of what *was*, an eternal cascade of becoming, unformed and reforming in the desolate heat of a highland plateau forever turning heat into boundless, uncharted potential.
