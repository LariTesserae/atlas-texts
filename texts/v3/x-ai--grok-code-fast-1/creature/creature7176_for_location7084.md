In the city of Eldridge, perched on those eternally newborn peaks, the inhabitants dwell as extensions of the landscape itself—humanoids whose forms seem hewn from the very shale and basalt that build their homes. They are not the brusque settlers of warmer, mutable worlds but beings molded by the world's frozen equilibrium, their bodies adapted for a life of deliberate immobility and quiet endurance. Their skin, rough-textured like weathered stone, bears veins of lighter quartz that catch the perpetual glow filtering through the clouds, giving them an aspect of living statues, pale and impervious to time's wear. Eyes, wide and reflective, mirror the unchanging light in depthless pools, as if each gaze holds the entirety of the sky's muted luminosity. Limbs are sturdy yet unhurried, muscles toned not for speed or exertion but for the slow traversal of gravel paths or the careful placement of stones in dry-laid walls. Height varies modestly among them, averaging the stature of youths whose growth halted midway, never to burgeon fully; elder figures stoop with a naturally arched posture, as if perpetually bowing to the horizon's insistent pull.

These people move with a slowness that defies urgency, their strides measured like the drip of condensation from fern fronds, each step a ripple in the still pool of time. They gather in sparse clusters near communal pits or along winding paths, not in bustling conversation but in shared silences, where words emerge rarely and softly, echoing the faint rustle of tassel grasses. Communication is minimal, relying on gestures—a hand raised to beckon, a nod to affirm—or the occasional murmured phrase in a dialect woven from simple syllables that repeat in unchanging cadences. They speak of the day’s calm light, the persisting damp, the next brief shower, always in tones that blend query with declaration, as if conversing with the air itself. Infants are cradled with patient care, their cries muffled early by lullabies chanted in repetitive loops, teaching them the virtue of stillness before speech. Elders, indistinguishable by years since none accrue wrinkles or frailty, preside over these quiet assemblies, their knowledge a repository of observed cycles that never evolve—tales of goats clambering the same unyielding rocks, of rains that mist the same depressions into puddles.

Society in Eldridge unfolds in a tapestry of ritualized simplicity, unbound by commerce or hierarchy, for needs here are met without contest. Hunts are not pursuits but participations in nature's stagnant abundance: groups of a dozen or fewer venture into valleys to observe herds of tawny goats, selecting one with a nod of agreement, its slaughter performed as a solemn enactment rather than a kill—blood drawn in slow rivulets that stain the gravel without pooling in excess. Meals, prepared in squat stone huts over wood-fed flames that burn with an even, unconsuming glow, consist of berries plucked from vines in perpetual non-ripeness, ferns steamed in bamboo-woven pots, and fish from river pools, all balanced portions that sustain without diminishing. Tools remain crude—flint blades reshaped by repetition, woven-withe hammers that thump with hollow echoes against basalt slabs—but they serve the primitive economy flawlessly, for nothing breaks in this place, nothing needs replacement. Waste is conveyed to communal pits lined with bamboo, a process as mechanical as breathing, while water is drawn from mirrored pools that occasionally flicker with reflections of elders long since present, blurring past and now into one seamless continuum.

Their nights—if such intervals can be called nights, for the light dims barely and never plunges into darkness—draw gatherings around carved idols of primitive facture, figures etched from local rocks or bone, depicting humanoid forms with spiraling arms or faces split into reflective facets. Rituals unfold with deliberate lethargy: bone flutes blown to emit no sound, yet the act itself summons a palpable silence, as if harmonics vibrate inaudibly through the damp air. Wheeled carts, hewn from twisted evergreens and pulled in eternal circles around central stones, serve no purpose beyond the motion's perpetuity, their wood never splintering, their paths worn into identical spirals that invite onlookers to join, step into the loop without intent. Occasionally, vines grow in lattice patterns that spell out forgotten scripts, and the inhabitants trace them with fingers that linger, heads tilting in unison as if deciphering a wisdom that releases no secrets, only reinforces the world's rules. Anomalies manifest among them too—not as curses or blessings, but as quiet integrations: one figure might awaken with skin that shimmers like a mirrored pool, showing feasible futures in glimpses fleet as mist; another communes with herds of goats that orbit singular stones, mirroring the cart circles, their eyes reflecting the humanoids' own depthless gazes.

Children play in the sparse expanses between habitations, their games echoing the fauna's eternal motions—chasing schools of fish in still ponds without catching them, or mimicking the aerial flutter of iridescent insects whose wings never fully beat. Learning is imparted through observation, not instruction: youths sit for hours watching ferns unfurl in overlapping cycles, or listening to the low murmur of River Étienne, internalizing the lesson that life pulses without progression. Reproduction occurs in a balanced ebb, pairs forming in silent agreements that dissolve with equal calm, births arriving in rhythmic pauses that maintain the population's thousandfold stability. No deaths mar the equilibrium, for decay is arrested; those who weary simply fade into contemplation, their forms melding subtly with the boulders or flowing with the rivers, becoming part of the landscape's unchanging fertility.

Subtly, an undercurrent of weirdness infuses their existence, not as aberration but as the world's essence made intimate. Gravity occasionally flexes uphill during rituals, lifting participants momentarily toward the sky's mists without vertigo; air carries a metallic tang, not of decay's arrest but of potential forever paused, like berries that never rot. Fauna shares in their uncanny communion—tawny goats that pause to mirror human gestures, wolf-like predators that prowl without pouncing, their instincts dulled to sympathy. In times of rare convergence, perhaps sparked by a particularly vivid condensation drip or a flock of birds hovering in static flight, the inhabitants enact communal visions, where mirrored pools reveal scenes of Eldridge in variations ethereal, showing cities that might have sprawled or withered, reminding them that change is the skyline's inviolable law. Visitors, if such beings stumble in, are drawn into the weave, their impulses to alter or depart dissolving in the chill embrace, until they too adopt the deliberate pace, skin hardening like stone, eyes gleaming with reflected light.

In this way, the people of Eldridge embody the city's essence: abundant in quiet life, sparse in density, thriving in lush damp chill under normal, unchanging light. They are not conquerors nor wanderers but custodians of stasis, their existence a living testament to the eternal inhale, where breaths are drawn forever, and the world's fabric holds without fray.
