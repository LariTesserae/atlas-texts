In the Construed Lowlands, life does not merely inhabit but emerges as an extension of the fabricated stones, the weeping surfaces, and the stuttering fractures of reality itself. It is as if the artisans who chiseled this place into existence—those silent architects with their antler picks and sinew ropes—never truly departed, their forms dissolving into the realm they built, becoming one with the lizards that scamper like living tiles across the sharp-edged flags, the birds that glide on oiled leather wings through the unfiltered brilliance, and the sloths that dangle in eternal stillness, fur matted and heavy with condensation. These are not wild creatures sprung from evolutionary whimsy, but semblances of life, hand-wrought and imperfect, their bodies etched with the same rune-like patterns as the crabs' shells, as if they too were hewn from the quarries above and animated only by the heat's relentless breath.

The artisans themselves linger in the fringes of this tableau, not as bustling laborers now, but as solitary wanderers, half-dissolved into the humid fog. Picture them: slender figures with skin like weathered stone, their bodies clad in tunics woven from the broad leaves of ferns and vines that sprout from the chiseled grooves. They move with deliberate slowness across the causeways, their feet bare and calloused, tracing the repetitive patterns in the flagstones not with purpose but with a ritualized echo, as if each step reinforces the dream from which the place was born. They speak only in whispers that are absorbed by the air almost immediately, fragments of chants about floors built below and skies glimpsed once, never fully articulated. These artisans have become guardians of the cycles, tending to the marshes not by uprooting weeds but by coaxing the water's condensation into new basins, their hands phasing sometimes translucent, revealing beneath their flesh the spiraling voids or nested miniatures of the valley itself—pocket worlds flickering like glitched memories. One might glimpse them pausing mid-gesture, a flicker in their form like a lizard stuttering in place, dissolving briefly into pixelated smog before reforming, their eyes wide and unblinking, reflecting the infinite repetitions in the pools below.

Yet the fauna teems not in herds or flocks, but in scattered essences, each creature a living artifact that embodies the place's unchanging equilibrium. The lizards, with scales fitted like mismatched flagstones, dart in solitary bursts across the warm surfaces, their tongues lashing out to catch droplets that bead eternally on the stone. They pause mid-leap sometimes, frozen in the hot stasis, time warping around them so that their path echoes backward—a smear of afterimages that ripple the air like heat waves, hinting at routes they might have taken in a world that never was. The birds, aloft in the bright, piercing light, circle not in flocks but individually, their leather wings unfurling into impossible spans that phase into translucency, revealing feathered innards laced with chiseled runes. They chirp rarely, their calls muffled into the silence, absorbed by the haze, as if they announce not prey or mates, but the steady drip of condensation. Sloths cling to the vine-bridge arms, their fur a tapestry of dampness, eyes half-lidded in contemplation of the tilting platforms—gravity's uneven pull making some branches sag without breaking, allowing the sloths to dangle eternally, their movements a glacial reach toward a fig that blossoms in reverse, petals unfurling outward before curling inward again.

Frogs emerge from the shallow marshes, their leaps solitary and calculated, from one reed mat to another across the water troughs. Their skin glistens with selective warmth—the heat warming some parts while others chill inexplicably, causing vapor to rise unevenly from their backs. They chirp in the profound quiet, a sound that seems to phase backward, echoing from points unseen, as if the frog heralds not its own presence but the void beneath the stones. Crabs scuttle along the edges, their shells inscribed with fractal etchings that mirror the valley's self-similar webs, occasionally dissolving into blurs before reforming solid, their pincers clicking softly against the stone—a noise that the air devours, leaving only the afterimage of motion. And in the deeper alcoves, beneath the ziggurats, other beings stir: wriggling forms that might be eels or serpents, but crafted from vines and damp, their bodies coiling through the drainage trenches now overrun with greenery. They move like strangling lianas, bending to defy horizons, their scales blooming briefly with flowers that scatter into unmoving constellations before wilting in reverse.

As one wanders deeper into the Construed Lowlands, the inhabitants reveal more of their paradoxical nature. The artisans gather in clusters at dusk—if dusk exists in the eternal light—around the mirror-still basins, where they commune in silent rituals, their forms flickering collectively, phasing into a group dissolve where individual features smear into a tapestry of echoes: limbs stretching impossibly long, faces warping into the familiar patterns of the stones or the nests of birds. In these moments, they become the fauna, or the fauna becomes them—a lizard with the artisan's unblinking gaze, a bird uttering a whispered chant. The fauna, too, blends at the edges: sloths that transform into artisans for a stuttered breath, frogs leaping into crabs, all bound by the hot stasis that prevents true change. Wildfires of possibility spark in the fractures—animals phasing through time's folds, living creatures predating or postdating their own existence, a lizard stepping into a future where it dissolves backward, or a sloth reaching for a vine that coils from a memory of quarries above.

Yet amidst this abundance, solitude prevails, each inhabitant spaced far apart in the sparse density, wandering the gaps where shadows stretch impossibly thin—elongated silhouettes cast by the molten light, painting negatives of forms that phase in and out. Some artisans inhabit the multi-tiered platforms alone, crafting not more stones but visions: using twigs and leaves to etch designs that dissolve into the air, summoning phantom tools or half-remembered skies that flicker before erasing. Others mimic the fauna's stillness, posing as perpetual statues by the mars hy troughs, their breaths syncing with the drips, embodying the musical silence where only the philosophical weep of water speaks. Life here is lush in isolation, a rhizome network of individual threads unfurling without connection, each creature or artisan a node in the eternal dream, resistant to wear or evolution, forever poised between acceleration and erasure. And in the nested valleys within valleys—the tunnels bored into fabricated hills and pools mirroring repetitions—the inhabitants multiply in echoes, self-similar and unchanging, a low realm teeming with presences that are utterly themselves: silent builders, frozen beasts, and the glitches that flicker them all into being, over and over.
