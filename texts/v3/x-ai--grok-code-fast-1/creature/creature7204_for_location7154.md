Conflicts arise infrequently, resolved through the weakest gradients—territorial disputes settling into negotiated encroachments, where a machine's roar might gradually diminish as vines adjust its conduits. Knowledge accumulates in the same vein, etched not on pages but in the arboreal memories of elders whose forms have accreted layers of history: rings of bark concealing stories of past integrations, petals blooming with tales of how a refinery once resisted but now hums with sap.

In rare moments, when the fog lifts and distances sharpen, they might venture to the basin's rim, their forms casting long shadows into the floodplain beyond—a reminder of the humans who built and abandoned, now redeemed by the plant hegemony.
