In the submerged citadel, where basalt pores exhale warmth and saline currents caress every carved facet, live the indelibly etched custodians: the Aquons, a people shaped by eons of undersea immersion, their forms a meld of primordial stoneworker and abyss-adaptive flesh. They are not mere survivors or interlopers; they are the citadel's inheritors, descendants of those hammer-wielding ancestors who rived the pinnacle from the seamount's hide, their lineage a slow percolation through the ages, distilled into beings who breathe the ocean's rhythm as effortlessly as they chisel its memory. Born in densities that press identities into seamless collectives, they exist not as individuals in isolation but as layered presences, bodies intertwined in communal hubs where faint bioluminescent pulses from their skin mingle with the citadel's own glows, creating a living tapestry of warmth and whisper.

Physically, they are creatures of saline grace, evolved to embody the structure's archaic symmetries. Limbs long and sinuous, clad in scales that shimmer with ingested glow-stones—flecks of mineral luminescence embedded like veins in flesh—allow them to glide through the twisting corridors and spiraling ramps with a fluidity that mirrors the serpentine eels patrolling the plazas. Their bodies, slightly gelatinous yet firm, adapted for the unyielding density, boast subtle pockets of adipose tissue to buoy against the ocean's embrace, the skin a mottled blend of basalt gray and coppery iridescence, cracked in places like ancient stone with embedded fossils of shells that once whispered dry air. Eyes are large and multifaceted, eyeless in the true sense but Sensitive to the faintest vibrations of pressure and phosphorescence, sensing the dance of currents as surely as vision perceives light in shallower realms. No external lids protect these orbs; instead, translucent membranes flex like slow shutters, filtering the eternal green pallor into dreams of former skies. Hands, deft and multi-jointed, ended in flattened palms sharpened by generations of chipping bone tools, serve as both chisels and collectors, harvesting fungal mats or etching walls with precision born of tactile intimacy. Breath emerges not as explosive gouts but as a steady, transformative alchemy—gills fringed along the neck and flanks draw in the warm, saline flow, exuding a faint, silken mist that carries trace minerals, contributing to the citadel's gradual accretion even as they devour it. Reproduction is a communal affair, slow and deliberate, pairings that unfold over seasons in the crowded nexus-rooms, where tiny, translucent podlings bud from stone-woven hammocks, their early forms indistinguishable from the faunal filigrees, only maturing into self-aware custodians through the dense press of kin and the whispered harmonics of the architecture.

Society among the Aquons is a symphony of patience and accretion, where time dilates to the span of erosion's sigh, and hierarchy dissolves into the fluid exchanges of barter and ritual. They cluster in dense enclaves—hundreds, sometimes thousands, layered in the pinnacle's tiered plazas and domiciles—sharing space in hovels carved directly into the walls, their heavy basalt doors ajar to admit the slow eddies of warmer currents from below. In these confines, isolation is anathema; every presence touches another's, undulations of sleep entwining with the languid swim of wakefulness. Communication unfolds not in hurried words but through the press of flesh, vibrations sent along shared walls or the release of colored inks—bioluminescent secretions that stain stone with glyphs of intent, fading like memories across the citadel's surfaces. Language is itself a chiseled art, evolved from the etchings-parchments: symbols hammered into basalt with bone knives, narratives of the pre-flood era—tales of a dry peak beneath open skies, where ancestors wielded hammers under a sun now banished to myth. These stories, performed in rhythmic recitations amid the muffled echoes of chambers, speak of primordial deities of depth, invitations to settle into the eternal submersion, their voices distorted now into burbling nonsense by pressure's warp.

Daily life pulses to the ocean's vast cycle, the imperceptible tides that drag warmer or cooler waters through voids. In the quieter fluxes, Aquons tend to the citadel's primitive tech—levering bone and shell mechanisms to channel thermals upward, ensuring the spread of fungal bounty without scarcity. They herd the fauna not with cruelty but with mimicry: coiling like eels around pillars, they guide schools of pallid fish toward harvesting troughs, their membranous fins folding to gesture directions that the wingless flyers echo in their hunts. Rituals abound in the silence, offerings of glow-stone shards exchanged in the nexus-rooms—each transaction a quiet intimacy, body against body in the darkness, reinforcing bonds that endure beyond the fleeting. Scavengers among them dive to the pinnacle's base, scraping mineral deposits from the seamount's skirts, returning with caches to fortify eroding walls or enflesh new accretions. Conflict, if it arises, manifests as subtle shifts: dislodged dwellers cycling through enclaves, their exhalations altering currents just enough to recalibrate space. Over eons, the Aquons have embraced a philosophy of permanence in flux—their own forms reflecting the pinnacle's architecture, with shells exuded and shed to mimic buttresses, claws curved like ramps. They do not resist the densification; instead, they chisel counterpoints, engraving voids to keep niches alive, viewing erosion as a partner in the slow breath of existence.

In the higher reaches, near the summit where waters thin slightly, the elders dwell—those whose skins have accreted the most copper veining, their movements glacial, wisdom distilled from eons of percolation. They commune with the built stone, sensing tectonic shifts as distant tremors, etching predictions of subsidence in the walls, symbols that warn of gradual falls over millennia. Children, fluid and probing, cluster in the vents, learning to sculpt bubbles or chase midge swarms, their laughter attenuated into murmurs that echo the builders' chants. Fauna integrates seamlessly; an Aquon might share a hovel with a gelatinous fish, its fins trailing like comets in synchronized sleep. Yet through it all, a weird harmony persists—a society where animals are allies, machines are extensions of self, and the indieABLE boundary between flesh and stone blurs into unity. They are not conquerors of the abyss but its custodians, perpetuating the citadel's fabricated warmth in a ballet of density and delicacy, enduring as the pinnacle endures, their breaths rising lazily to meet the attenuated horizon of the endless ocean above.
