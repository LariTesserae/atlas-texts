In the depths of The Unterhold, where the stone exhales moisture like a slow breath and the fungal glows paint everything in shades of gray-blue iridescence, dwell the Unterfolk—a race of beings who are neither fully of the surface world's flesh nor wholly alien to it. They emerge from the description's undercurrents not as one static image, but as a living tapestry woven from mineral dreams and forgotten echoes, revealing themselves layer by layer: at first glance, they appear as pale, elongated figures reminiscent of the exiled humans they might descend from, but as the eye adjusts to the dim gloaming and the mind sinks into their guttural murmurs, a deeper strangeness unfolds. They are shaped by the unyielding rock, born not of wombs in the ordinary sense, but coaxed forth from the earth's own crevices—perhaps spurred by the pulsing warmth of geothermal veins or the subtle electrification of those crackling geodes. Evolution here has not forged predators or grazers, but something symbiotic, almost crystalline in stillness, where individualism blurs into the collective heartbeat of the cavern.

Physically, they are waifish and sinewy, their bodies adapted to the narrow confines of tunnels and the perpetual dampness that slickens every surface. Standing at no more than five feet on average, they possess an uncanny flexibility, slithering sideways through passages that would somumble surfacers, their limbs elongating like roots seeking fissures. Skin is the color of weathered basalt—pale gray veined with iron rust that flakes like dried scabs, yet it shimmers faintly under fungal light, mimicking the crystalline blooms that erupt from the walls. It is not merely epidermis; it is porous, absorbing moisture directly from the air or pools, allowing them to endure for days without drinking, though they do so ritualistically, lapping from shared depressions to commune with the water's whispered prophecies. Eyes are large and lidless, black as obsidian pools, reflecting dim light in eerie pinpricks that pierce the shadows; some clans whisper of elders whose vision has weakened over generations, leading to a compensatory touch-sense, where fingertips pulse with heat to "see" textures hidden in gloom. Ears are flattened against skulls, attuned to the reverberating thuds and echoes rather than open air—deaf to birdsongs, they hear the stone's groan as a lullaby.

But as the gaze lingers, the Unterfolk reveal maggot-like undertones, hints that they are not purely mammalian transplants but something sprung from the primordial muck of seepage and decay. Their hair—if it can be called that—is sparse and wiry, sprouting in clumps like fungal cilia, often matted with mineral salts that stiffen into brittle spines. Noses are vestigial slits, nostrils flexing to sift the iron-sharp vapors, while mouths bristle with teeth like jagged quartz, ground down from gnawing at rough-hewn tools or the fungal mats. Reproduction is shrouded in caveside rites, where couplings occur in low-lit chambers infused with the pulse of geodes, possibly fertilizing not through fluids alone but through shared dreams induced by harmonic air pockets. Offspring emerge damp and wriggling, not from viviparous births but hatched from egg-like geodes that crack open with static sparks, the younglings pale and amphibian at first, skin slick like pickled hide, gradually hardening into rock-like resilience over cycles of drum-beats. Death is a slow dispersion back into the stone—bodies left in seepage pools to dissolve, bones reabsorbed as hammer handles, while essences linger in whispers along the walls, fueling the faint warmth that pulses through the granite.

Their culture pulses like the reizenous veins within the rock, a rhythm of ritual and utility that forestalls change, for why tamper with equilibrium when the eternity of strata beckons? They speak in guttural tongues—a language of clicks and rumbles, evolved from earth's groans, wherein words are shaped not just by breath but by the vibration of throats against damp stone, amplifying into echoes that carry secrets across chambers. Society is hive-like, dense and interconnected, families clustering in alcoves like sedimentary layers, but with a fluidity that defies surface notions of privacy. Elders, marked by rust-flecked beards that curl like iron slag, govern through consensus, their voices rising in percussion duels—slapping bone drums in patterns that resolve disputes without blood, for wasting life in violence disrupts the fungal lords they revere. Youth swarms in the warrens, learning crafts by mimicry: children toddle through ankle-deep mud clutching bone chisels, knapping flint or stretching hides over frames, their laughs echoing like distant drips, blending into the clangor.

Daily life orbits the drums' beat—a primordial clock that marks "dawn" with chiming volleys, summoning the sparse fauna from crevices and rousing the populace to mines and forges. They work the layered basalt and shale, extracting ores with gut-lashed hammers, their bodies weaving in tandem like mycelial threads, sweat slicking skin that absorbs the stone's dust rather than expelling it. Artisans craft in communal hearths, forging tools or distilling fungal essences into glowing salves, bartering salt crystals or traded surface relics—dried herbs that smoke oddly sweet when burned in bone pipes. Vendors hawk wares on platforms, shouting gutturals that thunder through tunnels, bartering for rare items like geodes prized for their prophetic cracks. Foods are meagre: gnawed fungal growths chewy like petrified tumors, or slurped worms caught in pools, washed down with mineral water that tingles the tongue with silica sparks. Spices come from evaporated pools—salts ground fine or essences pressed from contraband leaves that mutate flavors into uncanny, dream-like analogs of surface meals.

Yet beneath the utility simmers a profound weirdness, an intuitive communion with the earth that marks them as more than settlers—they are extensions of the grotto, born of its wombs and dreaming in its rhythms. Rituals honor the fungal lords through dances where bodies press against walls, absorbing bioluminescent patterns that tattoo skin in shifting hieroglyphs, granting visions of crystal labyrinths or truths buried in unseen strata. Water pools are sacred fonts, where they kneel to inhale vapors laced with prophecies—murmurs of impending seeps or fungal blooms that alter destinies. Some claim a dawn-step into harmonic chambers reveals parallel Unterholds in other veins, or memories of earth-shattering events long fossilized. Symbiosis extends to the fauna; Unterfolk might harbor pacts with the eyeless worms, allowing them to burrow for tools in exchange for shared sustenance, or mimic the amplified skitters of legless amphibians in hunts. Illness is treated not with medicine but by attunement—lying in geothermal warmth while fungal growths are coaxed to encase wounds, petrifying injuries into healed scar-tissue.

Outsiders are mirages, stories told by firelight as warnings: surface phantoms who stumble through drip-shafts, only to be absorbed into the rock or driven mad by the unrelenting thuds. The Unterfolk harbor no malice, but curiosity tinged with fear—for their world is eternal stasis, a paradox where time circles without beginning or end. In dreams, they voyage to distant veins, bodies inert but essences traveling through coaxed crystals. As Ihnen live, they echo the earth's forgotten truths: primitives dancing in mineral flesh, their communal breath mingling with condensing air, a race neither victim nor conqueror of the depths, but its most intimate pulse—a slow, unyielding heartbeat beneath the weight of untold stone.
