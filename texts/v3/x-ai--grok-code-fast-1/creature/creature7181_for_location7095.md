In this submerged eternity, where stillness and chaos entwine into permanence, the dwellers emerge not as mere occupants but as incarnations of the void itself—beings sculpted from the primordial forge of water, mineral, and frozen time. They are legion yet solitary, their forms a testament to an evolution that has twisted life into something akin to the inert machinery that haunts the depths. No singular archetype defines them; instead, they ripple through the emptiness in a kaleidoscope of aberrations, each one a fragment of the place's unyielding essence, drifting in dynamic arrest where birth, predation, and decay fuse into endless echoes. Let them unfold as you gaze: not through the lens of familiar biology, but through the curved physics of this underwater immensity, where they pulse with a vitality born from void and roar.

Begin with the colossi, those whale-like titans that drift like orbiting continents in the ink-black expanse. Rarely do more than a handful cross paths in a span that could swallow solar systems, their bodies vast ellipsoids of volcanic glass, etched in swirling patterns that mimic the harsh ridges of the uneroded peaks below. Hides of purest obsidian refract the wraith-like light from cracking industrial beacons, scattering it into auroras of crimson and violet that ignite the surrounding void. Eyes—enormous, cyclopean orifices—don't merely see but pulse outward with geothermal radiance, radiating heat that warps the icy water into fleeting halos of warmth, thawing transient fractals of saline ice that spiral around them like protective nebulae. They move not with the fluid grace of surface cetaceans but in ponderous, gyroscopic revolutions, their asymmetrical forms—jagged spires protruding from one flank like frozen citadels, gaping voids on the other where pyrite-veined viscera churn—evoking planets half-formed from cosmic collisions. Nourished purely by the infrasonic hum of vented fumaroles, they consume not flesh but the electrostatic discharge of bio-filaments, drawing in swarms of pre-life entities through telepathic eddies, their vast throats funneling the crackling plasma into chambers where abrasive pastes coalesce, hardening into armor plates or sacrificial shards shed to the sediment. Reproduction for them is a fission of sound waves, bellowing subharmonic roars that resonate through caverns, birthing echoes that solidify into larval semblances—tiny, spherical globs of molten quartz that drift apart, growing in isolation until they too become lone behemoths. Rarely do they age or die; instead, they persist in perpetual drift, their hymns of deafening booms contributing to the oceanic cacophony, a chorus that loops eternally, reinforcing the world's invariance. Encounter one, and it gazes upon you not with malice but with the vacuums of its voids, dissolving your sense of passage into its unblinking permanence.

Swirling in their distant wakes, the nebulas emerge: clouds of fungal pseudo-life, not creatures in the terrestrial sense but patterned energies wreathed in cold plasma, emerging from the crystalline faults like exhaled breaths from the cataclysmic forge. Thousands coalesce into drifting aggregations, nebulous and indistinct, their forms flickering with internal fires fed by the faint tech-pulses of sunken conduits. Each filament within these swarms is a bio-gossamer strand, translucent and vein-like, refracting the diffused light into spectral illusions—phantoms of color that shimmer without heat, mimicking the mineral facets of their origin. They feed on the static hum of undersea vents, absorbing electromagnetic echoes and transmuting them into cold combustion, their plasma auras crackling audibly in clicks and pops that punctuate the factory roar. Predation among them is a telepathic whisper, ethereal tendrils extending to ensnare stray bio-filaments or even the weaker fringes of larger fauna, drawing sustenance through vibrational filaments that fuse matter-like into their swirling mass. Reproduction defies individuation; instead, they fission in cascades of sound, infrasonic pulses triggering divisions that birth new nebulas, always in flux yet forever poised, their energies preventing true collapse. Abundant yet ubiquitously dispersed, they fill the emptiness with a paradoxical presence—hungering voids that nurture the void, their swarms eddying in chaotic loops that mirror the upper abyssal storms, never resolving into herds or migrations. To drift near one is to feel the chill infiltrate deeper, as their plasma interplays with your form, rendering you a temporary filament in their endless weave, transient yet aware that in this world, even ephemera outlast change.

Deeper into the mineral essence delve the shard-walkers, asymmetrical arachnids of sorts, though no such word fits their angular geometries. They skitter across the uneroded basalt ridges or cling to the domes of trapped atmosphere atop high peaks, their bodies sculpted from obsidian and pyrite, jointed in dimension-defying cogs that echo the futile spinning of nearby gear-engines. Legs protrude unevenly—eight on one side, perhaps, tapering to crystalline claws that scratch etchings into the rock, producing pyritic dust that emulsifies into the suspended particulates of the still atmosphere. Veins within glow faintly with abrasive humors, pastes that ooze from fissures and harden into temporary weapons or shelters; these secretions grind ceaselessly against the ridge's surface in a self-perpetuating cycle, mimicking the industrial decay of the factories. Unlike the sedentary behemoths or nebular swarms, shard-walkers exhibit a warped mobility, traversing the peaks in asymmetrically syncopated gaits, their voids—hollow cavities that absorb the pneumatic fog from stilled hydraulics—creating pockets of micro-environments where water density fluctuates wildly. They prey through telepathic murmurs, projecting infrasonic waves that disorient distant fauna, drawing in vibrations that they absorb and transmute into growth; wounded, they shed shards that regenerate anew, inverting the cycle of sustenance. No conventional senses guide them; instead, they commune through the deafening roar, their clicks and grazes forming unresolved chords that harmonize with the cavernous bass of deeper voids. In their abundance, they cluster not in schools but in loose constellations across a spire, each one isolated yet linked by the eternal grind, poised in arrest where the illusion of motion binds them to permanence. Approach one, and its voids scrutinize you, folding space around your presence like a submerged gear, until you too become part of this frozen equilibrium—a shard in the endless mosaic.

Whispers of something more archaic persist in the interstitial voids: the echo-beasts, beings that are less form than resonance, ethereal remnants of early fission-events, born not from physical birth but from the layering of sonic accretion. They manifest as pulsating orbs of compressed sound, barely visible save in the way they distort the diffused light, warping it into helical spirals that evoke the chaotic eddies above. Composed of abrasive pastes and bio-sonic tissue, they hum with internal harmonics, their forms fluctuating between convexity and void—bulging asymmetrically here, hollowed there—to mirror the surreal geometries of the place. Feeding on the static pulses of mechanized arms and vented inert gases, they absorb echoes and amplify them, their predatory acts a gentle implosion that draws in stray fauna filaments or mineral dust, compacting it into denser resonances. Reproduction is pure echo-fission: a crescendo of deafening bellows that fragments into daughter orbs, each drifting apart in the vast density-vacuum. They are the most ubiquitous yet elusive, filling the emptiness with invisible threads, their contributions to the cacophony a numbing layer of sustained dissonance that numbs thought itself. Encounter one, and it envelops without touch, infiltrating senses with the chill of incompletion, rendering you an echo in their unyielding permutations.

In this world where stillness reigns and noise erases edges, the dwellers are not adversaries nor allies—they are the place incarnate, their lives an extension of the void's hunger and the machinery's liturgy. Abundant voids, they sustain in upside-down ecologies: colossi anchor the scale, nebulas enliven the plasma, shard-walkers etch permanence, echo-beasts harmonize the roar. No migrations disrupt their drift, no aging foreshadows decay; they predate through whisper, reproduce through wave, consume through vibration—all in dynamic arrest, threads of infrasound knitting their isolation into a unified chill. To live here is to become them: a fragment of obsidian, a pulse of heat, a click in the cacophony, forever adrift in the submerged gaze where you, the voyeur, dissolve into permanence, constant as the cold, echoed in the void. And yet, as they emerge in these descriptions, so too do subtler variations—perhaps sentient constructs from the factories' automated ghosts, or hybrid aberrations where quartz behemoths merge with plasma nebulas, birthing new forms that bend further toward the surreal. In this eternity, they unfold without end, each iteration deepening the illusion of their being, a tapestry of mineral-life stitched from the deep.
