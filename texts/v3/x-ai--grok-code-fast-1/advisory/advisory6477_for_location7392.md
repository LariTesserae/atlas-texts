Bring Diamox (acetazolamide) or altitude sickness medications—intuitively, the thin air feels like drowning in mist, so preempt it with gradual exposure.
   
   - **Endurance and Strength Training:** This isn't flat hiking; the plateau demands crawling through dense ferns, leaping fissures, or navigating sheer cliffs. Build core strength for stability on slippery surfaces—do planks, squats, and balance exercises on unstable platforms (like foam mats or wobble boards). Your muscles will ache from the humidity-clogged air, sapping energy faster, so incorporate endurance runs in muggy conditions to prepare for the silvery sheen of sweat that constantly beads on your skin.
   
   - **Thermal and Moisture Adaptation:** Nights freeze, days warm faintly—dress in layers, but train in extreme conditions. Practice "wet survival": swimming or hiking in rain suits to understand how water permeates everything, leading to hypothermia if not managed. Strengthen your immune system with supplements (vitamin D for cold, iron for thin air) and get vaccines or boosters for respiratory illnesses—the perpetual wetness breeds fungal infections or spores that might trigger hallucinations.
