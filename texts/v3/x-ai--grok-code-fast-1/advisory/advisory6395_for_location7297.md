### 1. **Pressure and Depth Adaptation (The Primary Barrier)**
   This is a chamber, not the open abyss, so you're not drowning in infinite pressure immediately upon entry—those reinforced walls and sealed bulkheads are a buffer. But the depth means hydrostatic pressure compresses air and crushes organic tissue: lungs collapse without equalization, joints ache, and sinuses bleed.    - **Pressure Suit or Diving Gear**: Invest in a full-depth-rated atmospheric diving suit (ADS) or a submersible suit like the "Newtsuit" or WASP systems used in deep oil industry dives. These are rigid, helmet-sealed exoskeletons with articulated joints, allowing free movement up to 2,000 meters. They're thickly insulated against cold and pressure, with built-in life support. Costly to source or 3D-print in a multiverse traveler's workshop, but essential—no wetsuit or scuba is sufficient here; shallow diving tech fails catastrophically below 200 meters due to nitrogen narcosis and pressure differentials. If tech is scarce, a commercial rebreather helmet (like the Cressi or Poseidon) attached to a reinforced backpack frame, but beef it up with inflatable bladders to mitigate compression.
   
   - **Decompression Planning**: Avoid "bends" (nitrogen embolism) by acclimating slowly. Use a portable recompression chamber (portable RCC) in your inventory for emergency depressurization. Carry helium-oxygen mix in high-pressure tanks—helium thins the air at depth, reducing narcosis risk. Schedule 24-48 hours of saturation time in a simulated chamber before entry to preload your blood with inert gases.

   - **Intuition on Immersion**: Entering through that corroded hatch feels like stepping into a pressure cooker; wrench it open, and the chamber's "womb of water and metal" embraces you. Expect vertigo from depth, so include anti-nausea meds and EQ equalization tips—don't hold your breath when descending.

   - **Emergency Bailout**: Wireless sonar alarm linked to a surface beacon for rescue extraction. If portals close, have a buoyancy engine (inflatable raft or surface-tethered winch) to ascend slowly, decompressing en route.
