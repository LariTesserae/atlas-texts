   - **Medical Kit Essentials**: Assemble a portable kit with items that align with primitive tech levels to "blend in." For thin-air issues, carry stimulants like caffeine-rich leaves (or synthetic equivalents) to ward off fatigue. Get a full physical check-up, including cardiology for heart strain in cold hypoxia.

   - **Dietary Prep for Energy Demands**: The body's caloric needs spike in cold, thin air. Bulk up on fats—eat oily, nutrient-dense foods (fish, nuts, animal fats) for insulation. Take vitamin supplements (C for cold immunity, B for energy, iron to combat anemia from altitude). Avoid dehydration traps; the perpetual mist tricks your sense of it, but the air's dryness at this elevation demands constant sipping. Hydrate with warm, salted broths to mimic how locals consume condensed water.

   Expect your body to adapt slowly—arrive sluggish, with initial chills and fogged lungs. Listen to it; rest often, even if there's no "night." Undernourished travelers could succumb to the cold void within days, their breath freezing from inside.
