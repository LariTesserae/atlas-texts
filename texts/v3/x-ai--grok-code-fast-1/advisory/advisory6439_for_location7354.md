### Preparation Guide for an Ordinary Human Visiting the Submerged Citadel

As an ordinary human multiverse traveler—meaning you're equipped with everyday knowledge of Earthly science, survival skills, and perhaps basic adventuring gear but no superhuman powers, advanced alien tech, or innate resistances to extreme environments—you'll need to approach this visit like a deep-sea expedition combined with an archaeological dig into an alien, semi-sentient underworld. The citadel isn't just a sunken ruin; it's a living, breathing ecosystem warped by eons of submersion, primitive ingenuity, and a bizarre, symbiotic relationship between architecture and biology. Visiting it would feel like plunging into the subconscious of an ancient, forgotten civilization: claustrophobic, silent, warm yet oppressive, with a pervasive strangeness that blurs the lines between artifact and organism. It demands meticulous preparation to survive the physical hazards (pressure, darkness, adapted wildlife) while adapting your mindset to its weird rhythms—slow time, muffled senses, and an instinctive intimacy with stone that could subtly change you if you're not careful. Below, I'll break down your preparation step by step, drawing from intuitive dives into similar extreme environments (think abyssopelagic expeditions on Earth, but amplified by this place's fabricated eeriness). Plan for a multi-day stay if possible, as its slow cycles defy quick jaunts.

#### 1. **Assess and Secure Entry: The Multiverse Transition and Initial Dive**
   - **Portal or Transportation Logistics:** Since you're a multiverse traveler, assume you have access to a stable portal that deposits you above the ocean surface in this realm—perhaps hovering over a vast, sunless sea. Narrow down the exact coordinates; the citadel is a solitary spire rising from the abyssal floor, so scan for geomagnetic anomalies from the primordial seamount. Use a drone or surface probe if possible to confirm its glow and geothermal warmth. Upon arrival, don't just leap in—set up a buoy or surface beacon with a homing signal in case currents or pressure dislocation shifts your entry point.
   - **Edging into the Depths:** The ocean here is endless and sunless, with dense, sun-warmed waters from below. Start with a controlled descent: Use a submersible capsule or reinforced atmospheric diving suit (ADS) instead of traditional scuba to handle the immense depth (potentially miles to the seamount). Surface-to-floor pressure could exceed 10,000 psi, so prepare for blood nitrogen saturation risks akin to Earthly deep dives. Bring decompression chambers—portable ones that fit in your portal backpack—as mandatory stopovers during ascent to avoid bends.

#### 2. **Physical and Environmental Gear: Surviving the Submerged Abyss**
   - **Diving and Pressure Suits:** Your primary barrier against the ocean's "warm, saline embrace" at steady but bone-crushing depths. Opt for a high-density polymer suit (like an advanced wetsuit, but halogenated for extreme pressure) rated for seafloor extremes. Include haptic feedback gloves for navigating rough, eroded basalt surfaces without lacerating your hands. Thermal regulation is key—the water is welcomingly warm (geothermal pulses keep it steady around 70-80°F/21-27°C), but sudden cooler inflows from tidal cycles could chill you fast, potentially leading to hypothermia in denser areas. Integrate heating coils powered by waterproof batteries or kinetic energy from your movements.
   - **Lighting and Visibility:** Darkness is unrelenting, broken only by the citadel's faint phosphorescent veins—greenish, dancing glows from alchemical residues and bioluminescent flora. Bring multiple light sources: waterproof LED torches with focused beams for corridors, and diffuse glow-sticks for wide-area illumination (mimicking the natural pallor). Avoid bright whites; they'll hurt your adaptation and might alarm fauna. Portable sonar goggles or heads-up display (HUD) units for mapping in pitch-black ramps and plazas—essential, as the architecture twists like veins and relies on tactile features. Stock up on spares; the glow here is "subdued and muffled," so your lights could fade unexpectedly due to mineral interference.
   - **Mobility and Navigation:** The pinnacle is like an inverted well, with sloping floors, spiraling ramps, and tiered plazas—easy to get lost in. Equip with magnetic boots or suction grips for the eroded, slick stone (which feels "primed" and yielding underfoot). A personal propulsion system (like a thruster pack) is crucial; currents are languid but dense, and the "glacial scraping" of erosion creates undertows that could whisk you away. Carry inertial navigation chips synced to your portal beacon for dead reckoning, as compasses might spin wildly from the seamount's tectonics.
   - **Protection Against Erosion and Corrosion:** Everything here is eroding glacially, with drips turning to cascades on buttresses. Your gear needs rust-proofing: copper-veined reinforcements on suits to mimic the citadel's materials and resist the saline action. Bring sealant sprays for etching or chiseled surfaces that might corrode over time. Also, anticipate the "primordial slump"—rounded, slumping forms due to slow pressure; your base camp (a portable inflatable hab docked to the stone) should have adjustable anchors to shift with any tectonic sways.

#### 3. **Biological and Ecological Precautions: Navigating the Dense Fauna and Odd Symbiosis**
   - **Wildlife Awareness:** The citadel is teeming with adapted, abundant fauna that cluster in its niches—eyeless fish, stone-melded crabs, serpentine eels, and bat-like swimmers. They're peaceful except in predation cycles, but density means you'll be constantly surrounded, their undulations stirring the water intimately. Prepare like a biologist in a coral reef maze: Wear a layered, armored suit with a repellent mesh (infused with non-toxic, phosphorescence-dissuading compounds to mimic natural glow). Carry tranquilizer darts or nets for self-defense, but aim to observe rather than disrupt—the creatures seem to "commune" with the architecture in a silent ballet.
   - **Health and Contamination Risks:** The water is saline and warm, but fungal mats, bioluminescent inks, and mineral deposits in vents (exhaling slow thermals) could introduce unknown microbes. Bring a full medkit: Antibiotics for potential infections from scraped claws or porous stone, anti-fungal salves for mats clinging to seams, and a portable water purifier (despite endless submersion, you'll process food from home). Beware the "weirdness" where buildings blur with biology—shell-embedded fossils or ingested glow-stones might cause hallucinations if ingested accidentally. Stock nutrient pouches; no plants here, so pack caloric-dense foods that don't spoil in warmth.
   - **Interaction Protocols:** Resist touching or harvesting carelessly—fauna leave "imprints" on stone, and your presence might alter the slow accretion. If you need specimens (for study), use sterile containment bags. The inhabitants (if any primitive descendants remain) might barter in silence via gestures, offering glow-stone or etched shells. Prepare communication aids: A universal translator app (adapted for muffled echoes) and symbolic gestures book based on the chiseled wall symbols. Avoid startling flocks of flyers; their folded fins navigate densely.

#### 4. **Mental and Sensory Preparation: Adapting to Silence, Strangeness, and Slow Time**
   - **Psychological Fortification:** The citadel's hush—broken by bumps, sizzles, and burbling echoes—is profoundly alien, with "weird echoes" warping into nonsense murmurs from ancient chants or inventors' invocations. Train for sensory deprivation: Practice meditation in dark, soundproof rooms to build tolerance for the "attenuated boundary" silence. Bring noise-canceling earpieces that amplify subtle sounds (e.g., rising bubbles) but block disorienting echoes. The place's "patient, minimal fluxes" (vast, imperceptible cycles) will make time feel elongated—prevent cabin fever with a journal for recording "slow durations," etching symbols into a portable board to mimic the inhabitants.
   - **Cultural and Intuitive Mindset:** Intuit that boundaries blur—stone "accretes living fragments," and creatures mimic architecture. Prepare spiritually: Study oceanic folklore or survival rituals from cultures like Polynesian voyaging, embracing the "unusual harmony" as a mantra. The pinnacle's densification (presences layering intimately) fosters empathy for claustrophobia; practice confined-space drills. Weirdness might seep in—hallucinations from bioluminescence or pressure-warped senses—so carry grounding tokens: A photo of surface worlds or a playlist of muffled ocean sounds for sanity checks. Avoid prolonged stays; the gradual reshaping (tides densifying voids) could make you part of it, subtly changing your form or thoughts via "arcane mimicry."
   - **Emergency Protocol for Weird Phenomena:** If the "rules" twist (e.g., a room densifying too fast or glyphs staining your suit), have an exit word—a mental trigger to activate your portal. Rotate watch shifts even solo, as density breeds intimacy that might erode personal space.

#### 5. **Supplies and Logistics: Sustaining a Prolonged Visit**
   - **Essential Pack:** Eco-friendly porta-potty (for waste in dense waters), inflatable shelter with reinforced walls, modular tool kit (bone-like chisels for experimentation, shell-hooks for guiding fauna), and energy-dense rations (freeze-dried, high-salt tolerant). Power everything with rechargeable kinetic cells or a small geothermal harvester mimicking the citadel's pulse.
   - **Duration and Exit Strategy:** Limit to 3-5 Earth days max; the slow cycles (erosion, tides) unfold over millennia, making brief visits feel like eons. Mark your descent path with biodegradable markers. On ascent, decompress in stages, monitoring for "primordial slump" effects (e.g., delayed bends mimicking eroded corners). Post-visit, quarantine for anomalies—neural scans to detect any phosphorescent or saline "integrations."
   - **Backup Plans:** Portal malfunction? Cache surface depots with oxygen backups. If currents strand you at the zenith habitational tier, use crude levers (replicate from artifacts) to manipulate vents for safety.

Visiting this place isn't conquest—it's submersion into something ancient, where human ambition meets the ocean's eternal patience. Prepare holistically: gear for survival, mind for the weird, and heart for its silent, stone-bound breath. If you return, you might find echoes of its density lingering in your dreams—a warning and a wonder. Safe travels through the multiverse.
