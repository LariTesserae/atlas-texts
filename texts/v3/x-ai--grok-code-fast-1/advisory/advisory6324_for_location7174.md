### Physical Gear and Supplies
Bring everything multiplied by layers of redundancy; one failure here could mean permanent entrapment in the cold murk. Expect to lug around 50-100 pounds of gear if possible, distributed in waterproof, sealed compartments. Use buoyancy aids to counteract the dense particulate in the water.

- **Oxygen and Breathing Equipment**: The air pockets are stale and briny, with low oxygen—don't rely on them for more than shallow breaths. Bring a full-face diving mask or rebreather system that interfaces with bottled compressed air or a portable electrolysis oxygenator (if your multiverse has advanced tech access). For underwater movement, wear a full drysuit with integrated snorkel/demand-valve system, but test for leaks since the pressure (if any—assume moderate depths like 100-500 feet based on the plateau's flatness) could worsen them. Intuitively, the cold water might feel like a numbing embrace, so practice switching from air to water breathing quickly if your rebreather fails.

- **Insulation and Warmth Management**: Temperature stays at 0.5°C (just above freezing), leeching heat relentlessly. Primary: A heavy-duty drysuit made from neoprene or triple-insulated Gore-Tex, lined with electric heating elements powered by rechargeable lithium batteries (bring 10+ spares). Secondary thermal layers: Wool baselayers, insulated gloves, and booties to prevent numb fingertips (erosion from fingering stone could abrade bare skin). Tertiary: Heat packs (chemical or electric) taped to joints and core—enough for 48-72 hours of continuous use. Anticipate the chill making you drowsy and sluggish; test gear in a cold pool to simulate the "cold crush."

- **Power Sources**: Everything runs on batteries here—no sunlight or energy fields to harvest. Portable solar chargers (paradoxically useless submerged, but keep for entry/exit), hand-crank generators, and lithium-ion power banks (at least 10,000mAh each). - **Lighting**: Absolute darkness (0.2 light level) means you'll grope blindly. Primary: Helmet-mounted LED dive lights with red-shift modes for preserving night vision (penetrate the murk better at 5,000-10,000 lumens). Secondary: Glow sticks or chemiluminescent tracers attached to gear for signaling in zero-vis. Tertiary: Bioluminescent implants or patches (if multiverse tech allows skin-safe glowing fungus symbionts) to mimic the world's own light, reducing the jump scare effect of sudden glows from algae. Bring prismatic lenses to amplify the faint veins in stone—rotate them to catch more pulses.

- **Food, Water, and Waste Management**: No edible natural resources (the vegetation is fibrous and possibly toxic, engineered for this alien ecology). Pack dehydrated nutrient bars (high-calorie, 2,000+ per day for 2 weeks), powdered electrolytes to ward off hypothermia-related cramps, and a ration heater. Wear jet-powered fins or a small thruster pack (electric, battery-operated) for boosts in currents. Add momentum-retaining gloves to grip fibrous hooks on nets/pillars. For emergencies, a lightweight inflatable raft or buoyant emergency pod (like a life raft ball) to surface clots in air pockets.
