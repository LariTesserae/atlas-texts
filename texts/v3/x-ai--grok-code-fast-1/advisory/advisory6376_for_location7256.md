### Immediate Preparations: Gear, Supplies, and Logistics

Aim for at least 6-12 months of supplies if you plan to stay long, scaled by your metabolic needs—humans can't just "adapt" to a mineral diet without consequences like kidney failure from crystalline ingesta.

- **Clothing and Protective Gear**: 
  - Layer with durable, heat-resistant fabrics like Nomex or aramid blends (firefighter suits) to handle the constant subterranean warmth (probably 70-80°F/21-27°C, humid and stagnant). The air is heavy with particulate dust that never settles, so wear full-body coverings: hooded jumpsuits with integrated gloves, boots, and face masks to filter fine mineral silt that could coat lungs and eyes. Use Gore-Tex or similar breathable synthetics to avoid overheating—sweat won't evaporate much in the still air.
  - Bring polarized goggles or full-face helmets with night-vision augmentations (military surplus or mountaineering gear). The faint phosphorescence and forge lights cast strange shadows, and crystalline veins can induce optical illusions or glimpses of "other plateaus" that might confuse your vision. Without wind or moisture, UV exposure isn't a risk, but eye strain from low light could cause migraines.
  - Footwear: Vibram-soled boots with extra padding; the particulate terrain crunches softly but doesn't compress like sand, so you'll feel every shift. Bring spares— the perpetual stillness means equipment won't degrade from weather, but overuse in vast traverses could wear them out quickly.
  - Miscellaneous: A lumbar pack with insulated compartments for temperature-sensitive items, and reflective tape on clothing/gecko boots for emergency signaling (though to whom? In such emptiness, it might just mark your location).

- **Shelter and Navigation Tools**:
  - Portable shelters: Inflatable tents or bivouac sacs made from reflective materials (like Mylar) to retain body heat without convection (air doesn't move). Set them up near forge vents for warmth—do *not* build permanent structures, as the world's stasis might reject change, causing collapses.
  - Navigation: GPS won't work (no satellites or magnetism, probably), so rely on inertial navigation devices (e.g., pedometers, compasses calibrated to the plateau's flat expanse). Mark paths with biodegradable or mineral-safe flagging tape—landmarks are kilometers apart, and the undulating terrain can hide them. - **Food, Water, and Sustenance Supplies**:
  - Water: Gallons of it—hydration is critical in dry, warm air. Purify with iodine tablets if needed, but pack at least 1-2 liters per day planned. No rehydration feasible here; condensation isn't a thing.
  - Food: Non-perishable, calorie-dense rations like MREs, jerky, energy bars, nuts, and gels. Aim for balanced nutrients—vitamins could stave off deficiencies from the mineral-heavy air.   - Medicine: Antidotes for mineral poisoning (if you ingest crystals—symptoms might include vertigo or visions); electrolytes for dehydration; painkillers, antacids, anti-nausea meds. Antibiotics for any self-inflicted wounds, since no natural healing factors exist. Sedatives for sleep induction— the stillness amplifies insomnia.
  - Energy Alternatives: Portable generators or solar panels to power devices. The industrial structures might have exposed cabling you could jury-rig, but beware of arcing electricity—it's erratic and could shock unpredictably.

- **Tools for Interaction and Survival**:
  - Climbing gear: For ridges or factory access—harnesses, ropes, pitons (though mineral surfaces might be too friable).
  - Analytical tools: A geologist's kit (magnifying glass, spectrometer) to examine crystals or particulate silt. Samples might encode realities, so bring containers to transport them safely.
  - Communication: Satellite phone or long-range radio (if multiverse travel allows signals; otherwise, useless). Emergency beacon flares visible in the dimness.
