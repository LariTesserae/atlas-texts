### Physical Protection
The High Weep's moisture and mineral surfaces demand armor-like clothing to prevent injury, hypothermia, and immersion in the element-saturated environment. The "wet shroud" of vapor and drips means everything is slick and cool, with a chill that could drop your core temperature if exposed.

- **Layered, Waterproof Outerwear with Insulation:** Start with a full-body suit made from durable, water-resistant synthetic materials like Gore-Tex or Kevlar blends (e.g., a reinforced climber's onesie or hazmat-style jacket/trousers). It must be breathable to wick away condensation, but impermeable to moisture to avoid constant soaking. Include high-collared, hood-integrated layers to protect your neck and head from drips that could run into cracks or clothing seams. Insulate with thick, fleece-lined undergarments—think wool or synthetic blends beneath—because the persistent chill (think 40-50°F / 4-10°C year-round) feels like standing in a foggy freezer. Add reflective panels or UV-blocking patches to deflect the unrelenting glare, which could burn exposed skin after prolonged exposure.
  
- **Footwear and Hand Protection:** Opt for heavy-duty, grippy boots with thick, non-slip soles (e.g., mountaineering boots with crampons for traction on stone waves and crystalline clusters). The ground's undulating, packed surface means slippery falls are common due to moisture and tight spaces where you must brace against crystals. Gloves are essential—thick, waterproof ones with reinforced palms to handle sharp mineral edges. Scuff or cut them in tight spots, and crystals could slice through fabric, causing lacerations that might not heal well in this barren, vibration-filled air.

- **Full-Face and Head Gear for Moisture and Impact:** A wide-brimmed, ventilated hat or hood isn't enough; go for a full-face shield or balaclava integrated with your suit to deflect drips. Moisture could collect in masks or eyes, leading to fog or irritation. For accidental impacts (e.g., bumping into overhangs), include a hard-shell helmet reminiscent of spelunking gear, padded to cushion against the porous, weeping rocks.

- **Body-Based Hazards:** The "glass fauna" (those quartz-shard beings) are slow and non-aggressive, but their blade-like forms could pose risks if stepped on or brushed—treat them like razor-sharp glass. Crystals vibrate subtly, potentially causing micro-vibrations that hum through touched surfaces; this might feel like mild tinnitus in your bones if prolonged. Avoid prolonged contact with clusters to prevent aches.

The High Weep assaults sight and sound relentlessly. Unprotected eyes could be blinded by kaleidoscopic refractions, and the roar could drive you mad.

- **Multi-Sensory Balance:** The brightness and noise overlap, creating dizziness. Practice grounding techniques before arrival, like meditation in dim, quiet spaces, to train your brain to filter overload.
