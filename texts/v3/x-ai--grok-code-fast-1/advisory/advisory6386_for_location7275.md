### Physical Health and Biological Preparation
The cavern's warmth and desiccation would hit your biology hard—your body isn't adapted to this mineral-sustaining "non-aqueous alchemy." Prepare as if heading to a desert but inside a pressure cooker with no respite. Focus on hydration, heat management, and pathogen/spore exposure, as the ecosystem sustains life without water viahumidity and salts.

Supplement with electrolyte packets or salt tablets to mimic how locals "inhale" and distill the cavern's salty aerosol—your lungs aren't built for that, so fake it by adding sodium and potassium to what you drink. Stockpile non-perishable, high-calorie foods like energy bars, dried fruits, nuts, and jerky; local fungi and fronds might be edible (inhabitants bake resins into food), but test everything cautiously—bring iodine tablets or a portable water purifier in case you find any sneaky liquid pockets. Aim to consume 4-6 liters of fluid daily to stave off potassium imbalances from the pervasive salts, which could cause muscle cramps or heart issues. If you overdo it, you'll sweat uncontrollably in the heat, exacerbating dehydration—find shade during "shifts" when the bioluminescent glow wanes slightly.
  
- **Heat and Humidity Adaptation:** The air is consistently warm (like a steam room at 80-90°C but without true sweat-evaporative cooling) and hazy from fungal vapors, which can feel oppressive. Train your body beforehand: Spend weeks acclimating in a sauna or hot yoga classes to build tolerance, reducing the risk of heat exhaustion (dizziness, nausea, faintness). Dress in layers for the balmy embrace, but pack anti-heat clothing once there. Inhabitants exhale "crystals like breath-fog," so protect your respiratory system—bring a silk scarf or thin breathable mask to filter spores and mineral dust, especially if the dense fungal mats release irritants. Avoid overexertion; the cavern's heat rises from below, making vertical climbs (on those pinnacles) even more taxing. If you have chronic conditions (e.g., asthma), consult a multiverse medic for inhalers that work in sulfurous/mineral environments.

- **Pathogen and Contaminant Risks:** The air teems with spores and salts, and vegetation is resinous—your immune system might react with allergies, fungal infections, or worse. Bring a basic first-aid kit with antihistamines, antifungals (like clotrimazole), and antibiotics for puncture wounds (from spiky vines or climbing mishaps). The ground is abrasive (crystalline sands crunching like shells), so risk foot/leg infections; pack sterile gauze, antiseptic wipes, and thick-soled shoes. Touching mineral veins could expose you to iron oxides or unspecified salts—wear gloves, and wash exposed skin with dilute vinegar (a humble disinfectant) if possible. Reproduction here is "lethargic," but avoid close contact with wildlife or locals to dodge parasitical transfers; bring insect repellent if small fauna (lizards, rodents) bite.

- **General Fitness:** This isn't a hike—it's a scramble through vertical chaos. Build core strength and climbing stamina beforehand (rock climbing or stair training). The dense population means crowded paths, so improve balance to avoid toppling into craters. Dental issues? Pack floss; the salty air might erode enamel. Sleep will be erratic in the perpetual dusk/noise—aim for rest cycles mimicking the locals' slow rhythms.
