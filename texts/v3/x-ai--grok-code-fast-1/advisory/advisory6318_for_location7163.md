- **Base Layers:** Moisture-wicking synthetics like merino wool or polyester blends (e.g., thermal underwear pants and long-sleeve tops). Avoid cotton—it absorbs water and clings horribly. Pack 4-5 sets; you'll need to rotate as sweat soaks through. Include socks made of similar materials to prevent fungal foot infections (the ground's perpetually wet, so athlete's foot or equivalents are likely).
  
- **Outer Layers:** Lightweight, waterproof jackets and pants (e.g., Gore-Tex or seam-sealed nylon) that can handle drizzle but aren't bulky. These should vent to release steam buildup. Tuck pants into high waterproof boots to keep out silt mud and fungal spores.

- **Footwear:** Over-the-knee, waterproof rubber boots (like muck boots or Wellingtons) with good grip soles—essential for the soft, slipping silt and puddles. The ground is uneven basins and curves, so something that molds to your foot without blisters. Bring 2-3 pairs; they'll wear out from constant drying/re-wetting. Liners for dryness.

- **Head and Hands:** Wide-brimmed hats with mesh vents for sun/shade (though the weak sun is filtered, it prevents heat stroke). Sunglasses to combat glare off wet surfaces in rare clear patches. Lightweight gloves for handling fungal mats or tools without direct skin contact—they could be mildly irritating.

- **Accessories:** Scarves or neck gaiters for covering mouth/nose against spore-laden air during migrations or stirrings. Belts or harnesses for tying gear securely in the windless, humid breeze.

- **Intuition Tip:** Test everything in a sauna or hot shower beforehand to simulate the stickiness. Pack extras for spontaneous drenching; a change of clothes mid-hike could prevent chafing rash in the perpetual twilight (you won't see the sun set, but exhaustion builds).
