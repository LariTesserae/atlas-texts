The expanse unfolds like a petrified dream, a high plateau perched on the shoulders of primordial ridges that have weathered into jagged, near-fractal decay over eons unfathomable. These are not mere mountains; they are the remnants of cataclysmic upheavals from a time before water ever pooled or flowed—ancient spires and corrugations of mineral bone, eroded not by storms or rivers but by the relentless abrasion of time itself, grinding them down to a final, settled form. The plateau itself is a vast, regional sprawl, miles upon miles of undulating flats broken by shallow valleys and truncated peaks, each feature a monument to endurance, scored with furrows that speak of forces long extinguished. No dust devils rise here, no wind to carry them; the air hangs heavy, still, infused with the warmth of subterranean currents that pulse eternally beneath the surface, heating the ground to a constant, sunless glow. Light is a fugitive thing, barely a whisper—perhaps a faint phosphorescence from deep fractures in the rock, or the dim embers of long-abandoned industrial forges leaking through vents in the earth, casting everything in a palette of bruised grays and aching blues. Shadows dominate, absolute in the voids between those scant points of illumination, swallowing distances and blurring edges.

This is a world of minerals beyond mere stone or sand; it's a construct of dry, crystalline lattices that glint faintly in the rare flickers of light, built not from aqueous dissolution but from the slow alchemy of vacuum decay and thermal pressures. Salt-like efflorescences coat some surfaces, but these are not salts born of evaporation—they're hypermineral aggregates, veined with iridescent threads that twist like fossilized veins in a body long turned to dust. No water etches patterns here; instead, the terrain bears the scars of ancient sublimations, where gases once condensed and reformed into glassy sheets or powdery drifts that blanket vast swaths in a fine, shifting particulate. It's sparse, this density—scarce outcroppings rise like solitary sentinels, kilometers apart, their bases lost in smothering embankments of particulate silt that never compacts, never drifts in breezes that don't exist. Between them stretches emptiness, measured emptinesses that amplify the stillness: you could traverse for hours without encountering another landmark, the uniformity of the plane broken only by occasional low mounds, eroded to the point of abstraction, their forms suggesting crumpled folds in an eternal origami of decay.

Amid this mineral desolation, the urban construct emerges as a riddle, a layered accretion of built forms that feel as ancient as the rocks they perch upon. Towns and cities here are not huddled clusters but spread-out constellations of structures, their density optimized for the vastness—industrial hubs scattered across the plateau, connected by faint, linear traces: perhaps buried conduits for energy or substances long since commoditized in this world. The buildings are factories and mills, scaled for mechanized production on a grand, humanless scale, their facades hulking shells of warped metal alloys that have oxidized to a dull, matte sheen under the relentless erosion. Stacks vent no smoke, for there's no fuel source that burns; instead, they hum faintly—engines running idempotent cycles, churning ores from the depths, smelting them in forges that never cool, assembling parts in assembly lines frozen in perpetual grind. Electricity arcs sparsely through exposed cabling, illuminating control panels in erratic bursts that reveal interiors cluttered with conveyor belts coated in mineral grime, tools suspended mid-task in a stasis unbreakable. It's industrial not for human toil but for an instinctual, self-sustaining rigor—the machines maintain themselves, or perhaps each other, in a system of nested redundancies that has never known interruption.

Fauna flickers rarely at the edges, ethereal and unrecognizable creatures woven from the weave of weirdness that permeates this place. They are not animals in any biological sense, but presences: translucent filaments that phase through the particulate drifts, pulsing with internal lights that mimic the plateau's faint glow. Rare indeed—one might glimpse a cluster of spindly forms coalescing around a factory vent, feeding on the thermal effluvia or the electromagnetic leakage, their forms shifting like gas clouds in slow eddies, only to dissipate into silence when approached. No cries or movements betray them; they are as quiet as the rest, their existence a curious anomaly in this sterile ecology. There are rules here, perhaps evolutionary dictums inscribed in the mineral matrix itself: no vegetative life, of course, but even the fungal hints are absent—only these strange, emergent entities inhabit the fringes, bound by the equilibrium that forbids true change. Dirt and decay are concepts swept away; biological cycles collapsed into mineral stability, where decomposition means the slow sublimation of solid to subliminated dust, reforming elsewhere in crystalline patterns that repeat infinitely.

Sounds are a rarity, a tapestry of near-silence woven with the faintest harmonics. The distant thrum of machines, muffled by rock, emerges as a low vibration underfoot rather than noise—pumps cycling in abyssal wells, conveyors rattling emptily. Occasionally, a metallic groan escapes a structure as some internal pressure shifts imperceptibly, a sigh in the void. But mostly, quiet reigns, the kind that amplifies the mind's whispers; in darkness so profound, even footsteps on the particulate terrain deaden to soft crunches, disappearing into the expanse. The atmosphere itself contributes to this hush—no wind whispers, no distant echoes from neighboring valleys, for the elevation isolates each pocket of sound like sealed chambers.

Dynamic, it is not; this landscape exists in a frozen equilibrium, where time has halted its gradients and settled into negation. Temperatures hover at the same warm constancy, no diurnal shifts or seasonal whims. The erosion ceased long ago, leaving everything in poised decay—no further crumbling of peaks, no new formations rising from below. Machines operate with unwavering rhythm, their cycles locked in loops that neither accelerate nor falter. Change is an alien concept here, perhaps a myth from earlier eras; instead, the world persists as it is, a self-contained tableau where every element reinforces the others in perpetuity. To walk these paths is to glimpse permutations without progression: a factory might hum a fraction louder at rare intervals, but only to reset, unchanged. Fauna phases in and out, but their appearances are ritual echoes, not evolutions.

And yet, strangeness seeps through, a quality that twists the familiar into the uncanny. The crystals that vein the rocks aren't mere minerals but repositories, encoding faint, quandary visions—gazing into them reveals glimpses of other plateaus, mirrored infinities where urban sprawls diverge into impossibility. The industrial outputs? Not machines in the human vein, but proto-artefacts assembling themselves into forms that defy utility—spherical devices that rotate eternally in static orbits, or latticework frameworks that shift subtly in the darkness, forming geometric alphabets in a language no throat could voice. Rules of the world? An alchemy where energy patterns substitute for life; sustenance drawn from the thermal veins, existence tethered to the mineral cycle. In the sparse lights, shadows sometimes congeal into illusory depths, invitations to voids that aren't there, or reflections of structures that mirror the real ones in warped exaggeration. It's a place where nothing decays, yet everything feels like a relic of dreams unending, a high, dry, industrial void unfolding without bottom or boundary.
