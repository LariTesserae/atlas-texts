In the shadowed depths of the Encircling Abyss, where the ancient sea broth once claimed the skies and reshaped the world after the Great Subsidence—when the waters rose not in fury but in an endless, patient crawl, pulling the land down like a shroud—there lies a singular place called the Hanging Grove. It is not a vast sprawl, nor an endless plain; it is bounded, intimate, like a vast cathedral chamber hollowed from the sea itself. Four stone pillar-mountains rise from the abyssal floor far below, converging into a broad, undulating plateau some fifteen leagues across at its widest, perched at an elevation that defies the fathomless pull of the depths. This elevation is no mere accident—it is a relic of the old dry world, a mesa of weathered basalt and metamorphic rock that has stood resolute against the tides for eons, its edges carved into dramatic, sloping cliffs by the slow, grinding erosion of currents that have flowed and eddied here since the waters came.

The Hanging Grove is fully immersed, a world drowned and transformed, where every breath is of water—brine-laden, cool as the lingering chill of forgotten polar winds, the temperature steady and unyielding at depths where sunlight still penetrates through a crystal-clear veil. The sea here is not the frothing tempest of storms above but a vast, pressure-laden stillness, with faint ridges of salinity gradients creating subtle haloclines that ripple gently over weeks, layering the waters into strata of density. Saltwater clings to the plateau's summit, sculpting every surface into organic curves softened by millennia of microscopic dissolution, where minerals and solubles have long since reached a weary balance. Erosion here is the art of patience: slow-worn hollows in the stone where eddies swirl endlessly, depositing fine sediments that crust the edges in calcified patterns, like veined marble etched by unseen sculptor-winds. The plateau itself slopes up from the four pillars, rising in a gradual incline to the central apex, a broad elevated bowl where the waters pool deepest at the heart, shallowing toward the rims where the thermal gradients allow just enough buoyancy for the drifting ecologies to anchor and thrive.

Dominating this submerged haven is the vegetation—almost ubiquitous, a tapestry of living mass that has overrun and redefined the space. It is not the aggressive tangle of a surface forest but a primal integration, where plants are not mere inhabitants but the very substrate, architecture, and medium of existence. Rooted algal mats form the "ground," vast carpets of fibrous seaweed interwoven with colonial coral thallus, sprouting in sparse clusters that leave wide swaths of open, echoing space between them. These mats creep upward into the water column, twining into vertical gardens: kelp fronds as tall as ancient oaks, their stipes thickened into structural beams, braided with creeping vines of luminescent phosphorescent algae that pulse softly in the filtered light. Where the urban craftedness emerges, ancient dwellers have used primitive tools—flint adzes, bone chisels, and tensile fibers harvested from the same kelp—to shape this biomass into habitable forms. Elevated platforms are woven dwellings, nests of interlaced plant fibers reinforced with calcified shells, suspended on buoyant rafts of cork-like fungal growths that rise and fall with the slowest tidal rhythms. Stairways spiral up twisted trunks of coral-accommodated rhodophyta, their ramps engineered through pruning and seeding to create layered terraces, each one a "floor" in the submerged city. These constructions are sparse, their density low and deliberate, leaving vast corridors of open water—aisles wide enough for schools of creatures to dart without hindrance, where emptiness balances the organic sprawl. The vegetation is alive in symbiosis, every frond contributing to a pulse that maintains the structure: root systems filter sediments, photosynthetic zones pump oxygen, and vines exude resins that bind the edifice against the incessant, subtle currents. Yet it is unusual, this verdant dominion—whispers speak of the plants as more than passive growth, sentient in their slow, vegetative way, responding to the rhythms of the grove with subtle shifts: a wall of kelp parting for favored dwellers, or blooms that alter the flow of hallways during the moon's distant pull high above.

Fauna teems here in abundance, a riot of life woven into the sparse but verdant urbanity. Fish dart in glittering clouds—sleek mackerel and silvery herrings navigating the open corridors, while territorial wrasses defend etched trails in the algal streets. Larger denizens patrol the edges: sharks with ancient scars, floating lazily in the thermal layers, and rays undulating like living carpets between the platforms. Invertebrates flourish in the plant-sculpted niches—squid ink their way through vine-choked tunnels, octopuses reshaping soft coral lairs into ingenious dens with salvaged rock and discarded tools, while crabs scuttle along root systems, their pincers busy sorting through the benthic detritus. Birds? None, no—those are memories of the air—but avian echoes persist in the wings of giant manta rays that glide overhead like forgotten skies, their fins laced with symbiotic barnacles. Amphibious hybrids roam the structured edges, frog-like salamanders clambering the kelp ramps with adhesive feet, feeding on aplenty: spirulina clouds bloom in quiet eddies, plankton swarms churn in the central bowl, and the vegetation itself yields fruits—gelatinous pods of harvested algal cysts that drift like offerings. Nurtured by primitive husbandry—nets of woven fiber set to catch the currents, hand-dug pits in the mats for spore propagation—the fauna integrates into the urban weave, their movements a constant, shifting presence that fills the sparsity without crowding.

Sounds reverberate loudly through this aqueous realm, amplified by the dense medium and the enclosed scale of the grove. The constant rush of currents against the cliff faces and through hollowed depressions creates a deep, resonant hum, punctuated by sharp cracks: fish grinding algae to powder with pharyngeal teeth, or the pop of expanding gas bladders in buoyant organs. Fauna adds layers—pods of dolphins echolocating in playful chatter that echoes off stone and stalk, their clicks a staccato thunder; the low-frequency groans of whale songs carrying from distant abyssal trenches, distorted and looping through the kelp forests like organ notes in a submerged cathedral. Mortar-and-pestle sounds from urchins scraping stone, the wet slaps of octopus tentacles rearranging their lairs, and the chorus of unseen pit critters—burrowing worms and molting crustaceans—creating a symphony of scrabbles and squeaks that never ceases. Even the plants contribute: fronds rustling in the flow, releasing bubbles that hiss and pop like distant fireworks, building to crescendos during the rare, gradual upwelling events that stir the waters over seasons.

Here, the rules of the world are dictated by slow change and enduring equilibrium. Tech remains primitive, handed down through ancestral grottings: dwellers carve bone hooks to fish from elevated platforms, weave nets from vinestrain husks for gathering, and use obsidian-edged tools to prune the living architecture, fostering a craft that mends with nature's own inversions. There are no fires, no machines—only the patient work of hands and tides, where innovation means coaxing a vine to grow in a new direction over generations. Society clusters in sparse enclaves, nomadic yet rooted, with taboos against over-harvesting the dual nature of plant and home. Rituals honor the Weird—offerings of scavenged metal remnants from the old world, dropped into the central bowl to feed the slow calcification, evoking the Sentience within the growths. Dynamic is glacial: a building settlement accrues sediment over decades before dwellers transplant to fresher spots; thermal shifts gently melt ice-like algal blooms across cycles that span lifetimes; and the plateau, once amassing, now settles, its erosion a guardian against upheaval. In this Hanging Grove, existence is a meditation on immersion and growth, where the loud pulse of life echoes in the stillness, and the boundaries of the watery chamber hold worlds within worlds, unchanged yet ever-unfolding in the cool, drowned heart of the depths.
