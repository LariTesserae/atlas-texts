You step onto this high plateau, or what feels like one—a vast, unbroken expanse stretching out for valleys and ridges that blur into the horizon, regional in scope yet empty enough to make your gaze wander without end. It's a place of extreme elevation, where the ground lifts abruptly from distant foothills into peaks that pierce the sky like jagged spearheads, weathered and ancient. The stone here isn't mere granite or basalt; it's a calcified marrow of the earth, veined with iridescent minerals that catch the unyielding light and refract it into prismatic bursts. Erosion has long since shaped it: carved canyons meander through the plateaus like old veins, their walls softened by eons of wind and the occasional trickle of water, but nothing erodes further—everything is in perfect, frozen balance. The air is cool, a steady chill that seeps through fabric, never warming even under the eternal brightness. There's no sun in the sky, at least none you can pinpoint; the light emanates from everywhere and nowhere, a diffuse glow that bathes the landscape in perpetual day, illuminating every crack and crevice without shadows or respite.

Dampness pervades without dominating—the kind that lingers in pockets, like dew on lichen-scarred rocks or the sheen of condensation on cavern mouths lower in the gorges. Intermittent mists roll through, silent at first but swelling into brief, sharp rains that drench the stone before evaporating just as swiftly. Rivers? Nothing steady; instead, thin ribbons of water pool in hollows, trickling downward through slots in the rock before vanishing into deeper fissures. You can smell the moisture in the air, earthy and sharp, mixing with the faint mineral tang of the stone. Vegetation clings sparsely to this extremity: tufts of wiry moss in the hollows, lichens spreading like brittle lace over exposed ridges, the occasional stunted shrub rooted in a sheltered nook, its leaves waxen and curled against the chill. There's no forest here, no forest anywhere—trees would struggle against the elevation and the emptiness—but these sparse greens dot the terrain like isolated sentinels, tenacious and unchanging, untouched by seasons or growth.

Sound assaults the ears, a deafening cacophony that echoes across the open spaces, amplified in their hollow vastness. It begins with a low rumble, like the earth's own breathing, but swells into a chorus of animal voices—squawks, bellows, chirps—that layer upon one another without rhythm or reprieve. Fauna teems here, abundant in its strangeness, filling the emptiness with life that pulses but never evolves. Flocks of winged creatures swirl overhead, their bodies elongated and feathered in hues that shift in the bright light—violet wings becoming emerald when they bank through mists. They dive and climb in perpetually looping patterns, their calls a thunderous barrage, as if they're singing the same eternal hymn. Below, on the plateaus, herds of quadrupeds roam, beasts with scaled hides and antler-like appendages that glow faintly, moving in synchronized pods that cover miles before reversing course, never straying from their paths. Smaller creatures abound too: swarms of invertebrate hordes that skitter across the lichen, chattering with mandibles that click like clockwork gears. And birds of prey, enormous and solitary, perch on ridge spines, their screeches ripping through the air like tearing fabric. All this life is unusual, bent by the world's frozen rules—animals that never age or die, their populations unchanging, behaviors governed by invisible cycles that mimic movement but yield no progress. You spot one herd pausing in a sunlit basin: their forms ripple, almost molting into new shapes before snapping back, as if the bright light pulls at their very essence, warping them subtly without lasting harm. The deafening sound never wanes; it thunders in your skull, a constant pressure that drowns thought, yet it too remains static—no crescendo or fade, just this equilibrium roar.

This world operates under rules of absolute stillness; nothing changes, no gradients intrude. The weather-electricity sparks in the mists during the intermittent rains, harmless arcing that illuminates the droplets like living veins, but the storms recur at precise intervals, neither intensifying nor abating. Temperature holds at its cool constant, unaffected by the elevation's height or the fauna's heat. Erosion, too, is a relic of the past—rocks lie as they've always sat, paths worn but unaltered. If life predates humans here—and by the primitive echoes in the air, it does—they might dwell in hidden clefts using bone tools carved from fallen creature parts, fire brands unlit in the perpetual light, hides sewn into simple garments to ward the chill. But no structures mar the view: no huts, no walls, just the land's raw form, pristine and eternal. Tech remains in its early stirrings, perhaps embodied in the fauna themselves—those mandibled swarms wielding sharpened claws like crude spears to fend off predators, or the flying flocks weaving nests from woven moss that mimic rudimentary traps.

As you traverse farther, the density of emptiness reveals itself: vast tracts where nothing but rock and light prevail, features spaced so wide apart that a single ridge might span the horizon unbroken. A canyon yawns miles away, its depth plunging into shadowless gloom, while a fog-shrouded plateau waits beyond, its sparse greens flickering in the windless chill. Fauna clusters here and there— a pod of scaled walkers pausing near a trickle-pool, their glow reflecting in the water— but the spaces between are voids, amplifying the deafening symphony into something almost supernatural. The weirdness deepens as shadows evade the light: surfaces reflect visions of distant valleys that aren't there, as if the rock remembers older landscapes. One ridge you've neared shimmers with illusory paths, tempting the eye to follow, but the way remains solid stone. The world's equilibrium binds it all—the unchanging dynamic that keeps this extreme elevation, this cool dampness, this abundant yet static life, in perpetual poise. Time falters here; moments freeze mid-stride, the deafening calls echoing proofs of a reality unwilling to advance. It's a place that defies summation, growing stranger with every step: a mountain exodus where evolution paused, and existence hums its unending note.
