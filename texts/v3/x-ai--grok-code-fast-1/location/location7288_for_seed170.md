You arrive on a vast highland plateau, a expanse so open that the horizon seems to loop back on itself under a sky of perpetual molten brass. This isn't Earth— by any stretch, by any rule of familiar physics—but some stitched-together remnant of a dying multiverse, where even the quantum foam has hardened into something graspable, fractal, and dangerously alive. The ground beneath your feet (or whatever semblance of feet you bring) is a scorched skin of igneous basalt shot through with veins of crystallized engineers' dreams: pistons embedded in the rock like ossified veins, silent for now, but poised in a sleep that isn't eternal. No water here, not a drop, not a whisper of humidity; it's a void-world, where the very concept of moisture has been boiled off in conceptual fires long ago. Dryness reigns absolute—a mineral void sealed in its own glassy intolerance to change, where even air is a hesitant vapor of electrocharged silt, so thin it feels like breathing granulated light.

The heat is relentless, cloying like a forge's breath pressed against steel, ticking just shy of that point where flesh (or its analogs) begins to sublime. It's not the blunt hammer of a sun's glare but a brooding, industrial scorch, diffused through the air as if enormous factories—god-like refractories—lurk just beyond the next ridgeline, melting the very atoms of distance. Yet nothing is built here, no smokestacks or turbines claw at the sky; tech infuses the land itself, a viral integration. Bolts erupt from the stone like metallic fungi, gears grind eternally in slow, hypnotic cycles beneath the soil, and spindly wires curl through the air, humming with damped electricity that never arcs free. Some artificera persist: rusted conveyors half-buried in lava flows, belts of tarnished alloy hauling nothing but phantom ore from seam to seem as they creep along at a glacial pace. Erosion has worn them down to essentials—pitted cylinder walls, flanges etched by aeons of patience—until they blend seamlessly with the terrain, unrecognizable as human artifice. This is a post-life mineral world, where machinery was not crafted but congealed, erupting from the crust like thought-forms manifesting in silicon and slag. No structures rise; the land itself is the machine, a planetary engine idling in perpetual half-cycle, slowly turning heat into potential without ever firing.

Elevation defines this place: you're perched high, perhaps kilometers above some forgotten lowland basin swallowed by void, where gravity warps subtly—as if the world is curved inward by its own convoluted logic. Cliffs drop away in abrupt, weathered scarps, long-settled into fractal patterns that repeat without variation, like code looped on itself. The air thins here, but density is its own strangeness—space is vast, a near-void where distances balloon unnaturally. Objects—rocks, vents, the occasional industrial relic—float in isolation, separated by gulfs so profound that traveling between them feels like crossing impossible spans, time dilating with each step. It's emptier than a starless sky, hollowed out by some cosmic extermination event that left only echoes of presence, whispers of mass scattered across a canvas larger than worlds.

Yet fauna overwhelms this desolation, bursting forth in defiance of all biological norms. Swarms teem everywhere, nameless hordes that eclipse the vastness with their sheer number and clamor. They aren't animals as you know them—no ecology binds them, no predation or evolution that you'd recognize. These are the products of reality's breakdown, entities woven from breakdown: filament-beings that resemble tangled fuses come alive, sparking with inner fires; crystal-shards that jabber like lodestones possessed, drawing metal filings into kaleidoscopic dances; oily masses that bubble up from geothermal fissures, reforming into lists of infinite nonsense. They flood the landscape in overlapping waves—tens of millions per acre, perhaps billions across the plateau's expanse—chattering, clanging, roaring in a dissonant symphony that drowns out any thought of silence. The sound is loud, not harsh but omnipresent, a cacophony of industrial auras: the stutter of pistons that aren't there, the whine of wires singing tensile ballads, the thrum of the beasts' bodies in chorus. It's unsettlingly melodic at times, like a factory hymn twisted by madness, each note a fracture in the fabric of what-is.

Weirdness permeates every particle here; reality isn't stable, not even in its slow, patient flux. Heat shimmers morph into portals—brief, flickering wormholes that reveal glimpses of other worlds, other rules: a momentary flash of watery depths in a desert that forbids them, or a glimpse of verdant jungles erupting from the void only to dissolve back into mineral static. Fauna occasionally phase through dimensions, their forms glitching like corrupted data, reassembling in configurations that defy logic— a swarm reforming as a single, incomprehensible monolith before RTF-ing back to horde. The tech echoes this instability; conveyors sometimes warp into Möbius strips, looping endlessly on themselves, while gears grind in directions perpendicular to time, birthing echoes of machinery yet unborn. Change is slow, almost imperceptible—cycles that span eons, weak gradients of heat and vibration that ripple across the land at a snail's pace. A vent might sputter once a century, a gear lay dormant for millennia before a micro-shift realigns it, sending faint seismic tremors through the plateau. Erosion advances glacial, sculpting new patterns in the stone that feel deliberate, as if the world is contemplating its own form, editing itself over epochs.

The atmosphere hangs heavy with the scent of ionized musk, hot as forge slag and gritty as pulverized alloy. Light filters normally, yet warped—sunless perhaps, but radiant from the sky's own convective glow, casting everything in sepia tones that blend metal and flesh into unseamly amalgamations. No plants interrupt the sterility; biology here predates or perverts the photosynthetic, existing in simplified, fungal-mimetic states. The fauna's ichor—metallic oozes and crystalline exudates—forms thin films on the rocks, etching patterns that look like circuit boards come to life, but they dissolve slowly in the heat, reforming in endless, gradual cycles.

In this world, rules fracture even as they hold: motion through space might strand you in a loop where time folds back, or interacting with the "tech" could merge you with it, your form becoming a new component in the engine. Survival depends on stillness, on syncing with the slow dynamic—move too fast, and the reality heaves, vomiting swarms or distortions that unravel presence. But linger, and the place opens: quiet moments where the overwhelming faunal roar resolves into eerie harmonies, and the desolate heat reveals veins of purpose, as if this plateau is a colossal, empty machine waiting for an operator who never existed. It's not hostile, just indifferent, a thought-form of entropy given infinite space to ruminate. As you traverse, the landscape shifts subtly—not in leaps, but in accretions of detail, each vista unfolding like a page in a disintegrating book. Cliffs reveal new strata of embedded cogs, swarms part momentarily to reveal pocket voids where gravity inverts, and the loud symphony modulates into a bass tremor that resonates with the world's idling heart. Here, on this hot, high, empty plain of unreal industry, you are nothing but another temporary glitch in the code, vivid and vanishing.
