The abyss swallows you whole, or rather, it rises to meet you— an endless liquid expanse pressing in from all sides, cold as forgotten tombs, a briny shroud that fills your lungs if you let it, or whispers of salinity if you breathe it in through gills you've always had. This is not a natural sea, though: the world beneath the waves is sculpted, every curve and plateau hewn by hands long vanished, by architects whose tools were not machines but perhaps the slow churn of currents they've invited or the patient erosion of eons. There is no metal here, no visible technology—nothing forged in fires or hummed with electricity—only the raw brute force of construction on a scale that dwarfs continents. Think of it as a submerged metropole, eternal and self-repairing, but abandoned to the tides.

Your vantage is high, improbably so in such depths. You perch on a pinnacle that juts upward like the spire of some drowned cathedral, its surface etched with the deep grooves of ancient wear: wind-worn stone might look this way, but down here, it's water's relentless kiss that carves, smoothing once-sharp edges into rounded, tactile forms. Two miles below stretches the flats—vast, flattened expanses of this built world, where erosion has settled everything into its final shapes, primordial in their stillness yet scarred by the tireless rasp of currents. The pinnacle you're on culminates in what might be a balcony or a ledge wide enough for a procession, but sparse in its details: pillars rise intermittently, their mineral-like bases strung with translucent threads that glow faintly, emitting a dim, twilight-grade luminescence. Light here is scarce, seeping from crevices in the structure itself or from bioluminescent motes that drift lazily in the sparse swirls.

Temperature clings to the bone, a numbing chill that seeps through your skin like liquid nitrogen diluted in seawater— nowhere hotter than the surface melt of a distant glacier, everywhere reminding you that heat is an intruder. The air, if you can call it that, is thick with suspended particulates—tiny mineral flakes from the built world's decay, swirling in the turbulent eddies that govern everything. Dynamic is the rule here: the water pulses with chaotic energy, not like storms that rage and pass, but like the constant reweaving of a tapestry by unseen hands. Currents twist and reverse unpredictably, lifting you off your feet one moment, dropping you into tranquil spirals the next; structures flex and groan under the pressure, their surfaces rippling as if alive, cracks knitting shut only to tear open elsewhere in an anhill frenzy of destruction and rebirth. It's not destruction for ruin's sake—new forms emerge, perhaps a tower collapsing into a cascading slope of rubble that rebuilds itself into a series of interlocking shelves, or fauna-driven spires where creatures mob together into living scaffolding.

The scale of it opens up as you gaze outward: regional in scope, this landscape sprawls for hundreds of leagues, a multi-featured dominion of elevations that rise and fall in undulating waves. From your high perch, the vista reveals interspersed plateaus—sparse, with vast voids betweenthents, emptiness dominating the view like a grid of absence. To the east, a ridge of stepped terraces, each level eroded to a brushed smoothness, acid-etched by mineral-rich fluids leaching from the built substrate. Southward, a valley-like depression, not carved by rivers but by the weight of sedimented water pressure, forming a scattering of narrow canyons where visibility drops to mere feet in the dim murk. To the west, an array of floating islets—built fragments that have broken loose and now bob in the currents, sparse islands in a sparse sea, their surfaces colonized by fauna that lend them weird, organic mobility. Density here is sparse throughout; you can scan for miles and see only isolated landmarks: a lone archway spanning a chasm, its stonework peppered with pocked erosion; a cluster of bulbous domes huddled in the distance, more suggestion than presence; or the skeletal remnants of what might have been bridges, now frayed into dangling tendrils swaying with the turbulence.

What is built, here? Everything. There is no natural substrate—no bedrock born of volcanoes or tectonics, no primordial rock predating this world's architecture. It's all constructed, layer upon layer of fabricated matter: vitreous minerals pressed into existence by some pre-tool alchemy, perhaps the byproducts of hyper-evolved civilizations long extinct, or the slow secretions of entities now indistinguishable from the landscape itself. The materials feel ancient—volcanic glass fused with spongy aggregates that absorb and refract the dim light, creating prismatic shifts in the water. Sounds are mostly quiet, a hush broken only by the muffled gurgle of currents looping through hollow chambers, the soft scrape of eroded surfaces shifting like sand dunes, or the occasional pop of pressure diffusers releasing trapped gas from the built gaps. Weapons of voice would be drowned here, reduced to bubbles rising lazily; even fauna maintain a hushed communion, their calls swallowed by the liquid medium.

And the ecology? Fauna abound, swelling the sparse densities with motion—teeming despite the barrenness, adapted to a world without vegetatives, their sustenance drawn from mineral seeps or chemosynthetic vents woven into the fabric of the constructions. They glide through the turbulent flows, schools of elongated fish-like forms with translucent hides that shimmer dim phosphorescence, their fins fringed with cilia that catch eddying particulates. Larger leviathans slumber on the high terraces, coiled masses of flesh clad in armored plates grown directly from the built stone, exhaling slow puffs of luminescent vapor that stabilize the currents around them. Stranger presences emerge: ambulatory pods that scuttle across the pinnacles, pod-like beings with multiple appendages sprouting crystal spines, harvesting microscopic ore particulates that trickle from the eroding surfaces. Interspersed are pulsating nodes—faunal or symbiotes?—that adhere to the structures, blooming briefly in turbulent surges only to deflate in quieter phases. In the depths of the canyons, denser swarms gather: eel analogs twisting in chaotic knots, their bodies entwining with the rails and beams of the built world, rerouting currents like living hydraulics. Some fauna verge on the mechanical, with protruding gears or conduits that mimic the constructed landscape, blurring the line between organism and architecture. Their abundance is balanced by the sparsity—a sparse swarm here, a lone behemoth there—leaving room for the emptiness, the quiet voids where less turbulent pockets allow meditation on the weird ecology: how these creatures thrive without photosynthesis, their energies fed by the world's own turbulent metabolism.

Rules of this world? Change is the constant, but ancient erosion ensures it arrives in slow crescendos—the built forms may warp and rebuild, but the overarching stability of age prevails, flattening novelties into enduring features. Water drowns and sustains, yet submerges everything; no escape upward to drier realms. Tech is absent, all advancements forgotten or erased, leaving only biological improvisation on a scaffold of making. Life persists in the cold, the dim, the sparse, but with a twist of unusual resilience: creatures that inhabit prismatic illusions within the glass-like surfaces, or symbiomedia that swarm structures to form protective blooms during peak turbulence. Life without plants feels incomplete, yet here it flourishes in mineral symbiosis—fauna drawing from crystalline veins that pulse with stored energies, exhaled as heat or light in the chill.

As you drift from your perch, riding a capricious updraft, the landscape asserts itself further: a colossal aqueduct engineered into the ridge to your left, its channels etched deep by erosion, now rerouting currents in a stabilized flow; salt flats of built crystallizations where the sparse fauna rake patties for nutrient-rich scraps; roaming clouds of motile motes that cluster and dissipate, illuminating transient architectures born of turbulence. In the far horizon, where elevation peaks in distant mounts, pinprick flashes hint at deeper instabilities—perhaps eruptions of built matter reforming into twin towers that collapse in synchronized chaos. The world breathes, quietly, turbulently, a submerged construct in perpetuity, cold and dim and ever-shifting, where abundance teems in the voids and the ancient makers' imprint endures through erosion's patient hand. Here, you are part of the flow, or you are stones in the edifice—your presence merely another element in the endless reweave of form.
