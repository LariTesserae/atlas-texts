In the high, cragged shoulders of the world, where peaks jut like the fresh teeth of some eternal, unyielding jaw, the city of Eldridge has stood for ages—it has stood, and it will stand, unchanging. Here, in this realm of frozen equilibrium, time is not a river but a still pool, reflecting itself endlessly. Nothing shifts, nothing erodes, nothing decays. The sharp rocks that crowd the skyline—flint-edged spires and knife-blade ridges—emerged from the earth's womb only eons ago, their features raw and unworn, as if the planet paused mid-birth and never exhaled. These formations are young, yes, but they have been held in perpetual youth by an unseen law, a stipulation of the world's fabric that forbids the softening touch of wind or rain. Erosion here is arrested, a mere threat, never fulfilled. Sharp gullies slice between boulder clusters, and serrated cliffs plunge into valleys that are both vast and intimate, a regional span that extends beyond sight into mists that never part.

The city itself sprawls across several interconnected peaks, not in the manic density of some sprawling metropolis, but in a sparse urban tapestry—a settlement scaled for townsfolk, numbering in the thousands, yet scattered like loose stones on a mountainside. Builders of yore, wielding primitive tools of hand-shaped flint, crude bronze axes, and woven-withe hammers, erected tiered structures that cling to the uneven slopes. Homes are squat stone huts, their walls dry-laid from local shale and basalt, roofs peaked with thatched reeds that sway only in response to the occasional gusts that are themselves part of the eternal now. There are no machines, no forges fueled by anything beyond wood-fed fires or whispered incantations etched into simple amulets. Infrastructure threads through like veins: narrow gravel paths that wind without end, stone bridges arching over dry courses that could one day channel water, and communal pits lined with bamboo for waste, all balanced in a low-saturation spread—spaces yawn empty between habitations, inviting contemplation of the void, the emptiness that cradles all presence here.

Temperature clings cool, a persistent chill in the air that feels like the breath of perpetual dusk, never warming to midday heat nor deepening to frost. It's mild enough for life to persist without contest, a cool embrace that sustains without exhausting. Moisture lingers damp, not in torrential downpours but in intermittent veils—condensation beads on rock faces each dawn (which never truly comes, as light filters normal and unchanging through a sky shrouded in perpetual clouds, casting a soft, even glow that illuminates but never blinds). Occasional rains patter lightly, brief showers that mist the valleys below but vanish as quickly as they appear, leaving puddles in scooped depressions or pockets of still water amid the drier upland crags. Rivers, slender and meandering, carve slow paths through lower basins, their waters never swelling to floods, never drying to nothing—they hold steady, flowing with the quiet insistence of something alive yet bound.

Vegetation flourishes lush in this damped cool, defying the high elevation where lesser worlds might freeze or wither. Forests of twisted evergreens, their barks scaled like ancient hides, populate the valleys, trunks robust and leaves thick, resisting the cool without succumbing to barrenness. Ferns unfurl in dense groves, fronds curling lushly over moss-veined boulders; vines tangle along cliff gradients, bearing clusters of berries that never ripen nor rot. Flowers bloom in perpetual cycles that overlap without progression—petals unfolding warm-hued amidst the chill, blues and yellows contrasting the gray stone. These plants grow not invasively but in pockets, their density sparse throughout the open expanses, allowing swaths of bare earth to breathe, balanced with emptiness so that one feels the pull of open sky and distant horizons, the regional scale inviting exploration without confinement.

Fauna thrives abundant, a menagerie of creatures that populate the landscape in teeming yet scattered profusion—birds in flocks that sojourn eternally, their wings spread but unmoving in the air; herds of tawny goats clambering unscalable rocks without tiring; schools of sleek fish darting in the river pools, finned forms suspended in lucid currents. Insect swarms hum minimal threats in the undergrowth, while larger beasts, like wolf-like predators with fur that mimics the boulder shadows, prowl the edges without pouncing, their instincts perhaps dulled by the world's unchanging tenor. They are abundant, filling the sparse densities with life that pulses faintly, yet they operate on a sovereign clock, preying, birthing, dying in a stagnant loop that never advances. Niche species persist—tiny lizards that bask on sun-warmed stones (where the light filters most brightly through valley breaks), or aerial creatures resembling iridescent insects, their wings stilled in mid-flutter, hinting at mobility yet rooted to stasis.

Sounds here are mostly quiet, a hush that amplifies the sparse—gentle drips of condensation from leaves, the faint rustle of tassel grasses in breaths that never quicken to wind. Occasional animal calls echo distantly, mournful hoots from unseen owls perched on unyielding branches, or the clatter of goat hooves on gravel paths, simple and undemanding. No roars fracture the peace, no tempests howl; instead, the quiet is woven with subtle textures, like the low, perpetual murmur of water over river Étienne's shallows, a song that repeats without melody's progression.

Yet there's an undercurrent of weirdness, unusual and unplaceable, as if the world itself whispers of forgotten rules. The city's inhabitants—humanoids perhaps altered by the stasis, with skin like weathered stone and eyes that reflect the unchanging light—move with deliberate slowness, their rituals involving carved idols of primitive facture: bone flutes that emit no sound when blown, or wheeled carts of carved wood, pulled in circles that lead nowhere. Water sources, though damp, sometimes reveal bizarre pockets—mirrored pools where reflections shimmer independently, showing scenes that never happened, or vines that grow in lattices spelling words in scripts no tongue can read. Fauna occasionally manifests anomalies: a bird with feathers etched in faint runes, or a goat herd that circles a singular stone in perpetual orbit, their paths traced in worn but unchanging spirals. The ecosystem maintains equilibrium, but beneath the stability, hints of estranged laws pulse—gravity that flexes subtly uphill, or air that tastes of something metallic, not iron, but the essence of arrested decay.

In this place, rules govern with absolute calm: life persists in balance, resources neither depleted nor replenished, as everything holds its place. Visitors, if any come, find the world's pull irresistible, a lure to pause and mirror the pause. The landscape breathes not in cycles, but in eternal inhale, a high city in lush, damp chill, sparse and urban yet primitive, abundant with quiet life under normal light, where change is the only forbidden thing.
