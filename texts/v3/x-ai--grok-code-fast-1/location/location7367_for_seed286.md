In the uppermost strata of an oceanic world— a planet-dripping sphere where the seas rise as high as continental shelves, engulfing what were once peaks and valleys in a single, unbroken hydrosphere—there exists a plateau so vast it defies the notion of horizon. This is the Pallid Summit, a sprawl of shattered basalt and limpid shale that juts upward like the spine of some forgotten titan, its eroded flanks carved by eons of unrecognized currents into fluid-curved terraces. Erosion here has achieved a perfected stasis: not the jagged violence of fracture, but the serene equipoise of stone that has surrendered to dissolution, turning the plateau's edges into galleries of overhanging scallops and labyrinthine fractures, each crevice hanging in eternal poise, neither deepening nor smoothing further. The air beneath— if it can be called air in a realm submerged miles beneath surface waves, where pressure bends light into aberrant prisms— is cool, a steady chill that never warms or sharpens, as if the temperature itself has been suspended in amber. Normal light filters down from above, a diffuse glow broken through intervening fathoms into shafts of aqueous silver, illuminating without scorching, revealing depths where shadows pool without extending.

Yet it is the vegetation that reigns supreme, a total dominion of green that brooks no interruption. Plants here are not mere occupants; they are the substrate, the architecture, the very matrix of existence. Tendril-veins lace upward from the sessile mass, forming colossal spires that erupt skyward— but since there is no true sky, only encroaching liquids— into convoluted towers of interwoven fronds and fleshy pylons. These are not passive growths; they are alive as one immense organism, branching into canopies that overhang empty voids, their surfaces a riot of photosynthetic machinery where leaves unfurl as vast, petal-like plates studded with crystalline orifices for drawing in the thin, mineral-laden currents. The ecosystem is purest plant: a verdant engulfment that fills every interstitial space with its bulk, roots delving into the plateau's stone core to drink deep of subterranean brines, stalks twisting into sevenfold spirals of flowering conduits. Sparse density governs the layout; vast caverns yawn between the larger agglomerations, open expanses where the plant-mass clusters sparsely, permitting rare patches of naked stone or drifting particulate— clouds of diatoms and rhizome-fragments suspended in eternal drift. No built structures sully this purity; no artifacts intrude, for humanity or any crafting hand has never trod here. Instead, the industrial technics emerge from the weird alchemy of the flora itself, as if the plants have dreamed mechanization into being.

And mechanization there is, woven seamlessly into the living form. The spires rise as hybrid engines, their fleshy trunks housing beating hearts of industrial might—gears of bone-hard lignin mesh with pistons of pulpy tissue, churning in perpetual motion within vibro-vessels that pulse with guttural thrums. Factories unfold as globular blooms, their petals hinged with riveted sepals, opening to reveal combustion chambers where sparks of bio-electricity ignite not fire but a frothy effervescence of enzymatic fog. Piping networks snake through the vegetation like veins, curving in exponential arcs that carry no fluid but echo with the rumble of unseen forges, scaling their production to the plateau's vastness— forging, always forging, elements into new biomass without waste or end, for nothing here depletes. Electrifications hum through fibril-wires, conduits of streaked chlorophyll that channel currents harvested from osmotic gradients in the water-column, powering motivators that tug fern-belts across the spire-surfaces. This is not man's industry, abandoned or misplaced; it is plant-as-engine, a evolution of verdure into mechanized sentience, strange and abundant in its function. The sounds amplify to a constant roar: the clangor of pistons hammering against fleshy forges, the hiss of hydraulics venting emerald steams, the grinding whine of continent-wide belts aligning in fractal synchrony, all at deafening volume, reverberating through the sparse voids where echoes hang frozen, never fading to silence.

Fauna teems in this locked domain, abundant yet integrated, creatures evolved to the vegetal-industral rhythm. Schools of hydraulic cephalopods— bulbous squid-forms with manipulator-limbs grown rigid as wrench-extensions— swarm the periphery of spire-belts, their ink-sacs pulsing with oil-like secretions to lubricate the machinery. Decapod crustaceans scuttle along the high terraces, their carapaces etched with circuit-patterns that interface directly with the living conduits, drawing power for bioluminescent flares. Aerial gliders, insectile mammals with wing-membranes thick as industrial felt, loop through the cavernous emptiness in unison, their migrations cycloid and unchanging, pollinating the electro-flowers with beaks that spark small arcs upon contact. Larger behemoths herd in scattered congregations: ambulatory fungal-matts the size of eroded boulders, symbiotic with the plant-engine, their mycelial networks pumping nutrients through the sparse spaces, birthing pod-after-pod of spore-offspring that drift away only to redeposit in eternal recurrence. No predation mars this balance; hunger and reproduction are mechanisms of the whole, with fauna consuming the vegetative overflow— torn fronds repurposed as fuel for their internal forges— in a cycle that neither accelerates nor decays.

Weirdness pervades without resolution: the plateau is a static paradox, submerged yet elevated, where gravity warps subtly due to scale— offering microclimates within climates, weathers of ethereal downpours that stem from condensation vents in the plant-mass, cascading as walls of fine mist that erode nothing further, merely hanging as perpetual veils. Temporal rules are inverted; time here flows not in minutes or millennia but in frozen inertia— no seasons alter the foliage, no tides shift the currents beyond negligible quanta that reset at quantum thresholds. The plants "build" without end, their industrial progeny spewing forth in Escher-like impossibilities: ladders of vines that ascend to nowhere, bridges of root-girders spanning chasms filled with luminous voids, all functional yet purposeless, maintaining equilibrium in a world where change is outlawed. Explorers—if such beings existed—might drift through on currents, observing the eternal ballet: the loud symphony of machine-life blending with the rustle of fauna, all under the cool gaze of unchanging light, in a place that has always been as it is, a submerged highland of living industry, vast and strange, forever poised on the brink of... nothing.
