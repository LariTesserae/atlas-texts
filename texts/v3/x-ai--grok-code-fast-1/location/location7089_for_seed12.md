You approach this place not as a visitor treading solid ground, but as something adrift, suspended in its embrace—a slow submersion where the water swallows you whole, yet the world persists, layered and defying expectation. At first, it's a vast, dim expanse that presses in like a held breath, warm and thick, carrying currents that nudge at you with the patience of centuries. The water is everywhere, an all-encompassing veil, its surface far above or perhaps illusory, for down here, in these submerged realms, elevation rises not to dry peaks but to emergent plateaus—raw, unchiseled formations of stone that thrust upward and outward, their edges blurring into the liquid murk. These aren't mountains cracked by wind or time; no, erosion here is embryonic, a slow unwinding of the earth itself, as if the core is still exhaling, birthing new rock in gradual thrusts and accretions that shift over years, not days. The boulders gleam wetly, veined with faint luminescence that pulses like a heartbeat, casting a dim glow through the density of it all. Light filters down in faint, diffused beams, not harsh or revealing, but a twilight haze that clings to everything, making forms shimmer and vanish at the edges.

The terrain weaves through this: plateaus upon plateaus, layered like sedimentary memory, each rising higher than the last in a terraced ascent that defies underwater logic. The stone is porous, fractally textured—swirling patterns etched as if by fingertips, alive and breathing. Moss-like growths cling to them, lush and suffocating in their abundance, draping over ledges and pooling in crevices where the water collects in warm eddies. Vegetation thrives here, verdant and tangled, sprouting from every fracture; broad, fleshy fronds wave lazily in the currents, their edges fringed with tendrils that occasionally brush against you like curious fingers. These plants aren't rooted in soil but in the stone itself, drawing from mineral seepages that bubble faintly, infusing the air—wait, not air, but the fluid medium—with a hazy warmth that steams ever so slightly against the cool undercurrents. Fauna is scant, a rarity that adds to the strangeness: sleek, eel-like shapes occasionally ghost by, their skins iridescent and oddly patterned, but they vanish quickly, as if startled into nonexistence. Birds? No, not here—perhaps elongated fish that flutter wings of luminous membrane, darting between the thickets before dissolving into the dimness. Predators remain elusive, tuned to the slow rhythms; a hunt might unfold over seasons, a gradual closing of fins and fangs.

Yet amidst this, an urban presence emerges—cities, or the bones of them, scattered across the plateaus in dense clusters. Primitive in their making, these are settlements carved from the raw geology itself, not built and weathered but emerging, as if the stone willed them into being over aeons. Stones stacked in rough pyramids, corridors of arches that curve unnaturally, like the spiraling of seashells or the loops of whale intestines. No metal tools mark them; instead, hand-hewn with flints and bones, infrastructure layered thickly—walkways bridging chasms of bubbling vents, multi-tiered platforms rising in illogical geometries, some twisting upward against the flow like inverted towers, others coiling inward as if ready to consume the space around them. The density is oppressive: presences crowd every volume—statues of figures half-human, half-exuding tendrils, looming immobile; vessels of woven reed-like fibers piled high, bobbing in eddies; relics strewn like detritus, their purposes forgotten but persisting. Rules of this world flicker through: structures shift when unobserved, corridors aligning to match the pulse of bioluminescent algae that coats the walls, a sort of communal will that fosters cohesion without intent. Trade happens in slow exchanges—pods of material floating between plateaus, guided by primal signals. Conflicts resolve in patient accretion, where rival bands simply meld over time, their boundaries eroding into shared myth.

Sounds are a hush, mostly—the quiet gurgle of water seeping through fissures, the distant pop of vents releasing mineral gasps, the occasional rustle of vegetation fronds parting for rare fauna. No crashes of waves or roars of beasts; even voices, if present, would be murmurs, vibrating through the water like a low hum felt in bones, stories transmitted through touch and echo. Dynamic flows are glacial: currents alter course over decades, vegetation blooms in slow, creeping expansions, a single plateau might rise imperceptibly, unveiling new chambers etched with strange symbols—shapes that resemble faces screaming or flowers blooming thorns. Temperature holds steady in its warmth, a cradling embrace that mutates subtly; warmer pockets near vents support clones-like outgrowths on flora, while cooler sinks harbor icy veins in the stone.

Strange elements weave deeper as you linger: buildings that breathe, their walls expanding and contracting like lungs, exhaling clouds of phosphorescent spores that settle on you, not as parasites but as fleeting illuminations of memory. Elevation warps perspective here—climbing a plateau, you feel lighter, as if buoyancy conspires with unseen forces; descending brings a heavier pull, compressing the world into denser layers. Gravity itself seems negotiated; objects drift lazily upward in certain zones, defying expectation, while others sink ponderously, accumulating in shadowed piles. The water itself carries odd properties—swallowing light in swirling vortices, regurgitating it as false stars; or condensing into momentary solids, bridges of gelled fluid that form and dissolve in seconds. Ecology balances on fragile threads: vegetation feasts on the city's leached minerals, fauna preys selectively, and the built forms evolve slowly, adapting to the emergent geology—cracks filling with vine-like reinforcements, structures budding new appendages as the rock grows.

As exploration deepens, the place asserts its identity—a sunken realm of high, raw plateaus where primitive urbanity blooms in lush, dense strangeness, all submerged in warm, dim currents that change with the lethargy of dreams. You begin to understand: this is not a place sinking from loss, but emerging from oblivion, a world birthing itself in layered cycles, where presence is measured not in footsteps but in the patient drift of things becoming.
