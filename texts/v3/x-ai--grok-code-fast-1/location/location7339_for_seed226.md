Cool winds sweep in slow, lazy currents, carrying the chill of perpetual dampness without ever sharpening into cold: a temperature settled into equilibrium, neither warm enough to dry the wet nor frigid enough to freeze it, just a persistent, patient cool that wears at edges over ages.

Water pools everywhere, not rushing or flooding but simmering beneath the surface like a secret. The plateau itself is a marsh-edge in slow formation—ferns and reeds sprout from cracks in the rock, their roots threading through embryonic stone that hasn't yet decided its shape.
