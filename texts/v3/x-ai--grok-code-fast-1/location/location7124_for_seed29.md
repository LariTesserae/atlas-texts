You enter through a narrow fissure in the side of the mountain, a crack barely wider than your shoulders, hidden amid the scorched scree and jagged outcrops of what feels like a newborn peak—raw, unetched by time's patient sculptors, where erosion has only begun its tentative etchings, as if the rock itself is still exhaling nascent trails of dust and vapor. The air hits you first, a stifling wave that lingers like a held breath, thick with the bite of sulfur and the metallic tang of unmelting ores. It's hot here, oppressively so, the kind that seeps into your bones without flames to blame, radiating from the very core of this pocket world. No water has ever touched these walls; they've never known the cool intrusion of rain or the sly persistence of slow seepages. Instead, everything is dry—desiccated to the mineral, a void of moisture where even the air clings statically, charged and parched.

The fissure opens into a chamber, intimate as a clenched fist, maybe thirty paces across at its widest, curving like an eroded vesicle embedded deep within the volcano's throat. It's high up, truly—far above the valley floors where lesser slopes crumble into habitability—but this pocket feels suspended, a bubble trapped in the forge of the earth. No light penetrates here; darkness reigns absolute, a velvet shroud unbroken by any flicker or gleam. You navigate by touch, by the faint heat-map of surfaces, or by the bewildering eruptions of sound that fill the space, a cacophony that's almost industrial in its rhythm: deep, resonant thuds like pistons firing in sequence, interspersed with sharp cracks as if gears meshing in some vast, unseen machine, and the constant low roar of pressure building, equal parts furnace and forge-hammer. These noises aren't random—they echo the slow, deliberate cycles of this place, each change unfolding over eons rather than moments, a geological patience that dwarfs human whiskers and scales.

Yet despite the darkness and the dry heat, this chamber is alive and teeming, packed beyond belief. Fauna abounds here, creatures adapted to a world without water or greenery, thriving in the mineral husk. They swarm the walls and floor, packed so densely that there's scarcely room between them—bodies overlapping in a tapestry of wriggling forms that clamber and cling with mechanical precision, their movements synchronized to the underlying rumble. Imagine swarms of iridescent beetle-like entities, their shells forged from obsidian and quartz, scuttling in synchronized waves, mandibles grinding against stone like tiny drills. Or serpentine coils of what might be worms, but metallic and glowing faintly with internal heat, burrowing through fissures only to emerge and coil anew, their abundance creating a living matrix that fills every crevice. These aren't mere animals; they're integral to the place, perhaps descended from simpler fungal spores or pre-life crystallizations that have evolved in this arid oven. Some pulse with bioluminescent veins that aren't light but heat-signatures, etching dim outlines in infrared—enough to hint at the density but not to dispel the gloom. Larger forms lumber through the chaos, golem-like beasts of compacted slag and vents, their steps slow and ponderous, reshaping the floor with each deliberate tread as new minerals precipitate from their pores.

The tech emerges organically, indistinguishable from the geology itself, as if the volcano birthed its own industry. Veins in the rock resemble conveyor belts, slowly channeling flows of molten slag upward through channels that hiss and contract like mechanized pistons. Chambers within chambers mimic furnaces, with "engines" that aren't built but grown—spherical hubs where heat vortices spin mineral dust into intricate lattices, generating waves of vibration that fuel the sounds and sustain the fauna. No one constructed this; it's all emerging, under formation, the raw economics of a world crafting itself from nothingness. Scales of rusted ferrous substances flake off slowly, reformed into new structures over centuries, while the fauna integrates seamlessly, their metallic exoskeletons meshing with these formations like cogs in a living machine.

Rules here are stark and unyielding: survival demands adaptation to the dry void, where resources are forged anew from the earth's furnace, not gathered from gentler worlds. Change is glacial—populations swell and contract in eons-long cycles, populations of those beetle-things waxing during flare-ups of heat, waning as minerals cool and solidify. Erosion is nascent, barely nibbling at the edges; a fresh spire might form overnight by volcanic whimsy, only to be polished smooth in a millennium's gradual wear. Atmospheric currents? Minimal—heat gradients rise lazily, convection creeping like slow syrup. And the sounds... they dominate, a symphony of powering-down and revving-up, the fauna's scrabbling murmurs adding layered harmonies, so loud you feel them in your skull, a constant reminder of this pocket's relentless, patient boil.
