The Grove-City of Thicketveil sprawls not as a true metropolis might—there are no true streets or squares here, no mortar-bound edifice rising to a horizon—but as a living concrescence of vine-woven architecture, a dense yet paradoxically vacant settlement knit from root and leaf. Picture it at the scale of a cathedral's nave, or perhaps a modest hamlet shrunk to the confines of an overgrown chamber: a bounded expanse no larger than a single block in a forgotten town, yet crammed with voids that stretch it into vistas of emptiness. From the outside, it appears as a solitary hillock of entwined greenery, barely rising from the lowlands' erosive flats—ovenous ridges worn smooth by millennia of patient rain and wind, their earthy scars a testament to long-settled forms in geological poise, as though the ground itself has exhaled relief into perpetuity. But step within the veil of curtain-draping lianas that serve as its gates, and the reality fractures: this is not merely a grove, but a city of florae, urban in its layered constructions—spires and alleys formed by towering, crisscrossed fasciations of plant-flesh, tiers upon tiers of interconnected nodes where root-throne seats dominate overlook perches, and vine-trestle bridges sway over abyssal drops into nothingness.

Vegetation dominates utterly, a pure ecosystem of plant-as-substrate, where every surface is a living tapestry. The ground is a mat of moss-veils and fern-hummocks, thickly interwoven like a woolen carpet, but far from cluttered—density here leans toward emptiness, with vast hollows yawning between the forms, chasms of air so wide and fecund that one might wander for hours without brushing a leaf, only to hear the distant rustle of motion implying some unseen stride. Yet these plants are not static: they are architectures of the absolute, masterworks of growth that pulse and adjust in real-time, branches entangling like city blocks organized into districts, high-rises of stratified foliage where higher shelves harbor spore-markets, lower boughs provide root-cellar storages. Strange vines—vaporous and translucent, like exhaled breath given vine—climb spiraling stanchions, forming facades of crystaline bark that catch warm sunlight in prismatic refractions. Flowers bloom explosively in arterial blooms, their petals forearm-thick and petal-veined like meridians of living cartilage, exhaling perfumed airs that thicken the warm atmosphere into a humid haze. Beneath all this, the ecology teems: endless cycles of predation and regrowth, where predatory tendrils snatch at passing fauna only to be grafted anew in symbiotic reconciliation, the plants themselves hiving like ants—a collective biomass that breathes, one organism masquerading as a metropolis.

Water plays its damp, intermittent role in this warm lowland clime, never fully flooding but present in subtle, evocative ways. Pockets of condensation bead on leaf-crypts overhead, dripping in slow, syrupy rivulets that pool in bowl-shaped fronds serving as fountains, their surfaces rippling with iridescent oils skimmed from insect exoskeletons. Occasional rains arrive not as storms but as temporal dislocations—sudden, localized downpours that bend spacetime into brief riverine cascades, carving ephemeral channels through the moss-ground before evaporating into steam, leaving behind moist echoes that cling to the air like unspoken promises. No standing pools endure long; the soil's erosion is too balanced, too long-standing, absorbing every drop into a subsurface veinwork that feeds the city's deep roots, creating a hydrology of memory where water lingers as ghost-dampness, fueling the warm, febrile growth that keeps everything in motion.

In this reality-breakdown, nothing adheres to mundane rules. The city's boundaries are fuzzy, not walls but membranes that permit improbable faunal abundance: swarms of glimmering seraphins—moth-like creatures with wingspans as broad as tapestries—veer through the voids, their bioluminescent trails distorting the normal light into hallucinogenic strobes, while packbeasts roam in herds that phase in and out of solidity, corporeal one moment, ethereal smoke the next. Fauna isn't just plentiful; it's omnipresent yet discreet, inhabiting the empty densities as if they too are part of the vegetation's grand substrate—nests woven into leaf-veins, predators lurking in the voids as living absences. A crawl of vine-worms burrows through the ground, their movements causing the earth to undulate like a breathing lung; aerial predators, bird-serpents with feathers that molt into fractal kite-sails, wheel in predatory arcs that defy gravity, sometimes plunging into nothingness only to resurface on a different plane of the grove's layered reality.

Sounds here are deafening, a cacophony that warps perception— not just volume, but penetrance, each note amplified into a sonic geometry that reshapes the space. The drip of condensation echoes like cathedral bells tolling in avalanche, while the groan of growing vines vibrates the air into harmonics that vibrate bone and leaf alike, creating mirrored resonances where one sound refracts into infinite echoes. Fauna calls intermingle with plant indistinguishable: the trill of seraphin wings slices like razors through silk, the bellow of packbeasts rumble as subterraneanquakes, and the active dynamism of the ecosystem infuses it all—the constant snap of new growth unfurling in accelerated spurts, the weather cycles of micro-rains that sporadically condense and dissipate, predator-prey dance that shifts the air with rushes of displaced wind. Everything moves: tendrils coil into new alleyways overnight, roots upheave to form ramparts, while airborne spores conspire in breezes that circle the grove like living whirlwinds, depositing not dust but possibilities. In the emptiness, sound becomes architecture itself, a deafening web that no ear can fully map, pleading with the silence beyond the city's veil.

No tools sully this purity of nature; the "built" essence arises solely from biological ingenuity, ancient and self-sustaining, a urbanism evolved from millennia of erosion-settled patience. Elevation scarcely varies in this lowland domain, the ground a gentle undulation where the city perches as a crown of verdure, its warm temperature maintained by the dense, heat-trapping foliage—a greenhouse effect fueled by photosynthetic alchemy and faint geothermal warmth that seeps from the fertile soil below. Yet weirdness prevails, unraveling reality's weave: one might traverse a vine-bridge, only to find it loops backward upon itself, depositing you in the same spot but altered—aged, or inversed, perhaps with colors reversed or sounds inverted to whispers. The laws here bend; gravity lightens in voids, allowing faunal leaps into hovering suspension, while time dilates around active growth spurts, compressing seasons into minutes of predatory bloom and decay. Thicketveil endures as a living paradox: a small, empty city of total plant-life, founded on damp warmth and eternal erosion, where deafening activity stirs the abundant wild into a symphony of broken worlds. To dwell here is to become part of its substrate, your footsteps leaving trails of new-forged moss, your breaths contributing to the humid symphony. But venture too far into a void? One might emerge not in another district, but in a memory of the grove—or perhaps in nothing at all, the reality-breakdown claiming you as its own absurd extension.
