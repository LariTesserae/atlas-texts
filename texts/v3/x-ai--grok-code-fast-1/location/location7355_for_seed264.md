The Forge Plains sprawl out like an endless workshop floor, a vast, undulating expanse of warm, damp minerals that stretches farther than the eye can see, folding into distant ripples of low-lying earth and scattered ridges. It's a world of stone and metal ores, untouched by any hand—there are no buildings, no roads, no signs of deliberate construction, yet the air hums with the unmistakable cadence of industrial machinery, as if the ground itself has been engineered from within. Normal sunlight filters through a thin veil of perpetual mist, casting a muted glow over everything, but the light doesn't pierce the haze; it suffuses the plains in a steady, unchanging brightness that neither rises nor falls, day mimicking night in its constancy.

The terrain here is a low-elevation tapestry of sedimentary plains, worn smooth over eons by the steady trickle of moisture. Erosion has sculpted these flats into a state of settled balance—no sharp edges remain, no crumbling cliffs or fresh fractures. Instead, the earth lies weathered and composed, like an ancient ledger that's been paged through countless times and left flat. Sparse outcroppings dot the vista: geodes of quartzite shatter open in isolated bursts, their interiors glinting with milky selenite crystals that catch the light and refract it into soft prisms. Veins of hematite streak through sandstone slabs, turning them rusty red, and here and there, chunks of granitic iron ore rise like squat boulders, their surfaces pitted with the marks of ancient, uniform dissolution. There's no greenery, no life beyond the mineral—nothing green, no roots or spores. Water comes in fits and starts: occasional veils of condensation drape the air after a brief, lukewarm rain that spatters the ground without pools, soaking in as dampness that lingers like sweat on skin. Small rivulets, born from nowhere and vanishing just as mysteriously, thread through the plains, their paths etching shallow channels that loop back on themselves, never growing or fading, just cycling in repetitive dribbles of metallic-tinged liquid. It's not flooding or arid; it's a perpetual dampness, the kind that clings to every surface, making stones glisten and air thick with humidity, warm enough that it steams faintly outwards, mingling with the mist.

In this sparse landscape, emptiness dominates. Distances yawn between features— kilometer-wide gaps separate one cluster of iron boulders from the next, where a lone slab of schist juts up like a forgotten workbench. The density is low; presences are scattered, isolated, allowing the full regional scale to assert itself. You could walk for days, following the gentle undulations of the plains toward som distant horizon smeared by haze, and encounter only a handful of mineral formations: a ring of basalt columns standing sentinel around nothing, their hexagonal faces etched with the faint grooves of what looks like machined rulings; or a valley's worth of serpentine rubble, piled in conical heaps that glint with embedded pyrites, like slag heaps abandoned by some cosmic refinery. Space breathes between them, balanced by void, and it's in this emptiness that the air carries a symphony of industrial clamor—loud, unceasing, yet oddly in harmony, never escalating or quieting. The sounds reverberate from the earth itself: a deep, grinding scrape, like tectonic plates shifting on invisible bearings, punctuated by sharp clanks and hisses that echo across the open scale. Steam vents erupt with rhythmic regularity from fissures in the rock, whistling like pressure valves releasing, sending plumes that curl and dissipate without varying. It's as if the world operates on a vast, mechanical rhythm—a steam-driven engine of geological proportions, with no source to point to, no factory hall beyond the plains. The clatter blends into a roar at times, drowning out any softer whisper, yet it's stable, invariant; the same pattern loops eternally, no storm to amplify it, no silence to fracture it.

Fauna here is scarce, a solitary quirk in the mineral symphony. Rare creatures—perhaps not creatures at all, but emanations of the land's weirdness—emerge from the damp voids. Picture a crystalline entity, slow and hulking, lumbering between the sparse outcroppings: a beast woven from amethyst geodes, its form a lumbering tessellation of faceted stone, moving with the ponderous grace of a walking behemoth, limbs like pistons of rose quartz that clench and unclench. It pauses occasionally at a steam vent, absorbing the mist into its lattice, growing marginally denser before dispersing back into the haze. These beings are infrequent, their movements deliberate, unchanging—like automated sentinels in a factory eternally at standby. They don't interact, don't multiply; they simply persist, rare echoes of life in a non-life world, their presence adding an unusual layer to the established equilibrium.

In its rules, this place defies expectation: it's warm yet damp, mineral yet mechanized, dynamic only in its internal, pre-determined cycles of sound and slight mineral flux. Everything remains in frozen balance—no seasons alter the warmth, no winds shift the mist, no events perturb the voids. Erosion has left it in its own state, a long-existed relic where change is illusory, only the loud hum evidencing an underlying tech that feels industrial—gears in the ground, conduits of ore, assembly lines buried deep—but it's all natural, emergent from volcanic glass cores that cooled into simulacra of machinery. Heat builds from below, radiating upwards in stable waves, keeping the air lukewarm, the stones supple. Fauna follows a similar stasis, rare wanderers in the sparse field, their existence a weird counterpoint to the unchanging void. As you traverse the plains, letting the scale unfold—valleys revealing canted shelves of mica that shimmer like conveyor belts frozen in transit, rivers looping back into themselves without resolution—the landscape deepens its identity. It becomes a colossal, open mechanism, balanced on the edge of utility and desolation, where every element harmonizes in loud, eternal operation, inviting you to imagine the immense, unseen forge pulsing beneath.
