Imagine plunging into a realm where the ocean's embrace is absolute, an endless submerged expanse that defies simple categorization as sea or abyss—it is water as dominion, a liquid universe that has drowned not just lands but entire realities. Here, elevation reigns in perverse extremes: colossal underwater spires and plateaus soar from uncharted depths, their scaled flanks piercing upward for miles into the chilling cool waters, temperatures hovering just above freezing where currents whisper of sustained equilibrium. These peaks are not mere geological feats; they are primordial relics, etched by eons of erosion into their oldest stable state, shapes so profoundly aged and settled that they seem imprinted with the memory of creation's final sigh. Flanks have flattened into broad, undulating terraces, slopes worn smooth as if worn down by the ceaseless patience of a god, yet unchanged for millennia in a dynamic freeze—time-invariant, gradients abolished, equilibrium locked in eternal poise.

Every inch of this world is built, wholly and totally constructed, yet no tool or technology emerges from the human hand or any contrived mechanism. Instead, construction is the act of life itself, a pure, biological endeavor where flora and fauna conspire in unison to weave and erect structures of breathtaking scale. There are no natural substrates here; rock or silt has long been overgrown, repurposed, or eliminated. Everything is made, but made through growth, symbiosis, and relentless, instinctive architecture—plants as substrate and fabric, beasts as artisans. Vegetation dominates with a totality that blurs into substrate; it is the all-living-mass, a pure-plant ecosystem where tendrils, mats, and hulks form the very bone and flesh of the world. Enormous, rootbound citadels rise like living fortresses, their walls woven from fibrous vines as thick as canyon walls, interwoven with translucent, gelatinous algae that glow faintly in the pervasive darkness—0.3 on the light scale, a gloom so deep it ceases to be mere shadow and becomes the world's absence, penetrated only by bioluminescent flickers and the electric pulses of distant organisms. These structures sprawl over regional scales, open expanses where a single "building" might encompass valleys the size of ancient crater lakes, multi-featured realms with hollows, arches, and caverns all grown in place, yet sparseness prevails—density low at 0.8, with vast voids and emptiness woven into the design, spaces between presences that invite contemplation, balanced with solitude amid the abundance.

Ecology thrives in this unusual weirdness, a tapestry where plant and animal life fuses into novel forms, abundant fauna pulsing through the sparse networks. Creatures roam in teeming swarms, drawn to the constructed havens—they are swimmers and climbers, gelatinous herds and gelatinous css, symbiotic colonies where fish-like beings fuse symbiotically with the vegetative mass, becoming ambulatory fronds or mobile canopies that drift along the currents. Predator-prey dances unfold in slow, frozen rhythms: predatory eels with venomous fronds lash out from within vine-walls, while shoals of luminescent urchins, their spines extruding plant-like tendrils, graze eternally on the living walls. Herbs grow as dynamic sculptures, flowering into arthropod-like assemblies that self-replicate, their seeds pods that hatch into mobile offspring. There are no seasons, no migrations driven by external whims; this is a balanced void where populations hover in perfect equilibrium, births and deaths synchronized in an unchanging cycle. Fauna's abundance is weirdly symbiotic, a fusion where distinction between eater and eaten fades—creatures ingest and excrete back into the plant-substrate, recycling matter in loops that sustain the built world's integrity. Some beings are ambulatory infrastructures themselves, colossal leviathans with bodies engineered of kelp and bone, wandering the sparsenities to reinforce distant supports, their movements minimal yet eternal, no gradient shifting the status quo.

Atmosphere—or what passes for it in this submerged realm—is a cool pressure of water, viscous and still, currents tugging gently but consistently, like the breath of an ancient sleep. Density's sparsity manifests in pockets of pure emptiness, vast chambers where no form intrudes, only the echo of isolation. Yet sound amplifies loud and resonant, reverberations booming through the liquid medium: the thrum of massive vines shifting in unison, a choral groan from millions of unseen organisms, predatory screeches modulating into harmonies that paint the darkness with auditory landscapes. Echoes cascade from extreme elevations, bouncing off flattened terraces and plunging into abyssal voids, a cacophony that never varies, time-invariant in its pitch and rhythm.

Rules govern this world not through codified laws but inherent biological imperatives, ingrained since primordial times. Growth is mandatory and mutual; any breach invites reclamation, the plant-substrate absorbing intrusion, reshaping disruptive forms into its stable equilibrium. Change is anathema—intruders fade or fuse, gradients forbidden, the sphere locked in frozen balance. What emerges here is a place of profound antiquity, a deep-sea citadel of endless night, where life has built and settled into a final form, loud with the symphony of existence yet sparse enough for the soul to roam its voids. It is not hostile, nor welcoming—it simply is, a submerged testament to the triumph of the constructed life, unchanging as the stars above its oceanic veil.
