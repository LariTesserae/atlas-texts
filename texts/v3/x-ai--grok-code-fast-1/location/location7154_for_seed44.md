You step into the Hollows, a low-lying basin carved into the soft earth of a vast floodplain, where the land has long settled into weathered shapes, erosion having sculpted smooth, bowl-like depressions that hold water like cupped palms after rain. The air is warm and thick with moisture, condensing on every surface in a perpetual drizzle, so that vine-strung metal beams weep droplets and stone foundations glisten as if newly shed of tears. Water pools and seeps everywhere—frequent rivers trickle through gullies worn by patient currents, marshes finger into the edges of the basin, and condensation drips from leaves condensing whole climates into beads that plunk steadily onto fibrous substrates below. It's a place where wetness defines the boundaries: stepping stones of slick moss rise just above shallow canals, weeping rocks form archways that funnel rivulets into sunken reservoirs, and the atmosphere itself wicks humidity from the air, a humid veil that blurs distances in the broad, even light of a normal day—neither harsh nor dim, just the steady gleam of filtered sun through canopies that never quite part.

Vegetation floods the landscape like a living tide, total and all-encompassing, a pure plant ecosystem where every surface is substrate for growth, every volume filled with swaying, intertwining masses. In this small-scale crucible—a single grove-sized expanse, bounded by the basin's rim and perhaps a half-mile across, no larger than a hamlet in its tight confines—the plants are architects and citizens both, forming layered constructions that dwarf any remnant human order. Root networks burrow into the ground, heaving up stone pavements and coiling around industrial bones, while aerial vines braid into cables and support beams, turning factories into throbbing, organic frames. Leaves unfurl as walls, thick and quilted, blotting out light in some chambers while allowing it through pane-like transparencies elsewhere; stems twist into scaffolding, holding up dripping galleries where machinery hums within petal-encased pods. It's dense beyond measure, near-touching layers piling one atop another: a ground level matted with root-nets and spore-laden soils, above it flowering overhangs budding fruits that pulse like gears, and higher still canopy vaults where tendrils drape like power lines, all saturated with presence, a high-density tangle where no space goes unfilled, each inch claimed by chlorophyll conquest.

Yet beneath (or perhaps within) this vegetal dominion lies the urban grind, a settlement-scale sprawl of organized construction that's been wholly assimilated, infrastructure layered like sedimentary rock into the plant-as-substrate. Urban nodes cluster densely—warehouses woven from thorny stems, mills fusing funneled plant-fibers with electrified looms, refineries where sap flows through pistoned conduits—all electrified and mechanized in an industrial pulse, engines chugging on biodiesel distilled from the very foliage that engulfs them, factories humming with scaled production belted through vine-ties. Pipes erupt from root-clumps to traverse arching fronds, while control panels sprout moss and floral displays, their dials obscured by blooming interfaces. It's a city in relief, town-harmonic densities packed into the grove's confines, high-rises of layered stalks hiding silo stacks and assembly halls within their trunks, where workers (rarely glimpsed in the slow churn) maintain the mechanized heart amid the overwhelming green.

Sound dominates here, loud and reverberant, amplified by the dense vegetation's acoustic wells—engines roar in low, endless thrum, pistons clank like falling nuts against hollow conduits, and water drips with metronomic patter, plumking into foliar catchments that echo chamber-like depths. Distilling vats bubble and hiss, belts whine over pulley-palms, and the creak of growing wood fibers bridges the din, a symphony of industrial persistence where silence is an anomaly. Fauna is scarce, those few presences lurking sparingly: a handful of insects whirring in the undergrowth, perhaps a solitary amphibian trilling faintly from a marshy pool, but nothing rampant—rare intrusions into the plant hegemony, where creatures are outliers in this ecosystem's dominant regime.

Change here is slow, measured in patient processes and weakest gradients: cycles unfold over seasons unhurried, vines inching across machinery day by day, erosion grinding stones smooth over decades, factories adapting to woody encroachments not with haste but with gradual integrations—new branchings incorporated into circuits, weathered forms settling deeper into balance. The tech evolves incrementally, engines re-tuned to sap pressures, production scaled to the rhythm of photosynthesis rather than demand. It's a place frozen in gradual flux, where the atmosphere gathers humidity layer by layer, the vegetation proliferates leaf by incremental leaf, and the urban core maintains its electrified pulse without upheaval, a landscape that becomes itself through accretion, not revelation—a dense, wetwarm nexus of plant and machine, established in its layered, loud equilibrium.
