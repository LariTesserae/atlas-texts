In the Construed Lowlands, as this place names itself in the echoing hum of its own making, the ground is a masterpiece of fitted stones—each the girth of ten men and hewn by hand from quarries long sealed, using only chiseled antler picks and woven sinew ropes, a labor of primitive artisans who never speak of the sky they glimpsed during their ascent but only the floor they built below. There is no earth beneath; every inch is fabricated, a vast floor of irregular flagstones laid in vast patterns that repeat without rhyme, as if copied from a half-remembered dream. The stones are young and unweathered, their edges knife-sharp where they meet, barely a scratch from the passage of time, for here erosion is newborn, the very idea of wear just budding in the crevices like an infant's first tooth. Water seeps from everything. From the undersides of alcoves that jut like misplaced teeth along the floor's gentle undulations, droplets condense and weep, falling in steady, soundless plips onto lower stones, pooling into shallow, mirror-still basins that never deepen or evaporate, their surfaces dappled with the condensation of a perpetually muggy air. It is hot here, a furnace breath that clings without wind to stir it, every surface slick and humid, as if the world has just emerged from a rain that will never end yet never flood. Marshes form in the hollows between stones, not mud but crafted reed mats sunk in shallow water troughs, their boards floating like islands in a sea of fabricated wetness, edges fractal and fresh, channels carved by hand-tools into grids where water lingers near the surface, condensing on the air into a haze that blurs all horizons.

The vegetation is a sprawl of invention, lush and unchecked, sprouting from deliberate grooves and potholes chiseled into the stonework—trenches meant for drainage that now brim with ferns and vines, their leaves broad and dripping, climbing the stepped ledges of multi-tiered platforms that rise like ziggurats from the low-lying floor. These towers are not natural outgrowths but sculpted entirely by pre-metal hands, stacked slabs forming levels where boughs of engineered figs and strangling lianas coil thickly, their greenery thick enough to form arboreal corridors overhead, bright with blossoms that unfurl in eternal bloom, petals vivid as the light that bathes everything. The scale is open, a valley-wide expanse that pulls the eye past the immediate, regional in its sprawl, the valley bottom studded with clusters of these constructs—here a tiered mound of fused huts, there a causeway of bridged stones arching over wet depressions, their forms multiplying like reflections in broken glass, each echoing the others without end, a multi-feature low-elevation realm where distant silhouettes repeat the same spires and depressions, the air shimmering with heat waves that elongate them impossibly, yet never shift.

Density is sparse, a balanced emptiness that amplifies the vastness, each element spaced far apart amid the openness, stones scattered across the valley like cast dice on a cloth, vegetation clusters dotting the field without crowding, leaving gaps wide enough for long shadows—though shadows here are peculiar, stretched thin by the light's intensity, painting the gaps in negative space where nothing grows but the eye can wander, seeing echoes of forms that aren't there. Fauna teems in this scarcity, abundant but solitary, primitive creatures that seem hand-crafted too—lizards with scales like fitted tiles scampering across warm stones, birds with wings of oiled leather circling high in the bright, unfiltered light that pours down like a molten stream, never dimmed, piercing every crevice. Sloths hang in motionless contemplation from vine-bridge arms, their fur matted with damp, frogs that chirp rarely in the quiet, leaping between marshy mats in solitary arcs, crabs with shells etched in runes scuttling along water edges. They move with a frozen grace, for nothing changes here; this is a stable tableau, time-invariant, gradients absent, an equilibrium where hot stasis binds all, the condensation forever beading, the vines forever unfurling without growing taller, the animals pausing mid-stride in an endless pose.

The quiet is profound, a mostly silent hum where only the faintest drip of weeping condensation registers, no rustles, no calls, as if the air itself absorbs sound, muffling it into nothingness. But within this silence lurks the breakdown, reality's fracture lines etching the whole. Stones phase subtly, their surfaces flickering like screens glitching between now and not-now—here a block solid, there translucent to reveal impossible innards of spiraling voids or repeating patterns of the valley itself nested within, miniatures of the outer expanse trapped in seams. Animals sometimes stutter in place, dissolving into pixelated blurs before reforming, the light warping time's fabric so that a lizard's step forward echoes backward in a stutter, or a vine bends to kiss something unseen, the air rippling with afterimages of places that might have been—perhaps the high peaks this low land was built from, or the tools that shaped it, phasing in as phantom smudges before dissolving. The atmosphere condenses not just water but possibilities, a humid fog where rules fray: gravity pulls unevenly on platforms, causing them to tilt without toppling, the heat warming selectively so some stones steam while others chill inexplicably, the flora bending to angles that defy horizon lines, flowers blooming in reverse or scattering their petals into constellations that hang unmoving.

In this Construed Lowlands, the built realm is absolute—no shard of unbeaten rock or untrammeled soil intervenes—yet it wears the illusion of wilderness, a low swampish valley hand-forged into being, its sharp-born youth resisting erosion's touch, its wetness ever-renewed in perpetual post-rain stillness, where life—lush, hot, bright, abundant, quiet—rhizomes through the overlays of weird impossibility, unchanging and utterly itself. Walk here, and you might trace the patterns in the stones, wondering if they spell words from the builders' tongues, or if the weeping rocks cry for shores they never knew, the soundless philosophical drip the only message in a world that loops forever on its own facets. The scale invites wandering, valleys within valleys revealing more sharp-edged constructions—tunnels bored into stone hills (though hills are fabricated, the low elevation never rising high), pools that mirror infinite repetitions of the fauna, the vegetation tangling in self-similar webs. Yet emptiness persists, sparseness ensuring that each element stands distinct, a space-filled sculpture where nothing encroaches, only the bright light illuminating fractures in reality's code, heat-lent shimmers where time folds in on itself, never progressing, a frozen equilibrium of crafted dreams.
