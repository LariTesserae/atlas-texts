Here, on the summits of the World-Wound, the air hangs warm and still—a perpetual balmy hush that clings like mist to every crag, though no mist exists, no vapor, no drop of water to condense or weep. It is a place of high elevation, where vast plateaus sprawl like the backs of dormant giants, rising jagged and raw from the underworld's slow push. These heights stretch outward in open scale, not as a single peak but as a region of emergent contours: ridges and hollows just sketching themselves into existence, their edges soft with nascent erosion, like clay mid-mold, frozen in the act of becoming. No wind wears them down; the dynamic here is eternal stasis, a frozen equilibrium where nothing shifts, where gradients of form have been paused at the brink of completion. It is a landscape that never ages, never crumbles further—mountains held in their embryonic curl, basalts and granites emerging vein by vein from some deeper, unseen forge.

The materials of this place are mineral kin to industry, but no hands have shaped them. They pulse with a quiet electrification, veins of ore threading the stone like circuitous wiring, humming subdued through the rock. Crystals jut in lush profusion, not like barren jewels but as vegetation, verdant in their strangeness: broad leaves of translucent quartz, veined in iron filaments that flicker with dim, industrial glow, drawing sustenance from the earth's mechanized core. There is no water here—no seas, no streams, no dew—to sustain life, yet these plants thrive in non-aqueous alchemy, their roots delving into pure mineral beds, siphoning the raw hum of factories unseen. Where soil might form in softer worlds, here it's a dry amalgamation of powdered bismuth and cobalt, scuffed by the slow, halted creep of emergent rocks. The vegetation is lush in its own arid way, carpets of spiny fern-like fronds blooming from cleavage points in the stone, or towering spires of gypsum that twist skyward like pistons, their surfaces etched with faint, machine-like engravings—gargoyles of brass and copper clinging in relief, though they were never carved, only precipitated from the slow, electrostatic births of the world.

Atmosphere is thin and sparse, carrying no scent of decay or bloom, only the faint, electrified tang of warming metals. It is warm without heat's bite—a constant embrace, as if the sun above, that normal orb of light casting even shadows across the plateaus, has warmed the stones for eons without relent. Vast spaces yawn between presences: a promenade of crystals might crown one ridge, barren for leagues before another sprouts distant on the horizon, the emptiness hollow and inviting nothingness. No fauna stirs this void—no birds to wheel overhead, no insects in the crystalline thickets, no crawlers in the mineral dust. Life here is a silent edifice, static and singular, ruled by the world's unspoken laws: motion is a thing of mild vibration only, time looped in perpetual now, where the slow throb of underground engines—industrial pulses, throttling like idle furnaces—drives the barest ripening of form. Erosion emerges but never fully; a cliffside might be fracturing, cracks spreading like fork lightning, but they halt mid-fissure, suspended. Tech infuses all as natural fact: not built structures, but the land itself mechanized, gears grinding faintly in the bedrock, chains of alloy snaking through veins, factories of scale etched into the unformed stone—all untouched, undeveloped for hands or minds save the planet's own industrial dream.

Sounds are scarce, a quietness that wells up from the ground, the low drone of machinery in repose: a subsonic thrum, like distant turbines cooling, or the tick of relays settling into equilibrium. Occasionally, a crystalline branch flexes with a faint crackle, discharging static across empty air. Wind? None stirs; it is silence's domain, broken only by the minor harmonies of mineral expansion—slow expansions that are the world's breath, never accelerating, never waning.

Ecology here bends to its own chemistry, life without predation or cycle, where vegetation feeds on the industrious hum alone, growth a slow accumulation in this frozen state. Crystals bloom in odd symmetries, their geometries imperfect yet poised, fractals waiting for release that never comes. Below, the plateau-thinned soil hollows into caverns of sparking limestone, lit by veins of luminescent ore, vast chambers echoing with the faint clang of auto-forming machinery—furnaces firing without fuel, belts conveying ore dust in endless loops across conveyor floors that emerged organically from the stone. Rules govern invisibly: no diurnal rhythm save the sun's unwavering gaze; no night-fall to chill or disrupt; no alteration beyond the subtlest accretion. It is a world where becoming is arrested, where the lush wilderness of crystal-scapes stands sentinel in perpetuity, a testament to a geology that labors industrially within, ever on the cusp of fuller revelation, yet held fast by the unbreakable law of stillness. One might wander these open wastes, stepping lightly over fallen spars of engineered quartz, feeling the warm pulse beneath, and know that in this strange, high-warm desert of becoming, nothing ever truly forms or falters—just endures, quietly, as the machines of the deep grind on in their eternal idyll.
