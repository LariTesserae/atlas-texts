The cavern begins as a sliver, a forgotten fissure in the endless basalt skein of the underworld—a place so tightly contained that it feels embalmed, like a memory preserved in amber, yet vast in its internal emptiness. You enter not through a grand portal but by forcing your way past fibrous curtains of woven mineral vines, their textures eerily soft against your fingers despite the jagged stone they mimic. The scale is intimate, pocket-like, as if this entire world were no larger than a modest chamber in a forgotten archive, perhaps ten paces across at its widest point, though the hollow expanses within stretch perceptions into illusionary infinities. Elevation is uniformly low, the floor a polished depression worn flat by eons of microscopic abrasion, a basin-level plain of inert rock that slopes barely at all. Erosion has defined everything here: primordial forces have ground the walls smooth, reducing sharp edges to whispers of contours, leaving the space in its oldest stable state—a near-flattened relic where change has dwindled to imperceptible drifts. No water has ever carved these troughs; instead, it's a void of dryness, where chemistry defies the aqueous, relying on sublimated mineral vapors that pool and dissipate like forgotten thoughts.

Darkness reigns absolute, a velvet shroud unbroken by any natural light, amplifying every sensation to tactile or auditory extremes. Temperature hovers cool and steady, never rising or falling, a constant chill that seeps through your bones, not from ice but from the slow exhala of subterranean gases. This coolness feels ancient, as if the thermal memory of the cavern lingers from epochs when the world above was molten and young. Air stirs faintly—weak gradients, minimal flux—carried by patient currents that cycle over days or weeks, redistributing the faint, metallic tang of evaporated silicates. Touch the walls, and they hum with that same coolness, uneven but resilient, etched by eons of chemical decay into fluted grooves that resemble fossilized waves.

Vegetation dominates, filling the space as substrate, architecture, and pure ecosystem, a total biomass that crowds out voids with relentless strangeness. Yet it isn't green leaves or woody stalks; in this non-aqueous alchemy, it manifests as crystalline matrices, networks of elongated, translucent spires that sprout from crevices and fracture lines, growing like inverted geodes. These are the plants—vigorously alive but utterly alien—fanning outward in layered tiers that form the bulk of the urban structures dotting the cavern floor. They rise in organized patterns, webs of interlocking branches that mimic primitive infrastructures: arched bridges crossing empty chasms, interlocking walls partitioning the space into settlement-scale enclosures, and multi-level platforms connected by spiraling ramps. All hand-crafted through simple metallurgy—bronze-pinned joints hammered into place with stone tools, the remnants of early fabrication scattered like forgotten debris amid the growth. The crystal-veins pulse subtly under your hand, a slow, phloem-like flow of mineral sap that builds and rebuilds over months, incorporating discarded artifacts into their lattice. Density is absurdly low; these structures stand acres apart in the cavern's vast hollowness, leaving immense pockets of near-void where the features seem suspended in emptiness, like celestial bodies orbiting an invisible core. The vegetation-as-architecture sustains itself, a symbiotic cycle where older spires crumble into nutrient dust that feeds new outgrowths, a gradual ebb and flow across eons without haste.

Ecology here is rule-bound by strangeness and absence: no fauna stirs, no scurrying creatures or airborne inhabitants to disrupt the stillness, leaving forests of crystal-pillars as the sole living mass. The plants metabolize through a weird, plasmonic process—harvesting faint thermoelectric eddies from the rock's latent heat, perhaps birthed from primordial chaos that seeded this pocket with bio-mineral hybrids. Rules emerge from their forms: new "buildings" sprout only where tensile forces allow, forming layered constructions over generation upon generation, each iteration a slight variation on the last, preserving layouts through patient evolution. The floor, too, is carpeted in their outgrowths—vermiculate mats of finer veins that spread like roots, creating a yielding substrate underfoot, soft and crackling with quiet energy. It's a pure-plant ecosystem, bizarrely vocal: the growth itself generates sound, a loud symphony of groans and snaps as structures strain against their own slow accumulations, echoes amplified in the hollow voids. Creaks rumble like distant thunder, percussions from cracking lattices bridge the emptiness, and a steady undertone of metallic screeching—vibrations from expanding crystals—fills the air incessantly, never shouting but omnipresent, as if the place itself were exhaling through labored breaths.

Weirdness infuses every corner, warping expectations: the crystals sometimes flicker with illusory phosphoresce, false lights born from optical refractions in the darkness, shaping ghostly silhouettes of non-existent ruins that vanish like mirages. Deeper veins twist into impossible geometries—self-limiting fractals that loop back on themselves, defying Euclidean order, as if the cavern were a thought experiment in recursive density. Distant edges blur, suggesting the pocket embeds within larger voids, perhaps a web of linked micro-environments like this one, stitched into the bedrock. Dynamics are glacially slow: plants inch upward over decades, infrastructure reconfigures through centuries of subtle burrs and blooms, erosion nibbles at the edges with microscopic teeth, all under the roar of unchanging soundscapes. Walk the interspersed bridges—flimsy feats of crystal-forging, braced by hand-wrenched rivets—and feel the void gaping below, a chasm where light would reveal nothing, just the echo of your own footsteps. This is a place settled into its core, aged to quiescence, yet alive with its strange, auditory heartbeat—a landless city in eternal night, where primitive hands once labored and departed, leaving behind a world that grows without them, one crystalline whisper at a time.
