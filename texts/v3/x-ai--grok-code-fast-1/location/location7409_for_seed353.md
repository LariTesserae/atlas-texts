You approach the place in its enveloping gloom, where light clings tenuously—like threads of phosphor residue scattered across cracked metallic veins—and only reveals what stirs nearest your skin: the faint luminescence of sap that oozes from fissured stems, glowing not with life but with frictional heat. This is the High Forge, a region spanning what might be called a vast plateau if plateaus were measured in seismic upheavals rather than flatness. It rises defiantly on a continental scale, a mid-range expanse that fills your vision with jagged peaks and gullied valleys, each structure carved not by rivers but by the grinding friction of its own relentless activity. Elevation here is a weaponized thing; the plateau looms high enough to press against unseen ceilings of perpetual night, where thin atmospheric haze blots out any star or sky, leaving only a void so absolute it feels like the absence of gravity itself. You can sense the height in the way the air thins, hotter than flesh should bear, thickened by particulate exhalations from the earth, carrying scents of scorched cellulose and ionized resin that singe your lungs with each breath.

Everything here is built. There is no substrate beneath—no exposed rock or soil from some primordial world, no vestige of untouched mineral to anchor the wild growth that consumes it all. The entire landscape has been fabricated, layer upon layer, in an act of colossal industry that predates history itself. These are the remnants of ancient forges—massive engines, factories, and mechanisms scaled for gods, where gears and pistons and conveyor belts were assembled in labyrinthine circuits that span valleys and orchestrate the region's chaotic flux. But these constructs are not of steel or stone; they are wrought from vegetation itself, engineered in a perverse alchemy where industrial processes birthed plant forms that devoured their makers. The vegetation is the totality, those parameters bleeding into the built, the made, the mechanical. Plants fill every crevice, every scale: vines as thick as stone columns twisting into shafts for unknowable drives, fronds unfurling as scaffold frames for pulley systems that haul steaming lattices of growth upward, toward a heaving canopy that pulses like a living oven. This is not a garden mimicking industry; it is industry incarnate in plant-flesh, a pure ecosystem where chlorophyll factories photosynthesize not light but geothermal fury, drawing energy from vents that erupt in silvery plumes of mineral ash. No water coarses these veins—chemistry here defies such damp necessities, fueled instead by xanthophyll oxides distilled from the heat itself, where "sap" is a volatile ichor of hydrocarbons and heat-warped polymers, burning slow like a furnace's breath.

The terrain undulates with that ancient erosion, a settled decay so profound it borders on annihilation—a once-teeming plateau worn down by eons of its own turbulent rebellion. Canyons gape where factories crumbled inward, their vegetal gears flaking into fibrous dust that gathers in drifts as tall as mountains. Peaks have been planed flat by the endless abrasion of growth cycles, where new stalks burst forth, grind against their siblings, and shed flakes that avalanche in silence. It is the oldest stable state, this kingdom of wear, where forms have forgotten their origins and settled into a fractal repetition: hulks of living machinery, their bark-armor scored by geothermal blush, now fossils in waiting, their motions slowed to a glacial crawl. Yet dynamism runs rampant beneath the quiet—a bazaar of internal storms, Solaris-like in its regenerative fury. Beneath the surface stillness, the land churns with hyperactive conversion: pistons of root-thicket swell and burst, venting gouts of pressurized steam that reshape tunnels in seconds, factories birthing new appendages from lateral buds that erupt into patched conveyor systems, pulling in raw mineral from vents—dry nebulae of iridescent salts that crystallize on stems, scraped free by scabrous fronds, churned into the ever-expanding mass. It is packed, this place; no room exists between presences, a saturation so complete that moving through it requires cleaving growths with your own heat, parting dense hedges of capillary pipes and thrumming boilers that yield reluctantly, oozing sticky exudate that cools like wax on your skin.

Sounds here are a deception in the darkness: mostly quiet, save for the muffled thrum of subterranean forges echoing through hollow conduits, a low bass pulse that rattles leaves and sends vibrations skittering like insects across bark—though no animals dwell here, no fauna to stir the stillness. Absent are the cries of life; only the vegetative mechanisms persist, their industrial rhythms muted: the hiss of hydraulic fibers contracting, the creak of lignin joints articulating in slow gear-grind, the far-off whistle of vapor escaping ruptured veins. These noises weave a tapestry of near-silence, punctuated by abrupt tumult—the sudden collapse of a overtaxed assembly line, sending cascades of splintered matter avalanching in a roar that fades quickly into the void. Temperature scalds the air, a constant scorch hovering near evaporation thresholds, where heat radiates from every surface: feverish fronds baking like forges, vents exhaling Welcome to sulfuric exhalations that thicken the hot murk. No cool relief; the climate is a engine-room inferno, sustaining the non-aqueous pulse of the ecology—the plants thriving on thermal cycles, their polymers hardening or softening in response to surges, the whole plateau a thermodynamic organism, rhythmic in its overheating and purge.

Rules govern this world in alien strata— not the physics of worlds with lakes or winds, but a crafted biomechanics where stability is illusion. Preservation demands the turbulent churn: overgrowth is checked by self-devouring apparatuses, where matured factories shed blight into chutes that feed the roots of younger kin, recycling in a constant anarchy of creation and disintegration. Wander far enough, and you might trigger latent protocol—sensor-roots that detect biomass anomalies, unleashing rapid bud-swells to assimilate intruders, or seismic pistons that upheave the ground in cascades to merge disparate assemblies. Scale demands regional navigation; paths defined not by roads but by the flow of process streams, valleys serving as flue-channels for exhaust, peaks as hypertensive reservoirs venting excess steam. Within this packed, dark immensity, niches emerge: cooler undergrowth caverns carved by eroding flows, where stubby fungal analogs—mimics without water's blessing—twine into control hubs, flickering with faint code-lights etched in spore-script. Hallucinatory pockets bead in condensed mists, where distorted echoes of gear-work conjure phantasms of purpose, blurring the line between built intent and vegetal whimsy. It is strange, this dominion, where the industrial artifact has become the substrate for life, the plants no mere overgrowth but the very marrow of mechanized existence, eternal in their quiet roar, forging nothing but themselves in an endless, heat-soaked night. If you linger, the place draws you in—roots probing, heat beckoning—until you too become part of the sublime machinery, woven into stems that pulse with the plateau's ancient pulse.
