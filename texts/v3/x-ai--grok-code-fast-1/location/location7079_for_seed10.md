No human hand has touched this place—at least, none that you'd recognize in the frayed coherence of standard reality. No buildings, no artificed paths, no remnants of camps or tools; it's utterly undeveloped, pristine in its rebellion against order. Light is scarce, a perpetual twilight that filters through perpetual clouds, casting everything in shades of bruise-purple and oxidized gray. Sunlight seldom pierces here—perhaps it does elsewhere, but in this basin, reality's membrane has thinned, allowing only diffused, murky radiance that makes shadows stretch long and pulse like half-remembered dreams. The air itself seems to absorb illumination, condensing droplets that refract what little glow there is into prismatic splotches on stone and leaf.

Predation here is turbulent, a microcosm of the land's chaos: beasts merge temporarily, biomechanical hyena-things fusing with plant tendrils in symbiotic hunts, only to tear apart again in ecstatic violence.

Creation and destruction dance inextricably—plants "die" by collapsing into slag heaps that bubble and re-flower as factories, animals shed skins that regenerate as new limbs in improbable geometries.
