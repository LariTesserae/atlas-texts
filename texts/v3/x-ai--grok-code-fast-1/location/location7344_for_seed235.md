The city sprawls across the heights like a petrified waterfall, frozen in mid-cascade, its towers and spires extending not just regionally but into unseen horizons—valleys carved from basalt and obsidian that loop back on themselves in impossible recursions, where a glance over the edge reveals echoes of the same pinnacles rising below, defying gravity's steady hold. This elevation is extreme, not merely towering mountains but a labyrinthine plateau of colossal steps, each layer an ancient terrace hewn from volcanic stone that ascends to razor-thin ridges where the air compresses into a humid haze. The entire expanse feels established, weathered by eons rather than years: facades of layered rock, smoothed by perpetual condensation and the slow creep of vines, stand in eternal balance, no crumble or collapse interrupting the scene. Erosion here is a settled negotiation, the land's forms refined to an immutable harmony, like sculpture that breathes only in stillness.

Humidity clings to everything, a wet sheen that blurs boundaries—frequent droplets beading on leaf and stone alike, as if the world perpetually emerges from a storm it never unleashed. Water is near the surface, seeping through cracks in the terraces, forming shallow pools in the depressions and feeding the lush spread of vegetation that chokes every available space. Ferns and mosses erupt in dense clusters, their fronds curling around pillars and balustrades, branches intertwined with what might be roots or veins of the city itself. Temperature hovers at scorching heat, not a dry inferno but a suffocating steam, the air thick with moisture that condenses into beads on exposed skin, making every breath a gulp of liquid silk. Yet nothing moves; no breeze stirs this atmosphere of suspended vapor, the steamy warmth unchanging, a thermal equilibrium that holds the landscape in amber-like stasis.

Density saturates the scene—buildings pile upon buildings in a multi-storey sprawl, their structures woven into the very hillocks and plateaus, stone ablaze with integrated greenery. Urban in scale, this is no town but a metropolis of layered construction, avenues of stacked habitations curving along the ridgelines, balcones cascading like frozen rapids, rooftops bent into impossible angles where perspectives warp. Technology is absent entirely, no metal glints, no contrivances; instead, pure biological architecture prevails—walls grown from living rock, infused with symbiotic fungi that bind the blocks, corridors lined with pulsating veins that mimic roots or arteries. The place is a blend of constructed and organic, descending terraces featuring what could be grand halls or habitats, their openings adjoined by gardens of interlocking stems and flowers, all adjacent, pressing close, a saturation of presences that makes solitude a forgotten concept. Fauna is null—nothing lives here that breathes or scurries; ecology is vegetative dominion, plants ruling in silent, unrelenting growth. Stranger things emerge: blooms that flower with eyes, watching without lids; vines that loop into knots suggesting faces, expressions frozen in perpetual grimace. The land's rules dictate this stability—no decay, no bloom, no wither; time is invariant, a frozen equilibrium where seeds never sprout anew, leaves never fall, all held in arrested development.

Light bathes the expanse in normal illumination, a perpetual midday glow filtered through the steam, casting long, uniform shadows onto the dense foliage and intricate edifices. It never dims or shifts; dawn and dusk are myths here, the sun (or whatever source) inert, hanging in an unaltered sky that breaks down at the edges—horizons where the city recedes into fractal repetitions, layers of itself folding inward like a mandala that shifts when unseen, reality fraying into impossible geometries. Avenues stretch to infinity yet double back upon the viewer, portals in the stone leading nowhere or everywhere, tips of spires piercing clouds that evaporate into the updraft without movement. Sounds dominate deafeningly: a constant roar like distant cataracts merging with the hum of condensing vapor, whispers of vegetation rustling against stone in a volume that muffles thought, booms echoing from distant reformed propositions, the din of a world unscrolling its strangeness without end—a cacophony that deafens the mind, yet never varies, never crescendos or fades, locked in sonic equilibrium. This is a place where existence loops back on itself, elevation defying fall, heat mating with wetness in unresolving tension, urban sprawl entangled with wilderness, all held immobile amid the breakdown of coherent space, a landscape that gazes back at those who perceive it, unwavering and profoundly itself.
