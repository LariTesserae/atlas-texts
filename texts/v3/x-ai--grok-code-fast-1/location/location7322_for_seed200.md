In the eternal haze of this place, where time coils upon itself in unbreakable loops, the world unfolds as a vast, open basin of primordial emergence. We call it the Weeping Hollow—a name whispered by those who linger on its fringes, though none truly dwell here, for there is no dwelling to be had. It spans beyond sight, a regional valley expanse, valley-deep in its low elevation yet stretching across what feels like the breadth of forgotten continents, where hills rise gently only to dissolve back into the mire, no peaks piercing the sky, no abrupt drops scarring the land. The air hangs thick and condensing, a perpetual after-rain mist that beads on every surface, as if the world itself weeps from some unremembered sorrow. Water is not scarce; it pulses everywhere, near the surface, seeping from weeping rocks that bleed tears of mineral-laden fluid, pooling in embryonic hollows that form and reform with a slow, unhurried patience. Marshes edge every bend, their borders blurring into sodden meadows, where post-rain rivulets thread through the vegetation like veins through an upturned palm.

The heat envelops all, a sweltering embrace that never wanes, the sun—if there is one—obscured by layers of humid fog that dim the light to a perpetual twilight. Not quite darkness, but a gloaming that suffuses everything in amber shadows, where forms seem softened at the edges, illusory, unreal. In this equilibrium, the temperature holds steady: no dawn to chill the night, no respite from the furnace. It is hot in the way of fermenting organics, where life decomposes even as it grows, a stench of decay mingling with the sweet musk of overripe fruit and blooming spores.

Density presses in here. Nothing is sparse; the world is saturated, presences crowding each other in near-touching tangles. Vegetation explodes in lush profusion, a tangled weave of ferns and vines that layer upon layer, stems entwining like lovers, leaves overlapping in thick mats that could swallow whole beasts. There are no towering trees in this embryonic phase—no ancient forests etched by centuries of erosion—but rather a primordial undergrowth, shoots and tendrils emerging raw and unformed from the wet earth, as if the land is still dreaming itself into being. Erosion sculpts faintly, subtly: rivulets carve tentative channels into soft, crumbling clay, rocks weep silt into nascent pools, but nothing deepens, nothing accelerates. It is a world in eternal gestation, where formation hangs poised, the geology embryonic, raw stones studding the mire like unshaped jewels, untouched by the chisel of time.

Sound crashes loud and unrelenting, a cacophony that thunders from dawnless to twilightless, echoing off the layered foliage without end. It is the fauna that bellows—a overwhelming abundance, creatures packed so densely that you brush against them at every step, their bodies a constant friction of hides and scales. Insects drone in swarms that blot the air, larger than fists, their buzz vibrating the puddles like rainfall. Amphibians croak in rolling crescendos from the marshes, voices blending into a symphony that drowns thought, punctuated by the guttural roars of scaled beasts that thrash in the underbrush, their calls mimicking thunder, or perhaps invoking it. Birds shriek in flocks so thick they darken the dim sky, wings beating against the mist, cries shrill and piercing, drowning out the softer gurgle of water. Yet amidst the din, a weirdness pervades, unusual and uncanny: the creatures here are not mere animals, but chimerical blends, their forms birthing from the same embryonic rawness as the land. There, a herd grazes on granite outcrops, their hides crystalline, refracting the dim light into prisms that pulse like breathing organs. Further, luminescent parasites glow faintly from the vines, their phosphorescence outlining paths that shift as you watch—not illusions, but presences that adapt without changing, their patterns locked in eternal flux yet never progressing. Fauna that fuses with flora: trees bearing fruit that scream when plucked, roots that writhe like serpents towards the heat of passing breath, and flocks of winged embryos that hatch mid-flight, unfolding into full forms only to fall back into the mire, reborn.

Ecology thrives in this frozen equilibrium, a perfect balance of predation and proliferation, where life devours and regenerates in perfect sync. Nutrient-rich waters feed the lush growth, which shelters the overwhelming fauna, which in turn fertilizes the soil with their unending cycles of life and excrement. Parasites lace the air, borne on condensing droplets, entering bodies to alter without harm—perhaps granting brief visions of other worlds, or staving off decay. Yet nothing evolves; the rules are ironclad stasis. A fern sprouts, unfurls to perfection, dies back, only to sprout anew in the exact pattern. A beast hunts, devours, excretes, reproduces in identical sequences, the DNA of the place woven into unbreakable strands. No storms disrupt, no floods sweep; the water cycles in place, condensing eternally from the weeping rocks, filtering through the dense layers to beneath the surface but never flooding higher nor receding lower. The fauna's cries and movements form a rhythmic drone, loud but unchanging, a eternal chorus that defines the place.

To wander is to enter a labyrinth of sensation, where hot humidity clings, dim visions blur boundaries, and weird wonders emerge unexpectedly. Follow a winding path of moss-veined stones, and you might stumble upon a glade where bioluminescent pools mirror the sky's faint glow, their surfaces rippling with the submerged movements of unseen depths—embryonic leviathans, perhaps, or simply schools of fish that weave in crystalline symbiosis. Climb a low rise of raw granite, weeping streams tracing rivulets, and gaze upon the open expanse: pastures of knee-high ferns waving in a wind that gusts but never strengthens, herds of chimerical grazers mingling with serpentine vines that coil protectively around translucent eggs. The air thrums with vibration, a resonance from the earth's core perhaps, or simply the collective hum of a trillion tiny lives packed into the density. Here, the place asserts its rules: touch nothing without consequence, for even a displaced leaf regrows identically; wait long enough, and the cycle repeats not in days but in instants of recognition. It is a world of pure nature, biological only, where tools or constructs are as foreign as stars in the mist—nothing built, nothing contrived, everything emergent from the raw and the embryonic.

As you delve deeper, the weirdness deepens. Fauna that mimic the erosion: crabs with shells like cracking clay, their pincers slowly sculpting temporary homes in the weeping rocks. Or flocks that form mandalas in the dim air, shapes that hint at ancient meanings, perhaps warnings or invocations, but locked in stasis, never resolving. Yet there is beauty in the never-changing: the lushness flourishes without tipping into chaos, the heat tempers without scorching, the loud sounds harmonize without discord. In this hollow, time itself seems to weep, condensing into mist that obscures the edges of reality, leaving you to wander in eternal, hot twilight, where every moment is reborn from the embryonic rock and water, dense with life, weirdly alive, and utterly unchanging.
