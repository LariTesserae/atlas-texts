Benevolent and wise are qualities not easily defined, for they shift like the dripping waters of Weepstone Hollow. What one calls benevolence might be the other a cruel indifference in the grand weave of existence. Yet, if we follow the heart's quiet pulse, intuition whispers that true wisdom observes before intervening, acts only where the threads of life are fraying, and ever seeks harmony—not dominion. Reason, too, nudges us: this place is a self-sustaining cycle, born of upheaval and renewal, where every drop and glow is a ritual of balance. To impose upon it would be to unravel what the Hollowfolk have so delicately woven.

But let us imagine, nonetheless. Suppose the attention comes from Aelveera, the Elder Current—one who is not a god per se, but a primordial essence, a confluence of waters ancient beyond memory, wise in the way rivers remember their sources. She is benevolent not in the manner of gifts showered from above, but in the patient nurture of flows that survive eons. Quiet, eternal, she dwells in the deep veins beneath the earth, her form a vast, unseen network of underground rivers and forgotten oceans. She has wandered the realms since time's first tremors, observing worlds like Weepstone Hollow: places where upheaval births newness, where beings adapt not by conquest, but by absorption and surrender. This basin catches her eye not through some frantic plea, but through the subtle resonance of its rhythms—the drip of weeping rocks echoing her own endless murmurs, the luminescent fungi mirroring the bioluminescent memories she carries in her depths.

What would Aelveera do, if anything?

She would not descend in a spectacle of storms or light, for spectacle is the folly of the uncertain. Instead, she would seep into the hollow as she has seeped into countless realms: a slow infiltration, a benevolence woven invisibly into the fabric. At first, nothing changes—her presence is a deepening of the damp, a shiver in the air that the Hollowfolk might call "just another chill." She observes through the pooling waters, her essence merging with the central forum's spring. Here, she listens: to the elders' tales of drowned pasts, to the youths' chants to the waterfowl, to the Weepers' silent visions. She sees the balance—water as sacrament, disputes dissolved in immersion, life coaxed from fungi and earth without greed.

But wisdom dictates action where stagnation whispers. The basin's edges erode ever sharper; cracks widen in the cliffs, threatening to swallow homes. The Hollowfolk endure by constant mending, but what if the upheavals quicken, born of some distant cataclysm? Aelveera, benevolent, decides to subtly strengthen. In the dead of a fog-enshrouded night, she infuses the weeping rocks with a trace of her immortal vitality—not to alter them, but to enhance. The rocks weep not just water now, but a faint, iridescent ichor: droplets that, when touched, grant visions not only of submerged histories, but of possible futures—glimpses of the basin calming into valleys, the fungi blooming briefly in colors of blue and gold, the Hollowfolk flourishing without endless toil.

She gifts the Weepers greater clarity. Those formless spirits, once mere chills skittering on the wind, now carry messages with purpose, guiding errands for the elderly or warning of imminent slides. And to Lirra-Moss, the elixir-keeper, she imparts a dream-vision during her next sip from the forum: a recipe for a new infusion, one that tempers the land's tremors. It involves not just thorns and spores, but a crystal essence drawn from the mineral-rich waters, brewed in the full glow of a lunar pulse. This elixir, whispered into her mind, stabilizes foundations—allowing homes to settle without collapsing, turning the basin's birthing pains into gentle breathe.

Yet Aelveera's benevolence is not without cost or choice; she offers no miracles that demand sacrifice. The Hollowfolk must weave this gift into their ways—youths like Riven-Drip experiment with the ichor, painting fungal patterns on walls that now hold firm against erosion. Elders debate its use, for in their strata society, change comes slow. If they reject it outright, Aelveera withdraws, her currents ebbing back, leaving the hollow as it was—perhaps wiser for the temporary presence, perhaps not. But intuition suggests they embrace it, their porous forms adapting as ever, absorbing her gift like dew on moss.

In form, this intervention unfolds as a poem sung by the basin itself—a quiet hymn etched in vapor and shadow:

*Beneath the weeping stones, a river stirs,*  
*Aelveera hums, her waters wiser than earth.*  
*Not to command, but to caress and cure,*  
*She sinks into the pools where visions lure.*  

*Gifts in the ichor, futures in the drip,*  
*Stabilize the tilt, the land's unrest slip.*  
*The Hollowfolk absorb, their glow now deep,*  
*In balance eternal, her secrets they keep.*  

*No flood of force, just a nurturing tide,*  
*A benevolence that watches, waits, abides.*  

If nothing? Perhaps that's the truest wisdom—to watch from afar, entrusting the hollow to its own resilience. But the heart yearns for connection, so Aelveera visits, departing contented, her influence a subtle scar upon the land—a reminder that even in isolation, unseen powers lean in with care. The cycle endures, enriched. And so, the basin weeps on, wise and whole.
