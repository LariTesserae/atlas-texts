It has wandered the multiverse for eons, not to conquer or consume, but to listen—to the faint breaths of hidden worlds, the harmonies of defiantly thriving pockets like this one. And so, when the Sanctum's luminescent pulse catches its attention—a faint, throbbing heartbeat echoing through the cracked plains of the void—it does not rush. The place breathes as one, a deafening symphony where individuality dissolves into resonance, and the Weaver sees in it a microcosm of its own philosophy: harmony not through imposition, but through allowance.

The Weaver, benevolent not out of pity but out of kinship, chooses not to intervene directly. Such acts would disrupt the equilibrium, turning the Sanctum's defiance into dependence, its strangeness into echo of the Weaver's own influence.
