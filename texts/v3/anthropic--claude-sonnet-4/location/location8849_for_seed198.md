The Sunken Terraces of Vaelthara rise from what was once a mountainside, now half-drowned in the warm, mineral-rich waters of an ancient inland sea. Countless generations ago, when the waters first began their inexorable rise, the inhabitants carved their entire civilization into the living rock—not fleeing the flood, but embracing it, reshaping their world to breathe both above and below the waterline.

The city cascades down the submerged slopes in thousands upon thousands of terraced platforms, each one a marvel of primitive engineering. Hand-carved channels and bamboo aqueducts guide the flow between levels, creating a symphony of rushing water that echoes through stone corridors and flooded chambers. The sound is everywhere—gurgling, cascading, dripping—a constant liquid percussion that drowns out most attempts at quiet conversation. Stone hammers ring against chisels as masons work by touch in the perpetual twilight, their strikes adding sharp counterpoint to the water's endless song.

Thick curtains of luminescent moss drape every surface, fed by the mineral-rich spray. Vines with broad, waxy leaves spiral around carved pillars, their roots drinking directly from the flowing channels. Gardens of kelp-like plants wave in the artificial currents, while floating mats of flowering vegetation drift between the platforms like living carpets. The air itself seems green and growing, thick with humidity and the earthy sweetness of decomposing plant matter.

Every inch has been claimed, carved, cultivated. Stone walls curve to follow the natural flow of water, their surfaces smoothed by centuries of passage. Narrow bridges span the gaps between terraces, woven from living vines that grow stronger with age. Chambers burrow deep into the rock, their walls lined with fitted stones and sealed with tree sap and clay. Where one structure ends, another begins—stairs become walls become floors become channels in an unbroken flow of human intention.

The water itself glows faintly with phosphorescent algae, providing the only light in this drowned realm. Deeper chambers exist in near-total darkness, their inhabitants navigating by sound and touch alone. The glow shifts and pulses with the water's movement, casting ever-changing shadows that make the carved reliefs seem to dance.

In the deeper pools, rare creatures drift like ghosts—pale fish with unseeing eyes, translucent crustaceans that click and scuttle through flooded galleries. Occasionally, something larger stirs in the depths below the lowest terraces, sending ripples upward through the entire structure.

The people move through this world like amphibians, equally at home diving through submerged passages or walking the spray-slicked platforms above. They work constantly—expanding, maintaining, adapting. Wooden pulleys creak as they haul carved stones into place. Reed boats loaded with harvested algae navigate the broader channels. Children splash and play in pools that serve as both reservoir and playground, their laughter mixing with the omnipresent sound of moving water.

Time moves differently here, measured not by the sun but by the slow cycles of growth and decay, the gradual wearing of stone, the patient accumulation of moss and mineral. The warm water never freezes, never stops flowing, creating a world suspended between seasons, between earth and sea, between the ancient mountain and the endless, patient flood.
