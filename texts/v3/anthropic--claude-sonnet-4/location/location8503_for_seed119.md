**The Thermal Hollows**

In absolute darkness, a cathedral of carved stone rises from depths that have never known sunlight. The entire structure—every arch, every pillar, every flowing curve—has been hewn by hand from living rock, its surfaces polished smooth by centuries of careful tending. Yet this is no ordinary cathedral, for it exists in a pocket of superheated water, a thermal sanctuary carved into the heart of an underwater mountain.

The water here moves thick and viscous, heated to near-scalding by geothermal vents that were deliberately channeled through hidden conduits in the walls. Steam doesn't rise—cannot rise—in this submerged realm, but instead creates strange, dense currents that spiral through the vast hollow spaces between the carved columns. The architecture is sparse yet monumental: towering buttresses support a vaulted ceiling that disappears into black water above, while the floor stretches smooth and empty between only a handful of massive stone formations.

Every surface bears the marks of primitive tools—stone chisels and bone scrapers, copper implements worn smooth by generations of builders who worked entirely by touch. The carved reliefs that spiral up each pillar tell stories in raised bumps and flowing channels, meant to be read by trailing fingers rather than seen. No metal harder than copper exists here; everything was shaped through patience and pressure rather than advanced technique.

Yet the space vibrates with life and sound. From hidden alcoves carved into the walls comes a constant percussion—the rhythmic clicking and chattering of creatures that have adapted to this strange sanctuary. Crustacean-like beings scuttle along the carved channels, their shells creating metallic tapping as they navigate the stone maze. Larger forms—perhaps descended from deep-sea fish—emit low, thrumming calls that resonate through the water and stone, creating harmonic frequencies that make the entire structure sing.

The strangest inhabitants are translucent, jellyfish-like entities that pulse with their own faint heat signature, barely visible as warm spots in the thermal water. They seem to follow the carved channels deliberately, as if the entire architectural layout serves as a vast circulation system for these ethereal beings. When they cluster together, their combined movement creates powerful water currents that whoosh through the hollow spaces between pillars, adding to the symphony of sound.

Despite the constant activity of its inhabitants, the place exists in perfect equilibrium. The thermal vents maintain their steady heat. The water level never changes. The carved stone shows no sign of further erosion or growth. Even the creatures seem locked into ancient patterns of movement and sound—their clicking, thrumming, and flowing as regular as clockwork, as if this entire underwater cathedral exists outside the normal flow of time.

In the darkest alcoves, where the carved walls curve inward like cupped hands, the water grows hottest of all, and here the sparse vegetation finally appears: strange, fleshy growths that need no light, drawing sustenance from the mineral-rich thermal water and the waste of the scuttling creatures. They grow in careful, geometric patterns that echo the carved reliefs—as if they too are part of this vast, dark, constructed ecosystem where every element serves a purpose in the eternal, rhythmic dance of sound and heat and ancient stone.
