The Hanging Gardens of Aethros stretch across a highland plateau so vast that its edges blur into atmospheric haze, a continent-sized mesa worn smooth by eons until its surface undulates like a sleeping giant's breath. What was once jagged mountain peaks has been ground down by countless millennia into gentle, rolling swells of earth, creating a topography that feels both ancient and eternal—the final form this land will ever take.

Every inch of this immense tableland pulses with plant life. Not merely covered by vegetation, but transformed into a living architecture where the distinction between plant and place has long since dissolved. Massive root systems, thick as subway tunnels, weave through the soil in visible humps and channels, creating the very bones of the landscape. Tree-networks stretch for hundreds of miles, their canopies merging into a single, breathing ceiling that rises and falls like green hills overhead. Ancient vines, grown thick as bridge cables, span valleys and create living archways that frame distant vistas.

The air hangs heavy with moisture, not quite rain but more than mist—a perpetual near-saturation that beads on every surface. Water weeps constantly from the plant-walls, trickling down bark-columns and pooling in the cupped leaves that serve as natural reservoirs. The humidity creates its own weather systems within this green world: localized fogs that drift between the plant-structures, gentle rain that seems to fall upward as much as down, and air so thick with moisture you can almost swim through it.

In the dim, filtered light that penetrates the layered canopies, everything glows with a soft phosphorescence. Fungi the size of houses dot the landscape, their caps serving as platforms and their stems as pillars in this living cathedral. Moss carpets stretch for miles, so dense and spongy that footsteps disappear without trace. The entire ecosystem has reached such a state of saturation and interconnection that it feels less like a forest and more like the interior of some vast, breathing organism.

The temperature holds at a constant, gentle warmth—perfect for the slow, patient processes that govern this place. Nothing here happens quickly. Ancient trees add single rings across decades. Seed pods the size of boulders take centuries to crack open, releasing clouds of spores that drift for years before finding purchase. The very air seems to move in slow, tidal rhythms, as if the entire plateau breathes with geological patience.

Scattered throughout this green infinity are the faintest traces of something once-deliberate: curves of stone too perfect to be natural, arrangements of certain plants that suggest intention, clearings that might once have been courtyards. These hints are so weathered, so overgrown and integrated into the plant-architecture, that they feel more like memories than ruins. Whatever built them has long since become part of the living system itself.

Life moves through this verdant maze in abundance, though glimpsed more than seen. The soft pad of paws on moss, the whisper of wings between leaves, the gentle splash of something drinking from a root-hollow pool. Creatures have evolved to be part of the plant-infrastructure itself: bark-colored mammals that sleep curled in tree-joints, translucent insects that pollinate from within flower-chambers the size of rooms, and things that might be animals or mobile plants, the distinction lost in this ancient green dream.

Sound here is swallowed by the omnipresent vegetation. Footsteps vanish into spongy moss. Voices are absorbed by walls of leaves before they can travel far. What remains is the constant, nearly subliminal whisper of the place breathing—the soft susurrus of moisture condensing and dripping, the almost-inaudible creak of growing wood, the patient rustle of leaves adjusting to catch the dim, filtered light.

This is a landscape that has found its final form and settled into it like a vast creature into sleep. Time here is measured in the slow cycles of sap and season, in the patient accumulation of humus, in the gradual migration of root-highways across the plateau's endless expanse. It is a place where the earth has become completely alive, where every surface breathes and every structure grows, where the boundary between geology and biology dissolved long ago into something altogether stranger and more whole.
