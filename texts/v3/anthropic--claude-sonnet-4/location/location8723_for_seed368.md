In a forgotten hollow carved into the ancient bones of what was once a mountain range, countless eons of wind have worn the stone into impossibly smooth curves. Here, in this hidden pocket no larger than a cathedral nave, rises a vertical city of living crystal.

The structures grow rather than having been built—translucent spires and branching terraces of what might once have been mineral veins, now pulsing with an internal luminescence that shifts from deep amber to pale green. These crystalline formations have been carefully tended and shaped by generations of inhabitants into dwelling-spaces, workshops, and communal chambers. Simple copper pipes and hand-forged iron brackets wind around the crystal growths, channeling not water but streams of luminous gas that seep from cracks in the ancient bedrock.

The air itself seems thick here, heavy with spores and floating seeds that drift in lazy spirals through the dim, filtered light. Where they settle on the crystal surfaces, they sprout immediately into cascading gardens of moss-like organisms that aren't quite plant, aren't quite fungus. These growths drape every surface in overlapping curtains of deep jade and silver-gray, pulsing gently as they photosynthesize the strange gases rather than sunlight. The vegetation grows so densely that walking paths must be constantly pruned clear with simple bronze shears.

Above, the hollow's opening admits only a sliver of sky, casting everything in perpetual twilight. The temperature stays cool and constant, as if the stone walls themselves remember ancient winters and refuse to warm. Sound behaves strangely here—whispers carry far while shouts seem muffled, and the crystal formations themselves emit a constant low harmonic as the gases move through their internal chambers, a sound felt as much as heard.

The inhabitants have learned to work with what grows rather than against it. Their tools are simple—bone needles for coaxing crystal growth in desired directions, wooden forms for training the vegetation, small hammered-metal bowls for collecting the condensed gases that serve as both fuel and nourishment. No beasts move through this vertical maze, yet everything pulses with alien life, breathing and shifting in rhythms that follow no earthly season.

The oldest crystal formations at the bottom have grown so massive and smooth they seem more liquid than solid, their surfaces worn by millions of gentle touches into shapes that hurt to look at directly—geometries that suggest vast age and purpose beyond comprehension.
