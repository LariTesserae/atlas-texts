The Nursery Terraces stretch in careful tiers down the gentle slope, each level no more than knee-high, carved from what seems to be a soft, porous stone that weeps constantly. Water seeps through every surface—not flowing, but emerging, as if the very walls are exhaling moisture into the cool, dim air. The stone itself appears recently shaped, still rough at the edges, with tool marks visible in the perpetual dampness that makes everything glisten faintly in the near-darkness.

Each terrace holds its own shallow pool, perfectly level, perfectly still. The water arrives not from above but from within—beading up through the stone floor, condensing from the air, gathering with infinite patience. Between the terraces, narrow walkways of the same weeping stone connect the levels, worn smooth by countless careful footsteps but never quite dry.

Moss carpets every horizontal surface in thick, velvet cushions of deep green and amber. It grows in deliberate patches, cultivated rather than wild, with spaces between growths that seem planned rather than accidental. Ferns unfurl from carefully placed stone vessels, their fronds catching the constant mist. Small, precise plantings of what might be herbs or tender vegetables emerge from raised beds built into the terrace walls—everything lush but contained, abundant but ordered.

The air thrums. Every pool, every seeping wall, every moss-covered surface hosts its own community of creatures. Frogs of a dozen sizes call from different terraces in overlapping choruses—some deep and resonant, others high and crystalline. Insects drone in shifting harmonies. The sound builds in waves, sometimes unified into a single overwhelming voice, sometimes fracturing into intricate polyrhythms that seem almost architectural in their precision. The stone itself seems to amplify and shape the sound, each terrace adding its own acoustic signature to the endless composition.

Fish move in the pools—silver flashes in the darkness, sometimes breaking the surface with soft plops that add percussion to the creature symphony. Birds roost in the planted spaces, their calls weaving through the amphibian voices. Small mammals dart along the walkways between the levels, their chittering and scrabbling absorbed into the greater sound.

The darkness is nearly complete, broken only by the faintest phosphorescence from certain patches of moss and the rare, pale gleam of a fish scale catching some invisible light source. Yet the space feels intimate rather than vast—perhaps fifty paces across, with maybe a dozen terraces stepping down through the murk. The sound creates its own geography, each pool and planting announcing its presence through the voices of its inhabitants.

Water continues its patient work, slowly reshaping the soft stone, gradually deepening the pools, drop by drop transforming this carefully constructed ecosystem. The builders—whoever they were—seem to have understood that their creation would grow and change, that the emerging stone would be shaped as much by time and water as by their initial vision. Everything here is both ancient and newly born, built yet still becoming.

The temperature holds steady in the cool range, the moisture in the air creating a microclimate that nurtures without overwhelming. Condensation runs constantly down the walls in thin films, feeding the moss, filling the pools, sustaining this chorus of life that fills every corner of the terraced space with deafening, beautiful sound.
