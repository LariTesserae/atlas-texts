**The Hanging Basins of Keth-Mor**

High in the cloud-shrouded peaks, where ancient stone reaches toward perpetual mist, there exists a series of natural stone bowls carved into the mountainside—each one a perfect, water-filled mirror suspended in the thin air. These are the Hanging Basins, formed not by millennia of erosion but by something far stranger: they are still *becoming*, birthed moment by moment from the living rock itself.

The stone here pulses. Not metaphorically—the granite literally contracts and expands with a deep, rhythmic thrumming that reverberates through bone and water alike. Each basin measures perhaps thirty feet across, carved with mathematical precision that defies natural formation. The rock walls undulate gently, growing smoother with each pulse, as if the mountain is learning how to hold water. Fresh stone flowers bloom at the edges—not carved, but *expressed* from within, crystalline formations that emerge finger-length by finger-length, then dissolve back into the matrix, only to emerge again slightly different.

The water within each basin is impossibly deep, dark as obsidian but clear as air. It reflects not just the pewter sky above but something else—glimpses of chambers and spaces that couldn't possibly exist within the solid mountain. Schools of translucent fish, no larger than minnows, appear and vanish in coordinated flashes, moving in patterns too precise to be natural. They seem to be writing something in their movements, characters that form and fade before comprehension.

Between the basins, the mountain face is nearly bare. Thin clusters of silver-leafed moss cling to crevices, trembling not from wind but from the stone's pulse. Occasionally, a single twisted pine emerges from what seems solid rock, its needles black with cold, roots somehow finding purchase in stone that reshapes itself hourly. These trees appear and disappear—not dying, but reabsorbing into the mountain, only to emerge elsewhere days later, identical yet somehow changed.

The sound is overwhelming. Each pulse of the mountain creates a fundamental note so deep it's felt more than heard, but layered atop this are the harmonics: the singing of water as it responds to the stone's rhythm, creating frequencies that make the air itself seem to ring like struck crystal. The sparse fish contribute their own impossible sounds—not splashing, but something like whispered conversations in a language of pure tone. When the mountain pulses, all the basins sing together, a chord that builds and builds until it seems the air might shatter.

The few structures here are bewildering in their simplicity and strangeness. Stone platforms extend over some basins—not built, but encouraged from the mountain itself using tools that leave no marks: wooden levers worn smooth as driftwood, bone wedges that somehow convince rock to flow like thick honey. These platforms serve no obvious purpose; they simply exist, as if the mountain demanded witnesses to its constant becoming.

A handful of figures tend these growing basins. They move with the patience of geological time, carrying vessels carved from single pieces of translucent stone that seem to hold more water than their size should allow. They dress in rough-woven cloth that changes color with the mountain's pulse—now grey as the stone, now blue as the deep water, now silver as the thin air. They speak in whispers that carry impossible distances, their words becoming part of the mountain's song.

The light here is strange—not dim, but somehow concentrated, as if the ordinary daylight has been distilled into something more essential. It pools in the basins, turning the water into mirrors that reflect not just images but *possibilities*—showing the basins as they were an hour ago, as they will be tomorrow, as they might be if the mountain's dreaming took a different turn.

This is a place caught in the act of creation, where the boundary between what is solid and what is possible remains beautifully, turbulently undefined. The mountain sings its deep song, the water answers in crystalline harmonics, and slowly, patiently, something entirely new struggles to be born from stone and dream and the endless, deafening music of becoming.
