Deep within the mountain's throat, where geological violence once carved cathedral chambers, a hidden foundry realm thrums with ceaseless industry. This is the Thermal Gardens—a pocket universe wedged between stone ribs, where escaping geothermal vents have been captured and channeled into something between workshop and greenhouse.

The space reveals itself in tiers, each level carved from the raw rock face that still weeps mineral condensation. Massive brass pipes snake along the walls like metallic vines, carrying superheated gases that would scald flesh but instead feed the garden's impossible abundance. The air shimmers with heat-distortion, yet somehow maintains the bite of high altitude cold wherever the thermal channels don't reach. Your breath fogs even as sweat beads on exposed skin.

Vegetation crowds every surface with almost violent density. But these are not earthly plants—they are copper-leafed and silver-stemmed, their roots drinking directly from mineral-rich thermal springs that bubble up through engineered channels. Crystalline fruits hang heavy from branches that ring like tuning forks when the wind catches them. Moss-like organisms carpet the walls in sheets of living circuitry, pulsing with bioluminescent veins that provide the chamber's primary illumination—a dim, shifting network of blue-green light that makes shadows dance constantly.

The foundry infrastructure weaves between and through this botanical machinery. Steam hammers pound rhythmically against anvils carved from single blocks of basalt, their strikes echoing in thunderous cascades that never quite fade before the next blow lands. Conveyor systems built from grown wood and salvaged metal carry mysterious products through the foliage—ingots that gleam like captured starlight, mechanical components that sprout like seed pods from assembly trees.

Occasionally, something moves through the undergrowth—a flash of fur or scale, creatures adapted to this hybrid environment. They are few, these thermal garden dwellers, but perfectly suited: salamander-like beings with metallic patches that seem to conduct heat, or small mammals whose breath comes out as visible steam even in the warm zones, suggesting metabolisms running far too hot for ordinary flesh.

The soundscape layers itself in waves: the deep, organ-note hum of the thermal pipes, the crystalline chiming of wind through metal leaves, the arhythmic percussion of the steam hammers, and underneath it all, a subtle crackling—the sound of rapid growth, of plants consuming minerals and converting them to living matter at unnatural speed.

At the chamber's heart stands a collection of smaller structures—workshops built into massive tree trunks whose bark gleams with embedded copper veins. These buildings seem grown rather than constructed, their walls breathing slowly, their windows formed from sheets of amber-like resin that glow from within. Thin streams of luminous sap drip from their eaves, pooling in collection basins that overflow into the thermal channel system.

The entire space feels newborn despite its industrial maturity—as if it achieved this complex equilibrium only yesterday, as if the marriage of plant and machine is still settling into its final form. Stone surfaces show fresh tool marks. Some of the larger botanical specimens still have the stretched, elongated look of rapid growth. The very air tastes of possibility and hot metal, of experiments still in progress, of a world that discovered it could remake itself and is still learning what that means.
