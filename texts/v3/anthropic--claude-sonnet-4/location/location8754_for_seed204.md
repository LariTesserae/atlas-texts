The Bone Spires rise from a sea of crystallized breath, their surfaces catching and fracturing the merciless light that pours from three suns wheeling overhead in impossible orbits. These are not mountains as earthly minds would recognize them—they are the mineralized exhalations of some vast dreaming thing buried continent-deep, frozen mid-thought into towers of calcium and phosphorous that stretch beyond the curve of sight.

The city clings to these spires like a parasite of purpose. Stone fitted to stone without mortar, each block carved with obsidian tools by hands that understand the secret geometries of bone. The builders—if builders they were—worked with patient deliberation across centuries, creating terraces and walkways that spiral up the spire faces in patterns that hurt to follow with the eye. Simple pulleys and counterweights move massive blocks even now, operated by figures that cast no shadows despite the blazing triple-star above.

Between the spires stretches emptiness—not void, but a kind of crystalline atmosphere that rings like distant bells when the wind moves through it. The ground far below (if ground it is) cannot be seen; only an endless recession of pale geometries fading into luminous haze. Here in the heights, the air itself has texture, forming faceted sheets and spiraling ribbons that catch light and throw it in directions that seem to bend back upon themselves.

The inhabitants move in vast migrations that follow no pattern the mind can grasp. They are neither quite animal nor quite mineral—creatures of living calcite and hollow bones that sing subsonic hymns as they flow in ribbons through the city's carved channels. Some are small as thumbnails, others vast as buildings, but all share the same translucent quality, as if carved from frozen breath. They cluster around certain architectural nodes—places where the ancient stonework achieves particular harmonic resonances—and there they pulse in slow unison, their combined luminescence creating auroras that dance between the spires.

The silence is not empty but full—so densely packed with near-sound that actual noise would shatter like glass. The creatures' songs exist just below hearing, felt more as pressure in the skull than music. The wind through the crystal air produces tones that exist in mathematical rather than audible space. Even footsteps are swallowed, transformed into resonances that travel through the bone-stone and emerge as gentle warmth or coolness in distant chambers.

Reality grows thin here where the triple suns converge their light. Shadows fall upward. Reflections in the polished bone surfaces show not what stands before them but what stood there centuries ago, or will stand centuries hence. The city's builders carved not just stone but space itself, creating chambers that are larger inside than outside, walkways that return to their own beginnings through paths that never curve. The accumulated weight of their primitive but precise engineering has pressed wrinkles into the fabric of what-is.

Time moves differently at different elevations. In the highest towers, sunsets occur before dawn. In the deep clefts between spires, conversations echo their own beginnings. The city is ancient and newly-built simultaneously—its stones still warm from carving, yet weathered by ages that have not yet arrived.

The light never dims. Three suns in their impossible dance ensure that no point among the spires knows true darkness, only the shifting interplay of primary shadows—red, blue, and gold—that move in patterns that trace the deep mathematics the builders somehow intuited. This light has weight here, pooling in carved basins, flowing down channels cut specifically for its passage, transforming gradually from radiation into something more akin to liquid mineral that the creatures drink with their translucent throats.

Above, always above, the city climbs toward some apex that exists beyond seeing. Below, always below, the crystal atmosphere thickens into uncertainty. Between these absolutes, the inhabitants conduct their patient worship of angles and intervals, building ever upward with tools of knapped stone and accumulated dreams, following blueprints written in the resonant frequencies of their own hollow bones.
