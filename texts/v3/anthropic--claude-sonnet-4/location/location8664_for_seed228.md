In the hollow of a limestone sink, no larger than a cathedral's nave, water seeps eternally from the pale walls. The stone here has been carved smooth by millennia of patient dissolution—every surface curves like the inside of a shell, polished to a dull gleam by countless seasons of moisture. Droplets bead and roll down the carved walls, gathering in shallow pools that reflect the weak light filtering down from the opening forty feet above.

The floor is a maze of standing water—some pools ankle-deep, others barely filming the worn stone. Between them, raised walkways of mineral deposit wind like the ribs of some ancient creature, stained ochre and rust-red by iron-rich seepage. No plant has ever taken root here. The stone itself seems almost organic in its smoothness, as if grown rather than carved, yet utterly barren.

But the silence you might expect is shattered by sound. Thousands of small creatures—not quite frogs, not quite crickets—fill every crevice and depression. Their bodies are translucent, showing the dark shadow of internal organs, and they pulse with bioluminescent spots that flicker like dying stars. When one calls, others answer, until the entire chamber throbs with their chorus—a sound like glass bells struck underwater, reverberating endlessly off the curved walls.

They seem drawn to the constant dripping, clustering beneath each seep in writhing masses. Some have grown to the size of house cats, their bodies stretched and pale from the limestone minerals they've absorbed. They move with deliberate slowness, as if underwater themselves, their calls becoming deeper, more resonant as they age.

In the pools, smaller versions dart like living jewels, their light-spots creating auroras beneath the surface. The water itself glows faintly where they've passed, leaving trails of phosphorescence that fade over minutes. The sound they make together is overwhelming—not painful, but so dense it becomes almost tangible, vibrating through your bones.

Scattered around the chamber's edge lie remnants of crude visitation: shallow stone bowls, their edges worn smooth, positioned to catch the eternal drip. Simple iron hooks driven into crevices, green with verdigris, suggest someone once hung offerings here. A single stone platform, no larger than a table, sits in the center—its surface worn concave by countless depositions of... something. Water collects there too, but the creatures avoid it, their chorus momentarily quieting when they drift too near.

The light above shifts slowly as clouds pass over the opening, sending shadows racing across the water's surface. In these moments of dimness, the creatures' bioluminescence grows brighter, and their calls become more urgent, as if they remember some ancient darkness that once filled this place entirely.

The air is cool and thick with moisture, each breath tasting of minerals and the strange sweetness of the creatures' secretions. Every surface weeps. Every echo amplifies. Every moment stretches like the slow formation of stalactites that will never grow, in this place where water has carved away everything but sound and light and the patient gathering of strange life in the deep hollow of the world.
