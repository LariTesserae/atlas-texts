The Hollow Terraces stretch endlessly across a mountainous continent that exists in permanent defiance of physics, where the impossible architecture of an ancient civilization creates a labyrinth of empty spaces that sing.

These are not mountains in any earthly sense, but vast stepped pyramids of black stone and tarnished metal that rise into cloud-shrouded peaks. Each terrace—some as wide as valleys, others narrow as ledges—has been carved, molded, and constructed into perfect geometric forms that interlock across impossible distances. The builders, long vanished, left behind a world that is entirely artificial yet has achieved its own organic rhythm over geological time.

Water is everywhere and nowhere. It condenses constantly from the hot, humid air that rises from the hollow spaces below, beading on the dark surfaces and running in thin rivulets down carved channels that spiral around the terraced faces. The condensation never stops—the air shimmers with moisture despite the blazing heat that radiates from the stone itself. Steam rises in lazy columns from the depths, carrying with it the mineral scent of heated metal and ancient rock.

But the most striking feature is the emptiness. Between each terrace level yawn vast hollow chambers, some as large as amphitheaters, others stretching into caverns that could swallow cities. These voids create a vertical maze through the mountain-structures, connected by bridges of impossible thinness and tunnels that seem to bend space itself. The hollow spaces generate their own weather systems—updrafts and downdrafts that whistle and moan through the geometric channels, creating a constant symphony of tones that ranges from deep organ notes to crystalline chimes.

Vegetation clings sparsely to this manufactured world. Strange, succulent plants with silver-green leaves grow in the moisture channels, their roots somehow finding purchase in the seamless stone. Occasionally, a lone tree with bark like burnished copper will emerge from a crack where two terraces meet, its branches reaching across empty space toward another tree on a distant level, as if the sparse forest is trying to rebuild itself across the voids.

The fauna has adapted to this vertical maze. Creatures that might once have been birds now navigate the hollow spaces with membrane wings stretched between elongated limbs, their calls echoing strangely through the chambers. They nest in the geometric alcoves and move between levels through aerial highways invisible to earthbound observers. Small, nimble things—neither quite mammal nor reptile—scurry along the carved channels, their movements creating cascades of tiny sounds that add to the constant acoustic tapestry.

The reality breakdown manifests in subtle but persistent ways. Distances refuse to behave consistently—a terrace that appears to be a short climb away remains unreachable after hours of walking, while stepping through certain archways can deposit you on a level that should be miles distant. Water sometimes flows upward, following channels that curve impossibly through space. Shadows fall in directions that don't match the position of the sun, creating patterns of light and dark that shift according to rules that seem almost comprehensible but never quite resolve.

The sound is relentless but not harsh—a deep thrumming that comes from the hollow spaces themselves, punctuated by the musical dripping of condensation, the whisper of steam, the calls of the aerial creatures, and the strange harmonics created when wind moves through the precisely calculated spaces. It's as if the entire continent is a vast instrument, playing itself.

Despite the artificiality, there's an ecosystem here, active and cycling. The creatures follow migration patterns through the three-dimensional maze, timing their movements to the condensation cycles and the mysterious thermal rhythms that pulse through the stone. Predation happens in mid-air as much as on the surfaces. The sparse plants bloom in synchronized waves that sweep across multiple terrace levels, creating brief moments when silver-green flowers cascade through the hollow spaces like living waterfalls.

The heat never breaks, radiating from surfaces that seem to store and amplify the sun's energy, but the constant moisture and the movement of air through the voids creates microclimates that shift and flow. In some chambers, cool mists gather despite the blazing temperature outside. In others, the air shimmers with heat that makes the geometric patterns waver like mirages.

This is a world that was once built with purpose beyond imagination, now left to find its own balance. The absence of any recognizable technology makes the precision of the construction even more mysterious—no tools could have carved these perfect forms, no known engineering could support these impossible spans. Yet here it stands, weathered into its own strange stability, a testament to builders who understood principles of construction that bend reality itself.
