The thermal pools lie scattered across a weathered plateau like jade teardrops caught in ancient stone. Here, where the mountain's spine has been worn smooth by countless seasons, the earth breathes warmth through a dozen small springs that bubble up between rounded granite boulders. The stone itself tells stories—surfaces polished to silk by wind and water, crevices deepened into perfect curves, everything settled into forms that speak of deep time and patient weather.

Steam rises in gentle veils from each pool, the water a startling turquoise against the pale granite. The springs are small, intimate—none larger than a room, most no bigger than a bathtub—but they create their own weather. Moisture beads on every surface. The air shimmers. Condensation drips steadily from overhanging rocks, each drop adding its small percussion to the constant low murmur of bubbling water.

Between the pools, sparse tufts of mountain grass emerge from cracks in the stone, their green made vivid by the constant humidity. Cushions of moss claim the dampest hollows. Here and there, a stunted pine has found purchase in some deeper fissure, its roots drinking from hidden seeps. The vegetation is lush where it exists, but it exists in patches—emerald punctuation marks scattered across the pale stone canvas.

This is their highway, their meeting ground, their amphitheater. The animals come in impossible numbers and variety, drawn by the warmth and the reliable water. Mountain goats descend from the peaks above, their hooves clicking on wet stone as they navigate between pools with casual precision. Families of marmots emerge from boulder piles, their whistled conversations echoing off the granite walls. Eagles spiral overhead in patient thermals, while smaller birds—finches, sparrows, mountain bluebirds—dart between the steam plumes like living jewels.

But it's the chorus that overwhelms the senses. The marmots whistle and chirp in overlapping conversations. Water voles splash and rustle in the vegetation. Pikas bark their territorial claims from hidden rock gardens. The eagles cry overhead while ravens croak commentary from their stone perches. Beneath it all runs the constant liquid murmur of the springs themselves—bubbling, trickling, dripping in a hundred different tones as water finds its way between stones.

The soundscape pulses with the rhythms of the plateau. Mornings bring the tentative explorations of creatures venturing down to drink. By midday, when the sun warms the already-warm stones, the pools become social centers—groups of animals sharing the space in an ancient détente born of mutual need. Afternoons see the territorial disputes, the splash of a goat stepping too close to another's drinking spot, the sharp alarm calls when a hawk's shadow passes over.

As evening approaches and the air cools, the contrast between the warm springs and the mountain air creates its own weather. Fog forms and dissipates in moments. The steam grows thicker, and the animals become shadows in the mist. Night brings different voices—the splash of nocturnal visitors, the distant howl of coyotes from the valleys below, the deep hush of stone cooling under stars.

The plateau exists in perfect balance. The springs have carved their channels through thousands of seasons. The animals have established their rhythms around the thermal oasis. Even the stones seem to know their place, worn into shapes that guide water and provide perfect perches and shelters. It's a small world, bounded by the edge where the plateau drops away to valleys below, contained and complete unto itself—a pocket of warmth and abundance where the mountain's bones break the surface and offer their gifts to any creature bold enough to make the climb.
