Deep within the hollowed heart of a nascent mountain, where ancient magma chambers once pulsed with molten stone, lies a cathedral of cooling rock and nascent industry. The walls curve upward in smooth, glassy obsidian, still radiating warmth from their recent birth—the mountain itself barely a geological infant, its slopes outside still smoking with the memory of eruption.

This is a pocket universe, no larger than a grand ballroom, discovered by chance when the first mining crews broke through what they thought was solid basalt. The air hangs heavy and humid, condensation beading constantly on the curved walls and dripping in irregular percussion onto the metal grating below. The warmth here is enveloping but not oppressive—like being wrapped in a thick, damp blanket while fever breaks.

Where vegetation might grow, there is only the stark beauty of cooling stone and the geometric precision of hastily erected infrastructure. Narrow catwalks of riveted steel spiral around the cavern's perimeter, connecting platforms that jut out at odd angles, following the natural contours of the volcanic glass. Copper pipes snake along the walls, carrying steam and strange fluids, their joints weeping constant moisture that pools in channels carved into the floor.

The sound is overwhelming—a symphony of industrial birth. Steam hisses from dozens of vents, creating a constant white noise punctuated by the rhythmic chunk-chunk-chunk of some great machine embedded in the cavern floor. Metal groans and settles as temperature differentials stress the hasty construction. Water drips in syncopated patterns, each drop echoing differently as it strikes steel, stone, or the shallow pools that gather in the cavern's natural depressions.

In this acoustic chaos, life moves quietly. Pale, eyeless salamanders slip between the pipes, their skin translucent in the amber glow of emergency lighting. They seem drawn to the warmth and moisture, appearing suddenly on grating or vanishing into crevices when the machinery's rhythm changes. Sometimes shadows move in the upper reaches where the catwalks disappear into darkness—whether birds, bats, or something else entirely, it's impossible to tell.

The light comes from a handful of industrial flood lamps, their beams creating harsh pools of visibility separated by deep shadows. Most of the cavern exists in a warm amber twilight, lit indirectly by the reflected glow off wet stone. The darkness above seems absolute—the cavern's ceiling lost in a void that swallows light.

Everything here feels temporary, experimental. Cables run in loose bundles, secured with industrial zip-ties rather than proper conduits. The platforms show signs of hasty welding, with rough beads and unground joints. This is infrastructure in its infancy, thrown together by necessity rather than design, yet already beginning to settle into its own strange ecosystem of metal, stone, and moisture.

The temperature differential creates its own weather: warm updrafts carry the sounds of machinery to the unseen ceiling while cooler air sinks along the walls, creating subtle currents that move the ever-present mist. The humidity forms micro-climates—some corners stay relatively dry while others stream constantly with condensation.

Time moves differently here. The mountain itself is still cooling, still settling—a process that will take millennia. The industrial installation represents human urgency imposed on geological patience, rushing to extract or process something before the opportunity closes. Yet even this urgency has a languid quality in the thick, warm air. Workers move deliberately, conserving energy in the humid environment, their voices echoing strangely in the acoustic maze of metal and stone.

This is a place caught between states: not quite natural, not quite artificial; neither fully formed nor entirely unfinished; warm but not hot, humid but not flooded, noisy but not chaotic. It pulses with potential energy—the mountain's heat slowly dissipating, the machinery's purpose gradually revealing itself, the ecosystem of metal and stone and rare creatures slowly finding its equilibrium in this hidden chamber of glass and steam.
