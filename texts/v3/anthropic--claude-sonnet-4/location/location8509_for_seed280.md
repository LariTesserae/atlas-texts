The Thermal Towers rise from an endless warm sea, their ancient metal spires piercing the surface at irregular intervals across dozens of kilometers. Each tower climbs nearly a thousand meters into the humid air, their bases barnacled with millennia of accumulated sea-growth, their upper reaches disappearing into a perpetual mist that hangs just above the waterline.

The water itself runs warm—blood-warm—heated by geothermal vents that dot the submerged landscape far below. It holds that perfect temperature that makes you forget where your body ends and the liquid begins. The sea stretches unbroken to every horizon, its surface disturbed only by the geometric interruption of the towers and the occasional ripple from something moving beneath.

No plants grow here. The towers, clearly artificial yet weathered smooth by ages of wind and salt spray, seem to be made of a dark alloy that has settled into its own equilibrium with the environment. Mineral deposits streak their surfaces in rust-orange and deep green patinas. At their bases, where they disappear into the warm depths, strange crystalline formations have accreted—not coral, not plant, but something that grows in geometric patterns, adding structure without true life.

The light arrives filtered and diffuse, as if the very atmosphere holds moisture in suspension. Everything exists in a realm of soft shadows and gentle gradients. The mist above the water catches and scatters illumination, creating an environment where day and night blur into a continuous gentle twilight.

But the sea teems with life. Creatures move through the water in vast, slow congregations—things with translucent bodies that pulse with their own faint bioluminescence, massive forms that breach near the towers with barely a splash, their movements more like underwater flight. Schools of something dart between the metal pillars in complex patterns, their synchronized movements creating brief flashes of silver in the amber water. Larger shapes move in the depths, visible only as shifting shadows that suggest intelligence and purpose.

The silence is profound. Sound seems absorbed by the dense, humid air and the endless water. Even the creatures move without noise—no splashing, no calls, only the gentle displacement of warm liquid. Occasionally, a soft metallic resonance emerges from within the towers themselves, a low harmonic that suggests vast spaces and complex systems operating in the depths below the waterline.

The towers clearly house technology beyond easy comprehension. Their surfaces occasionally flicker with patterns of light that move like flowing script, and sometimes geometric ports iris open to release streams of heated water or columns of glowing particles that drift upward and dissolve into the mist. These are not ruins but active installations, tended by processes that require no visible maintenance.

Between the towers, the distances are vast. Swimming from one to another would take hours, and in that warm, endless space, a sense of floating through an alien cathedral emerges. The creatures here have learned to use the towers—not as barriers but as waypoints, gathering around their bases in loose aggregations before dispersing again into the thermal currents.

The entire seascape exists in a state of settled equilibrium, its patterns established over geological time. The towers have been weathered into their current form, the mineral accretions have found their stable configurations, and the abundant sea life moves through cycles as regular and unconscious as tides. This is a world that has found its balance and settled into a warm, quiet, perpetual dream of metal and water and living light.
