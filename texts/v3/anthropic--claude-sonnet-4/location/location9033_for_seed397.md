The foundry district rises on a terraced plateau, its geometric stone platforms carved fresh from the mountain's shoulder where the rock still bleeds mineral-rich water from every joint and fissure. Steam lifts constantly from the streets—not the violent eruption of geysers, but the patient exhalation of a young landscape where groundwater meets warm stone and industrial heat in perpetual, gentle congress.

Here, in this compact quarter of perhaps thirty city blocks, the builders have learned to work with the mountain's weeping. Channels carved into the living rock guide the seepage along predetermined paths, feeding the district's mills and foundries while keeping the streets merely damp rather than flooded. The water emerges amber-tinted from iron deposits, staining the fresh-cut granite facades in rust-bloom patterns that will, in centuries to come, be the district's signature beauty—though now they simply mark it as raw and new.

The architecture follows the water's logic. Buildings rise in stepped formations, their foundations anchored deep into the mountain's flesh where the stone is still sharp from recent quarrying. No softening vegetation masks the construction; instead, the geometric precision of cut stone and mortared joints creates its own stark aesthetic. Copper pipes and iron gutters, their surfaces already acquiring the first patina of mineral deposits, trace obvious paths along building faces, functional and unashamed.

The air thrums with industrial purpose. Steam engines drive the mills that process ore hauled up from lower elevations. The sound is layered but constant—the methodical chunk-hiss of piston engines, the grinding whisper of metal against stone, the bubble and flow of water through the district's arteries. It creates a sustained backdrop against which individual sounds occasionally emerge: the clang of a dropped tool, the brief whistle of a pressure valve, the splash of feet through persistent puddles.

Wildlife has discovered this warm, wet enclave. Mountain birds cluster on the perpetually damp ledges, their numbers surprising for such a stark environment. Small mammals dart between the building foundations where warm springs create microclimates. Most notably, clouds of insects thrive in the humid air, creating shifting veils of movement in the dim light that filters through the industrial haze.

The light itself carries weight here—thick with moisture and particulate from the foundries, it seems almost tangible as it slants through steam and the geometric shadows of the terraced construction. Everything appears slightly softened despite the sharp edges of new stone, as if viewed through a faintly tinted lens.

This is a place caught in a moment of industrial becoming, where human ambition has carved out space for itself in living rock, and where the mountain's response—its patient, endless weeping—has been incorporated into the machinery of daily life. The district feels simultaneously ancient in its stone bones and startlingly new in its purpose, a small world complete unto itself where the marriage of water, stone, and industry has found its own stable rhythm.
