The Foundry Peaks rise like jagged teeth against a perpetually overcast sky, their crystalline faces catching and fracturing what little light filters through the thick, mineral-laden atmosphere. These are not mountains carved by wind and water, but something far more deliberate—towering spires of fused silicate and metal that thrust skyward in perfect geometric formations, their surfaces still bearing the fresh, knife-sharp edges of recent creation.

The air shimmers with heat, thick and metallic, tasting of copper and sulfur. Vents along the mountainsides release steady columns of superheated gas that rise straight up in the still air, creating thermal columns that never waver, never bend. The temperature here hovers at the edge of human tolerance, a dry, oven-like heat that seems to emanate from the very rock itself.

Between the towering peaks, the valleys are packed solid with an intricate honeycomb of structures—not buildings exactly, but geometric forms that seem grown rather than built. Hexagonal chambers, spiral tubes, and faceted domes cluster together with mathematical precision, leaving no gap, no wasted space. The material is uniform throughout: a dark, lustrous substance that might be ceramic or metal or something else entirely, all bearing the same fresh-minted quality as the peaks above.

Scattered throughout this dense matrix are the remnants of industrial machinery—great wheels and gears that once turned, networks of pipes that once carried unknown fluids, massive cylindrical forms that might have been reactors or engines. They stand silent now, their surfaces beginning to show the first signs of integration with the crystalline growth around them, as if the landscape itself is slowly digesting these mechanical remains.

The only sound is a barely perceptible hum, so low it's felt more than heard, emanating from deep within the mountain cores. Occasionally, a distant metallic ping echoes across the valleys—thermal expansion, perhaps, or some deep process still working far below.

No wind stirs here. The air hangs motionless, heated and heavy. In the deeper crevices between the geometric formations, small arthropod-like creatures occasionally emerge—things with too many joints and surfaces that reflect light in impossible ways. They move with mechanical precision, following pathways that seem predetermined, carrying out purposes that remain opaque.

The light comes from no visible source, filtered through atmospheric layers until it becomes a dim, amber glow that casts no shadows. Everything exists in a state of even illumination, as if lit from within the air itself. The landscape extends beyond sight in all directions, peak after peak, valley after valley, all following the same fundamental pattern with only minor variations—a vast engine of unknown purpose, recently formed and now waiting, perfectly preserved in its moment of completion.

Time seems suspended here. The sharp edges show no wear, the geometric forms no settling or shift. Even the ancient machinery appears frozen at the moment of its final shutdown, as if the entire landscape exists in a state of perpetual now, neither growing nor decaying, simply being.
