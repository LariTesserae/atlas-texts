The Foundry Fields stretch across a high plateau where the air shimmers with dry heat, but not the heat of sun—rather the residual warmth of ancient furnaces that once burned here, their thermal memory still radiating from the crystallized ground beneath. This is a place where industry died so long ago that its bones have become geology.

The terrain rolls in gentle, weathered swells, each rise and hollow precisely shaped by millennia of wind carrying abrasive mineral dust. What were once factory complexes and manufacturing districts have been ground down to their essential elements—iron oxides staining the earth in rust-red spirals, pools of hardened slag now smooth as river stones, and everywhere the glitter of metal shards worn to harmless fragments smaller than rice grains. The landscape has digested its industrial past completely, transforming smoke stacks into wind-carved spires of fused brick, conveyor systems into long ridges of compressed aggregate.

Under the perpetually overcast sky—clouds the color of old pewter that never quite rain—the light filters down weak and diffuse. Visibility extends for kilometers across the plateau, revealing the subtle repetition of this processed terrain: parallel furrows where rail lines once ran, now filled with sediment and polished smooth; geometric depressions that hint at vast foundation pours, their corners softened by ages of patient wind.

The density here is not of life but of remnants. Every cubic meter of soil contains countless tiny artifacts—ball bearings worn to perfect spheres, fragments of ceramic insulators, threads of copper wire green with patina. They create a faint metallic music when the wind is strong enough, a barely audible chiming as millions of small pieces shift and settle, but most of the time the Fields rest in complete silence.

The air tastes of minerals—copper pennies and chalk dust, with an undertone of something that might once have been coal smoke but has aged into something earthier, like the smell of dry caves. There is no water here, not even the memory of it; whatever moisture falls from those grey clouds evaporates before touching the warm ground, leaving behind only the faintest crystalline residue.

Nothing grows, nothing moves, nothing changes. The Foundry Fields exist in perfect stasis—a landscape that has found its final form through the complete weathering of human ambition into something that looks almost natural, if nature had ever been made of rust and glass.
