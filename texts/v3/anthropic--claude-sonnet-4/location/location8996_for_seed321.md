The Drowned Terraces of Vaelthros stretch across seven miles of ancient mountainside, where countless centuries of mist and seepage have carved a city from living stone. Water weeps eternally from every surface—not rain, but something more fundamental, as if the mountain itself perspires memories through its porous skin. The terraced levels rise in gentle sweeps, each platform thick with buildings that seem grown rather than built, their walls bulging and flowing like cooled wax, their surfaces slick with perpetual moisture.

The architecture defies immediate understanding. Structures meld into one another, their boundaries uncertain, as if the entire settlement were one vast organism that has differentiated into chambers and passages. Doorways oval and soft-edged lead into spaces that expand impossibly inward. Windows weep condensation that forms intricate patterns on the walls before pooling into channels worn smooth by ages of flow. The stone itself—if it is stone—has a fleshy quality, warm to the touch and yielding slightly underfoot.

Everywhere, water moves. It seeps from walls in beaded curtains, pools in the worn depressions of walkways, and cascades in thin sheets from terrace to terrace. The sound is overwhelming—not the crash of waterfalls but the susurrus of a thousand whispers, the constant murmur of moisture finding its way through every crevice, every pore. The noise builds upon itself, creating harmonics that seem almost linguistic, as if the city were speaking in some wet tongue of echoes and drops.

Between the flowing structures, vegetation erupts in dense profusion. But these are not quite plants as they should be. Broad fronds push through gaps in the walls, their surfaces mirror-bright with collected moisture. Vines spiral up surfaces that bend away from them, yet somehow they maintain their grip. Moss grows in perfect geometric patterns, forming mandalas of green that shift and reform when observed too closely. The chlorophyll here has an intensity that makes the air itself seem verdant, and breathing feels like drinking light.

The population is sparse—shapes glimpsed through translucent walls, shadows that move with too much fluidity, voices that contribute to the endless aquatic chorus. Sometimes a figure appears on a distant terrace, tall and wavering like a reflection in disturbed water, before melting back into the architecture. They leave no footprints in the perpetual damp, and their presence feels less like inhabitation than like a dream the city is having about being populated.

Reality grows soft here. Walls sometimes refuse to remain solid, allowing passage where none should exist. Distances behave strangely—a terrace that appears near requires hours of climbing through spaces that fold back on themselves. Time pools and eddies like the ubiquitous water; conversations can be heard from rooms that existed decades ago, and shadows fall from suns that have not yet risen. The boundary between the city and its inhabitants blurs until it becomes unclear whether the buildings house the people or the people house the buildings.

The sound never stops. Layer upon layer of liquid percussion creates a symphony that would be beautiful if it weren't so relentless. Water striking stone, stone singing in response, the harmonic resonance of chambers filling and emptying in rhythm with something vast and slow—perhaps the mountain's own heartbeat. Voices join this chorus, but whether they belong to the hidden residents or to the city itself remains impossible to determine.

Here, in this place where stone dreams and water speaks, the normal rules of existence bend like light through moisture. The city breathes, the water thinks, and somewhere in the endless wet harmony, something vast and patient continues its work of dissolution and transformation, turning reality itself into something more fluid, more forgiving, more alive.
