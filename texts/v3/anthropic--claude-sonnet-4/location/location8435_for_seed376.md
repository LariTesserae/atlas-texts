The Hollow Terraces sprawl across a gentle depression in the earth, a bowl-shaped valley no more than half a mile across, where an ancient settlement once carved elaborate stone platforms into the hillsides. The terraces rise in perfect geometric steps, their edges sharp and clean as if carved yesterday, though no tool marks remain visible on their surfaces. Each platform stands empty, a vast stone courtyard large enough to hold a cathedral, yet containing nothing but air and the occasional pool of collected rainwater.

Moss creeps across every surface in thick, velvet carpets of emerald and jade. It climbs the terrace walls in cascading sheets, transforms the stone steps into soft green waterfalls, and spreads across the platforms in patterns that seem almost deliberate—spirals and crescents that shift subtly when observed from different angles. Between the moss, delicate ferns unfurl from every crack and crevice, their fronds creating miniature forests in the joints between stones. Trailing vines hang from the upper terraces like curtains, heavy with moisture that beads and drips in a constant, gentle percussion.

The air hangs cool and thick, perpetually damp without being oppressive. Mist rises from the stone surfaces as condensation meets the slightly warmer air, creating ghostly wisps that drift between the levels. Rain comes in brief, soft showers that seem to emerge from nowhere—one moment the air is merely humid, the next it's filled with fine droplets that barely disturb the silence as they patter against stone and leaf.

No birds call from the vegetation. No insects buzz. No small creatures rustle through the undergrowth. The silence is so complete it seems to have weight, broken only by the soft drip of water, the whisper of mist moving through the air, and the nearly inaudible sound of moss growing—a sensation felt more than heard, like the earth itself breathing.

Yet the place pulses with activity of a different kind. The moss patterns shift gradually, rearranging themselves over the course of hours. New pools form on the terraces as if the stone itself is adjusting its surface to catch and hold water. The mist moves in currents that follow no earthly wind, spiraling up from certain terraces and flowing down into others in slow, hypnotic eddies. 

The light comes from above, filtered through layers of perpetual haze that soften but never quite dim the illumination. Everything glows with a subtle luminescence—the moss seems to hold light in its fibers, the wet stone reflects it in ways that feel almost intentional. Shadows are soft-edged and seem to move independently of their sources, creating the impression that the terraces exist in several overlapping versions of themselves simultaneously.

Water collects in the centers of many platforms, forming perfectly circular pools that reflect nothing but sky, even when observers lean directly over them. These pools never overflow, never diminish, and their surfaces remain mirror-still despite the gentle rain that occasionally strikes them. Sometimes shapes move beneath their surfaces—not fish or aquatic life, but geometric forms that pulse and drift like living architecture exploring the boundaries of liquid space.

The settlement's original purpose remains unclear. No buildings crown the terraces, no foundations mark where structures once stood. Instead, each platform feels designed to hold nothing—vast, empty stages waiting for performances that never come, or perhaps have already concluded. The engineering is flawless, the drainage perfect, the proportions harmonious, yet everything suggests a civilization that built not to contain or shelter, but to create spaces for absence itself.

As hours pass in the Hollow Terraces, visitors begin to notice that the emptiness isn't vacant but full—pregnant with possibilities that hover just beyond perception, like words about to be spoken in a language not quite remembered.
