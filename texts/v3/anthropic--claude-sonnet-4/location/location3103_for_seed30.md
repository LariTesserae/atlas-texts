The thermal spring emerges from what was once solid basalt, but the rock here is still learning how to be rock. Steam rises in lazy spirals from pools that shift their borders hourly, the mineral-rich water eating new channels through stone that hasn't quite decided on its final form. The basalt weeps constantly—not from rain, but from the mountain's own slow exhalation, condensation beading on surfaces that glisten like fresh wounds in the dim, sulfurous light.

This is a small world, perhaps fifty paces across at its widest, cupped in a shallow depression where the mountainside buckles slightly. The elevation gives it a cathedral's hush, yet the place thrums with sound—the wet pop and hiss of thermal vents, the liquid murmur of water finding new paths, and beneath it all, a subsonic humming that might be the mountain's pulse or something stranger.

Sparse tufts of grass emerge between the rocks, their roots drinking from the warm, mineral-heavy moisture that never quite becomes soil. These are pioneer plants, hardy and stunted, their leaves a peculiar blue-green that suggests they've learned to metabolize compounds no earthly grass should touch. Between them, the stone is bare and gleaming, carved into shallow channels by water that carries more than H2O—traces of elements that make the pools glow faintly in the gathering dusk.

The fauna here defies easy categorization. Creatures dart between the thermal pools—not quite fish, not quite amphibians, their translucent bodies pulsing with bioluminescent organs that respond to the mineral content of the water. They sing, these pool-dwellers, their voices creating the melodic overtones that weave through the spring's constant murmur. Above them, moths with wings like sheets of mica spiral in the steam, their flight patterns traced by hair-thin streams of light that dissipate seconds after they pass.

But it's the builders that make this place strange beyond its geology. Scattered throughout the spring system are structures—if that's the right word—that seem grown rather than constructed. Delicate spires of what might be coral or crystallized minerals rise from the pool edges, honeycombed with chambers that pulse gently with their own light. They're clearly artificial, yet they breathe. Inside the chambers, glimpses of movement: not the pool creatures, but something else entirely. Something that works.

The technology here operates on principles that bend around conventional physics. The structures appear to be harvesting something from the intersection of thermal energy, mineral content, and the mountain's slow geological becoming. Occasionally, one of the spires will emit a sound like singing crystal, and all the pool creatures will freeze mid-motion, their bodies brightening until the note fades. Then they resume their eternal dance, perhaps slightly changed.

As night deepens, the pools' edges blur and reform. The water level rises and falls in rhythms that have nothing to do with evaporation. The mountain is still making itself here, still deciding what shape to hold, and the thermal springs are both symptom and catalyst of that becoming. The builders' structures adapt in real-time, their coral-crystal walls flowing like slow liquid to accommodate each shift in the water's path.

The air tastes of copper and ozone, with an underlying sweetness that might be sulfur transformed by processes not yet named in any textbook. Each breath carries moisture that seems to know things—the lungs learn, almost against their will, the mountain's deepest rhythms. The sound builds as the night progresses: thermal pops crescendoing into percussion, creature-song weaving through it, the structures adding their crystalline harmonies until the small space rings like a bell cast in living stone.

This is a place still writing itself into existence, where the boundary between natural and artificial has dissolved in the warm, impossible water. The sparse vegetation watches. The mountain dreams. And something builds, endlessly builds, structures that grow like thoughts made manifest in the steaming intersection of stone and possibility.
