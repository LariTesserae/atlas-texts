The Nursery Fields stretch beyond the curved horizon, a vast bowl of amber dust punctuated by crystalline formations that pulse with their own faint luminescence. The air shimmers with heat that seems to rise not from any visible sun, but from the ground itself—from deep thermal vents that crack the surface in precise hexagonal patterns, each no wider than a hand span. These geometric fissures glow with a soft orange light that barely penetrates the perpetual twilight overhead.

Sparse clusters of what might charitably be called vegetation dot the landscape—tall, segmented structures that resemble enormous tuning forks made of translucent chitin. They stand motionless, fifteen to twenty feet high, their branching tips collecting something from the dim air. Closer inspection reveals they are not plants at all, but the discarded molting shells of creatures long departed, now serving as inadvertent gathering points for the strange atmospheric condensates that substitute for moisture here.

The ground beneath your feet crunches with a sound like walking on compressed sand, though the material is something else entirely—microscopic geometric shapes, each one a perfect dodecahedron of some mineral that hasn't finished deciding what it wants to become. The landscape bears the raw, unfinished quality of a world still crystallizing from its component elements, as if you've arrived during the slow-motion moment of a planet's birth.

But you are not alone here.

They move through the amber dust in rivers—millions upon millions of creatures no larger than your thumb, each one a writhing knot of silver filaments that seems to exist partially outside normal space. When they cluster together, reality bends around them like heated glass. The swarms flow in perfect silence between the standing chitin monuments, following paths that curve and weave according to some vast mathematical principle. Occasionally, a stream of them will pause, pool into a temporary lake of living mercury, then disperse again without apparent cause.

The silence is profound but not complete. If you listen carefully, you can hear the faint harmonic resonance of the thermal vents—a deep, barely perceptible humming that suggests vast machinery working far below. Sometimes there's a crystalline chime when the wind (what little there is) catches the edge of a chitin structure. But mostly, there is only the soft whisper of millions of tiny bodies moving through dust that sparkles with its own inner geometry.

In the distance, barely visible through the dim haze, stand the artifacts of whoever came before—or whoever is still coming. Simple structures, if structures they are: perfect spheres of some dark material that seem to absorb light rather than reflect it, scattered across the plain with no discernible pattern. Some hover a few feet above the surface. Others have sunk partially into the crystallizing ground, as if the planet is slowly digesting them.

Near one of these spheres, something extraordinary becomes apparent. The swarms of silver creatures flow toward it, then around it, but in the space immediately surrounding the sphere, they move differently—their filaments stretch and contract in slow, rhythmic pulses, as if the dark artifact is teaching them a new way to exist. The effect extends perhaps a hundred feet from each sphere, creating islands of synchronized motion in an ocean of random flowing.

The heat presses against you like a living thing, radiating up from below and seeming to emanate from the very air itself. Your instruments, if you have them, would register impossible readings—temperatures that should melt the crystalline formations, atmospheric pressures that should not support the delicate chitin monuments, gravity that seems to shift subtly as the silver swarms pass overhead in their impossible densities.

This is a place where physics is still negotiating with itself, where the fundamental forces haven't quite settled into their permanent relationships. The sparse beauty of it holds you suspended between wonder and unease, as if you've stumbled into the dreams of a world that hasn't yet decided what it wants to wake up as.

The silver swarms continue their eternal dance, the thermal vents breathe their geometric patterns into being, and the dark spheres wait with infinite patience for whatever they were designed to witness or become.
