The Gallery of Echoing Glass stretches no more than thirty paces in any direction, a hidden chamber carved deep within the heart of an ancient crystalline formation. Every surface—floor, walls, ceiling—has been painstakingly crafted from thousands upon thousands of interlocking glass panels, each one hand-cut and fitted with obsessive precision by generations of nameless artisans. The panels vary in thickness from hair-thin membranes to finger-thick slabs, creating a tessellated maze where not a single natural stone remains visible.

Cool air moves sluggishly through this sealed space, carrying no moisture whatsoever. The atmosphere feels desiccated, almost brittle, as if the very air might crack like old parchment. Sparse clusters of pale, thread-like fungi emerge from hairline cracks where panels meet, their ghostly filaments the only living things that dare colonize this geometric wilderness. These growths appear ancient and unchanging, having found their equilibrium centuries ago in the chamber's stable conditions.

The light is overwhelming—a thousand brilliant stars fracturing and multiplying through the prismatic surfaces. Each panel catches and throws illumination in cascading chains of reflection and refraction, creating a blazing constellation that fills every cubic inch of space. The brightness has no single source; it seems to generate itself through the endless interplay of glass meeting glass, surface answering surface in perpetual luminous conversation.

And the sound—the sound is magnificent and terrible. Every footfall, every breath, every microscopic settling of glass against glass triggers avalanches of chiming resonance. The chamber sings constantly in crystalline harmonics, each frequency finding its perfect complement in the precisely tuned dimensions of the space. The reverberations never fade; they layer and build upon themselves, creating a thunderous symphony of pure tone that seems to shake the very foundations of the world above.

Nothing here changes. The fungi neither grow nor wither. The light neither brightens nor dims. The echoes maintain their perfect pitch across decades, perhaps centuries. This is a place locked in crystalline stasis, a monument to the impossible dream of permanence made manifest in glass and geometry and endless, blazing sound.
