The Drowned Hollows stretch beneath a ceiling of black water that hangs impossibly overhead, defying every law you thought you knew. What should be sky is ocean—a vast, dark mass suspended thirty feet above the valley floor, its surface rippling with unfelt currents. Schools of pale fish drift through this inverted sea, their movements casting fleeting shadows on the boggy ground below. Sometimes a droplet falls, but slowly, as if gravity itself moves through thick honey.

The terrain sprawls across miles of waterlogged lowlands, a labyrinth of shallow pools connected by raised ridges of fresh-turned earth. The ground squelches underfoot—black mud shot through with veins of clay that gleam like wet bone. Strange geometric patterns emerge in the muck: perfect spirals, hexagonal depressions, lines that run ruler-straight for hundreds of yards before vanishing. These formations appear overnight, as if pressed by invisible tools, their edges sharp enough to suggest they emerged only hours ago.

Water seeps everywhere. It bubbles up through the soil in clear springs that taste of minerals you cannot name. It collects in every depression, pooling in perfectly circular ponds that reflect not the water-sky above, but glimpses of other places—fragments of sunlit meadows, burning deserts, star-filled voids. The pools crowd together so densely that dry ground exists only as narrow causeways between their mirrored surfaces. Each step requires careful navigation.

Vegetation clings to existence here in sparse, deliberate clusters. Tall marsh grasses rise from the pools in perfect rings, their seed-heads clicking softly against each other in breezes that seem to blow from several directions at once. Stunted willows dot the ridges, their bark scored with spiraling cuts that weep amber sap. The trees grow in impossible formations—some with roots that arch high above their canopies, others whose trunks twist into perfect helixes before straightening again. Between them, beds of moss spread in mathematical patterns: fractals that repeat at different scales, carpets of green that pulse with their own faint luminescence.

The air hangs cool and thick, heavy with the scent of wet earth and something else—ozone, perhaps, or the smell that comes before lightning. Mist drifts in knee-high layers, never quite obscuring the view but softening every edge, making distances uncertain. The temperature rarely varies, holding steady in that narrow band between comfort and chill that makes you pull your sleeves down but never quite warm enough to shed them.

Scattered across this drowned landscape, structures emerge from the mud like half-remembered dreams. A stone bridge spans a pool barely three feet across, its arches elaborately carved but leading nowhere—its far end simply stops in mid-air. Fragments of walls rise at odd angles, their fitted stones still perfectly mortared despite having no foundations. A watchtower stands complete but for its missing top half, as if cleanly sliced away by some enormous blade. These ruins show no signs of age or weathering; they appear freshly abandoned, tools still scattered at their bases, though the tools themselves defy identification—curved implements of carved wood and polished stone whose purposes remain opaque.

The silence here has weight. Sounds, when they come, seem muffled and distant even when close at hand. The soft plip of water dropping from the sky-sea above. The whisper of grass-heads brushing together. Occasionally, a low harmonic tone emerges from somewhere deep underground, felt more than heard, causing ripples to spread across every pool simultaneously. These tones follow no pattern you can discern, sometimes hours apart, sometimes in rapid succession like a conversation in a language of pure resonance.

Life moves here, but sparsely, as if the world can only support so much at once. Large shapes drift through the overhead water—things with too many fins, or bodies that seem to flow like liquid themselves. They never descend. On the ground, creatures appear and vanish with dreamlike inconsistency: a heron with silver feathers that dissolve in sunlight that isn't there, six-legged deer that walk on water and leave no prints, schools of flying fish that swim through the air in formation before disappearing into the earth itself as if it were just another medium to navigate.

Time moves strangely in the Drowned Hollows. The mysterious formations in the mud grow more elaborate each day, following some vast, patient geometry. The ruins shift position when unwatched—a bridge that faced north in the morning may point east by evening. The pools slowly deepen, or perhaps the land around them slowly sinks. Changes happen on the scale of seasons compressed into heartbeats, or heartbeats stretched into geological ages.

In the deepest silence, when even the water-drops pause in their falling, you can hear it: a sound like vast machinery turning far below, all gears and slow reciprocations, as if the world itself were being continuously assembled by hands you cannot see, according to plans you could never understand. The Hollows are not just a place but a process—reality in the act of becoming something else, something that follows rules not quite aligned with the ones you brought with you.

And overhead, that impossible sea continues its dark witness, holding fish and secrets in equal measure, a reminder that in this place, the very notion of up and down, of water and air, of what should be and what is, must be constantly renegotiated with each careful step through the yielding earth.
