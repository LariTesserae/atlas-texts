In the perpetual dusk of the canopy city, where twilight never quite surrenders to full dark, the Woven rises like a living cathedral from the highland bog. Here, at the edge of an ancient crater lake now mostly claimed by sphagnum and sedge, the tree-builders have grown their settlement rather than built it.

The foundation is **Heartwood** - a single massive trunk, perhaps once a giant fig or strangler vine, now hollowed and carved into spiraling chambers. Its living walls weep a constant film of moisture that collects in carved channels, feeding the structure's circulatory system. The wood itself pulses faintly with sap-flow, and in the dim light, bioluminescent fungi trace the grain in hair-thin lines of blue-green fire.

From this central spine, the city spreads through **Bridgewood** - living branches trained and grafted into walkways, platforms, and dwelling-hollows. The growth is deliberate but organic, following the mathematical curves that plants know instinctively. Rooms bulge like fruit from the main branches, their walls thin enough that occupants can hear the tree's heartbeat, the slow percussion of xylem rising and phloem falling.

The air hangs thick with moisture and the green breath of photosynthesis. Condensation beads on every surface, dripping in an endless percussion that masks footfalls and conversation alike. The sound is of water finding its way - drop by drop, trickle by stream - down bark-gutters and through root-drains back to the bog below.

**The Understory** spreads beneath the elevated city like a carpet of eyes. Here, in the permanent half-light, creatures have evolved for this specific niche of perpetual dusk. Pale salamanders with translucent skin cluster around the tree's weeping roots. Their bodies glow faintly, and they pulse in slow unison - a breathing constellation in the moss. Overhead, something like flying squirrels but with membranous wings that trail luminous spores glide between the platform-branches, their soft chittering the only sound that rises above the constant drip.

The bog itself exhales methane in lazy bubbles that sometimes ignite in brief, cold flames - will-o'-wisps that dance between the sedge clumps before winking out. The water table sits just beneath a skin of moss so thick it could support a person's weight, though breaking through would mean sinking into water black as old tea, sweet with tannins and decay.

**Tools and Craft** here are grown rather than forged. The tree-builders cultivate specialized branches - some hard as bone for cutting edges, others hollow for carrying water, still others that secrete adhesive resins. They shape living wood with patient pressure and careful pruning, crafting implements that remain connected to the parent plant, drawing strength from roots sunk deep into the bog's rich decay.

The city's population moves like sap - slow, deliberate, following pathways worn smooth by generations of passage. They tend their tree as gardeners and lovers, knowing which branches need pruning, which chambers require new drainage, where the bark has grown too thick and must be carefully scored to let the building breathe.

As night deepens - though true darkness never comes - the bioluminescence brightens. The salamanders below pulse faster, the spore-trails of the gliding creatures grow more vivid, and the Heartwood's fungi trace increasingly complex patterns along its grain. The tree dreams, and its dreams are visible in the slow dance of living light that plays across bark and leaf, branch and root, in this small pocket of the world where the boundary between organism and architecture dissolved long ago.
