The Hollows stretch beneath the world's skin like a fever dream of geology still learning to breathe. Here, in chambers that could swallow small cities, the earth hasn't quite decided what it wants to become. Limestone walls weep constantly—not the steady drip of ancient caves, but an irregular percussion as moisture beads, gathers, and falls in fat drops that echo like scattered applause across the vastness. The air itself seems to exhale humidity in waves, thick and warm as blood, carrying the green scent of things growing too fast in the perpetual twilight.

Vines cascade from ceiling fissures in impossible profusion, their leaves broad as dinner plates and luminescent with trapped moisture. They spiral down in baroque tangles, some dangling freely for hundreds of feet before pooling on the uneven floor in verdant heaps. Ferns unfurl from every crack and crevice with prehistoric enthusiasm, their fronds catching what little light filters down through collapsed sections of the ceiling—pale shafts that reveal floating galaxies of spores and steam.

The sound is overwhelming, a living symphony that builds from whisper to roar without warning. Not just the constant drip-drop-splash of water finding its level, but the deep groaning of the earth still settling into its bones. Somewhere in the distance, mechanical rhythms pulse—the distant thrum of pumps or generators, their industrial heartbeat threading through the organic chaos. And over it all, the creatures.

They are everywhere and nowhere, a chorus that defies cataloging. Chittering swarms move through the vegetation like visible sound, while larger things crash through the undergrowth with casual destruction. Wings beat overhead—not birds, but something else, their calls bouncing off stone in alien harmonics. The darkness conceals most of them, but their presence presses against the air like a living weight. Eyes gleam from unexpected angles, too many, too large, too knowing.

Here and there, the detritus of human ambition rusts in the humid embrace. A section of cast-iron pipe, green with oxidation, disappears into a wall of moss. Concrete steps lead nowhere, their edges rounded by persistent moisture and the passage of uncountable small feet. A single electric bulb, somehow still functional, casts a weak amber circle that only emphasizes the darkness beyond its reach. These fragments suggest some abandoned industrial project—perhaps mining or construction—but the earth has been busy reclaiming them, wrapping them in roots and lichen until they seem more grown than built.

The floor rises and falls in gentle swells, pooled with mirror-dark water that reflects nothing. The pools connect through narrow channels carved by the restless movement of moisture always seeking lower ground. Sometimes the water moves against gravity, defying expectation, drawn upward by forces that might be magnetic, or thermal, or something else entirely.

Steam rises in lazy spirals, carrying with it pheromones and chemical signals that make the skin tingle and the mind drift. The warmth is oppressive but not unpleasant, like being held in the palm of something vast and patient. Everything here exists in a state of becoming—the cave system still expanding as water works its slow magic on soluble stone, the vegetation still colonizing new territories each day, the creatures still adapting to chambers that didn't exist when they were born.

In the furthest reaches, where the mechanical sounds grow stronger, strange lights flicker—not electric, but something more organic, like bioluminescence scaled up beyond reason. They pulse in patterns that suggest communication, or perhaps digestion, casting brief illumination on walls that gleam with moisture and move with the passage of things too large to comprehend fully.

This is a place caught between states—not quite cave, not quite surface, not quite natural, not quite tame. It exists in the space where the earth's dreams begin to solidify, where industry's bones become nurseries for impossible life, where sound itself takes on weight and substance. The Hollows remember being nothing, and they're still deciding what to become.
