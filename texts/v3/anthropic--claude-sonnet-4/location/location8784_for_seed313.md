Deep within the hollowed bones of an ancient titan, a pocket city thrives in defiance of the wasteland above. The creature died eons ago in the great salt flats, its massive ribcage creating a cathedral of yellowed bone that rises forty feet overhead. Between these arched supports, the inhabitants have woven their settlement from living vines that drink not water, but the rich mineral oils that seep eternally from the bone marrow above.

The vegetation here grows in impossible abundance despite the waterless world—thick, rubbery vines with leaves like leather pouches that store concentrated nutrients. Curtains of flowering creepers hang between the ribs, their blooms opening and closing in synchronized waves that create constant rustling whispers. The plants have evolved to metabolize the oily secretions that drip steadily from the bone ceiling, creating a closed ecosystem that hums with strange vitality. Trumpet-shaped flowers catch the precious drops and funnel them to root systems that snake along the curved walls in complex networks.

Heat radiates constantly from the bone itself—not the dry heat of the salt desert above, but a thick, organic warmth that makes the air shimmer. The temperature never varies, locked in a perpetual fever-dream climate that keeps the settlement's unique biology in constant bloom. Steam rises from pools of amber-colored marrow oil that collect in the deepest hollows, creating a greenhouse effect that nurtures the impossible garden-city.

The settlement follows the natural architecture of the ribcage, with walkways and platforms suspended between the bone struts by braided vines. Dwellings are grown rather than built—hollow gourds cultivated to human size, their walls translucent and warm to the touch. The inhabitants have learned to guide the growth of support vines, creating bridges and towers that sway gently but hold firm. Everything organic, everything alive, pulsing with the slow metabolism of the bone-city.

Sound defines this place more than sight. The constant drip-splash of marrow oil creates a polyrhythmic percussion that echoes off the curved bone walls. The rustling of countless leaves mingles with the creaking of vine-structures under their own weight. But loudest of all is the breathing of the place itself—for the bone cavity acts as a vast resonating chamber, amplifying every footstep, every conversation, every sigh into a continuous murmur of human presence. The inhabitants have learned to speak softly, knowing their words will be caught and multiplied by the architecture.

A few creatures share this space: phosphorescent beetles that nest in the marrow pools, their green glow providing the only artificial light. Occasionally, a family of rat-like mammals with oil-slick fur can be glimpsed darting between the vines, feeding on fallen flower petals. They move with nervous energy, adding quick scurrying sounds to the audio tapestry.

The settlement itself pulses with manic activity. Markets shift location hourly as the vine-platforms sway and reconfigure themselves. Vendors selling concentrated flower nectar call out from swaying perches, their voices echoing in overlapping waves. Children race along the flexible walkways, their footsteps drumming against hollow gourds. The whole community exists in constant, gentle motion—nothing here stays still, nothing here is permanent except the great bone frame that holds it all.

Above, through gaps in the rib-ceiling, the harsh white glare of the salt desert is visible, a reminder of the hostile world beyond. But here, in this breathing cave of bone and vine, life has found a way to be lush, loud, and gloriously chaotic—a fever dream of growth sustained by the eternal weeping of an ancient giant's bones.
