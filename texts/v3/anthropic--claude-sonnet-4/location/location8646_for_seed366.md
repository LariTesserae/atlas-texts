The Foundling Terraces rise from the desert floor in a series of narrow, knife-edge plateaus, each no wider than a city block but stretching for perhaps half a mile in crystalline precision. These formations are young—so recently thrust from the earth that their edges still gleam with surgical sharpness, unmarked by wind or weather. The rock itself is a pale, almost translucent stone that radiates heat like banked coals, creating shimmering curtains of air that dance silently above each terrace.

Moisture seeps from hidden springs within the stone itself, beading on the surface in perfect droplets that never quite evaporate in the dry, scorching air. This condensation follows invisible channels carved into the rock with mathematical precision—not natural waterways, but deliberate grooves that guide each drop along predetermined paths. The water moves without sound, creating a network of hair-thin streams that vanish into carefully drilled holes, only to emerge again on lower terraces in an endless, whispered circulation.

No plants grow here. No fungi cling to the moisture-slicked stone. Yet the terraces teem with something that defies easy categorization. Crystalline formations—not quite mineral, not quite alive—emerge from the rock in sparse, geometric clusters. They pulse with their own inner light, casting no shadows but creating pools of cooler air where they grow. Between them, vast stretches of empty stone stretch unbroken, polished to mirror smoothness by unknown hands.

The settlement spreads across three adjacent terraces, connected by bridges of the same pale stone that seem grown rather than built. Simple dwellings carved directly into the rock face line the edges, their doorways perfect circles that breathe with the terraces' own rhythm. Tools of bone and worked stone lie scattered in orderly arrangements—awls, scrapers, and grinding stones that show signs of use but no wear, as if time itself moves differently here.

The inhabitants are everywhere and nowhere. Shadows that move independently of their casters slide between the crystalline growths. Shapes that might be human flicker at the edge of vision, always just beyond clear sight. They cast no reflections in the polished stone, make no footprints in the thin dust that accumulates in corners. Yet their presence presses against the air itself—a weight of attention, of countless watching eyes. The very stones seem to lean inward when they pass, as if the terraces themselves are aware of their movements.

The silence is profound. No wind disturbs this high place. The mysterious streams make no sound as they flow. Even footsteps—should any solid being walk here—would be absorbed by stone that seems hungry for vibration. Yet within this silence lives a deeper music: the almost-audible hum of the crystalline growths, the whispered circulation of water through stone veins, the patient breathing of rock that has learned to live.

Heat rises in visible waves, but the temperature never fluctuates. Day and night bring no change. The light—neither sun nor moon but something that simply *is*—illuminates everything with the same steady glow, casting the world in permanent clarity. Seasons do not exist here. Weather is a foreign concept. This is a place locked in its own eternal moment, where the strange inhabitants tend their crystalline gardens with tools that will never break, in silence that will never lift, on stone that will never age.
