The Fulgurite Peaks rise from a vast plateau of crystallized lightning, their jagged spires reaching toward a perpetually overcast sky that rumbles with distant electrical storms. This is a world born from the marriage of raw elemental fury and industrial hunger—a place where the very mountains are composed of glass tubes and branching formations created when massive lightning strikes fused the mineral-rich earth into towering, translucent sculptures.

The air here is thin and cool, carrying the sharp ozone scent of electrical discharge and the metallic tang of ionized particles. What little light filters through the heavy cloud cover takes on an eerie blue-white quality as it refracts through the glass peaks, creating prismatic rainbows that dance across the landscape in fleeting moments before being swallowed again by shadow.

Scattered across the plateau between these fulgurite mountains stand massive Tesla coils and conductivity arrays—industrial monuments to humanity's attempt to harvest the raw power that created this place. The installations are sparse but imposing: copper frameworks spiraling hundreds of feet into the air, crowned with metal spheres that hum with barely contained energy. Steam vents between the structures release pressurized gases with sharp hissing sounds, while electromagnetic fields cause loose metal debris to levitate and spin in slow, hypnotic orbits.

The constant electrical activity has created an ecosystem unlike anywhere else. Creatures have evolved to feed on pure electrical energy—jellyfish-like beings that drift through the air, their translucent bodies pulsing with bioluminescent patterns as they absorb charge from the atmosphere. Serpentine forms slither along the glass surfaces of the peaks, their scales crackling with static discharge. In the shadows between installations, metallic insects with copper-wire wings create droning symphonies as they vibrate in harmonic frequencies with the ambient electromagnetic fields.

The soundscape is overwhelming: the deep, resonant hum of the Tesla coils building toward discharge, punctuated by the sharp crack-boom of lightning strikes hitting the peaks. Electromagnetic interference creates an underlying static that rises and falls like electronic breathing, while the creatures contribute their own electric chorus—chittering, buzzing, and harmonic tones that seem to respond to the fluctuations in the electrical field.

Everything here exists in a state of constant flux. The glass peaks continue to grow with each lightning strike, their formations becoming more complex and beautiful. The industrial equipment sparks and surges unpredictably, sometimes overloading in spectacular displays that light up the entire plateau. Steam geysers erupt without warning from cracks in the crystallized ground, and the electromagnetic fields shift and dance, causing the levitating debris to suddenly drop or shoot skyward.

The very air seems alive with potential energy, creating an atmosphere where the boundary between the organic and inorganic blurs. This is a landscape that defies conventional categorization—neither fully natural nor entirely artificial, but something new and strange that has emerged from the collision of elemental forces and human ambition.
