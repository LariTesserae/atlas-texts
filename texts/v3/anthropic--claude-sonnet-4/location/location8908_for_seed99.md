The Verdant Hollows stretch across a broad basin where ancient volcanic flows once pooled and cooled, leaving behind a gently undulating terrain of dark, porous stone that drinks in moisture like a sponge. Here, in this low-lying expanse that extends beyond the horizon in all directions, plants have claimed absolute dominion over the world.

The foundation rock, still sharp-edged from its relatively recent formation, creates a honeycomb of natural channels and shallow depressions. Into these carved networks, morning mists settle and linger, condensing on the countless surfaces of leaves that form living walls, floors, and canopies. The moisture arrives not as dramatic rainfall, but as gentle exhalations from the earth itself—steam that rises from hidden thermal vents beneath the plant-mass, creating pockets of humidity that sustain this green empire.

What might have been separate plants elsewhere have here grown into a single, continuous organism-architecture. Massive vine-trunks, thick as ancient trees, weave horizontal bridges between towering succulent spires that rise like living pillars from the basalt flows. Sheet-moss carpets span between rock formations, creating suspended floors that give slightly underfoot, while curtains of aerial roots hang like living beadwork, filtering the intense light that beats down from above.

The heat here is substantial but not oppressive, maintained by the thermal activity below and the greenhouse effect of this dense plant-mass. The living architecture creates its own climate zones—cooler grottos where broad leaves overlap into natural pavilions, steamy chambers where hot springs bubble up through root systems, and bright terraces where flowering mats spread like colorful rugs across the volcanic shelves.

Light finds countless ways through this verdant maze, creating a constantly shifting pattern of emerald and gold. The plant-mass is so dense that walking means following tunnels and chambers formed by growth patterns, climbing vine-stairs that spiral around mineral chimneys, or traversing the spongy bridges of interwoven branches that span the broader gaps between rock formations.

The silence here feels almost sacred, broken only by the soft drip of condensation, the whispered rustle of leaves responding to thermal currents, and the occasional gentle hiss of steam emerging from the earth. No animal voices interrupt this vegetable peace—this is a world where plants have evolved to fill every niche, some developing thick, leather-like protective sheaths, others growing translucent to maximize light capture in the deeper chambers.

Scattered throughout this living landscape are the remnants of crude stone tools—simple scrapers and cutting implements left by some long-departed people who perhaps once harvested the endless bounty of edible shoots, medicinal leaves, and fibrous materials that this place provides. These artifacts lie where they were dropped, slowly being incorporated into root systems and moss-beds, suggesting visitors rather than inhabitants, people who came to gather and departed, leaving the plant-world to continue its patient, inexorable expansion.

The thermal vents that feed moisture into this system create subtle variations in the growth patterns—here a grove of towering succulent columns reaches toward the bright sky, there a dense thicket of broad-leafed shrubs creates a cool, dim understory. The recent volcanic origins mean that new vents occasionally open, sending up fresh columns of steam that nurture pioneer plants in previously barren patches, slowly extending the green dominion across the dark stone expanses.

This is a place of profound vegetable intelligence, where the distinction between individual organisms has dissolved into a single, vast, living system that breathes moisture and exhales oxygen, that captures light and transforms it into an ever-expanding architecture of leaf and stem and root, growing slowly but inexorably across the young volcanic landscape like a green dream made manifest.
