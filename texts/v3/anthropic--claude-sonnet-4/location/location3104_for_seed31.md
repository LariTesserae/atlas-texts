The highland city of Kethara spreads across a series of stepped plateaus, its stone foundations carved directly from the young volcanic rock that thrust these heights skyward not so long ago in geological time. Sharp-edged mesas and knife-thin ridges define the horizon, their surfaces still bearing the crisp geometry of recent upheaval. Between these dramatic formations, broad terraces catch and hold the constant moisture that rises from the valleys far below.

Water is everywhere here, though rarely seen flowing. It seeps perpetually through the porous volcanic stone, emerging as steady weeping from cliff faces and pooling in the countless shallow basins that pock the terraces. The air itself feels thick with moisture – not oppressive, but present, like breathing through silk. Morning mists cling to the carved channels between districts, and by midday they have lifted into a soft haze that blurs the distant peaks into watercolor suggestions.

The city's builders worked with simple tools – bronze chisels, stone hammers, wooden levers – yet achieved something magnificent through patience and an understanding of the rock's natural fracture lines. Wide plazas stretch between clusters of low, rectangular buildings, all hewn from the same dark volcanic stone that forms the plateaus themselves. The structures seem to have grown from the earth rather than been imposed upon it, their straight lines echoing the geological strata visible in every exposed cliff face.

Sparse groves of hardy trees dot the urban landscape – gnarled specimens with waxy leaves that shed water efficiently, their roots finding purchase in cracks between stones. Moss and small ferns colonize the perpetually damp walls, creating hanging gardens that require no tending. The vegetation has a purposeful sparseness, each plant claiming its territory in the mineral-rich but challenging environment.

The silence here is profound. No birds call from the scattered trees, no insects hum in the moist air. Even human voices seem muted by the thick atmosphere and the sound-absorbing qualities of the porous stone. Water makes the only consistent noise – the gentle plink of droplets falling into stone basins, the whisper of moisture condensing on leaves, the barely audible seep and trickle through hidden channels in the rock.

The warm air moves in predictable patterns, rising from the heated stone during the day and settling into the carved channels at night, but these currents follow ancient rhythms unchanged since the city's founding. The moisture content never varies significantly; the temperature holds steady in its gentle warmth. Even the patterns of shadow and light remain constant as the sun traces the same path between the geometric peaks, creating a timeless quality that makes hours feel like minutes, years like seasons.

From the highest terraces, the view extends across an entire region of similar mesas and plateaus, some bearing their own modest settlements, others standing empty and waiting. The landscape reveals its youth in every vista – no softened hills or rounded valleys, but an endless succession of sharp edges and clean breaks where the earth still remembers its violent birth. The city exists in this moment of geological adolescence, neither ancient nor new, carved into stone that has not yet learned to be gentle.
