The Singing Peaks stretch across a vast mountainous realm where ancient stone spires rise like weathered titans from mist-shrouded valleys. These are mountains so old they have forgotten their youth—their peaks worn smooth by eons of wind, their faces carved into impossible geometries by time itself. What were once jagged summits now stand as rounded monuments, their surfaces polished to an almost glassy sheen that catches and scatters the amber light of perpetual afternoon.

Between these colossal pillars, the air itself seems alive with moisture that never quite becomes rain. Condensation beads constantly on every surface, creating temporary streams that appear and vanish like silver threads in the warming air. The humidity rises from hidden thermal vents deep within the ancient stone, carrying with it the metallic tang of minerals and the green sweetness of moss. Pockets of water collect in the countless bowl-shaped depressions worn into the rock faces—natural cisterns that overflow and spill in delicate cascades, only to evaporate halfway down and reform as mist.

Despite the scattered moisture, lush vegetation clings to every conceivable foothold. Primitive ferns unfurl from cracks in the stone like green fountains, their fronds reaching spans longer than a person is tall. Ancient cycads cluster in the thermal pockets, their prehistoric forms unchanged since the world was young. Moss carpets stretch across entire cliff faces in patterns that shift from emerald to chartreuse to deep forest green, creating living tapestries that pulse with their own rhythm as moisture levels rise and fall.

The few structures that dot this landscape are humble and half-forgotten—stone circles that might have been ritual sites or simple shelters, their original purpose lost to time. Occasionally, a lone tower of fitted stones emerges from a moss-covered ridge, so weathered it's impossible to tell whether it's natural formation or human construction. Tools of bone and polished stone lie scattered in shallow caves, alongside pottery so crude it seems to predate civilization itself.

But it is the sound that defines this place above all else. The Singing Peaks earn their name from the constant, overwhelming chorus that rises from their stone throats. As wind moves through the countless fissures, holes, and caverns worn into the ancient rock by eons of erosion, it creates a symphony of impossible complexity. Deep bass notes that vibrate in your chest mingle with piercing harmonics that dance at the edge of hearing. The sound changes constantly—thunderous one moment, ethereal the next—as thermal currents shift the air through different chambers and passages.

The acoustic phenomena create zones where sound behaves strangely. In some valleys, whispers carry for miles while shouts are swallowed completely. Certain peaks seem to sing in harmony with themselves, their multiple voices creating chord structures that feel almost intentional, as if the mountains themselves were composing. The moisture in the air serves as a conductor, amplifying some frequencies while dampening others, so that the very humidity becomes part of the instrument.

Wildlife is sparse but deeply adapted to this sonic environment. Pale lizards with enlarged inner ears navigate by echolocation, their own clicks and chirps lost in the mountain's greater song. Occasionally, a flock of birds passes overhead—silent, their communication entirely visual, having evolved beyond the need for calls in a world where sound belongs to the stones themselves.

The few humans who venture here move with deliberate care, understanding that this is not a place for hasty action. They wear simple leather and fiber garments, carry tools of bone and obsidian, and have learned to read the mountains' moods by the shift in their endless song. Some say the peaks are alive, others that they're the instrument of some sleeping god. All agree that the sound is slowly changing them, working into their bones and blood until they too become part of the ancient chorus.

As day cycles into evening and back to perpetual afternoon, the thermal currents shift, the condensation patterns change, and the great song continues its endless, chaotic variations—sometimes building to crescendos that shake the very air, sometimes falling to haunting lullabies that seem to call from the heart of the world itself.
