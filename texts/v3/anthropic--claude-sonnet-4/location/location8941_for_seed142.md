The Weeping Plateau of Veth-Korim stretches across the highland steppes like a vast, ancient wound that has learned to garden itself. Here, at elevation where the air grows thin and cool, reality has settled into something both impossibly stable and quietly impossible.

The plateau itself is a testament to deep time—worn smooth by eons until its edges curve like the rim of a vast, shallow bowl. Yet this is no barren moonscape. The stone here weeps. Not metaphorically, but literally: the dark granite and weathered schist surfaces glisten perpetually with moisture that condenses from air that should be too thin and cool to hold such humidity. Droplets gather in microscopic pores and ancient stress fractures, running in hair-thin rivulets that trace the same paths they've followed for millennia, carving barely perceptible channels that will deepen over geological ages.

Between the scattered outcroppings of weeping stone, the vegetation grows in careful abundance—not the dense tangle of a lowland marsh, but a precise, almost mathematical arrangement of grasses, sedges, and broad-leafed succulents that seem to know exactly where to place themselves. The plants cluster around the stone formations, their roots threading into the network of perpetually moist cracks. Moss carpets emerge in perfect circles around the weeping points, their boundaries sharp as if drawn with a compass. Strange ferns unfurl in spirals, their fronds catching the condensation and channeling it down their stems in helical streams that pool in natural basins worn smooth as ceramic.

The city—if it can be called that—exists in the spaces between. Veth-Korim was built not upon the plateau but through it, structures that phase in and out of visible reality like architectural mirages. The buildings appear as translucent geometries when viewed directly, but become solid and detailed in peripheral vision. They follow the ancient drainage patterns of the weeping stones, their foundations somehow integrated with the very physics that keeps water condensing from dry air. Towers spiral upward in impossible helixes, their surfaces reflecting light that comes from no visible source, casting illumination across the plateau that makes every dewdrop sparkle like a fragment of crystal.

The technology here transcends the merely advanced—it has become environmental. Street-lights that exist as pure probability distributions, casting photons that sometimes arrive before they were emitted. Transport tubes that move matter by convincing space to rearrange itself. The infrastructure doesn't fight the ancient landscape but converges with it, as if the weeping stones were always meant to be the foundation for impossibilities.

Wildlife moves through this space with the patience of deep time. Herds of something resembling elk, but with too many legs and eyes that reflect starlight even in day, graze on the mathematical grass arrangements. They appear and disappear not by moving, but by deciding whether to exist in any given moment. Birds that might be cranes trace slow spirals overhead, their flight paths forming mandala patterns that persist in the air as faint contrails long after they've passed. In the moisture-rich pockets around the weeping stones, colonies of crystalline insects build hives that sing in frequencies that shouldn't propagate in this thin air, yet somehow harmonize with the barely audible whisper of water condensing on stone.

The sound of this place is the sound of deep listening—the nearly inaudible percussion of droplets striking stone in rhythms that never quite repeat but never quite change. Occasionally, a note rises from the crystal hives or the impossible architecture, but these sounds feel less like interruptions than like the plateau briefly remembering it has a voice. The wind here moves differently, flowing around the probability-buildings in patterns that sometimes precede the structures' visible manifestation.

Time itself has settled here into something stable beyond stasis. The condensation continues its endless cycle, but each droplet follows the exact path of countless predecessors. The city exists in a state of perpetual construction and perpetual completion, its buildings finishing themselves in recursive loops that never progress and never fail. The wildlife follows migration routes through dimensions that fold back on themselves. Even the light seems to cycle through the same eternal afternoon, bright enough to make every surface gleam, steady enough that shadows never shift.

This is a landscape where the impossible has become mundane through the simple act of lasting long enough. The weeping stones have been crying their perfectly regular tears since before continents learned their current shapes. The city has been almost-visible for ages beyond counting. And everything continues, will always continue, in precisely this configuration—ancient beyond measure, impossibly advanced, and utterly, perfectly still.
