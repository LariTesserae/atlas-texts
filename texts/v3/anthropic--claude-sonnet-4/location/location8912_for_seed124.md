The Ascending Gardens of Khartabad rise from the valley floor in seven terraced tiers, each platform carved into the young granite faces of what were once violent volcanic plugs. Steam seeps constantly from the rock itself—not from any visible source, but as if the stone remembers its molten birth and exhales accordingly. The city spreads across these shelves like a vast amphitheater facing the morning sun, its white-washed buildings climbing toward peaks that still show the sharp, unweathered edges of recent geological violence.

Water is everywhere, though no rivers flow here. Instead, the very air condenses against the cooling rock faces, creating a perpetual mist that gathers into rivulets, pools into catching basins, and feeds the elaborate aqueduct system that threads through every quarter. The citizens have learned to read the rhythm of this atmospheric harvest—how the pre-dawn hours bring the heaviest condensation, how certain walls weep more generously than others, how the interplay of hot stone and cool air creates pockets of plenty and zones of scarcity.

The vegetation responds to this marriage of heat and moisture with an almost violent enthusiasm, though constrained by the urban framework. Climbing vines surge up the sides of factories, their broad leaves creating natural cooling systems for the machinery within. Rooftop gardens spill over their borders in cascades of green, while engineered moss carpets the public walkways, drinking humidity directly from the air. Every courtyard becomes a microhabitat where cultivated palms and engineered succulents create dense, layered canopies that filter the brilliant light into dappled patterns on the ground below.

The industrial heart of Khartabad beats in the middle terraces, where steam-powered looms and atmospheric engines harness the endless supply of condensed water. The machinery operates with a deliberate rhythm—not the frantic pace of coal-fired industry, but the patient turning of wheels and pistons that work with, rather than against, the natural cycles of condensation and evaporation. Workshops open their great windows wide, allowing the moist air to flow through, carrying away heat while feeding the water-hungry processes within.

The light here has a quality unlike anywhere else—brilliant and diffused simultaneously. The sun burns down from the high, thin atmosphere while the ever-present mist softens its edges, creating a luminosity that seems to emanate from the air itself. Buildings glow as if lit from within, their limestone and marble facades drinking in the light and releasing it slowly throughout the day. Even the shadows pulse with reflected radiance.

Yet for all its density and activity, Khartabad maintains an uncanny quietude. The moisture in the air muffles sound, while the careful engineering of its streets creates acoustic pockets where conversations can happen in near-whispers. The machinery runs with well-oiled precision, producing only the softest hiss of steam and the gentle clicking of well-maintained gears. Footsteps are absorbed by the moss-carpeted walkways, and even the occasional call of a merchant seems to dissipate quickly into the humid air.

The absence of animal life is not immediately noticeable, so rich is the plant ecosystem, but gradually the stillness becomes apparent. No birds nest in the abundant foliage, no insects hum among the flowers. The gardens exist in perfect, sterile abundance—a green world without the small movements and sounds that usually accompany such lushness. This silence adds to the city's contemplative atmosphere, as if Khartabad exists in a state of perpetual meditation.

The city's growth follows the patient logic of its geological foundation. New terraces are carved slowly, following the natural stress lines in the granite. Building proceeds at the pace of stone-cutting and the gradual expansion of the water-harvesting systems. Districts evolve over decades, their character shaped by the subtle variations in microclimate and the slow accumulation of architectural decisions. Even the social rhythms match this geological patience—festivals marking the completion of major terraces, ceremonies celebrating the first water harvest from newly carved walls, traditions that span generations and grow more meaningful with time.

As evening approaches, the interplay of heat and moisture intensifies, creating thermal columns that carry the day's accumulated vapors upward. The city's upper reaches disappear into cloud, while the lower terraces bask in the golden light reflected from the mist above. The industrial machinery shifts into its night cycle, operating at reduced capacity while the atmospheric engines take advantage of the increased temperature differential between the hot stone and the cooling air.

In this place, human ingenuity has found a way to build with the landscape rather than against it, creating a civilization that feels both ancient and impossibly refined, as if the city has grown from the stone itself over countless ages, despite its sharp-edged geological youth.
