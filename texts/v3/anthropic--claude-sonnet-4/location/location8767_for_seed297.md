The Carved Reaches stretch endlessly across a high plateau, where millennia of patient hands have transformed raw stone into a vast honeycomb city. Every surface—from the towering spires that pierce the thin air to the deepest foundations carved into living rock—bears the mark of deliberate construction. The original mountain has been entirely consumed, replaced by an intricate maze of chambers, galleries, and bridges that spiral upward and burrow downward in equal measure.

Cold winds howl through the countless openings, creating a symphony of haunting tones as air rushes through carefully shaped apertures and resonating chambers. The architects understood sound; each district hums with its own harmonic signature, and the whole city rings like a massive stone organ played by the relentless gale. Temperature drops sharply as altitude increases, and frost forms intricate patterns on the carved surfaces, only to be scoured away by the next gust.

Water is precious here, nearly absent. What little moisture exists comes from the breath of inhabitants and occasional ice that forms in shadowed crevices. The builders solved this through engineering—carved channels collect and concentrate every drop, feeding it through stone conduits to cisterns deep within the structure. The absence of flowing water gives the place a crystalline quality, all sharp edges and perfect angles unmarked by erosion's softer touch.

Sparse vegetation clings to existence in protected alcoves and on narrow ledges. Hardy lichens paint the stone in subtle grays and yellows, while occasional tufts of wiry grass sprout from accumulated debris in corners where the wind can't reach. These small patches of green seem almost defiant against the overwhelming stonework, creating tiny ecosystems in the gaps between intention and perfection.

The fauna here has adapted remarkably to the vertical maze. Colonies of cliff-dwelling birds nest in the higher reaches, their calls echoing through the stone corridors in raucous waves. Smaller creatures—mice-like beings with adhesive pads, and insects that seem to dance on air currents—have claimed the middle levels. They move in great numbers through the passages, their chittering and scurrying adding texture to the wind's song. At the deepest levels, pale things that never knew sunlight pad through the darkness on soft feet, their presence felt more than seen.

The city is in constant motion, though not from collapse or growth—it has achieved a kind of dynamic equilibrium where its inhabitants flow through it like blood through arteries. Crowds surge through the main thoroughfares in patterns that seem almost choreographed, while smaller groups branch off into tributary passages. The stone itself seems to pulse with this movement, as if the carved channels were designed to accommodate these tidal flows of life.

Normal daylight filters down through the maze unevenly, creating a complex interplay of illumination and shadow that shifts throughout the day. Some chambers blaze with concentrated sunlight focused through crystal lenses set into the stone, while others remain in perpetual twilight. The builders planned this carefully—different levels receive light at different times, creating temporal rhythms within the spatial complexity.

There's something subtly unsettling about the precision of it all. The angles are too perfect, the acoustics too calculated. The way crowds move suggests an intelligence beyond mere urban planning, as if the city itself guides its inhabitants through optimal paths. Sometimes, in the deepest chambers, you can hear sounds that don't quite match the movements above—whispers in the stone, or rhythmic tapping that follows no pattern anyone remembers making.

The Carved Reaches extend beyond sight in every direction, a testament to either devotion or obsession, where an entire civilization chose to remake their world one chisel-strike at a time, creating something that lives and breathes despite being entirely artificial.
