**Preparation Guide for the Conservatory of Tides**

**Physical Conditioning & Health Preparations**

Begin cardiovascular conditioning at least two months before departure. The saturated atmosphere will strain your respiratory system—practice breathing exercises in steam rooms and saunas to acclimate your lungs to processing thick, moisture-laden air. Your body will work harder to extract oxygen from the humid environment, so build endurance gradually.

Undergo thorough medical screening, particularly for respiratory conditions, claustrophobia, and pressure sensitivity. The psychological weight of knowing you're suspended in crushing deep water can trigger panic responses even in stable individuals. Consider consulting with a diving psychologist familiar with deep-water psychology.

**Environmental Protection Gear**

Pack multiple sets of lightweight, moisture-wicking clothing made from synthetic materials—cotton will become permanently saturated and never dry. Merino wool can work but will retain odors. Bring quick-dry underwear and several pairs.

Waterproof everything electronic in sealed containers with desiccant packets. Even "water-resistant" devices will fail in the perpetual humidity. Consider a dehumidifying chamber or dry box for essential electronics.

Your boots must have excellent grip on wet metal and organic surfaces. Maritime deck boots with deep tread work well. Pack extra socks—your feet will constantly be damp.

**Breathing & Air Quality**

Bring a portable air purifier if possible. The metallic taste and organic decay notes in the air suggest complex atmospheric chemistry that may stress your system. N95 or P100 masks can help initially while you acclimate, though the humidity will make them uncomfortable.

Nasal strips and decongestants may help with the thick air. Some visitors report difficulty sleeping due to the dense atmosphere—practice breathing techniques and consider bringing a small fan for air circulation around your sleeping area.

**Psychological Preparation**

Study images of deep ocean environments and large aquariums to familiarize yourself with the visual sensation of being surrounded by water. The awareness of the crushing depths beyond the glass creates a unique form of psychological pressure.

Practice meditation in confined spaces. The combination of enclosure, humidity, and the organic-mechanical environment can be deeply unsettling. Develop mental anchoring techniques to maintain equilibrium.

Prepare for profound silence broken only by mechanical whispers and organic movements. The absence of familiar sounds (birds, insects, human activity) can be disturbing. Consider bringing white noise generation if permitted.

**Interaction with the Environment**

Do not touch the plants or mechanical systems. The ecosystem has achieved its own balance through precise engineering—human interference, even seemingly harmless touching, can disrupt growth patterns and mechanical timing.

The metal surfaces will be constantly slick with condensation. Move slowly and maintain three points of contact when climbing or navigating complex areas.

Observe the light patterns to understand the passage of time—normal circadian rhythms are disrupted by the filtered, shifting illumination. Keep a mechanical watch as backup timing reference.

**Health Monitoring**

Monitor yourself for signs of respiratory distress, skin irritation from constant moisture, and psychological strain. The environment can exacerbate existing conditions in unexpected ways.

Watch for fungal issues on your skin and clothing. The warm, moist conditions are ideal for growth. Antifungal powder and regular clothing changes are essential.

Stay hydrated despite the humid environment—you're still losing moisture through respiration and will need to replace it, though your normal thirst mechanisms may be suppressed by the saturated air.

**Specific Hazards & Precautions**

The "stranger things" mentioned in the deep corners represent unknown biological/mechanical hybrids. Maintain distance from any glowing plants or constructions that seem to move with purpose. Document but do not investigate directly.

Sharp edges of metal infrastructure are often hidden under moss growth. Feel carefully before grasping any surface.

The structural integrity depends on precise pressure differentials. Avoid creating sudden pressure changes—no rapid movements, no attempts to open sealed systems, no impact against the glass walls.

**Supply Considerations**

Bring extra batteries stored in moisture-proof containers—the humidity will drain them faster than normal and corrosion is a constant threat.

Pack high-energy foods that won't be damaged by moisture. Avoid anything that might grow mold or attract the attention of the hybrid organisms.

Bring basic tools for equipment maintenance—the corrosive environment will require constant attention to metal items.

**Duration Limits**

Plan for shorter visits initially. The combination of physical stress from the atmosphere, psychological pressure from the environment, and potential health impacts from the modified air make extended stays inadvisable until you understand your personal tolerance.

Most importantly, remember that this is not just a garden or a machine—it's a living hybrid ecosystem that has begun to evolve beyond its original parameters. Approach with the respect and caution you would show to any alien environment, because in many ways, that's exactly what it has become.
