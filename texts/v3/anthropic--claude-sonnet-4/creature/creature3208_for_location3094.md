The Tenders move through this place like living equations, their forms shifting between states of matter with the same deliberate precision that governs the pools' geometry. They are not quite energy, not quite flesh—something between crystalline growth and conscious thought made manifest. When glimpsed directly, they appear as tall, angular beings composed of the same translucent basalt as the columns, their surfaces reflecting not light but the mathematical relationships that bind this place together. Yet they resist direct observation, becoming more visible in peripheral vision, more solid when approached indirectly.

Each Tender appears to be responsible for a cluster of pools, moving between them in patterns that trace invisible geometric connections across the basin floor. They work with tools that exist only when needed—implements of focused intention that materialize from the humid air and dissolve back into possibility once their task is complete. A Tender might spend hours adjusting the isotope ratios in a single pool, their elongated fingers—if fingers they are—stirring equations into the water until reality settles into new configurations.

They communicate through harmonics, their voices joining the basin's natural resonance to create conversations that exist as much in the spaces between sounds as in the tones themselves. These discussions seem to span years, with pauses measured in seasons. One Tender might pose a question in spring, another respond in autumn, the conversation threading through the constant percussion of dripping water and the whisper of grass that moves without wind.

The copper-wire heron visits them regularly, and they welcome it as a colleague rather than an animal. When it calls, the Tenders pause in their work and listen to reports encoded in those impossible harmonics—news from other places where reality experiments with new forms of stability. The bird serves as their connection to a wider network of such sites, carrying messages between laboratories scattered across landscapes where the ordinary rules have learned to bend.

Sometimes, the Tenders gather around the floating spheres in arrangements that mirror the pools' perfect circles. During these congregations, the spheres' rotation accelerates slightly, and the electromagnetic fluctuations intensify. The Tenders seem to download information from these artifacts—or perhaps upload observations to them. The spheres serve as both memory banks and computational cores, processing centuries of data about the subtle manipulations required to maintain this place where star-water pools in perfect circles and stone learns to cooperate with orchids.

Occasionally, a new Tender emerges from one of the pools, not born but crystallized from the accumulated intentions of those already present. These newcomers begin small and angular, like geometric sketches, but gradually develop the fluid precision of their elders. They learn by observing the established patterns, adding their own innovations only after decades of apprenticeship to the basin's fundamental rhythms.

The Tenders' work appears focused on maintaining the delicate balance that allows this place to exist as a stable pocket where different physical laws can coexist peacefully. They are gardeners of reality itself, tending not just the visible pools and growing things, but the underlying mathematics that permit water to condense from stellar vacuum, stone to collaborate with living organisms, and time to flow at angles that create morning pools that exist only until evening.

They ignore human visitors with the polite disinterest of researchers absorbed in long-term projects. Occasionally, one might pause in their work to observe a human with what might be curiosity, but they make no attempt at contact. They seem to understand that their work operates on timescales that make human lifespans momentary fluctuations, like the overnight pools that appear and vanish. Yet they show no hostility—merely the patient focus of beings engaged in experiments that began before human civilization and will continue long after it has become another layer in geology's memory.

In the rare moments when all the Tenders gather simultaneously—perhaps once in each human generation—the entire basin resonates like a struck bell. During these convergences, new pools appear overnight and remain permanent additions to the array. The floating spheres pulse with intensified energy, and the carved arch shimmers with patterns that seem to map the successful completion of some phase in the ongoing experiment.

The Tenders embody the patient intelligence that shaped this place—not its creators, but its faithful curators, ensuring that each element continues to participate in the vast, collaborative exploration of what reality might become when given permission to transcend its apparent limitations. They are the living proof that consciousness and mathematics are not separate phenomena, but different expressions of the same fundamental curiosity about the possible.
