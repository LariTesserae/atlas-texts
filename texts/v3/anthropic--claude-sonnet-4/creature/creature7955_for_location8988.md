In the spaces between the tiles—gaps so narrow they might be mistaken for mere grooves in the stone—dwell the Interstice Keepers. They are not quite creatures, not quite formations, but something that exists in the liminal space between mineral and flesh, between growth and intention.

Each Keeper begins as barely more than a strand of living crystal, no thicker than spider's silk, threading through the hairline fissures where opalescent tiles meet. Over decades—or perhaps centuries, for time moves differently in their domain—they slowly accrete layers of calcified awareness. Their bodies become delicate traceries of semi-translucent bone-coral, branching in impossible patterns that mirror the mineral growths above them but remain distinctly alive, distinctly purposeful.

They have no eyes, for they see through resonance. The harmonic whispers that occasionally drift through the pocket are their conversations—complex discussions conducted through minute variations in the frequency at which their crystalline bodies vibrate. They speak of the weight of water droplets, the precise angle of light refraction through limestone, the gradual shift of mineral content in the eternally weeping walls. What might seem like mundane observations to others are, to them, profound philosophical discourse about the nature of perpetual becoming.

The eldest among them have grown large enough to span multiple tile intersections, their bodies forming bridges of living calcite between distant points. These ancients serve as the memory of the pocket, their tissue encoding centuries of microscopic changes in the space's delicate equilibrium. Newer generations cluster around them like thoughts gathering around a central idea, learning the intricate discipline of maintaining perfect balance.

Each Keeper tends to a specific aspect of the pocket's preservation. Some regulate the rate of condensation, their breath—if it can be called breath—creating the precise humidity that prevents both drought and flood. Others tend to the light itself, their crystalline bodies serving as prisms and filters, ensuring that the glow from each tile remains constant and that the slow circulation of illumination continues its eternal dance.

The Keepers feed on transition itself—the moment when water vapor becomes droplet, when light passes from one medium to another, when silence gives birth to harmonic. They are nourished by the countless tiny transformations that maintain the pocket's impossible stasis, growing stronger as they help orchestrate the very changes that keep change at bay.

When footsteps echo from the alleys beyond, the Keepers pause in their ministrations, their bodies stiffening into perfect stillness. To the casual observer, they become indistinguishable from the mineral formations they tend—just another layer of calcified beauty in an already impossible space. But once the intruder passes, they resume their work with renewed purpose, for they know that their vigilance is what keeps this pocket of eternity intact.

They dream, these Keepers, though their dreams are unlike any mammalian reverie. They dream in geological time, envisioning the slow dance of continents, the patient work of water carving canyons, the birth and death of mountains. In their longest dreams, they sometimes glimpse other pockets like their own scattered throughout the world—similar spaces where the impossible has found purchase, tended by different kinds of guardians but sustained by the same fundamental commitment to preserving what should not exist.

The youngest Keepers, barely more than threads of conscious mineral, sometimes wonder what lies beyond their pocket. But as they grow and their awareness deepens, they come to understand that they are not trapped here but rather have discovered the secret that the broader world has forgotten: that some kinds of beauty can only exist in the spaces too small for conquest, too perfect for improvement, too strange for anyone to claim.

They are the living proof that magic, properly tended, can indeed last forever.
