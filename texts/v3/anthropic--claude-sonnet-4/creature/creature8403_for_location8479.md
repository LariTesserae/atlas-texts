In the deepest chambers where the root-columns grow thickest, the **Tenders** move with the slow certainty of those who measure time in decades. Their skin has taken on the pale, luminescent quality of cave-dwelling creatures, but with a faint green undertone—as if chlorophyll has begun to seep into their very being through centuries of symbiosis. They are tall and willowy, their limbs elongated from generations of reaching into high root tangles and narrow crevices. Their fingers end in hardened, bark-like nails perfect for coaxing wayward roots into proper channels or scraping moss into precise arrangements.

The Tenders speak rarely, and when they do, it's in whispers that mimic the sound of leaves brushing together. They have learned to read the language of the Undergarden—the way certain mosses change color to signal shifts in humidity, how the pattern of water drips can indicate the health of root systems dozens of feet above. Their movements are ritual-slow and purposeful; they might spend an entire day training a single vine to grow along a carved channel, or sit motionless for hours listening to the almost-inaudible sounds that indicate whether a root system is thriving or struggling.

Between the moss carpets and within the hollow spaces of the largest root buttresses live the **Whisperkin**—creatures no larger than house cats but possessed of an uncanny intelligence. Their bodies are covered in fine, fur-like moss that allows them to blend seamlessly with their surroundings, becoming visible only when they choose to move. The Whisperkin serve as the Undergarden's memory keepers and messengers, their chittering calls carrying information about water levels, root health, and the location of new growth throughout the vast network of chambers.

They build elaborate nests in the junction points where multiple root systems converge, weaving together shed bark, crystallized sap, and the strongest moss fibers. The Whisperkin can navigate the entire Undergarden through scent trails and sonic mapping, using echolocation calls so subtle they blend with the ambient dripping and rustling. They've developed a complex relationship with the Tenders, serving as their extended senses throughout the vast underground realm.

In the water-filled basins and the perpetually damp soil pockets that line the pathways, communities of **Rootshapers** thrive. These beings appear almost plant-like themselves—their skin is rough and bark-textured, and rootlike appendages extend from their backs and limbs, allowing them to directly interface with the Undergarden's living systems. The Rootshapers are the descendants of the original builders, but their bodies have adapted so completely to their underground realm that they can no longer survive on the surface.

The Rootshapers spend their lives in direct communion with the root networks, guiding growth patterns not through external tools but by merging their own root-appendages with the tree systems and sharing nutrients, chemical signals, and even electrical impulses. Through this biological internet, they can sense the health and needs of the entire Undergarden simultaneously. When adjustments are needed—when water needs redirecting, or when a section is growing too densely—the Rootshapers coordinate responses across miles of underground space with the efficiency of a single organism.

Deep in the cathedral chambers where the greatest root buttresses create spaces vast enough to echo, ancient **Breathkeepers** maintain their eternal vigil. These beings have grown so large and still that they might be mistaken for part of the architecture itself—living statues wrapped in moss and trailing vines, their slow inhalation and exhalation helping to maintain the air currents that keep the entire system healthy.

The Breathkeepers are the oldest inhabitants, possibly descendants of the original architects who chose to merge completely with their creation. They move perhaps once per season, shifting their massive forms to new positions that optimize airflow or to tend to root systems that have grown beyond the reach of the other inhabitants. Their breath carries spores and chemical signals that help coordinate the growth patterns of the entire Undergarden, making them the inadvertent conductors of this vast green symphony.

And in the smallest spaces—between the moss fibers, in the droplets of water that bead on leaves, within the rich soil that collects in carved hollows—countless microscopic communities flourish. These are the true builders and maintainers of the Undergarden: bacteria that fix nitrogen for the roots, fungi that facilitate communication between plant systems, and countless other organisms that break down waste, regulate pH, and maintain the delicate chemical balance that allows this underground paradise to thrive.

Together, all these inhabitants exist in such perfect symbiosis that the line between living creature and living architecture has all but disappeared. The Undergarden breathes as one entity—its inhabitants not separate from their environment but woven so completely into its fabric that they have become the thoughts and dreams of this vast, dreaming place.
