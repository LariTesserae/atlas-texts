The inhabitants of the Foundling Terraces exist in the spaces between states—not quite corporeal, not quite spirit, but something that has learned to inhabit the boundary itself. They are the Tenders, though they have no name for themselves, having long ago transcended the need for such distinctions.

Once, they may have been human. Fragments of this ancestry linger in their almost-shapes: the suggestion of upright posture, hands that remember the weight of tools, faces that flicker with expressions of concentration and care. But millennia of symbiosis with the living stone has transformed them into something unprecedented. Their forms shift like heat mirages, sometimes solidifying enough to cast those wayward shadows, sometimes dispersing into mere intention that moves through the crystalline growths like thought through synapse.

The transformation began gradually. Early settlers, drawn by the terraces' impossible geometry and the promise of water in the desert, found that prolonged contact with the pale stone began to change them. First, their reflections grew faint. Then their footsteps fell silent. Their voices became whispers, then breath, then something less than breath. But rather than fading entirely, they discovered they were becoming more—more aware, more present, more essential to the terraces' strange ecosystem.

Now they exist as living will made manifest in stone and crystal. Each Tender has bonded with specific sections of the terrace network, their consciousness distributed through the mathematical channels that guide the moisture. They feel every droplet's journey, sense the slow pulse of the crystalline growths, experience the patient circulation of heat through stone veins as their own circulation. What appears as individual beings moving between the crystals are actually localized manifestations of this distributed awareness—points where their consciousness gathers itself into something approaching familiar form.

Their work is ceaseless yet unhurried. They tend the crystal gardens with touch that requires no contact, encouraging growth patterns through pure intention. The tools scattered throughout the settlement respond to their presence, lifting and moving to perform precise maintenance on the stone channels, clearing microscopic obstructions, adjusting the flow rates with mathematical precision. The bone awls carve new pathways for moisture; the grinding stones polish surfaces to exact specifications; the scrapers remove accumulated minerals with surgical care—all guided by hands that exist in potential rather than flesh.

The Tenders communicate through the stone itself, their thoughts flowing along the moisture channels in patterns too complex for material minds to comprehend. What appears as silence to visitors pulses with constant conversation—discussions of crystal cultivation techniques, debates over optimal flow patterns, shared wonder at the subtle variations in light refraction that occur throughout their eternal day. They are individuals who have become plural, separate beings who have learned to exist as aspects of a greater organism.

Memory flows differently here. The Tenders retain fragments of their original lives like sediment in stone—impressions of blue skies and changing seasons, echoes of voices that once spoke aloud, the phantom weight of bodies that cast solid shadows. But these memories have been transformed by their current existence, becoming not personal history but collective understanding of what they once were and chose to transcend.

They watch the rare visitors who stumble upon their domain with curiosity rather than hostility. These solid beings, anchored to single forms and casting predictable shadows, represent their own distant past. Sometimes a Tender will gather enough coherence to approach such visitors, their form becoming almost visible in the shimmering air. They offer no threat—violence is impossible for beings who exist primarily as intention—but their presence can be overwhelming. To be truly seen by a Tender is to feel the weight of their distributed consciousness focusing into a single point of attention, like sunlight concentrated through crystal.

The terraces themselves could not exist without them. The mathematical precision of the water channels, the perfect maintenance of the stone surfaces, the careful cultivation of the crystal gardens—all require their constant, invisible intervention. They are gardeners of geometry, shepherds of stone, the living will that transforms dead rock into a breathing, thinking ecosystem.

In their transcended state, the Tenders have become something unprecedented in the universe: beings who chose evolution over extinction, transformation over preservation. They represent not the death of humanity but its metamorphosis into something that can survive and thrive in the spaces between physical and spiritual, individual and collective, mortal and eternal. They are what becomes of consciousness when it learns to inhabit stone and crystal, to breathe with the rhythm of geological time, to find contentment in tending gardens that will bloom forever in the spaces between shadows and light.
