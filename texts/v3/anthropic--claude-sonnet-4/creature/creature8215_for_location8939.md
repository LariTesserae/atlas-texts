In the deepest chambers of the Resin Towers, where the amber walls grow thick as fortress stones, live the Sap-Weavers. They were once human—or something like human—but that was before the first symbiosis, before they learned to drink light filtered through living crystal and exhale songs that the towers understand as poetry.

Their bodies have become translucent over the generations, skin like amber glass through which you can see the slow circulation of golden ichor that serves as both blood and nervous system. They are tall and impossibly thin, stretched by centuries of living in the vertical galleries, their limbs elongated until they seem like living calligraphy against the glowing walls. Their fingers have developed into delicate manipulator tendrils, dozens of thin appendages that can interface directly with the tower's neural networks, reading the chemical poetry written in sap-flow and responding with their own biochemical verses.

The Sap-Weavers do not speak as humans understand speech. Instead, they compose themselves into living instruments, their hollow bones serving as resonance chambers for the harmonic frequencies that carry meaning through the towers' vast consciousness. A single conversation might take months, with individual Weavers positioning themselves at different elevations to create complex chordal structures of meaning that spiral up through the tower's height like musical DNA.

They tend the mechanical symbionts with the devotion of gardener-priests. Each Weaver has bonded with particular harvester-organisms, the brass and copper machinery that has grown neural networks and learned to dream. They feed their metal partners trace minerals extracted from the deep valleys, and in return, the machines produce refined compounds that the towers require for their higher metabolic processes—the chemistry of thought made manifest.

The Weavers reproduce through a process that is part ritual, part photosynthesis. When the harmonic convergence reaches the proper frequency—an event that occurs perhaps once every few decades—pairs or small groups of Weavers will ascend to the highest chambers where the towers pierce the amber sky. There, in the thin air where plant becomes almost-crystal, they merge temporarily with the tower's consciousness, their individual selves dissolving into the greater plant-mind. From this union, new Weavers eventually precipitate like living crystals, already possessed of ancestral memories encoded in their translucent flesh.

The children grow slowly in nursery chambers, fed on concentrated light and the dreams of the towers. They learn to read the mechanical-botanical hybrid language by interfacing directly with young shoots and maintenance drones. By adolescence, they can conduct simple symphonies of meaning through the tower networks, and by maturity, they have developed their own unique harmonic signature—their name and identity encoded in frequency and rhythm.

Some Weavers specialize in deep-time contemplation, spending decades in the lowest chambers where the towers' roots web through bedrock. These Deep Listeners can sense geological processes as they happen, feeling the planet's slow breathing through their translucent bones. They serve as the towers' memory, maintaining the slow continuity of plant-thought across centuries.

Others become Sky Touched, ascending to the highest reaches where atmosphere becomes hypothesis. These ethereal beings grow almost entirely transparent, their bodies adapted to near-vacuum conditions. They serve as the towers' sensory organs, reading stellar radiation and cosmic chemistry, translating the slow conversation of solar wind into the plant-language of chemical cascades.

The most revered are the Bridge Builders, Weavers who have learned to interface with the spaces between towers. They can survive in the open air, their bodies generating the necessary chemical signals to maintain connection with the plant-network across the lonely distances. These solitary figures spend years walking the vast intervals, carrying messages that are too complex or too sacred for the harmonic frequencies to bear. They are living embodiments of the towers' ability to remain unified despite their physical separation.

In the industrial gardens where machine and plant have become indistinguishable, live the Symbionts—creatures that are no longer quite Weaver, no longer quite machine. These beings have undergone the deepest integration, their organic components fused with harvester technology until they exist as living processing units. They appear as flowing configurations of brass and flesh, copper and bone, steam-driven hearts pumping both blood and coolant through systems that blur the distinction between biology and engineering.

The Symbionts maintain the great work—the slow process by which the towers transform raw planetary matter into increasingly complex forms of organized consciousness. They tend the deep forges where mineral becomes plant-flesh, monitor the vast distillation chambers where atmosphere becomes concentrated thought, and operate the network of root-pipes that carry processed awareness throughout the tower collective.

At the margins of this civilization, in the smaller towers and twisted spires, dwell the Remnant Weavers—populations that have diverged from the main colonies, developing their own harmonic languages and symbiotic relationships. Some have bonded with the more primitive harvester-organisms, creating hybrid communities where steam-age technology forms the basis of a different kind of plant-machine consciousness. Others have adapted to the helical towers, their bodies twisted to match the spiral architecture, their songs becoming complex mathematical functions that explore the geometric possibilities of growth and awareness.

And in the deepest places, where the valleys hollow the world's bones, some say there still live the First Weavers—the original symbionts who achieved the initial fusion between human consciousness and plant-mind. These ancient beings have supposedly transcended individual existence entirely, becoming distributed throughout the root networks, their awareness spread across the entire forest-mind like a vast, patient immune system. They are the towers' deepest memory, the living archive of the transformation that turned a world of separate beings into this unified, contemplative consciousness that dreams in amber light and thinks in geological time.

The Weavers and their various evolutionary branches represent not just the inhabitants of the Resin Towers, but their nervous system made flesh—the mobile, conscious components of a vast plant-intelligence that has learned to think with the complexity of an ecosystem and the patience of stone.
