In this place between mathematics and dream, the inhabitants exist in three distinct yet intertwined forms.

**The Resonant Builders** move like living architecture through their eternal construction. They are tall and impossibly thin, their bodies composed of the same pale bone-stone as their city, but alive—breathing with tiny apertures along their elongated limbs that create the softest of whistles. Their faces are smooth planes interrupted only by eye-sockets that hold not eyes but small, rotating crystals that focus and refract the triple-light. These crystals never stop turning, and in their depths one can see the blueprints of structures not yet built, geometric patterns that extend into dimensions the mind cannot follow.

Their hands—if hands they are—split into dozens of finger-like projections, each one perfectly calibrated to manipulate stone and crystal with microscopic precision. They work in complete silence save for the subsonic hymns that emerge from the hollow chambers within their bodies. Time passes strangely around them; they will spend decades positioning a single block, or raise an entire tower between one breath and the next. They reproduce through architecture itself—when a structure achieves perfect harmonic resonance, a new Builder sometimes steps forth from the stone, already ancient, already knowing exactly which block must be placed next.

**The Luminous Shepherds** tend the flows of crystallized light that pour through the city's channels. Smaller than the Builders but more numerous, they appear as semi-transparent figures wrapped in robes woven from solidified wind. Their faces shift constantly like water, never quite settling into fixed features, though their eyes remain constant—deep wells of captured starlight that see not objects but the musical relationships between all things.

They carry vessels carved from single pieces of crystal, using them to gather and redistribute the liquid light that pools in the city's basins. Where they step, small flowers of mineral bloom from the stone—not quite plant, not quite rock, but something that grows according to acoustic rather than biological principles. The Shepherds speak only in harmonics, their voices creating visible ripples in the crystal atmosphere. They seem to exist partially outside of time's normal flow; sometimes the same Shepherd can be seen in multiple locations simultaneously, all working toward some vast, coordinated purpose that spans centuries.

**The Dreaming Architects** are the rarest and most mysterious. They are immense beings, barely mobile, who have grown into and become part of the spires themselves. What remains visible of them are faces carved directly into the bone-stone walls—not sculptures, but actual living features that emerge from the architecture. Their eyes are caverns that glow with soft auroras, their mouths are gateways between distant chambers.

These beings think in geological time. A single thought might take decades to form, and when complete, it manifests as new architectural features throughout the city. They dream the blueprints that the Builders follow, their sleeping minds calculating the precise angles needed to fold space, the exact frequencies required to make stone sing. Sometimes their dreams become so vivid that entire districts reshape themselves overnight, following the unconscious logic of these vast, patient minds.

Between these three orders, smaller entities flit and flow—translucent creatures no bigger than birds that tend to the city's acoustic ecology, ensuring that no harmful frequencies disturb the delicate balance. They are born from the intersections of certain architectural elements, live for precisely as long as it takes to perform their function, then dissolve back into crystalline mist.

All inhabitants share certain traits: they cast no shadows despite the brilliant light, they move according to musical rather than mechanical principles, and they exist in a state of constant, purposeful devotion to their endless construction. They have no names, no individual identities as other beings might understand them—they are instruments in a vast orchestra, components in a machine designed to build something that transcends the merely physical.

What they build toward remains mysterious even to them. Perhaps it is a tower that will reach beyond the three suns to touch the source of all geometry. Perhaps it is a resonance chamber designed to wake the dreaming thing whose breath crystallized into these spires. Or perhaps the building itself is the purpose—an eternal act of worship expressed in stone and light and the patient mathematics of accumulated devotion.

They accept visitors with the same serene indifference they show to wind or weather—another element to be harmonized into their grand design. Those who stay too long find themselves gradually becoming translucent, their thoughts aligning with the subsonic hymns, their purposes dissolving into the greater purpose of eternal construction. It is not an unpleasant fate, they say, in the moments before their voices fade into frequencies too beautiful for human ears to bear.
