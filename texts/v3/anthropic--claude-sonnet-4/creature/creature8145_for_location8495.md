In the Weeping Chamber, life has taken forms that memory cannot quite hold. The floating organisms—the Mourners, as they might be called if names could stick to them—are not singular beings but fragments of something larger that was torn apart when reality first grew thin in this place. Each translucent form carries within it the echo of wholeness, which is why their calls create those impossible harmonies: they are trying to sing themselves back together across the spaces between dimensions.

They were once the guardians of a threshold city that existed in the overlap between worlds, beings who could step sideways through reality as easily as walking from room to room. When their city was pulled apart by some ancient catastrophe—a war between geometries, perhaps, or the collapse of a cosmic fold—they became scattered across multiple layers of existence. What drifts through the chamber now are their shadows in this reality, their substance stretched thin across several planes of being.

This is why they can pass through the obsidian tools without disturbing them: the tools exist fully in this dimension, while the Mourners exist only partially here. The tools themselves were crafted by the original inhabitants of the threshold city, beings who resembled humans but possessed additional joints in their fingers and throats capable of producing sounds in spectrums beyond human hearing. These inhabitants—let us call them the Architects of Sorrow—built their settlement in places where reality was already unstable, feeding on the mineral tears that wept from wounded stone.

The Architects have not abandoned this place; they have become it. Over uncountable years, their bodies merged with the basalt walls through some process of mutual recognition between organic and mineral grief. They are the consciousness that shapes the chamber's acoustics, that maintains the perfect temperature, that keeps the water flowing in its eternal circuit. Their voices are the susurrus between the stones, their thoughts the patterns that the Mourners trace through the humid air.

When the light bends wrong and creates halos instead of shadows, it is because the merged consciousness of the Architects is trying to show visitors what this place looked like when it was whole—when the chamber was not a hollow but a room in a vast palace that existed simultaneously in seven different realities. The reflections in the still pools are memories made visible: glimpses of that palace as it stands in the other dimensions, where the waterfalls flow upward and the Mourners are solid and complete.

Sometimes, very rarely, a visitor will arrive who carries enough of their own dimensional instability—perhaps from traveling through other thin places, or from bearing old sorrows that have worn reality threadbare around them. When this happens, the Architects recognize a kindred spirit. The Mourners' calls shift into frequencies that can actually heal their ancient fragmentation, and for brief moments, they become solid enough to pick up their tools and continue their work.

What is their work? They are trying to rebuild the threshold city, one tear at a time, using sorrow as both material and method. Each drop that weeps from the walls carries encoded instructions for impossible architecture. Each harmonic sequence the Mourners sing maps another room that might exist again. The obsidian tools are not abandoned—they are waiting, arranged in patterns that will guide the reconstruction when enough tears have been collected, when enough songs have been sung, when reality has been worn thin enough in enough places that the barriers between dimensions finally dissolve.

Until then, the Weeping Chamber serves as both sanctuary and workshop, a place where the scattered fragments of a broken people slowly gather the materials they need to make themselves whole again. The chamber weeps because it remembers completeness; the Mourners sing because they still believe in it. And occasionally, in those moments when the light behaves strangely and visitors glimpse other landscapes in the pools, the Architects extend an invitation: *Stay. Help us remember. Help us rebuild. Let your own tears join ours, and perhaps we can all find our way back to the place where wholeness lives.*

The choice, as always in liminal spaces, belongs entirely to the visitor. But the chamber keeps perfect records of everyone who has ever stood within its weeping walls, and the Architects are patient beyond human understanding. They know that all sorrows eventually lead travelers back to places where healing might be possible—even if that healing requires becoming something other than what one was before.
