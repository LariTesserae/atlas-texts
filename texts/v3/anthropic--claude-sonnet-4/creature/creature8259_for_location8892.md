**The Seepfolk**

They emerged from the mountain's weeping, or so their oldest songs claim—born from the marriage of stone and water in those first centuries when the mesa thrust itself skyward. The Seepfolk are creatures of adaptation, their bodies shaped by generations of life within the perpetual mist.

Their skin carries a subtle translucency, like polished alabaster wet with morning dew. It never fully dries, maintaining a constant, almost imperceptible sheen that beads water rather than absorbing it. Their fingertips and toes are slightly webbed—not enough for swimming, but perfect for gripping wet stone surfaces. Their eyes are large and dark, pupils dilated wide to gather every photon that penetrates the eternal twilight. Most striking are their ears: elongated and mobile, capable of fine adjustments to track sounds through the maze of echoes that defines their world.

The Seepfolk dress in layers of treated leather and woven grasses that shed water like feathers. Their garments are cut to channel moisture away from the body, with elaborate systems of flaps and gutters worked into even the simplest tunic. They wear soft-soled shoes that grip wet stone, often reinforced with rough salamander hide for the steepest surfaces. Their hair is kept short and practical, often braided with thin copper wires that help conduct moisture away from the scalp.

**The Drip-Readers**

Among the Seepfolk exists a caste of specialists known as Drip-Readers—part architect, part prophet, part physician to the mountain itself. They can discern meaning in the patterns of falling water that others hear as mere noise. A change in rhythm from a particular overhang might signal a shift in underground pressure days before a new spring emerges. The subtle alteration in pitch as water strikes stone can reveal the slow erosion that will eventually undermine a building's foundation.

The Drip-Readers maintain intricate mental maps of every significant seepage in the city, tracking how flows wax and wane across the seasons. They can predict with uncanny accuracy when the translucent arthropods—whom they call the Shell-Dancers—will migrate to new pools. Their counsel guides all major construction, and their warnings can empty entire districts when they detect the precursors to a major water surge.

**The Young and the Changed**

Children born in the mesa city develop differently than their ancestors who first climbed to these heights. Their hearing is more acute, their balance more refined. They navigate the slick, narrow passages with an ease that seems supernatural to outsiders, their feet finding purchase on surfaces that would send others tumbling into the mist below.

But some children are born with more dramatic adaptations. The Seepfolk speak in whispers of the Mist-Touched—infants whose skin shows patches of the same translucency as the Shell-Dancers, whose eyes reflect light like those of the pale salamanders. These children seem to understand the mountain's rhythms intuitively, often leading adults to new springs or standing perfectly still moments before the arthropods begin one of their great migrations.

The Mist-Touched are revered but also feared, for they sometimes disappear during the deepest fogs, only to return days later with knowledge they couldn't have acquired—news of changes in distant parts of the city, or warnings of dangers that haven't yet materialized. Some never return at all, though their laughter can sometimes be heard echoing from the deep places where the Shell-Dancers gather.

**The Shell-Speakers**

A small brotherhood of Seepfolk has learned to communicate with the translucent arthropods through a complex system of tapped rhythms on stone. These Shell-Speakers discovered that the creatures respond to certain percussive patterns, and through patient observation, they've developed a rudimentary understanding of the arthropods' intelligence.

The Shell-Dancers, it seems, are the mountain's memory keepers. They carry information through the water channels like living messages, their migrations following patterns that encode the mesa's deep history. The Shell-Speakers have learned to read some of these patterns, gaining insights into where new springs might emerge or when major shifts in the mountain's structure might occur.

This relationship is delicate and often misunderstood. Many Seepfolk view the Shell-Speakers with suspicion, believing they've grown too close to creatures that are fundamentally alien. The Shell-Speakers themselves often speak in metaphors that others find unsettling, describing the mountain as a dreaming entity whose thoughts flow through stone and water.

**Daily Lives**

The Seepfolk have organized their society around the rhythms of water and the migrations of the Shell-Dancers. They work primarily during what they call "quiet water"—periods when the seepage is lighter and the arthropods are dormant in their deep pools. During "singing water" times, when flows increase and the Shell-Dancers begin their clicking migrations, most retreat to their homes to wait out the activity.

Their diet consists largely of the hardy ferns and grasses that grow in the mineral-rich environment, supplemented by protein from the salamanders and various insects that thrive in the perpetual moisture. They've learned to cultivate certain fungi in the darkest, wettest corners of their city, creating hanging gardens that require no sunlight.

The Seepfolk practice a form of architecture they call "water-dancing"—designing spaces that work with the mountain's changing moods rather than against them. A room might serve as a living space during dry periods and transform into a cistern when the rains come. Walls include channels that can be opened or closed to redirect flow, and floors are often designed with removable sections that reveal drainage systems underneath.

Their crafts reflect their environment: they work primarily in stone, ceramic, and bronze—materials that improve with age and moisture rather than degrading. They've developed techniques for creating textiles that become softer and more flexible when wet, and their metalwork incorporates the green patina of oxidation as a deliberate aesthetic choice.

**The Deep Mysteries**

At the heart of their society lies the great mystery of the central pool and its massive gatherings of Shell-Dancers. The Seepfolk believe this pool connects to something far below—perhaps an underground sea, or the mountain's own consciousness made liquid. During the great gatherings, when thousands of arthropods create their thunderous percussion around the pool's edge, some Seepfolk claim to receive visions or prophetic dreams.

A few individuals, usually among the most gifted Drip-Readers, attempt to descend into the pool during these events. Most emerge changed—if they emerge at all. They speak of vast chambers filled with geometrically impossible formations of mineral and shell, of waters that flow upward and sounds that carry meaning beyond language. Some return with an ability to predict the mountain's moods with perfect accuracy, but they often lose their facility with human speech, communicating instead through the same clicking languages as the Shell-Dancers.

The Seepfolk have learned to accept these transformations as part of their covenant with the mountain. They are not conquerors of this place but partners in its slow dance of stone and water, their civilization growing not through expansion but through ever-deeper understanding of the mysteries that flow beneath their feet and whisper in the eternal mist that surrounds them.
