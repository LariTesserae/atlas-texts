**The Geometers**

In the deepest hours before what passes for dawn, they emerge. The Geometers rise from the perfect circular pools not as creatures breaking surface tension, but as living equations resolving themselves into flesh. Their bodies are translucent, shot through with veins of that same bone-pale clay that threads the mud, and they move with the deliberate precision of compass points finding true north.

They are tall—nearly eight feet when fully extended—but their height shifts as they work. Sometimes they compress themselves into squat, toad-like forms to press intricate patterns into the yielding earth. Other times they stretch impossibly thin, becoming living plumb lines that measure angles only they can perceive. Their limbs end not in hands or feet but in specialized appendages: perfect circles, precise right angles, curves that follow mathematical functions too complex for names.

The Geometers do not speak, but they harmonize. As they work—and they are always working—they emit those low, resonant tones that ripple through every pool simultaneously. These sounds are not communication but calculation, each note corresponding to some vast equation they collectively solve. Their music shapes the very fabric of this place, bending reality into forms that please their incomprehensible aesthetic.

They create the overnight formations, these patient architects of impossibility. Working in groups of three or seven or thirteen (never other numbers), they press spirals into the mud with the devotion of monks illuminating manuscripts. Each shape serves a purpose in their grand design—a blueprint written in earth and water, in angles and curves that somehow contain meaning beyond their mere geometry.

**The Builders**

Where the Geometers are ephemeral, flowing between states of matter like thoughts becoming words, the Builders possess a more solid presence. They appear human at first glance—until you notice they cast no reflections in the countless pools, and their shadows fall upward, disappearing into the dark water overhead.

The Builders wear clothes that might once have been familiar: work shirts, heavy boots, tool belts that clink softly as they move. But the fabric shifts color constantly, cycling through shades that have no names, and their tools... their tools are wrong. Hammers with heads shaped like teardrops. Saws whose teeth are tiny, perfect spirals. Levels that measure angles that shouldn't exist.

They are responsible for the ruins—not destroying them, but building them into their current impossible states. The bridge to nowhere was their masterpiece of purposeful incompletion. They construct with the same methodical care others might use to demolish, each missing piece as deliberately placed as every stone that remains. Their work follows blueprints written in languages of pure structure, plans that call for buildings to be exactly as broken as they are meant to be.

The Builders work in perfect silence save for the soft sounds of their tools against stone and wood. They appear at no set schedule, sometimes laboring for days without pause, sometimes absent for weeks. When they work, time bends around them—you might watch a Builder place a single stone and turn away for a moment, only to find an entire wall completed when you look back.

**The Keepers of Pools**

At the edge of sight, always peripheral, the Keepers tend their circular waters. They might be glimpsed as ripples that move against any wind, or as briefly-seen figures walking beneath the surface of pools only six inches deep. The Keepers are guardians of the reflections that show other worlds—curators of visions, librarians of impossible views.

Each Keeper tends exactly seven pools, no more, no fewer. They are bound to these waters as surely as roots to soil, and if you watch long enough, patient enough, you might see them: figures of indeterminate shape that seem to be made of reflection itself, all silver surface and liquid light. Their faces are the faces shown in their pools—sometimes human, sometimes not, always changing as the reflected scenes shift from burning deserts to star-filled voids.

The Keepers maintain the delicate balance that keeps each pool showing its designated elsewhere. Too much attention from observers can destabilize the visions, causing pools to show only their own murky depths, or worse, to reflect things that should not be seen. When this happens, the Keepers must perform careful rituals of restoration, dancing in patterns that mirror the mathematical formations the Geometers create, their movements causing the proper reflections to slowly reform.

**The Willow-Weavers**

In the twisted trees, another form of life persists. The Willow-Weavers are creatures of bark and sap, of living wood that has achieved something approaching consciousness. They inhabit the helixed trunks, their essence flowing through the amber tears that weep from the spiral cuts.

These beings exist as pure craftsmanship given life. They are the sculptors of their own bodies, constantly reshaping the trees they inhabit, growing their wooden forms into ever more elaborate configurations. The roots that arch above canopies, the trunks that twist into perfect mathematical forms—these are not accidents of growth but conscious artistry.

The Willow-Weavers think in seasons compressed into moments, in growth patterns that follow rules of aesthetics rather than biology. They communicate through the release of sap, each amber drop carrying chemical messages that can be read by others of their kind. Their conversations might span decades, single thoughts expressed through the slow transformation of entire grove formations.

When the harmonic tones sound from deep underground, the Willow-Weavers respond by altering their growth patterns, adding new spirals to their bark, extending roots in directions that somehow harmonize with the subterranean music. They are living tuning forks, their wooden bodies resonating with frequencies that help maintain the strange stability of the Hollows.

**The Drift-Swimmers**

In the impossible sea overhead, the Drift-Swimmers navigate currents of meaning rather than water. These creatures—if creatures they are—appear as amalgams of form and function, their bodies shifting between states like living liquid given temporary shape. They possess too many fins because they need to move through more than three dimensions. Their forms flow like mercury because in their realm, the distinction between substance and space becomes negotiable.

The Drift-Swimmers serve as messengers between the water-sky and the drowned earth below. They carry information in their bodies—literally incorporating data into their shifting forms, becoming living libraries of everything that has ever fallen as rain in the Hollows. Each droplet that descends carries messages from above, instructions for the Geometers, inspiration for the Builders, new coordinates for the pools the Keepers tend.

Sometimes, when conditions align in ways that follow no calendar, the Drift-Swimmers descend briefly into the air-space of the Hollows. During these visitations, they appear as impossible things: creatures of pure motion that leave trails of displaced reality in their wake, beings so adapted to their inverted ocean that they seem to swim through solid air as easily as they navigate their overhead currents.

**The Echoing Ones**

Deep below, beneath even the deepest foundations of the ruins, something vast and patient operates the machinery of reality. The Echoing Ones exist in the spaces between seconds, in the pause between one gear-turn and the next. They are felt rather than seen, known by their influence rather than their presence.

These beings—if they are beings rather than forces given form—speak in the language of pure structure. Every harmonic tone that rises from the depths is a word in their ongoing conversation, a dialogue conducted across geological time scales about the proper shape of reality. They are the ones who decide which laws of physics will apply where, who determine that water should hang overhead while earth pools below.

The Echoing Ones might be glimpsed in moments of perfect stillness, when even the water-drops pause in their falling. In those suspended seconds, shadows move in the depths of the deepest pools—not reflections of anything in this world, but cast by forms moving in the spaces beneath space, massive and purposeful and utterly alien.

**The Visitors**

And then there are those who do not belong, who arrive and vanish according to no schedule that residents of the Hollows recognize. The silver-feathered herons that dissolve at the touch of light that isn't there. The six-legged deer that treat water as solid ground. The schools of flying fish that swim through air and earth with equal ease.

These Visitors exist at the intersection of what is and what might be. They are refugees from other configurations of reality, creatures who found their way to the Hollows during moments when the barriers between worlds grew thin. They cannot stay long—their forms are too alien to the particular impossibility that governs this place—but they serve as reminders that the Hollows is not the only place where reality has been taken apart and reassembled according to different rules.

The Visitors appear when the work of the other inhabitants reaches certain thresholds, when their patient geometric progressions achieve temporary harmony. In those moments, the Hollows becomes briefly permeable to other forms of existence, other ways of being impossible.

**The Harmonious Cycle**

All of these beings exist in careful balance, each serving functions in a vast, patient work that has no name because it exists beyond the reach of language. The Geometers create the patterns that guide reality's restructuring. The Builders construct ruins that serve as anchor points for impossibility. The Keepers maintain windows to elsewhere that prevent the Hollows from becoming too isolated. The Willow-Weavers grow living architecture that resonates with deeper harmonies. The Drift-Swimmers carry information between levels of existence. The Echoing Ones conduct the grand symphony of restructured natural law. And the Visitors remind them all that change is possible, that even the impossible can be reimagined.

Together, they have created a place where water hangs overhead not through magic but through the patient application of different rules, consistently applied. The Hollows is not chaotic but profoundly ordered—just ordered according to principles that emerged from the collaboration between beings whose very existence requires reality to be more flexible than most places allow.

They are all, in their various ways, artists of possibility, sculptors of the fundamental, musicians whose medium is the basic structure of what can be. And their great work continues, one perfect spiral at a time, one impossible angle at a time, one carefully placed stone in a bridge that leads exactly where it is supposed to lead: nowhere at all, which in the Hollows, is somewhere very specific indeed.
