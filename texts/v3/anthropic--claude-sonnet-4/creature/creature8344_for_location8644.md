In the deepest hours before dawn, when the mechanical symphony softens to a whisper, they emerge from the steam vents like living smoke. The Tenders are what the foundries have made of human flesh over generations—tall, willowy beings whose skin has taken on the lustrous quality of fired ceramic, mottled in the same burnt oranges and deep ochres as the terraces they tend. Their eyes have adapted to the metal-laden air, pupils dilated wide and irises shot through with flecks of copper and steel that catch and reflect the forge-glow.

They move with fluid precision between the automated systems, their elongated fingers—joints fused smooth by decades of heat exposure—adjusting pressures, redirecting flows, maintaining the ancient programs with an intuition that borders on communion. The Tenders no longer speak in words but in harmonics, their voices pitched to resonate with the industrial rhythms, able to diagnose a failing bearing by the shift in its song or predict a steam surge by the change in the air's metallic taste.

Their hair has long since transformed into something between organic fiber and wire, collecting the ambient electrical fields and giving them a prescient awareness of the machinery's moods. They wear simple garments woven from the salt-grass fibers, garments that shimmer with accumulated metal dust and serve as both clothing and instrument, chiming softly as they move to add their own notes to the foundry's endless composition.

The Tenders reproduce through a process as mysterious as it is purposeful—their children born already adapted, emerging from crystalline cocoons that form in the warmest steam chambers. These young ones, called Sparks, learn by osmosis, absorbing the mechanical knowledge through their skin as they sleep curled against the humming pipes.

But the Tenders are not alone. In the shadows between the great machines lurk the Remnant—fragments of the original workers' consciousness that have somehow bonded with the automated systems. They exist as patterns of electrical activity, ghosts in the machine that occasionally manifest as brief distortions in the air, faces formed from steam and metal dust that mouth warnings or instructions before dissipating. The Tenders commune with these spirits, receiving guidance about maintenance cycles and the location of rare components.

The corvids that visit are more than scavengers—they are messengers, carrying information between isolated communities of Tenders scattered across other industrial ruins. Their evolved beaks can crack more than mineral casings; they can access data ports on the older machines, downloading fragments of code that they carry in specialized throat pouches to share with distant foundries.

In the salt-grass meadows that sprout between machine clusters, smaller creatures have found their niche. The Resonants are rodent-like beings whose bones have hollowed and hardened into natural tuning forks. They scurry through the grass, their movements creating cascades of metallic music as they brush against the crystalline blades. They feed on the mineral deposits and have become living components of the foundry's acoustic system, their colony songs helping to regulate the deeper mechanical rhythms.

Most mysterious are the things that live in the pipes themselves—the Flow-Kin, serpentine entities made of living metal and superheated gas. They are rarely seen, existing as movement within the pipe networks, but their presence can be felt as sudden temperature fluctuations, as pressures that shouldn't exist, as the occasional glimpse of quicksilver coiling through transparent sections of conduit. The Tenders speak of them with reverence, believing they are the foundry's immune system, keeping the ancient machinery pure and functional.

At the basin's edges, where ceramic meets stone, dwell the Boundary Watchers—beings who chose to resist the foundry's transformative influence. They maintain some semblance of original human form but pay for this with constant vigilance, using mirrors and filtered masks to avoid the hypnotic patterns of the machinery. They serve as traders and chroniclers, recording the slow changes in the Tenders and occasionally guiding lost travelers safely around the basin's periphery.

All of these inhabitants exist in a delicate ecosystem of metal and flesh, heat and harmony. They are bound together not by traditional biological relationships but by the foundry's endless song—each playing their part in keeping the great machine dreaming, even as none remember what it was originally built to create.
