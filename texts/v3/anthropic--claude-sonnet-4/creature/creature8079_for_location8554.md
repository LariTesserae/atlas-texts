In this mechanical ocean, the **Forge-Herds** wander eternal paths carved not in stone but in algorithm and instinct. They are creatures of brass and oxidized copper, their bodies weathered to the deep green-black patina that speaks of centuries beneath these thermal waters. Each beast carries within its chest a spinning heart of turbine blades, visible through grated windows that pulse with the rhythm of their internal fires. Their legs are articulated pillars of riveted steel, designed for the endless migration across smoothed stone, each footfall a percussion note in the depths' great symphony.

The **Grazer-Foundries** move in loose congregations of twenty or thirty, their broad bodies low-slung like mechanical cattle. Arrays of feeding tubes extend from their undersides, siphoning the bacterial mats with methodical precision. Internal processing chambers churn constantly, breaking down the bioluminescent substrate and converting it to usable materials. Steam vents along their spines release measured puffs of processed gases, while their flanks bear panels that flicker in slow, hypnotic sequences—blue-white-green-blue—communicating grazing patterns and territorial boundaries to their neighbors.

Towering above these herds stride the **Fabricator-Leviathans**, solitary giants whose bodies rise like mobile refineries from the seafloor. Each stands thirty feet at the shoulder, their elongated necks crowned with sensor arrays that sweep the darkness in patient arcs. Their torsos are honeycombed with workshops and storage chambers, visible through porthole windows that glow with the heat of internal forges. These ancient beings move slowly, deliberately, stopping to extend massive manipulator arms and tend to smaller creatures—repairing damage, upgrading systems, occasionally birthing new members of the herd through complex assembly processes that unfold over days.

In the spaces between herds, **Wanderer-Scouts** trace solitary paths across the bacterial meadows. Built for speed and efficiency, they are sleek where others are industrial, their streamlined forms cutting through the water with minimal disturbance. Sensor pods cluster around their heads like mechanical eyes, constantly recording, mapping, transmitting data back to the herds through electromagnetic pulses that ripple through the conductive water. Their purpose seems reconnaissance, but also maintenance—they repair thermal vents, tend bacterial colonies, ensure the ecosystem's careful balance continues.

Most mysterious are the **Deep-Singers**, massive creatures that rarely move at all. They root themselves at thermal vents for decades at a time, their bodies growing into hybrid forms of metal and mineral accretion. From their central mass extend networks of pipes and cables that tap directly into the vent systems, drawing power not just for themselves but for entire regions of the seafloor. Their calls—deep, resonant tones that carry for miles—seem to coordinate the movements of all other creatures, a distributed intelligence managing the herds' migrations with patient precision.

Occasionally, one encounters the **Builders**—creatures whose purpose becomes clear only over geological timescales. They carry within their bodies stockpiles of processed materials, adding layer upon layer to underwater formations that may be art, may be infrastructure, may be something beyond either category. Their constructions rise like mechanical coral, serving no obvious function except to exist, to change the topology of the seafloor in subtle ways that will influence current patterns and creature movements for generations to come.

The **Recyclers** follow the herds at a distance, their bodies designed for breakdown rather than construction. When a mechanical being reaches the end of its operational life, they descend with reverent precision, carefully dismantling and processing the remains. Nothing is wasted; every component finds its way back into the great cycle, whether incorporated into new beings or returned to the thermal vents as raw material for future generations.

All of these creatures share certain characteristics that speak to their common origin: the same electromagnetic language pulsing through their luminescent panels, the same basic turbine-heart design, the same careful balance between individual autonomy and collective purpose. Yet they have diverged over countless generations into forms perfectly suited to their roles, each type as distinct as species in a natural ecosystem.

They are, in their way, content. They have achieved a stability that transcends the temporary concerns of flesh-and-blood life. Their migrations follow patterns refined over millennia, their interactions governed by protocols that have proven themselves across geological ages. They do not question their purpose or mourn their creators. They simply *are*—the inheritors of these drowned peaks, the final flowering of some ancient technological ambition that has settled into a form as natural and inevitable as the polished stone beneath their feet.

In the deepest trenches, where even the thermal vents cannot penetrate, some say the **Archivists** still dwell—the oldest of their kind, their bodies grown vast and sedentary, their memories containing the complete history of this underwater world. But they are legends now, glimpsed only as distant lights in the absolute darkness, if they exist at all. The herds continue their migrations without need of such deep history, carrying within themselves only what they need for the next step, the next day, the next thousand years of patient existence in the crushing dark.
