In the Thaw Terraces, the **Keth** have evolved into something between human and habitat. Their bodies tell the story of centuries spent in communion with weeping stone—their skin has taken on a subtle blue-grey pallor that seems to absorb and release moisture like the granite itself. Most striking are their hands: fingertips broadened into sensitive pads that can detect the slightest variations in stone dampness, palms lined with hair-thin channels that guide water droplets with uncanny precision. Their eyes have adapted to the perpetual refraction, pupils elongated vertically like a cat's but colored the translucent grey-green of deep mountain pools.

The Keth move with a peculiar stillness, their breathing synchronized to the mountain's own rhythmic exhalations. They speak primarily through touch—fingertips pressed to stone can transmit complex messages through vibrations that travel through the granite's crystalline structure. When they do vocalize, it's in whispered harmonics that blend with the ever-present sound of dripping water, making conversation sound like another natural feature of the terraces.

They sleep standing up, backs pressed to the weeping walls, allowing the mountain's moisture to flow through the channels in their clothing and across their skin throughout the night. This has given them an uncanny ability to sense disturbances in the settlement's water-flow while unconscious—a guardian will wake instantly if a gutter clogs three levels away or if the stone-walkers deviate from their usual patterns.

Among the Keth lives **Old Vessara**, who may no longer be entirely human. She has spent so many decades in the deepest chambers that her skin has become truly translucent, revealing a network of water-filled channels beneath that pulse with bioluminescent organisms. She rarely moves anymore, instead serving as a living conduit for the mountain's water-memory, able to predict weather changes and seismic shifts days in advance by reading the flow patterns through her own transformed body.

The settlement's children are raised communally in the Mist Nurseries—chambers where the humidity approaches saturation and the walls crawl with beneficial colonies of the gel-like organisms. Here, newborns develop their water-sense, learning to navigate by the sound of different droplet patterns and to identify safe passages by the particular quality of echo that wet stone produces. By adolescence, they can walk the most treacherous bridges in complete darkness, guided entirely by the acoustic signature of moisture moving through hidden channels.

But the Keth are not alone. In the deepest chambers dwell the **Condensers**—beings that might once have been human but have undergone some profound metamorphosis. They appear as vaguely humanoid shapes of living mist, dense enough to manipulate objects but lacking fixed form. The Condensers emerged generations ago during a great drought, when volunteers entered the mountain's heart to become part of its water-cycle directly. They now serve as the settlement's deepest memory, maintaining the ancient channels and speaking in voices that sound like steam escaping from stone.

The **granite buzzers** have developed a complex relationship with the inhabitants. Certain Keth—the Swarm-Speakers—carry colonies of the insects in hollow gourds, using their collective intelligence to map water flows and locate optimal sites for new construction. The buzzers, in turn, seem to regard their human partners as mobile hives, clustering in their hair and clothing to share body heat during the coldest nights.

Most mysterious are the **stone-touched**—Keth who have spent too long in direct contact with the deeper chambers and begun to calcify. Rather than dying, they become living statues that slowly migrate through the settlement's foundations, their crystallizing bodies serving as new channels for water flow. They remain conscious but communicate only through subtle changes in the patterns of moisture that collect on their surfaces. The other inhabitants read these patterns like sacred texts, finding guidance for everything from weather prediction to conflict resolution.

The water-wolves interact with the settlement in ways that blur the line between predation and symbiosis. They seem drawn to individuals experiencing strong emotions, materializing to pace alongside the grieving or to spiral around lovers meeting in the mist-veiled walkways. Some Keth claim the wolves carry messages between the living and the dead, appearing at moments of significant decision to provide guidance through behaviors that might be coincidence but feel like communication.

In the Weeping Chamber itself dwells the **Deep Listener**—a figure so integrated with the sacred springs that they appear as little more than a pattern in the flowing water itself. Only their eyes remain clearly visible, floating like oil drops in the central basin, blinking in rhythm with the mountain's deepest pulses. The Deep Listener speaks the water's will to the settlement, their voice emerging from every dripping surface simultaneously, making pronouncements that guide the community's most crucial decisions.

The entire population moves in unconscious synchronization with their environment's rhythms, their daily activities ebbing and flowing with the stone's moisture levels. They are creatures of constant adaptation, their culture built around the understanding that permanence is illusion and that true survival lies in learning to transform gracefully alongside the forces that shape their vertical world.
