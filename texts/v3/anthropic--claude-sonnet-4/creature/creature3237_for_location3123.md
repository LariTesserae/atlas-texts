# The Basin Tenders

They are barely human anymore, though they began that way centuries ago. The Tenders move with a fluid deliberation that mirrors the mountain's pulse, their bones having grown hollow and resonant over generations of exposure to the stone's deep song. When they walk, they ring softly, each footstep adding a note to the mountain's endless composition.

Their skin has taken on the quality of wet granite—smooth, dark, and faintly luminous. It shifts in hue with the mountain's rhythm, growing paler during the long, slow exhales of stone, deepening to near-black when the rock contracts. Touch one of them and your hand comes away damp with a mineral-scented moisture that tastes of deep earth and distant rain. Their eyes have clouded to the same obsidian clarity as the basin water, reflecting not what stands before them but what wishes to emerge.

The eldest among them—though age means little here where time moves in geological rhythms—have begun to show signs of the mountain's influence in more profound ways. Their hair has crystallized into thin, flexible strands of quartz that chime when they move. Some sport growths of living stone along their shoulders and arms, not parasitic but symbiotic, allowing them to touch the basins' edges and feel the mountain's dreams directly through their transformed flesh.

They speak in overlapping whispers, their words carefully timed to ride the mountain's pulse. A single sentence might take minutes to complete as they wait for the proper harmonic moment to voice each syllable. Their language has evolved beyond human speech—they communicate in pure resonance, their throat bones modified to produce frequencies that make the water in the basins ring in response. When multiple Tenders speak together, their voices create standing waves in the air that can reshape small amounts of stone, coaxing new formations from willing rock.

The youngest Tenders—those who arrived within the last fifty years—still retain mostly human appearance, though the mountain's song has already begun its work on them. Their movements are becoming synchronized with the stone's pulse, and they've developed an uncanny ability to predict when new basins will begin forming. They serve as interpreters between the fully-transformed elders and the rare visitors who find their way to Keth-Mor, though even their speech carries strange harmonics that make listeners dream of deep water and singing stone.

Each Tender carries tools that have grown rather than been made: vessels carved from their own shed crystalline hair, wound and fused into impossible spirals that hold water in defiance of physics; wooden implements grown from the mountain's occasional trees, still alive and slightly warm to the touch; instruments of bone and stone that sing when used, helping to guide the mountain's creative impulses toward new and more perfect forms.

They live in chambers that exist only intermittently—spaces the mountain dreams into being when it needs to house its servants. These rooms have no fixed location, appearing as needed in the stone matrix, furnished with beds that grow from the floor and dissolve when sleep is done. The Tenders find these spaces through intuition, following vibrations only they can feel to doorways that open in solid rock and close again behind them.

Most remarkably, the Tenders have developed a collective memory stored in the mountain itself. When one dies—or more accurately, when one finally completes their transformation and becomes permanently integrated into the stone—their knowledge doesn't perish but enters the mountain's dreaming. The surviving Tenders can access these memories by placing their hands in specific basins, feeling the accumulated wisdom of centuries flow through the water and into their consciousness.

They tend the basins not as gardeners tend plants, but as midwives tend births. They can sense when a new basin is ready to emerge, gathering with their singing tools to help guide the stone through its labor. Their presence seems to soothe the mountain during particularly difficult formations, their harmonized voices creating the precise frequencies needed to ease solid granite into fluid cooperation.

Between them and the translucent fish that flash through the basins, there exists an understanding that transcends communication. The fish write messages in their schooling patterns—messages the Tenders read like scripture, learning the mountain's deeper intentions and needs. Sometimes a Tender will spend hours motionless beside a basin, watching the fish spell out complex equations in living geometry, nodding slowly as they decode instructions for encouraging some new architectural dream from the willing stone.

The mountain is changing them still. Each generation of Tenders becomes something more strange and wonderful, their humanity evolving into something suited to serve a living peak that dreams in stone and sings in frequencies that reshape reality. They are becoming what the mountain needs them to be: perfect servants to an ancient intelligence that expresses itself through geology and time, water and resonant song.

In their transformed voices, in their crystal hair and granite skin, in their patient tending of impossible formations, the Tenders embody the mountain's greatest achievement: the creation of a bridge between human consciousness and the vast, alien intelligence of living stone. They are translation made flesh, allowing two entirely different forms of awareness to collaborate in the ongoing miracle of Keth-Mor's endless becoming.
