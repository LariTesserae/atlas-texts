In this place of eternal respiration, life has learned to dance with impossibility.

During the held moment at the peak of each inhalation, when the earth hangs suspended between breathing in and breathing out, the Tide Walkers emit complex clicking patterns with their throats. These communications ripple through the ground itself, creating messages that other Tide Walkers can feel through their feet miles away. They have no permanent settlements—how could they, when the very concept of permanent is negotiated anew with each breath? Instead, they follow the deep currents of the breathing patterns, reading the earth's mood through subtle changes in rhythm and pressure.

The **Glass Fish** inhabit the trembling pools, though "inhabit" suggests a permanency that doesn't exist here. They are creatures of pure adaptation, their transparent bodies capable of existing in three states: as fluid beings when the pools are full, as crystalline statues when the water retreats, and as something between gas and liquid during the transition moments. During inhalations, they school together in complex geometric patterns that seem to solve equations written in the water's interference patterns. Elder beetles, whose shells have grown thick with accumulated harmonics, can replay breathing patterns from decades past, teaching younger generations the deep rhythms that underlie the apparent chaos of the Flats' respiration. The elongated birds—the **Breath Riders**—have evolved beyond the need for individual consciousness as humans understand it. They sing of the time before breathing, when the land lay still and silent, and of the first breath that changed everything. Among all these breathing-adapted lives move the **Wandering Witnesses**—beings that might once have been human, or might be human still, depending on how one defines the term.
