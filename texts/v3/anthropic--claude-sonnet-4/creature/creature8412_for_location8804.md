**The Inhabitants of Rookery Gorge**

In this impossible sanctuary, creatures exist in defiance of geography and sense. The seabirds that carpet every surface are not quite what they appear from a distance. Up close, their eyes hold depths that reflect not sky but something darker—the volcanic glass of their chosen home seeming to have seeped into their very souls. These are guillemots that have never seen the ocean, yet their webbed feet are perpetually damp with brine that seeps from somewhere deeper than the mountain's heart. Their calls carry harmonics of waves against distant shores, though no memory of sea-sound should live in their hollow bones.

The murres press together in their thousands, but each bird maintains a territory measured in millimeters, defended with a fierce precision that borders on the obsessive. They have developed an intricate social hierarchy based on proximity to the weeping walls—those closest to the perpetual moisture hold the highest status, their feathers bearing a subtle iridescence that marks them as the gorge's chosen. When they spread their wings, tiny droplets scatter like benedictions over the lower ranks.

Among them move the cormorants, darker and more serpentine, their necks extending and retracting with a mechanical rhythm as they scan for threats that never come. These birds have grown strange in the perpetual twilight—their wings span wider than their oceanic cousins, adapted for navigating the narrow air between the obsidian walls. Some have lost the ability to fly altogether, instead climbing the mirror-smooth surfaces with an uncanny agility, their talons finding purchase where none should exist.

In the hidden pools below, where the mountain's tears collect before vanishing into the scree, dwell creatures that should not exist at such altitude. Seals with coats of charcoal black surface occasionally, their whiskers twitching as they taste air that has never known salt spray. These are not the seals of any earthly ocean—they are sleeker, more elongated, their movements carrying an unsettling grace as if they swim through liquid obsidian rather than water. Their eyes, when they surface, reflect the gorge's strange light with an intelligence that seems to recognize the impossibility of their existence.

But it is the nearly invisible inhabitants that truly rule this place. In every crack where the stunted vegetation clings, minute creatures tend their impossible gardens. They are translucent things, no larger than rice grains, with bodies that seem crafted from the same condensed moisture that weeps from the walls. These gardener-spirits move with deliberate slowness, their crystalline limbs manipulating the growth of moss and lichen with a precision that borders on the artistic. They communicate through vibrations in the stone, their conversations adding an almost subliminal harmony to the gorge's overwhelming symphony.

The dwarf willows themselves seem more sentient than plant, their twisted forms responding to the presence of observers with subtle movements that suggest awareness. Their roots delve not just into stone cracks but into something deeper—perhaps into the dreams of the mountain itself. When the wind moves through their miniature branches, it carries whispers in languages that predate the volcanic birth of their home.

At night, when the birds settle into an approximation of rest, other inhabitants emerge. Shadows that are more than absence of light drift along the obsidian walls, their forms occasionally solidifying into creatures that appear to be made from volcanic glass and starlight. They glide between the roosting birds without disturbing a single feather, their purpose unknown but their presence adding another layer to the gorge's inexplicable ecology.

And threading through all of this life is something that might once have been human. It appears rarely, and only in the peripheral vision of the most sensitive visitors—a figure that seems to be part of the weeping stone itself, tending to the impossible sanctuary with movements that suggest it has been here since the mountain first cracked open to birth this narrow world. It knows the name of every bird, the secret paths of every shadow, the dreams of every lichen-patch. It is the gorge's memory made manifest, ensuring that this pocket of impossibility continues to thrive in defiance of all natural law.

Together, these inhabitants create an ecosystem that exists outside the normal flow of time and causation, sustained by forces that flow from the heart of the obsidian mountain and maintained by a collective will that transforms the impossible into the inevitable.
