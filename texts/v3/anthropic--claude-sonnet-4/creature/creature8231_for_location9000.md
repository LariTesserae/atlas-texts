In the Ossuary Garden dwells the Keeper—though whether it is one being or many, whether it was once the builders themselves or something that grew from their absence, remains unclear. The Keeper exists primarily as presence rather than form, manifesting most often as a gentle disturbance in the suspended droplets, a slight warming of the cool air, or the faintest impression of breath against the back of one's neck.

When it does take shape, the Keeper appears as a figure woven from the silver mist itself, tall and impossibly thin, with limbs that seem to flow like slow water. Its face, when glimpsed, resembles the negative space between raindrops—not quite there, but undeniably present. It moves with the same patient rhythm as the weeping walls, each gesture taking minutes to complete, each step a careful placement that disturbs nothing yet acknowledges everything.

The Keeper tends the moss with hands that pass through matter as easily as air, encouraging the spirals to grow in their perfect patterns. It knows each tuft by the frequency of its pulse, each strand by the particular mineral it has learned to cherish. Sometimes visitors catch glimpses of it crouched beside a pool, fingers trailing through the collected tears, reading messages in the mineral sediment like others might read tea leaves.

It speaks rarely, and when it does, the words arrive not as sound but as understanding—concepts that settle into consciousness like sediment settling in still water. The Keeper remembers the builders, not as individuals but as intention, as the collective dream that shaped this place. It recalls their final days, when they chose transformation over ending, dissolution over death, becoming the very substance of their sanctuary.

The translucent creatures that drift through the garden are extensions of the Keeper's attention—tiny fragments of its awareness that have learned independent motion. They serve as its eyes and ears, though what they observe is not sight or sound but the slow mathematics of decay and renewal, the precise chemistry of transformation. Through them, the Keeper monitors the imperceptible shifting of bone to stone, the gradual deepening of the carved channels, the minute changes in the suspended moisture's patterns.

Occasionally, other beings find their way to the Ossuary Garden—refugees from other folded spaces, wanderers displaced by time, or seekers drawn by half-remembered dreams. The Keeper greets them with the same gentle presence, offering neither welcome nor dismissal but simple acknowledgment. It provides what the garden itself provides: coolness, moisture, the strange comfort of deep time, and the understanding that all things are constantly becoming something else.

Some visitors stay for what feels like hours but proves to be years. Others remain for what seems like centuries but discover upon leaving that mere moments have passed. The Keeper makes no distinction—it exists in all moments simultaneously, patient as the bone walls, persistent as the falling droplets, eternal as the slow song the building sings to itself.

In the deepest hours, when the fractured sky above shows strange constellations through the gaps, the Keeper sometimes grows more solid, more present. During these times, it tends to tasks that exist only in the liminal space between need and fulfillment—adjusting the flow of tears so they carve their channels in more pleasing harmonies, encouraging the moss to spiral in configurations that will be beautiful centuries hence, or simply sitting in contemplation beside the pools, adding its own patient breath to the garden's eternal rhythm.

The Keeper is the memory of the place made manifest, the consciousness that emerged from the builders' final gift to time itself. It is both guardian and prisoner, caretaker and inhabitant, the living embodiment of the sanctuary's purpose: to provide a space where ending and beginning dance together in the slow time of stone dreams and water songs.
