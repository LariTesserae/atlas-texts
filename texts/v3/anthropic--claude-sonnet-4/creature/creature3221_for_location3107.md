In the Forgotten Stair-City, life has learned to live lightly.

The **Mistwalkers** move through the upper reaches like living fog—beings so translucent they might be mistaken for condensation given form. They are tall and impossibly thin, their bodies seeming to draw substance from the very air they breathe. Their skin holds the pearl-grey luminescence of cloud-light, and when they pause—which they do often, sometimes for hours—they become nearly indistinguishable from the weathered statues that dot the terraces. They tend the ancient water channels with reverence, their elongated fingers tracing the carved routes that guide moisture from level to level. The Mistwalkers never speak above a whisper, and even then, their voices sound like wind through stone. They are the city's gardeners and hydrologists, understanding instinctively where each droplet wishes to flow.

In the middle terraces, where the moss grows thickest, live the **Stoneweavers**—compact, patient beings whose skin has taken on the grey-green patina of the mountain itself. Over centuries, they have grown small ecosystems in their hair and the creases of their weathered hands: tiny lichens, microscopic gardens, symbiotic relationships with the spores that drift through the mist. They are the city's rememberers, carrying its history not in books or songs but in the patterns of growth they cultivate on their own bodies. Each Stoneweaver is a living archive, their skin a map of the districts they tend. They move slowly through their territories, encouraging growth in carefully chosen cracks, guiding the spread of moss to protect the most vulnerable stone from erosion.

The **Depthkeepers** inhabit the lowest levels, where shadow pools deepest and the stone still remembers the mountain's inner heat. These beings have grown long and limber, able to navigate the narrowest passages and deepest wells. Their eyes have adapted to the permanent twilight, becoming large and dark as still water, able to detect the slightest variations in moisture and temperature. They are the city's foundation-tenders, ensuring that the vast weight above remains stable, that the slow settling of stone happens in harmony rather than catastrophe. The Depthkeepers know every vibration, every stress point, every place where the mountain still shifts and dreams. They communicate through touch, their sensitive fingers reading messages in the stone that they pass from one to another through a network of tunnels known only to them.

Most mysterious are the **Echolings**—child-sized beings who appear briefly in distant glimpses before vanishing around corners or through doorways that seem to seal themselves afterward. They may be the young of one of the other peoples, or something else entirely. The Echolings seem to exist partially in memory, their footsteps heard long after they've passed, their laughter occasionally rippling through the acoustics of the deep courtyards though no source can be found. They are drawn to the places where the city's original inhabitants left the strongest impressions, and some theorize that they are those impressions, given just enough substance by the mountain's slow dreaming to briefly walk again.

All of these peoples share certain traits: they move with the measured patience of stone itself, they speak rarely and never loudly, and they exist in perfect equilibrium with their environment. They do not build new structures or alter the city's ancient design—instead, they have become part of its maintenance, its preservation, its slow conversation with time. They eat sparingly: moisture gleaned from the air, nutrients drawn from the mineral-rich stones, energy absorbed from the subtle temperature differences between levels. 

They reproduce slowly, if at all, preferring to tend those who already exist rather than create new life. When one of them reaches the end of their extremely long life cycle, they simply find a suitable niche in the stone and settle there, becoming part of the architecture itself—though whether this is death or transformation, none can say.

The peoples of the Stair-City have learned that survival here means becoming part of something larger than themselves. They are not the city's masters but its symbionts, its quiet partners in the long work of enduring. In their presence, the city lives—not with the bustling energy of a human settlement, but with the deep, slow pulse of geological time made manifest in careful hands and patient hearts.

On rare occasions, when the mist clears just enough and the light falls just right, you might see them all together: the Mistwalkers flowing through the upper reaches, the Stoneweavers tending their gardens, the Depthkeepers emerging from their tunnels, and the Echolings dancing between the shadows. In such moments, the Forgotten Stair-City reveals itself as anything but forgotten—it is profoundly, intentionally, lovingly inhabited by those who have made themselves worthy of its vast and ancient trust.
