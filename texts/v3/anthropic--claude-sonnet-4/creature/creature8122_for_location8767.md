The Carved Reaches pulse with life that has grown as intricate as the stonework itself.

**The Stone-Singers** flow through the primary arteries in great communal rivers. Tall and willowy, their bodies have elongated over generations to navigate the vertical maze with fluid grace. Their skin bears faint ridged patterns that mirror the carved channels around them—not mere mimicry, but evolutionary adaptation. When they move in groups, their footsteps create rhythmic percussion that harmonizes with the wind's song. They've learned to read the city's moods through sound alone, navigating by acoustic landmarks and following the stone's guidance through passages that shift subtly with the seasons. Their voices, when raised together, can make entire sections of the Reaches resonate with purpose.

In the higher spires dwell **the Breath-Weavers**—smaller, more compact beings whose lungs have adapted to the thin air. Their breath is visible even in moderate temperatures, forming elaborate patterns that they've turned into both art and communication. They tend the crystal lenses and wind-channels, adjusting the city's breathing with delicate interventions. Their hands are scarred from centuries of stonework, each family bearing responsibility for specific acoustic chambers. They rarely descend to the lower levels, claiming the heavy air below makes them sluggish and deaf to the stone's subtler voices.

The middle reaches teem with **the Aggregate**—collective beings that exist as flowing colonies rather than individuals. What appear to be swarms of small creatures are actually components of larger consciousnesses that think in distributed patterns. They maintain the water collection systems, their countless tiny bodies able to reach into the finest carved capillaries to clear blockages or adjust flow. When they need to communicate with the singular beings around them, hundreds will briefly coalesce into rough humanoid shapes, their chittering voices speaking in unison before dissolving back into purposeful streams.

In the deepest foundations move **the Hollow Ones**. Pale and soft-footed, they've lost their eyes over generations but gained something else—the ability to feel the city's structural tensions through their skin and bones. They are the memory-keepers, their bodies storing the vibrations of every chisel-strike their ancestors made. When they place their hands on the stone, they can read its history, knowing which sections were carved in haste, which in meditation, which in sorrow. They guide the others away from instabilities and toward the strongest paths, though they rarely explain their knowledge in words the surface-dwellers understand.

Moving between all levels are **the Hollow-Touched**—individuals of various species who have spent too long listening to the deepest chambers. They carry messages between the different communities, but their minds have been subtly altered by prolonged exposure to the city's more unsettling harmonics. They speak in overlapping whispers, their conversations threading through multiple topics simultaneously. Other inhabitants treat them with respectful wariness, knowing they're necessary but changed.

And there are others, glimpsed in peripheral vision: **the Echo-Children** who exist only in the reverberations between carved surfaces, taking temporary form when sound waves align just right; **the Patient Ones** who sit motionless in meditation chambers for decades, slowly becoming part of the stone itself; **the Drift-Shepherds** who ride the wind currents through the largest galleries, their translucent forms barely distinguishable from the air itself.

All these beings share certain traits born of their environment. Their movements have become unconsciously choreographed, following patterns carved into the stone and into their very instincts. They waste nothing—every breath becomes part of the water cycle, every sound contributes to the city's symphony. They've developed an intricate gift-economy based on breath, sound, and movement rather than traditional resources.

But perhaps most remarkably, they've begun to merge with their city in ways that blur the line between inhabitant and architecture. New chambers appear periodically, carved by sleepwalking citizens whose hands move of their own accord. The city grows through them, using their bodies as extensions of its will. They don't resist this—they've come to understand themselves as temporary expressions of something far larger and more patient than individual consciousness.

The Carved Reaches didn't just shape them. They became its living components, thinking thoughts carved in stone, breathing dreams of perfect acoustic mathematics, flowing together in patterns too vast for any single mind to comprehend.
