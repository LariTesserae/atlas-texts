In the spaces between stone tears and probability architecture, the Keth'mori have made their dwelling for epochs unmeasured. They are a people shaped by permanence, their forms reflecting the patient geometry of their impossible home.

The Keth'mori appear, at first distant glance, almost familiar—tall, graceful figures that might pass for unusually elegant humans in poor light. But proximity reveals the subtle wrongness that marks all things of the Weeping Plateau. Their skin holds the same faint translucency as their city, shifting between solid presence and gossamer suggestion depending on the angle of observation. When they stand perfectly still, which they do with supernatural frequency, they become nearly invisible—not through camouflage, but through a kind of existential discretion, as if they've learned to temporarily excuse themselves from the act of being observed.

Their eyes are the color of condensation itself—that perfect clarity that exists in the moment between vapor and droplet. These eyes never blink in the conventional sense, but occasionally phase in and out of focus, like camera lenses adjusting to capture light from different dimensional frequencies. When a Keth'mori looks directly at something, that thing often becomes slightly more real, more definite in its boundaries. They are a people who see by conferring existence.

The Keth'mori move through their world with the deliberate precision of ritual, each gesture following patterns worn smooth by millennia of repetition. They walk the moisture-paths between weeping stones in single file, their bare feet finding purchase on surfaces that would be treacherously slick to any other species. Their clothing appears to be woven from light itself—garments that exist as pure geometry, following the mathematical perfection that governs their landscape. Robes that shift through impossible colors, always complementing whatever hue the weeping stones are reflecting in any given moment.

They are gardeners of reality. The Keth'mori tend the mathematical arrangements of vegetation not through tools or traditional cultivation, but by gentle suggestion. A Keth'mori might spend entire seasons sitting beside a spiral fern, not touching it, barely even looking at it, but somehow encouraging its growth through presence alone. They whisper to the crystalline insects in frequencies that match the stone-song, maintaining the delicate harmonies that keep their world stable. The elk-like creatures with too many eyes appear and disappear in response to the Keth'mori's collective attention—existing more fully when observed with proper reverence, fading when ignored.

Their society operates on principles of consensual reality. The Keth'mori govern themselves through a process called the Deep Agreement—a form of democracy that transcends mere voting. When decisions must be made, the entire population enters a shared meditative state, gradually converging on solutions through a process that resembles crystallization more than debate. Their conclusions emerge with the same mathematical inevitability as the moss circles around weeping stones. Leadership, when it exists at all, rotates according to patterns as ancient and complex as the city's architecture.

The Keth'mori do not age in any conventional sense. Instead, they accumulate existence. Young Keth'mori are barely visible, existing as faint suggestions that must concentrate to maintain corporeal form. As they mature, they become more substantial, more present, their translucency gradually solidifying into something approaching opacity. The oldest among them—the Deeply Present—have become so thoroughly real that they begin to influence the physics of their surroundings simply by existing. These elders can weep true water from their own eyes, adding their tears to the endless moisture cycle of their home.

They speak rarely, and when they do, their language consists of harmonics that blend with the ambient song of stone and crystal. Keth'mori conversation resembles the sound of wind through probability architecture—meaningful resonances that convey complex concepts through tonal relationships rather than discrete words. They have no concept of lies, not from any moral prohibition, but because their language is too directly connected to reality itself. To speak falsehood would risk unweaving the careful stability of their world.

The Keth'mori reproduce through a process of Shared Manifestation. When the time is right—a rightness determined by the same deep mathematics that governs their plateau—pairs or small groups will converge around one of the deeper weeping pools and begin a ritual of mutual visibility. Over the course of several seasons, their combined attention slowly precipitates new consciousness from the abundant moisture and ancient stone. Keth'mori children emerge not as infants but as barely perceptible possibilities, whispers of personhood that must learn to believe in their own existence before they can be seen by others.

They are scholars of duration, keepers of memory so vast and precise that they serve as living archives of their impossible realm. Each Keth'mori carries within their translucent form the complete record of every dewdrop's path, every wind pattern, every variation in the crystal-song. They do not actively study these phenomena so much as exist in harmony with them, their consciousness serving as natural repositories for the accumulated experience of deep time.

The Keth'mori have no desire to leave their plateau, no concept of exploration or expansion. They are not confined by their impossible home but fulfilled by it. They have achieved something beyond mere adaptation—they have become expressions of their environment's deepest nature, living embodiments of the principle that the impossible, given enough time and patience, becomes inevitable.

In the eternal afternoon of the Weeping Plateau, they continue their patient tending, their gentle guidance of reality's more fragile aspects. They are gardeners not just of plants and crystals and crystalline insects, but of existence itself, ensuring through their presence and attention that the impossible remains stable, that the beautiful contradictions of their world maintain their perfect, impossible balance through all the centuries yet to come.
