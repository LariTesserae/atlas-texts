In this transformed realm, the Gardeners tend their endless groves with movements that flow like meditation made manifest. They were once human, perhaps, though that distinction has grown as fluid as the water that fills their lungs and flows through their modified circulatory systems. Their forms have elongated over the centuries—limbs stretched graceful and pale, fingers webbed with translucent membranes that catch the filtered light like stained glass windows. Their skin has taken on the pearlescent quality of abalone shell, shifting between deep greens and silver-blues as they move through their aquatic cathedral.

The Gardeners breathe water as easily as their ancestors breathed air, their chests rising and falling in the ancient rhythm even as gills flutter almost imperceptibly along their elongated necks. Their eyes have grown large and dark, adapted to read the subtle bioluminescent languages of their charges—the pulse-codes of the bio-industrial systems, the chemical signatures that drift between the grove-networks, the emotional states of the fish-flocks they shepherd.

They speak, when they speak at all, in harmonics that travel through water and plant fiber alike, their voices joining the endless submarine chorus. But mostly they communicate through touch—hands placed against the glass-smooth bark of the forest-trees, fingers reading the hydraulic messages that flow through trunk and branch. They have learned to think with the forest-mind, their own neural patterns synchronizing with the vast root-networks until individual consciousness becomes just one note in a larger symphony.

The Gardeners move through their domain with purpose that appears slow but never ceases. One might spend decades guiding a single grove through some vast structural renovation, encouraging trunk-splits that will create new clearings, training root-systems to interface more efficiently with the mineral-processing organs. Another might dedicate years to teaching a school of fish new pollination routes, swimming alongside them in patient repetition until the patterns become instinct.

They are neither entirely individual nor truly collective. Each Gardener maintains their own specialized knowledge—this one knows the deep chemistries of the processing-organs, that one reads the migratory patterns that carry genetic material between distant groves. But their consciousness flows together during the great seasonal adjustments, when the bio-industrial systems shift their output and the entire ecosystem prepares for its ancient cycles.

Occasionally, one of them will drift to the edges where the plateau falls away, staring out into the profound depths with an expression that might be longing or might be memory. In these moments, they seem to remember something of what they once were—surface-dwellers who chose transformation rather than abandonment when the waters rose. But the memory passes like a thermal layer, and they return to their endless, patient work.

The Gardeners age, but slowly, their modified biology sustained by the same mineral processes that feed the forest. The eldest among them have been tending these groves for a thousand years or more, their bodies so adapted to their environment that they barely register as distinct from the bio-architecture they maintain. Their movements have become purely efficient, no energy wasted, every gesture part of the great maintenance that keeps their drowned world in perfect balance.

In the deepest quiet of the grove-clearings, you might glimpse young Gardeners—newly transformed volunteers who rise from the processing chambers with skin still shifting between human-pink and aquatic silver, learning to read the light-languages and chemical-whispers of their new home. They are guided by the ancient ones through patient decades of education, until they too learn to think in geological time and tend gardens that will outlive stars.

And in the spaces between the groves, other things have learned to live in this transformed world. Schools of bio-engineered fish that sing in complex harmonies, their songs carrying data between distant processing nodes. Vast filter-feeding organisms that drift like living clouds, processing water and waste with the efficiency of living cities. Smaller creatures that might once have been insects, now adapted to life in the water-gardens, pollinating the stranger plants and maintaining the delicate balances that keep the ecosystem stable.

All of them are part of the great work that the Gardeners began centuries ago and will continue for centuries hence—the patient cultivation of a world that chose water over air, synthesis over nature, and the deep dream of eternal growth over the quick burn of surface existence.
