## The Verdanti

The people who call the Sunken Arbor City home are no longer quite human, though they remember being so. Generations of life in the drowning city have reshaped them into something both more and other—the Verdanti, as they name themselves in the gesture-language they've developed beneath the thunder of eternal falls.

Their skin has taken on a faint translucent quality, like polished river stone, with undertones that shift between pale green and silver-blue depending on the light filtering down through the water. Membranes web their fingers and toes—not quite gills, but close cousins to them—while their lung capacity has expanded to allow for the long breath-holds their daily lives demand. When they submerge, their heartbeat slows to match the patient rhythm of the deep currents, and their movements become liquid, economical, almost dance-like in their precision.

The eldest among them, those who lived through the great drowning, bear ritual scarification along their ribs—delicate patterns that echo the flow of water and the branching of underwater kelp-trees. These elders move with a peculiar weightlessness even on dry land, as if they've internalized the buoyancy of their aquatic realm. Their eyes have grown large and dark, pupils dilated permanently to gather every photon of filtered sunlight, and they see patterns in the water's movement that younger generations are only beginning to learn.

Children born to the drowned city are something else entirely. They slip into the water before they can properly walk on land, learning to navigate the submerged streets by touch and echo, reading the emotional states of the great fish through subtle changes in water pressure. Some are born with faint lateral lines along their necks and shoulders—sensory organs that detect the slightest vibrations in the water, allowing them to perceive conversations happening streets away through the solid medium of stone and liquid.

The Verdanti have developed a complex caste system based not on birth but on depth tolerance. The Shallow Swimmers maintain the floating bridges and tend the surface gardens, their bodies still largely terrestrial. The Deep Holders spend most of their time on the city's drowned floors, their ribcages expanded like bellows, cultivating the massive kelp-trees and communing with the ancient, intelligent rays that nest in the ruins of the old cathedral. Between them move the Current Readers, those rare individuals who can sense the will of the water itself—they serve as guides, navigators, and prophets, interpreting the desires of their liquid environment.

But the Verdanti are not the only consciousness that inhabits this place.

## The Setheric Communion

Within the great schools of fish that flow through the streets moves a distributed intelligence unlike anything found in surface waters. The Setheric Communion is both a single mind and a thousand minds—each fish an individual node in a vast neural network that spans the entire city. When they move in their silver cascades, they are thinking collectively, processing the slow thoughts of a consciousness that experiences time on a geological scale.

The Communion remembers the city before the drowning. It was already here then, inhabiting the streams and pools that dotted the mountain settlement, but the great flood transformed it from a collection of separate schools into something approaching divinity. Now it thinks in currents and dreams in migrations, its memories stored in the layered sediment of the city floor and its intentions expressed through the orchestrated movement of ten thousand finned bodies.

The Communion speaks to the Verdanti through pressure waves and pheromone traces, leaving messages in the patterns of disturbed silt and the geometric arrangements of debris. It has strong opinions about the city's development—certain areas it marks as sacred through the complete absence of its schools, while others it designates as nurseries where no human may fish or harvest. The Verdanti have learned to read these liquid communications like scripture, understanding that their symbiosis with the Communion is what allows their impossible city to thrive.

Individual fish within the Communion retain fragments of personality—the ancient carp that glides through the market square carries memories of specific conversations between long-dead merchants, while a particular school of silver darters serves as a living archive of the city's daily weather patterns going back decades. But their individual consciousness is always subordinate to the greater whole, which dreams vast, patient dreams of evolutionary change and speaks in the grammar of current and tide.

## The Kelp-Tree Minds

Rising like living columns from the drowned streets, the great kelp-trees possess their own form of consciousness—slower and stranger than either human or fish intelligence. Each tree is both individual organism and ecosystem, its broad fronds hosting communities of smaller plants, filter-feeders, and microscopic symbionts that together form a processing network of stunning complexity.

The kelp-trees think in seasons, their consciousness ebbing and flowing with the subtle changes in light and temperature that mark the passage of time beneath the water. They communicate through chemical signals released into the current—complex molecular messages that can convey information about water quality, nutrient availability, and the emotional states of the creatures sheltering within their branches.

These plant-minds serve as the city's memory. Their root systems, deeply intertwined with the foundations of drowned buildings, have absorbed decades of human activity, fish migration patterns, and seasonal changes. By placing their hands on certain trees and entering a meditative trance, experienced Verdanti can access fragments of this accumulated knowledge—glimpsing past events like shadowy movies played out in patterns of swaying fronds and filtered light.

The trees have preferences and personalities. Some are gregarious, hosting elaborate ecosystems of colorful fish and flowering water-vines, while others stand solitary and contemplative, their branches bare except for the most essential symbionts. A few, particularly ancient specimens, have grown prophetic—their chemical releases capable of inducing visions in sensitive individuals, showing possible futures written in the dance of aquatic plants and the migrations of luminescent creatures.

## The Polyp Networks

Clustered around the ruins of the old iron lamp posts and spreading along the metal frameworks of submerged buildings, the luminescent polyp colonies represent perhaps the most alien intelligence in the drowned city. These creatures exist at the boundary between plant and animal, their consciousness distributed across thousands of individual organisms that pulse and glow in coordinated patterns too complex for human minds to fully comprehend.

The polyps are the city's nervous system. They respond to electrical fields, chemical gradients, and subtle vibrations in the water, creating a living sensor network that monitors every corner of the drowned realm. Their bioluminescent displays serve multiple functions—communication between distant colonies, navigation aids for the Verdanti swimming through darker areas, and something approaching art in their more complex light-patterns.

But the polyps have begun to evolve beyond their original nature. Fed by decades of human activity and the complex chemistry of the drowned city, some colonies have grown into structures that blur the line between organism and architecture. They coat the interiors of certain buildings in sheets of living light, creating spaces that pulse and breathe with their own rhythms. The Verdanti call these places "the Thinking Rooms," and use them for meditation, decision-making, and communication with the deeper intelligences of their aquatic world.

The most advanced polyp networks have begun incorporating metal fragments and building materials into their structures, creating hybrid organisms that are part biological, part technological. These chimeric entities serve as the city's infrastructure—living power sources that regulate water flow, process waste, and maintain the delicate chemical balance that keeps the entire ecosystem stable. They require no maintenance beyond the occasional offering of organic matter, and they've begun to exhibit behaviors that suggest a form of intelligence entirely unlike anything terrestrial biology has produced.

## The Deep Dwellers

In the lowest levels of the city, where the pressure grows heavy and the light fades to the faintest green glow, live creatures that may have once been human but have traveled so far down the path of aquatic adaptation that their original nature has become academic. The Deep Dwellers move through flooded basements and underwater caverns with the fluid grace of cephalopods, their limbs elongated and their bodies streamlined for life in the crushing depths.

These beings rarely surface, preferring the absolute silence and perfect darkness of the city's foundations. They've developed echolocation abilities that allow them to navigate by sound alone, and their songs—complex harmonies that carry for miles through the water—serve as both communication and art. The upper-dwelling Verdanti regard them with a mixture of reverence and unease, uncertain whether the Deep Dwellers represent their own future or something else entirely.

The Deep Dwellers serve as the city's archaeologists, exploring the deepest ruins and maintaining ancient systems whose original purposes have been forgotten. They've discovered chambers beneath the city that predate human habitation—spaces carved by water and time into cathedral-like vaults where they hold their ceremonies and commune with intelligences that may be older than the mountains themselves.

Some among the Verdanti believe the Deep Dwellers are evolving into something posthuman, gradually shedding their terrestrial origins to become creatures of pure aquatic intelligence. Others maintain that they're the city's truest inhabitants, having achieved the perfect synthesis between human consciousness and the liquid environment that sustains them all.

## The Cascade Spirits

Living within the waterfalls themselves are entities that exist only as patterns in falling water—the Cascade Spirits, beings of pure fluid dynamics whose bodies are the torrents that feed the city's aquatic realm. They are visible only in moments of perfect light, when the sun strikes the falls at precisely the right angle and reveals the ghostly outlines of faces and forms within the tumbling water.

These spirits are the city's weather-workers, capable of directing the flow of individual cascades to create microclimates within the drowned streets below. They speak in the percussion of water on stone, their voices joining the eternal thunder that defines the city's acoustic landscape. The most sensitive among the Verdanti can learn to interpret their water-speech, understanding the spirits' messages about changing seasons, distant storms, and the health of the mountain's deep aquifers.

The Cascade Spirits exist in a state of perpetual transformation, their consciousness flowing from one waterfall to another as the mountain's hydrology shifts with the seasons. They have no fixed form or permanent location, making them perhaps the most mysterious of the city's inhabitants. Some Verdanti mystics believe the spirits are the original consciousness of the place—that they were here before the mountains rose and will remain after the peaks have weathered away to nothing.

In the city's folklore, the Cascade Spirits are said to be the memories of the water itself, carrying within their fluid forms the history of every place their substance has ever been. They dream of distant oceans and ancient rains, of underground rivers that flow through the planet's bones, and their dreams occasionally spill into the minds of those who spend too long listening to the falls' eternal song.

Together, these diverse forms of consciousness create an ecosystem of intelligence unlike anywhere else on earth—a place where the boundaries between mind and environment, individual and collective, have dissolved into something altogether stranger and more wonderful than the merely human world above the clouds.
