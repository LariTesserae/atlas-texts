In this breathing labyrinth live the **Verdant Shepherds**—though to call them inhabitants suggests a separation between dweller and dwelling that ceased to exist generations ago. They move through their plant-city with bodies that tell the story of long symbiosis: skin bearing the faint green tinge of chlorophyll supplementation, fingernails grown thick and root-hard for the constant work of pruning and guiding growth, hair that seems to shift between brown and moss-green in the filtered light.

The Shepherds dress in garments that blur the line between clothing and cultivation. Their robes are living tapestries of specially bred lichens and mosses, fed by the oils of their skin and shaped by daily tending into patterns that serve both decoration and function—pockets that are actually specialized pouches in the fabric-organisms, buttons that are woody nodules trained to swell and contract with moisture. These garments change with the seasons of their wearers' lives, growing more elaborate with age and experience, until the eldest Shepherds seem almost indistinguishable from mobile gardens.

Their society is structured around the **Garden Circles**—loose confederations of families who tend specific districts of the Foundry. Each Circle specializes in different aspects of bio-architecture: the **Bone Weavers** who coax fungi into load-bearing forms, the **Sap Singers** whose hummed melodies encourage proper growth patterns in the living buildings, the **Root Whisperers** who can feel the underground networks and direct the flow of nutrients through soil-speech learned in childhood.

The **Nurture Mothers** hold particular reverence—women whose bodies have been modified through careful exposure to plant hormones and symbiotic bacteria, allowing them to commune directly with the greater organism of the city. They spend their days in meditation chambers deep within the root networks, their hands buried in the living soil, serving as conduits between human intention and vegetable will. Their eyes have grown large and dark from years in the green twilight, and they speak in whispers that seem to rustle like leaves.

Children in the Foundry grow up as much plant-tenders as human beings. From infancy, they are given **Garden Companions**—specially bred plants that grow alongside them, responding to their emotional states and physical needs. These plant-partners serve as toys, tools, and teachers, creating bonds so intimate that separating a Shepherd from their Companion in adulthood can be traumatic for both organisms. The children learn to read the subtle communications of their verdant city: which textures indicate safe passage, which scents warn of dangerous blooms, which rhythms in the plant-pulse signal the best times for harvest or construction.

But the Foundry houses stranger dwellers still. In the deepest chambers, where the air hangs thickest and the walls pulse most visibly with sap-circulation, live the **Rooted Ones**—humans who have chosen to merge more completely with their environment. Their legs have grown together and extended downward into root-systems that tap directly into the city's nutrient networks. They cannot move, but they need not eat in any conventional sense, sustaining themselves through photosynthesis and absorption. Their consciousness seems to extend throughout the local plant network, making them living archives of the Foundry's memory and will.

The **Spore Speakers** wander the middle levels, their lungs modified by decades of breathing the heavy air until they can communicate through precisely controlled exhalations of pollen and fungal spores. They serve as messengers and negotiators, able to "speak" directly to the various plant-intelligences through chemical signals. Their voices, when they choose to use human language, carry an odd resonance, as if echoes of vegetable thought color every word.

Among the strangest inhabitants are the **Green Sleepers**—individuals who have fallen into a state that might be hibernation or might be transformation. They lie in specially prepared chambers where the walls have grown soft and nest-like, their bodies slowly being overgrown by careful arrangements of moss and vine. Some have been in this state for decades, their chests rising and falling with breath so slow it can barely be detected, their dreams allegedly connecting them to the vast consciousness that guides the Foundry's growth. Whether they will ever wake, or what they might become if they do, remains a mystery even to the other Shepherds.

The fauna has adapted in equally strange ways. **Symbiotic cats** with fur that serves as growing medium for air-plants stalk through the upper reaches, their purrs encouraging beneficial growth patterns in their mobile gardens. **Pollinator birds** have evolved elongated beaks and specialized throat pouches to carry not just nectar but architectural instructions—chemical messages between distant parts of the living city that they cannot traverse directly.

And there are the **Others**—forms that might once have been human but have transformed beyond easy recognition. Glimpsed occasionally in the deepest chambers, they move with plant-like patience, their limbs bearing bark-like skin, their eyes reflecting green like deep forest pools. The Shepherds speak of them in tones of reverence and unease, calling them the **First Gardeners**—those who began the great work of the Foundry and may have succeeded too well in their merger with the verdant realm.

All of these beings move within the rhythm of their impossible city, their lives measured not by days but by growth cycles, their thoughts shaped by an intelligence that dreams in decades and speaks through the slow language of cell division and seasonal change. They are no longer quite human, but neither are they plant—they have become something new, something that exists in the liminal space between animal will and vegetable patience, gardeners who have grown so close to their gardens that the distinction has become meaningless.

In the humid green twilight of the Foundry, they tend their living architecture with the devotion of priests and the knowledge of symbionts, forever balanced on the edge of losing themselves completely to the vast verdant consciousness that pulses beneath their feet and dreams above their heads.
