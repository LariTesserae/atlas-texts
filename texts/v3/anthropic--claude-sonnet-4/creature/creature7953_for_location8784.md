The **Marrow-Touched** dwell here, and they are no longer quite human.

After generations within the bone cathedral, they have become something symbiotic with their environment. Their skin holds a subtle translucency—not ghostly, but with the warm, amber glow of backlit honey. Veins trace visible patterns beneath the surface, pulsing not with blood alone but with the mineral-rich oils they've learned to metabolize. They are taller than their surface ancestors, stretched upward by a lifetime of reaching toward the dripping marrow above, their limbs grown ropey and flexible like the vines they tend.

Their hair grows in thick, cord-like strands that they braid with living vine-shoots, creating symbiotic crowns that bloom with small, cup-shaped flowers. These hair-gardens collect the precious marrow drops directly, feeding their wearers with a constant trickle of nutrients. The eldest among them sport magnificent cascades of flowering hair that sway and chime softly as they move, marking their status as master cultivators.

They dress in garments grown rather than woven—living fabric cultivated from fungal threads that feast on oil-soaked organic matter. These clothes shift color subtly throughout the day, deepening to rich amber-browns when well-fed, fading to pale yellows when hungry. Buttons are seed pods that open and close with temperature changes. Pockets are small, symbiotic pouches that digest organic waste and excrete useful compounds. Their clothing breathes, sweats, and sometimes even sheds useful spores.

The Marrow-Touched move with fluid, deliberate motions, their joints looser and more flexible than baseline humans. They climb the vine networks with spider-like grace, their elongated fingers ending in tough, slightly pointed nails perfect for gripping organic surfaces. When they walk the swaying platforms, they flow with the motion rather than fighting it, their bodies reading the subtle rhythms of their living city.

Their society revolves around the **Cultivation Castes**. The **Dripweavers** are master architects who guide the growth of the settlement's living infrastructure. They sing to the vines in complex harmonic patterns, their voices trained to resonate through the bone chamber at frequencies that encourage specific growth patterns. Their songs blend with the city's natural acoustics, creating a constant, barely-audible chorus that shapes the very walls around them.

The **Marrow Shepherds** tend the pools of bone oil, understanding its moods and flows. They wade through the amber liquid in ritualistic patterns, their oil-darkened skin immune to its caustic properties. They've developed a peculiar form of divination, reading the surface patterns of the marrow pools to predict everything from weather changes above to the emotional state of the community. Their predictions prove uncannily accurate, though whether through genuine foresight or deep ecological intuition, none can say.

**Nectar Traders** form the merchant class, their bodies adapted with throat pouches that can store and transport concentrated plant essences. They develop an almost supernatural sense of taste, able to identify the nutritional and medicinal properties of any flower essence with a single drop on their tongues. During trade negotiations, they communicate as much through shared tastings as through words, building trust through the intimate exchange of rare flavors.

Children here are born different from the start. **Seedlings**, as they're called, emerge with skin already showing the characteristic transparency and oil-adapted metabolism. They spend their early years literally growing up through the vine networks, learning to read the plant-city's moods through touch and vibration before they can properly speak. Many develop synesthetic abilities, experiencing the city's sounds as colors or its chemical signatures as music.

The most revered among them are the **Bone Speakers**—those rare individuals who claim to hear the dreams of the dead titan. They press their ears against the smooth inner walls and translate what they hear into guidance for the community. Whether they truly commune with an ancient consciousness or simply interpret the complex acoustic phenomena of the bone chamber, their counsel proves invaluable. They tend to be the most physically transformed, their skulls slightly elongated, their hearing preternaturally acute.

Yet for all their adaptation, the Marrow-Touched retain a fundamentally human core. They laugh, love, grieve, and dream. Their children play games of tag through the swaying networks, their lovers court each other with gifts of rare flower essences, their elders tell stories that echo and re-echo through the bone cathedral until they become part of the city's permanent acoustic memory.

They've developed a unique form of art—**echo sculpture**—where sounds are crafted to bounce through the bone chamber in specific patterns, creating auditory experiences that can last for hours. Master echo sculptors are capable of composing sonic pieces that unfold differently depending on where in the city you stand to listen.

Above all, they are a people defined by **flow**—physical, social, spiritual. Nothing is static, nothing is permanent except change itself. They've learned to read the moods of their living city and move with its rhythms, understanding that resistance leads to exclusion, while acceptance leads to integration. In their fluid society, status shifts like the platforms themselves, and wisdom lies not in rigid knowledge but in the ability to adapt, to grow, to become something new while honoring what came before.

They are children of symbiosis, these Marrow-Touched, no longer entirely human but not yet something else entirely—caught in a beautiful, eternal process of becoming, sustained by the dreams of giants and the endless creativity of life refusing to surrender to the wasteland above.
