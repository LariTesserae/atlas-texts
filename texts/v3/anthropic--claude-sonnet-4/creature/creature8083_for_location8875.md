In the deepest pools of the lower terraces, something ancient stirs. The Choir-Keeper has lived here since the first stones were carved, though "lived" may not be the proper word. It is vast and patient, a presence more felt than seen—a congregation of consciousness that inhabits the water itself. When the rare visitor glimpses movement in the pools, they see only the suggestion of flowing robes made of liquid shadow, or perhaps tentacles that might be underwater fronds. The Choir-Keeper tends the symphony, conducting through subtle shifts in water temperature and chemical balance, orchestrating the voices of ten thousand smaller lives.

Its children are everywhere. The finger-long translucent salamanders that patrol the terrace edges, their bodies pulsing with bioluminescent messages. They serve as the Choir-Keeper's messengers and gardeners, pruning the moss with delicate precision, relocating tadpoles between pools to balance the acoustic sections. Their skin shifts color to match their mood and purpose—silver when hunting the tiny flies, deep blue when tending eggs, golden when singing their own crystalline harmonies into the greater composition.

In the middle terraces, the Builders work tirelessly. No larger than a child's fist, they appear to be some hybrid of crab and stone, their shells perfectly mimicking the porous rock. Their claws secrete a cement-like substance that hardens slowly in the constant moisture, allowing them to reshape pool edges and create new channels with infinite patience. They are mute by nature, but their work speaks—each modification changes the acoustics subtly, adding new resonances to the endless song. They seem to dream in architecture, following blueprints only they can perceive.

The Singers occupy the upper terraces in organized communities. Frogs the size of thumbnails arrange themselves in precise geometric patterns along the pool rims, their calls forming complex mathematical relationships—fibonacci spirals of sound, fractal harmonies that repeat at different scales. Larger cousins, broad as dinner plates and deep brown with silver throats, serve as the bass section, their calls so low they vibrate through the stone itself. Between them, dozens of species of intermediate size fill every acoustic niche, each with its assigned role in the composition that never ends, never repeats.

In the planted spaces, the Gardener-Moths tend their charges with devotion that borders on worship. Their wings shimmer with scales that match the colors of the plants they serve—emerald for the ferns, amber for the moss, silver-green for the herb gardens. They are pollinators and pruners both, their proboscis equally suited to drinking nectar and trimming excess growth. They work primarily in the brief periods when the song diminishes slightly, as if even they must pause to listen to certain movements in the grand composition.

The fish are perhaps the most mysterious inhabitants. They school not by species but by song, silver bodies moving in formations that mirror the acoustic patterns above. Some have learned to leap in rhythm, breaking the surface tension at precise moments to add percussion to the symphony. Others remain deep, their swim bladders resonating with subsonic frequencies that provide the foundation upon which all other voices build. The largest among them bear scars that suggest great age and wisdom—perhaps they remember when the first stones were laid.

And in the spaces between—in the moss, in the water films, in the very pores of the stone—countless microscopic communities thrive. Bacteria that bioluminesce in response to the acoustic vibrations, creating light-patterns that pulse with the music. Microscopic architects that slowly, grain by grain, modify the mineral content of the stone to improve its acoustic properties. They are the hidden foundation upon which this living cathedral depends.

Above it all, roosting in the highest plantings where the mist is thinnest, small birds with voices like flutes add the highest harmonies. Their feathers glisten with perpetual moisture, and their songs weave between the amphibian voices like silver thread through a vast tapestry of sound. They are the only inhabitants that regularly venture beyond the terraces, returning with seeds and stories from the outer world, though they always return to add their voices to the eternal composition.

The Nursery Terraces pulse with purpose. Each creature, from the vast Choir-Keeper to the tiniest bacterium, participates in something greater than themselves—a living symphony that reshapes stone through sound, that builds meaning from music, that transforms a simple gradient of pools into something approaching the sacred. They are all gardeners here, all musicians, all architects of sound and stone and flowing water, creating beauty that exists primarily for itself, for the pure joy of perfect, endless, ever-changing harmony.
