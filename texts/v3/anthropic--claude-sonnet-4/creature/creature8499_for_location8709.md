In this city of becoming live the **Lithosync**—a people who have learned to breathe with stone.

They are small and compact, their bodies shaped by generations of navigating vertical spaces. Their fingers are long and sensitive, capable of reading the mountain's intentions through the subtlest vibrations in rock. Their palms bear intricate patterns of calluses that match the stone's texture so precisely that they can grip seemingly smooth surfaces with gecko-like certainty. When they place their hands against the walls, the mountain's mineral tears bead around their touch like recognition.

The Lithosync do not age as other peoples do. Instead, they **accumulate**. Their hair grows thick with mineral deposits that catch and hold the mountain's moisture, becoming living repositories of the settlement's memory. The eldest among them wear crowns of crystallized mist, their locks heavy with the compressed dreams of decades. Their skin takes on the subtle luminescence of the weeping stone, a faint silvery sheen that grows more pronounced with time until the most ancient among them seem carved from the same living rock they inhabit.

Their eyes are their most remarkable feature—pale as morning fog but shot through with threads of copper and iron that pulse gently with their heartbeat. These metallic veins in their irises allow them to perceive the mountain's moods with startling clarity. A Lithosync child can tell you which passages will widen before dawn; an elder can predict where new chambers will bloom by watching the play of light in their companions' eyes.

They speak in two voices simultaneously: their audible words carried on breath, and a deeper communication that travels through touch—palm pressed to stone, transmitting thoughts that resonate through the mountain's body to be received by others in the settlement. This stone-speech allows them to coordinate their building projects across vast vertical distances and gives their community a profound intimacy. Secrets are nearly impossible to keep when emotions can be felt through the very walls.

The Lithosync wear clothing woven from the silver vines and mist-tree bark, garments that shimmer with trapped moisture and seem to breathe with the mountain's rhythm. Their fabrics are never fully dry, never fully wet, existing in the same liminal state as everything else in this place. They adorn themselves with small pieces of the mountain's gifts—transparent pebbles that formed in their presence, tiny crystals that grew in response to their touch, fragments of mineral veins that chose to surface near their homes.

Among them move the **Threshold Dancers**—Lithosync who have formed such perfect communion with the mountain that they can step partially into its substance. These rare individuals can phase through stone walls when the rock dreams deeply, emerging from cliff faces like thoughts becoming manifest. They serve as the settlement's deepest architects, working within the mountain's core to encourage the formation of new spaces. Their bodies flicker at the edges, sometimes solid flesh, sometimes living geology.

The children of the Lithosync are born with an intuitive understanding of balance and vertical space. They climb before they walk, their small bodies naturally finding purchase on surfaces that would challenge seasoned mountaineers. As they grow, they undergo the **Resonance Trial**—a ceremony where they must find their unique harmonic frequency that matches some aspect of the mountain's song. Some resonate with the deep geological rumbles, becoming the settlement's foundation-readers. Others match the crystalline ring of new mineral formations, destined to become the community's jewel-singers who encourage the growth of the transparent window-stones.

The settlement's leaders are the **Stone Whisperers**—Lithosync whose communion with the mountain has become so complete that they can feel the echo of its birth-dreams. They guide the community not through commands but through patient interpretation of the mountain's desires, helping to align human needs with geological ambition. When they speak, their words carry the weight of deep time, and their decisions unfold slowly, like the formation of crystals.

There are also the **Mist Shepherds**—those who tend to the pale, salamander-like creatures that flicker through the settlement. These beings are neither fully animal nor mineral, but something between—fragments of the mountain's consciousness given form. The Mist Shepherds understand their alien communication, which consists of rapid temperature changes and patterns of bioluminescence visible only in complete darkness. Through these creatures, the Lithosync learn of chambers forming in the mountain's depths, of new passages about to breach the surface, of the rare times when the mountain's dreaming turns troubled.

The settlement's artists are the **Form Weavers**—Lithosync who collaborate with the mountain's creative impulses to guide the growth of new structures. They do not carve or build in any traditional sense, but rather encourage and shape the mountain's own architectural dreams. A Form Weaver might spend months in meditation beside a promising bulge in the rock face, their presence gradually coaxing it toward the form of a graceful arch or an elegant chamber. Their art is measured not in hours but in seasons, their masterpieces unfolding with geological patience.

Among the most mysterious inhabitants are the **Echo Holders**—ancient Lithosync who have become living archives of the settlement's memory. They rarely speak aloud, instead storing the community's history in the mineral veins that thread through their transformed bodies. When crucial decisions must be made, the other inhabitants gather around the Echo Holders and listen with their palms pressed to stone, feeling the resonance of past choices thrumming through the mountain's structure.

And in the deepest chambers, it is whispered, dwell the **First Listeners**—the original Lithosync who heard the mountain's call to inhabit its growing body. They have become so integrated with the geological matrix that they exist more as consciousness woven through living stone than as discrete beings. Their thoughts flow through the mineral veins like slow rivers of awareness, their dreams shaping the mountain's continuing evolution. Sometimes their voices can be heard in the deepest singing that rises from the settlement's heart—not quite human anymore, but not yet fully stone.

All of the Lithosync share a profound acceptance of impermanence coupled with an infinite patience for the slow work of creation. They understand that they are not separate from the mountain but part of its dreaming, temporary forms given to consciousness that will one day flow back into the greater geological symphony. In the meantime, they tend their vertical gardens, weave their moisture-heavy fabrics, and listen to the mountain's stories with the devotion of lovers learning each other's deepest secrets.

Their city grows around them and through them, each day bringing new possibilities as the mountain continues its patient work of becoming. In this place where earth dreams of its future form, the Lithosync have found their calling as midwives to the impossible, shepherds of stone's slow ambitions, dancers in the space between what is and what might yet be.
