In the deepest hearts of the great root-buttresses, the **Archivists** tend their living libraries. These pale, elongated beings have adapted over millennia to life in the humid darkness, their skin translucent enough to reveal the slow pulse of green-tinted blood beneath. Their fingers have grown impossibly long and sensitive, able to read the chemical stories written in bark and root-flesh, translating the forest's vast memory into songs that echo through the hollow spaces. Each Archivist bonds with a particular grove in childhood, learning to interpret the subtle shifts in sap-flow and mycelial whispers that record decades of weather, growth, and change. They are the forest's memory keepers, and their haunting chants can be heard echoing from the root-chambers during the deep night hours—not quite human anymore, but not yet fully plant.

High in the canopy cities, the **Weavers** dance between branches on limbs that have grown lean and strong, their bones hollow like birds' but their minds sharp as blades. They tend the great networks of communication vines, guiding their growth with patient hands and careful cultivation. A Weaver can feel the pulse of messages traveling through the vine-networks—the chemical signatures of distant groves, requests for resources, warnings of storms brewing beyond the horizon. Their settlements are aerial gardens where hammock-nests hang from branches thick as bridge supports, and their children learn to walk on swaying bridges before they can properly stand on solid ground. The Weavers have developed a complex sign language performed with their whole bodies as they leap and swing through their verdant highways, visible to others across vast distances through the filtered green light.

In the mist-valleys where the temporary pools form and vanish, the **Tide-Shepherds** follow the water's rhythm with an almost mystical precision. These amphibious people have developed gills that flutter behind their ears and webbed digits that help them navigate both the flooded hollows and the dry periods between rains. Their skin shifts color with the changing light and moisture, allowing them to blend seamlessly with the dappled environment of their ever-changing domain. They are masters of the ephemeral, cultivating creatures and plants that exist only in the brief windows when conditions align perfectly. The Tide-Shepherds speak in clicks and liquid sounds that carry perfectly through water, and they have an uncanny ability to predict exactly when and where the next pools will form, arriving just as the first drops begin to fall.

The **Chorus-Callers** are perhaps the most transformed of all the Sprawl's inhabitants. Living symbiotically with the great resonant trees, they have developed throat sacs that can produce sounds across an incredible range—from the subsonic rumbles that help coordinate forest-wide biological processes to crystalline high notes that can be heard for dozens of kilometers. Their bodies have grown tall and thin, with enlarged ribcages that serve as natural amplifiers. Each Chorus-Caller learns the vast repertoire of the forest's song-language, able to translate bird calls, insect drones, and wind-whispers into the complex symphonies that keep the Sprawl's countless communities synchronized. During the great seasonal changes, when the forest shifts its breathing patterns or adjusts its moisture cycles, the Chorus-Callers can be heard night and day, their voices weaving together across impossible distances to guide the transition.

And then there are the **Deep-Dreamers**, who have chosen to merge almost entirely with the forest itself. These beings are more myth than flesh now, their consciousness distributed through networks of fungal connections that span vast territories. They are said to sleep for years at a time, their bodies gradually becoming indistinguishable from the root-walls they rest against, covered in flowering vines and phosphorescent mosses. But their dreams shape the Sprawl's growth, guiding the slow dance of evolution and adaptation that has created this perfect equilibrium. Other inhabitants speak of encountering them in the deepest tunnels—figures more plant than human, their eyes glowing with the soft bioluminescence of the deep forest, offering cryptic guidance to travelers who have lost their way in the green labyrinth.

Between and around all these specialized peoples flow the **Wandering Kin**—those who have chosen to remain more traditionally human, serving as traders, messengers, and bridges between the various adapted communities. They travel the hollow-vine highways and root-tunnel networks, carrying news and goods, maintaining the social connections that keep the Sprawl's diverse inhabitants united despite their physical transformations. These wanderers often bear the marks of partial adaptation themselves—enhanced lung capacity from breathing the thick, humid air, skin that has grown resistant to the countless pollens and spores, eyes that have adjusted to read meaning in the forest's subtle light-patterns.

All of these peoples share certain traits born of their ancient symbiosis with the Sprawl: an intuitive understanding of biological rhythms, the ability to navigate by scent and sound as much as sight, and most remarkably, a form of collective consciousness that emerges during the forest's great breathing cycles. When the entire Sprawl synchronizes its respiration—usually at dawn and dusk—all its inhabitants briefly share in a vast, distributed awareness that encompasses the health and needs of the entire ecosystem. In these moments, the distinction between human and forest dissolves entirely, and every creature within the Sprawl's embrace becomes part of a single, ancient, breathing meditation on the perfect balance of life.
