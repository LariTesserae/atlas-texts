In the living hollows of the Woven dwell the **Tenders** - a people who have forgotten the sharp edges of metal and stone. Their skin carries a faint green undertone, like light filtered through leaves, and their fingertips are stained permanent amber from a lifetime of handling sap and resin. They move with the unhurried patience of growing things, their voices soft and deliberate, pitched to harmonize with the tree's own susurrus of moisture and growth.

The Tenders' eyes have adapted to read the subtle language of bark and branch - they can diagnose a chamber's health by the pattern of condensation on its walls, predict the weather by the way certain fungi pulse, determine a person's emotional state by which bioluminescent traces brighten in response to their presence. Their children learn to navigate by touch and sound as much as sight, running bare-handed along bark-paths, feeling for the tree's pulse beneath their palms.

They wear clothing grown from the tree itself - garments of woven bark-fiber that never quite dry, that smell of earth and green sap. These clothes live in symbiosis with their wearers, the fabric's embedded fungi processing sweat and dead skin, while root-hair threads slowly self-repair tears and wear. Elders' robes bloom with small, pale flowers that open at dawn and close as the eternal dusk deepens.

**The Listeners** form a caste apart - individuals born with the rare ability to hear the Heartwood's deeper communications. They dwell in the most sacred chambers, closest to the tree's core, their bodies gradually merging with the living wood through patient symbiosis. Root-threads pierce their skin in delicate traceries, and their heartbeats synchronize with the great tree's rhythm. They speak rarely, and when they do, their voices carry harmonics that seem to come from within the walls themselves. The Listeners translate the tree's needs to the community, determining when new chambers should be grown, which branches need tending, where the city should expand.

Below, in the perpetual twilight of the bog's edge, dwell the **Swimmers** - pale, elongated beings who tend the water table and maintain the tree's root systems. Their lung capacity has expanded over generations, allowing them to work beneath the bog's dark surface for long periods. Their skin secretes a thin film of protective oils that lets them slip through water and mud like quicksilver. They emerge from the depths carrying information about the bog's health, the water table's needs, and the dreams that rise from the deepest roots.

The **Cultivators** work the middle branches, their bodies adapted for climbing and hanging work. Their hands have developed almost prehensile flexibility, and many sport decorative scarification that mirrors the bark patterns of their assigned sections. They are the artists of the Woven, training branches into functional beauty, grafting new varieties, creating the living sculptures that serve as both art and infrastructure. Their workshops are chambers where experimental growths spiral in impossible curves, where they nurture seeds that will become tomorrow's tools and furnishings.

Among them move the **Spore-Singers** - children and young adults who have formed a symbiotic relationship with the luminous gliding creatures. These beings can summon the gliders with subsonic humming, using them as living messengers and scouts. The spores that trail from the gliders' wings settle in the Singers' hair and along their shoulders, creating shifting patterns of light that serve as a complex form of communication. They dance as they work, their movements choreographed to encourage the spore-clouds into beautiful, meaningful configurations.

The community includes **Brewers** who cultivate specialized fungi in hidden grottos, fermenting the bog's methane-rich waters into mildly intoxicating beverages that glow faintly in the dark. Their craft requires precise understanding of the bog's chemistry, and they often disappear for days into the deepest, most dangerous sections where the water runs richest with minerals and decay.

Finally, there are the **Flame-Keepers** - those who tend the will-o'-wisps that dance above the bog. These mystics have learned to encourage and direct the cold flames, using them for illumination and ceremony. Their bodies carry a faint phosphorescent sheen, and they claim to dream the dreams of burning marsh gas, to understand the messages written in methane and starlight.

All of these peoples share one trait: they have grown into their environment as surely as the tree has grown around their needs. They breathe the thick, humid air as easily as others might breathe thin mountain atmosphere. They find the constant drip and whisper of moisture comforting rather than oppressive. They read time not by sun or moon, but by the tree's growth rings and the subtle shifts in the bioluminescent patterns that mark the Heartwood's deepest rhythms.

Their conflicts are subtle - disputes over pruning rights, disagreements about expansion patterns, the occasional territorial friction between Swimmers and surface-dwellers. But their grievances tend to resolve like sap flowing around obstacles, finding new paths rather than breaking through with force. They are a people shaped by patience, by the understanding that some things cannot be hurried, only nurtured toward their proper growth.

In the deepest chambers of the Heartwood, the oldest Tenders speak of memories that might be the tree's own - visions of the crater lake when it was still water rather than bog, of the first careful grafting that began the Woven's growth, of the slow conversation between human need and plant wisdom that created this symbiosis. Whether these are true memories or collective dreams, none can say. But in the Woven, the distinction has grown less important with each passing season.
