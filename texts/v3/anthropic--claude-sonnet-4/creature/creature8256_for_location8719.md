In the Hanging Gardens of Kythros, humanity has grown strange and wonderful, shaped by generations of vertical living until they move through their cliff-city like water finding its path through stone.

The **Kythrosians** themselves are lean and long-limbed, their bodies adapted to a lifetime of climbing, their fingers strong and calloused from gripping wet stone and rope. Their lung capacity is enormous—chests broad and deep to pull what oxygen they can from the thin air. But it's their eyes that mark them most clearly: large and luminous, pupils dilated wide to catch every photon of the filtered light that penetrates their misty realm. In the eternal twilight, they see details that would be invisible to lowlanders, reading the subtle variations in stone color that indicate safe handholds, or spotting the shimmer of fish swimming through thick air.

Their hair grows in thick, water-resistant waves that shed the constant moisture without becoming waterlogged. Many braid it with living vines that continue growing, creating elaborate living headdresses that bloom with flowers or fruit. Their skin has a faint luminescence—not enough to light a room, but sufficient to make them visible to each other in the deepest mists. This bioluminescence grows stronger with age, so the elders move through the gardens like gentle ghosts, their skin casting a pearl-white glow that marks them as repositories of wisdom.

The Kythrosians have developed three distinct castes, though the boundaries between them are fluid as the ever-flowing water:

**The Shapers** work the living rock, but they are part geologist, part surgeon. They listen to the mountain's heartbeat, pressing their ears to stone to hear where the water wants to flow. When they carve, they're not imposing their will but revealing the city the mountain already holds within itself. Their tools are extensions of their hands—hammers that ring with perfect pitch, chisels that can split stone along fault lines invisible to others. The greatest Shapers develop a symbiotic relationship with the stone; their touch can encourage the mountain's seepage to flow where needed, and ancient Shapers are said to eventually become one with the cliff face, their consciousness spreading through the rock like veins of precious metal.

**The Tenders** are the gardener-shepherds of this vertical ecosystem. They move through the hanging gardens with an intuitive understanding of how life flows in this impossible place. Each Tender is bonded from childhood to a section of the gardens, learning to read the needs of every plant, to encourage the bioluminescent organisms, to guide the aerial fish through their daily migrations. They wear living garments—vests woven from vine fibers that continue growing, sprouting pockets and tools as needed. Their most prized possessions are their seed pouches, made from specially prepared fish-scales that keep moisture at the perfect level. The Tenders know secrets: which fruits will ripen only under the touch of certain hands, how to call the glowing insects that pollinate the night-blooming harvest, why some plants sing in harmony with the mountain's water-music.

**The Weavers** bind the city together, literally and figuratively. They create the ropes that are lifelines, the nets that catch falling objects and sometimes falling people, the bridges that connect platform to platform. But they weave more than fiber—they weave relationships, stories, the social fabric that keeps people from going mad in this vertical isolation. Their looms are architectural features, great frames stretched between buildings where they work patterns that tell the city's history. The rope they create is stronger than it should be, infused with plant extracts that make it flexible in cold, resistant to the constant moisture, and faintly bioluminescent for safety in the dim light. Master Weavers can create rope that responds to tension, tightening automatically when stressed, or cloth that changes color to indicate weather patterns.

But the human inhabitants share their city with others:

**The Pale Shepherds** are what the mountain goats have become after generations in this mineral-rich environment. They're larger than their wild cousins, with opalescent horns that spiral in perfect spirals and eyes like polished mirrors. They've developed a rudimentary form of communication with the Kythrosians, leading them to new water sources or warning of unstable rock. Some say the Shepherds were once human themselves, transformed by drinking too deeply of the mountain's seepage. They move through the city as they please, their hoofbeats a familiar percussion on the stone paths.

**The Garden Singers** are bird-like creatures with gossamer wings and voices like silver bells. They tend the highest gardens where humans cannot safely climb, carrying seeds between platforms in specialized throat pouches. Their songs coordinate the city's rhythms—different melodies signal weather changes, feeding times for the aerial fish, or the rare moments when the mist clears enough to glimpse the lands below. The most ancient Garden Singers have learned human speech and serve as messengers between the most distant platforms.

**The Current Swimmers** are the strangest residents: fish that navigate the thick air as easily as water, their scales adapted to extract oxygen from mist. They follow predictable routes through the city, swimming in schools through the most humid corridors. The Kythrosians have learned to harvest them sustainably, and the fish seem to accept this as part of their life cycle. Some grow quite large, becoming almost cetacean in their movements through the aerial currents. The largest are treated with reverence—ancient individuals who remember the city's construction, their presence a sign of the ecosystem's health.

At the city's heart, in the deepest chambers carved directly into the mountain's core, dwell **The Listeners**—a small group of individuals who have given up speech to better hear the mountain's voice. They are pale from their underground existence, their bodies adapted to the intense pressure and humidity of the deep chambers. Their role is crucial: they monitor the mountain's health, predicting when seepage might increase or decrease, sensing geological changes that could threaten the city. They communicate through a complex system of hand signals and carved symbols, their messages carried throughout the city by an intricate network of pulleys and bells.

The children of Kythros are raised communally, moving freely between platforms as they learn the skills they'll need. By adolescence, their natural talents typically draw them toward one of the three castes, though some rare individuals become **Bridge-Walkers**—those who belong to all castes and none, serving as mediators and messengers between the different levels of society. These individuals develop an almost supernatural balance and become the city's memory keepers, carrying news, stories, and genetic diversity between the various platform-communities.

In this vertical world, the elderly don't retire—they ascend. The highest platforms are reserved for those whose bodies can no longer handle the constant climbing but whose wisdom is most valued. From their elevated positions, they serve as lookouts, weather-readers, and keepers of the deepest knowledge. The most revered elders eventually choose to undergo the **Stone-Sleep**, allowing themselves to be incorporated into the mountain itself, their consciousness joining the vast, slow intelligence that some believe guides the city's impossible existence.

Life in Kythros flows in daily rhythms as regular as the water's flow: the morning mist-harvest when dew is collected in specially woven nets, the midday weaving when the light is strongest, the evening song when all three species add their voices to a harmony that echoes through the stone chambers. And through it all, the constant background music of water finding its way down the mountain, carrying with it the minerals that make this vertical paradise possible—and perhaps, the dreams of the mountain itself.
