In the humid shadows between the iron ribs and verdant curtains, the **Cultivators** tend their endless garden with movements as rhythmic and purposeful as the mechanical systems they've grown to mirror.

They are tall and willowy, these keepers of the Foundry, their limbs elongated from generations of reaching between platform levels and stretching toward high-mounted growing apparatus. Their skin has taken on a pale, translucent quality—not unhealthy, but adapted, like deep-cave fish or underground roots. It gleams faintly with perpetual moisture, and when they move through the mist, they seem to shimmer in and out of visibility like living condensation.

The Cultivators' hands are their most remarkable feature: fingers grown long and sensitive as root tendrils, capable of detecting the slightest changes in soil chemistry, moisture content, or plant health through touch alone. Their fingertips are stained permanent green-black from handling growing medium and compost, and they move with a surgeon's precision when grafting, pruning, or adjusting the intricate irrigation networks. They rarely speak above a whisper—the constant din makes louder conversation pointless, and over time they've developed an elaborate tactile language conducted through the vibrations they send through the iron framework itself.

They dress in garments that blur the line between clothing and equipment: woven vests with countless pockets for seeds, tools, and specimens; trousers with knee-high waterproof sections for wading through the deeper channels; broad-brimmed hats that catch and funnel condensation into small reservoirs. Everything they wear serves the garden first, their comfort second. Patches of moss have begun growing on their clothing in the perpetual dampness, and they don't seem to mind—or perhaps they encourage it.

Their daily rhythms match the great mechanical cycles. When the filtration systems surge to life with their dawn purification sequence, the Cultivators rise from alcoves carved into the foundation walls, emerging from beneath blankets of living moss that helped regulate their body temperature through the night. They move through their domain in practiced routes, hands trailing along familiar pipes and railings, reading the health of their ecosystem through touch and sound.

Some focus on the high gardens, spending their days suspended on rope and pulley systems, tending to the aerial meadows and ensuring the hanging root networks receive proper nutrition. Others work the foundational levels, managing the complex composting systems and monitoring the ceramic channel networks for blockages or imbalances. The most senior Cultivators—distinguished by the way moss grows thicker on their shoulders and water beads more readily on their skin—oversee the integration points where mechanical systems interface with living ones.

They've developed an almost supernatural awareness of their environment. A Cultivator can detect an impending mechanical failure by the subtle change in harmonics it creates in the constant symphony. They know which sections of walkway will be slippery with condensation before the moisture fully forms. Their internal clocks align with every cycle in the Foundry—they know when the pressure valves will hiss, when the birds will be most active, when the salamanders will emerge for feeding.

The younger Cultivators, still pale-skinned but not yet translucent, learn by following their elders through the endless maintenance routines. They develop calluses on their hands from handling the rough iron surfaces, and their eyes adjust to reading the subtle variations in green-filtered light that indicate the health and needs of different plants. Some show aptitude for the mechanical systems, learning to listen to the language of gears and steam. Others develop an almost psychic connection to the living systems, sensing disease or stress in plants before any visible symptoms appear.

In the deepest foundations of the Foundry, the eldest Cultivators have begun an almost mystical transformation. They move slowly, their translucent skin now truly glass-like, and moss grows so thickly on them that they resemble mobile gardens themselves. These ancient tenders are said to communicate directly with the great mechanical heart of the place, understanding its needs and moods as they would those of an old friend. They rarely emerge to the upper levels anymore, instead tending the most crucial and mysterious systems in the depths—the original growth chambers where the first artificial soil was created, where the prototype filtration systems still hum with their original purpose.

The Cultivators have no need for external seasons or natural light cycles. Their world exists in the eternal present of growth, decay, renewal, and mechanical precision. They are the living bridges between the industrial and the organic, having become something that is neither fully human nor fully plant, but entirely suited to their strange and beautiful domain. In the Greenhouse Foundry, they are both gardener and garden, tending and tended, mechanical and organic, individual and part of the vast breathing system that encompasses them all.
