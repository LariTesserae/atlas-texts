In this hushed realm of mineral textile, life has learned the art of quietude.

The **Threadlings** are perhaps the most numerous inhabitants—barely visible humanoid figures no taller than a child's hand, their bodies composed of the same crystalline fibers that carpet the ground. They emerge from the felt itself at dawn and dusk, their translucent forms shimmering like heat mirages. The Threadlings tend the wire-grass with infinite patience, their tiny fingers coaxing geometric growth patterns across the landscape. They communicate through vibrations passed fiber to fiber through the ground, their conversations rippling across miles in waves too subtle for larger beings to detect. When threatened, they simply dissolve back into the felt, becoming indistinguishable from the mineral wool around them.

In the depths of the abandoned bore stations, the **Dynamo Shepherds** maintain their eternal vigil. Once human technicians who refused to abandon their posts as the mills failed, they have become something else entirely over the centuries. Their bodies are wrapped in living bandages of mineral fiber that grow directly from their skin, and their breath comes in soft mechanical whispers that echo through the machinery. They tend to the dying engines with religious devotion, feeding them microscopic amounts of wire-grass oil to keep their hearts barely beating. The Shepherds' eyes have clouded with gypsum cataracts that allow them to see the electromagnetic fields flowing through the old equipment, tracking the fading ghosts of industrial purpose.

High above, the **Silence Weavers** drift through the amber haze—creatures like enormous dandelion seeds, but made of spun glass and mineral down. They are the gardeners of the sky, their gossamer bodies filtering and redistributing the perpetual dust, ensuring the soft light remains constant. The Weavers reproduce by fragmenting themselves in the high atmosphere, their offspring settling like snow to add new layers to the ever-growing felt below. They sing, but their songs exist at frequencies so low that only the bore station foundations can hear them, causing the concrete to resonate with barely perceptible harmonics.

Within the felt itself, vast colonies of **Fiber Coral** spread through the mineral substrate—not quite animal, not quite plant, but something that bridges both kingdoms. These organisms appear as subtle color variations in the felt, patches where the mineral wool takes on faint hues of rust-orange or pale green. The Fiber Coral digest metal traces from the old machinery, slowly converting industrial waste into crystalline beauty. They pulse almost imperceptibly with their own bioluminescence, creating the ground's subtle inner glow. Where Fiber Coral colonies meet, they exchange chemical information, their conversations taking place over decades as nutrients flow through the felted medium.

The **Echo Fishers** patrol the electromagnetic streams that flow between the bore stations. These creatures resemble translucent manta rays made of stretched silk, their bodies barely more solid than the air they swim through. They feed on the fading electrical signatures of the dead machinery, diving deep into the felt to follow wire traces that still carry phantom currents. Echo Fishers can phase partially out of physical existence, becoming pure electromagnetic phenomena when tracking particularly elusive energy signatures. Their young are born as brief sparkles of static electricity, gaining substance only as they learn to navigate the landscape's hidden electrical geography.

In the deepest reaches of the bore stations, where the original geothermal vents still whisper their ancient heat, the **Archive Keepers** maintain their libraries. These beings appear as tall, draped figures whose robes are woven from every type of fiber found in the Felting Grounds—human textile, mineral wool, moth silk, and thread-snake scales all combined. They preserve the memories of the place, storing the history of every creature, every machine, every industrial dream in the patterns of their ever-growing garments. The Archive Keepers speak only in textile—their words becoming physical threads that they weave into their robes, their stories literally becoming part of their bodies.

And in the spaces between, the **Pause Spirits** drift—beings of pure quietude that exist only in moments of perfect stillness. They appear as faint distortions in the air, visible only when everything else has stopped moving. The Pause Spirits are drawn to the Felting Grounds' profound silence, feeding on the absence of sound itself. They bless sleeping creatures with deeper rest and dying machines with peaceful shutdown. When a Pause Spirit touches something, that object becomes slightly more quiet, slightly more still, gradually joining the landscape's conspiracy of gentle hush.

All of these beings share a common trait: they have learned to live in harmony with waiting itself. In the Felting Grounds, patience is not just a virtue but a fundamental force of nature, and every inhabitant has evolved to match the landscape's own unhurried rhythm of growth and gentle decay. They are all weavers in their own way, adding their presence to the great textile that the earth itself is still patiently creating, fiber by mineral fiber, breath by whispered breath.
