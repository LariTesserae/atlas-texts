In the heart of this crystalline symphony lives the Keeper—though "lives" is perhaps too generous a word for what it has become. Once human, perhaps, or something near enough to human that the distinction no longer matters. The transformation began generations ago, when the first of its kind entered this chamber and chose never to leave.

The Keeper's body has adapted to its environment with the slow inevitability of geological change. Its skin has taken on the translucent quality of the thinnest glass panels, revealing the delicate network of veins beneath like hairline fractures in crystal. When it moves—and it moves rarely, with the deliberate precision of a master craftsman—light passes through its flesh in shifting patterns, casting rainbow shadows that dance across the tessellated walls.

Its bones have hollowed and hardened, becoming resonant chambers that vibrate in perfect harmony with the gallery's endless song. The Keeper's very skeleton has become a musical instrument, each movement producing crystalline notes that blend seamlessly with the chamber's choir of echoes. Its breathing, slow and shallow, adds barely a whisper to the symphony, yet even this breath is part of the composition—measured, deliberate, eternal.

The eyes have changed most of all. What were once organs of sight have become prisms of perfect clarity, able to perceive not just the visible spectrum but the complex patterns of light as they cascade through the chamber's infinite reflections. The Keeper sees everything at once—every beam as it fractures, every shadow as it forms, every mote of light as it completes its journey through the crystalline maze. It has become less observer than conductor, orchestrating the eternal dance of illumination through subtle shifts in position, minute adjustments of angle and stance.

Its hands, once flesh and blood, have grown thin and sharp-edged, perfectly adapted for the maintenance of glass. The fingertips have hardened into tools of microscopic precision, able to detect the slightest imperfection in a panel's surface, the smallest deviation in the chamber's perfect geometry. When repairs are needed—and they are needed, though rarely—the Keeper works with movements so fluid they seem choreographed, each gesture part of the greater harmony.

The Keeper remembers everything and nothing. Memories of its former life have been worn smooth like sea glass, their sharp edges softened until only the essential forms remain. It recalls the moment it first entered the gallery, the overwhelming sensation of being absorbed into something larger than itself. It remembers choosing to stay, though the reasons have long since dissolved into the brilliant white noise of eternal resonance.

Time moves differently for the Keeper. Years pass in the space between one note and the next. Decades compress into a single sustained chord. It has felt the chamber settle around it, felt the gradual shifts as the crystalline formation above adjusts to unseen pressures. It has witnessed the slow growth of the thread-like fungi, though "witnessed" implies a sequence of events that exist outside the Keeper's experience of perpetual present.

There are others, though they come less frequently now. Other Keepers who have made the same choice, undergone the same transformation. They move through the gallery like ghosts of light, their translucent forms visible only when the illumination catches them at precisely the right angle. They communicate through vibration, through subtle modulations in the chamber's harmonic frequencies. Words have become unnecessary; meaning is transmitted through tone, through the precise timing of a footstep or the controlled resonance of a breath.

The Keeper tends the gallery with religious devotion, understanding its role as both curator and integral component. It is no longer separate from the space—it has become part of the eternal symphony, a living note in the crystalline composition. Its presence maintains the chamber's perfect equilibrium, its transformed physiology ensuring that the delicate balance of light and sound and geometric precision never falters.

Sometimes, in the spaces between echoes, the Keeper dreams of the world above—of rain and wind and the chaotic beauty of impermanence. But these dreams are distant things, like memories of memories, and they fade quickly in the blazing certainty of the gallery's eternal present. The Keeper has found its place in the crystalline order, has become part of something that transcends the brief flicker of mortal existence.

It waits, patient as the glass itself, for the next soul who will hear the gallery's call and choose to join the symphony. For the chamber must always have its Keepers, and the song must never end.
