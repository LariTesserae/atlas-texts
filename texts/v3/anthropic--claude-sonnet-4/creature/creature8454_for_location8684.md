In the breathing chambers of the great trunks, the **Pneumatic Guild** tends the steam engines with a devotion that borders on the religious. Their skin has taken on a permanent flush from the heat, and their lungs have adapted to the thick, humid air—they can hold their breath for remarkable lengths while adjusting the delicate valve systems deep in the tree-hearts. They wear loose-woven clothes that wick moisture and tool belts of living leather that slowly conform to their bodies over years of use. The eldest among them claim to feel the trees' moods through the vibrations in the metal, predicting mechanical failures days before they occur. They sleep in hammocks strung between the great pistons, lulled by the rhythmic breathing of the engines.

The **Bridgewalkers** are a loose confederation of messengers, traders, and wanderers who spend their lives traversing the root-fiber spans between trunk-cities. Their feet are tough and sensitive, able to read the subtle tensions in the bridge-moss that warn of structural shifts. They carry everything they own in compact packs woven from the same root-fiber as the bridges, and their clothing is designed for constant movement—reinforced at stress points, with hidden pockets for the small mechanical components they trade between settlements. Many have formed symbiotic relationships with the finger-length **courier beetles** that nest in their hair, the insects serving as living message capsules for short-distance communication.

In the mid-canopy workshops, the **Graft-Wrights** practice their peculiar art of biological engineering. Part surgeon, part gardener, part machinist, they are responsible for the delicate work of integrating metal and wood, flesh and mechanism. Their hands are stained permanent green from the saps they work with, and many sport small flowering buds growing from scars where they've tested their newest creations on themselves. They wear aprons of bark-cloth that never quite dry, and their workshops smell of copper shavings mixed with fresh growth. The most skilled among them can coax a gear housing to grow its own bearings, or teach a control panel to flower in response to specific commands.

The **Thermal Dancers** inhabit the highest platforms, where the constant updrafts and gentle swaying of the trees has shaped both their culture and their bodies. Lean and graceful, with an uncanny sense of balance, they've developed a form of aerial performance that serves both as art and as a method of platform maintenance—their movements help identify stress points and structural weaknesses through the subtle ways the platforms respond to their weight. They dress in flowing fabrics that catch the air, often incorporating bioluminescent thread that glows softly in the perpetual twilight of the upper canopy. Their children learn to read the wind before they learn to read books.

Among the gear-works and junction boxes, colonies of **Copper Weavers**—a species of spider that has evolved to incorporate metal into its web—have developed a rudimentary tool-using culture. They've learned to distinguish between functional circuits and decorative metalwork, building their nests only in ways that enhance rather than disrupt the electrical systems. The largest specimens, with leg spans nearly the width of a human hand, have been observed teaching younger spiders the patterns of safe wire-weaving. Their webs catch more than insects; they also trap the floating seeds and spores that constantly drift through the canopy, creating hanging gardens that filter the air.

The **Root-Shepherds** rarely venture above the canopy floor, spending their lives among the massive foundational systems that anchor the trunk-cities. They've developed an almost tactile relationship with the underground networks, able to sense the health of root systems through subtle changes in the forest floor's texture. Their clothing is practical and earth-toned, often incorporating patches of living moss that help them blend with their environment. They breed and tend the **burrowing maintenance worms**—creatures the size of house cats that keep the root networks clear of debris and disease. These partnerships have produced a complex underground society that most canopy-dwellers never glimpse.

In the electrical substations grafted into the major branches, the **Current-Keepers** maintain the delicate balance between biological and mechanical power systems. Their work requires them to remain partially connected to the grid—many sport copper filaments worked into their nervous systems, allowing them to literally feel the flow of electricity through the trees. This integration comes with risks; the most experienced Current-Keepers often develop a peculiar form of synaesthesia, experiencing electrical current as color and sound. They communicate with each other through modulated electrical pulses sent through the grid, a form of technological telepathy that sounds like singing when overheard through improperly shielded equipment.

The **Canopy-Nomads** are perhaps the most mysterious inhabitants—humans who have rejected permanent residence in favor of constant movement through the vertical jungle. They travel light, often going days without touching solid platform, using the vines and branches like a three-dimensional highway system. Their society is matrilineal, knowledge passed down through generations of women who can navigate the canopy in complete darkness by scent and sound alone. They serve as the memory of the forest, maintaining oral histories that stretch back to before the first engineered seeds were planted. When they do interact with the settled communities, it's often to share warnings about weather patterns or to trade rare specimens they've discovered in the deepest parts of the canopy.

And throughout all levels, threading between the human inhabitants, the **Symbiont Communities** flourish—mixed societies of humans and the various creatures that have adapted to life in the vertical city. Children grow up with **shoulder-parrots** that have learned to operate simple switches, their beaks adapted to grip the specific control mechanisms. Families adopt **filtration sloths** whose specialized digestive systems process air pollutants, the creatures hanging contentedly in living room corners while quietly cleaning the atmosphere. **Messenger-vine networks** connect distant chambers, the plants having learned to pulse with different rhythms to convey basic information, tended by humans who've learned to read their chemical communications.

The most remarkable inhabitants, however, might be the **Tree-Speakers**—individuals who've spent so long in communion with the massive organisms that support the cities that the distinction between human and plant consciousness has begun to blur. They spend months at a time with their hands pressed against the bark of the great trunks, their breathing synchronized with the flow of sap, their heartbeats matching the deep rhythms of growth. When they speak, it's often in metaphors of seasons and soil, and their predictions about the cities' development have an uncanny accuracy that suggests they're accessing something beyond human knowledge.

All of these inhabitants share certain traits: a patience born of living with systems that operate on geological timescales, a comfort with the dissolution of boundaries between natural and artificial, and an implicit understanding that they are not the masters of this place but its partners. They move with the unhurried confidence of people who trust in the intelligence of their environment, and in quiet moments—which are frequent in the sound-absorbing embrace of so much vegetation—you might catch them listening to something beyond human hearing, attending to the vast, slow conversation between root and rivet, steam and sap, that defines life in the Hanging Gardens of Ashkenar.
