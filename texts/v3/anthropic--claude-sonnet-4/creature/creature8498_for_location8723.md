The Shapers move through their vertical realm with a fluid grace born of countless generations dwelling among the living crystal. They are a tall, slender people whose skin has taken on a faint translucency over the millennia, pale as moonstone with veins of deeper color that pulse gently beneath the surface—echoing the rhythms of their crystal home. Their eyes have grown large and luminous to gather the cathedral's dim light, shifting from amber to green like the formations they tend, pupils that dilate wide in the perpetual twilight.

Their fingers, elongated and sensitive, can read the crystal's intentions through the slightest vibration. Each Shaper's hands tell the story of their craft—some stained permanently green from coaxing the moss-creatures, others bearing the subtle geometric scars that come from learning to touch the ancient formations without being overwhelmed by their alien purpose. The eldest among them move with an uncanny stillness, their breathing synchronized to the crystal's harmonic resonance until they seem more mineral than flesh.

They speak little, having developed a complex language of gesture and touch that travels through the crystal itself. A palm pressed to a spire can send messages throughout the city, vibrations that other Shapers read through their feet, their shoulders, their foreheads as they lean against the living walls. When they do vocalize, their voices have adapted to the hollow's strange acoustics—low, thrumming tones that blend with the crystal's song rather than fighting it.

The Shapers dress in woven fibers from the moss-creatures, garments that shift color and texture as they move, breathing fabrics that respond to the gas-heavy air. Their tools are extensions of themselves, passed down and modified through generations until each bronze shear and bone needle carries the subtle imprint of hundreds of hands. They craft nothing new, only guide what already grows, patient as stone, intuitive as water.

Children are born translucent as jellyfish and gradually develop their color through interaction with the crystals, each generation more adapted to their vertical world. The eldest, having tended the formations for centuries, begin to crystallize themselves near the end of their lives—not death, but a deeper kind of becoming. Their consciousness disperses into the harmonic network, their wisdom becoming part of the city's song.

There is among them a deep quietude, a sense that they are caretakers of something far older and more significant than themselves. They tend their gardens of impossible organisms, guide the growth of new chambers, and listen always to the crystal's ancient memory singing in frequencies just below the edge of hearing. In the deepest part of the hollow, where the oldest formations pulse with geometries that strain comprehension, a few Shapers spend their entire lives in contemplation, slowly learning to read the mountain's dreams.

They are a people shaped by patience and wonder, growing like their city—not built, but becoming.
