**The Foundry Dwellers**

In the shallower pools where copper minerals bloom like underwater gardens, the **Tinkers** make their homes. No larger than a child's hand, these creatures appear to be living amalgamations of flesh and metal—their pale, translucent skin shot through with veins of actual copper that pulse with their heartbeat. They have learned to cultivate the mineral deposits, coaxing them into useful shapes: delicate tools, temporary shelters, even crude instruments that chime in the mineral wind. Their fingers end in natural styluses of bone that they use to etch patterns into the growing crystal formations, creating spiraling galleries where they store their collections of perfectly spherical water droplets, each one containing a different mineral concentration.

The Tinkers speak in soft percussion—tapping messages against pipe walls, drumming on metal surfaces with fingernails that ring like small bells. They seem to understand the machinery better than it understands itself, often found gathered around a stuck gear, their combined weight just enough to help it complete another revolution. When they sleep, they float just beneath the surface tension of their chosen pools, breathing through their skin like the salamanders, their copper veins glowing faintly in the refracted light.

Deeper down, in the pools where iron deposits create rust-red caverns beneath the surface, dwells **Old Brass**—though whether it's a creature, a spirit, or simply the accumulated consciousness of the foundry itself is unclear. Those who glimpse it describe something massive and serpentine, its body incorporating actual pieces of the ancient machinery: brass fittings that have become part of its spine, iron wheels that serve as joints, copper pipes that might be ribs or might be parasites. It moves through the deepest pools with surprising grace, maintaining the equipment through some alien understanding of its purpose.

Old Brass never surfaces completely, but its presence explains the steady rhythm of the breathing chimney, the way broken pipes occasionally repair themselves overnight, the reason certain gears continue to turn despite having no visible power source. The salamanders treat it with reverence, often seen swimming in formation around its submerged bulk. The Tinkers leave offerings at the edges of its domain—perfectly crafted metal flowers, or arrangements of colored stones that dissolve after exactly three tides of the mineral pools.

In the highest terraces, where the water is barely ankle-deep and the mineral concentration creates an almost prismatic surface, live the **Prism Dancers**. These beings appear to be made entirely of living crystal, their bodies refracting light into impossible colors. They exist primarily as light patterns, becoming solid only when they choose to, their crystalline forms growing directly from the mineral deposits of their pools. During the day, they're nearly invisible—just dancing patterns of reflected light on stone walls. But at dawn and dusk, when the angle of light transforms the entire valley into a cathedral of illumination, they become radiant, their movements creating aurora-like displays that paint the steam in colors that have no names.

The Prism Dancers feed on light itself, absorbing the solar radiation filtered through the steam and concentrated by the water's surface tension. They communicate through color—complex conversations painted across the walls in hues that shift from copper green through sulfur yellow to deep iron red. They seem to be the foundry's archivists, encoding information about each day's light patterns, each shift in the mineral composition of their pools, each subtle change in the ancient machinery's rhythm.

And in the deepest pool of all, where the water stays mysteriously clear around the breathing chimney, something else waits. The Tinkers whisper about **The Keeper**—not quite seen, never quite absent, responsible for maintaining the perfect clarity of this central basin. Some describe it as a presence felt rather than observed: a gentle current that guides debris away from the thermal vents, a subtle adjustment of mineral flows that preserves the transparency of the water.

Others insist they've seen it—a figure that seems to be made of the clear water itself, visible only as a distortion, like looking through the curved surface of a lens. The Keeper tends the thermal vents with infinite patience, ensuring they continue their ancient exhalation, maintaining the delicate balance that keeps this impossible pocket of the world stable. It's said that the Keeper remembers the foundry's original purpose, holds the memory of what all this machinery was meant to accomplish—though what that might be remains as mysterious as the Keeper itself.

When storms threaten the high valley, when geological shifts might disrupt the thermal vents, when time itself seems to press too heavily on this place caught between eras, it's the Keeper that holds everything together. The other inhabitants sense its presence like a deep, steady heartbeat beneath their own smaller rhythms—the foundational pulse that allows all their delicate, mineral-bright lives to continue in this steaming sanctuary where industry and nature have learned, impossibly, to dream together.
