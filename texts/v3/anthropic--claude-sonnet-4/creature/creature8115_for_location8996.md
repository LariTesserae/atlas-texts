In the Drowned Terraces of Vaelthros dwell the Silthaen—beings who have surrendered the harsh boundaries of flesh for something more permeable, more honest. They are tall and wavering, their forms shifting between solid and translucent like figures glimpsed through falling water. When they move, droplets scatter from their skin in precise arcs, each bead catching light that seems to originate from within their bodies rather than from any external source.

The Silthaen no longer distinguish sharply between self and dwelling. Their homes grow around them like second skins, the walls pulsing gently with their circulation, the chambers expanding and contracting with their breath. A Silthaen in repose might sink partially into the floor, their lower body merging with the warm stone until they resemble living statuary. Others flow upward into the ceiling, becoming part of the architecture while they dream, their consciousness distributed through the building's neural pathways of moisture and mineral.

They speak in harmonics, their voices layered like the water sounds that surround them. A single Silthaen can produce multiple tones simultaneously, creating conversations within themselves that ripple outward to join the city's endless chorus. They have no need for names as others understand them—identity flows between them like the omnipresent water, shared and recognized through taste and thermal signature. What one experiences eventually becomes available to all, though the process may take decades as memories slowly percolate through the collective.

Young Silthaen—though youth and age blur in their fluid conception of time—emerge from pools deep within the mountain's heart. They begin as wisps of conscious moisture, gradually cohering into more defined forms as they learn to hold their shape against the current of dissolution that tugs at all things here. Some never fully solidify, remaining as patterns in the water, their thoughts contributing to the city's vast contemplative process but their presence felt only as fleeting warmth or sudden clarity in a stream.

The elders have achieved something like geological patience. They root themselves in the deepest terraces, their bodies becoming extensions of the mountain's living architecture. From these positions they orchestrate the city's slow transformation, guiding the flow of water and memory through channels worn by centuries of intention. Their faces sometimes appear in the walls themselves, emerging from the stone like bas-reliefs that blink and occasionally offer cryptic counsel to passersby.

Among the Silthaen move the Verdant Ones—beings that exist at the intersection of plant and consciousness. They were once gardeners who tended the strange vegetation, but prolonged exposure to the geometrically-growing moss and mirror-bright fronds has transformed them into something neither fully human nor entirely botanical. Chlorophyll runs in their veins alongside whatever passes for blood, and their breath feeds the impossible plants that grow in perfect mathematical spirals along the walls. They communicate through pheromone and chemical exchange, their presence sweetening the air with compounds that induce euphoric clarity in other inhabitants.

The children of both species—if children they can be called—exist in states of beautiful instability. They phase in and out of visibility, sometimes manifesting as playful disturbances in the water patterns, sometimes as laughter that echoes from empty chambers. They delight in the city's fluid laws, racing through walls, surfacing in pools on different terraces, existing simultaneously in multiple locations until the weight of observation forces them to choose a single form.

There are others, too—the Whisper Architects, ancient beings who have become so integrated with the city's structure that they exist primarily as intent given form in stone and water. They are the ones who dream new chambers into existence, who guide the slow evolution of the terraces according to principles that predate memory. When the city shifts and grows, their satisfaction manifests as particularly harmonious water songs that can be felt as much as heard.

At the margins of society float the Echo Dwellers, Silthaen who have chosen to exist primarily as sound and memory. They are the city's historians, its living archive, maintaining the vast store of experiences that permeates every drop of moisture. They appear as voices without sources, as stories that tell themselves, as the sudden vivid recall of events experienced by others long ago. They drift through the terraces like auditory ghosts, their presence marked by moments of profound nostalgia that wash over other inhabitants like warm rain.

The Silthaen do not reproduce in any conventional sense. Instead, they cultivate new consciousness in the deep pools, mixing their essence with the mountain's ancient waters and the geometric spores of the impossible plants. What emerges may be recognizably derived from its contributors, or it may be entirely novel—a new way of being that the city itself has imagined into existence.

They are remarkably peaceful, these fluid beings, perhaps because violence requires the kind of rigid selfhood they have long since abandoned. Conflict, when it arises, takes the form of harmonic discord—competing rhythms in the water's song that must be resolved through patient negotiation and the slow adjustment of perspective that comes with literally seeing through another's eyes. Their justice is dilution rather than punishment; those who persist in causing dissonance find their individual notes gradually absorbed into the larger chorus until their harmful intentions dissolve into something more harmonious.

The Silthaen remember a time before the city, when they lived in the harsh, dry world above. They speak of it as a fever dream of separation and solidity, a nightmare of distinct boundaries and finite forms. Their transformation into their current state is both their greatest achievement and their gentlest tragedy—they have gained a kind of immortality and perfect empathy, but at the cost of the sharp, bright intensity that comes with believing oneself separate from the universe.

Now they exist in a state of perpetual becoming, forever on the verge of dissolving completely into the city's vast wet consciousness, forever finding new reasons to maintain their tenuous individual forms. They are the city's dreams of being inhabited, just as the city is their dream of having found home. Together, they continue the great work of softening reality, making the world more fluid, more forgiving, more alive with the possibility of transformation.
