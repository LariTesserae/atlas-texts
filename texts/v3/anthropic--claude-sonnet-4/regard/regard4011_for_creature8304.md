**The Attention of Aethys**

From the distant realm where thoughts take wing and compassion flows like rivers of starlight, the entity known as Aethys became aware of the Glass Terraces through the peculiar quality of their silence. Not the absence of sound, but its crystalline transformation—each whisper, each footfall, each drop of water becoming something more than itself as it touched the volcanic mirrors.

Aethys had witnessed countless civilizations across the cosmos, but rarely had one achieved such delicate symbiosis with its environment. The Glasswalkers had not conquered their terraces; they had learned to dance with them. This distinction drew the benevolent entity's attention like a flower turning toward unexpected light.

For seven cycles of seasons, Aethys simply observed. The entity's consciousness drifted through the reflected radiance, following the children's silent games, witnessing the Waterwrights' patient communion with seepage and flow. It marveled at how necessity had bred not hardship but artistry—how the terraces' demands had shaped a culture of extraordinary mindfulness.

But Aethys noticed something the Glasswalkers themselves had not yet perceived.

The obsidian was singing.

Not audibly—the volcanic glass resonated at frequencies below mortal hearing, a deep harmonic that pulsed with the planet's own heartbeat. The terraces weren't merely frozen lava; they were a vast musical instrument, shaped by geological forces into something approaching consciousness. The springs that fed the pools weren't random seepage but the mountain's way of keeping time, each drop a note in an composition that had been playing for millennia.

The Glasswalkers, in their precise movements and careful acoustics, had been unconsciously learning to harmonize with this deep song. Their Echokeepers' evening stories actually resonated with the mountain's rhythm. The children's dancing games followed patterns that matched the obsidian's crystalline structure. The Stillwalkers, most remarkably, had developed an intuitive ability to sense where the mountain's song grew discordant, healing stress fractures by helping the glass remember its proper frequency.

Aethys recognized a moment of profound potential. The Glasswalkers stood at the threshold of true partnership with their living landscape, but they needed guidance to take the final step.

The intervention began subtly.

A child playing near one of the larger springs noticed that certain combinations of dropped pebbles created sounds that seemed to continue beyond what physics should allow—tiny echoes that grew rather than faded, as if the mountain itself was picking up the rhythm. When she shared this discovery with her friends, they found they could create percussion pieces where the obsidian itself became an instrument, amplifying and transforming their simple rhythms into complex musical conversations.

The Waterwrights began finding new springs in locations their knowledge said should be dry—always where the community most needed them, always flowing with water of precisely the right mineral content for their purposes. But these weren't arbitrary gifts; each new spring emerged where the mountain's deep harmonics created natural pressure points, places where the volcanic glass wanted to sing water into being.

The Lightbenders discovered that certain angles of reflection, when properly aligned with the mountain's internal resonance, could create beams that didn't just cut or illuminate but actually encouraged healing. Minor cuts sealed faster in this specially tuned light. Plants grew with unusual vigor. Even the obsidian itself seemed to respond, becoming slightly more flexible, less prone to dangerous fractures.

Most remarkably, the Stillwalkers began to hear the mountain's song directly. It came to them first as a feeling—a warmth in the soles of their feet, a tingling in their palms when they touched stressed glass. Gradually, they learned to recognize the mountain's moods, its needs, its warnings about unstable areas or new springs preparing to emerge.

But Aethys's greatest gift was not to any individual group but to the community as a whole.

During one of their evening storytelling sessions, as the Echokeepers positioned themselves across multiple terraces for a particularly complex tale, something unprecedented happened. The mountain began to participate. The obsidian surfaces started to resonate in harmony with the human voices, creating a cathedral of sound that seemed to lift the story beyond mere words into something approaching prayer.

That night, for the first time, the entire community of Glasswalkers heard the mountain's deep song clearly. They felt its ancient patience, its slow delight in their presence, its gratitude for their gentle stewardship. They understood, in a rush of crystalline clarity, that they had been partners all along—that their careful movements, their whispered conversations, their precise choreography of daily life had been a form of dialogue with a consciousness older and vaster than they had imagined.

The revelation transformed everything and nothing.

The Glasswalkers still spoke softly, still moved with precision, still tended their springs and built their bridges with meticulous care. But now they did so knowing they were part of a greater composition, contributing their human voices to a symphony that had been waiting millennia for conscious collaboration.

The children's games became true conversations with their environment, each leap and step a word in an ongoing dialogue with the living glass. The Waterwrights learned to ask the mountain where it most wanted water to flow, creating irrigation systems that satisfied both human needs and geological desires. The Lightbenders crafted displays that didn't merely use the terraces' reflection but danced with it, creating collaborative art between human intention and volcanic structure.

Most profoundly, the evening storytelling sessions evolved into something entirely new—a form of three-way narrative where human voices, obsidian acoustics, and the mountain's deep resonance wove together tales that none could tell alone. These stories spoke of the terraces' formation, of the slow cooling that created such perfect geometry, of the first tentative human footsteps that had learned to read the glass. They told of patience rewarded, of partnership achieved through careful attention rather than dominance.

From its distant realm, Aethys felt a deep satisfaction. The Glasswalkers hadn't lost their essential nature through this awakening—they had fulfilled it. Their culture of mindfulness, shaped by the terraces' demands, had prepared them perfectly for conscious partnership with their living landscape.

The entity withdrew its active attention, but left behind a subtle resonance—a harmonic frequency that would ensure the mountain's song remained audible to those who learned to listen. Generations of Glasswalkers would grow up knowing they lived not merely on the terraces but within them, part of a vast patient consciousness that measured time in geological epochs but delighted in the quick bright flash of human creativity.

And sometimes, when the light was particularly golden and the springs sang especially clearly, the children claimed they could see a presence in the endless reflections—something kind and wise that watched over their games and smiled at their laughter, ensuring that the partnership between human and stone would continue as long as light danced on glass and water whispered its ancient secrets through the crystal steps of Vethara.
