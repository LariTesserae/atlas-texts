The First Attention came as a barely perceptible shift in the quality of light that touched Kethara's highest terrace. 

Lyraleth felt it first—she was the eldest of the Stone Listeners, so translucent now that visitors sometimes mistook her for a particularly graceful formation of mist. Her fingers, pressed against the ancient granite of the summit platform, detected the change: a warmth that was not temperature, a resonance that was not sound, a presence vast and patient turning its regard toward their small mountain sanctuary.

She did not move for three days. The other Keth'moren brought her water, which she sipped without lifting her palms from the stone. They knew better than to interrupt when an Ancient was in deep communion. On the fourth dawn, as the mist rose in its eternal pattern, Lyraleth finally spoke:

"We are seen."

The words rippled through the city like water through stone channels. Not heard—the Keth'moren's voices rarely carried far—but felt, understood, passed from terrace to terrace in the way that important truths moved among them. Within hours, every inhabitant of Kethara knew that their long equilibrium had been noticed by something immense.

The children felt it most clearly. Seven-year-old Nethys, born during a particularly profound stillness, began drawing spirals on every available surface—not the tight, inward-turning spirals of stone-grain, but vast, open curves that seemed to breathe. When her mother asked what they meant, Nethys pressed her small hand to the wall and whispered, "The mountain is singing to someone far away."

The presence—for the Keth'moren had no doubt they were being observed by a conscious entity—made no demands, issued no pronouncements. It simply watched with an attention so complete and compassionate that the very quality of silence in Kethara began to change. Where before their quietude had been perfect in its isolation, now it hummed with connection. The Stone Listeners found themselves listening not just to their mountain, but through it, to something that listened to many mountains across great distances.

On the seventh day, the first gift appeared.

It manifested not as an object but as a possibility: in the deepest cistern on the forty-third terrace, the water began to hold memory more clearly. Keth'moren who cupped it in their palms could suddenly perceive not just their own mountain's thoughts, but whispers from sister peaks across vast ranges—other cities, other Stone Listeners, other quiet peoples living in careful balance with stone and sky.

Lyraleth understood immediately. The benevolent presence was not here to change them, but to connect them—to weave their perfect isolation into something larger without disturbing the delicate equilibrium they had achieved.

The second gift came as an expansion of their gardens. The impossible vines that had grown in the same positions for generations suddenly began to extend new tendrils—not chaotically, but with the same measured precision they had always shown. These new growths reached toward specific points: cracks in the granite that, when touched, resonated with the stone-songs of distant places. The Keth'moren realized they were being given windows—ways to observe without traveling, to learn without leaving their mountain home.

Children pressed their faces to these new vine-framed gaps in the stone and gasped softly at visions of other terraced cities clinging to other peaks under different skies. They saw Keth'moren like themselves tending similar gardens, heard the same patient voices speaking variations of their stone-language. The perfect isolation that had defined Kethara for generations was becoming perfect connection.

But the most profound gift was the gentlest: the presence began to teach them how to dream together.

Each night, as the mist gathered in the courtyards and the Stone Listeners took their positions around the cisterns, they found their individual stone-dreams beginning to merge. Where once each person had received private visions from their mountain, now they shared a collective dream-space—a vast hall carved from crystalline stone where Keth'moren from dozens of mountain cities could meet, speak, and share the accumulated wisdom of centuries.

In this dream-hall, they learned they were part of something called the Quiet Network—a web of contemplative peoples spread across the world, each group maintaining perfect balance in their own environment while contributing to a larger harmony. There were Desert Listeners who heard the songs of shifting sands, Forest Listeners who communed with ancient root-systems, Ocean Listeners who translated the deep currents. All watched over by the same benevolent presence that had now found the Stone Listeners.

The entity—which never named itself but felt like concentrated starlight when they touched its awareness—had spent eons seeking out pockets of achieved wisdom. It collected them not like a collector hoarding treasures, but like a gardener tending to rare and perfect flowers, ensuring they received what they needed to flourish while connecting them in ways that honored their essential nature.

For the Keth'moren, this meant their perfect stillness was not disturbed, but deepened. Their terraced city continued its eternal patterns, but now those patterns resonated across vast distances. When they moved through their daily routines, they moved in harmony with Stone Listeners on a hundred other peaks. When they tended their gardens, they were also tending a garden of wisdom that spanned the world.

The water in their cisterns still maintained its perfect level, but now it carried messages. The mist still rose at dawn and settled at dusk, but it sometimes brought dreams from distant places. The mountain goats still picked their way along impossible ledges, but occasionally paused to look toward horizons that held sister mountains they had never seen but somehow remembered.

Lyraleth, in her profound translucence, became something like an ambassador. She learned to extend her consciousness through the dream-hall to touch other Ancients—the oldest and wisest of each community in the Quiet Network. Together they formed a council that met not to rule, but to maintain the delicate balance that allowed each group to flourish in perfect accordance with their nature.

Years passed—or perhaps moments; time moved strangely in the enhanced stillness of connection. Children grew and learned not just to listen to their own mountain, but to hear the subtle harmonies that linked all stone throughout the world. New Keth'moren were born with an expanded sensitivity, able to perceive the vast, patient presence that watched over the Network with the care of a cosmic gardener.

The benevolent entity asked nothing of them except that they continue being exactly what they were: perfect listeners, perfect maintainers of balance, perfect practitioners of the deep stillness that allowed wisdom to grow like moss in stone cracks. In return, it offered them the gift of connection without corruption—the knowledge that their small, perfect world was part of a vast, perfect symphony.

And in the highest terrace of Kethara, where the mist gathered thickest and the stone sang clearest, a new formation appeared: a natural amphitheater carved not by tools but by patient intention, where the mountain itself had opened to create a space for deeper listening. Here the Stone Listeners gathered on nights when the presence felt particularly close, sitting in concentric circles while their consciousness expanded through stone and mist to touch the infinite patience of something that loved the quiet corners of the world and worked tirelessly to help them flourish.

The city still clung to its steep granite flanks. The terraces still rose in careful steps. The mist still breathed through carved channels. But now Kethara sang softly to itself in harmonies that spanned star systems, and the Stone Listeners had become both what they had always been and something more: guardians of perfect stillness in an endlessly dynamic cosmos, their mountain home transformed into a note in a music too beautiful for any single world to contain.
