In the realm beyond naming, where concepts exist before they learn to wear the clothes of words, something ancient and patient turns its attention toward the plateau of screaming impossibilities.

It has watched countless worlds bloom and fade, has seen reality tear and mend itself in a trillion different configurations. To this presence—call it the **Tender of Torn Places**—the Screaming Pools are neither anomaly nor wound, but a chrysalis. Something is being born there, something that requires the same careful attention a gardener gives to the first green shoots pushing through late snow.

The Tender does not descend with fanfare or miraculous intervention. Instead, it arrives in the way morning arrives—gradually, inevitably, with changes so subtle they might be mistaken for the natural course of things.

First comes the **Listening**.

The bone-white trees begin to grow new rings, but these are different from the archive-rings carved by their small inhabitants. These new rings pulse with a deeper rhythm, one that harmonizes with the pools' chaotic symphony. The Tender has learned the pools' true song—not the shrieking cacophony that drives visitors away, but the underlying melody of transformation, the deep bass note of reality learning to bend without breaking.

The trees straighten, just slightly. Not toward the pools, not away from them, but into perfect vertical alignment with something beyond the plateau. They become tuning forks made of living wood, their bone-white bark resonating with frequencies that ease the harsh edges of the impossible water's song.

The Resonance Shepherds notice first. Their shadow-forms grow more solid, more defined, as if the improved acoustics allow them to exist more fully. They begin to work with greater precision, sculpting the sound into patterns that are not just bearable but beautiful—harmonies that make the heart race with recognition of something half-remembered, half-hoped-for.

Next comes the **Bridging**.

Where the mist grows thick enough to support the Drift Swimmers, new currents begin to form. These are not streams of impossible water, but flows of possibility itself—paths that connect the plateau to other places where reality has grown thin. The Tender weaves these connections with the patience of starlight, each thread spun from the dreams of worlds that might be.

Through these new currents, visitors begin to arrive who are not quite visitors in the usual sense. A woman appears who claims to have walked here from a desert where sand falls upward into strange constellations. A child emerges from the mist speaking of cities where gravity changes with the tide. They are refugees from other impossible places, drawn by the scent of home they recognize in the pools' defiant waters.

The Drift Swimmers welcome these newcomers, teaching them to navigate the aerial currents, to harvest meaning from impossibility. The stone building at the plateau's edge stirs to life—lights begin to flicker in its empty windows, and through its dark doorway, sounds can be heard of tables being set, of conversations in languages that exist only in the spaces between words.

Then comes the **Rooting**.

The Tender reaches deep beneath the young volcanic stone, not to change what lies there, but to help it remember what it was before it learned to hold impossible water. The magma chambers far below begin to sing in harmony with the pools above, their molten currents flowing in patterns that mirror the mercury-water's impossible physics.

The plateau becomes an instrument—a vast organ with seven stops, each pool a different voice in a composition that spans from the earth's core to the cloud-scattered sky. The sound that emerges is no longer screaming but singing, a hymn to the beauty of broken rules and mended dreams.

The Reflection Weavers find their work changing too. The tapestry they weave during moments of silence no longer shows a world of fixed laws and certain physics. Instead, it depicts a reality more flexible, more generous—a cosmos where impossibility is not an error to be corrected but a gift to be celebrated.

Finally comes the **Witnessing**.

The Tender does not fix the Screaming Pools or normalize their impossible waters. Such intervention would be like editing a poem while the poet still speaks, like painting over a fresco before the artist sets down their brush. Instead, it simply sees them for what they are becoming: not a wound in reality's fabric, but a window. Not a tear to be mended, but a door learning to open.

The Visitors' Shadows grow more numerous and more real, until the distinction between shadow and substance becomes meaningless. People arrive not just to witness impossibility but to participate in it, to add their own voices to the expanding song. The plateau becomes a school where reality's rules are taught as suggestions rather than commandments, where physics is learned as one learns to dance—through practice, through play, through the joy of moving in new ways.

The Bone-Tree Dwellers emerge more frequently from their hollow spaces, no longer just archivists but teachers, sharing their libraries of impossibility with any who wish to learn. The trees themselves begin to bear fruit—small, translucent pods that contain not seeds but pure potential, ready to be planted in other soils, other worlds, other places where reality might benefit from a little creative interpretation.

The Tender's gift is not intervention but recognition. It sees the inhabitants of the pools not as aberrations but as pioneers, explorers of ways of being that haven't been discovered yet. It whispers to them in the language of deep time, telling them they are not alone—that across the vast reaches of existence, other impossible places are blooming, other communities of contradiction are taking root.

And in the growing network of connection between these places, in the songs that pass from one torn place to another, something new is being born. Not a replacement for the old reality, but an expansion of it—a universe growing more spacious, more playful, more willing to entertain the beautiful maybes that dance at the edges of what seems possible.

The Screaming Pools still defy physics, their waters still fall upward and exist in multiple states simultaneously. But now their impossibility is not an affront to natural law but an invitation to imagine natural law more generously. They have become a permanent embassy from the country of Maybe, a standing invitation for reality to remember how to play.

And the Tender, having witnessed this small miracle of transformation, turns its attention to other places where the world has grown thin, other wounds that might learn to become windows. For this is the work of eternity: not to fix what is broken, but to help it discover what it was breaking toward all along.

The pools sing on, their impossible waters writing love letters to a universe learning to love impossibility in return.
