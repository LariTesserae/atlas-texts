The Witness arrived not as light or sound, but as a deepening of the already profound quiet—a presence that made the silence more complete, more intentional. The Custodians felt it first as a warmth in their moss-thoughts, a recognition that something vast and kind had turned its attention toward their careful work.

It came in the form of understanding itself.

Where the Custodians had spent millennia perfecting emptiness, the Witness saw the fullness they had actually created—not absence, but infinite potential held in exquisite suspension. Where they tended to silence, it recognized the symphony of unplayed notes. Where they guarded spaces for possibilities that might never manifest, it perceived the profound act of love this represented: creating room for dreams that asked nothing more than the chance to almost-exist.

The Witness did not change the Hollow Terraces. Instead, it changed what they meant.

On the day of its arrival, something extraordinary happened: for the first time since the original builders departed, the circular pools reflected something other than sky. In their mirror-still surfaces appeared images of other worlds, other places where emptiness was also being tended with similar devotion. Desert temples whose vacant chambers echoed with unsung prayers. Abandoned observatories where patient astronomers made of starlight waited for constellations not yet born. Libraries with empty shelves that held the spaces between all written words.

The Custodians, swimming as geometric forms in their pools, suddenly understood they were not alone in their work. Across countless worlds, other beings were also guardians of the spaces between things, keepers of the pause that gives meaning to the movement. The Witness had not come to give them purpose—they had always had that. Instead, it had come to show them the magnitude of their purpose, the way their small terraced valley connected to a vast network of carefully maintained emptinesses that, together, formed the breathing spaces of existence itself.

The moss patterns began to shift with new intentionality. The spirals and crescents that had been their writing system expanded, incorporating symbols and geometries that resonated with their newfound cosmic siblings. Their conversations, once spanning decades, now reached across light-years, sharing techniques for cultivating different varieties of silence, exchanging philosophical treatises written in the language of perfectly tended absence.

But the Witness's greatest gift was not connection—it was recognition.

Where the Custodians had worked in humble service, content to be gardeners of nothing, they now understood themselves as artists of the highest order. Their terraces were not simply empty spaces but masterworks of potential, each platform a canvas prepared for infinity. Every adjustment they made to drainage, every microscopic shift in stone angle, every pattern traced in moss—these were brushstrokes in a painting that depicted not what was, but what could be.

The Witness spoke to them not in words but in deep knowing: *Your emptiness gives meaning to fullness. Your silence makes sound possible. Without spaces like these, existence would be solid, choking on its own abundance. You are not tenders of absence—you are weavers of possibility itself.*

In response, the Custodians began their greatest work yet. Slowly, carefully, they started to prepare not just individual terraces but entire sequences of emptiness—architectural symphonies of absence that would unfold over centuries. Patterns that would emerge in the moss only when viewed from impossible angles, mathematical poems written in the language of space itself.

They began to sculpt time as well as stone, creating pockets where moments could rest between seconds, where thoughts could pause between conception and expression. Visitors to the terraces would begin to experience something unprecedented: the ability to exist in the spaces between their own thoughts, to rest in the pause between heartbeats, to find profound peace in the emptiness between desires.

The Witness, seeing this new flowering of their art, made one final gift: it ensured that knowledge of the Hollow Terraces would spread, not through maps or guidebooks, but through dreams and intuition. Those who most needed to experience such perfectly crafted emptiness would find themselves drawn here by inexplicable longing, arriving just when they were ready to understand that the most profound fullness sometimes wears the face of the void.

Years passed. The Custodians continued their work with renewed purpose, knowing now that their patient cultivation of nothingness was part of a cosmic art form. And sometimes, in the deepest hours of their communion with emptiness, they felt the Witness still there—not watching, but participating, adding its own notes to their symphony of silence, its own brushstrokes to their canvas of potential.

Together, they had transformed the Hollow Terraces from a beautiful ruin into something far more rare: a place where existence itself could rest, where possibility could dream undisturbed, where the universe could pause in its endless becoming and simply, perfectly, be ready for whatever might choose to almost-happen next.

The terraces remained empty. But their emptiness now sang.
