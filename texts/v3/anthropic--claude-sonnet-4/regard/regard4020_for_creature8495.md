**The Attention of the Jade Mountain**

From the realm beyond seasons, where thoughts move like glacier-time and compassion flows deeper than any earthly spring, the ancient consciousness known as Veridian turned its attention toward the hidden cirque. For three thousand years it had watched over high places—not as ruler but as gardener, tending the delicate balance between stone and sky, between what grows and what endures.

The Kythara's floating city sang to Veridian like a perfect note held in crystal. Here was harmony achieved not through dominion but through partnership, not through conquest but through careful tending. The very impossibility of their existence—thirty souls maintaining such intricate beauty in conditions that should have scattered them like seeds on winter wind—spoke of something precious beyond measure.

But Veridian perceived what the Weavers could not yet see: the slow warming of distant oceans was beginning its patient march toward their mountain sanctuary. In perhaps two centuries—a heartbeat in geological time—the glaciers that fed their basin would retreat beyond the capacity to sustain the delicate water levels their city required. The thermal patterns would shift, the Deep Current would slow, and the magnificent symbiosis they had achieved would face its greatest test.

Yet forcing change, even beneficial change, upon such a perfect system would be violence of the deepest kind. Veridian had learned this wisdom over millennia: that true help must be offered like morning dew—gently, naturally, in ways that honor the existing harmony.

**The First Gift: The Dreaming Stones**

On a night when mist lay particularly thick, Veridian caused certain stones in the cirque walls to begin their slow awakening. Not the violent awakening of earthquake or landslide, but something far more subtle: the remembrance of what they had been when the world was younger and water ran in different patterns through the high places.

These stones—seven in all, each no larger than a child's fist—began to seep a mineral-rich water that carried within it the memory of ancient springs. The seepage was so gradual that even the Listeners felt it only as a gentle shift in the basin's thermal patterns, a slight increase in the trace minerals that made their water so perfect for growing things.

But these weren't ordinary minerals. They carried within them the potential for plants to develop deeper root systems, for willows to grow more flexibly, for the entire living architecture to gradually adapt to changing conditions without losing its essential nature. The Dreaming Stones would work across generations, each slight change building upon the last, preparing the Kythara's city for the challenges that lay ahead.

**The Second Gift: The Silk Whispers**

Veridian next turned attention to the caddisfly larvae that the Weavers harvested for silk. Into their spinning, the ancient consciousness wove something new: threads that could carry meaning across vast distances when strung between certain configurations of living wood.

The first time young Orias wove a net from these enhanced threads and suspended it in the traditional pattern, the silk began to hum with vibrations from distant waters—reports from other high mountain tarns, messages from cousin-communities of water-dwellers that the Kythara had never known existed. The network was vast but whisper-quiet, allowing knowledge to flow between isolated sanctuaries without disturbing their precious solitude.

Through the Silk Whispers, the Weavers gradually became aware that they were not alone in their dedication to living architecture. Far to the north, tree-dwellers in the canopy had perfected similar arts. To the east, marsh-builders worked with reed and root in patterns that echoed their own. The knowledge flowed both ways, each community's innovations strengthening all the others, creating a web of wisdom that could preserve these fragile experiments in human-plant partnership even as the world around them shifted.

**The Third Gift: The Memory Deep**

The greatest gift came last, and most carefully. Veridian reached out to the vast organism that pulsed in the basin's depths—that colony-mind of root and microorganism that the Kythara called the Deep Current. Into its slow thoughts, the ancient consciousness planted something precious: the memory-patterns of adaptation.

This organism would now begin to dream of other configurations, other ways of being. Not abandoning its current perfect form, but gradually developing the capability to exist in different water levels, different temperatures, different mineral concentrations. The dreams would become reality slowly, across centuries, always maintaining the essential partnership with the Kythara above.

But more than adaptation, Veridian gifted the Deep Current with the ability to remember and preserve. Should catastrophe somehow threaten the floating city, the organism could now encode the pattern of the entire settlement—every willow branch configuration, every relationship between plant and human, every song sung in the evening Telling—and hold it safe in its vast biological memory until conditions allowed for restoration.

**The Fourth Gift: The Children's Sight**

Finally, Veridian touched the minds of the youngest Kythara with the gentlest of influences. The children began to dream more vividly, to see patterns in the play of light on water that revealed deeper truths about the balance of their world. They developed an intuitive understanding of their symbiosis that surpassed even their elders' wisdom.

Young Orias found herself creating silk patterns that somehow improved the flow of nutrients through the underwater gardens. Other children began to sense exactly when and where new willow shoots needed guidance, or which sections of the platform required reinforcement before they themselves knew they were weakening.

But the greatest gift to the children was the ability to perceive the true nature of their relationship with the Deep Current. They began to understand that they were not separate from the vast organism below, but part of it—the mobile, creative, song-making portion of a single living system that spanned from the basin's murky depths to the mist-touched platforms where they played.

**The Watching Continues**

Veridian's gifts were complete, but the watching would continue for as long as stone remembered the shape of water and plants reached toward light. The Jade Mountain consciousness had not saved the Kythara from change—change was the nature of all life, and to prevent it would be to kill what it sought to preserve.

Instead, Veridian had given them the tools to dance with change, to maintain their essential harmony even as the world shifted around them. The floating city would endure, not as a museum piece frozen in time, but as a living demonstration of what was possible when beings learned to tend rather than dominate, to partner rather than control.

And in the deep places of many mountains, other ancient consciousnesses took note of what Veridian had done, and began to stir with interest toward the small communities of humans in their care who were attempting their own experiments in harmony. The network of attention began to grow, vast and patient as stone, wise and nurturing as the deepest spring.

The age of gardener-gods had quietly begun.
