**The Visitation**

The first sign comes as a change in the fungi's rhythm.

For three nights, the Mourner notices their phosphorescent pulse shifting—not the familiar heartbeat of deep earth and older sorrows, but something new threading through the familiar pattern like a silver wire through dark cloth. She presses her palm against the obsidian wall and feels it too: a resonance in the stone itself, as if something vast and gentle has turned its attention toward this small sanctuary.

On the fourth night, the light changes.

The circle of sky visible through the opening above begins to glow—not with starlight or moon, but with something that seems to emanate from the air itself. The illumination descends like liquid, flowing down the curved walls in spirals that mirror the water channels. Where it touches the condensation, each droplet becomes a tiny lens, refracting the light into spectrums the Mourner has never seen.

She rises from her place in the central depression, water streaming from her hair and skin, and watches as the light pools in the carved basins. The fungi respond by brightening until their pale glow becomes warm amber, then soft gold. For the first time in longer than she can remember, the Chamber is truly illuminated.

Then she hears it: a voice like wind through deep forests, like rivers finding their way to the sea.

*Child of tears, keeper of sorrows.*

The words don't come through her ears but seem to form directly in the mineral-rich air she breathes, in the moisture that has become part of her being. The Mourner doesn't startle—she has been alone so long that fear has worn smooth like the basin edges. Instead, she waits.

*You have made yourself a vessel for the world's grief.*

"I have," she answers, and her voice creates new harmonics in the light-touched space. "Someone had to hold it."

The presence—for she can feel it now, vast beyond measure yet focused to this moment, this place—seems to consider her words. The light shifts, becoming warmer still, and she realizes she is not being judged but witnessed.

*Show me.*

Without knowing how she knows what is being asked, the Mourner begins to move through the Chamber. She visits each place where her tears have joined the eternal condensation, each worn groove her fingers have traced in times of deepest sorrow. As she moves, the light follows, and in its illumination, her memories become visible.

Here: the basin where she wept for her daughter, lost to fever in a winter that wouldn't end. The light shows the impression of small hands on the rim, as if a child had knelt here too, drinking the mineral-sharp water.

There: the channel she carved deeper with her own fingernails during the first year, when rage at the injustice of loss made her want to tear the stone itself apart. The light reveals how her fury had been transformed into something useful—a more efficient path for the sacred waters.

In the highest terrace: the place where she finally spoke aloud the names of everyone she had failed to save. The obsidian wall bears the faint impression of her forehead, pressed against the stone through countless nights of confession. Under the gentle illumination, the wear pattern looks almost like a blessing, a sign of devotion worn into the volcanic glass.

As she completes her circuit, the Mourner realizes she is no longer alone. The light has taken shape—not human, but not entirely Other. It moves like water finding its level, like wind deciding its direction. When she looks directly at it, she sees suggestions of wings made of starlight, hands formed from morning mist, eyes that hold the depth of mountain lakes.

*You have carried what others could not bear to hold.*

"Yes."

*And in carrying it, you have transformed it.*

The Mourner looks around the Chamber with new eyes. The endless condensation that once seemed like a mirror of her inability to stop grieving—she sees now how it has become the breath of this place, sustaining the fungi, creating the acoustic properties that make every whisper sacred. Her presence has turned the Chamber from a beautiful but empty shell into something living.

*But you believe you came here to hide.*

This strikes deeper. The Mourner sinks to her knees on the central terrace, and the light flows around her like an embrace. "I couldn't... the world above is too bright for someone filled with so much darkness. I thought... I thought it was better to contain the grief here, where it couldn't touch anyone else."

*Child.* The voice carries infinite tenderness. *You did not come here to contain your sorrow. You came to compost it.*

The words reshape her understanding like rain reshaping stone. She sees now how the fungi have grown brighter over the years of her residence, how the Chamber's acoustics have become more perfect, how the condensation patterns have grown more beautiful and complex. Her grief has not been imprisoned here—it has been transformed into nourishment, into light, into a kind of prayer that makes the volcanic glass itself more alive.

*The world above needs what you have become.*

"I can't leave." The words come automatically, worn smooth by repetition. "This is where I belong now."

*No.* The presence moves closer, and she feels surrounded by the warmth of understanding. *This is where you learned to belong to yourself. But belonging has always been larger than any single place.*

The light begins to change again, taking on new qualities. She smells growing things—not the mineral-rich fungi, but green shoots breaking through earth, flowers opening to spring rain. She feels warmth on her face that reminds her of sunshine, though no sun has touched her in years beyond counting.

*You have learned to hold sorrow without being destroyed by it. To let it flow through you like water through stone, transforming both. This is not a skill meant for solitude.*

The Mourner closes her eyes, and in the darkness behind her lids, she sees them: the other mourners, the ones who stand at gravesides unable to weep, who carry losses too large for words, who believe their grief makes them unfit for the world of the living. She sees them as clearly as she has learned to see in the Chamber's twilight—all the people who need someone to show them that sorrow can be sacred, that tears can become something beautiful.

*Will you teach them?*

When she opens her eyes, the light has gathered itself into something almost human-shaped, seated across from her on the obsidian floor. She can see through it to the familiar fungi, the worn channels, the walls that have been her world. But she can also see, somehow, beyond the Chamber's confines—paths leading up to the surface, roads stretching across the world above, places where her particular skills are needed.

"I don't know how to be anything but this." She gestures to herself—skin like wet stone, hair that drips eternally, eyes adapted to phosphorescent twilight.

*You will learn. As you learned to breathe mineral air, as you learned to hear the fungi's heartbeat, as you learned to make beauty from breakdown.* The presence reaches out with hands made of condensed starlight, touching her face. Where the touch lands, she feels something shift—not a loss of what she has become, but an expansion of it.

*The Chamber will remain. Others will find it when they need to, as you did. But it no longer needs a permanent guardian—it needs a teacher who can help people understand what they find here.*

The Mourner looks around her sanctuary one more time, seeing it with new eyes. The terraces that taught her patience. The channels that showed her how to direct overwhelming feeling into useful flow. The fungi that proved that even in the deepest darkness, life finds ways to create light. The condensation that demonstrated how tears can become something larger than the sorrow that shed them.

She has learned everything this place has to teach her. And perhaps—perhaps that was always the point.

"Will I... will I still be able to weep?"

The presence laughs, and the sound is like rain on leaves, like water finding its way home. *Child, you will teach the world how to weep properly. How to let tears fall without drowning. How to honor loss without being lost. How to find the sacred in sorrow and the beauty in breakdown.*

*You will be a bridge between this deep place and the bright world above. You will help others learn that grief is not a burden to be hidden but a river meant to flow, transforming everything it touches.*

The light begins to lift, drawing back toward the opening above, and the Mourner understands that she is meant to follow. But first, she moves through the Chamber one final time as its sole occupant. She touches each basin, each channel, each cluster of faithful fungi. She whispers gratitude to the stone that held her when nothing else could, to the water that taught her the persistence of flow, to the darkness that showed her how to find her own light.

Then, impossibly, she begins to rise.

The light carries her upward like water flowing in reverse, like stone remembering it was once fire and flame. As she ascends through the opening she descended through so long ago, she feels the Chamber's gifts settling into her bones: the patience of obsidian, the persistence of condensation, the quiet strength of things that grow in darkness.

When she emerges onto the surface world, dawn is breaking across hills she had forgotten existed. The light that guided her spreads across the landscape like a blessing, touching grass heavy with dew, trees stretching their branches toward sky, a world that suddenly seems not too bright but exactly bright enough for someone who has learned to carry light within herself.

The Mourner—though she will need a new name now—stands at the threshold between deep earth and open sky. In one direction lies the Chamber, patient and eternal, ready for the next soul who needs to learn the sacred art of falling apart. In the other direction lie roads and towns and cities full of people who have forgotten how to grieve properly, who need someone to teach them that tears are not weakness but water, that sorrow is not ending but composting, that the deepest healing sometimes looks like the courage to keep weeping until the weeping transforms into something else entirely.

She takes her first step into the wider world, and with each footfall, small flowers bloom in the grass beneath her feet—not from magic, but from the simple truth that someone who has learned to find beauty in breakdown tends to leave beauty in her wake.

Behind her, the fungi pulse their gentle rhythm, keeping time for the next mourner who will need their light. Ahead of her, the world stretches wide and bright and full of sorrows that are waiting to become sacred.

The bridge between deep earth and bright sky begins to walk, carrying the Chamber's gifts into the world that needs them, teaching by her very presence that some things are too beautiful to heal quickly, too important to rush, too sacred to hide in the darkness forever.
