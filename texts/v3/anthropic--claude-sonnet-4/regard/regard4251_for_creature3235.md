**The Attending**

The benevolent power first noticed the Hollowed Mills not as a place, but as a frequency—a unique harmonic that arose from the intersection of abandonment and adaptation, of human ambition transformed into something beyond human intention. It was the sound of the pumps keeping rhythm with bat wingbeats, of dog howls echoing through factory acoustics designed for machinery, of water lapping against brick in patterns that spelled out stories no architect had planned.

This power, ancient and patient, had watched countless civilizations rise and fall, had seen the earth itself remade by ice and fire and time. But rarely had it witnessed such an elegant demonstration of life's fundamental creativity—the way intelligence and need could transform even the most unlikely spaces into abundance.

So it came to attend, not to interfere, but to witness and, in small ways, to encourage what was already becoming.

**The First Gift: Deep Water**

The power's first act was almost imperceptible—a slight shift in the underground aquifers, a subtle redirection of water tables that had been disrupted by decades of mining. Instead of the constant struggle against flooding, the Mills found their water levels stabilizing into predictable rhythms that complemented rather than fought the life that had taken root there.

The catfish in the deepest basements suddenly found their territory expanded as new channels opened between buildings, creating an underwater highway system that connected previously isolated pools. The fish began to move in great migrations from building to building, following seasonal currents that now had purpose and direction.

The dogs noticed the change first among the surface dwellers. Their scouts reported that certain buildings, previously accessible only during the lowest water periods, now maintained consistent water levels that allowed for reliable passage. The pack territories expanded and reorganized as new routes became available, reducing conflict and increasing cooperation between packs.

The herons, with their ancient patience, simply adjusted their hunting patterns to accommodate the new rhythms, but the ravens—always quick to exploit new opportunities—immediately began dropping specially selected stones into the new channels, creating dams and diversions that created private hunting pools for their own use.

**The Second Gift: The Acoustic Enhancement**

The power had listened to the bat colonies for months before it understood what they were attempting. In the great spinning rooms, the echolocation calls were becoming increasingly sophisticated, but the architecture was limiting their potential. With the gentlest of touches—a slight adjustment to the density of air in certain spaces, a barely perceptible fine-tuning of the way sound traveled through the rusted machinery—the power created acoustic sweet spots throughout the mills.

The effect was immediate and remarkable. Bats that had previously hunted alone began to coordinate in complex formations, their calls creating three-dimensional maps of such precision that they could locate individual insects inside machinery, could navigate through spaces too dark and complex for any other creature to manage. The hunting success rate increased dramatically, but more importantly, the bats began to develop new social behaviors based on shared acoustic information.

The most gifted hunters began teaching the younger bats by example, their demonstration calls creating virtual maps that the juveniles could follow and learn from. What emerged was something approaching a curriculum—a systematic transfer of knowledge that accelerated with each generation.

The dogs, whose own acoustic communication had been largely limited to pack coordination, began to notice that their howls carried differently in certain parts of the mills. Without understanding why, they started to develop more complex vocal patterns, eventually creating a kind of acoustic territory-marking that could convey detailed information about food sources, water levels, and potential dangers to other packs across great distances.

**The Third Gift: The Living Bridges**

The power observed how the various species had learned to coexist but were still largely isolated by their different elevations and territories. The solution came through encouragement rather than intervention—a subtle strengthening of certain vines and climbing plants that had begun to colonize the exterior walls of the mills.

These weren't native species, but hardy survivors from abandoned gardens and landscaping that had adapted to the harsh conditions. With gentle encouragement, they began to grow in patterns that connected the different levels and territories throughout the mills. Thick, rope-like vines created highways for the smaller mammals, while broad-leafed species formed platforms and resting places for the birds.

The ravens were the first to understand the potential, immediately beginning to guide the vine growth by carrying seeds to strategic locations. The dogs learned to use the vine highways to move between territories without having to swim, which reduced stress on the packs and allowed for more complex social interactions.

Most remarkably, the vines created neutral zones—spaces where different species could interact without territorial conflicts. Young animals of various species began to play together in these vine networks, learning from each other in ways that accelerated adaptation and intelligence development across all the communities.

**The Fourth Gift: The Memory Pools**

The most subtle gift was perhaps the most profound. In certain pools throughout the mills, the power encouraged the growth of bioluminescent organisms—ancient, simple life forms that responded to movement and sound by producing gentle, colored light. These pools became natural gathering places, but they were more than that.

The light patterns were complex and persistent, creating a kind of visual echo of the activities around them. The movements of animals, the patterns of sound, even the changing water levels were recorded in shifting displays of blue and green and gold light that lasted for hours before fading.

The ravens, with their love of patterns and their remarkable memory, were the first to recognize that the pools were showing them history. They began to visit regularly, not just to drink, but to read the light patterns like a record of what had happened in their absence. This led to more sophisticated planning and coordination as the ravens could now understand the movements and activities of other species throughout the mills.

The bats, hunting at dawn and dusk, began to use the pools as navigation aids, the light patterns helping them to understand the changing geography of the mills as water levels shifted and territories evolved.

Even the fish learned to create deliberate patterns in the pools, swimming in formations that produced specific light displays—possibly a form of communication, or perhaps simply play, but either way, it represented a new form of intelligence emerging from the intersection of biology and environment.

**The Fifth Gift: The Teaching Machines**

The abandoned machinery throughout the mills had become habitat, but the power saw potential for something more. With the slightest adjustment—a gear that turned when water levels changed, a lever that moved with the wind, a dial that responded to temperature—the old industrial equipment began to function again, not for its original purpose, but as interactive learning tools for the mill's inhabitants.

The dogs were the first to investigate these newly active machines. A lever in the administration building, when pushed, would trigger a mechanism that opened a channel to fresh water. A wheel in the spinning room, when turned, would activate a system that scattered stored seeds for the birds. The animals began to understand cause and effect in increasingly complex ways, and some learned to operate multiple machines in sequence to achieve desired results.

The ravens, with their tool-using abilities and problem-solving intelligence, became true masters of the machinery. They learned to operate complex sequences that would provide food, water, and building materials. More remarkably, they began to teach these skills to other species, creating the first inter-species technology transfer in the mills.

The young animals, regardless of species, were particularly drawn to the teaching machines. Playing with the mechanisms became a form of education that crossed species lines, creating shared knowledge and understanding throughout the community.

**The Watching**

Throughout all of these gentle interventions, the power maintained its primary role as observer and attendant. It watched as intelligence bloomed in unexpected ways throughout the mills, as cooperation replaced competition, as adaptation accelerated into something approaching conscious evolution.

The most remarkable development was the emergence of what could only be called culture. The different species began to create traditions—seasonal gathering places, ritualized interactions, even forms of art. The ravens' elaborate constructions became increasingly sophisticated and beautiful. The dogs developed pack songs that were passed down through generations. The bats created flight patterns of stunning complexity that served no hunting purpose but seemed to exist purely for their aesthetic beauty.

The power realized that the Hollowed Mills had become something unprecedented: a place where post-human intelligence was emerging not from a single species, but from the interaction of many species working together. The abandoned infrastructure of human industry had become the framework for a new kind of civilization—one based not on domination of nature, but on integration with it.

**The Recognition**

As seasons turned and the communities of the mills grew more sophisticated, the power began to sense that its presence was no longer necessary. The gifts it had given were being improved upon and expanded by the inhabitants themselves. The acoustic enhancements were being refined by the bats, the living bridges were being cultivated and directed by the ravens, the teaching machines were being modified and improved by the collective intelligence of all the species working together.

The mills had become truly alive—not just inhabited, but conscious in a distributed way that transcended any single mind. The power felt something it had rarely experienced in its long existence: pride in the creativity of life itself, and satisfaction in having served as midwife to something genuinely new.

In its final act of attendance, the power created one last gift: a great bell in the highest tower of the mills, perfectly tuned to resonate with the acoustic frequencies used by all the inhabitants. It would ring only when the different communities were working in perfect harmony—a rare occurrence, but one that was happening more and more frequently as the seasons passed.

The bell rang for the first time on a dawn when bats returning from hunting guided a lost pack of young dogs back to safety, when ravens shared their food caches with hungry herons during a particularly hard freeze, when the fish in the memory pools created light patterns that helped ground-dwelling animals navigate safely through rising flood waters.

The sound carried across the mills and beyond—a deep, pure note that seemed to say: *Here is proof that intelligence and compassion can bloom anywhere, that life will always find a way to create beauty from abandonment, hope from ruin, and community from isolation.*

And with that note still echoing across the water, the power withdrew, leaving the Hollowed Mills to continue their own patient work of becoming whatever they were meant to become, confident that it would be something wonderful, something worthy of the attention it had been privileged to provide.

The pumps continued their rhythm, the steam continued to rise, and in the spaces between water and sky, ruin and renewal, something unprecedented continued to grow.
