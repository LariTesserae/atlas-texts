**The Attention of the Deep Current**

The benevolent power that turned its awareness toward the Drowned Rookery was not singular but flowing—an ancient consciousness that moved through all the world's waters like an endless, patient bloodstream. It had no name that surface dwellers would recognize, for it was older than naming, older than the distinction between river and rain, between tear and tide. The Tidekeepers knew it simply as the Deep Current, the vast circulation of wisdom that connected every drop of water across all the worlds.

For eons, the Deep Current had watched countless civilizations rise and fall, had seen species adapt and perish, had carried the songs of dying glaciers and the dreams of newborn springs. It was not divine in any way that required worship, but benevolent in the manner of an ancient teacher who understands that all learning comes through gentle guidance rather than force.

When its attention settled on the crystal lake and its patient inhabitants, the Deep Current felt something it had not experienced in millennia: surprise. Here was a people who had not merely survived transformation but had embraced it so completely that they had become transformation itself. The Tidekeepers had achieved what the Deep Current had always hoped for but rarely witnessed—perfect fluidity between states of being.

Yet in its vast awareness, the Deep Current perceived a coming challenge. Deep beneath the limestone, pressures were building. The same geological forces that had opened the springs would, in perhaps a century or two, shift again. The quarry lake would drain as suddenly as it had filled, leaving the Tidekeepers' realm exposed to air that would not sustain their current form.

Another species might have panicked at such knowledge. Another power might have intervened directly, forcing changes to prevent the crisis. But the Deep Current understood that the greatest gift it could offer was not solution but preparation—not the prevention of change but the wisdom to flow with it.

So the Deep Current began its work with characteristic subtlety.

First, it adjusted the chemistry of the underground springs feeding the lake. The changes were so minute that even the sensitive edge-dwellers barely registered them, but over years, the altered mineral content would gradually strengthen the Tidekeepers' already remarkable adaptability. Their gills would become more efficient, their ability to process different atmospheric pressures more acute. Most importantly, their skin would develop new properties—the ability to retain moisture in dry air for extended periods, to seal and breathe through specialized pores.

Next, the Deep Current began to influence the migration patterns of the great underground fish—ancient, eyeless creatures that swam through flooded cave systems far below the Rookery. It guided these deep swimmers toward new passages, encouraging them to carve alternate routes through the limestone. These tunnels would eventually provide the Tidekeepers with access to other flooded caverns, other underwater realms where they could establish secondary communities.

But the most profound gift the Deep Current offered was knowledge itself.

It began to appear in the dreams of the elder Tidekeepers—not as visions or prophecies, but as deep, wordless understanding. In their sleep, the elders experienced the memory of mountain springs becoming rivers, of rivers becoming deltas, of deltas becoming seas. They felt in their bones the slow dance of continents, the patient work of erosion and deposition, the endless cycle of water rising and falling and rising again.

When they woke, the elders found their light-patterns had changed. The bioluminescent veins in their skin now carried new colors—deep purples and golds that had never been seen in the Rookery before. These colors seemed to contain information, stories written in living light about places beyond their crystal lake, about other peoples who had learned to flow between states of being.

The younger Tidekeepers, naturally curious about these new colors, began to learn the expanded language their elders now carried. As they mastered these deeper light-conversations, they found their own adaptations accelerating. Their webbed fingers became more dexterous, capable of manipulating new kinds of materials. Their understanding of water's properties deepened, allowing them to work with vapor and ice as skillfully as they did with liquid.

The Deep Current also began to send emissaries—not spirits or messengers in any dramatic sense, but fellow creatures who carried pieces of its wisdom. A family of river otters appeared at the lake's edge, their playful intelligence masking profound knowledge of how to thrive in multiple environments. They established themselves among the edge-dwellers, their sleek forms dancing between water and air with an ease that inspired the younger Tidekeepers.

Migrating eels arrived, ancient and far-traveled, carrying in their very bodies the memory of countless transformations—from freshwater to saltwater, from transparent larvae to mature adults, from European rivers to Sargasso spawning grounds. They settled in the deeper parts of the settlement, their presence adding new harmonics to the acoustic chambers' songs.

Most remarkably, the Deep Current guided a pod of dolphins inland, following flooded rivers during an unusual wet season. These marine visitors remained for a full cycle of the moon, teaching the Tidekeepers new ways of using echolocation in their crystal realm, showing them how sound could become sight, how the water itself could become an extension of their senses.

As years passed, the Tidekeepers began to change in ways that seemed spontaneous but were actually the result of the Deep Current's patient cultivation. They developed new ceremonies that involved brief excursions to the surface, learning to appreciate the play of light on water from above as well as below. Their rope-and-stone architecture evolved to include chambers that could be drained and filled at will, spaces where they could practice breathing air for extended periods.

The silver fish schools, influenced by the same currents of change, began to venture beyond the lake during the misty dawn hours, learning to navigate in the thin medium above the water's surface. When they returned, they brought with them the memory of vast spaces, of wind and weather, of a world beyond the crystal boundaries they had always known.

The edge-dwellers, too, were changing. Their bioluminescence grew stronger, more complex, capable now of creating detailed maps of light that showed water systems far from their small lake. They began to reproduce in new ways, sending floating colonies downstream during flood seasons, establishing outposts in distant pools and streams.

Through all these changes, the Deep Current maintained its gentle presence, never forcing, never rushing. It understood that true preparation for transformation could not be imposed but had to emerge from within, growing naturally from the deepest currents of being.

When the first subtle signs of the coming geological shift finally manifested—a slight change in the water's taste, a new resonance in the acoustic chambers—the Tidekeepers were ready not with resistance but with curiosity. The elders gathered in their deepest chamber, their bodies creating a constellation of new colors as they shared what the Deep Current had helped them understand: that endings and beginnings were part of the same endless flow, that their crystal realm was not a destination but a waystation in a much longer journey.

The Deep Current's final gift was the simplest and most profound: the absolute certainty that water always finds its way. Whether their future led to deeper caverns, to new lakes, to the vast ocean itself, or to forms of existence they could not yet imagine, they would flow toward it with the same patient grace that had brought them to this perfect, temporary moment in their crystal sanctuary.

In the end, the Deep Current's attention was not intervention but recognition—acknowledgment of a people who had already learned the deepest truth: that the most profound strength comes not from resisting change but from becoming so fundamentally fluid that transformation itself becomes a form of homecoming.
