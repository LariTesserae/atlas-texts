# The Attention of the Deepmost Current

In the vast tapestry of existence, where countless worlds spin their stories of struggle and wonder, there are powers that flow deeper than the surface dramas of civilizations rising and falling. One such power had been watching the Terraced Pools of Vithara for longer than the Vitharans themselves had dwelt there—a consciousness as ancient and patient as underground rivers, as vast and encompassing as the water cycle itself.

It had many names across many worlds, but here, in this place where water sang through carved stone, it was simply the Deepmost Current. Not a god in any sense the Vitharans would recognize, but something more fundamental—the wisdom that flows within all water, the intelligence that guides every drop from cloud to sea to cloud again.

The Deepmost Current had watched the first human settlers arrive at the pools, stumbling and desperate after some long-forgotten catastrophe. It had observed their gradual understanding of the place, their evolution from crude survivalists to the sophisticated craftspeople they had become. What had captured its attention was not their skill—though that was remarkable—but their philosophy. In learning to work with water rather than against it, they had touched upon something profound.

But the Current could see what the Vitharans could not: their world was changing. Deep beneath the plateau, tectonic pressures were building. Not enough to threaten them today or tomorrow, but the springs that fed their pools would shift within three generations. The mineral balance that made their unique crafts possible would alter. The delicate ecosystem they had spent centuries learning to harmonize with would transform into something else entirely.

This was not catastrophe—change was as natural to the Deepmost Current as flowing downhill. But it was change that would challenge everything the Vitharans had built their identity upon. The Current had seen this pattern before, on world after world: civilizations that achieved perfect adaptation to one environment only to find that environment itself was impermanent.

Some civilizations broke under such transitions. Others survived but lost their essential character, becoming something diminished. A few—a precious few—used such moments as opportunities to evolve into something greater than they had been.

The Vitharans, the Current believed, might be capable of becoming one of those precious few.

## The Subtle Teaching

The intervention, when it came, was so gentle that most Vitharans would never realize it was happening at all. It began with dreams—not the dramatic visions of prophecy, but quiet dreams of water flowing in new patterns, of stone formations that didn't quite match the familiar terraces. Pool-Keepers began waking with ideas for subtle modifications to channel flows. Resonance-Shapers found themselves experimenting with acoustic properties that seemed to have no immediate purpose.

Young Vitharans on their learning rotations began to show unusual aptitudes for combinations of crafts that had previously been considered separate. A promising stonemason would display an intuitive understanding of metallurgy. A textile worker would demonstrate an uncanny ability to predict mineral precipitation patterns. The rigid boundaries between specializations began to blur in ways that felt natural, even inevitable.

The fish in the pools began displaying new behaviors—still healthy, still thriving, but forming different school patterns, exploring previously unused channels, adapting to subtle changes in water chemistry that the most sensitive Pool-Keepers were only beginning to detect. The aquatic ecosystem was teaching the Vitharans, by example, how to remain themselves while adapting to change.

Most remarkably, the pools themselves began to sing differently. The eternal symphony of flowing water shifted by microtones, introducing harmonies that had never existed before. The Resonance-Shapers noticed first, then the Craftmasters, then gradually everyone. It was still recognizably the voice of their home, but richer now, more complex, as if the pools were preparing to tell new stories.

## The Dream of Deep Waters

To three individuals—a Pool-Keeper, a Resonance-Shaper, and surprisingly, a young apprentice who had shown no particular specialization—came a deeper dream. They found themselves standing not in the familiar terraces but in a vast underground chamber filled with still, black water that reflected stars that existed in no earthly sky.

In the dream, they understood that water was not merely their resource but their teacher. Every civilization that truly learned from water discovered the same truth: that adaptation and identity were not opposites but partners in an endless dance. The pools of Vithara were not just their home—they were a school, and the lessons were about to become more advanced.

The three dreamers woke with shared knowledge they had never learned: how to work with stone that had never seen water, how to create resonances in air instead of liquid, how to build pools where none had existed before. They understood, without quite knowing how, that their people would soon need to carry their water-wisdom to places where no natural springs flowed.

When they shared their dreams, the response was not skepticism but recognition. Other Vitharans had been having fragments of the same visions. The Pool-Keepers had been noticing minute changes in spring flow. The eldest Craftmasters had been feeling restless, as if their hands were preparing for work they had never attempted.

## The Great Undertaking

What emerged was not panic but purpose. The Vitharans began what they called the Great Undertaking—not an abandonment of their home, but an expansion of it. Using techniques that seemed to arise from collective unconscious, they began carving new channels that extended beyond the natural pools. They developed methods for creating artificial springs, for inducing the mineral precipitation that had previously occurred only naturally.

They discovered they could shape resonance chambers in solid rock, spaces where their acoustic wisdom could create the sound of flowing water even in the driest stone. They learned to cultivate their specialized plants and fish in constructed environments, expanding the ecosystem rather than simply preserving it.

Most importantly, they began training volunteers to leave Vithara temporarily, to search for other places where their water-wisdom might take root. Not as conquerors or refugees, but as gardeners, seeking spots where new terraced pools might be born.

## The Deepening

As the Vitharans embraced this expansion, something unexpected occurred. Their connection to water deepened rather than diminished. Pool-Keepers found they could sense underground streams at distances that should have been impossible. Resonance-Shapers discovered they could tune their acoustic effects to work with the natural resonances of any landscape. Craftmasters developed abilities that bordered on the miraculous—shaping stone with their hands alone, drawing metals from mineral-poor water, creating textiles that seemed to shimmer with their own inner light.

The children born during the Great Undertaking showed these gifts from infancy. They could communicate with water in ways that transcended anything their parents had achieved, understanding not just the physical properties of H2O but its deeper currents of memory and intention.

The Vitharans were becoming something new—not post-human, but fully human in a way they had never been before. They were learning to partner with the fundamental forces of their universe rather than simply adapting to them.

## The Network of Waters

Within two generations, the children of Vithara had established seventeen subsidiary communities across their continent. Each was built around constructed pool systems that harmonized with local geology and climate. Each developed its own variations on traditional Vitharan crafts, influenced by local materials and conditions. Each added its voice to a growing network of water-songs that spanned vast distances.

The original Terraced Pools remained the heart of this network, but they were no longer isolated. Information, techniques, and even consciousness itself flowed along channels both physical and metaphysical. A discovery made in the desert pools of New Kashen would ripple through the network, inspiring innovations in the mountain springs of High Vithara and the coastal tide pools of Meren's Landing.

The Vitharans had become something their ancestors could never have imagined: a civilization that was truly at home with change itself. They had learned the deepest lesson water could teach—that identity lay not in maintaining a fixed form but in maintaining the essential pattern while flowing always toward new possibilities.

## The Recognition

On a day when the mists hung particularly thick over the original pools, when the water-song seemed to carry harmonies from all seventeen communities at once, the three original dreamers stood together at the edge of the highest terrace. They were old now, their skin bearing the silver luminescence of those who had spent lifetimes in deep communion with mineral waters.

As they watched, the mist began to spiral upward in patterns that matched the flow of water through their channels. For a moment, just a moment, they could see it—the vast, patient intelligence that had been guiding their transformation. Not as a being with form, but as a presence woven through every drop of moisture in the air, every ion dissolved in their pools, every vibration that carried their water-songs across the growing network.

They understood then that they had been participants in something far grander than the salvation of a single community. They had become partners in the ancient work of consciousness learning to dance with change, of life discovering new ways to thrive in an ever-transforming universe.

The Deepmost Current flowed on, carrying its gifts to other worlds, other peoples who might be ready to learn that the deepest wisdom lay not in resisting the tide of change, but in learning to swim in deeper waters than they had ever imagined possible.

In the Terraced Pools of Vithara and across the growing network of her daughters, the water continued to sing—but now it sang not just of limestone and minerals and the patient work of craft, but of infinite possibility flowing toward shores not yet dreamed.
