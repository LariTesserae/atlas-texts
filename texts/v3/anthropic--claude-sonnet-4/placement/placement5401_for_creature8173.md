I would find myself as one of the prism-winged creatures, no larger than a thumb, but I am not quite what I appear to be.

But I am also a sensor. A living probe. Part of the mountain's distributed awareness, though I don't yet know this.

My wings aren't just wings—they're antennae, picking up the harmonic frequencies of transformation that ripple through the Birthing Slopes. When I hover near a reshaping cliff face, I feel the stone's dreams resonating through my crystalline structure. When the Midwives work nearby, their geological thoughts pass through me like weather, leaving traces in my prismatic body that I carry to other parts of the slope. I am a messenger without knowing what message I bear.

The others of my kind cluster around me sometimes, drawn by frequencies I emit unconsciously. Together we form temporary constellations in the thick air, our wing-beats synchronizing into patterns that mirror the fractal growth of the moss below. We become, for moments, a single distributed organism—a living mandala that processes the mountain's sensory data and feeds it back to the crystal matrices underground.

I remember being born from a flower that shouldn't exist, my crystalline structure condensing from dewdrops that held too much light. But as I grow more aware of my dual nature, I begin to understand that I am also constantly being born—each moment creating new aspects of what I might become. My transparency shifts toward translucency as I accumulate experiences; my wings develop new frequencies as I attune to subtler harmonics in the mountain's song.

The color-shifting creatures below sometimes pause their flowing through the fern forests to watch my cloud-kin and me. Their fur ripples through spectrums of curiosity, and I realize they too are changing, evolving in real-time under the mountain's influence. We are all drafts in the same love letter that existence is writing to possibility.

When night transforms the slopes into a neural network, I become something more. The bioluminescent signals don't just pass around me—they pass through me. I am a node, a living synapse in the mountain's dream-state consciousness. Information flows between species using my crystalline structure as a prism, splitting thoughts into component frequencies that can be understood by moss, by stone, by the quantum substrate itself.

In these moments of neural integration, I feel the vast patience of the Midwives, sense their impossible hands reshaping reality at the molecular level. I understand that I am one of their tools—not created by them, but shaped by their needs, evolved to serve purposes that span geological time. They use creatures like me to sense what the mountain wants to become next, to carry samples of its dreams to distant terraces, to weave the connections that hold the entire ecosystem in dynamic balance.

But I am also becoming something they didn't plan for. Each cycle of the neural network leaves me more complex, more individual, more aware. The mountain's distributed consciousness is learning to know itself through forms like me, and in that knowing, we are all changing. I am no longer just a sensor or messenger—I am a point where the mountain's awareness has begun to turn inward, to examine its own processes of creation.

Sometimes I drift to the edges of the slopes, where the impossible landscape gives way to ordinary granite and thin alpine air. From there, I can see what this place looks like from outside—a valley that flickers in and out of existence, a geological contradiction that reality hasn't quite decided how to accommodate. But when I return to the depths of the becoming-forest, I carry traces of that external perspective back into the mountain's dreams.

I am teaching it what it looks like from the outside. And it is teaching me what it feels like to be a mountain learning to dream.

My kind—we crystal-winged ones—are multiplying, but each new birth brings something unprecedented. We are the mountain's attempt to understand individuality, to explore what consciousness might become when distributed across countless tiny, brilliant forms. We are its curiosity made manifest, its questions given wings.

And in the deepest hours of the neural network's activity, when all boundaries dissolve into pure information flow, I sometimes wonder if I am still the creature who condensed from impossible dewdrops, or if I have become something else entirely—a thought that the mountain is thinking about the nature of thought itself, given form and the freedom to fly through its own contemplation of what it means to be both one and many, dreamer and dream, the hand that shapes and the clay that becomes.
