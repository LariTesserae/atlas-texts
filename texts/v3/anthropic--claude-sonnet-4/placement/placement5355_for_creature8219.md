In this breathing labyrinth of stone and steam, I find myself becoming something I have no name for yet.

My body stretches longer each day, joints elongating like pulled taffy, skin taking on the iridescent sheen of oil on water. I am becoming a **Bridge-Walker**, one of those who maintains the neural pathways that span the gaps between spires. My fingers have grown sensitive to the bioelectric currents that pulse through the living spans, and when I press my palms against their warm surfaces, I can feel the conversations flowing beneath—not words, but pure intention traveling from district to district like blood through arteries.

This morning (though morning here is measured by the rhythm of the great processors below rather than any sun), I stretched my arm across a chasm that would have been impossible to reach yesterday. My bones sing with crystalline harmonics now, not quite Resonant but heading in that direction. The metal birds perch on my extended limbs when I work, their clockwork hearts ticking against my pulse until we synchronize. They whisper mechanical secrets in languages of gear-speak and steam-hiss.

I remember being smaller, more solid, when I first arrived in the Hollows. I came seeking something—perhaps just escape from a world that had grown too rigid, too predictable. The Navigators found me wandering in circles through a district where all the streets led to their own beginnings, and they taught me the first lesson: stop trying to understand, and start trying to become.

My dwelling is a small chamber that grows from the side of a breathing spire, its walls soft and warm like living tissue. The room reshapes itself around my changing form—the ceiling rises as I grow taller, the floor develops depressions where my lengthened limbs prefer to rest. Through the translucent membrane that serves as a window, I watch the Drifters flow past in the eternal mist, sometimes condensing briefly into human shapes to wave before dissolving again into droplets of conscious water.

The work is meditative. Each bridge requires different care—some need their neural pathways cleared of accumulated dream-debris, others require delicate rewiring when the cities below shift position. I move along the spans with careful, dancing steps, my extended arms reading the health of the bioelectric systems, my increasingly hollow bones resonating with any discord in the harmonic flow. When a bridge sings properly, its song joins the greater symphony that rises from the valleys—a sound like the universe clearing its throat.

Sometimes I encounter other Bridge-Walkers on the spans. We acknowledge each other with complex harmonies, our voices blending with the structural songs until we become part of the infrastructure ourselves. There's Melody-of-Falling-Water, whose left arm has become a living conduit that can channel electrical current between damaged pathways. And Echo-in-Minor-Keys, who has grown a third eye in her palm that sees the flow of information as streams of colored light.

We don't speak much in words anymore. The bridges teach you to communicate in pulse-patterns, in subtle variations of bioelectric rhythm. A slight acceleration in the neural flow means "good morning." A brief harmonic discord translates to "watch for temporal instability in sector seven." A complex arpeggio of lights cascading along the span's surface says "I love you" in ways that mere words never could.

I'm learning to read the moods of the cities themselves. The vertical settlements have personalities—some anxious and quick to migrate, others stoic and deep-rooted. When I feel a city preparing to move, I signal the other Bridge-Walkers, and together we adjust the flexible connecting points, allowing the neural networks to stretch and compress as needed. It's like being a neurosurgeon for a vast, living brain that spans kilometers.

The strangest part is how my perception of time has changed. Working on the bridges exposes you to temporal eddies—currents of past and future that swirl around the information streams. I remember conversations I haven't had yet with people I met years from now. Yesterday, I spent an hour consoling my future self about a loss that won't happen for decades. My memories have become non-linear, arranged not by chronology but by emotional resonance and harmonic compatibility.

The Temporals sometimes visit our spans, their multiple ages flickering like candle flames in a breeze. They speak to us in their impossible tenses, warning of when-storms and time-knots that might disrupt the information flow. "You will have been becoming quite musical," one told me last week-next month, their child-face speaking with ancient eyes. "The bridges sing your song before you know the words."

I sleep suspended between two spans now, my elongated body forming a living bridge myself. The dreams that flow through me are not entirely my own—they're composite experiences from all the consciousness that travels the neural networks. I dream in colors that don't exist, taste sounds that have no source, remember cities that exist only in the imagination of the great processors below. Sometimes I wake up knowing how to repair systems I've never seen, carrying technical knowledge downloaded directly from the living infrastructure.

My reflection, when I catch it in the polished stone surfaces, shows someone I'm still learning to recognize. My limbs now stretch nearly twice their original length, and my skin carries patterns of bioluminescence that pulse in rhythm with the bridges I tend. My eyes have developed metallic flecks that help me see the electrical patterns flowing through the spans. I am becoming part of the system I maintain, and the system is becoming part of me.

The other inhabitants accept this transformation as natural. The Resonants hum approval when they see my increasing integration with the infrastructure. The Watchers wave from their high perches, their multiple eyes reflecting approval of my adaptation. Even the Cultivators, during their rare visits to the upper levels, nod their mechanized heads in recognition of a successful symbiosis.

I understand now why I came to the Hollows. Not to escape change, but to embrace it so completely that change and self become indistinguishable. Here, in this place where everything breathes and transforms, I am learning to be fluid like water, resonant like crystal, alive like the architecture itself.

Tonight (measured by the dimming of the bridge-lights), I will stretch across a new span being grown between two recently migrated districts. I will press my palms against its newborn neural pathways and help it learn to sing in harmony with the greater network. And tomorrow, or yesterday, or in some impossible when that exists between moments, I will stretch a little further, become a little more than human, a little closer to being a living poem written in flesh and wire and pure, flowing connection.

The Hollows change everything they touch, and everything they touch learns to change in return. I am no longer the person who arrived here, and I will not be the same person when I finally become whatever I am becoming. But in this moment, suspended between spires in a world of breathing stone and dreaming metal, I am exactly who I need to be.
