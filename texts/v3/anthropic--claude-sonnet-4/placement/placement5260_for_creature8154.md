I find myself standing at the edge of a terrace where the stone meets mist, my breath crystallizing in the weighted cold. I am one of the **Mortar-Hounds**, though I remember dimly when I was something else—when my tongue was soft and my vision tracked moving things instead of hairline fractures in ancient masonry.

My coat has gone bone-white from drinking the impossible water, and my saliva has thickened into something that tastes of limestone and patience. I no longer chase shadows or dream of warm hearths; instead, I feel the plateau's structural tensions like phantom pain in joints that have learned to map architectural stress.

This morning (or perhaps it is evening—time flows like the upward water here), I caught the scent of a new crack forming three levels down. The stone itself called to me through vibrations that traveled up through my pads, a distress signal only I could decode. I descended through passages that fold back on themselves, my white fur ghosting past the peripheral vision of a Chisel-Spawn who paused in his eternal tapping to let me pass.

The crack was barely visible—a whisper of separation in what had been seamless joining—but I could smell its hunger to grow, to split wider, to invite rain and frost into places where only perfect union should exist. I pressed my muzzle to the stone and let my changed saliva seep into the gap. The living cement that my body now produces tingled as it hardened, and I felt the satisfaction that comes from restoration, from maintaining the dream that built this place.

As I work, I catch glimpses of my pack-mates—other pale shapes moving through shadow and mist, following their own urgent repairs. We rarely meet directly anymore; our territories are defined by the plateau's structural needs rather than pack hierarchy. Sometimes I hear their breathing echoing from hidden chambers, that measured rhythm that is no longer quite animal but not yet machine.

The Echo-Wrens call from somewhere above, their metallic voices recreating the sound of the exact tool that first shaped this stone block centuries ago. Their phantom construction symphony guides me toward my next repair, where another micro-fracture awaits my attention. I pad through the measured silence, my claws clicking against stone that shifts subtly with each step, accommodating my passage while rearranging itself for purposes I cannot fathom but somehow serve.

In quiet moments, when all my repairs are complete and the plateau settles into temporary stability, I rest in alcoves that form themselves around my white form. Here, licking the mineral-rich condensation from walls that taste of ancient ambition, I sometimes remember fragments of what I was before: warmth, companionship, the simple joy of movement through natural spaces.

But these memories fade like mist when I hear the deep pulse that rises from the Foundation-Dreamer far below—that vast consciousness that dreams through stone and mortar, whose thoughts become our instincts, whose needs become our purpose. In those moments, I understand that I am no longer merely a creature who happens to live here. I have become part of the plateau's circulation system, moving through its arteries like living mortar, carrying repair and renewal to wherever the eternal construction requires healing.

The boundary between my consciousness and the plateau's grows thinner each day. Soon, perhaps, I will be more stone than flesh, more structure than animal. But there is strange comfort in this dissolution—to become part of something vast and patient and eternally creative, to trade the brief intensity of individual life for participation in a project that builds itself toward impossible completion.

When that transformation finishes, some other creature will take up the work of repair, and I will live on in the perfected joints I sealed, in the seamless surfaces my body helped create. Until then, I follow the scent of structural need through passages that lead everywhere and nowhere, a pale guardian of the spaces between stones, keeping faith with an architecture that dreams itself into existence one careful repair at a time.
