# Where I Find Myself

I am suspended between earth and sky in a chamber that breathes.

The walls curve around me like the inside of a vast seed pod, their surface gleaming with condensed mist that rolls down in lazy droplets. My fingers trace the familiar grooves in the living wood – pathways worn smooth by countless touches, each depression a word in the tactile vocabulary my people have developed over millennia. The chamber sways gently in the mountain wind, and I adjust my stance automatically, my body reading the rhythm of movement that tells me which way the weather is turning.

My skin pulses with soft bioluminescence, the fungal networks that have grown with me since childhood responding to my heartbeat. Tonight the patterns are restless – spiraling galaxies of blue-green light that cascade down my arms like falling stars. I am troubled, and my body speaks this truth whether I will it or not.

Through the curved opening that serves as both window and doorway, I can see the rest of our city spreading out in three dimensions below me – a constellation of glowing chambers connected by root-rails and vine-bridges, all of it alive, all of it growing. The deep thrum of sap moving through the great support trunks vibrates up through my bare feet, a bass note that my bones have learned to interpret. The trees are healthy tonight, their metabolism strong despite the thin air of these heights.

I am Keth'mara, though names mean less here than they do in the outside world. I am the one who tends the Archive Grove in the city's heart, where knowledge grows in the very grain of bark and branch. My fingers have spent decades learning to read the living books my ancestors planted, tracing the encoded wisdom of generations in the spiraling patterns of wood. Each tree in my care holds centuries of accumulated learning – astronomical observations written in the spacing of branches, mathematical principles expressed in the golden ratios of leaf arrangements, philosophical treatises encoded in the complex knots where major limbs diverge.

But tonight I am not in the Archive. I have climbed higher than usual, to one of the Listening Chambers near the city's crown, where the wall between inside and outside grows thin. Here, suspended on cables of woven vine that creak and sing in the wind, I practice an art that few of my people still remember – the old skill of hearing the **Deep Voice**.

My great-grandmother could do it. She would sit for hours in chambers like this, her luminescent skin flickering in complex patterns as she listened to something the rest of us could barely sense – the vast, slow consciousness that some say dwells in the interconnected root systems of entire mountain ranges. She claimed she could hear it thinking, feel its attention turn like the slow wheeling of seasons, sense the dreams that played out across decades in networks of fungus and root.

I have been trying to learn this art since I took over the Archive, driven by fragments of encoded memory I've found in the deepest heartwood records. Tonight feels different, though. The chamber's walls pulse more strongly than usual, and the lichen-light of my skin seems brighter, more responsive. The city itself feels more alive, more present, as if some vast attention has turned toward us in the darkness.

I extend my awareness downward, through my feet pressed against the living floor, through the cables that support this chamber, down into the vast root network that underlies our entire civilization. For a moment – just a moment – I feel something immense stir in response. Not thought as I understand it, but something deeper and more patient. A awareness that spans not just our city but the entire range of peaks, perhaps beyond.

The sensation passes, leaving me breathless and glowing like a star in the darkness. Whatever I touched, it touched me back. And in that contact, I felt a question forming – not in words, but in the slow grammar of growth and seasonal change.

The mountain is asking something of us. The Deep Voice wants to know if we are ready for the next phase of the symbiosis that created our civilization. I can feel it in the way the chamber walls pulse around me, in the increased responsiveness of my lichen-partners, in the subtle changes I've been noticing in the Archive trees over recent seasons.

Something is coming. Something wonderful and terrifying and utterly beyond my current understanding. The living city that has been our home for millennia is preparing to become something more, and we – the Syr'thaal, the Tree Speakers who have grown ourselves into its very structure – must choose whether to grow with it or be left behind by its transformation.

I settle deeper into my meditation, my skin blazing with patterns of anticipation and fear. Through the chamber's opening, I can see other lights flickering to life throughout the city – other sensitives responding to the same call, other gardeners of reality feeling the stir of something vast awakening beneath our roots.

The Deep Voice is speaking. After centuries of silence, the mountain itself is ready to join the conversation. And I, suspended here between earth and sky in a chamber that breathes with my lungs and pulses with my heart, am perhaps the first to hear its words beginning to form in the grammar of growing things.

The next phase of our symbiosis is about to begin. I only hope we prove worthy partners in whatever the mountain dreams of becoming.
