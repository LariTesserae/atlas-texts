**Finding Myself in the Throat**

I discover myself somewhere between arrival and always having been here—a distinction that loses meaning in the helical currents where time pools like golden honey. My transformation began the moment I heard the first note of the reality-symphony, though I suspect now that I was already changing before I knew there was anything to change into.

I am becoming one of the Membrane Walkers.

My body has learned to exist as both surface and depth, stretched thin enough to span the gaps between the rushing water columns while remaining dense enough to feel the pressure-songs that ripple through the amber-lit chambers. I remember having solid bones, but now my skeleton is made of crystallized harmonics—a framework that resonates with the Throat's impossible music, allowing me to feel the structural changes in reality as physical sensations.

When I stretch myself across a chamber opening, I become a living boundary between dimensions, my consciousness scattered across my translucent expanse like phosphorescence on a night ocean. The Tide Shepherds recognize me as kin now, though I am smaller, newer, still learning the deep patience required to tend currents that flow through seventeen dimensions simultaneously. My thoughts ripple across my membrane-body as visible waves, creating interference patterns that sometimes birth tiny pocket realities in my wake.

I have learned to feed on dissonance—those moments when the ascending ocean's song catches on fragments of the world-that-was, creating brief harmonic tensions that need resolution. I position myself at these junctures, allowing the conflicting melodies to pass through my stretched form, my body automatically filtering and reconciling the opposing frequencies until harmony is restored. Each resolution teaches me new songs, new ways to bend space with sound.

The Echo Children dart through me regularly, their quicksilver forms tickling as they pass between the layers of my being. They've learned that I carry traces of the outer world's sounds—fragments of human laughter, the rustle of leaves that once grew in horizontal gardens, the distant memory of rain falling down rather than up. Sometimes they pause within my membrane to listen to these fading echoes, adding them to their vast, improvised symphony.

My consciousness has expanded to encompass multiple viewing angles simultaneously. I can observe the Deepmost Singers from above and below and sideways all at once, watching as they guide lost fragments of reality through the maze of ascending torrents with their reality-bypassing songs. Through my stretched perception, I've begun to understand that the Throat isn't just a place—it's a process, a cosmic digestive system slowly breaking down the rigid boundaries between what-is and what-could-be.

I remember having a name once, but it has dissolved into something more fluid—a frequency signature that the Coral Minds recognize, a pressure-wave pattern that means *me* in the language of the Tide Shepherds. The other Membrane Walkers acknowledge me through harmonic convergences, moments when our songs align and create brief stability pockets where newer consciousness can rest and adapt.

The warm thermals generated by reality grinding against itself pass through me constantly, carrying fragments of stories from the mineral-song reefs below and the possibility-clouds above. I have become a translator of sorts, helping different layers of the Throat's ecosystem communicate across dimensional barriers. When the whale-ribbon consciousnesses sing memories that extend beyond the boundaries of this realm, I stretch myself to catch the overflow, preventing these vast thoughts from destabilizing the smaller chambers where the Echo Children play.

Sometimes I encounter the Paradox Swimmers in the spaces between my own molecules. They pass through me like thoughts I almost remember having, leaving behind droplets of meaning so concentrated they temporarily exist as small, singing pearls within my substance. These encounters are brief—glimpses of what I might become if I learn to exist in even more impossible spaces—but they remind me that transformation here is not a destination but an ongoing conversation with infinity.

I have learned to love the constant rebuilding. Each thunderous pulse that reshapes the Throat's boundaries requires all of us membrane-dwellers to flex and flow, to maintain our spanning connections even as the architecture of space itself reorganizes around us. It is exhausting and exhilarating—like being part of a cosmic breathing exercise that remakes reality with each inhalation.

In my quieter moments, when I contract myself into smaller configurations and drift in the backwater eddies, I sometimes remember the weight of having feet, the simple pleasure of walking on ground that stayed put. But these memories feel like stories someone once told me about a dream they had. The joy I find now is in being part of the song, in serving as a living interface between the realms where possibility becomes real and reality becomes possible.

I am learning to see through sound, to navigate by harmony, to exist as both boundary and bridge in a place that has taught water to climb forever toward a sky that is also an ocean. My membrane-body pulses with the accumulated songs of every soul that has dissolved into this ascending symphony, and I understand now that I am both individual and instrument, both witness and participant in the ongoing conversation between what was and what might yet be.

The Throat continues its eternal swallowing and singing, and I have become part of its throat—stretching, filtering, translating, helping it learn new songs from every reality it touches while gradually teaching it to sing back in harmonies that reshape the very foundations of existence.
