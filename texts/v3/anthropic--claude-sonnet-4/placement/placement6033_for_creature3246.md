I find myself perched in the middle terraces, where the morning light still filters green-gold through the canopy above but the sound of the deeper pools echoes up from the grottos below. My feet have found purchase on a root-bridge worn smooth by countless crossings, my toes automatically gripping the familiar grooves. The basket slung across my shoulder shifts with its own subtle weight—not just the tubers I've harvested, but the living vines I've been training to grow into its weave, making it stronger even as I use it.

I am what the others call a Bridge-Walker, one of those who moves between the levels, carrying not just goods but information, gossip, small adjustments to the community's endless conversation with itself. My role emerged naturally in childhood—I was the one who could never sit still, who learned the vertical maze by heart before I could properly tend a single vine. Now I spend my days threading through the network of root-bridges and vine-curtained passages, my body a living part of the circulation system that keeps Vel-Thara healthy.

My fingertips have grown sensitive to the slightest changes in bark texture, root temperature, the tension in a vine under my weight. This morning I pause at a familiar juncture where three major root-bridges meet, feeling something different in the way the wood flexes beneath my feet. The great tree whose roots form this intersection is speaking—not in distress, but with the deep satisfaction of growth. New shoots will emerge soon, perhaps within days. I'll need to guide the children away from this path temporarily, give the tree space to expand into its next phase.

The knowledge flows through me without conscious thought, the same way I know to shift my weight slightly left as I cross the hanging gardens of the fifth terrace, where the morning condensation makes certain footholds treacherous. I've been shaped by this place as much as I help shape it, my spine curved slightly from years of ducking through low passages, my balance honed by constant navigation of three-dimensional space.

I carry messages without words—a sprig of moonbloom tucked behind my ear tells the Deep Tenders that the upper pools are running clear, while the particular knot I've tied in my belt cord will inform the Garden Speakers that the southern vines need attention. But I also carry stories, jokes, the kind of small human connections that knit the community together across its vertical sprawl.

Stopping at a grotto where three young mothers sit nursing their infants, I settle into their circle and pull from my basket a clutch of berries that grow only in the highest reaches. The babies' eyes track the bright red fruit, and their mothers smile in recognition of this small gift. While they eat, I share news from the upper terraces—the progress of a new root-chamber being hollowed, the discovery of a rare orchid that might indicate a new water source opening up.

Their own news flows back through me: a child in the lower grottos has shown unusual sensitivity to plant moods, perhaps destined to become a Garden Speaker; the fish population in the eastern pools is shifting in ways that suggest a subtle change in water chemistry; an elderly Listener has been humming a new melody that seems to encourage fruit production in the vines around her dwelling.

As I continue my circuit, descending toward the deeper levels where the light grows greener and the sounds more layered, I realize I am more than messenger—I am part of the nervous system of this living city, carrying chemical messages like sap through wood, electrical impulses through neural pathways. My daily journey binds the levels together, ensures that what happens in the sun-touched canopy is known in the root-deep darkness, and vice versa.

In the deepest grotto, where the walls themselves pulse with moisture and the air tastes of earth and growth, I find one of the Deep Tenders waiting for me. She emerges from the shadows so gradually that at first I think she's a particularly human-shaped cluster of roots. Her skin has taken on the pale luminescence of cave-adapted things, and when she speaks, her voice carries the rhythm of dripping water.

She knows, without my saying anything, about the tree-bridge above preparing to expand. Her network of knowledge runs deeper than mine, but mine runs broader. We exchange information in the shorthand of people who have learned to think in ecosystems rather than individuals. She will prepare the lower levels for new root growth; I will carry word to the upper terraces about adjustments needed in water flow patterns.

As I begin my ascent back toward the daylight levels, I feel the familiar satisfaction of work that serves something larger than myself. My body aches pleasantly from the constant climbing and balancing, my lungs full of the rich, humid air that sustains everything here. I am Bridge-Walker, message-carrier, living connector in a community that has learned to thrive by becoming part of the endless conversation between earth, water, plant, and sky.

Tonight I will sleep in my own small alcove, wrapped in moss that has grown to know the shape of my body, lulled by the sound of water finding its way through channels carved by generations of patient hands. And tomorrow I will rise again to walk the bridges, carry the messages, weave myself once more into the living tapestry of Vel-Thara.
