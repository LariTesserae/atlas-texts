I am **Veleth of the Deep Listening**, and I have lived forty-three seasons in the embrace of these stones.

My dwelling clings to the cliff face some sixty body-lengths above the hidden stream, carved into an alcove that my grandmother's grandmother first discovered when she was young and the mist spoke to her of secret spaces. The entrance is curtained with maiden-hair ferns that brush my shoulders as I pass through—they have grown wild since my mother joined the deep gardens, and I lack her gentle hand with the growing things.

This morning I wake, as always, to the sound of water finding its way downward. My breath fogs in the chill air as I rise from my bed of woven moss-fiber, my feet finding the familiar depressions worn smooth in the granite floor. Through the narrow window—barely a crack in the stone, but widened over generations—I can see the faintest suggestion of dawn light filtering down through the mist.

My hands know the ritual of morning without conscious thought. I cup water from the collecting bowl that sits beneath the window's weeping stone, splash it across my face and feel the mountain's chill wake my blood. My hair, heavy with moisture, I braid with the small stones my mother gathered—river jade and iron pyrite that caught her eye in the deep places. The living moss wound through the braid has grown since yesterday; I can feel its soft expansion against my scalp.

Today I carry my grandmother's listening rod—a length of ironwood that has drunk deeply of the mountain's moisture until it rings like a bell when struck against stone. I am a Stone Listener, one of perhaps eight in our settlement, and the youngest by twenty seasons. The others say I have the gift, that I can hear what the mountain speaks in its deep voice, but today I feel uncertain. Yesterday, Kaelen of the Mist Paths reported sounds from the Third Terrace—a grinding, a settling that might mean nothing or might mean the great shelf is finally surrendering to time and water.

I make my way across the morning-slick platforms, my feet reading each step through the soles that have never known shoes. The wood beneath me is soft with decades of moisture, giving slightly under my weight but holding firm—like walking on the skin of some vast, patient beast. Around me, the settlement stirs to life. I hear Myren's voice from the Seventh Alcove, singing her plants awake in the old tongue. Below, someone's child laughs, the sound echoing strangely off the close stone walls.

The path to the Third Terrace requires three crossings—spans of ironwood and twisted root that bridge gaps no lowlander could contemplate. I pause at each crossing, listening. The mountain breathes around me, its exhalation carrying the scents of deep earth and growing things. A salamander, brilliant orange against the grey stone, watches me with ancient eyes before sliding into a crevice.

At the Third Terrace, I kneel and place my ear to the stone. The listening rod I hold against the granite, feeling for the mountain's pulse through its grain. Here—yes—a vibration that shouldn't be. Not the healthy settling of stone finding new equilibrium, but something sharper. Urgent.

I trace the feeling through the stone, moving methodically across the terrace. My fingers find the crack—hairline thin, invisible to eyes that haven't learned to read stone, but speaking volumes to my touch. It runs from the terrace's edge deep into the cliff face, following a flaw that has waited perhaps centuries for the right combination of frost and pressure to awaken it.

This is why I exist, why my bloodline has tended this gift through generations. I am the translator between human time and stone time, between the quick pulse of mortal concern and the vast, slow thoughts of the mountain itself. The crack will grow—not today, perhaps not this season, but inevitably. The Third Terrace, where old Thessa keeps her prize fungi and young Daven has recently built his first dwelling, will need to change.

I sit back on my heels, feeling the weight of knowledge. This is what it means to be **Kethani**—to live in constant dialogue with forces larger and slower than ourselves, to find our place within patterns that stretch far beyond our brief lives. The mist swirls around me as I plan my words, considering how to speak this truth so that it can be heard without causing fear.

Above me, the terraces rise into grey obscurity. Below, they descend into deeper green shadow. I am suspended between earth and sky, between the quick bright world of growing things and the patient darkness of stone. My people sleep and wake, love and work and dream, their lives interwoven with the mountain's breath, and I—I listen to the deep songs that will outlast us all, translating them into words that can guide us gently through the changes that are always coming, always arriving, like water finding its way through stone.

The mist thickens around me as I rise to return home, to begin the careful conversations that will reshape our small corner of the vertical world. This too is part of the pattern—the endless dance of adaptation, the eternal conversation between what is and what is becoming. I am **Kethani**. I am of this place, and it is of me, and together we breathe with the mountain's patient rhythm, finding our way forward one careful step at a time.
