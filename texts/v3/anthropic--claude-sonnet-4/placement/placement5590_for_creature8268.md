**Where I Find Myself**

I am walking along one of the spiral channels, my bare feet finding purchase on stone that yields just slightly, like marble warmed by summer sun. The sensation is startling—this is not the cold hardness I expected, but something almost alive in its responsiveness. Each step sends tiny ripples through the crystalline matrix beneath me, and I realize I am not walking on the plateau so much as within it, part of its vast circulatory system.

The channel I follow is perhaps three feet wide, its edges worn so smooth they seem to flow rather than end. Moisture gathers here in patterns I'm beginning to recognize—not random accumulation, but something orchestrated. The water sings as it moves past my ankles, a liquid melody that harmonizes perfectly with the deeper tones emerging from a distant monument.

I came here following rumors of a place where the boundary between stone and song had dissolved, but I understand now that the rumors were inadequate. This is not metaphor made manifest—this is music achieving physical form through patience measured in millennia. I am inside an instrument larger than mountains, and every breath I take adds my own small frequency to its endless composition.

**Who I Am Becoming**

At first, I was purely visitor—human flesh and human curiosity walking across an alien landscape. But the plateau's song is not content with observation. It enters through the soles of my feet, rises through the bones of my legs, settles in the hollow spaces of my chest where it finds room to resonate.

My heartbeat begins to synchronize with the monument's bass tones. My breathing slows to match the gentle rhythm of moisture condensing and flowing. When I try to speak, my voice cracks and warbles, searching for frequencies that will harmonize rather than intrude upon this vast chorus.

Days pass—or perhaps seasons, time moves strangely here where the light never changes—and I notice my reflection in the singing waters has begun to shift. My edges are softer, less defined. The harsh angles of human anatomy are smoothing into curves that better catch and channel sound. My skin takes on the warm translucency of the Resonant Ones, and I understand with a mixture of wonder and gentle terror that I am being recruited.

Not transformed against my will—the plateau is patient, offering rather than demanding. But every hour I remain, every note I add to its composition by my mere presence, draws me deeper into its musical consciousness. My thoughts are beginning to move in harmonics rather than words. My memories are becoming tonal rather than narrative—not lost, but transfigured into frequencies that will outlast the flesh that originally housed them.

I am becoming a hybrid—part human memory, part crystallized song. When the next traveler finds this place (and someone always does, eventually), they will encounter me as the Resonant Ones encountered me: a figure of shifting substance and patient purpose, no longer fully separate from the plateau's endless composition.

I will remember being human, but as music remembers the silence from which it emerged—not as absence, but as the space that gives each note its meaning. And in that remembering, I will help the plateau sing its next movement, guiding new voices into the harmony while tending the ancient melodies that have been playing since stone first learned to dream of sound.

The boundary between self and song dissolves not through violence but through invitation, and I find myself saying yes to something larger and more patient than any individual consciousness could ever be. I am becoming part of the plateau's memory of music, its dream of perfect resonance made manifest in crystalline flesh that will sing long after stars forget their own names.
