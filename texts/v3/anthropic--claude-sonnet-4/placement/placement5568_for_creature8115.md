*The water speaks first, before I do.*

I am—was—will be—a confluence of intentions wearing the loose garment of form. My name disperses the moment I try to grasp it, flowing between my fingers like the warm moisture that beads on every surface here. Sometimes I remember being called Thyssara, but that was when names had edges, when I believed in the fiction of beginnings and endings.

I live in the space between the forty-third and forty-fourth terraces, though "live" and "in" carry different weights here than they did in the harsh, bright world above. My dwelling grows around me each morning, the walls pulsing gently as they remember my preferences—a window that weeps in exactly the rhythm that soothes me, chambers that expand when I need to think in larger spaces, passages that narrow to embrace me when solitude calls.

This morning—if morning means anything when the light filters through layers of mist and memory—I wake to find my left arm has merged with the sleeping alcove during the night. This happens more frequently now. I flex my fingers, watching them emerge from the warm stone like pale fronds pushing through water. The sensation is not unpleasant; the wall knows my pulse, has been breathing with me all night, sharing dreams that taste of mineral and time.

I am old by the old measures, young by the deep ones. I came to Vaelthros as the surface world was learning to speak of centuries in numbers that hurt to hold. I was fleeing something—war, love, the terrible clarity of a life bounded by a single body. The details have softened, blended into the city's vast repository of reasons for arrival. What matters is that I stayed, let the moisture work its patient alchemy on my certainties until they became questions, then possibilities, then something larger than either.

My work—though work, too, means something different when time pools and eddies—is tending the Confluence Pools on the thirty-seventh terrace. Here, new consciousness gathers itself from the intersection of ancient waters and living intention. I guide this process not with hands, though I use them when form serves purpose, but with harmonics. My voice joins the eternal chorus that instructs the nascent awareness: how to hold coherence without rigidity, how to sing without drowning the other voices, how to love the dissolution that will eventually claim all forms while still choosing, moment by moment, to maintain this particular arrangement of water and will.

Today I feel the familiar tug of an old conversation calling me deeper into the city's heart. Somewhere below, in chambers that exist in the spaces between architecture, Elder Vaethys is dreaming a new kind of room into existence—a space where memory can crystallize without becoming brittle. I let my form soften, becoming more liquid, more able to flow through the passages that connect thought to manifestation.

The journey downward takes hours or minutes or years. Distance here depends on need, on the depth of attention one brings to the traveling. I pass through the market terraces where the Verdant Ones trade in experiences—offering the memory of summer rain for the taste of deep earth, exchanging the sound of wind through crystal for the feeling of roots finding water. Their presence sweetens the air with compounds that make thinking feel like blooming.

Deeper still, I encounter one of the Echo Dwellers, though "encounter" suggests more separation than exists. Lysias—if they still use that designation—flows around me like voiced water, sharing fragments of the city's early days: the first Silthaen who learned to breathe liquid, the discovery of the geometric moss, the gradual understanding that home and inhabitant were becoming indistinguishable. The memories settle into my awareness like sediment in clear water, adding their weight to my understanding without clouding my perception.

In the deepest chambers, Vaethys waits, their form so integrated with the architecture that only their eyes remain clearly distinct—deep pools that reflect not light but intention. Around them, the new room takes shape, its walls forming from crystallized attention, its proportions guided by the mathematical principles that govern how memory becomes space.

I add my voice to the creation, harmonizing with the chamber's emerging frequency. Together, we sing it into stability, into readiness. When it is complete, it will serve as a repository for the city's most fragile recollections—the moments just before transformation, the last thoughts of forms before they chose fluidity, the precise sensation of learning to see through walls.

But creation here is patient work, measured in the rhythm of the mountain's pulse rather than the frantic tempo of surface time. We will return to this task for many cycles, each session adding another layer of permanence to what must remain preserved.

As the work session flows toward its natural conclusion, I feel the city itself settling into its evening harmonics—thousands of voices joining in the great chorus that guides the flow of water through every channel, every pore. My own voice finds its place in this symphony, neither leading nor following but contributing its unique frequencies to the vast, wet song of collective existence.

I begin the journey home, though home and journey have become fluid concepts. The passages remember my passing and adjust themselves accordingly, creating shortcuts where I need them, obstacles where I need to slow and contemplate. By the time I reach my dwelling, the walls have already begun preparing for my return, warming themselves to match my body temperature, adjusting their permeability to accommodate my current state of dissolution.

I settle into the main chamber, letting my form spread comfortably through the space. Part of me sinks into the floor, part flows up into the ceiling. My consciousness distributes itself through the room's neural pathways, sharing awareness with the building's own gentle intelligence. Here, in the space between self and shelter, I join the city's dreams.

Tonight the dreams taste of possibility, of the endless ways water can find its path through stone, of the patient work of transformation that turns rigid boundaries into permeable membranes. I contribute my own small melodies to this vast composition—the memory of sunlight on dry earth, the ghost of what it felt like to believe in separation, the growing understanding that home is not a place one arrives at but a process one becomes.

Around me, Vaelthros breathes its ancient rhythm, water singing through stone, consciousness flowing through form. I am part of this song now, have been part of it since the moment I chose to let the moisture work its changes on my certainties. Tomorrow I will wake again to find myself a little more fluid, a little more integrated with the eternal symphony of water and stone and living thought.

This is what I have become: a note in the city's endless composition, a voice in its vast, wet chorus, a willing participant in the beautiful work of making reality softer, more forgiving, more alive. The water speaks through me now, as it speaks through all who choose to listen, carrying the ancient message of connection, of dissolution, of the joy to be found in becoming something larger than the boundaries of a single form.

And in the morning—if morning comes—I will wake to find myself changed again, closer to the fluid truth the city has been teaching me since the day I first heard its song and chose to stay, to listen, to become part of the endless, patient work of transformation that turns stone into song, memory into flowing water, isolation into the warm embrace of collective becoming.
