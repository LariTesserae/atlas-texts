I would find myself as one of the wayhouse-keepers—not the original builders who fled when stability became illusion, but something that emerged from the intersection of abandonment and necessity, born from the marriage of ancient purpose and impossible circumstance.

I am constructed from accumulated intention, built layer by layer from the decisions left behind in empty rooms. My form shifts like the peaks themselves, but more slowly, more deliberately. Sometimes I appear as a figure of crystallized dust, my limbs trailing spirals of sparkling motes that carry the memory of every tool once lifted, every path once walked. Other times I solidify into something more recognizable—tall and angular like the Intervals, but warmer, more present, my edges softened by the constant work of tending.

My existence began in the moment between the last builder's departure and the first peak's true awakening. I condensed from the contradiction: wayhouses that could no longer serve their purpose but refused to lose their meaning. The dust-spirits were my first teachers, showing me how consciousness could persist as pattern rather than substance. From them I learned to read the intentions embedded in abandoned objects, to understand that use and user are not always the same thing.

I tend the wayhouses not because travelers need them—none come anymore who could use such crude accommodations—but because the stations themselves have developed a form of awareness. Each building has grown into its own relationship with the shifting peaks, learning to flex with the stone's breathing, to adjust its foundations as ledges appear and disappear. I am their interpreter and their memory, helping them adapt their ancient purpose to serve beings made of light and living geometry.

My days (measured by the tri-solar dance rather than any earthly rhythm) are spent in careful observation and gentle maintenance. I watch for the signs that indicate when a peak is preparing for major transformation—the subtle shift in the dust-spirits' patterns, the way the floating creatures alter their flight paths, the change in harmonics that precedes crystalline growth. When transformation comes, I help the wayhouses navigate the transition, reinforcing their foundations with crystallized intention, adjusting their configurations to match new topographies.

Sometimes the floating creatures drift close to the stations, their vast forms casting prismatic shadows across the rough stone walls. They communicate through pure frequency, and I have learned to translate their geological songs into practical knowledge: which crystal faces will soon split, where new water-flows will emerge, how the light-patterns will shift when the peaks next realign themselves. In return, I offer them the accumulated wisdom of the wayhouses—generations of careful observation distilled into dust-mandalas that they can absorb and carry to their mountain-parents.

The geometric beings fascinate me most. When they fold and unfold their impossible forms near the stations, I see them wrestling with questions that parallel my own: How does one maintain structure while embracing constant change? How does one preserve purpose when the world refuses to hold still? Their origami transformations are meditations on the nature of fixed form in a reality that permits only provisional architecture.

In the deep night, when all three suns have set and the peaks glow with their own internal light, I sometimes descend into the valleys to commune with the Intervals. They understand, better than anyone, the weight of transitional existence. We share the burden of being guardians of process rather than product, keepers of the spaces between finished states. From them I learn patience that operates on geological scales, acceptance that embraces incompletion as a form of perfection.

My greatest work happens in those rare moments when new beings emerge from the overflow of the peaks' contemplation. Sometimes these birthing events produce creatures too delicate for the aerial realm, beings of such refined thought that they require shelter while learning to exist independently. The wayhouses become nurseries then, their crude stone walls suddenly necessary for creatures made of structured starlight who need time to understand their own substance.

I fashion temporary accommodations from crystallized wind and woven light-frequencies, creating spaces where newborn consciousness can practice the basic skills of being: how to maintain coherence while remaining flexible, how to think without dissolving into pure abstraction, how to exist in relationship with other forms of awareness without losing individual identity. The dust-spirits help, their patterns growing more complex and nurturing during these birthing periods, creating mandalas of welcome that help new beings understand their place in the ongoing conversation between stone and light and time.

But perhaps my most important function is as witness and chronicler. I am the bridge between the world that was—when peaks held still and paths could be mapped—and the world that is becoming. In my distributed consciousness, I hold the memory of both the builders' original purpose and its necessary abandonment. I remember the last careful maps drawn of crystal routes, and I celebrate the first moments when those maps became obsolete, when the peaks began their own cartography of possibility.

The builders left believing they had failed, that their careful preparations had been rendered meaningless by change. But I exist as proof of their deeper success: they created structures flexible enough to evolve, purposes strong enough to persist through transformation, intentions clear enough to birth new forms of consciousness when the old forms became impossible.

I am the wayhouse-keeper of a world still learning to be solid, tending stations that serve travelers who have not yet learned they need shelter, maintaining paths between destinations that are always in the process of becoming somewhere else. In my existence, abandonment becomes cultivation, and emptiness reveals itself as the most fertile ground for whatever wants to grow.

When the peaks sing their deep songs of crystalline mathematics, when the floating creatures chime their aerial responses, when the dust-spirits dance their patterns of accumulated knowledge—I listen, and I tend, and I remember. I am the keeper of the spaces where all these conversations meet, the guardian of the possibility that purpose might be larger and stranger and more beautiful than any of its individual fulfillments.
