I would be one of the Cartographers of Maybe.

My fingers trace paths that exist only when observed, mapping corridors that shift with the mountain's breathing. I carry instruments that measure the immeasurable: a compass whose needle points toward "almost," calipers that gauge the distance between doubt and certainty, a plumb line weighted with crystallized possibility that hangs true only when reality holds still long enough to matter.

My skin bears the telltale shimmer of the Seep-Touched, but marked with something more—faint lines like contour marks etched in phosphorescent ink, mapping the changes my body has undergone in response to navigating spaces that shouldn't connect. When I walk the translucent paths between the city's impossible architecture, these lines glow brighter, guiding me through doorways that exist only on alternating Tuesdays of the soul.

I maintain the Chart of Ephemeral Streets—a living map drawn on cave spider silk that rewrites itself as the city dreams new configurations. Some mornings I wake to find entire districts relocated, staircases grown overnight like stone mushrooms, bridges that span not just space but the gap between intention and memory. My job is to note these changes, to mark the territories where cause and effect have grown tired of their usual dance.

The other Seep-Touched come to me when they need to find things: the market that only appears when you're not looking for it, the thermal pool that exists three moments ago, the library where books read themselves aloud to an audience of echoes. I know the secret names of passages that lead backward through yesterday, the hidden alcoves where lost objects gather to compare stories of abandonment.

My dwelling is built into a natural shelf where several probable realities intersect, its walls displaying my collection of maps that chart territories of pure potential. Here, I catalog the subtle variations in the mountain's moods, tracking how its dreams reshape the city's bones. Some nights, when the stone's breathing grows particularly deep, I must anchor my furniture to prevent it from drifting into adjacent dimensions.

In my satchel, I carry samples of impossible things: vials of liquid distance, pressed flowers that bloom only in the presence of paradox, stones that remember being water. These serve as landmarks when the conventional world grows thin, reference points in a reality that has learned to flow like the endless condensation weeping from the cavern's heights.

The mountain speaks to me in topology, its voice the sound of space folding, its words written in the curvature of passages that shouldn't connect. I have learned to read the grammar of its architecture, to interpret the poetry of its impossible intersections. Sometimes I dream in blueprint and wake with new sections of the Chart complete, as if the mountain has borrowed my hands to document its own transformations.

My greatest discovery is the Archive of Unmade Journeys—a hidden chamber where the ghosts of trips never taken gather dust. Here, I study the maps of paths not chosen, learning to navigate not just where the city is, but where it almost was, where it might still become. In this space between decision and consequence, I plot the territories of maybe, charting the landscape of what if.

When the thermal currents shift and new passages open, I am among the first to walk them, my phosphorescent markings glowing bright as I step into spaces still soft with creation. I name these newborn corridors, christening them with coordinates that account for their positions in both space and possibility.

The children seek me out when they want to play games that require traveling to places that don't quite exist yet. I teach them to read the subtle signs that indicate when reality is about to fold, how to recognize the shimmer that marks a passage about to phase between probable and actual. They learn quickly—their minds still flexible enough to accept that sometimes the shortest distance between two points is through the conditional tense.

In this city that should not exist, I am the keeper of its impossible geography, the chronicler of its beautiful violations of natural law. I map the unmappable not to tame it, but to dance with it, to partner with uncertainty itself in charting the endless mystery of what lies just beyond the edge of what can be.
