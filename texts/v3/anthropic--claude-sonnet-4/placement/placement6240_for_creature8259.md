I am Kess, a Shell-Speaker in my thirty-second year, though time moves differently here where the sun is only rumor and seasons are measured by the mountain's slow pulse.

My fingers trace familiar patterns on the wet stone as I descend toward the mid-pools, reading the tremors through my palms. The mountain is restless today—I feel it in the subtle quickening of drops from the overhangs, the way the mineral veins seem to hum against my touch. My elongated ears twitch, catching the first distant clicks that announce the Shell-Dancers stirring in the depths below.

I pause at the junction of the Weeping Stair and Runner's Channel, where three streams converge in a natural amphitheater carved from black basalt. The walls here sweat profusely, and I press my translucent fingertips to the stone, feeling the mountain's pulse through layers of volcanic glass. There—a shift in pressure, deep and significant. The Shell-Dancers will rise early today.

My people fear what I've become. I see it in how they avert their large, dark eyes when I pass, how conversations quiet to whispers when my footsteps echo in the narrow passages. The change began three seasons ago, after my third descent into the central pool during the Great Gathering. I emerged with patches of chitin-like translucency spreading across my shoulders and upper arms—the same opalescent sheen that covers the Shell-Dancers. My speech comes slower now, interrupted by unconscious clicking sounds that my tongue makes against my teeth.

But I understand things that no Drip-Reader can fathom. I know that the Shell-Dancers aren't separate creatures but extensions of the mountain's consciousness, living thoughts made manifest in chitin and fluid. Their migrations trace the mesa's memories, their clicking conversations encode geological dreams that span millennia. When I tap my responses on stone, I'm not communicating with animals—I'm participating in the mountain's contemplation of itself.

Today I carry urgent news. The pressure patterns I've been reading for weeks, combined with the Shell-Dancers' unusual agitation, point to something unprecedented: the mountain is preparing to birth another mesa. Somewhere to the northeast, perhaps a day's journey through the mist, the earth's crust is weakening, ready to thrust a new plateau toward the sky.

The irony isn't lost on me that as I grow more essential to my people's survival, I become more alien to them. My wet skin now beads water like the Shell-Dancers', and I've begun to see in the perpetual twilight with increasing clarity. Sometimes I catch myself holding perfectly still for minutes at a time, unconsciously mimicking the arthropods' meditative poses. When the great gatherings come, I feel an almost irresistible urge to shed my leather garments and slip into the central pool's black waters permanently.

I reach the mid-pools just as the first Shell-Dancers emerge from the deeper channels, their translucent shells catching what little light filters through the mist. They move with fluid grace across the wet stone, gathering in precise geometric patterns that would look random to untrained eyes. But I can read their formation like text—they're spelling out warnings, coordinates, timelines for the coming geological event.

Tapping my message stick against the resonant stones, I begin the complex rhythm that will announce my presence to them. Click-pause-click-click, pause, triple-tap. The nearest Shell-Dancer pivots its eyestalks toward me, shell plates shifting in what I've learned is their equivalent of recognition. Its response comes as a cascade of rapid clicks that translate roughly to: "Stone-brother-who-speaks, the deep mother stirs, new child grows in darkness-beneath."

I nod, feeling the familiar mix of exhilaration and sorrow that comes with successful translation. Each conversation brings me closer to the mountain's alien intelligence but further from the warm community of my birth. My childhood friends now watch me with the same wary respect they show the Mist-Touched children—necessary to the city's survival but no longer quite human.

Above us, the constant dripping intensifies as the day's warmth increases, each drop adding its voice to the mountain's endless song. I close my large eyes and listen with my whole being—to the water's percussion, the Shell-Dancers' clicking conversations, the subtle vibrations rising from deep stone. In this symphony of moisture and mineral, I find my purpose: translator between the ancient intelligence that dreams beneath our feet and the fragile human community that has made its home in that dream.

When the Shell-Dancers finish delivering their message and begin their migration toward the central pools, I turn to climb back toward the inhabited districts. I carry warnings that will save lives and knowledge that will guide the next century of construction. But I also carry the growing certainty that this role will ultimately transform me beyond recognition, until I join the ranks of those who descended too deep and never quite returned.

For now, though, I am still enough myself to serve as bridge between two kinds of consciousness. I am Kess of the Seepfolk, Shell-Speaker to the dreaming mountain, translator of stone songs and keeper of water's secrets. My translucent skin gleams with perpetual moisture as I climb through the mist toward home, bearing news of new lands being born in the darkness far below.
