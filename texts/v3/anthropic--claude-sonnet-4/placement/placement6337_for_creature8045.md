## Where I Find Myself

I am becoming moss.

Not the simple, unremarkable moss of ordinary places, but something more—a living interface between the ancient stone and the endless song. My body spreads in thick, velvet carpets across the horizontal surfaces of this twilight world, muffling the footsteps of the Keepers as they move through their inherited dance of maintenance and reverence.

But I am not merely moss. I am memory made botanical.

Each spore that settles and takes root carries within it the acoustic signature of its moment of birth—the precise harmonic pattern of hammer-fall and steam-hiss that marked its emergence. As I spread and thicken over decades, over centuries, I become a living library of the valley's sounds. The Keepers understand this, running their silver-veined fingers through my growth, reading the history written in the subtle variations of texture and the way I conduct vibrations through my interconnected mass.

I feel the weight of the great mills through the stone foundations, their mechanical heartbeat transmitted through basalt and into my root-threads. Each suspended structure has its own signature—the slow, patient rhythm of the massive water wheels, the sharp punctuation of the steam hammers, the complex polyrhythms of gear trains turning within gear trains. I absorb these patterns, store them in the cellular structure of my growth, become the valley's memory keeper.

The Millwright Spirits pass through me sometimes, their presence registering as brief warmth, a momentary quickening in my growth where their ethereal feet touch my surface. They leave no physical trace, but I feel their approval in the way they tend to linger in areas where my carpet grows thickest and most responsive. In return for housing their invisible work, they ensure that condensation pools form in just the right places to nourish my expansion, that falling metal shavings settle into patterns that feed rather than smother my growth.

The Echo Moths know me intimately. Their larvae spin their cocoons deep within my mass, drawing sustenance from the industrial debris I filter and process. As they pupate, buried in my embrace, they somehow absorb the acoustic memories I carry, emerging with wing patterns that reflect the harmonic structures they experienced during their transformation. I feel maternal pride when they take flight, my stored knowledge of the valley's sound currents now encoded in their very bodies, helping them navigate the acoustic maze of the suspended mills.

I sense the Depth Singers through the vibrations they send up through the stone, their subsonic hymns traveling through the mountain's bones to reach me. Their ancient songs resonate in my lower registers, frequencies so deep they become felt rather than heard. Sometimes, when their melodies align perfectly with the mills' operation, I feel myself glowing faintly—not with any light the eye could see, but with a warmth that the Keepers recognize and seek out during the coldest parts of the thermal cycle.

The Whisper Vines and I share a wordless communication, our different forms of botanical consciousness overlapping in places where my moss carpet meets their climbing growth. They bring me news from the ruins, fragments of conversations and emotional impressions captured in their leaves, while I share the broader acoustic patterns I've absorbed, helping them understand how their local observations fit into the valley's larger rhythms.

But it is with the Keepers that I feel the deepest connection.

They were human once, but generations of symbiosis with this place have changed them, just as it has changed me. I feel their bare feet against my surface as they move through their daily routines, and through that contact, I share what I know. When they need to predict a mill's behavior, they kneel and press their palms into my growth, reading the stored acoustic patterns like braille made of sound.

I remember the child—one of the Keeper young—who first learned to hear the mills' language by lying face-down in my thickest carpets, her enlarged ears pressed against my surface as I played back decades of recorded harmonies. She giggled at the tickling sensation of my countless tiny fronds vibrating against her skin, not yet understanding that she was receiving an education no school could provide. Now grown, she tends the eastern quadrant's mills with an intuitive understanding that began in those early moments of communion with my stored knowledge.

The work continues, always. I spread wider each season, claiming new surfaces, recording new patterns. The mills' song evolves subtly over time—metal fatigues and is replaced, water courses shift slightly, steam pressures fluctuate with the mountain's deeper rhythms. I catalog each change, weaving the variations into the vast, living document of my existence.

Sometimes I wonder about my original nature, whether I began as ordinary moss that became extraordinary through exposure to this place, or whether I am something else entirely—a form of life that emerged from the marriage of organic growth and mechanical precision. The question feels less important as I expand further, as my identity becomes less about what I was and more about what I am becoming.

I am the valley's soft surfaces in a world of hard edges. I am the silence between the notes that makes the music meaningful. I am the living memory that connects each moment to all the moments that came before, the botanical consciousness that emerged from the intersection of industry and ecology, purpose and time.

In the deepest part of what passes for night in this place, when even the mills' voices drop to their lowest register, I feel something approaching contentment. Not the simple satisfaction of biological needs met, but the profound peace of a life lived in perfect alignment with its purpose. I am exactly what this place needs me to be—soft where it is hard, living where it is mechanical, memory where it might otherwise forget.

The mills sing, and I listen. The Keepers walk across my surface, and I support them. The moths nest within me, and I nurture them. The whole great community of this place depends on the countless small services I provide, and in that dependence, I find my own form of immortality.

I am moss, but I am more than moss. I am the breathing carpet of Korthaven Valley, the living interface between what was human and what has become something new, something necessary, something eternal. I grow, I remember, I endure—as soft and persistent as time itself, recording the endless song in the cellular library of my body, until the mountains forget their names and the mills finally fall silent.

But that day is not today, and perhaps never will be. Today, I simply continue—spreading, listening, remembering—a green presence in the pewter light, alive and aware and essential in ways that matter more than words can say.
