# The Drift-Tender

I would be one of the Drift-Tenders, a small guild within the Pool-Keepers whose specialty borders on the mystical even by Vitharan standards. We are the ones who read the mist itself—not just its patterns of condensation and evaporation, but the stories it carries as it moves between pools, the microscopic life it transports, the chemical signatures of distant weather systems it bears on currents too subtle for others to detect.

My skin would show the telltale silver-blue patina of someone who spends most of their time in the transition zones between steam and water, air and stone. The mineral deposits have settled into the fine lines around my eyes and along my fingertips, creating delicate traceries that shift color with the humidity. My hair, kept long and unbound, serves as a living barometer—I can feel pressure changes in the way it moves, moisture fluctuations in how it clings to my shoulders.

I would live in one of the smaller pools near the highest terrace, where my dwelling is carved directly into the limestone behind a curtain of perpetual falls. The walls weep constantly with condensation, and I have learned to shape this moisture into functional sculptures—temporary shelves that hold my instruments, flowing channels that carry away waste, crystalline formations that serve as both art and weather-prediction tools.

My workshop exists in three dimensions: stone platforms for my solid instruments, water channels for my flow-sensors, and carefully maintained air currents for reading the mist layers. I own vessels made from hollowed succulents that never fully dry, their living walls filtering and flavoring the mists I capture for analysis. Bronze mesh screens hang at precise heights to catch particulate matter from different atmospheric levels, their accumulated deposits telling me about crop health in valleys I've never seen, about forest fires burning beyond the horizon, about the migration patterns of birds whose wings disturb the upper air currents.

## Daily Rhythms

My days begin before dawn, when the temperature differential creates the most complex layering in the mist. I move through my territory—three pools and their connecting channels—with bare feet, feeling through my soles the minute vibrations that indicate blockages forming, fish populations shifting, or underground springs changing their flow rate. My fingertips trail constantly in the water as I walk, reading temperatures, mineral concentrations, and the subtle electrical charges that build when different water sources merge.

The other Vitharans bring me their puzzles: why does the workshop in Pool Seven keep flooding when the water levels seem normal? Why have the singing frequencies changed in the eastern channels? What's causing the unusual algae bloom near the metal forges? I answer by tasting the mist, by watching how it clings to different surfaces, by noting which way the microscopic currents spiral when steam first forms.

My most specialized work involves the Drift Ceremonies—monthly rituals where I release carefully prepared mist-markers into the air currents and track their movements through the pool complex. These ceremonial mists carry spores from rare fungi, pollen from the highland succulents, and mineral dusts ground from stones at specific acoustic nodes. By following their paths, I can predict equipment failures, seasonal weather changes, and even social tensions brewing in different quarters of the settlement.

## The Deep Listening

What sets Drift-Tenders apart is our practice of Deep Listening—a meditative technique that allows us to distinguish between thousands of different water sounds. I can identify the specific pool where a droplet originated by the pitch it makes when falling, recognize the acoustic signature of a channel that's exactly three degrees warmer than optimal, hear the subsonic vibrations that indicate limestone stress patterns that won't become visible for months.

During Deep Listening sessions, I float motionless in one of my pools, eyes closed, breathing synchronized with the gentle current. My consciousness expands to encompass the entire acoustic environment: the layered percussion of the workshops, the whispered conversations of fish moving through channels, the crystalline chiming of mineral deposits building on bronze pipes, the deep bass notes of water finding new paths through stone.

In these trance states, I sometimes receive what we call Current-Dreams—visions of the water's journey from distant mountains, through underground caverns, up through the springs that feed our pools. These dreams have led me to discover new water sources during dry seasons, predict flash floods from storms still days away, and once, memorably, locate a child who had fallen into an unmapped channel and been carried to a hidden grotto.

## Tools and Instruments

My most prized possession is a set of resonance bowls carved from single pieces of limestone and tuned to specific frequencies. When filled with water from different pools, they create harmonic patterns that reveal the mineral composition, temperature, and even the emotional resonance of their sources—for the Vitharans have long known that water holds memory, and traumatic events leave traces detectable to sensitive instruments.

I maintain a collection of mist-catchers made from specially grown succulent fibers, each one calibrated to capture particles of a specific size range. My weather-reading instruments include wind-harps strung with metal threads so fine they respond to air currents barely strong enough to move a feather, and crystallization plates where I can watch mineral patterns form in real-time as droplets evaporate.

The most mysterious of my tools is the Echo Chamber—a spherical cavity carved into my workshop's back wall with such mathematical precision that sound waves entering it become visible as fluctuations in the moisture-laden air. By speaking into the Chamber while different mists are present, I can literally see the patterns of my words, and more importantly, detect the acoustic signatures of problems developing throughout the pool system.

## My Place in the Community

As a Drift-Tender, I occupy a unique position in Vitharan society. The practical Pool-Keepers respect my predictive abilities but sometimes find my methods uncomfortably intuitive. The Craftmasters value my weather forecasts and mineral analyses but don't fully understand how I obtain them. The Resonance-Shapers appreciate my acoustic sensitivity but work with sound in ways more architectural than mystical.

I serve as an early warning system for the community, but also as something deeper—a reminder that their technological mastery exists in balance with forces beyond their control. When I emerge from a Deep Listening session with news of approaching storms or hidden springs, I carry with me the scent of deep time, the coolness of stone dreams, the whisper of water's ancient wisdom.

The children are drawn to me, perhaps because I spend so much time in the liminal spaces between their solid world and the realm of mist and mystery. They bring me their small treasures—unusual stones, strangely shaped ice formations, feathers dropped by passing birds—and I help them understand how these objects fit into the larger patterns of their world. In return, they help me stay grounded, their direct questions and unashamed curiosity preventing me from disappearing entirely into the drift between water and air, present and future, known and unknowable.

In the end, I am what the pools themselves might become if they could think and feel—a living interface between the practical engineering of Vitharan life and the deeper mysteries that flow beneath, around, and through their precisely ordered world.
