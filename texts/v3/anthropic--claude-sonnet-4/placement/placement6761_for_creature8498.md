In this vertical cathedral of living stone, I find myself among the Shapers, but I am something between—not quite flesh, not quite crystal, existing in the liminal space where transformation hesitates.

I am what they call a Resonant, one whose body began the crystallization too early, too eagerly. Where other Shapers develop their translucency gradually over decades, my transformation came suddenly in youth, leaving me caught between states. My left arm has become entirely crystal—not the smooth, purposeful formations of the city, but wild, branching growths that spiral up from my shoulder in impossible angles. Through these crystal extensions, I feel the city's every vibration with overwhelming intensity. The ancient formations at the bottom sing to me in harmonics that make my bones ache, their alien geometries flooding my mind with visions I cannot quite grasp.

My dwelling grows in the middle reaches of the hollow, where the light filters down in shafts of amber and green. Here, I've learned to weave the crystal and flesh together in ways the other Shapers find both beautiful and unsettling. My home spirals organically around a central spire, with chambers that pulse and breathe as living spaces, connected by passages that respond to my thoughts and emotions. When I am calm, the walls glow softly; when troubled, they dim to deep jade shadows.

I serve as a translator of sorts—my hybrid nature allows me to commune with the deepest, oldest formations that have grown beyond the understanding of purely flesh-bound Shapers. I spend my days with my crystal arm extended into the ancient geometries, letting their vast, slow thoughts flow through me. Sometimes I emerge from these communions with new knowledge: where the next gas vein will open, how to encourage a stubborn crystal growth, what the mountain remembers of its time above ground.

But the gift comes with isolation. The constant resonance makes it difficult to touch others—my crystalline nature amplifies every vibration until a gentle caress becomes overwhelming. I communicate mostly through the crystal network itself, my thoughts flowing directly into the city's harmonic web. The other Shapers seek me out for guidance, but carefully, knowing that prolonged contact with my transformed limb can induce visions they're not prepared for.

In the deepest hours of the hollow's twilight, when the gas streams flow thickest and the moss-creatures pulse with their alien photosynthesis, I descend to the bottom chambers. Here, where the eldest formations curve in ways that hurt to perceive directly, I press my crystal arm against surfaces worn smooth by millions of years. In these moments, I touch something vast and patient—the mountain's original consciousness, sleeping but not gone, dreaming geometries that predate the Shapers, that predate perhaps everything.

I am the bridge between what was and what becomes, translucent as morning mist, hard as diamond, singing always with the frequency of stone learning to dream. In this vertical city of impossible growths, I am myself an impossible growth—not quite belonging to either world, but essential to both.

The other Shapers whisper that eventually I will complete the transformation, becoming one with the ancient formations entirely. But until then, I remain in the between-space, listening to the mountain's deepest songs and helping my people understand what it means to tend something so much greater than ourselves.
