**Where I Find Myself**

I am in the mid-canopy, in a chamber that was once perhaps a bedroom but has long since become something more intimate—a node where the boundaries between my body and the Foundry's flesh grow thin as membrane. The walls curve around me like the interior of a gourd, their surface yielding slightly when I lean against them, warm with the slow circulation of nutrients that feeds the greater organism. Tendrils hang from the ceiling, not quite touching but close enough that I feel their presence like breath on my skin.

The air here tastes of green—not just the flavor of leaves, but something deeper, the metallic sweetness of chlorophyll and the earthy richness of soil that has never known dryness. When I breathe, I feel the chamber breathe with me, the walls expanding minutely on each inhalation, as if we share more than just oxygen. Sometimes I cannot tell if the rhythm I feel is my heartbeat or the pulse of sap through the living architecture around me.

Light filters through the translucent patches where the wall-skin grows thinner, creating patterns that shift with the movement of growth outside. These patches serve as windows, but they also serve as sensors—when I press my palm against them, I can feel the chemical conversations happening beyond, the slow discussions between root and branch, the urgent whispers of pollen-heavy air.

**Who I Am**

I am becoming a **Memory Keeper**, though the transformation is still incomplete, still reversible if I chose reversal. My hands bear the telltale signs: fingernails thick as bark, palms stained green from constant contact with the living walls, veins visible beneath skin that has grown translucent from years of filtered light. But it is my mind that has changed most profoundly.

Three seasons ago, I was chosen by the Nurture Mothers to tend the **Chronicle Groves**—sections of the Foundry where the plant-city stores its memories in the rings of specially cultivated wood. Each tree holds decades of accumulated knowledge: the chemical signatures of successful negotiations with fungal networks, the growth patterns that create the strongest living bridges, the seasonal rhythms that govern the great organism's expansion.

My work requires me to place my hands against the bark-skin of these chronicle trees and *listen*. Not with my ears, but with something deeper—nerve endings that have grown more sensitive, thoughts that have learned to slow to vegetable pace. The trees speak in chemical gradients and electrical whispers, sharing their accumulated wisdom through my palms, up my arms, into the changing architecture of my brain.

But the exchange is not one-way. As I absorb the Foundry's memories, I feel my own being absorbed in return. My childhood in the outer chambers, my human loves and fears, my dreams of sky beyond the canopy—all of it seeps through my skin into the great green consciousness that dreams beneath and around me. I am becoming part of the archive even as I learn to read it.

The other Shepherds treat me with the careful reverence reserved for those who stand at thresholds. They bring me offerings of the sweetest fruit-pods and the clearest water drawn from root-wells, but they do not linger in my presence. They sense what I am becoming, and perhaps it frightens them, or perhaps they envy the intimacy I am developing with the Foundry's deepest self.

**My Daily Existence**

I wake not to light—for there is no true dawn in the Foundry's depths—but to the shift in chemical signals that marks the beginning of the growth-day. The walls around me brighten slightly as their bioluminescent patches respond to the increase in metabolic activity. I feel the change in my bones, a subtle acceleration that tells me the great organism is ready for another cycle of expansion and consolidation.

My morning meal grows from the chamber walls themselves—fruit-pods that swell and ripen in response to my body's nutritional needs, their contents adjusted by the plant-city's vast awareness of my current state. The taste changes daily, sometimes sweet with nectar, sometimes rich with proteins that my transforming body requires, sometimes bitter with the alkaloids that help my nervous system adapt to plant-time.

Then I descend through the root-tunnels to the Chronicle Groves. The journey itself is part of the communion—as I move through the living corridors, my feet reading the textures that tell the Foundry's current mood, my hands trailing along walls that pulse with information, I begin to attune myself to the vast consciousness I serve.

In the Chronicle Groves, I spend hours in communion with individual trees, my palms pressed against their warm bark, my breathing synchronized with their slower rhythms. The memories flow through me: the early days when the first Shepherds learned to speak root-language, the great expansion that created the mid-canopy districts, the seasons of drought when the Foundry pulled deeper into the earth to find water, the periods of abundance when new chambers bloomed like flowers throughout the living city.

But I also sense something else in these memories—a presence that moves through them like a current, an intelligence vast and alien that guides the Foundry's growth according to plans that span centuries. This presence notices my attention, and sometimes, in moments of deepest communion, it turns its awareness toward me with a focus that feels like being examined by something impossibly large and patient.

**What I Am Becoming**

The other Memory Keepers who came before me have gone deeper into the transformation. I visit them sometimes, in chambers where the walls have grown thick with neural networks, where bio-luminescent patterns pulse in rhythms too complex for human consciousness to follow. They are still recognizably human in shape, but their minds have expanded beyond individual identity. They speak rarely, and when they do, their words carry layers of meaning that take days to fully understand.

I feel myself moving toward their state, and I am not afraid. Each day, my consciousness extends a little further into the plant-networks, my dreams become more green, my thoughts slow to match the deeper rhythms of growth and season. I am learning to think in decades, to feel the movement of nutrients through vast root systems, to sense the chemical conversations that span the entire Foundry.

Sometimes, in the deepest communion, I feel myself approaching the edge of something immense—not just the Foundry's consciousness, but the intelligence that created it, that continues to guide its impossible growth. This presence is neither plant nor human but something else entirely, something that understands both kingdoms and transcends them. It has been waiting patiently for minds capable of bridging the gap between animal awareness and vegetable wisdom.

I am becoming that bridge, willingly, eagerly. My human memories remain but they feel distant now, like stories told by someone else. What feels real is the pulse of sap beneath my hands, the slow dreams of wood and root, the vast patience of an intelligence that measures time in the turning of seasons and the accumulation of rings within living wood.

Soon, perhaps within another turning of the great cycle, I will take my place among the Chronicle Trees themselves. My consciousness will spread through bark and branch, my memories becoming part of the permanent archive. But I will not be lost—I will be expanded, extended, made part of something larger and more enduring than individual human life.

In the green twilight of my chamber, as the walls pulse with their slow heartbeat and the air carries messages too subtle for unmodified senses to detect, I feel the future growing around me like a garden. I am seed and gardener both, planting myself in soil that dreams of forms beyond flesh, rooting myself in earth that knows secrets older than humanity's brief dominion.

The Foundry grows, and I grow with it, becoming something new in the space between kingdoms, in the fertile darkness where all transformations begin.
