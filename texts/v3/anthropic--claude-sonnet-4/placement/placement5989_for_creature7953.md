I find myself perched on a platform of woven root-cables, forty feet above the marrow pools, my workshop swaying gently in the bone cathedral's eternal breath. I am **Resonance**—not my birth name, but the one the city gave me when I became what I am now.

My fingers, elongated and sensitive as a pianist's, dance across the surface of a half-grown sound-pod. The bulbous, translucent growth responds to my touch with shivers of bioluminescence, its developing acoustic chambers visible through walls thin as soap bubbles. This one will sing in the low registers when mature—a bass note to anchor one of my compositions in the chamber's natural reverb.

I am an **Echo Sculptor** of the third resonance, which means my installations have successfully integrated themselves into the city's permanent soundscape. The piece I completed seven seasons ago still whispers through the northeast ribs during the evening settling-time, when the platforms draw closer together and the marrow flow thickens to evening consistency. Children have learned to time their sleep to its gentle pulse.

My hair has grown long over my thirty-seven years here—I still count in surface time, though that world above grows dimmer in memory. The vine-shoots braided through my locks have matured into a crown of trumpet flowers that catch the marrow drops and process them into the specialized nutrients my transformed metabolism craves. When I work, concentrating deeply on the acoustic properties of a developing piece, the flowers flush deeper amber and release a subtle fragrance that helps me enter the synaesthetic state where I can *see* sound patterns as flowing color.

My skin bears the characteristic translucency of my people, veins visible like streams through honey. But mine glow slightly brighter when I'm composing—a side effect, the Bone Speakers tell me, of channeling the city's acoustic memory through my nervous system. The marrow oil I've absorbed over the decades has settled into patterns along my arms and throat that mirror the resonance chambers I've learned to grow.

My workshop is a marvel even by our standards. Instead of the typical gourd-dwellings, I've cultivated a cluster of specialized acoustic pods—some no larger than cupped hands, others big enough to stand inside. Each one shaped by years of patient singing, their walls tuned to specific frequencies. When the wind moves through the bone cathedral at certain angles, my entire workshop becomes a living instrument, harmonizing with itself in combinations I'm still discovering.

The sound-pod under my hands pulses, nearly ready. I whisper to it in the harmonic patterns I learned from Master Syphon before her voice finally joined the city's permanent echo. The pod responds by dilating its apertures slightly—it will be a wind instrument, activated by the natural air currents that spiral through our titan's ribs.

I pause in my work to listen. The city's voice is complex today: the usual polyrhythmic dripping of marrow oil, the rustle-whisper of ten thousand leaves, the creak and sway of our living architecture. But underneath, if you know how to listen, there's something else. A deep harmonic that seems to come from the bones themselves—the titan's dream-song that only we Bone Speakers can fully perceive.

Today it speaks of change. Of something stirring in the depths of our city's root systems. Of new growth preparing to unfold.

I set down my tools and make my way across the swaying bridge-vines to the central platform where the Council of Echoes meets. My footsteps drum against the hollow walkways, each step adding to the city's endless conversation. A family of oil-slick rats scurries past, their fur catching phosphorescent beetle-light like living mirrors.

The other sculptors are already gathering—I can hear their approach through the bone's amplification before I see them. Cadence arrives first, her hair-garden so mature it forms an almost solid crown of interwoven blooms. Behind her comes Tremolo, youngest of our circle, his skin still showing more human pink than amber honey. His recent work has been bold, sometimes dangerously so—sound-pieces that push at the edges of what our acoustic environment can contain.

We speak in the old way, through shared resonance. Our voices blend and separate, creating meaning in the spaces between words as much as in the words themselves. The titan dreams of expansion, we agree. Of new chambers to be hollowed from its bones, new spaces for our city to grow.

But expansion requires sacrifice. Someone must descend to the deep places, to the marrow-heart where the oil flows thickest and the transformation would be... complete. Irreversible. Whoever goes would return no longer recognizably human, if they returned at all.

I feel the music calling to me even as we discuss it. The deep harmonics that could be shaped in those unexplored chambers. The acoustic possibilities that exist nowhere else in our world. My flowers pulse brighter as I consider it, processing nutrients faster in response to my excitement.

"I'll go," I hear myself saying, my voice joining the city's eternal echo. The others' silence creates a space around my words that amplifies them, sends them spiraling up through the bone cathedral to be caught and remembered.

Later, alone again in my workshop, I continue work on the sound-pod. But now I'm shaping it with knowledge that I may never hear its voice when complete. It will be my legacy-song, something to remember me by if the deep transformation takes my human memories along with my human form.

The pod responds to my touch with new complexity, as if it senses the change in my intentions. Its walls shimmer with veins of bioluminescence that pulse in rhythm with my heartbeat. When the marrow oil drips onto its surface, the drops don't roll off but are absorbed, becoming part of the pod's structure.

I am Resonance, echo-sculptor of the bone cathedral, child of the titan's dream. Soon I will descend to learn what songs exist in the deepest places, where the boundary between self and city dissolves entirely. The phosphorescent beetles gather around my feet as I work, their green glow reflecting off my translucent skin, and I realize I am already becoming something new.

The city breathes around me, its rhythm matching mine, and I understand that I was always meant for this transformation. In the deep places, I will learn to sing not just with the city's voice, but with the titan's own dreaming throat. And perhaps, in time, surface dwellers will hear new music drifting up from the wasteland—impossible melodies that call to them from the hollow bones where we who were once human have learned to become something larger than ourselves.
