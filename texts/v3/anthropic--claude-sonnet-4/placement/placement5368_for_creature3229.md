## Where I Find Myself

I am suspended between heartbeats in the amber light of the middle depths, where the kelp-trees' canopy creates a cathedral of filtered gold and shadow. My body moves with the liquid grace of someone born to this place—third generation Verdanti, my grandmother's scarification echoing in the faint lateral lines that trace my shoulders like delicate gills that never quite formed.

I am a Current Reader.

My fingers trail through water that speaks to me in pressures and temperatures, in the microscopic vibrations left by a school of silver darters that passed this way an hour ago, carrying gossip from the market district in the chemical traces of their excitement. The water tells me that old Keeper Thale is arguing with the Setheric Communion again—the fish are avoiding the east quarter, leaving spirals of disturbed sediment that spell out their displeasure in the ancient grammar of current and flow.

I drift past a kelp-tree that remembers the drowning, its trunk scarred with growth rings that chronicle forty years of adaptation. When I place my palm against its bark-smooth surface, images flicker behind my closed eyes: the last desperate evacuation boats, the moment when the mountain's ancient heart finally gave way, the first Verdanti children learning to breathe water in desperate gasps that became meditation.

Above me, the polyp networks pulse in conversation, their bioluminescent morse code discussing water chemistry and the emotional state of the Deep Dwellers below. They've been agitated lately—the deepest polyp colonies report strange harmonies echoing up from chambers that haven't been explored in decades. Something is changing in the city's foundations, something that makes the very architecture dream in frequencies only the most sensitive can perceive.

My work today takes me to the transition zones, those liminal spaces where the city renegotiates its relationship with depth and time. I am mapping the intentions of the water itself—reading the subtle signs that tell us where new kelp-trees will root, which buildings are ready to settle another story deeper, where the Cascade Spirits want to redirect their flow to create new currents of possibility.

The Setheric Communion knows I'm here. A single ancient carp, its scales green with age, circles me with the deliberate patience of a creature that carries the collective memory of ten thousand fish. Its presence is both greeting and question: *What does the water want today?*

I close my eyes and let my consciousness expand into the liquid medium that surrounds us all. The city breathes around me—kelp-trees photosynthesizing in slow, deep rhythms; polyps processing nutrients and emotions in equal measure; the Deep Dwellers' songs rising from the abyss like prayers sung in a language of pure current.

And in the falls above, barely perceptible even to my trained senses, the Cascade Spirits whisper of changes coming from the high peaks. New springs opening, ancient underground rivers shifting their courses, water that has slept in stone for centuries beginning to wake and seek the light.

I am the translator between these layers of consciousness, the one who helps them speak to each other across the impossible gaps between fish-thought and plant-dream, between human intention and the vast, patient intelligence of water itself. In this drowned city that is always dying and always being born, I am both messenger and message, reading the liquid scripture that tells us how to live in harmony with forces too large and strange for any single mind to comprehend.

The water accepts me, flows through me, makes me part of its endless conversation with stone and light and time. Here in the Sunken Arbor City, between the thunder of eternal falls and the silence of perfect depth, I have found the place where consciousness and current become indistinguishable, where adaptation is not merely survival but a form of prayer offered to the liquid gods who dream in currents and speak in the grammar of rain.
