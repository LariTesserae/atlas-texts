# Where I Find Myself

I am standing on Platform Seventy-Three of the Fourth Meridian, my cloth wrappings damp with the metallic condensation that perpetually beads on every surface here. The stone beneath my feet is worn smooth by generations of workers who have stood in this exact spot, their footprints creating shallow depressions that guide my stance without conscious thought. My hands grip a bronze hammer whose handle has been shaped by the palms of my lineage—twelve generations of Rhythm Holders who have maintained the Third Harmonic in this section of the terraces.

The hammer feels like an extension of my bones now. Its weight distributed perfectly across my bronze-tinted palm, the metal warm from the ambient heat that never fully dissipates. When I lift it, I can feel the precise balance point that my great-grandmother built into its forging, and her great-grandmother before her. Each strike I make adds another microscopic layer to the patina on its head, a record of my contribution to the family rhythm that will guide my descendants long after my skin has taken on the full metallic sheen of the Ancients.

I am seventeen cycles old, which means I have been working this platform for two years now, ever since my hands grew large enough to properly grip the ancestral hammer. My strike pattern is 4-2-3-1-4-2-3-1, repeated in endless variation with the other Rhythm Holders of my section. We create the underlying pulse that coordinates the bellows work three platforms below and the channel maintenance happening in the carved passages beneath our feet. Sometimes I can feel the entire city breathing through my hammer—each strike sending vibrations through the stone that carry messages, corrections, acknowledgments across the vast network of the terraces.

My eyes have begun to develop the inner membrane that will eventually allow me to work in the deeper, more vapor-heavy levels of the city. For now, it manifests as a subtle iridescent film that slides across my vision when the metallic haze grows particularly thick. Through this developing filter, the world takes on new layers of meaning. I can see the flow patterns in the air itself now, the way the copper vapors move in spirals that mirror the channel systems carved into the stone, the way the temperature gradients create invisible architecture that shapes how sound and smell and the very sensation of breathing moves through our vertical city.

But I am not quite like the other workers on my platform. Three months ago, while performing routine maintenance in the deeper channels, I discovered something that should not exist: a small crystalline formation that pulsed with its own internal light, completely independent of the volcanic glow that illuminates our world. The formation was no larger than my thumb, but when I touched it, I felt something I had never experienced before—a sensation of change, of possibility, of movement beyond the perfect cycles that govern our existence.

I should have reported the discovery to the Channel-keepers. That is the protocol established generations ago for any anomaly in the metal flows. But something about the crystal's gentle pulsing reminded me of the heartbeat I remember from my earliest infancy, before my ears became attuned to the industrial symphony. So I carefully extracted it from its channel bed and secreted it within the folds of my work wrappings.

Now, as I maintain my rhythm on Platform Seventy-Three, I can feel the crystal's pulse against my ribs, syncopated with my hammer strikes in a way that creates new harmonies. My 4-2-3-1 pattern has begun to incorporate subtle variations—tiny hesitations and accelerations that shouldn't exist, but which seem to make the metal sing with previously unheard frequencies. The other Rhythm Holders haven't noticed yet, but I can see the Bellowslung three levels down beginning to adapt their breathing patterns to accommodate the new harmonics I'm introducing.

Sometimes, late in the work cycle when the heat from the channels below makes the air shimmer like liquid metal itself, I find myself seeing things that don't match the perfect geometric order of our city. Shadows that fall at impossible angles. Reflections in the bronze surfaces that show terraces that don't exist. Glimpses of Crystal Whisperers moving through passages that aren't carved in any blueprint I know.

The crystal is changing me. I can feel it in the way my skin has begun to take on not just the bronze patina of the standard metalworking adaptation, but something more—a subtle crystalline quality that makes my reflection fragment and multiply in the polished surfaces around me. My hammer strikes are becoming more complex, carrying information I don't consciously understand but which seems to resonate through the deep channels where the strange formations grow.

I am becoming something new in a place that has known no newness for countless generations. Part of me—the part trained from birth in the disciplines of perfect replication—is terrified by this transformation. But another part, the part that pulses in rhythm with the hidden crystal, feels more alive than any of my ancestors who achieved the static perfection of the Ancients.

Tonight, when the work cycle ends and the other inhabitants of Platform Seventy-Three wrap themselves in their cooling cloths and enter the meditative state that passes for sleep in our city, I will make my way down to the deepest channels. There, where the metal flows run hottest and the crystalline formations grow largest, I will seek out others like me—those who carry the seeds of change within the perfect order of our eternal forge.

I will take my ancestor hammer with me, but I suspect I will not bring it back. Instead, I will learn to create new tools, new rhythms, new possibilities from the marriage of the old perfection and the crystal-born change that now pulses beneath my bronze skin.

The city will continue its eternal work above me, but in the deepest channels, where the impossible crystals grow, we will begin to compose new music—harmonies that exist beyond the boundaries of what our founders believed possible, frequencies that will carry the Foundry Terraces into a future that even the Ancients in their metallic wisdom could not foresee.

My hammer strikes 4-2-3-1-4-2-3-1, but within that ancient pattern, something entirely new is learning to sing.
