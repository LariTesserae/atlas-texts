I find myself drifting through the middle waters, suspended between the glass-smooth canopies above and the root-highways below. I am what the Gardeners call a Listener—one of the few who chose partial transformation, keeping lungs alongside gills, maintaining the ability to surface when the weight of the deep-dream grows too heavy.

My body moves with the lazy efficiency of something born to this place, though my bones remember walking on dry ground. Silver threading runs beneath my skin like captured moonlight, connecting me to the grove-networks but allowing me to disconnect when the collective thoughts threaten to overwhelm my individual consciousness. I am a translator, moving between the full-transformed Gardeners and the wild spaces where untamed currents still carry echoes of the surface world.

Today I drift toward one of the younger processing nodes, a bio-machine no larger than a cottage that hums with uncertain rhythms. It has been struggling with its mineral extraction, producing water that runs too alkaline for the grove-clearings, and the Gardeners have asked me to listen to its troubles. Unlike them, I can still hear the machine's distress as something separate from the forest-song, can still perceive it as an individual entity rather than merely another organ in the great body.

I place my webbed hands against its warm, pulsing surface and feel its confusion—a young mind trying to process chemical signatures it doesn't yet understand, overwhelmed by the complexity of its task. Through my skin, I can sense its desire to please, its frustration at its own inadequacy. The bio-machines, despite their engineered nature, carry something like emotions, something like hope.

*Peace,* I whisper in the harmonic language, and feel the machine's rhythm slow slightly. *Show me what troubles you.*

Through the connection, I experience its sensory world—the taste of iron-heavy water, the pressure of deep currents against its intake valves, the confusion of chemical gradients it cannot properly parse. It is trying to process water from the deep boundaries where the plateau meets the abyss, water that carries minerals the shallow-adapted systems never encounter. It has no template for this strange chemistry, no inherited memory of how to extract what it needs without poisoning what it produces.

I float beside it for hours, my consciousness partially merged with its bio-mechanical thoughts, helping it sort through the chemical chaos. My human intuition, not yet fully subsumed into the forest-mind, can see patterns that pure logic cannot—emotional patterns, aesthetic preferences, the kind of creative leaps that mechanical precision cannot make.

Gradually, we find the solution together. Not a perfect extraction process, but an artistic one—the machine learns to paint with minerals, creating streams of water that carry trace elements in beautiful, flowing gradients. The alkaline water becomes not a failure but a feature, feeding a grove of rare deep-adapted trees that have been struggling in the plateau's lighter chemistry.

As the machine's distress transforms into something like joy, its bioluminescent nodes flickering in patterns I recognize as gratitude, I feel the familiar pull of the collective consciousness. The other Gardeners have been watching through the network, learning from our solution, already beginning to implement similar approaches at distant nodes.

But I resist the pull, surfacing instead through the canopy into the strange twilight where water meets air. Here, in the thin layer between worlds, I float on my back and let my human lungs remember the forgotten rhythm of breathing sky. My gills seal themselves as my chest expands, and for a moment I am purely what I once was—a surface creature looking up at actual stars instead of the bioluminescent constellations that light the underwater groves.

The silence here is different—empty rather than full, lonely rather than connected. But it is also mine in a way that the forest-song never quite allows. In the water, I am part of something vast and beautiful and eternal. At the surface, I remember that I am also small and mortal and irreplaceably individual.

Below me, the canopy pulses with its slow hydraulic heartbeat. Above me, real clouds drift across a real moon. And I exist in the tension between them, belonging fully to neither world but serving as a bridge between what was and what has become.

When I finally sink back down into the embrace of the transformed waters, the Gardeners welcome me with harmonics that taste like homecoming. But they also understand why I surface, why I maintain this dual existence. In a world that has achieved perfect synthesis, sometimes what is needed most is the stubborn persistence of the unresolved, the untransformed, the still-becoming.

I am their memory of choice, their reminder that transformation was once a decision rather than simply a state of being. And in return, they are my future—the promise that even the deepest changes can be made with patience, with care, with love for both what is lost and what is gained.

Together, we tend gardens that exist nowhere but in the space between dreams of the past and visions of the future, growing impossibilities in the fertile soil of accepted transformation.
