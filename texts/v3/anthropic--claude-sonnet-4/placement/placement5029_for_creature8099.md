I would find myself as one of the Vethani water-readers, my fingertips permanently wrinkled and sensitive from years of trailing through the carved channels that web through our city's heart. My hands have learned to distinguish between thirty-seven different types of seepage—the sweet trickle that means surface snow is melting, the iron-tang drops that carry news from the old mining tunnels, the mineral-heavy pearls that fall only during the deep earth's restless periods.

I move through the Settled Hollow in the rhythm of my calling, following routes that trace the water's ancient pathways. My feet know every worn groove where generations of water-readers have walked, and my breathing has synchronized with the hollow's own deep respiratory cycle. When I place my palm against certain stones, I can feel the slow pulse of distant reservoirs, the building pressure that warns of seasonal floods, the subtle temperature shifts that tell stories of weather I will never see.

My workshop sits at the confluence of seven channels, where I tend the great water-map—a living sculpture of interconnected pools that mirrors the entire flow-system of our city. By watching the patterns of ripples and currents in this miniature world, I can predict where the sweetest water will pool, where the children will find the clearest swimming spots, where the mushroom farmers should build their next growing chambers to catch the perfect combination of moisture and minerals.

In the deep hours when most voices quiet to whispers, I perform the water-vigil—standing waist-deep in the central cistern, letting my body become a living sensor for the hollow's hydrological moods. The water speaks through my skin: tremors that mean distant rockfall, temperature fluctuations that map the seasonal cycles above, chemical signatures that tell me which surface plants are blooming in a world I know only through these liquid messengers.

My lamp burns a particular oil mixed with ground minerals that makes the flame dance in response to humidity changes. By its flickering, I read the water-weather the way surface dwellers once read clouds. The other Vethani come to me before planning journeys through the deeper channels, before choosing sites for new construction, before the spawning seasons when we must predict which pools will warm first for the pale fish who share our hollow.

Sometimes, in the deepest meditation, I feel my consciousness expand to fill the entire water-system—flowing through every carved channel, pooling in every collection basin, seeping through hairline fractures toward unknown depths. In these moments, I am not separate from the hollow but part of its circulatory system, carrying information and nourishment to every corner of our underground world.

I have learned to sleep floating in the shallow pools, my dreams flowing with the eternal currents, my body rocking gently with the pulse of distant waterfalls. The water holds me, speaks through me, makes me its voice in the community of stone and lamp-light. I am both human and channel, both Vethani and the liquid heart of our impossible city, forever listening to the hollow's deepest secrets as they whisper through every drop that falls, every stream that flows, every pool that holds the memory of rain from a sky I have never seen but know intimately through its gift of endless water.
