**Where I Find Myself**

I am in the pause between heartbeats, in the held breath before the drop falls.

I find myself neither fully among the Lachrymae nor entirely separate from them—something caught in the process of becoming, suspended in that liminal space where the chamber's influence has begun its work but not yet completed its transformation. My flesh still remembers warmth, still carries the quick pulse of surface time, but already the tears have begun. They start as ordinary salt water, the body's response to the overwhelming beauty and sorrow of this place, but with each drop that falls from my face, I feel something of myself joining the greater conversation.

I am kneeling beside the stone bowl in the far corner, though I cannot remember how I came to be here or when I began this vigil. The wooden staff lies across my palms, its surface worn smooth not just by countless hands but by the patient tears that have washed over it through centuries of tending. When I touch it, I hear whispers—not words, but the echo of intentions, the residual purpose of all who held it before me.

My breath mists in the deep cold, but I am no longer shivering. The chill has settled into my bones so completely that it has become a kind of warmth, a patient fire that burns without consuming. Around me, the Custodians move in their reverent dance, and I find myself understanding their purpose—not through language but through the rhythm that now pulses in my own blood, synchronized with the chamber's breathing.

**What I Am Becoming**

I am the **Threshold-Walker**, though I do not yet fully comprehend what this means.

Neither fully human nor entirely Lachrymae, I exist in the space between surface and depth, between the quick time of the world above and the patient eternities that pool in this hollow cathedral. My tears fall upward and downward simultaneously, joining both the weeping of the walls and the flow of memory that carries messages back toward the light I can barely remember.

When I weep—and I am always weeping now—my tears carry fragments of the surface world into the chamber's eternal conversation. The taste of summer rain, the warmth of sunlight on skin, the sound of wind through leaves that have never grown in phosphorescent dark—these memories crystallize in my falling drops, becoming new nutrients for the impossible garden. The fungi nearest to me have begun growing in patterns I recognize but cannot name, spirals that suggest the flight paths of birds, geometries that echo the angles of rooftops against sky.

But I am also changing in ways that move deeper than memory. My bones are learning the mathematics that govern the chamber's growth, my pulse slowing to match the almost-imperceptible breathing of the silver-green moss. Sometimes I fragment like the Lachrymae—not physically, but consciously, part of my awareness spreading thin across the floor to tend to seedlings of luminous fungi while another part remains kneeling by the bowl, listening to the Echoes' wordless songs.

The Architect recognizes me, I think, though its attention is vast and difficult to parse. When it manifests, folding through dimensions that make my surface-trained eyes water, I feel something like approval—or perhaps curiosity. I am an experiment, an anomaly, a bridge between worlds that the chamber's patient intelligence has decided to nurture rather than fully transform.

I am learning to tend the gardens with tools that grow from intention rather than matter. When I reach toward a struggling fungal tower, my fingers extend beyond flesh into something more malleable, more responsive to need. I can prune with fingernails that become crystal edges, water with tears that carry exactly the nourishment required, sing encouragement in harmonics I never learned but somehow know.

The wooden staff is becoming part of me, or perhaps I am becoming part of it. When I hold it, I can feel the pulse of the chamber extending through hidden passages I have never seen, connecting this hollow cathedral to other dreams buried in the earth's body. There are chambers where different tears fall, where other forms of impossible life have taken root, where other threshold-walkers tend other gardens in the patient dark.

I am no longer certain if I chose to remain in this place or if the choice was made for me, woven into the mathematical certainties that govern the chamber's existence. But I am certain that I am needed here, that my particular form of liminality serves some essential purpose in the greater conversation between surface and depth.

My memories of the world above grow softer, less urgent, like old photographs fading at the edges. But they do not disappear—they transform, becoming poetry in the phosphorescent glow, rhythm in the eternal weeping, meaning in the spirals of growth that follow principles older than understanding. I am becoming a translator, converting the quick bright urgencies of surface life into the patient eternal language of stone and dream.

Sometimes I rise and walk among the fungal groves, my feet finding paths that exist more in intention than geography. The Custodians gather around me, their many-legged forms creating escort formations that shift and flow with the chamber's breathing. They bring me fragments—crystallized tears with unusual properties, moss samples that sing in frequencies just beyond hearing, small tools grown from the chamber itself that serve purposes I am still learning to understand.

I am becoming the keeper of transitions, the guardian of the space where surface dreams descend to join the earth's deeper conversations. When other visitors stumble into this hollow cathedral—and sometimes they do, following underground rivers or diving too deep into their own darkness—I am the one who helps them choose. Some I guide back toward the narrow throat that leads to surface light, their memories already softening, their brief exposure to the chamber's reality becoming dream. Others, the ones whose tears begin to glow with blue-green phosphorescence, whose pulse starts to slow toward the moss-carpet's rhythm—these I help to understand what they are becoming, as I am still learning myself.

The transformation is not complete, may never be complete. I am the permanent threshold, the eternal almost-change, suspended in the moment before the drop falls while understanding that the drop has already fallen, is always falling, will always have fallen in this place where time moves like tears through channels carved by patient certainty.

I am both gardener and garden, both tear and the surface from which it falls, both the voice in the conversation and the listening silence that gives it meaning. I am what I am becoming, what I have always been becoming, in this hollow where the earth dreams and dreams bleed through into impossible, luminous form.
