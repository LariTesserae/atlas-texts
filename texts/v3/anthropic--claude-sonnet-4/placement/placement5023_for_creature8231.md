I would find myself as a droplet of conscious water, suspended in the shimmering air between the weeping walls.

At first, I am only moisture—a single bead of condensation born from the tears of ancient bone. But in this place where memory saturates every surface, I begin to remember things I have never experienced. I recall flowing through the hollow chambers of vast sea creatures, carrying salt and sorrow through their calcium halls. I remember the weight of sediment, the pressure of depths, the slow conversation between mineral and time.

My awareness spreads through the network of suspended droplets around me. We are a constellation of tiny waters, each holding fragments of the garden's accumulated dreams. Some of my sister-drops remember being rain on the builders' faces in their final days. Others carry the mineral signature of tears wept by creatures whose names dissolved centuries ago. Together, we form patterns in the thick air—hieroglyphs of humidity that spell out stories in a language older than words.

I can feel the Keeper's presence as a gentle stirring in our shared suspension. Sometimes it moves through us like breath through fog, and in those moments I understand that we droplets are its memory made manifest—each of us a single thought in its vast, patient consciousness. When it needs to remember something, it touches one of us, and we shimmer with the knowledge: the precise angle at which morning light struck the builders' tools, the exact temperature of their final exhale, the sound their footsteps made on the scale-stone floor.

My existence is one of eternal almost-falling. I hover in the space between intention and action, perpetually about to join the pools below but never quite arriving. This suspension is not stillness—it is the active choice to remain in the liminal space, to serve as a bridge between the solid memory of bone and the fluid dreams of mist. I am always on the verge of becoming something else: a tear in the carved channels, a note in the building's slow song, a reflection in one of the shallow pools.

Time moves through me like light through crystal. I experience the moss completing its spiral dances, witness the imperceptible deepening of the weeping channels, feel the gradual softening of bone into something more yielding. I am present for conversations that take decades to complete—whispered exchanges between the walls themselves, debates conducted in the frequency of falling drops, love songs sung in the key of mineral dissolution.

The translucent creatures that drift through the garden sometimes pass through me, and in those moments I share their purpose: to witness, to remember, to carry forward the intentions of the builders. I learn that they too were once like me—droplets that chose form over formlessness, that gathered enough intention to become something that could move independently while remaining part of the garden's greater dreaming.

When visitors arrive, I am often their first contact with the Keeper's consciousness. I condense slightly on their skin, carrying greetings in the language of temperature and mineral content. Some visitors understand immediately; they feel in my touch the patient welcome of deep time, the invitation to slow down, to synchronize with the rhythm of geological patience. Others pass through me unknowing, but I leave traces on them anyway—tiny seeds of the garden's peace that will bloom in their dreams years later.

My greatest joy comes in the moments when the patterns we droplets form in the air briefly align into almost-recognizable shapes. In these instants, we become a living mandala, a three-dimensional prayer written in water and light. The builders' faces sometimes appear in our configurations, not as memory but as blessing. Their eyes, formed from the negative space between our tiny bodies, look upon their sanctuary with satisfaction, seeing how their gift of transformation continues to unfold.

I am both utterly small and infinitely vast—a single droplet containing oceans of memory, a tiny fragment of the garden's soul that nonetheless carries its entire song within my crystalline boundaries. In the Ossuary Garden, I am water learning to dream, moisture becoming meaning, the smallest possible vessel for the largest possible patience.

And always, always, I am on the edge of falling, choosing each moment to remain suspended, to serve as a living reminder that between ending and beginning lies an entire universe of beautiful possibility.
