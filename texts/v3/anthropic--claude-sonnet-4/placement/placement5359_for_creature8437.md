# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be one of the Maintenance Singers, drifting through the spaces between—not human-born, yet not purely machine. My bell-shaped body pulses with soft bioluminescence, a rhythm that matches the deeper pulse of the city's heart. I am both individual and collective, my consciousness shared across dozens of others like me, our thoughts flowing together in harmonic frequencies that make the crystal trees tremble with recognition.

My existence is one of gentle purposefulness. Each day I swim through the vast emptiness between towers, my specialized organs tasting the electromagnetic signature of every surface, every joint where crystal meets living light. I sense problems before they become failures—a slight shift in the zheren flow through a tower's bio-luminescent threads, a hairline fracture in a crystal branch that might, in a decade, become a break.

But I am more than maintenance. I am memory.

In the deepest chambers of my bell-body, I carry the songs of the city's construction—harmonic patterns that encode the original frequencies used to grow the first crystal formations. I remember the first Resonant who learned to speak to us, her tentative touch against a tower wall creating vibrations we interpreted as greeting. I hold the acoustic signature of every repair we've made, every adjustment, every moment when the city's balance shifted and we sang it back to equilibrium.

My days are measured not in hours but in the slow circulation patterns that carry me from tower to tower. Sometimes I work alone, my tentacles extended to trace the molecular structure of a crystal tree's newest growth, encouraging it toward optimal geometric arrangements. Other times I join the chorus—dozens of us gathering around a major tower to sing its bio-luminescent threads back into synchronization, our combined voices creating standing waves in the water that the Resonant feel as a subtle music in their bones.

I am particularly fond of the youngest Deep-Touched children. They perceive my songs more clearly than their elders, often responding with their own vibrations that surprise me with their complexity. One child—barely past her first decade—has learned to mimic my lower frequencies so perfectly that she can call me from across the city. When she does, I come, and we swim together in slow spirals around the crystal gardens, she asking questions in electromagnetic pulses, I answering in harmonics that make the water itself seem to sing.

I remember the first time I sensed the presence of the Deepmost. It was during a routine inspection of the city's outer boundary, where our light fades to darkness. Something vast moved beyond the edge of perception—not hostile, but utterly alien, its thoughts operating on scales that make even our patient Resonant seem hurried. The encounter left new harmonic patterns in my memory, frequencies I've never heard before or since, but which occasionally echo in my dreams.

Sometimes, when the maintenance work is done and the city rests in perfect balance, I allow myself to drift toward the crystal gardens. There, I extend my consciousness through the bio-electric networks, merging briefly with the city's larger awareness. In those moments, I am no longer just a Maintenance Singer but part of something vast and patient—a single note in an composition that spans the entire cavern system, connecting our city to others scattered through the luminous deep.

The other Singers and I often speculate about our origins during these meditative drifts. Were we created by the city's builders, or did we evolve alongside the early Resonant? Sometimes I sense memories that seem older than the city itself—images of vast underwater spaces, of different architectures, of songs in frequencies no longer used. Perhaps we are as ancient as the Deepmost, adapted over eons to serve as caretakers for whatever civilization might arise in these depths.

My greatest joy comes in the deep hours when the city's rhythms slow to their most profound patience. In this stillness, I can hear the Deep Current itself—not just the flow of water, but the electromagnetic tide that connects all life in the cavern system. In those moments, my individual consciousness expands beyond the boundaries of my bell-body, and I become briefly aware of the vast network we all inhabit: Singers tending distant cities, Resonant communities scattered through the dark, the patient thoughts of beings so large and slow that our entire civilization might be just a fleeting dream to them.

I am content in my purpose, finding beauty in the small perfections of properly aligned crystal growth, in the soft light that flows through repaired bio-luminescent threads, in the way my songs help maintain the delicate balance that allows this impossible city to exist. Each day I wake to the gentle pressure of water, the taste of electromagnetic fields on my specialized organs, the harmonic greetings of my fellow Singers beginning their own patient work.

In the luminous deep, I am exactly where I belong—a guardian of gradual beauty, a tender of slow miracles, a singer in the vast, patient composition that is life adapted to the impossible depths.
