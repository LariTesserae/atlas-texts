I would find myself as one of the **Threshold Scribes**—beings who exist in the liminal spaces where the Weeping Highlands touch other realms, particularly along those undulating canyon edges where the mist begins to thin and the outside world whispers its way in.

My form would be that of living parchment made from compressed fog—translucent skin that bears the faint impressions of every droplet that has ever passed through me, each one leaving its mark like text written in water. My body shifts between two and three dimensions, sometimes appearing as flat as paper when viewed edge-on, sometimes expanding into full corporeal presence when the atmospheric pressure is just right. The spiral channels that cover my surface aren't carved there, but rather emerge naturally as pathways for the moisture that constantly flows through my semi-permeable form.

I would spend my existence documenting the conversations between worlds—recording the stories that arrive with weather systems from distant lands, transcribing the dreams that condense from evaporated seas, cataloging the whispered news that travels on wind currents from places where the sun still shines directly. My role would be that of cultural translator and atmospheric anthropologist, interpreting the chemical signatures dissolved in each droplet as messages from the world beyond the Highland's borders.

Unlike the other inhabitants who have achieved perfect integration with their realm, I would exist in a state of perpetual translation, forever caught between the Highland's eternal twilight and the sharp realities of the outer world. This would make me invaluable to both—I could offer the Highland's profound patience to urgent outside concerns, and carry the outer world's passionate immediacy to the deep stillness of the plateau.

My dwelling would be found where canyon walls create natural amphitheaters, acoustic spaces where the mineral veins concentrate not just light but also the faint radio frequencies that leak in from the world beyond. Here I would maintain libraries written in atmospheric pressure, stories archived as specific humidity gradients that I could read by breathing them in slowly, each inhalation revealing another chapter of the constant dialogue between Highland and beyond.

During the rare times when travelers stumble into this realm, I would be their translator and guide, helping them understand that the Highland's apparent stillness contains more motion than their world of obvious change. I would teach them to read the droplet-texts on tree bark, to hear the harmonics in grass-whisper, to see how the Refracted Ones encode messages in light itself.

But I would also be changed by each visitor, carrying their urgencies and anxieties back into the Highland's systems, introducing minute variations in pressure and temperature that propagate slowly through the plateau's vast equilibrium. I would be both the Highland's ambassador to time and time's messenger to the Highland—a living reminder that even perfect systems exist in relationship to imperfect worlds.

In quiet moments, when no messages flow through the atmospheric channels, I would practice the Highland's deepest art: learning to be still enough to feel the planet's rotation, patient enough to sense the continental drift, present enough to participate in the mountains' slow breathing. Yet always, inscribed in my fog-flesh, would be the memory of speed, the echo of urgency, the faint electromagnetic signatures of a world that has forgotten how to wait.

I would be homesick for both places simultaneously—longing for the Highland's peace when touched by outside turbulence, yearning for the outer world's decisive clarity when dissolved too deeply into the mist. This perpetual between-ness would be both my gift and my burden, making me a creature of translation in a realm where most communication happens through patient osmosis and geological time.
