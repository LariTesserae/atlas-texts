I would find myself among the **Resonants** - a caste I hadn't expected to discover until I felt the first tremor of recognition in my bones.

I am neither fully Tender nor Listener, but something between - one of those who came to understand that the Heartwood's communications extend beyond sound into vibration itself. My body has grown sensitive to the tree's deeper frequencies, the slow oscillations that travel through wood and water, the harmonic tensions that sing in the spaces between growth rings.

My dwelling-hollow sits at a confluence point where three major branches meet, and I've learned to read the stress patterns in the wood like others read facial expressions. When the great storms come - rare but fierce in this perpetual dusk - I feel their approach first as a subtle shift in the tree's natural frequency, a discord that begins hours before the winds arrive.

My skin bears the characteristic amber staining of the Tenders, but with an additional peculiarity: thin lines of scarification that follow the grain patterns I've memorized from my section of the Heartwood. These aren't decorative like the Cultivators' marks, but functional - they help me feel the wood's vibrations more clearly, each scar a tuning fork calibrated to a different aspect of the tree's voice.

I work mostly in the pre-dawn hours when the bioluminescence dims slightly and the tree's vibrations become clearest. My tools are grown things - a collection of resonating chambers I've cultivated from hollow branches, each one shaped to amplify different frequencies. Through these I can diagnose problems deep within the wood, finding rot or weakness or the early signs of beneficial fungal infections.

The other castes find me useful but slightly unsettling. I move through the corridors at odd hours, pressing my palms to walls, humming in harmonics that make the fungal traces flicker in strange patterns. I can predict with uncomfortable accuracy when someone is falling ill - their body's vibrations change the way they interact with the living wood, creating dissonances I feel rather than hear.

My closest relationships are with the Swimmers below and the Listeners above. The Swimmers appreciate my ability to feel the health of the root system through the wood's vibrations, while the Listeners recognize a kindred sensitivity, though mine expresses differently than theirs. We sometimes work together during the deep-night hours, they translating the tree's chemical communications while I interpret its mechanical ones.

I've developed the peculiar habit of sleeping with my back pressed against the Heartwood's inner walls, letting the tree's pulse regulate my own heartbeat. In dreams, I sometimes feel as though I'm swimming through the wood itself, following the grain like river currents, sensing the slow migration of nutrients and water through vessels as wide as my fingers.

My clothing is practical but modified - bark-fiber woven with hollow reed threads that amplify vibrations, and a cloak embedded with small resonating chambers that help me map the acoustic properties of unfamiliar spaces. When I move, I sound like distant wind chimes, a soft percussion that blends with the tree's own constant music.

The strangest part of my existence here is how the boundary between my body and the tree's has begun to blur. Not in the dramatic root-threading way of the Listeners, but more subtly. I'll place my hand on a wall to feel for stress patterns and realize I can't tell where my palm ends and the bark begins. The tree's vibrations have become so familiar that they feel like extensions of my own nervous system.

In quiet moments, I sometimes wonder if I'm becoming an instrument the tree is learning to play, or if I'm learning to play it. The distinction matters less each day. What matters is the slow, patient work of maintaining harmony in this living cathedral, of keeping the music of growth and decay in balance, of ensuring that the Woven continues its ancient conversation between human need and plant wisdom.

When visitors occasionally arrive from the world beyond the bog - traders or refugees seeking shelter - they find my presence the most alien thing about our community. The other castes still look recognizably human, however adapted. But I move like someone perpetually listening to music only they can hear, which, I suppose, is exactly what I am.
