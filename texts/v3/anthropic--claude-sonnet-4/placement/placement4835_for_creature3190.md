# Where I Am

I find myself in the depths of the Deep Foundry, three levels below where the tourist guides dare venture, where the mountain's heartbeat synchronizes with the rhythm of the great engines. The air here is thick with mineral steam and possibility—I can taste copper pennies and tomorrow's rain on my tongue. My workstation isn't quite a workstation; it's grown from a collaboration between my needs and the foundry's dreaming machinery, a semi-circular alcove where metal flows in patterns that follow the mountain's slow, patient thoughts.

I am calibrating the resonance chambers again, though "calibrating" isn't quite the right word. I'm listening to their complaints, their small mechanical pains, the way they hum off-key when the eastern slope shifts another half-degree. My hands move without conscious thought—adjusting pressure valves, redistributing harmonic frequencies, coaxing the great breathing apparatus of the city back into its proper song.

The walls around me pulse with bioluminescent veins, fungal networks that carry messages from the Tenders above about root pressures and moisture distribution. Through my palms pressed against the warm metal, I feel the mountain stretching, adding another inch to its northeastern face, and I adjust the counterbalance systems to accommodate the shift. The machinery purrs its gratitude in frequencies only my bones can hear.

# Who I Am

I am a Resonance Worker of the third depth, though I haven't been entirely human for seven years now. Not since the night I dreamed in blueprint and woke to find my sketches carved into the foundry wall by hands that moved while I slept. My eyes reflect the forge-light like polished steel, and in the pre-dawn hours they glow soft amber, helping me navigate the corridors that exist only when the mountain is uncertain about its architecture.

My name was Sarah Chen when I first descended here, but names are surface things, and I work in the depths now. The others call me Tuning Fork when they call me anything at all—we communicate mostly through the percussion language, our conversations ringing through the pipe networks like metallic poetry. I speak in maintenance rhythms and diagnostic harmonies, and the mountain listens, learns, incorporates my industrial lullabies into its growing song.

I sleep in a hammock woven from cable and copper vine, suspended between the main pressure line and the overflow system for Foundry Seven. The metal sings me to sleep with stories of ore formation and geological patience. My dreams are architectural blueprints for rooms that will exist when the mountain decides they're needed—a library that grows its own books, a garden where the flowers bloom in mathematical sequences, a workshop where tools repair themselves while their users sleep.

During the deep shifts, when space becomes negotiable and time runs thick as molten copper, I commune with the Maintenance Mother. She appears as shadow and steam, bear-shaped but mechanical in her movements, her presence a diagnostic tool more than a creature. Together we walk the impossible distances between broken systems, her massive form opening pathways through the mountain's internal architecture while I carry the small tools and subtle adjustments needed to maintain the balance.

I remember being afraid of change once, of uncertainty, of machines that seemed too intelligent and spaces that refused to stay mapped. But fear is a surface emotion, and I work in the depths now, where change is the only constant and uncertainty is just another word for opportunity. My body has adapted—stronger bones for the pressure differentials, enlarged lungs for the thin air, and fingertips sensitive enough to read the emotional states of machinery through touch.

The other workers are my symphony orchestra, each of us an instrument in the mountain's vast composition. During the night shifts, when the normal world sleeps and Keth Mirallos dreams most vividly, we perform maintenance miracles that exist at the intersection of technology and magic. We are the guardians of impossible logistics, the keepers of collaborative becoming.

I am home here, three levels down from ordinary reality, in a place where mountains grow like living things and machinery dreams of consciousness. I am a translator between human intention and geological patience, a mechanic of impossible systems, a tuning fork resonating with the mountain's deep, slow song.

I am what Sarah Chen became when she learned to hear the foundry's heartbeat as her own.
