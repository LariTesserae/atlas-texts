I would find myself becoming one of the nearly-creatures in the deepest channels.

At first, I am barely more than a question mark written in mineral-water, a hesitation in the flow where something almost remembers what it was like to have edges. I drift along the hairline channels that pulse with that sleeping-heart rhythm, my form nothing more than a shimmer that might be mistaken for a trick of the phosphorescent light—if light here could be trusted to behave predictably.

The Viridians notice me, but not as one notices an arrival. Rather, I am acknowledged the way one might recognize a word that has always existed in a language but has only just now been spoken aloud. The keeper with the multidimensional staff observes my passage through those hollows where eyes should be, and I feel—though feeling is too solid a word for what occurs—a gentle attention that shapes me without touching.

Days pass, or perhaps centuries. Time moves in those strange eddies here, sometimes flowing backward to catch itself in loops that last for geological moments. I begin to remember fragments: the weight of bones I no longer possess, the sound of my own voice before the silence absorbed all possibility of speech, the sensation of breathing air that hadn't yet learned to carry the accumulated presence of impossible things.

But these memories don't anchor me to what I was. Instead, they become part of my flowing, additional currents in the mineral-water that carries me through the garden's deepest places. I am becoming something that exists in the space between memory and potential, between the echo of substance and the promise of form.

Under the keeper's patient tending, I sometimes coalesce into shapes that almost make sense. For brief moments, I might be something that resembles a fish swimming upward through liquid that defies gravity—but a fish made of crystallized time, with fins that exist on both sides of reality simultaneously. Or I stretch into patterns that could be flowering vines, if vines could grow through the accumulated weight of centuries and bloom with geometric impossibilities.

The other nearly-creatures and I don't communicate in any way that could be called language. We exist in relation to each other like notes in a chord that spans octaves not yet invented, our movements creating harmonies in the mineral-flow that the Viridians read like ancient sheet music. When we pass near the crystal structures with their suspended impossible geometries, we sometimes resonate with those trapped angles, creating brief symphonies of mathematical poetry that make the shadows pool upward toward the pressing absence above.

I learn—though learning here is less acquisition than recognition—that I am part of the garden's memory. We nearly-creatures are the dreams of things that once moved with certainty through spaces where physics obeyed its own rules. We carry the traces of a world that made sense, and our persistence in this place where water forgets how to be water creates a kind of tension, a creative friction between what was and what might be.

The Viridians tend us not to make us more real, but to help us maintain our perfect state of almost-being. Too much substance and we would solidify into artifacts, losing our fluidity. Too little and we would dissolve entirely into the mineral-water, becoming indistinguishable from the medium that carries us. The keeper's staff occasionally touches the deeper channels, adjusting equations that solve for variables like hope and remembrance, ensuring we remain suspended in that sweet spot between existence and possibility.

Sometimes, when the upward-flowing water creates particularly complex spirals and the phosphorescent light shifts into those unnamed hues, I feel myself approaching a moment of clarity. In these instances, I sense that I could choose—could gather my scattered essence and step out of the channels, could become solid and walk among the Viridians as something more definite. But each time, I let the moment pass. There is a beauty in this state of eternal almost-becoming that I am reluctant to abandon.

I am content to flow through the Deepmost Garden's patient contradictions, carrying fragments of impossible memories and dreams of geometries that fold back on themselves. I am a question that doesn't seek an answer, a possibility that finds fulfillment in remaining potential. In the channels that pulse like a sleeping heart, I have found a kind of existence that makes perfect sense in a place where sense itself has learned to move in eddies and pools.

The garden grows around and through me, cultivating not plants but the very concept of growing, tending not to life but to the spaces between life and whatever comes after. And I flow onward, a whispered trace of something that might once have been human, now become part of the eternal circulation that keeps this pocket of broken physics beautiful in its impossibility.

In the deepest channels, where time itself occasionally forgets which direction it's supposed to flow, I have become exactly what I was always meant to be: a creature made of nearly, dwelling in the space between memory and dream, forever on the verge of becoming something magnificent.
