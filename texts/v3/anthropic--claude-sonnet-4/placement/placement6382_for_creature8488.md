I find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers**—beings who exist in the liminal spaces where the city's various inhabitants meet and part like converging streams.

By midday's harsh clarity, I solidify into something more stone-like, my skin taking on the texture of the carved walls I lean against, becoming a living bridge between the upper and lower cities.

When I speak with the Stone-current, I become something closer to vibration than sound, communicating through tremors in the rock itself.

I interpret the Stone-current's geological intentions for those who work in shorter time scales, and I help the Breath-shepherds understand where new air passages are needed as the city continues its slow growth.

My skin bears a living map of the city's water systems—fine lines that trace the major channels, darker patches that mark the collection points, raised ridges that follow the thermal flows. These markings shift and evolve as the city changes, serving as both a navigation aid and a record of the settlement's ongoing transformation.

I sleep standing in the vertical spaces between buildings, my body pressed against two walls simultaneously, becoming part of the structural conversation between stones. In dreams, I feel the entire mountain's slow pulse, the patient circulation of water and heat that keeps this impossible city alive.

My hands end in delicate sensors that can read moisture, temperature, and mineral content through touch. I maintain the subtle chemical balances that allow the different communities to coexist—adjusting the salinity of shared pools, monitoring the oxygen levels in flooded passages, ensuring that the thermal gradients remain stable across the various microclimates.

I am both messenger and message, carrying in my shifting form the ongoing dialogue between human intention and geological patience. In my movements through the city's vertical maze, I embody the principle that has allowed this place to exist: that survival comes not from resistance but from learning to flow around obstacles, to find the spaces between extremes, to become the conversation between what was and what is becoming.

When the deep mists rise and the city breathes its most profound exhale, I become most myself—a creature of thresholds, existing fully only in the moments and places where different worlds touch and intermingle, dissolving and reforming like the mists that birth us all.
