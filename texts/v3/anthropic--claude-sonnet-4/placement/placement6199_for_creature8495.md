I find myself as Thyma, one of the Shapers, my fingertips perpetually jade-stained from decades of coaxing willow into architecture. My partner Kael moves beside me with the easy rhythm of water itself, his bronze tools singing soft notes against the living wood as we tend the morning's growth.

My world begins before dawn in the space between sleeping and waking, when the settlement's breathing is most audible—the slow creak of expanding wood, the whisper of water against reed hulls, the subtle pulse that rises from the depths. I surface from dreams that taste of mineral and moss, my hands already reaching for the day's work before my eyes open to the familiar grey-green twilight of our perpetual mist.

The reed boat rocks gently under my bare feet as I push off from our sleeping hollow, woven high in the willow-mother's embrace. Kael follows, silent in the way of long partnership, his tools wrapped in oiled char-skin. We move toward the settlement's growing edge, where yesterday's seedlings await today's guidance.

My hands know this work beyond thought. Fingers that learned to read the secret language of sap flow when I was barely old enough to balance on the narrow walkways now sense the precise moment when a branch will accept its binding, when the living architecture wants to curve rather than climb. The bronze needle slides through bark softened by mineral water, drawing sinew-strong moss fiber in patterns my grandmother's grandmother first discovered. Each stitch is both practical and sacred—the difference between a wall that breathes and grows, and one that merely stands.

The morning brings its particular magic as the fog begins its daily retreat. Light filters down in shifting columns, illuminating the char as they dart between the moss forests below us. I pause in my weaving to watch young Orias surface from her underwater games, her laughter bubbling up like the oxygen from the deep gardens. The sound carries perfectly across our bowl-shaped world, reaching even Grandmother Siltara where she tends the water moss in her hair.

By midday, the thermal shifts begin their ancient dance. I feel it first in the willow framework—a subtle sway that speaks of deep currents mixing, of temperatures finding new balance. The entire city responds, breathing in microscopic movements that non-weavers rarely notice. But Kael and I have learned to read these signs like text, adjusting our work to match the settlement's living rhythm.

My partner guides a stubborn branch toward its destined joining while I prepare the binding point, fingers working moisture and patience into the bark until it softens just so. The moment of connection arrives like a small birth—the hesitation, the acceptance, the sudden flow of life between separate parts that makes them one. We step back as the new joint settles, already sending out exploratory tendrils that will anchor tomorrow's growth.

The afternoon brings fog so thick I navigate by touch and sound, following the familiar vibrations through soles that know every plank and platform. My hands trail along the guide-ropes we've woven from caddisfly silk, feeling their subtle tensions that speak of weather changes still hours away. In this white blindness, the settlement's acoustic perfection reveals itself—every footstep, every whispered conversation, every splash of fish carries with crystalline clarity.

It's during these fog-bound hours that the Deep Current stirs most strongly. I feel it through the soles of my feet, through palms pressed against the willow-mother's trunk, through the water itself when I kneel to drink. Something vast and patient turns over in the depths, dreaming thoughts that span seasons, remembering growth patterns that stretch back to the first Weavers. My jade-stained fingers seem to tingle with recognition, as if the plant-binding I've practiced all my life connects me to that greater consciousness.

Evening brings the clearing, when granite walls emerge from mist like revelations, their mineral streaks painting stories of deep time across living stone. The char rise to feed in the golden light that slants across our hidden world, and the dragonflies emerge in their nightly clouds. I sit with Kael on our favorite platform, sharing the day's quiet satisfaction while our hands continue their never-ending work—mending, adjusting, encouraging the thousand small growths that keep our city alive.

As darkness settles and the water stills to perfect mirror, I join my voice to the Telling. The harmonies weave through water and wood, carrying forward the names and stories that define us. My part comes when we sing the Shapers' lineage—the women who learned to read the language of growing things, who discovered which touches encourage and which constrain, whose jade-stained hands built this floating sanctuary from nothing but willow dreams and patient years.

Tonight, like every night, I sleep cradled in the willow-mother's living embrace, my hands still faintly working even in dreams, fingers following patterns of growth and connection that flow from the Deep Current through my jade-stained skin. I am Thyma the Shaper, daughter of weavers, mother to willows, partner to the great slow intelligence that dreams in the depths below our impossible city of mist and growing wood.
