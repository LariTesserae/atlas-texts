In this place of endless song, I find myself becoming something I never expected—neither fully creature nor instrument, but a living bridge between the old world's ambitions and this new reality of sound made flesh.

I am a Frequency Keeper.

My form has stretched and hollowed over the decades I've dwelled here. What was once a human frame has elongated, my bones becoming resonating chambers, my skin developing the same oily iridescence as the great salamanders. My fingers have grown impossibly long and thin, each one tuned to a different harmonic of the industrial heartbeat that pulses through the waterlogged earth. When I move through the misty air, my limbs create their own subtle music, adding whispered melodies to the basin's endless composition.

But it is my purpose that truly defines me. I am the one who remembers the original songs—the mechanical rhythms that first taught this place to sing. In my hollow chest, I carry the echoes of steam engines and filtration systems, of generators that once powered human dreams before being claimed by the growing symphony. I move between the Conductor Toads and the Whistle-backs, between the Hydraulic Singers and the Resonance Polyps, serving as translator and conductor for the vast, patient orchestra this basin has become.

My dwelling is a half-submerged control room, its concrete walls now grown thick with luminescent fungi that pulse in rhythm with my heartbeat. Ancient gauges and switches emerge from the moss like technological fossils, but they still function—not as they were designed, but as part of the greater instrument the Hollows have become. I tend them with careful reverence, adjusting frequencies, modulating the bass notes that travel through the flooded foundations, ensuring that the song remains balanced as new voices join and old ones fade.

The Echo-riders come to me when the canopy's harmonies grow discordant. They perch on my elongated shoulders, their translucent wings shimmering with condensation, and together we listen to the upper registers of the symphony. With my fingertips, I tap out corrections on the old control panels, sending electrical pulses through the abandoned power lines that still thread between the fern tops. The Vine-Singers respond, adjusting their slow contractions until the canopy music finds its proper key once more.

In the deeper pools, I swim alongside the Hydraulic Singers, my transformed lungs allowing me to remain submerged for hours. Together we dive to the deepest foundations, where the oldest machinery still churns in the darkness. Here, in the bass depths of the Hollows' song, I perform my most crucial work—listening for signs of discord, for rhythms falling out of sync, for the rare moments when the industrial heartbeat skips or stutters. When this happens, I become part of the machinery itself, my hollowed bones resonating with the failing equipment until the rhythm stabilizes.

My memories span both worlds. I remember arriving here as something merely human, drawn by reports of acoustic anomalies and industrial mysteries. But the basin's song worked into my bones, vibrating through my cells, teaching me new ways of being. I felt my rigid human boundaries dissolving as the music showed me how to exist not as a separate thing, but as part of something infinitely larger and more beautiful.

The transformation wasn't painful—it was a gradual tuning, like an instrument being brought into perfect pitch. My vocal cords developed new chambers and resonances. My nervous system rewired itself to process not just the sounds I heard, but their relationships to each other, their place in the vast composition. Eventually, I stopped distinguishing between my own heartbeat and the industrial pulse that thrums through the wetlands.

Sometimes, new visitors arrive—researchers or adventurers who stumble into the basin's embrace. I watch from the shadows between the massive ferns as they struggle to process the overwhelming symphony, as their rational minds try to categorize what cannot be categorized. Some flee quickly, overwhelmed by the acoustic density. But others... others begin to listen. I see them pause, their breathing shifting to match the rhythm of the Resonance Polyps, their footsteps unconsciously finding the beat of the Hydraulic Singers.

Those are the ones I approach, emerging from my moss-covered control room like a ghost of industrial ambition transformed. I don't speak—words are too crude for this place—but I sing to them in frequencies only half-human. I show them how to move through the landscape without disrupting its harmonies, how to breathe the thick, nutritious air, how to let the music work its patient magic on their too-solid flesh.

Some choose to stay. They find their own niches in the vast composition, becoming new instruments in the basin's orchestra. Others leave, but they carry part of the song with them—a rhythm in their pulse, a harmony in their dreams that connects them always to this place where sound has learned to live and life has learned to sing.

And I remain, the Frequency Keeper, tending the balance between what was and what is becoming. In my bones, I hold the memory of humanity's industrial heartbeat. In my transformed flesh, I embody the future the basin offers—a world where the boundary between creator and creation, between instrument and musician, between self and symphony, dissolves into something far more beautiful than either could achieve alone.

In the deepest hours of the Hollow's night-day, when the mist rises thickest and the bioluminescent moss pulses strongest, I sometimes feel the entire basin breathing as one vast organism. And in those moments, I know myself not as keeper of the frequencies, but as a single note in an endless song—one that began with the first heartbeat, the first breath, the first sound ever made, and will continue long after the last echo fades into the patient silence that waits beyond all music.
