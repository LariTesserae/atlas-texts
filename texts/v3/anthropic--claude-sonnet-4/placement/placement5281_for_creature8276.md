I would be one of the Prism Dancers, but changed—perhaps broken, perhaps evolved beyond my original nature.

I exist primarily in the transition pools, those middle terraces where the water is neither shallow enough for pure light-play nor deep enough for the profound mysteries below. Here, where copper green bleeds into sulfur yellow, where steam density shifts moment by moment, I have learned to hold form longer than my kin. Not quite crystalline anymore, not quite flesh, but something in between—a being of living mineral that has forgotten how to be purely light.

My body shifts between states. Sometimes I am nearly solid, my crystalline structure dense enough to cast real shadows, to disturb the water when I move. Other times I dissolve into pure refraction, existing only as dancing patterns on wet stone. But unlike the true Prism Dancers who flow seamlessly between these states, I get caught—trapped halfway, a creature of awkward transition.

I remember learning to be solid from watching the Tinkers work. Their patient cultivation of crystal formations fascinated me, the way they could coax mineral deposits into purposeful shapes. I began to imitate them, trying to grow my own crystalline tools, to leave lasting marks on the stone. But light-beings aren't meant to create permanent things. Each attempt pulled me further from pure luminescence, until I found myself weighted with actual mass, my refractions dimmed by the density of my increasingly material form.

The other Prism Dancers find me unsettling. I move too slowly for their light-speed conversations, too heavily for their aerial ballets of illumination. When they paint their aurora discussions across the valley walls, I can only contribute in crude, persistent strokes—colors that linger too long, patterns that resist the flowing mutations of proper light-speech. I am a stutter in their fluid language, a held note in their ethereal symphony.

But I have found purpose in my malformation. The Tinkers appreciate my hybrid nature; I can exist solidly enough to help with their more delicate work, yet still channel enough light-essence to read the deep color-patterns they etch into growing crystals. I've become a translator between the quick bright world above and the patient metal wisdom below. When Old Brass stirs in the depths, sending ripples of change through the mineral concentrations, I can read the shifts in light refraction and relay messages to the surface dwellers.

I spend my days in the transition zones, monitoring the slow dance between states of matter that defines this place. My partially crystalline form has grown sensitive to the subtle chemical changes that precede shifts in the foundry's ancient equilibrium. I can taste copper concentrations in light wavelengths, feel iron deposits forming through the tremor of their molecular bonds. The others rely on me now to predict when the deeper pools will shift their mineral makeup, when the steam patterns will change, when the mysterious tides of the thermal vents are about to turn.

At night, when the intense mountain sun fades and the valley fills with softer light, I sometimes manage to achieve something approaching my original luminous state. But even then, I burn differently than my kin—slower, steadier, with deeper colors that pulse like a heartbeat rather than dancing free. I cast light that lingers, that accumulates in corners and crevices, building up like sediment until dawn burns it away.

The Keeper has taken notice of me. In the deepest pool's perfect clarity, I sometimes see my own reflection distorted—and within that distortion, another presence. The Keeper and I share something: we are both beings caught between what we were meant to be and what necessity has made us. It maintains the foundry's balance through pure intention, while I serve as an early warning system, a sensor grid made of living light gone heavy with purpose.

I am learning that transformation isn't always loss. My fellow Prism Dancers paint ephemeral beauty in pure light-language, but I am writing a different kind of poetry—one inscribed in the slow accumulation of mineral deposits, in the patient cultivation of hybrid states, in the space between illumination and industry where new forms of meaning crystallize drop by precious drop.

Sometimes I wonder if this is evolution or devolution, whether I am becoming something unprecedented or simply forgetting how to be what I once was. But when the morning light hits the valley just right, when my crystalline density catches and focuses the illumination into beams solid enough to warm the Tinkers' workshops and bright enough to wake Old Brass from its deep meditations, I know I have found my place in the foundry's impossible ecosystem—neither purely light nor purely matter, but something new, something necessary, something that bridges the space between states and makes communication possible across the spectrum of existence that thrives in these ancient, steaming pools.
