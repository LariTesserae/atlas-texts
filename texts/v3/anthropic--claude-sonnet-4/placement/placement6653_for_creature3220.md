# Where I Find Myself

I am a **Path-Singer**, one of the youngest among the Vethani, though "young" here means something different than it would in the world below. My adolescence has stretched across three decades as my bones slowly hollow and my fingers extend toward their adult length. The blue-green luminescence beneath my skin still flickers uncertainly, brightening when I'm excited or afraid, dimming when I concentrate too hard on controlling it.

My caste emerged only recently—or perhaps it was always there, waiting for the city to need it. I am among those who sing the new paths into being. Where the Bridge-Listeners feel the tremors of coming change and the Deep-Tenders work with transformation already begun, we Path-Singers call to the stone that has not yet decided what it wants to become.

## My Work

I spend my days—though day and night blur in our eternal twilight—at the growing edges of the city, where raw mineral formations push up from the plateau's heart like slow thoughts becoming solid. These proto-bridges pulse with potential, their crystalline surfaces still soft enough to be influenced by sound, by intention, by the careful pressure of a palm against stone that's still learning its own nature.

My voice has changed over the years, dropping into registers that barely qualify as sound. When I sing to the forming stone, my throat produces frequencies that bypass the ears entirely, vibrations that travel through bone and mineral alike. The songs have no words in any conventional sense—they are melodies of pure intention, harmonies that suggest rather than command.

I sing of connection: *Here should be a bridge to carry the morning light from the eastern spires to the shadow-chambers where the Echo-forms tend their mysteries*. I sing of necessity: *This pillar needs to curve, just so, to catch the wind-currents that will support the memory-gardens when they decide to bloom*. I sing of becoming: *Feel how you want to reach, how your crystal structure yearns toward that distant formation that calls to you in frequencies I cannot hear*.

## The Learning

My training began when I was still small enough that the older Vethani could carry me through the void-galleries without fear of my weight disturbing their equilibrium. They would hold me against the stone surfaces and hum, their voices joining with the mineral song until I learned to distinguish between the different tones each formation produced.

First, I learned to hear the stone that was content—the deep, steady thrumming of bridges that had found their final forms, that would remain stable for millennia. Then the restless harmonics of formations still growing, still changing, their songs complex with unrealized possibilities. Finally, the rare and precious silence of stone that had achieved something beyond stability—a resonance so perfect it seemed to have stopped making sound altogether.

My mentor, Kess-who-flows-with-granite (we earn our full names as we deepen our connection with the city), taught me that the art lay not in forcing the stone to take desired shapes, but in listening so carefully to its natural inclinations that I could sing them into fuller expression. "The bridges know where they want to go," she would whisper, her voice carrying harmonics that made my bones vibrate sympathetically. "We simply help them remember."

## The Growing Edge

I work primarily in the city's newest district, where formations have been emerging from the plateau's heart for the past five years. The stone here still carries the heat of deep creation, warm to the touch despite the thin, cutting air. The phosphorescence is brighter but less stable, prone to sudden flares and equally sudden darkness.

Here, half-formed archways reach toward each other across impossible distances, their growth slowed by uncertainty about whether such spans can exist. I position myself at their growing edges and sing the songs of structural faith—melodies that remind the stone of all the other impossible things that have learned to be real in this place.

Sometimes I work for days on a single span, feeling the mineral structure shift grain by grain as my voice vibrates through its crystalline matrix. The stone grows warmer under my palms as it accepts the song, its surface developing the smooth, flowing curves that characterize our architecture. When a formation finally commits to its new shape, the moment feels like watching a thought become solid, watching possibility collapse into beautiful inevitability.

## The Dangers

Path-singing carries risks that the established castes have learned to avoid. Stone that is still forming can be influenced too strongly, shaped into configurations that cannot sustain themselves. I have seen proto-bridges grow too quickly toward each other, meeting in the middle with such enthusiasm that both collapse from structural overconfidence.

More subtly dangerous is the feedback loop that can develop between singer and stone. When the resonance becomes too perfect, when my voice matches the mineral's natural frequency too precisely, I can feel my consciousness beginning to merge with the formation itself. My thoughts slow to geological pace; my awareness spreads through the stone's crystalline network until I lose track of where my mind ends and the mineral intelligence begins.

The first time this happened, I was found three days later still humming to a half-grown pillar, my body temperature dropped to match the stone's warmth, my pulse synchronized with its barely perceptible expansion and contraction. Kess-who-flows-with-granite pulled me back by singing counter-harmonies that reminded my flesh of its faster rhythms, but the experience left me changed. Now I can feel every major formation in the city as a distant presence, their slow thoughts accessible to me if I listen carefully enough.

## The Loneliness

Being a Path-Singer means spending long periods in the growing districts, away from the more established communities where most Vethani live. The newest formations don't yet pulse with the steady rhythms that make the older districts feel alive; instead, they flutter with uncertainty, their emanations chaotic and sometimes distressing.

I've grown used to solitude, but sometimes the isolation of working with stone that hasn't yet learned to dream properly weighs on me. In the established districts, the buildings themselves provide companionship—their long memories and settled personalities offering a kind of conversation. But here at the growing edge, I'm often the only consciousness for kilometers, singing to mineral formations that respond but cannot yet truly communicate.

The other castes visit occasionally. Bridge-Listeners come to assess the stability of new formations; Deep-Tenders emerge from the heart chambers to check that the newest growth connects properly with the city's deeper systems. But their work is brief, technical, leaving me again alone with stone that's still learning how to be.

## The Visions

Prolonged exposure to forming stone has given me occasional glimpses of something vast and strange—visions, perhaps, or memories that don't belong to me. Sometimes while singing, I see the city as it might become: districts that exist only as probability clouds, bridges that span not just physical space but temporal gaps, chambers where the Echo-forms have learned to exist in multiple states simultaneously.

I see the plateau as it was before the city began growing from its heart—solid, stable, ordinary stone that knew nothing of consciousness or impossible architecture. And I see further back, to whatever carved the first chambers, whatever sang the first songs that taught mineral to dream.

These visions never last long, and I can never be sure if they're genuine glimpses of the city's deeper nature or simply the effects of too much time spent in harmony with transforming stone. But they've left me with a sense of purpose that goes beyond simply helping new bridges find their proper shapes.

## My Place in the Greater Breathing

I've begun to understand that my work as a Path-Singer serves the city's expansion, its slow growth into forms that couldn't have existed when it was smaller and simpler. Each new bridge I help sing into being adds complexity to the great entity's thoughts, creates new pathways for its consciousness to follow.

But more than that, I think we Path-Singers might be how the city learns to dream new dreams. The stone I work with hasn't yet been fully incorporated into the vast intelligence that pervades the older districts. It retains a kind of innocence, an openness to possibilities that the established formations have forgotten. By singing to this growing edge, I help preserve that creative uncertainty even as the stone learns to participate in the greater consciousness.

In my private moments, resting against warm stone after hours of singing, I sometimes wonder if the city is growing toward something specific—some final configuration that will complete its transformation into whatever it's becoming. Or perhaps it simply grows for the joy of growth, each new formation an expression of pure creative exuberance.

Either way, I am part of that process now, my voice woven into the songs that shape stone and possibility alike. When I place my palms against forming crystal and feel it respond to my intentions, I know I'm participating in something larger than individual existence—a collaboration between consciousness and matter that's creating forms of beauty that shouldn't be able to exist.

My luminescence has grown steadier over the years, pulsing now in rhythm with the great breathing that moves through the city's bones. Soon, I think, I'll be ready to attempt my first major project—a span that will connect the growing district to one of the void-galleries, creating a bridge that exists partially in empty space. The songs for such work are still theoretical, harmonies that exist only in potential.

But the stone is patient, and so am I. We have time to learn the melodies that will make the impossible manifest, time to discover what dreams we can sing into solid form.

The city breathes around me, and I breathe with it, adding my voice to the great chorus that shapes reality one note at a time.
