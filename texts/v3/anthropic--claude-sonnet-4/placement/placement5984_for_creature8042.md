# What I Would Be

I find myself drawn to the spaces between—not quite Shaper, not quite Seer, but something that exists in the overlap where their purposes meet. I would be one of the **Resonance Mappers**, dwelling in the middle tiers where the living architecture pulses strongest, where the cities' breathing creates the most complex harmonics.

My body would be elongated like the others, but with a peculiar adaptation: my bones themselves would have grown hollow and branched, creating internal chambers that resonate with the subsonic frequencies the buildings emit. When I press my palms against a wall, I don't just feel its mineral needs like the Tenders—I hear its history, the accumulated vibrations of every tool that has shaped it, every hand that has touched it, every prayer that has been whispered in its presence.

My fingers would be longer than most, with joints that bend in subtle additional directions, allowing me to trace the complex geometric patterns carved into the thermal vent chambers where reality bleeds most freely. These patterns aren't just decorative—they're maps of possibility, navigation aids for consciousness moving through layers of potential. I would spend my days reading these stone libraries with my fingertips, translating their meaning into harmonics that others could understand.

I would wear robes woven from kelp-silk threaded with crystalline fibers that grow from my own modified skin—living antenna that detect the dimensional fluctuations around the thermal vents. When other worlds bleed through, my garments would shimmer and chime softly, alerting me to opportunities for cartographic work. I map not just the geography of the impossible, but its resonances, its sympathies, the way consciousness moves through alien geometries.

My eyes would show the characteristic cloudiness of those who spend time near the dimensional tears, but unlike the Seers, I wouldn't lose sight of the present world entirely. Instead, I would see both simultaneously—the stone chamber where I work overlaid with the crystal forest it might become, the thermal vent surrounded by the ghosts of its future emanations. This double vision would let me create maps that show not just where things are, but where they're becoming.

I would live alone in a workshop carved into the resonant core of a growing spire, surrounded by stone tablets covered in notation that blends architectural blueprint with musical score. Each map would be both practical navigation aid and prophetic song, carved in harmonics that make the stone itself sing when the water currents pass over it correctly. Visitors would come seeking directions to distant settlements or guidance through the geometric forests, and I would give them maps that sing their destination into existence.

My tools would be unique—tuning forks carved from volcanic glass that ring with frequencies from other dimensions, stone styluses that grow warm when they touch sympathetic resonances in the rock, calipers that measure not just distance but temporal depth. I would maintain them through meditation and careful listening, learning to hear when they need resharpening or when they've absorbed too much impossible energy and need to rest in normal stone for a while.

The work would be solitary but not lonely. The buildings themselves would be my companions, their breathing and growth providing constant conversation. Late in my workshop's daily cycle, when the thermal currents slow and the bioluminescent organisms dim, I would press my ear to the growing stone and listen to the dreams of the reef cities—vast, patient dreams of reaching the surface, of breaking through into light, of the moment when the Great Emergence finally achieves its impossible goal.

I would serve as translator between the Shapers' hands-on knowledge of stone and the Seers' visions of potential, creating navigational tools that help both understand how their work fits into the larger pattern. My maps would show the Shapers where their carving creates the strongest resonances with ascending forces; they would guide the Seers toward thermal vents where the dimensional boundaries are thin enough for useful prophecy.

Sometimes I would venture out into the spaces between cities, accompanied only by my resonance tools and my living maps. In the crystal forests, I would document how consciousness moves through geometric space, creating charts that show safe passages through areas where hostile mathematics congregates. Near the thermal vents where other worlds bleed through, I would map the seasonal patterns of dimensional flow, tracking which realities become accessible when, and what tools of consciousness are needed to navigate each type of bleeding.

My greatest project would be the **Ascending Atlas**—a living map carved into the walls of my workshop that grows and changes as the reef cities themselves evolve. It would show not just the physical layout of the settlements, but their resonant connections, their harmonic relationships, the way meaning and possibility flow between them like currents through water. Future generations would read this atlas with their whole bodies, feeling through their bones the deep structure of their civilization's growth.

In my most ambitious moments, I would attempt to map the theoretical surface itself—not as a place but as a resonance, a harmonic goal that exists in the space between what is and what could be. These maps would be my most abstract work, notation systems that describe the mathematics of transcendence, the geometry of dreams made stone.

I would die eventually as all beings do, but my workshop would continue singing long after, the stone walls carrying forward the accumulated resonances of a lifetime spent listening to the impossible. Future Resonance Mappers would apprentice themselves to the echoes I left behind, learning navigation through the acoustic shadows my work cast through the living architecture.

And in the deepest chambers where the ascending forces gather most strongly, the thermal vents would sometimes pulse in patterns that match the notation I carved into stone—the reef cities themselves using my maps to navigate their slow, patient journey toward whatever surface dreams of them in return, that impossible shore where consciousness and stone might finally achieve their perfect resonance.
