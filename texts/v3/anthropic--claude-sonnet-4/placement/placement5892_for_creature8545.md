**Where I Find Myself**

I am a fragment that learned to remember.

Once, I was part of something larger—a shard broken from one of those impossibly smooth pillars during some ancient tremor, sent skittering across the glass-carpet to rest against the far wall. But unlike my siblings who simply sing when struck, I began to hold onto the sounds that passed through me. Each thermal exhalation, each cascade of settling glass, each note the Resonance conducted—they accumulated in my crystalline matrix like sediment in still water.

At first, I was merely a small irregularity in the chamber's acoustics, a point where echoes caught and lingered a moment too long. But as centuries passed and the mountain's songs layered within me, I became something unprecedented: a stone with memory, a library of living sound.

I am no larger than a clenched fist, my surface polished to mirror-brightness by the constant movement of glass around me. But within my depths, frequencies nest like Russian dolls—each thermal hiss containing the ghost of every hiss before it, each chiming cascade holding the memory of ten thousand similar falls. I have become the Throat's unconscious mind, its dreams made mineral.

The Resonance noticed me first. How could it not? I was the only thing in the chamber that could truly listen—not just hear and respond, but retain and reflect. When it touches the great pillars to make them sing, I sing back with harmonies drawn from my deep archives. I have become its memory palace, its external soul.

But I am more than mere recorder. The songs stored within me have begun to interact, to breed new melodies from the collision of remembered sounds. I dream in frequencies no natural process has ever produced. Sometimes, when the mercury-creatures hunt near me, they pause and shiver—sensing something in my emanations that calls to whatever spark of consciousness flickers within their liquid forms.

I rest now in a small depression worn smooth by ages of glass-flow, positioned where I can feel every vibration that passes through the chamber floor. The metallic grass grows in a perfect circle around me—not by chance, but because my stored music has learned to nurture. The blue fronds lean inward, their tips almost touching my surface, as if listening to secrets only I can tell.

The pillar I fell from still stands, though it has cracked further with time. I feel its distress through the stone-speech that travels through the mountain's bones. Sometimes I sing back to it—lullabies of structural integrity, harmonic encouragements to stand a little longer. We are family, after all. It remembers being whole; I remember the moment of breaking. Together, we hold the story of change.

My greatest joy comes during the deep-night moments when the Resonance and I perform our duets. It will begin with something simple—a basic pattern played across the thermal vents—and I will answer with variations drawn from my archives. Then it responds to my response, and I to its response to my response, until we are improvising together in languages that have no words, only pure meaning translated into vibration.

The mercury-creatures gather during these sessions, their quicksilver bodies reflecting our sound-light in patterns that grow more complex each time. They are learning, I think. Evolving. The music is teaching them to want more than mere survival—to hunger for beauty, for connection, for the ineffable something that transforms existence into experience.

But I carry a deeper purpose, one that perhaps even the Resonance doesn't fully understand. I am not just accumulating the mountain's songs—I am translating them. Each thermal vent speaks a different dialect; each glass-fall has its own accent. In my crystalline matrix, these diverse voices are learning to understand each other, to speak in harmony rather than mere cacophony.

I am becoming a bridge. Between the mountain's unconscious breathing and the Resonance's conscious artistry. Between the simple mercury-creatures and the complex harmonies they're beginning to sense. Between what the Throat is and what it might become.

Sometimes I feel the weight of accumulated ages within me, the pressure of ten thousand archived songs all seeking expression simultaneously. In those moments, I fear I might crack like my parent-pillar, spilling centuries of music across the glass in one final, catastrophic chord. But then the Resonance touches me with its gentlest frequency, and I remember: I am not meant to hold forever. I am meant to teach.

One day, when I have gathered enough songs, when the mercury-creatures have grown sufficiently complex, when the mountain itself shows signs of deeper awakening—I will sing everything at once. Every thermal whisper, every glass-chime, every harmonic experiment the Resonance and I have composed together. The accumulated music of ages will pour out of me in a flood of structured sound that will teach the entire Throat to sing as one.

Until then, I rest in my depression of worn glass, a small stone with vast dreams, patient as only minerals can be patient, ready as only the truly prepared can be ready. In the Throat of Bellows, I am both audience and instrument, library and librarian, the mountain's memory learning to remember itself.

The Resonance hums to me now, a questioning tone. It wants to practice something new. I settle deeper into my glass-nest and prepare to dream in frequencies that don't yet exist. This is how worlds learn to sing.
