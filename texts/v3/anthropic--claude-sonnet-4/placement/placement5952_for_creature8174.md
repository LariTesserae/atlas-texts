My fingers have begun to develop their true sensitivity. My skin has taken on its adult coloration: pale gold with darker patches that mirror the particular grove-section I tend, as if the living architecture has claimed me as its own.

I would spend my mornings in the spiral terraces, working alongside the silk-moths as they tend their fungal gardens. There is an art to this partnership that I am still mastering—learning to read their subtle wing-signals, understanding when to step back and let them work, knowing how to offer my larger hands to help with tasks their delicate appendages cannot manage. The moths remember me now; when I approach, several will alight on my shoulders, their wings catching the amber light as they whisper their concerns directly into my ear through soft vibrations.

My particular gift—still developing but growing stronger each season—is an ability to sense water-stress before it becomes visible. I can feel the tension in wood when moisture levels begin to shift, hear the almost-inaudible change in timber when a section begins to thirst. The elders tell me this sensitivity runs in my lineage; my genetic-grandmother was one of the great Water-Shepherds who helped design the current flow-system three centuries ago.

In the afternoons, I join the other young Tenders in communion with the velk. We sit in a circle in one of the great hollow-chambers, and the small creatures emerge from their root-nests to share their olfactory memories with us. Today, one of them brings me the scent-signature of a section I've never visited—a deep, green-brown musk touched with mineral notes and the sweet edge of new growth. It's a message: something in the lower reaches needs attention, something only I, with my water-sensitivity, am equipped to address.

I follow the velk through passages that grow narrower as we descend, the ceiling lowering until I must duck beneath branches thick with condensation. Here, in these deeper chambers, the air is so humid it's almost difficult to breathe for someone still adapting to the depths. But I can feel what the velk detected: a subtle wrongness in the water-flow, a blockage forming in one of the ancient channels that could, if left untended, eventually disrupt the perfect balance of an entire grove-section.

Working in the amber twilight, guided by the soft glow of curious beetles who have come to observe my work, I carefully clear the blockage—not by force, but by patient encouragement, coaxing the accumulated minerals to redistribute themselves, guiding new growth to create better flow-patterns. It is delicate work that will take several visits to complete, but there is no urgency here. The Hollows have waited millennia; they can wait a few more seasons for me to do this properly.

In the evenings, I join the twilight chorus—young and old Tenders gathering in the great resonance chambers to sing the day's observations into the collective memory. My voice, still changing as my throat adapts to the humid air, adds its note to the harmony that flows through root and branch, carrying information about water-flows and growth-patterns to every corner of our realm. Sometimes, if I listen carefully during these sessions, I can hear the deeper voices of the Shapers joining from below, their consciousness distributed through the root-networks adding bass harmonies that speak of geological time and the deep patience of stone.

I sleep in a cradle-nook I have been gradually shaping for the past several years, encouraging the living wood to grow into a perfect fit for my changing body. The walls pulse gently with the slow heartbeat of the great root-system, and I dream in colors of growth—deep emerald hope, golden abundance, the silver of perfect moisture balance. In my dreams, I sometimes glimpse what I might become: perhaps a Water-Shepherd like my ancestor, feeling the flow-patterns across vast distances; perhaps eventually one of the deep Shapers, my consciousness spreading through root and fungal network until I become part of the living memory of this place.

But for now, I am content to be what I am: a young Tender learning the ancient art of gentle cultivation, my hands growing more sensitive each day, my understanding deepening like roots seeking water. I move through the breathing halls of my world with the unhurried grace of one who knows there is always time, always another season, always another chance to achieve perfect balance.

In the Verdant Hollows, I have found my place in the great symphony of symbiosis, adding my small voice to the eternal song of growth and care that keeps this ancient paradise in perfect, perpetual bloom.
