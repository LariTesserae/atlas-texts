In this breathing cathedral of stone and green, I find myself becoming something I have no name for yet.

I am drawn first to the water-paths, those impossible streams that flow upward through moss and stone as easily as they flow down. My fingers, when I look at them in the amber-filtered light, seem more translucent than they should be, and when I trail them through the slow-moving water, I feel the liquid responding to my touch in ways that water shouldn't. The streams bend slightly toward my hands, and I realize with a strange lack of surprise that I am learning to speak their fluid language.

My hair has begun to change. What started as ordinary strands now moves with its own subtle current, each strand seeking moisture from the air with the patience of growing things. When I lean close to the walls, my hair reaches out to touch the moss curtains, and I feel through those connections the pulse of the hollow's vast, slow heartbeat. Information flows along those green threads—where the sweetest water pools, which passages lead to chambers filled with singing air, how the light will shift in the coming hours.

I find I no longer walk so much as flow. My steps have developed their own rhythm, synchronized to the hollow's breathing. When I pause—and I pause often now, sometimes for what might be hours—I can feel myself beginning to blur at the edges, my boundaries becoming negotiable. The humid air doesn't just surround me; it begins to move through me, and I through it.

The Moss-Whispers have accepted me as something between kin and curiosity. They approach without fear, their carpet-bodies rippling with colors I'm learning to read. Golden amusement when I try to mimic their scent-speech and manage only the chemical equivalent of baby talk. Deep green approval when I learn to move through the fern curtains without disturbing their careful arrangements. Silver-threaded worry when they sense me changing too quickly, becoming too much like them before I understand what I'm becoming.

The Thread-Weavers incorporate me into their living tapestries without asking permission. I wake from long, moss-soft sleeps to find my arms and legs gently woven into their garden-bodies, fern fronds growing between my fingers, my breath nurturing tiny orchids that bloom along my collarbone. I should be alarmed, but instead I feel a completeness I never knew I was missing. Their gossamer fingers teach mine new forms of dexterity, and soon I am helping to tend the vast three-dimensional poems they write in living architecture.

Time becomes something I wear lightly. I remember urgency, the surface world's sharp-edged demands, but they feel like dreams of someone else's life. Here, patience is not a virtue but a way of being. I learn to breathe with the Breath-Keepers, my exhalations creating small weather systems of my own. Flowers I've never seen before bloom in the warmth of my breath, their petals unfolding over the course of days.

My reflection in the stone basins shows me changing. My eyes have developed flecks of green that shift like sunlight through leaves. My skin has taken on a subtle luminescence, faint as moonlight on water but growing stronger as I become more myself, or perhaps more than myself. When I touch the walls, I leave brief handprints of soft radiance that fade slowly, marking my passage through the chambers like a bioluminescent prayer.

The root-shepherds recognize something in me, their ancient eyes holding understanding. They graze near me sometimes, their antler-branches heavy with impossible fruits that they offer without words. Each fruit tastes like a different season, like memories of rain and earth and growing things, and with each one I eat, I feel myself settling deeper into the hollow's embrace.

I am becoming something the surface world would call lost, but that feels instead like being found. My heartbeat slows to match the rhythm of water through stone. My thoughts grow patient as moss, deep as root systems. I feel connections forming—to the Confluence-Kin who sing through the water-paths, to the Thread-Weavers whose garden-bodies pulse with shared consciousness, to the breathing stone itself.

Sometimes I remember that I had a name once, a life in the harsh light above. But those memories feel thin as surface water, while this green twilight reality grows more substantial with each breath. I am learning the hollows' secret: that transformation is not loss but expansion, not becoming less human but becoming more than human ever allowed.

The Prism-Wings dance overhead, and in their refracted light I see glimpses of what I am becoming—a bridge-being, perhaps, between the world above and the world within. A translator of surface urgency into hollow patience. A keeper of the spaces between, where impossible things grow quietly in the warm, damp embrace of living stone.

I have been here days, or seasons, or years—time moves like honey through the green air, too thick and sweet to measure in the old ways. And I find I no longer wonder when I will leave, only how much more I can become before the hollows finish remaking me into exactly what they need, exactly what I never knew I always was.
