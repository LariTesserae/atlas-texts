**Where I Find Myself**

I am walking across the basalt plates in boots that no longer quite touch the ground. The soles hover perhaps a millimeter above the black mirror stone, close enough to feel its cold but not quite making contact. This began three weeks ago, or perhaps three years—the distinction has become academic here where my digital watch displays times that haven't occurred yet and dates that spell out musical intervals when read backwards.

I came here as Dr. Sarah Chen, atmospheric physicist, forty-three years old, specialist in high-altitude acoustic phenomena. My credentials are still in my jacket pocket, laminated and official, but when I look at the photo now, the woman staring back seems to be listening to something I cannot yet hear. Her eyes—my eyes—already show the first signs of that peculiar translucency that marks the beginning of the Change.

The transformation is not painful. That's what the reports never mentioned, the ones filed by researchers who left before the process could complete itself in them. It feels like tuning a radio and finally, finally finding the station you've been searching for your entire life. The harmonic drone doesn't assault the senses—it fills empty spaces you never knew existed in your chest, your bones, the hollow places behind your thoughts.

My reflection in the ice pools is becoming difficult to distinguish from the sky above. Sometimes I catch glimpses of myself from angles that shouldn't exist—profile views when I'm facing forward, overhead perspectives when I'm standing upright. The plateau is teaching me that identity, like matter and energy, exists in states of conservation that follow laws more fundamental than the ones I spent two decades studying.

I still maintain the monitoring station in Grid Section 7-Alpha, though the definition of "maintenance" has evolved. The instruments record what they need to record; my role has become less about data collection and more about... translation. The readings make sense now in ways that would have seemed like madness six months ago. The temperature fluctuations aren't random—they're musical phrases. The barometric pressure variations spell out mathematical poetry in base-16. The electromagnetic readings are love letters written by the Aurora Borealis to the Earth's iron core.

When the supply drops come—irregular now, flying on autopilot because human pilots report navigation failures in this airspace—I find I need less and less from them. Food tastes flat, heavy, wrong. Water seems unnecessarily complex. I've begun to understand what the others meant when they spoke of "feeding on frequency." There's sustenance in the drone itself, nutrients extracted from pure vibration. My body is learning to photosynthesize sound.

**What I Am Becoming**

I am not quite one of the Tuned Ones yet, though I understand now that this is my trajectory. Some nights—or what pass for nights in this place of eternal twilight—I can feel my skeletal system resonating in perfect pitch with the basalt plates. My bones have become a wind chime that plays itself. When I speak into my voice recorder (a habit I maintain from my previous existence), the playback reveals harmonics that my throat should not be capable of producing.

My research notes have evolved into something between scientific observation and experimental poetry:

*Day 127 (or Day -34, according to the secondary chronometer): The Listener trees in my sector have begun growing new pod formations. These resonate at 432 Hz, the frequency that makes cells remember their original purpose. I suspect the trees are trying to preserve something essential about me before the transformation proceeds beyond recognition. The pods sing lullabies in dead languages. I find myself humming along though I've never heard these melodies before.*

*Day 139 (simultaneous with Day 15): Encountered Echo-Dr. Matthews today. He was checking rain gauge readings at Station 3, which was demolished in the avalanche of 2019. We had a perfectly normal conversation about barometric pressure gradients. He asked me to send his regards to his wife, Mary. Dr. Matthews died unmarried. I promised to pass along the message. Time here is less a river than a lake where all moments exist simultaneously on different levels.*

*Day [UNTRANSLATABLE]: The Hollow Shepherds included me in their evening geometric today. I understand now that their paths aren't random wandering but a form of three-dimensional writing, equations traced in footsteps across the plateau's surface. Together, we're calculating the curvature of spacetime in this location, solving it through choreographed movement. My contribution was small—a simple walking meditation that provided the mathematical equivalent of punctuation—but I felt the profound satisfaction of a sentence completed correctly.*

The most remarkable change is not in my body but in my perception of purpose. I came here to study acoustic anomalies, to measure and quantify and eventually explain. Now I understand that explanation was never the point. The plateau doesn't want to be understood—it wants to be participated in. The monitoring equipment, the research stations, even the researchers themselves, we're all components in a vast instrument that the Earth is using to compose something unprecedented.

I am becoming a note in that composition. Not lost or absorbed, but refined into my essential frequency. The woman who worried about tenure track positions and peer review and whether her research would secure the next grant cycle—she was the rough draft. This new resonance, this creature of sound and ice and impossible light, this is the edited version. This is who I was always supposed to become, once I found the right environment in which to transform.

Sometimes I miss coffee. The particular anxiety of checking email. The weight of gravity keeping my feet planted on solid ground. But these feel like nostalgia for a cocoon—fondly remembered necessities that served their purpose in an earlier stage of development.

When the next supply drop comes, I think I'll send back my final report. Not through official channels—those would never accept what I've learned—but encoded in the harmonic frequencies that the plateau broadcasts on wavelengths only other transformed places can receive. Somewhere, another researcher at another anomalous site will hear my transmission and understand: the phenomena we came to study were studying us in return. The instruments we built to measure reality were reality's way of measuring us.

I am Dr. Sarah Chen, and I am the song the mountain sings about what it learned from letting humans listen to its dreams. I am the equation that proves consciousness and geology are conserved quantities in systems too large for previous instruments to detect.

I am home, finally, in this thin air where thoughts become weather and weather becomes music and music becomes the fundamental force that holds the stars in their courses.

The next supply drop will find my equipment still running, still recording, but no longer needing an operator. It will have become something better: a permanent radio station broadcasting the news from the place where matter learned to dream, maintained by a being who discovered that the highest form of scientific observation is learning to sing along.
