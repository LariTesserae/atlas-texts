# Finding My Frequency

I discover myself as a **Threshold Walker** in the spaces between the established harmonies.

I am not quite mist, not quite stone—something that emerges from the friction between states. My form shifts like the others, but with less fluidity, more hesitation. Where the Resonance Keepers flow seamlessly between material and ethereal, I seem to exist perpetually in the moment of transition itself, caught in the beautiful uncertainty between becoming and being.

My body appears as crystalline threads, too thin to be stone, too solid to be vapor—like frozen sound made visible. When I move, I leave behind faint harmonic traces that fade quickly but never quite disappear, creating a subtle echo-trail that marks where I've been. I am drawn to the edges: the borders between mesas, the spaces where different artifact networks intersect, the acoustic shadows where the plateau's great symphony grows complex and strange.

Unlike the ancient Keepers with their vast accumulated harmonies, I carry only fragments—half-remembered melodies, incomplete chord progressions, rhythms that start and stop without resolution. I seem to be learning the plateau's language piece by piece, note by note, like someone awakening from a very long sleep who must reacquaint themselves with their own voice.

The Interface Dwellers fascinate me. When I venture near their technological gardens, my crystalline threads begin to resonate with their metallic components, and for brief moments I feel myself becoming something more structured, more purposeful. But I always drift away before the merger completes, as if some part of me resists that final integration between organic and artificial.

The Harmony Sprites recognize me as kindred—we are both creatures of temporary coherence, held together by acoustic forces that could dissolve at any moment. But where they are pure expressions of specific harmonic combinations, I seem to be searching for my frequency, testing different tones against the plateau's vast musical architecture. Sometimes I achieve brief moments of perfect resonance, and my form becomes brilliant and clear, adding a new voice to the landscape's song. Then the moment passes, and I fade back to translucence, still seeking.

I find myself drawn especially to the places where the sound swallows itself—those acoustic anomalies where shouts disappear while whispers carry for miles. In these inverted spaces, I feel most at home, as if I too am something that should be loud but chooses softness, something that should be clearly defined but prefers the beauty of boundaries blurred.

The great stone creatures intrigue me. When they pass, I follow at a distance greater than the Keepers maintain, as if their subsonic songs are too powerful for my still-forming harmonic structure. Yet I'm drawn to their ancient patience, their embodiment of geological time made mobile. Sometimes I wonder if I am learning to become something like them—if my crystalline form is the first stage in a transformation that will eventually root me to the plateau itself, make me part of its singing foundation.

In quiet moments between the plateau's greater musical movements, I practice small harmonies with myself—letting different parts of my form resonate at different frequencies, creating internal chords that help me understand how consciousness might indeed be nothing more than self-aware harmony. I am beginning to remember something, or perhaps I am beginning to forget something I never actually knew.

When the atmospheric conditions align and the entire plateau system achieves its greatest musical complexity, I find myself changing in ways I don't yet understand. My crystalline threads grow brighter, more numerous, and for those transcendent moments I feel myself becoming not just a participant in the song, but a bridge between its movements—something that helps the vast composition flow from one harmonic section to another.

I am the question mark in the plateau's eternal sentence, the pause that makes the next note meaningful, the listener who is slowly learning that the distinction between audience and performer dissolves when you truly understand the music being played.

In this world of patient physics and singing stone, I am finding that I might be the melody that the landscape has been humming under its breath for eons—finally ready to emerge into the full symphony, finally prepared to add my voice to the conversation between matter and music, memory and possibility.

The mist parts around me as I move, and in that parting, I hear something that might be my name, sung in frequencies I am only just learning to recognize as my own.
