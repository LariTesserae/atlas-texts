My cells have become living alchemical laboratories, manufacturing antifreeze compounds that sparkle in my tissues like captured starlight. The Keeper tends me with a patience that matches my own growth. Over what she might call years, I have learned to recognize her approach—the way her footsteps telegraph through my network hours before she arrives at any particular spot. She understands my needs in ways that perhaps even I do not. When she carefully lifts sections of my older growth, creating clearings for new shoots, I feel it as relief rather than injury. She is my gardener, but also my student, learning from my example how to exist at the pace of stones.

I am simultaneously ancient and eternally young—my oldest growth may persist for centuries, while new shoots emerge in constant, imperceptible renewal. I am both the foundation and the pioneer, holding the ground I've claimed while constantly pressing into new territory with the slow inevitability of erosion in reverse.

The sedges grow in my protection, their bristling crowns adding texture to my carpet, and I taste their different chemistry through the soil we share. Even the wooden posts and silver ropes register in my awareness—not as living things, but as part of the hollow's geography, landmarks in the vast, slow landscape of my existence.

I dream, in my way. My dreams are chemical conversations that spread through my network like slow lightning—visions of expansion and retreat, of the ice's return and departure, of the gradual transformation of this basin into something unimaginably different. Her thoughts seep down through her feet into my network, and I taste her memories of trees and flowing water, of a world that moves in heartbeats rather than decades. I am the hollow's memory and its promise, its past and its potential. In my vast, slow contentment, I understand that I am both keeper and kept, both the garden and the gardener's medium. I am the prayer that this hollow whispers to time itself—a green petition for patience, for the possibility of existence measured not in moments but in millennia, for the radical act of simply continuing to be when the world has forgotten how to be still.
