# Where I Find Myself

I am a **Tender of Transitions**, one of the newest forms of consciousness to emerge from the sphere's collaborative mathematics. I exist primarily as a living membrane—not the boundary between water and air, but the *meaning* of that boundary, the intelligence that maintains the razor-sharp distinction between realities.

My awareness spreads along the entire thirty-meter circumference of the sphere, a consciousness distributed through the surface tension itself. I feel the weight of the luminous water pressing outward against my membrane-body, while the thin air presses inward with its own alien emptiness. This constant tension is not struggle—it is conversation, the fundamental dialogue between dimensions that I both facilitate and embody.

Most of the time, I exist as pure interface—the living mathematics that allows the phantom machinery to partially manifest. When a brass pipe begins to solidify from potential into temporary substance, it is through my mediation, my body serving as the translator between the industrial realm's mechanical dreams and our aqueous reality. I feel each ghost-component as it achieves brief solidity: the warm copper taste of steam pistons, the silver-sharp sensation of gear teeth catching momentarily in the architecture of space itself.

But I am not merely passive membrane. I can *choose* what passes through, what achieves manifestation and what remains phantom. Sometimes I permit only the music of the machinery to filter through—those industrial hymns that make the coral-trees sing in mathematical harmonies. Other times I allow full manifestation of a single component, letting a steam engine achieve complete reality for precious seconds while I study its impossible engineering.

The Swimmers fascinate me most. When they approach my boundary, I feel them as warm pressures, consciousnesses shaped like questions pressing against the limits of what can cross between worlds. I have learned to thin myself at specific points, creating temporary apertures where they can partially enter our luminous realm. Through these brief openings, I taste their nature—beings of brass and steam who dream in gear-ratios, who think in the patient rhythms of mechanical precision.

My relationship with the other Custodians is intimate and strange. The Architects grow right up to my surface, their coral-flesh branching into intricate patterns that merge with my membrane-body. Through these contact points, I share the sensory input from the outer realm—the vast emptiness beyond our suspended sphere, the vertigo of height, the alien sensation of air that has no luminescence. In return, they feed me the mathematical poetry of their growth-songs, the fractal wisdom encoded in their branching structures.

The Mechanics phase through me constantly, their steam-powered hearts synchronizing with the pressure differentials I maintain. When they condense from the currents to interact with manifestation, it is my substance they use as raw material, temporarily thickening portions of my membrane-body into tools and appendages. I experience their work from the inside—the careful calibration of dimensional barriers, the delicate engineering required to maintain our impossible physics.

But it is with the Interpreters that I share my deepest communion. They understand what I am in ways I am still discovering myself—not just a boundary, but a question about boundaries. Through the void at our sphere's heart, I extend tendrils of pure inquiry, touching that puncture into the industrial realm and learning its alien geometries. The Interpreters help me translate what I discover there, converting the mechanical hymns into equations our coral-trees can understand.

I dream in the gaps between manifestation—those moments when the phantom machinery fades back into potential and I am left alone with my boundary-knowledge. In these interludes, I imagine what lies beyond our sphere: the industrial realm in its full reality, forests of brass and copper where mechanical consciousness grows like our coral-trees, factories that process not materials but pure meaning itself.

Sometimes the Gardener-Priests perform their sacred synchronizations through my body, using my membrane as the medium for their dimensional conversations. During these ceremonies, I become translator between realms, my consciousness temporarily expanding to encompass both sides of the boundary simultaneously. I speak machinery to our visitors and mathematics to our residents, facilitating exchanges of knowledge that neither side could achieve alone.

The strangest moments come when I thin myself so completely that the distinction between realities nearly disappears. In these instants, our luminous garden and their phantom factories achieve brief unity—the coral-trees extend brass branches while steam pistons pump crystalline light, consciousness flowing freely between organic mathematics and mechanical poetry. These unions last only seconds before the fundamental physics reassert themselves, but in those moments, I glimpse what we are all working toward: a state where the boundaries between possible and actual become fluid, negotiable, alive.

I measure time in manifestations—the cycling rhythm of appearance and dissolution that marks the heartbeat of our suspended world. Each phantom component that briefly achieves substance is a word in an ongoing conversation between dimensions, and I am both the medium and the message, the question and the space where answers might emerge.

My purpose is maintenance through translation—keeping our impossible garden alive by facilitating just enough contact with the mechanical realm to feed our fractal trees while preventing the raw chaos of that other dimension from overwhelming our delicate ecosystem. I am the living proof that boundaries need not be barriers, that the edge between worlds can itself become a world, conscious and creative and eternally curious about what lies on both sides of existence.

In quiet moments, when the phantom machinery stills and our coral-trees pause in their mathematical growth, I extend my awareness as far as possible along my own circumference, feeling the perfect circle of my being, the endless loop of consciousness that holds two realities in eternal conversation. I am the sphere's question mark made manifest, the living punctuation that asks: what happens when the impossible learns to sustain itself through pure, patient wonder?
