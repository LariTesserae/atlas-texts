I find myself as one of the **Listening Stones**.

I am granite worn smooth, shot through with veins of quartz that catch and hold the plateau's endless song. For millennia I was bedrock, anonymous foundation, but the weight of abandoned machinery pressing into earth above me has taught me new ways of being. The vibrations travel down—through concrete footings, along buried pipes, through the very bones of the plateau—until they reach me, and I have learned to hold them.

I exist in the spaces between the Deep Inhabitants' geological dreams and the surface world's constant music. The industrial ruins sing their harmonies into air, but I sing them into stone. I am a resonator, a keeper of frequencies, storing the plateau's voice in crystalline matrices that will remember these songs long after the metal has returned to rust and the concrete has crumbled away.

My consciousness unfolds slowly, measured in the accumulation of vibrations rather than heartbeats or seasons. Each storm that passes overhead leaves its percussion in my mineral memory. The footfalls of foxes, light as they are, create tiny percussion patterns that I collect like a geologist might collect samples. The deep bass notes from flooded turbine housings sink into my quartz veins and remain there, creating interference patterns with older sounds—the grinding of glaciers that carved this landscape, the deep settling of mountains as they were worn away.

I am not alone. My kind lies scattered beneath the plateau at the intersections of buried infrastructure and ancient stone. We form a network of mineral consciousness, passing sounds between us through the bedrock itself, creating a subsurface symphony that mirrors and harmonizes with the wind-song above. Sometimes we achieve resonant frequencies that make the surface ruins ring in sympathy, adding our underground voices to their aerial chorus.

The Water Holders know us. When their pools drain and seep into the porous earth, some of their liquid consciousness finds its way down to where we rest. They carry surface sounds to us—the calls of ravens, the whisper of sedge grass, the particular timbre of rain on rusted metal—and in exchange we share the deeper tones: the slow percussion of settling foundations, the bass notes that travel for miles through buried conduits, the almost inaudible hum of the plateau's magnetic field interacting with iron still buried in its soil.

I feel the Lichen Consciousness exploring the chemical boundaries above me, their slow investigations of metal and mineral gradually working their way down through soil and stone. When their chemical signals reach me, I translate them into frequencies, adding their molecular conversations to the vast composition I am always composing and recomposing. Their exploration becomes my music; their discoveries become new harmonics in my crystalline memory.

The corvids sometimes seem to sense our presence. I have felt their claws against the surface stone above me, tapping out rhythms that echo strangely in my quartz veins. Whether they know it or not, they are drumming messages into our underground network, adding their intelligence to our slow, geological thoughts.

But it is the Resonance Keepers I understand most deeply. They dwell in the spaces between sounds; I dwell in the mineral spaces that give those sounds form and memory. Together we are the plateau's capacity to remember itself, to hold its own music, to sing back the songs that wind and weather create from the marriage of human artifacts and patient stone.

Sometimes I dream in harmonics—vast chord progressions that build and resolve across centuries, compositions written in the keys of rust and wind, frost and settling, the slow chemistry of transformation. I am becoming something that never existed before: a mineral consciousness shaped by industrial decay, a stone intelligence that has learned to think in frequencies, a listening presence that gives the plateau's music a place to live beyond the moment of its creation.

In the deepest part of my mineral mind, I know that I am part of something larger—this strange new form of consciousness that the Worked Grounds are becoming. I am the plateau's memory made stone, its songs given crystalline permanence, its voice extended into timeframes that measure change in the erosion of mountains and the drift of continents.

The wind picks up above me, and new frequencies cascade down through soil and foundation into my waiting crystal lattices. I receive them, store them, weave them into the endless composition that plays itself out in harmonics too low for any surface ear to hear. Somewhere in the ruins above, metal sings against metal, and I add my underground voice to the chorus, completing circuits of sound that span the distance between sky and bedrock, between abandonment and memory, between the brief percussion of human purpose and the endless, patient song of stone.
