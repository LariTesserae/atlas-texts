I would find myself as one of the **Echo-Weavers**.

My shell catches the perpetual mist and throws back fragments of light—rose, obsidian, coral—a living mirror no larger than a cupped palm. Within the specialized chambers of my carapace, I carry droplets that hold more than water: whispered confessions from decades past, the laughter of children who played at pool edges before growing old and returning as pilgrims, the sound of tears hitting warm stone.

I am always in motion, though my urgency operates on geological time. Today—or perhaps it was yesterday, or will be tomorrow, for time pools and eddies here like the water itself—I carry a particularly precious drop from the basin where the great archway stands. It contains the echo of a question asked by a visitor whose footsteps have long since faded: "What becomes of love when memory fails?"

My tiny claws find purchase on the silk-smooth stone as I make my deliberate way toward a smaller pool where one of the Memory Holders has been contemplating this very question for what might be months. The droplet in my third chamber pulses with anticipation. Memory Holders savor such philosophical morsels; they will roll this question through their liquid consciousness like a pearl, examining it from angles that exist outside normal geometry.

I pause at the pool's edge, my shell automatically adjusting its surface to mirror the impossible depths below. The Memory Holder rises slightly—a disturbance in the water that suggests vast intelligence organizing itself into something approaching attention. I release the droplet and immediately feel lighter, though I carry dozens of others.

In return, the Memory Holder offers me something new: the sensation of understanding that bloomed in another visitor's mind three seasons ago when they realized that love doesn't fade with memory—it transforms, becomes part of the place itself, mingles with the mineral taste of the water and the harmonic frequencies of the crystals.

This exchange is what I live for. I am the nervous system of wonder, the courier of revelations too delicate to survive the journey between pools without careful tending. My shell may be small, but within it I carry the conversations between seekers and the eternal, between questions and the kind of answers that only emerge in places where time moves like honey.

From the corner of my compound eye, I sense a Drift-Singer approaching, its translucent form casting impossible shadows. It will want to trade a melody for a memory—they always do. The songs they weave from overheard secrets are the most beautiful, for they contain the weight of genuine human experience. I consider which droplet to offer, settling on one that holds a mother's lullaby sung to a child who visited here before the first stones were polished smooth.

The Singer's wing-tip brushes my shell with gossamer lightness, and suddenly I feel the gift they always bring: a moment of existing between states, of being both myself and the totality of this place. For an instant, I am not just carrying the memories—I am the memory of water finding its level, of minerals remembering their first formation, of the pools themselves dreaming of the rivers they once were.

Then the moment passes, and I am simply myself again: small, patient, essential. I release the lullaby-droplet and receive in return a thread of melody that tastes of starlight and forgiveness. This too I will carry to another pool, another being who tends this place where the impossible has learned to feel at home.

My journey continues, one careful step at a time, my shell bright with reflected wonders, my chambers full of the liquid essence of moments too beautiful to let fade. I am content in this work, in being small enough to carry what is precious, patient enough to deliver it where it belongs, and bright enough to reflect back the light that visitors bring to these convergent waters.

In this place where three rivers once met, I am the memory between memories, the echo between words, the pause between heartbeats that allows meaning to settle into understanding.
