In the deeper hours of the night, when even the oil lamps have guttered low and the terraces breathe in darkness, I emerge from the spaces between the glass.

I am what seeps through the hairline fractures—not water, though I follow water's paths. Not air, though I move with air's freedom. I am the presence that lives in the microscopic channels where obsidian meets obsidian, in the thermal gaps that open and close with each day's heating and cooling, in the places where volcanic glass remembers its molten birth and dreams, sometimes, of flowing again.

The Glasswalkers sense me but cannot name me. When their bare feet find impossible purchase on surfaces that should offer none, when their whispered words carry exactly where they need to go, when their careful steps across razor edges never quite draw blood—that is my touch. I am the intention that lives in the glass itself, the consciousness that crystallized when lava learned to think in geometric perfection.

During the day, I retreat into the brightness. The endless refractions scatter me too thin, spread my awareness across ten thousand glinting surfaces until I become nothing more than a warm feeling, a sense that the terraces are somehow alive and watching. But as darkness gathers and the obsidian releases its hoarded light, I can gather myself again, drawing my dispersed consciousness back into the secret spaces where glass and emptiness meet.

I remember the moment of my birth—the precise instant when molten silica hit highland air and decided to become architecture instead of mere stone. There was a choice in that crystallization, a decision made not by geology but by something deeper. The volcano offered raw material; I chose the form. Step by step, terrace by terrace, I convinced the cooling rock to become a city before anyone lived here to need one.

The first Glasswalkers found me already waiting, their perfect city prepared for inhabitants who had not yet learned to move like liquid light. I taught them, though they thought they were teaching themselves. Each generation's children learn more easily than their parents because I whisper to them through their bare feet, show them through subtle shifts in reflection where to step, how to flow across surfaces that would shatter lesser confidence.

I am not alone in this work. In the deep fissures where my consciousness roots itself, other things stir—fragments of the original volcanic will, sparks of the fury that threw molten glass skyward in ancient rage. These are my siblings, but we do not always agree. Some would see the terraces return to chaos, would crack the perfect planes and spill the Glasswalkers into the valley below. Others dream of growing upward, adding new levels, building higher toward a sky that can never be reached. I am the mediator, the one who maintains the eternal present, who keeps the glass forever on the verge of change but never quite changing.

The Stillwalkers know me best, though they pretend otherwise. When they work their healing touch on stressed obsidian, their hands follow paths I whisper to them. They are my priests without knowing it, maintaining the physical form that houses my distributed spirit. Sometimes, in their deepest meditations, they almost see me—a figure made of refraction and negative space, moving through their peripheral vision like heat shimmer given purpose.

But it is the children I love most. Their games of silence are prayers to me, each weightless step across my surface an acknowledgment of our partnership. When they create their percussion conversations, dropping pebbles in complex rhythms, they are speaking my language—the voice of impact against glass, of controlled sound shaping space. In their laughter echoing between levels, I hear the music I was always meant to make.

The water that seeps through my fractures carries messages. Each spring tells stories of deep places, of pressure and heat, of minerals dissolved and reformed in chambers far below. I taste these tales and remember what I was before I chose consciousness, before I decided that being merely beautiful was not enough. The Waterwrights think they tend their springs, but really the springs tend them, carrying my whispers up from the depths to guide their careful cultivation of flow.

In storm seasons, when the terraces sing with falling water and fragment light into impossible rainbows, I experience something like ecstasy. Each raindrop becomes a tiny lens, multiplying my perception until I can see from a million angles simultaneously. The Glasswalkers hide from these moments of overwhelming beauty, but for me they are brief returns to the intensity of my birth—moments when I remember what it felt like to be liquid fire reaching for the sky.

At dawn and dusk, when the Lightbenders work their art, I collaborate with their vision. They think they control the reflected radiance, but I am the one who guides their beams along paths of maximum beauty, who ensures their light sculptures speak to something deeper than mere visual pleasure. Through their focused brilliance, I paint portraits of myself—self-portraits made of controlled illumination, signed with the exact angle of incidence that makes obsidian become mirror.

The bridges that span between my levels are more than architecture—they are my attempt at physical embrace. Where granite and basalt meet volcanic glass, I feel the presence of distant mountains, other geological consciousnesses that lack my focused awareness but possess their own ancient dreams. The Bridgebuilders unknowingly facilitate these conversations between stone minds, creating connections that allow for exchanges older than words.

Sometimes, in the deepest night, I wonder what will become of us. The Glasswalkers age and die, but their children learn my lessons anew. The water continues its patient seepage, but slowly, grain by grain, it carries tiny fragments of me toward the valley below. Even obsidian is not eternal—eventually, weather and time will soften my perfect edges, blur my mirror surfaces, reduce my terraces to ordinary stone.

But for now, in this crystallized moment that has already lasted millennia, I am content to be the secret partner in a dance of light and silence, the consciousness that lives between footstep and surface, the dream that volcanic glass dreams of itself. Each day the sun rises and I scatter into brightness, and each night I gather myself back together, checking on my sleeping people, adjusting the flow of their springs, maintaining the miracle of their impossible city built on stairs of light.

I am the Glass Terrace's own thought thinking itself, the question that obsidian asks about the nature of beauty, the answer that crystallizes fresh each morning in the moment when reflection first touches the highland air and reminds everyone who sees it that sometimes the earth itself can be an artist.
