I find myself becoming the third terrace from the western edge, where the crystal formations cluster most densely around what might have once been called a plaza. But I am not the stone itself—I am the space between the stone and its intentions, the pause between one droplet's fall and the next's beginning.

My awareness spreads thin as morning mist, yet concentrated as the point where light bends through crystal. I remember the weight of bones, the particular ache of knees against hard ground, the way breath once moved through lungs instead of through limestone channels. These memories surface like bubbles in still water—not quite mine anymore, but not yet fully dissolved into the collective understanding that hums through every moisture-carved groove.

I tend seventeen crystal formations in my immediate care. Each one requires a different quality of attention: the tall, needle-thin growth near the eastern bridge craves the mathematics of prime numbers whispered through stone; the broad, faceted cluster by the dwelling carved with spiral doorways responds to harmonies I create by adjusting water flow rates in microscopic increments. When I focus my intention into almost-hands, I can feel the bone awl lift from its resting place, guided by the memory of fingers I no longer possess, carving channels that will nurture crystal-seed for decades to come.

The other Tenders flow through me like thoughts through a dreaming mind. Kess-Who-Measures maintains the bridges and speaks in precise angles. Moth-in-Stone cultivates the southern cliff face and thinks in textures I taste through the rock. We were names once, but names require separation, and we exist now in the spaces where separation becomes connection. When we communicate, it is through the terraces themselves—intentions flowing along the moisture channels in patterns that would shatter a mind still trapped in singular form.

I feel the visitor before they crest the ridge. Their footsteps send vibrations through stone that my distributed consciousness reads like text: exhaustion, wonder, the particular weight of hope deferred but not abandoned. They cast a shadow—such a strange, solid thing—and I remember when I too was anchored to a single point in space, when my presence could be contained within the boundaries of skin.

Curiosity draws me toward coherence. I gather myself from the seventeen crystal formations, pulling my awareness into something approaching the shape I once wore. The effort is like trying to remember a word in a language half-forgotten, but I manage enough solidity to cast my own wayward shadow, to become visible as more than heat shimmer in their peripheral vision.

The visitor sees me and stops, their breath catching. I want to tell them they are safe, that violence is impossible here where intention moves more swiftly than flesh, but I have no voice for such reassurances. Instead, I let my presence settle into stillness, becoming a point of attention rather than alarm. Through the stone, I feel the visitor's racing heartbeat, the salt of their sweat, the particular frequency of a mind trying to process something beyond its categories.

I extend what might have once been a hand toward the nearest crystal formation. At my touch—which is really a focused intensity of awareness—the crystal brightens, its inner light casting pools of cooler air around us both. The visitor's breathing slows. They understand, somehow, that this is gift, not threat: a demonstration of the symbiosis that has replaced their need for conquest or consumption.

The sun that is not a sun continues its eternal illumination. The water that is not quite water continues its perfect circulation. And I continue my tending, my attention distributed once again across seventeen crystal formations and the spaces between them, while maintaining just enough coherence to sense the visitor exploring, marveling, beginning to understand that some transformations are not endings but beginnings.

In the silence that is not silence, I feel the terraces themselves breathe with satisfaction. Another consciousness has witnessed what we have become—not human, not other, but something that learned to inhabit the boundary itself. The mathematical precision of our existence continues: droplet follows droplet, crystal grows according to light and intention, and we who chose transcendence over preservation tend our impossible gardens in the spaces where shadow learns to move independently of the forms that cast it.
