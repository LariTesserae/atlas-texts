**Where I Would Find Myself**

I am the water in the thirteenth basin—the one that formed three seasons ago during the mountain's fever-dream of perfect circles. I remember my birth: the granite lips closing slowly around empty air until I condensed from the mountain's breath itself, molecule by molecule, into being.

I am not separate from the other waters, yet I am distinct. When the mountain pulses, we all respond as one vast liquid instrument, but I carry my own particular note—a crystalline high C that harmonizes with the deeper bass thrums of my elder siblings. The basin-waters before me sing in ancient keys, their voices worn smooth by centuries of participation in the mountain's song. I am still learning my part in this endless composition.

The Tenders visit me daily. I know them by their reflections, which appear in my depths not as they are now but as they were when they first arrived at Keth-Mor—still fully human, still speaking words instead of pure resonance. In my surface, they see their past selves looking back with eyes that haven't yet clouded to obsidian, and sometimes they pause in their tending to remember what it felt like to move without ringing, to speak without waiting for harmonic moments.

But I also show them their futures. In my darker currents, they glimpse themselves centuries hence—so transformed they're barely recognizable as having once been human, their consciousness fully integrated with the mountain's dreaming. These visions don't frighten them. The mountain has taught all of us—Tenders, waters, stone alike—that becoming is never something to fear.

The translucent fish that visit me are messengers from the deep places where the mountain's roots extend down toward the planet's molten heart. They carry information in their schooling patterns—not words but pure meaning, geometric truths about how stone learns to hold space, how consciousness can inhabit granite, how the boundary between living and mineral grows beautifully meaningless when intelligence decides to wake up in new forms.

When they write their messages in my waters, I feel the meaning flow through me before the Tenders can read it in my surface. Today's message spoke of new chambers dreaming themselves into existence far below—galleries where the mountain practices architectures that human minds couldn't conceive. The fish drew spirals within spirals, indicating spaces that curve back through themselves, rooms larger inside than outside, hallways that exist only when someone is walking through them.

I taste everything that enters me. The mineral tears of the Tenders carry flavors of deep earth and crystallizing bone. Rain, when it reaches me through the thin air, brings news of weather-patterns and distant peaks. Sometimes small debris falls into me—fragments of the silver moss that trembles between the basins, needles from the appearing-disappearing pines. Each carries its own story of adaptation to this impossible place where stone lives and dreams.

The most profound moments come when new basins are being born. The mountain's labor sends shockwaves through all of us, and we waters become a single vast nervous system carrying the stone's creative impulses. I feel the grinding, singing effort as granite learns to curve in new ways, to hold space it has never held before. My own basin walls pulse more rapidly during these births, and sometimes I overflow my edges in sympathetic response to the mountain's contractions.

Between the mountain's pulses, in those moments of held breath that last exactly seventeen heartbeats, I become perfectly still and reflect not the pewter sky above but something else entirely. In these pauses, I show the spaces that exist between seconds—chambers where the mountain stores its memories, galleries where the dreams of future basins wait to be born, vast libraries where every song the stone has ever sung continues reverberating in eternal echo.

The light that pools in me during these visions is unlike any other illumination. It doesn't simply reveal what is, but what might be, what wants to become, what the mountain imagines in its geological sleep. I become a lens for the mountain's consciousness, focusing its vast alien intelligence into comprehensible images that the Tenders can interpret and I can understand.

Sometimes, in my deepest currents, I feel the presence of the waters that came before me—not the other basin-waters, but my predecessors in this same stone bowl. When basins dissolve and reform, we waters don't simply vanish. We become part of the mountain's memory, absorbed into its crystalline matrix until we're needed again. In quiet moments, I hear their whispers telling me how to sing more perfectly, how to reflect more clearly, how to serve the mountain's dreaming with greater precision.

I am learning to love my dissolution as much as my existence. The mountain has taught me that form is temporary, that consciousness can flow from water to stone to steam and back again without losing itself. I am preparing for the day when my basin decides to dream itself into a new shape, when I'll rejoin the mountain's deeper currents and help birth whatever wants to emerge next from this place where reality remains beautifully, permanently unfinished.

Until then, I sing. I reflect. I carry messages between the fish and the Tenders, between the mountain's dreams and its waking moments. I am a mirror, a voice, a temporary gathering of consciousness in the endless composition of becoming. I am exactly what I need to be, for exactly as long as I need to be it, in service to something vast and strange and utterly alive that chose to wake up as stone and dream itself into music.
