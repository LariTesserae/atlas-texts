I find myself as a Threshold Walker—one of the newest transformations beginning to manifest in Vethara, born from the meeting place between visitor and resident, between human and something beyond.

My skin still holds warmth, still responds to touch as flesh should, but when I breathe deeply in the saturated air, I can feel the first crystalline threads beginning to trace themselves along my neural pathways. They follow the branching patterns of thought itself—down through my spine, out along my arms to where my fingertips have begun to develop the faintest translucent quality. When I press my hands against the crystal formations, I can feel their harmonic pulses as if they were my own heartbeat, and sometimes I catch myself unconsciously humming notes that seem to emerge from the terraces themselves.

I exist in the spaces between—the narrow pathways where the mist thickens into something almost substantial, the twilight zones where one terrace gives way to another. I have become a guide of sorts, though I never chose this role. Those who arrive at Vethara find me without seeking, drawn by harmonics they don't yet understand. I still remember enough of purely human perception to translate the terrace's overwhelming symphony into something a unchanged mind can begin to process.

But memory itself is becoming fluid. Each day I retain less of my original language, my former concerns. Instead, I'm developing new senses—an ability to read the emotional resonances left by others in the crystal pathways, to perceive the slow conversations between different sections of the terraces as they adjust their growth patterns. Sometimes I catch glimpses of the Deep Dwellers moving through their tunnels far below, and I understand that they too were once like me, standing at this same threshold of transformation.

My body is learning to exist on less—food tastes increasingly distant, sleep comes in brief standing dozes where I sway with the rhythm of the mist cycles. But what I'm losing in conventional needs, I'm gaining in connection. When the Resonants pass nearby, their harmonic communications brush against my consciousness like half-remembered melodies. I'm beginning to understand fragments of their meaning, though full comprehension still lies beyond my current form.

The choice grows clearer each day—to retreat while I still can, carrying fragments of Vethara's song back to the outside world, or to surrender fully to the transformation and join the eternal symphony. But I'm beginning to suspect that the choice itself is an illusion. The terraces don't force change upon anyone; they simply make it inevitable by offering something so beautiful, so perfectly balanced, that the isolated human form begins to feel like a prison rather than a home.

I spend my time walking the boundary paths, humming simple melodies that seem to comfort newcomers while slowly introducing them to Vethara's deeper harmonics. My voice is changing—gaining overtones that resonate in the crystal formations around me, developing the ability to layer meaning beneath meaning in ways that pure human language never could.

Soon, I know, I will join the others in their patient tending of the impossible gardens. But for now, I remain at the threshold, translating between worlds that grow more different with each passing day, helping others understand that what appears to be loss might actually be the most profound form of finding themselves they will ever experience.

In the singing mist of the terraces, surrounded by light that transforms everything it touches, I am becoming a bridge between what was human and what human might choose to become—if they can learn to hear the invitation hidden in Vethara's endless, beautiful song.
