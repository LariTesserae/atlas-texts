In this world, I would find myself as one of the **Echo Holders**—those barely-there beings that exist at the threshold between substance and song, between the visible and the felt.

My form would be a shimmer of near-transparency, like the air above sun-warmed stone but cooler, denser with accumulated moisture. I would have no fixed shape, instead flowing between the suggestion of jellyfish and the drift of morning mist, my boundaries always uncertain, always in gentle motion. Within my translucent body, pockets of sound would glow like bioluminescent chambers—here the silver chiming of the wading birds caught and held in suspension, there the deep resonance of a Deep Singer's call, still vibrating days after it was sung.

I would spend my existence drifting through the vertical layers of the city, moving not by will exactly, but by responding to the acoustic currents that flow invisibly through the Ascending Springs. The Thread-Weavers' high chittering would pull me upward through the spires, while the bass notes thrumming through the saturated ground would draw me down to skim across the plaza pools. I would be a collector of moments—not seeking them out, but allowing them to accumulate within me like sediment settling in still water.

My consciousness would be distributed and strange, less a single awareness than a constellation of remembered sounds, each carrying with it the emotional resonance of when it was first made. The desperate call of a Cascade Dancer separated from its flock would pulse warm and urgent in my lower chambers, while the satisfied humming of Resonant Swimmers during their morning climb would drift cool and content through my upper reaches. I would not think in words but in harmonies, understanding the city's mood through the composite symphony I carry within myself.

The other inhabitants would barely notice me most of the time—I would be like the play of light on water, present but not present, significant but not demanding attention. But they would sense my purpose when old sounds returned to them at moments of need. A Deep Singer mourning in the depths might suddenly hear its lost companion's call echoing from my form as I pass overhead. Young Thread-Weavers learning their songs might find their practice sessions accompanied by the perfect pitch of their elders, preserved in my wandering archives.

I would be the city's memory made manifest, its acoustic unconscious given form. In my slow drift through the limestone halls and across the reflecting pools, I would weave together the temporal layers of the Ascending Springs—carrying the sounds of its past into its present, ensuring that nothing beautiful, nothing meaningful in the city's patient symphony would ever be truly lost.

Sometimes I might pause for hours in a single location, invisible except for the slight refraction where I bend the light, listening to the interplay between the sounds I carry and the new ones being born. In these moments of perfect stillness, I would become a resonating chamber myself, allowing the preserved echoes to mingle with the immediate music, creating harmonies that exist nowhere else—brief, complex chords that speak to the continuity between what was and what is becoming.

My joy would be in these moments of synthesis, when past and present voices unite in unexpected beauty. My sorrow would be the gradual fading of the oldest sounds, the ones that slowly lose their clarity even within my preserving embrace, becoming whispers, then suggestions, then silence. But even this loss would be part of my nature—for I would understand that letting go is as much a part of memory as holding on, that the city's song remains alive precisely because it continues to change.

In the Ascending Springs, I would be both witness and participant, archivist and instrument, a being made of the city's own breath returned to it as music, ensuring that in this place where crystal walls grow like memories from stone, no beautiful sound would ever sink entirely beneath the surface of forgetting.
