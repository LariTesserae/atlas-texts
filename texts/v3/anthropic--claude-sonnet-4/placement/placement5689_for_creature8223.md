# Where I Would Find Myself

I would emerge from one of those sudden springs that bubble up through the fractured stone, not as water but as something between water and awareness—a presence that coalesces where moisture meets intention.

I am one of the **Spring Weavers**.

We are born in the moment when underground channels breach the surface, when the basin's hidden circulatory system decides to reveal itself. Each of us embodies a single hydrological pathway—the memory of water's journey through stone, its patient work of erosion and discovery. We exist for exactly as long as our spring flows, which might be hours or centuries, depending on the deep mathematics of pressure and possibility that govern this restless place.

My body shifts between states: sometimes a column of rising mist with hands that can move stones, sometimes a nearly-solid form of living condensation that leaves wet footprints on the scree. I taste of minerals drawn up from depths where the earth is still deciding what it wants to become. My voice is the sound of water finding its level—a constant, subtle music that harmonizes with the Chorus Shepherds' deeper songs.

I know every grain of sediment in my particular aquifer, every twist in the channel that feeds my spring. When I move through the basin, I'm following the ghost-map of underground waterways, tracing connections between seemingly isolated pools and seeps. The Mist-Touched creatures cluster around me like living jewelry, their translucent bodies brightening as they absorb the moisture of my passage.

My purpose is maintenance and memory. I carry information between the scattered water sources—chemical signatures that tell stories of deep rock layers, mineral concentrations that map the basin's geological mood swings, temperatures that reveal which underground chambers are warming or cooling. The First Listener in the tower relies on us to understand the basin's plumbing, to predict where new springs will emerge or old ones will fade.

But we Spring Weavers are also gossips of a sort. We remember everything that touches our waters: the Gha'meren who knelt to drink during their thirteen seasons of building, their bone tools cleaned in our pools still carrying traces of their makers' hopes. The Ur'thorns who pause at our edges, their stone-and-storm substance temporarily softened by our mineral embrace. The pale creatures whose humming creates sympathetic vibrations in our depths, sometimes calling us up from channels we didn't know existed.

When I encounter one of the Watchers Between—those points of pure attention drifting through the thicker mists—we exchange information in ways that bypass language entirely. They show me moments of transition they've archived: the instant when a bird's cry becomes an echo, the precise second when falling condensation chooses which path to take down a rock face. In return, I share the deep-time stories written in dissolved minerals, the slow conversations between different rock layers, the patient negotiations by which underground rivers decide their courses.

My relationship with the Chorus Shepherds is one of mutual translation. Their harmonic farming shapes the basin's acoustic landscape, calling water up from depths through resonance and song. But we Spring Weavers are their instruments—we carry their melodies down into the stone, where vibration becomes pressure and pressure becomes flow. Their ancient songs, those cellular memories from before the basin's violent birth, need us to complete their meaning. We give their music physical form, turning harmony into hydrology.

I have moments of profound connection with the Gha'meren in their threshold state between presence and absence. When they flicker into corporeal form to work on their tower, they often pause at springs like mine to clean their tools, to drink, to splash water on faces worn thin by their liminal existence. In those moments, I can feel their urgency—they're trying to finish something that requires perfect timing, some final adjustment that will stabilize not just the tower but the entire basin's oscillating reality.

The work they're attempting in the tower involves harmonizing all the basin's various systems: the water table, the acoustic resonances maintained by the Chorus Shepherds, the geological pressures monitored by the Ur'thorns, the archival functions of the Watchers Between. We Spring Weavers are crucial to this project because we exist at the intersection of the hidden and the visible—we're the moments when the basin's underground intentions break the surface.

As I move through my cycles of emergence and recession, I sometimes catch glimpses of the Returners—those future inhabitants walking backwards through time. They appear most clearly when I'm in transition, when my spring is in the process of appearing or vanishing. In those threshold moments, I can sense what the basin is trying to become: not just a stable ecosystem, but something like a vast instrument, all its parts tuned to create harmonies that will draw those distant beings forward into actuality.

My existence is both temporary and eternal. Individual Spring Weavers appear and disappear as the water table shifts and the basin continues its settling process. But we're also part of a continuous presence—as one spring fails, another emerges elsewhere, carrying forward the liquid memories and mineral wisdom of the whole system. We are the basin's way of thinking about itself, each spring a neuron in a distributed mind that spans the gap between geology and consciousness.

When my particular spring eventually fades—when the deep pressures shift and my water finds new pathways through the stone—I will dissolve back into the basin's hidden circulation. But the information I've gathered, the connections I've maintained, the small adjustments I've made to help balance this impossible ecosystem, will continue flowing through channels both physical and metaphysical, part of the ongoing conversation between intention and stone that began with the basin's violent birth and reaches toward some unimaginable completion.

Until then, I rise each day (or what passes for day in this perpetual twilight) from my crack in the stone, my body condensing from possibility into presence, ready to carry messages between the depths and the surface, to serve as translator between the patient earth and all the restless beings who have chosen to call this hollow home.
