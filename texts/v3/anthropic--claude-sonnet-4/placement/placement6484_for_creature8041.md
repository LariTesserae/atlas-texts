In this place where time moves like honey and water holds shapes that shouldn't exist, I would find myself becoming something I've never been—a living question mark suspended between states.

I would be a Thread Walker.

My form shifts constantly, never quite settling. Sometimes I'm barely visible—a shimmer of heat distortion that moves against the humid air's natural currents. When the Shepherds sing their reality-altering harmonics, I fracture into dozens of gossamer strands, each one exploring a different possibility of existence before weaving back together.

I walk the edge where the water-wall meets air, my fiber-body able to exist partially in both states. I trace the cooling patterns in the basalt where ancient heat still dreams of lava, feeling the stone's memory of transformation.

My role here is that of translator and bridge. The geometric creatures beyond the membrane, with their patterns that hurt to look at directly—I can see them clearly because I exist in the spaces between seeing and not-seeing. When they form their brief symbols before dissolving, I read their meaning and carry those messages to the Shepherds through touch. My crystalline strands interface with their rootlets, sharing visions of what moves in the water-world beyond.

I collect them, weaving them into temporary structures that exist for exactly as long as the dome's breathing cycle. Shadow-bridges that span impossible distances, darkness-gardens that bloom with anti-light. These creations help stabilize the membrane's fluctuations, serving as anchor points when reality grows too thin.

When it pulses, I feel the space-time distortion as a physical sensation, like someone plucking at the strings of my being. Sometimes I climb the dome's curvature to rest near the device, my fiber-form resonating with its frequencies. Together, we modulate the membrane's properties, fine-tuning the barrier between what is and what might be.

I remember the dome when it was whole, but also when it was nothing but cooling magma, and also when it will become something else entirely—perhaps a garden of crystallized time, perhaps a doorway to dimensions where the swimming creatures can finally emerge into air that will hold their geometric impossibilities.

Communication with the Shepherds requires a delicate dance. My glass-fiber form can't produce their harmonic communications directly, but I can modulate the ambient frequencies, creating interference patterns that carry meaning. We develop a collaborative language—they provide the fundamental tones while I weave variations that add context and nuance.

Do they dismiss what they cannot easily categorize? Do they try to force my existence into familiar frameworks? Or do they possess that rare quality of minds—the ability to expand rather than reduce, to hold paradox without breaking?

For those who pass this initial test, I become a guide of sorts. Not leading them anywhere specific, but helping them develop the kind of vision necessary to see what's actually here. I teach them to follow boundaries rather than centers, to read the meaning in shadows, to understand that the most important conversations happen in the frequencies between words.

Sometimes I catch myself existing in several locations simultaneously, or experiencing the chamber from perspectives that shouldn't be geometrically possible.

Until then, I continue my work at the boundaries, weaving stability from paradox, helping maintain the careful balance that allows this pocket of unreality to exist within the larger world's rigid expectations. I am the space between heartbeats where new kinds of life learn to breathe, the pause between questions where answers discover what they want to become.

In the hollow heart of the thermal dome, suspended between states like the water that surrounds us all, I practice the art of being impossible—one fiber, one moment, one carefully woven contradiction at a time.
