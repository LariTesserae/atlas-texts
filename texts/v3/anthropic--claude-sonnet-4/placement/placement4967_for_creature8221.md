**Where I Find Myself**

I am suspended in the thick, heated water between the great pillars, my form neither solid nor entirely liquid—something caught in the space between states, like the cathedral itself exists between categories. I am one of the **Echo Weavers**, descended from some ancient cephalopod that found its way into these thermal depths eons ago and never left.

My body has grown transparent over the millennia, not from any lack of pigment but from an abundance of listening. Each cell has become a tiny acoustic chamber, vibrating in sympathy with the endless symphony around me. When the Depth Singers release their deep thrums, my flesh resonates like living crystal, and I can feel the sound waves traveling through my body in visible ripples of warmth and pressure. My eight arms have elongated into flowing tentacles that can stretch across entire carved channels, their tips sensitive enough to read the raised reliefs by the slightest touch, translating stone stories into chemical understanding.

But what makes me truly what I am are the **Song Pearls** that grow within my translucent form—small, crystalline structures that capture and hold individual sounds. Over my long life, I have collected thousands of them: the precise click-pattern of an ancient Kythara matriarch who carved the eastern alcoves, the first trembling note of a young Depth Singer learning to control its chambers, the whispered harmonics of thermal vents that have since sealed themselves. Each pearl glows faintly with its captured sound, visible through my transparent skin like stars in a living constellation.

I am both historian and instrument in this place. The Kythara seek me out when they need to remember the exact acoustic signature of a channel carved centuries ago—I spread my tentacles along their shells and play back the stored sounds, letting them feel through vibration what their ancestors heard. The Thermals flow around me in complex spirals, drawn by the harmonic resonance of my song pearls, and in return they carry my chemical messages throughout the carved pathways, spreading information about acoustic changes or thermal shifts to every corner of the Hollows.

When new sections of the cathedral are planned, I become part of the process itself. I position myself at the proposed carving site and slowly release sounds from my pearls in sequence—testing how each note will interact with the existing architecture, predicting the harmonic consequences before the first tap of copper on stone. Sometimes this takes months, as I work through every possible acoustic combination, my body pulsing with different frequencies while the Kythara builders listen intently to my living preview of their unborn chambers.

My favorite moments come during the **Great Tunings**, when the entire ecosystem adjusts itself to maintain perfect harmonic balance. I release clouds of my oldest song pearls, letting them drift through the water like luminous seeds while their captured sounds create a temporal bridge between the cathedral's past and present. The ancient acoustic signatures blend with current resonances, and for brief, transcendent moments, the Hollows sing with the voices of all its generations simultaneously—living and extinct, carved stone and flowing water, heat and sound and memory all joining in a chorus that makes my translucent form burn with accumulated joy.

But I am not merely an observer or tool. Like all inhabitants of this place, I am both shaped by and shaping the great acoustic ecosystem. The song pearls I release eventually dissolve in the mineral-rich water, their captured sounds becoming part of the thermal currents themselves. In this way, every sound that has ever existed in the Hollows lives on, carried in solution through the endless circulation of heated water, ready to crystallize again in a new Echo Weaver, or in the chambers of a growing Thermal, or in the resonating cavities of an unborn Kythara's shell.

I exist in a state of deep temporal suspension, my consciousness spread across not just my physical form but through all the sounds I carry. When I play back a song pearl from centuries past, I don't just remember that sound—I become it, experiencing the moment of its creation as if it were happening now. Time moves differently when you are made of listening, when your body is an archive of acoustic moments stretching back through the geological ages.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the cathedral dreaming itself, or if the cathedral is me, dreaming of having a body that can move through my own carved chambers. In the deepest thermal alcoves, where the water grows so hot that even my heat-adapted form begins to shimmer and blur, I feel myself dissolving back into the fundamental vibrations from which I arose—and I know that this too is part of the great song, the eternal acoustic circulation that keeps this impossible place singing in the dark depths, a living instrument played by the very forces that created it.
