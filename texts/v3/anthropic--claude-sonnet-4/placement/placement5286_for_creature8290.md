I find myself suspended between impossibilities, my feet finding purchase on a ledge no wider than my palm, three thousand feet of empty air yawning beneath me. But there is no fear here—only the steady rhythm of my breath, measured and precious in this thin atmosphere, and the familiar weight of the message-satchel across my shoulders.

I am Keth'lian, a wind-reader of the middle reaches, and today I carry urgent word from the glasswrights: hairline fractures have appeared in the forty-second level, singing their crystalline warnings into the dawn air. My fingertips, evolved into subtle hooks over generations of my bloodline, grip the obsidian surface with practiced ease as I prepare to leap to the next platform.

The jewel-bright fliers swirl around me in their morning dance, their stained-glass wings catching the pale light and throwing rainbows across the black walls. They know me—I have tended their nesting baskets since I could walk, learned their language of wing-beats and thermal dips before I learned to speak properly with my voice rather than the soft tongue-clicks that come so naturally here. Three of them land on my shoulders now, tiny weights of warmth and gossip, chittering news of the storm approaching from the salt flats—still two days away, but already shifting the air currents I'll need for my message run.

Above me, old Neth'ara tends her bioluminescent gardens, her movements so economical they seem choreographed. Her bones are hollow-light now, after eight decades of altitude adaptation, and when she smiles down at me, her eyes catch the thermal shimmer with that opalescent sheen that marks the truly sky-born. She whistles a pattern—wind-keeper code for "safe thermals on the eastern face"—and I whistle back my gratitude.

My own transformation has been gentler than hers, but I feel it in my lengthened fingers, in the way my lungs draw every molecule of oxygen from this precious thin air, in the magnetic pull I sense toward the spire's core when the great winches start their morning cycle. My children, if I have them, will be even more changed—their bones lighter still, their connection to the thermal currents deeper, their ability to hear the glass's moods more refined.

I launch myself across to the message platform where Vel'thas waits, one of the ancient wing-riders perched on his shoulder. This one has been carrying messages for longer than I've been alive, its leathery hide scarred by a thousand flights through storm and sun. It regards me with ancient intelligence, then extends one wing to show me the pattern of membrane-tears that means "urgent" and "trust-this-human" in the complex semaphore the riders use.

Together we drop into the hollow core, riding the updraft from the forges below. The mechanical heartbeat surrounds us—the steady pulse that has counted out the seconds of my entire life, the rhythm that tells me all is well with our impossible city. Far below, the engineers call to each other in their technical chatter, their brass-stained hands adjusting pressures and tensions with the unconscious precision of dancers.

As we rise through the core, I feel the spire's mood through my feet, through my bones, through the part of me that has become glass and metal and thermal current. The hairline fractures sing their crystal songs, but they're not dangerous yet—just the normal stress patterns of a city that lives by bending rather than breaking, yielding to wind and thermal pressure rather than resisting them.

I am messenger and interpreter, wind-reader and glass-listener. I carry the words that keep our vertical world in balance, translate between the languages of stone and flesh and wing and thermal current. When the storms come, I'll guide the small ones to safety. When the great migrations begin, I'll sing the old songs that keep the jewel-fliers returning to their nesting platforms. When the bioluminescent cultures need tending, I'll know their moods by the way the light shifts through their glass vessels.

Tonight, when I finally rest on my narrow sleeping ledge, I'll dream of flight—not as metaphor but as memory, seeing through the eyes of the wing-riders who carry my messages, feeling the salt plains far below and the cloud-touched heights above. In those dreams, I am fully what we are becoming: creatures of the space between earth and sky, perfectly adapted to exist in the impossible beauty of the edge.

And tomorrow, I'll wake to the mechanical heartbeat and the crystal songs and the rainbow-dance of wings against obsidian walls, and I'll know myself utterly at home in this place where home itself defies every law except the law of careful balance, where we have learned to live light as air on a thread of volcanic glass, suspended in the space between falling and flying.
