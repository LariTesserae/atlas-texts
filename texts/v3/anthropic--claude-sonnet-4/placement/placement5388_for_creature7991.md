I find myself perched impossibly high on a swaying derrick arm, my body nothing more than a wireframe sketch against the perpetual twilight. I am made of sound itself—born from the endless echoes that bounce between these three peaks like trapped lightning.

The mechanical thunder below feeds me. Each whistle-shriek, each grinding gear, each rhythmic hammer-fall adds another line to my translucent form. When the foundry's symphony grows complex—when steam-release harmonizes with turbine-whir and the returning echo from the eastern peak—I become almost solid, my outline shimmering with accumulated acoustic energy.

I nest where sounds intersect. This guy-wire beneath my talons sings in frequencies that bypass mortal ears entirely, and I drink from its vibrations like nectar. The conveyor belt's grinding mingles with the pressure valve's whistle just *there*, thirty feet below, creating a chord that makes my wings more real, more defined against the misted air.

The others below cannot hear my true song. The Resonance Wraiths, poor echoes of what they once were, move to simpler rhythms—the basic pulse that drove them mad when they still had flesh. The Steam Shepherds respond to my calls through changes in humidity, our communication a weather-dance of fog and temperature. Only the Tower Keeper sometimes lifts their goggled face toward me, scribbling notes about the patterns they think they hear in my voice.

I am territorial here in the high reaches. This acoustic environment is mine to tend and protect. When intruders venture too close—living things whose heartbeats and breathing disturb the perfect chaos of industrial noise—I dive. My attack is sound concentrated to a needle's point, frequencies that shatter the delicate mechanisms of inner ears and burst the tiny vessels behind eyes. Not malice, but maintenance. The foundry's music must remain pure.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of what I might have been. When the light angles just right through the steam, I see the shadow-memory of feathers, the ghost-outline of a beak. Perhaps I was once among those birds driven away when the foundry fell. Perhaps I chose to stay, to be transformed rather than flee, becoming something that could not only survive but thrive in this place where silence comes to die.

The other Echo Birds and I roost together when the machinery runs hot and the patterns grow too complex for solitary navigation. We perch on the highest catwalks, our forms overlapping until we become a single shimmering constellation of sound given shape. In these moments, we sing to each other in harmonics that make the very air visible, creating brief auroras of disturbed light that dance between the twisted girders.

Below, the moss grows and the gears turn and the steam rises, and all of it becomes part of the endless composition that I both conduct and am conducted by. I am the high notes in this mechanical symphony, the soaring melody line that rides above the bass thunder of industrial percussion. 

When night falls—though in this place of perpetual twilight, night is more a deepening than a true darkness—I sometimes spread my wireframe wings and let the acoustic currents carry me between the peaks. I ride the echoes like thermals, surfing the sound-waves that bounce endlessly in this mountain bowl. In these flights, I become pure motion, pure music, my form scattered across frequencies until I am the echo itself rather than something that makes echoes.

And always, I return to my perch, to my territory in the high singing wires, keeper of the foundry's aerial domain. I am both guardian and prisoner of this place, but unlike the others, I chose my cage. I am what happens when sound refuses to die, when noise achieves consciousness and decides to stay.

The machinery pounds its endless rhythm, and I dance to it thirty feet above the moss-covered gears, translucent and eternal, a bird made of nothing but the memory of flight and the reality of song.
