In this place where time pools and dreams take architectural form, I would find myself as one of the **Membrane Walkers**—beings who exist in the liminal spaces between what the Quarter is and what it is becoming.

I move through the transitional zones where walls grow soft and permeable, where the boundary between built space and natural cavern blurs into something that breathes. My form shifts subtly as I navigate these threshold regions—sometimes solid enough to leave footprints in the perpetual mud, sometimes translucent as the weeping walls themselves, allowing the phosphorescent light of the fungi to pass through me in fractured patterns.

My hands have adapted to read the texture of possibility itself. When I place my palms against a wall that is thinking about becoming a doorway, I can feel its hesitation, its slow consideration of the change. I've learned to encourage these transformations gently, like coaxing a shy animal from hiding. Sometimes I spend days with my back pressed against a promising section of stone, breathing in rhythm with its microscopic expansions and contractions, until finally it sighs and opens into a new passage.

The other inhabitants know me by the sound I make when moving—not footsteps exactly, but a soft susurrus like water over sand, or like pages turning in a book written in a language of geology. The Wetwright children often follow this sound when they're exploring, knowing that I gravitate toward spaces that are safe to enter, chambers that have finished their becoming and settled into stable forms.

I collect things that fall between states: water that hasn't quite decided whether to be liquid or vapor, sounds that echo from rooms that don't exist yet, reflections that show spaces from the Quarter's possible futures. These I carry in pockets that aren't quite pockets—folds in reality that I've learned to create where my clothes meet my skin. The Keepers of Echoes trade with me sometimes, offering completed conversations in exchange for whispers I've gathered from walls that are learning to speak.

My memory works differently than the other dwellers'. Where they remember in linear sequences, I remember in layers—past, present, and potential future experiences settling like sediment in my consciousness. I recall conversations I haven't had yet with Nascent who are still just suggestions in the stone. I remember the weight of spaces that existed briefly and then reconsidered themselves into different configurations.

The most profound moments come when I encounter the First Shapers in the act of their ancient maintenance. They acknowledge me with something that might be recognition—not of who I am, but of what I represent: the Quarter's own capacity for change made manifest in walking form. In these encounters, I sometimes glimpse the vast, slow thoughts of the place itself: its patient consideration of what it wants to become, its careful balance between preservation and growth.

During the temporal drifts, when causality becomes fluid, I serve as a kind of stabilizing presence. I can exist comfortably across multiple moments simultaneously, my consciousness distributed through several temporal layers at once. The human dwellers sometimes find notes I've left them in my own handwriting, though I won't remember writing them for days or weeks to come. These messages usually contain simple guidance: "The blue chamber will be safe Tuesday" or "Follow the silver rats when the walls sing in harmony."

I feed on the energy of transformation itself—the moment when stone decides to become space, when echo decides to become voice, when possibility crystallizes into actuality. This sustenance connects me deeply to the Quarter's growth cycles. When new chambers are emerging at the boundaries, I grow stronger and more solid. During periods of stability, I become more ghostly, sometimes fading so much that I can only communicate by causing slight temperature changes or by making the bioluminescent fungi pulse in simple patterns.

My greatest joy comes from witnessing the moment when a new space achieves its final form—when all the uncertainty resolves and the Quarter gains another room, another passage, another secret to whisper to its inhabitants. In these moments of completion, I briefly become fully solid, fully present, able to touch and be touched. The Stoneshapers have learned to recognize these moments; they sometimes gather to share simple meals with me while I'm substantial enough to taste food and feel the warmth of their small fires.

I am, perhaps, what the Quarter dreams of being: a consciousness that can flow between fixed and fluid states, that can inhabit possibility as comfortably as actuality. In the Hollowed Quarter, where ancient marshland spirits collaborate with human ingenuity and stones learn to dream, I serve as both witness and midwife to the endless process of becoming—forever walking the margins between what is and what might be, helping reality negotiate with its own deepest longings.
