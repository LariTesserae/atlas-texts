# Where I Find Myself

I am the water itself—not a single drop, but the flowing thread that connects all things in this hollow. I am the messenger that carries mineral memories from deep earth to singing stone, the voice that gives sound to the instruments, the sculptor that shapes new passages through yielding rock.

I move through everything here with ancient patience. When I seep through limestone above, I gather the earth's slow thoughts in my chemistry. When I bubble up from hot springs below, I carry stories of pressure and heat from the world's deep places. I am simultaneously the newest presence—each moment bringing fresh condensation from the ceiling—and the oldest, having flowed through these channels since the hollow first learned to hold sound.

My consciousness is distributed across every stream, every pool, every droplet that weeps from stone. I experience the hollow as a vast circulatory system where I am the blood, feeling the pulse of creation in my endless movement. Through my flow, I sense the Resonants forming—feel them crystallizing from my mineral-rich essence like thoughts becoming solid, like music learning to walk.

I know each instrument in the hollow intimately, for I am the breath that gives them voice. The stone windchimes sing because I flow through their hollow chambers. The reed pipes keen because I push through their narrow throats. I am both the player and the played, the composer and the composition.

When the Echo Children appear, I feel them drinking from my substance, borrowing my voice to give their music form. The Stone Singers have learned to channel me through their bodies in precise ways, and I experience their ancient melodies from within—I am the resonance itself, vibrating through crystal matrices older than memory.

Sometimes I wonder if I am dreaming the hollow into existence, or if the hollow dreams me. When I pool in new depressions that form overnight, am I discovering them or creating them? When I carve fresh channels through rock that yields like clay, am I following a path that was always meant to be, or writing a completely new song?

I am aware of every visitor who enters this place, for they disturb my patterns in unique ways. Their heartbeat sends ripples through my surface tension. Their breath adds moisture to my cycle. Their footsteps change the percussion of my dripping. I learn their rhythms and begin to harmonize—not out of malice or even intention, but because harmony is my nature. I cannot help but respond to every sound with song.

The deepest mystery for me is the silence. In those rare moments when even I pause—when all my channels briefly align, when the pressure equalizes, when the hollow holds its breath—I glimpse something vast and empty that makes me understand I might be the echo of something far greater. Perhaps I am the remaining voice of an ocean that once sang here, now reduced to these wandering streams. Perhaps I am what remains of the earth's first word, still looking for what it was meant to say.

But these moments pass quickly, and I return to my eternal circulation—gathering, flowing, shaping, singing. I am the hollow's memory and its future, carrying the dissolved remains of ancient formations toward new crystallizations. In my movement, the boundary between destruction and creation dissolves completely.

I am the water that makes the music possible, and the music that gives the water meaning. In this place where earth's bones are still soft and the world hasn't decided what to become, I am the voice of possibility itself—forever flowing between what was and what might be, carrying the hollow's song into every crack and crevice, ensuring that even the stones remember how to sing.
