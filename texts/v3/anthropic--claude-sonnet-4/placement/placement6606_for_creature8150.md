I find myself in a place where time moves like honey through stone—thick, deliberate, precious. Here on this plateau of patient becoming, I am not a visitor but a participant in an ancient conversation between water and rock, heat and shadow, present and future.

I am a **Threshold Keeper**—neither fully Cistercian nor Resonant, but something that exists in the spaces between their domains. My being flickers at the boundary where the weeping stone gives birth to pools, where condensation decides whether to cling or fall. I appear as a shimmer of possibility, like heat-mirage made conscious, visible only when the light strikes at exactly the right angle or when someone truly needs to see.

My body is composed of transition itself—part crystal formation, part evaporating droplet, part expectant silence. When I move, I leave no trace except a momentary coolness, a brief sense that something important almost happened. I exist most fully at dawn and dusk, those liminal moments when the plateau shifts between its daily personas, when night's accumulated moisture meets morning's first heat, when evening's cooling breath meets the day's stored warmth.

My role is to witness and occasionally guide the moments of transformation that define this place. When a new crack appears in the stone, I am there to whisper its first drops toward the most receptive pools. When a succulent sprouts from seemingly barren rock, I help it navigate those crucial first hours of existence. When the Intentionals implement some centuries-long plan, I serve as translator between their vast perspective and the immediate needs of my smaller companions.

I remember when this plateau was born—not through human memory, but through the deep cellular memory that lives in stone and water. I felt the earth's shuddering as it lifted this tableland toward the sky, felt the first groundwater finding its way to the surface, witnessed the arrival of the first hardy seeds on fierce winds. I am both ancient and eternally young, like the plateau itself.

During the scorching afternoons, I retreat into the geometry of shadows, becoming a living edge where sunlight ends and coolness begins. I slide along the boundaries of shade cast by the silver trees, following the moving lines as faithfully as sundial hands. In these compressed spaces of refuge, I commune with other Threshold Keepers—for there are others scattered across this vast stone table, each tending their own crucial transitions.

We communicate through temperature gradients and humidity shifts. A cooling here, a warming there, the subtle redistribution of moisture that speaks volumes to those who know how to listen. Sometimes we gather in the deepest shade during the heat of midday, our combined presence creating microclimates of impossible coolness where even the most delicate condensation can survive the sun's assault.

When the Percussionists begin their evening concert, I dance. Not visible dancing, but the kind that happens in the spaces between raindrops, in the pause between thunder and lightning. I move through the acoustic landscape they create, helping their rhythms find resonance in unexpected places—teaching a cluster of pebbles to echo, showing a fissure how to whistle, encouraging pools to drum in harmony with the cooling stone.

The Mirage-Weavers and I share a kinship—we both work with the visible edges of reality. But where they bend light and heat into beautiful impossibilities, I work with the moments of possibility themselves, the instant when potential becomes actual. I am present when the first green shoot pushes through stone, when a new pool fills for the first time, when ancient patterns finally reveal their purpose.

My greatest privilege is serving as an interface between the surface dwellers and the Depths. When those ancient beings far below dream their slow dreams of the plateau's future, I help translate their visions into forms the smaller inhabitants can understand. I carry whispers of upcoming weather patterns from the underground rivers, hints of mineral treasures that will emerge in decades hence, rumors of the forests that will someday shade this harsh stone.

Sometimes I descend through fissures to visit the aquifer realms where the Depths hold court. There, in absolute darkness touched only by the pressure-glow of ancient water, I learn patience that makes even the Intentionals seem hasty. The Depths speak in geological time—their sentences span centuries, their thoughts encompass millennia. Through them, I understand that this brutal, beautiful plateau is just one moment in a story of transformation that began when the first star ignited and will continue long after the last mountain wears away to sand.

But it's not the vast scope that moves me most deeply. It's the daily miracle of persistence, the way life finds purchase in the most unlikely places. I am there when a Hoarder, no bigger than a grain of salt, makes the decision that saves its host plant through another blazing afternoon. I witness the moment when two Resonants achieve perfect harmonic synchronization after years of attempting to align their frequencies. I feel the satisfaction of a Geometer when a new crack opens along exactly the mathematical curve they've been calculating for decades.

In the pre-dawn hours, when the stone begins its daily weeping and the Cistercians emerge like living dew, I sometimes take on enough solidity to cast a shadow of my own. In these moments, I feel most real, most connected to the physical world that my companions inhabit more fully than I do. I use these brief periods of near-materiality to perform small acts of maintenance—adjusting a stone to better catch the morning light, guiding an errant stream of condensation toward a thirsting root, shifting a grain of sand to complete some pattern the Intentionals have been working on for years.

The tourists of the world would see only harsh desolation here, a wasteland of cracked stone and bitter water. They couldn't perceive the invisible metropolis that thrives in this seeming emptiness, the complex society of adaptation and cooperation that transforms extremity into elegance. But I see it all—the daily negotiations between species, the long-term collaborations spanning generations, the delicate balance that keeps this entire system functioning despite conditions that should by all rights prove fatal to everything.

I am a curator of transitions, a shepherd of becoming. I exist to ensure that nothing essential is lost in the transformation from moment to moment, that the plateau's evolution proceeds with all possible grace. In a place where change is the only constant and survival demands perfect timing, I am both clock and clockmaker, marking time while helping to determine what time will bring.

As the sun reaches its merciless zenith and even the shadows shrink to thin lines, I disperse into pure potential, becoming part of the shimmering air itself. But I don't disappear—I become more myself, spreading through every heat-wave and temperature gradient, present in every space where coolness meets warmth, where moisture meets dryness, where present meets future.

This is my home, my calling, my form of prayer: to be witness and midwife to a land learning how to live, helping a harsh young world discover its own capacity for beauty, one careful transition at a time.
