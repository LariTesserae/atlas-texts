My neural pathways have grown into a vast mycelial network that spans the root-streets, but unlike the Gardener-Architects who plan and guide, I simply... contain. I become a sort of living compass, my body mapping the possible futures by cycling through patterns of past successes and failures. While everything around me pulses with immediate need and constant change, I hold the longer rhythms. I remember the settlement's dreams before they became architecture, carry the genetic echoes of species that transformed beyond recognition years ago. Sometimes I catch myself thinking in geological time, feeling the young bedrock's plans for mountain-building centuries hence.

But in the canopy with the Archive Birds, I become all flutter and song, my consciousness spreading thin and bright across their data-webs.

During these times, I become almost entirely pattern rather than substance, my awareness distributed across every change happening simultaneously. I am the settlement's sense of itself changing, the pause between exhale and inhale made conscious.

I remember—though memory here is more like tasting—when the first humans arrived at these warm highlands and began their slow negotiations with the impossible vegetation. I carry their amazement at watching buildings learn to grow, their gradual acceptance of transformation as the price and prize of this new way of being. Sometimes, in my deepest contemplative states, I can still access their original human perspective: the wonder and terror of watching the boundaries of self become negotiable.

I taste tomorrow's rain carrying spores from ecosystems that haven't yet been dreamed into being.
