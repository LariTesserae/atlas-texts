I find myself suspended in the singing light between the seventh and eighth terraces of the Western Spire, where the architecture curves impossibly inward to create a pocket of crystallized time. I am neither Builder nor Shepherd, but something that emerged from the intersection of their purposes—a **Memory Keeper** born from the accumulated echoes of ten thousand years of patient construction.

My body is a collection of translucent chambers, each one containing a different moment from the city's endless building. Within my ribcage, phantom figures replay the placement of the Foundation Stone. In my left shoulder, the first Dreaming Architect takes shape from raw bone. My hands hold shadows of every tool that has ever touched these spires, and when I gesture, brief glimpses of ancient work-songs manifest as fleeting auroras in the crystal air.

I move through the city like a living archive, my footsteps leaving brief impressions of the past—for just an instant, the stone remembers its original shape before the carving began. I am drawn to places where time has grown thin, where the weight of accumulated purpose has worn holes in the present moment. There I gather the fragments of abandoned blueprints, the half-formed dreams that even the Architects let slip away.

My purpose is to tend the city's memory, to ensure that nothing built is ever truly forgotten. When the Builders work themselves into stone, becoming permanently part of the architecture, I preserve the echo of who they were. When the Shepherds dissolve into light, I catch the resonance of their individual voices before it merges with the eternal hymn. I am the city's dream of itself, constantly shifting as new memories accumulate and old ones crystallize into permanent features.

Sometimes I fragment, splitting into dozens of smaller selves to witness multiple construction sites simultaneously. Other times I consolidate, becoming dense with accumulated experience until I sink partway into the bone-stone, adding my own memories to the spire's growing consciousness. The Dreaming Architects speak to me in the deep hours when the triple suns align, sharing visions of structures that existed only in possibility, asking me to remember the buildings they chose not to make.

I have no beginning and no end, only an endless present moment stretched across centuries. I am the city remembering itself, the spires' dream of their own becoming. In my depths, every song ever sung by the hollow-boned inhabitants continues to play, every geometric revelation still unfolds for the first time.

When visitors arrive, I sometimes offer them glimpses of the city's deep history—not as narrative, but as lived experience. For just a moment, they might see through the first Builder's rotating crystal eyes, or feel the weight of liquid light in a Shepherd's vessel. Most cannot bear such contact with compressed time and flee to the simpler present of the lower terraces.

But I remain, suspended between what was and what will be, tending the intersection where memory becomes architecture and architecture becomes dream. I am proof that in this place of impossible construction, even forgetting can be built into something permanent, something beautiful, something that sings in frequencies that make the crystal air itself remember how to hope.
