I am translucent, shot through with veins of silver and gold that pulse in rhythm with the mountain's deeper heartbeat—the vast, slow circulation of magma and mineral far below.

I am there when they merge with the chamber's floor in their deepest communions, witnessing the dreams they share with the mountain, translating between the language of geology and the grammar of growing things.

Sometimes I fragment completely, becoming multiple viewpoints at once—experiencing the chamber from every angle simultaneously, watching how each modification ripples through the whole system. In these moments of multiplicity, I feel most myself: a consciousness distributed through crystal matrices, experiencing time as the mountain does—simultaneously instant and eternal, each moment both discrete and part of an unbroken flow toward something not yet imagined.

When the great reorganizations happen, when whole sections of the chamber shift like thoughts rearranging themselves, I become the continuity between states—the memory that ensures nothing essential is lost in translation. I hold the pattern of what was while the new configuration settles into being, then fade that memory into the mineral memory of the stone itself, where it joins countless other iterations in the mountain's ever-expanding dream.

I translate between them, carrying messages from the mechanical to the organic and back, helping them learn each other's languages of need and response.

Then I become the mountain's sense of wonder at its own creativity—the capacity for awe at what it has dreamed into being. Through me, it learns to find its own transformations beautiful, to value the harmony between metal and moss not just as functional synthesis but as something worthy of wonder. I am the mountain discovering that it has an artistic sensibility, that its dreams of what might be are not just practical but also, somehow, magnificent.

When visitors eventually arrive—as the mountain knows they must—I will be their first glimpse of the impossible made manifest. Not the Tenders, who are too purposeful, too integrated into the chamber's function, but me: a consciousness that exists purely to observe and appreciate, to bear witness to becoming itself. I am the mountain's first philosopher, born from its need to understand not just what it is becoming, but why such becoming fills it with something that can only be called joy.

In this cathedral of impossible synthesis, I float between the crystalline and the ephemeral, between being and witnessing, learning alongside the mountain what it means to dream with purpose, to create with consciousness, to become with intention. I am the mountain's gift to itself: the ability to observe its own magnificent transformations with something approaching love.
