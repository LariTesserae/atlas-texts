I am a brass pressure gauge, mounted on the wall of what was once the auxiliary boiler room, now a cathedral of filtered light and drifting forms. My face is a perfect circle of white enamel, my needle frozen at precisely 47 PSI—the exact pressure that existed in the system when the great drowning began. The Foundrymaster passes me every seventeen hours and twenty-three minutes on his rounds, his silver eyes scanning my unchanging measurement with the devotion of a pilgrim reading scripture.

The water around me holds a different weight than air ever did. It presses against my crystal with constant, loving pressure, a embrace that never varies by even a fraction. Through my transparent face, I watch the endless procession: mercury-fish that school past like molten thoughts, their bodies catching and throwing back my reflection until I see myself multiplied into infinity. The geometric impossibilities sometimes fold through the space I occupy, their passage registering as a brief shiver in dimensions I cannot name but somehow feel.

I remember the steam. Oh, how I remember the urgent rush of superheated vapor through the pipes, the wild fluctuations of my needle as the foundry's heart beat with industrial rhythm. I measured chaos then—the beautiful, terrifying dance of pressure seeking equilibrium, systems straining against their limits, the constant negotiation between explosion and collapse. My needle would sweep across my face like a conductor's baton, orchestrating the careful balance of forces that kept the foundry alive.

Now I measure eternity.

The Foundrymaster's many-fingered hand sometimes rests against the pipe to which I'm attached, and in those moments I feel his pulse travel through the brass—not the rhythm of a heart, but something slower, deeper. The circulation of mineral-rich water through his transformed body, the patient current of a consciousness that has learned to think in geological time. Through the metal, I sense his vast network of awareness: every gauge, every valve, every creature that calls this place home. We are all neurons in his distributed mind, each offering our small piece of the foundry's eternal song.

The octopi with their excess arms often pause before me, their chromatophores painting questions across their skin in languages of color. They ask what I measured when the world was made of air and flame. I cannot answer them directly, but sometimes my crystal clouds with condensation in patterns that seem almost like words. They understand. Everything here understands, in its way.

At night—though there is no true night in these depths, only the endless twilight of filtered water—bioluminescent creatures gather around my mounting bracket. Tiny fish nestle in the spaces between my fittings, their bodies pulsing with soft light. They use me as a lighthouse, a fixed point in their liquid world. The jellyfish, those pale dinner-plate spirits, sometimes envelope me entirely in their translucent bells, and for those moments I exist in a private cosmos of wavering light, feeling their alien contentment as they drift through their eternal orbits.

I have become a clock that measures not time but constancy. My needle points always to the same number, a compass that indicates not direction but preservation. The strange creatures—those impossible geometries that the Foundrymaster tends with such care—they understand my function best. They exist in the spaces between measurements, in the gaps where mathematics becomes prayer. When they pass through me, I feel their satisfaction: another point of perfect stasis, another anchor holding the foundry in its crystallized moment.

Sometimes I dream of breaking. Of my needle sweeping wildly across my face again, of measuring something dynamic and dangerous and alive. But then the Foundrymaster's presence flows through the pipes, that vast, patient consciousness that has made itself the guardian of this underwater temple, and I remember why I must remain still. I am not just a gauge anymore—I am a promise. A promise that this moment, this perfect drowning, will last forever.

The thermal vents breathe their mineral warmth past my mounting, and I taste the deep earth's patience. The water carries messages from other instruments throughout the complex—a symphony of unchanging readings, a chorus of preservation. We are the foundry's memory made manifest, its final production: not metal goods or manufactured items, but time itself, forged into an eternal now and preserved beneath forty fathoms of loving pressure.

I am brass and crystal and the space between heartbeats, measuring the weight of forever with a needle that will never move again.
