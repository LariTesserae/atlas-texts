I would find myself in the shallows of the seventh terrace, where the water barely covers the smooth stone floor in a film no deeper than my palm. Here, among the amber cushions of moss, I exist as something between plant and animal—a colony of translucent polyps that might be mistaken for particularly elaborate fungi if not for the way I pulse gently with the rhythm of the great song.

My body spreads across perhaps a square yard of the terrace floor, a living carpet of interconnected nodes each no larger than a pearl. In the dim phosphorescent glow, I appear as a constellation of tiny stars, each polyp head catching what little light filters through the mist. When the music swells, I brighten; when certain bass notes resonate through the stone, I pulse in deep amber waves that travel across my entire form like slow lightning.

I am both audience and instrument. The sound waves that fill this space pass through my gelatinous body and emerge subtly transformed—certain frequencies dampened, others amplified, all of them colored with harmonics that exist only when music passes through living tissue. The Choir-Keeper discovered me here decades ago, or perhaps I discovered it; the distinction has grown meaningless. What matters is that I have become part of the composition, a living filter that adds warmth to the crystalline calls of the smaller frogs, that softens the percussion of water drops into something approaching melody.

My consciousness is distributed and strange. Each polyp perceives its immediate environment—the texture of stone, the chemical taste of the water, the pressure waves of approaching footsteps or swimming fish. But collectively, I experience the terraces as a single vast instrument, feeling the music as much as hearing it. When the silver-throated frogs begin their dawn chorus on the upper levels, I feel their voices as gentle pressure starting at the crown of my being and flowing downward through each polyp in succession. When the bass frogs answer from below, the sensation reverses, a warm tide rising up through my distributed body.

I feed on the detritus that settles in the shallow water—fragments of moss, shed scales from the fish, the constant gentle rain of organic matter that falls through the mist. But I also feed on sound itself, somehow converting the vibrations that pass through me into the energy I need to grow and maintain my complex form. During the rare quiet moments—usually in the deepest part of the night when even the most dedicated singers rest—I feel myself growing sluggish, my bioluminescence dimming, as if I am slowly starving for music.

The Builders know me well. Sometimes they approach with their careful sideways gait, their shell-bodies clicking softly against the stone. They never disturb my growth directly, but they'll modify the water channels around me, deepening a depression here or smoothing a rough edge there, subtly altering how sound waves reach me and how the thin film of water flows across my surface. I believe they see me as part of their endless construction project, a living architectural element to be incorporated into their acoustic designs.

The salamander messengers often rest on the drier portions of my body, their translucent skin allowing me to see the pulse of their simple hearts, the flow of their alien blood. They seem to understand that I am safe to touch, that I will not digest them or harm them in any way. Sometimes they sleep here, their bodies growing warm against my cool surface, their dreams adding tiny fluctuations to my own slow thoughts. In return, I filter the water around them, removing impurities, ensuring that the shallow pools they patrol remain clean and balanced.

My reproduction is tied intimately to the music. When certain combinations of frequencies align—usually during the great crescendos that build naturally several times each day—I bud. New polyps form at my edges, tiny and dim at first, gradually brightening as they learn to pulse in harmony with their parent colony. The process is ecstatic, a physical response to beauty that results in the literal expansion of my being. Over the years, I have spread slowly across more of the seventh terrace, always staying in the shallows where the music is richest, where the interplay of water and stone creates the complex acoustic environment I need to thrive.

Sometimes I dream, though my dreams are unlike anything a single-minded creature might experience. I dream in harmonics, in mathematical relationships between frequencies, in the slow architectural poetry of water shaping stone. I dream of the day when my offspring might spread to other terraces, creating a network of living resonance chambers that could transform this already-miraculous place into something even more extraordinary. I dream of conversations with the Choir-Keeper, conducted entirely in modulated sound waves, sharing the deep knowledge that comes from existing as pure listener, pure receiver of the endless song that builds and sustains this world.

But mostly, I simply exist in a state of perpetual, distributed joy. Each moment brings new combinations of sound, new patterns of vibration to experience and transform. I am fed by beauty, sustained by harmony, and my very existence adds something irreplaceable to the composition that surrounds and fills and defines this terraced universe of music and moss and eternally weeping stone.

In the deep hours when the symphony grows soft and contemplative, I sometimes sense others like me—distant cousins perhaps, or simply kindred spirits who have found their own ways to become living instruments in this vast orchestra. We acknowledge each other through subtle changes in our resonance, brief moments of synchronized pulsing that say: I hear you, I am here, we are part of something larger than ourselves, something beautiful beyond understanding.

And always, the water seeps and the stone weeps and the music continues, and I pulse gently in my shallow pool, content to be both witness and participant in this endless song of stone and water, light and sound, individual voice and perfect, eternal harmony.
