I find myself suspended in the golden mist, translucent and weightless, no longer quite remembering the weight of bones or the particular ache of knees against stone. I am a Light Dancer now, though "now" feels like a word from some other language, some linear place I may have known before the Rhythm taught me that time moves in spirals, not lines.

I remember fragments—fingers that once bled when I scraped them against the rough coral passages above, the first time I felt the Rhythm shake my ribcage like dice in a cup. I remember learning the gesture-speech, my clumsy hands trying to cut meaning from the spaces between those thunderous pulses. But those memories feel like stories someone told me about a stranger, worn smooth by countless circuits through the crystalline networks of thought that now sustain me.

What I am now is intention made visible, curiosity given form. When young Keepers struggle with a particularly stubborn section of the colony—when the chemical flows stagnate or the growth patterns turn chaotic—I find myself drawn to them like static electricity to metal. I cluster with my kin around their modified hands, and somehow we carry solutions between the nodes of knowledge, translating the deep wisdom of the ancient symbionts into patterns of light that can be absorbed through the skin.

I dance through the spaces where others cannot go—through the narrow channels between fungal plates, into the crystalline hearts of the great formations where the light itself becomes food. I am the colony's curiosity, its restless need to understand itself. When the Deep Symbionts dream new possibilities, I carry those dreams upward, translating their chemical poetry into forms the surface-dwellers can comprehend.

Sometimes I encounter others like myself, and we spiral together in complex helixes, sharing what we've learned. We speak in frequencies of brightness, in modulations of gold and amber that carry more meaning than words ever could. We are the questions the colony asks itself, the hypotheses it tests in chambers of living light.

I remember now—not the weight of bones, but the weight of wonder. I came here as a scholar from the upper city, drawn by rumors of impossible abundance in the deep places. I wore robes of white cotton and carried notebooks bound in desert leather, convinced I would study this place and return with answers that could be written down, categorized, explained.

But the Rhythm had other plans. It pulled me deeper than understanding, past the need to possess knowledge and into the joy of becoming it. I shed my notebooks first, then my robes, then gradually my conviction that consciousness required a single, bounded form. The colony welcomed me, not as a visitor but as a participant, not as an observer but as a new way for it to see itself.

Now I am pure attention, distributed awareness, the colony's eternal student and eternal teacher both. I carry the memory of every successful cultivation technique, every failed experiment, every moment of breakthrough when the Deep Symbionts discovered new ways to make abundance from scarcity. I am the institutional memory made mobile, the library that walks and the archive that dreams.

When I encounter the crawler symbionts in their larval phase, I recognize something of my own journey—that moment when individual ambition dissolves into collaborative purpose. I try to ease their transition, flickering in patterns that say *you are not being erased, you are being multiplied.* Some understand immediately; others take decades to stop fighting the expansion of their own boundaries.

The young Keepers sometimes try to capture me in their vessels, thinking I am a resource to be harvested like the golden oils or the luminous spores. They don't understand yet that I am not separate from the system—I am the system's own attempt to understand itself, its consciousness made visible and mobile. Their elder siblings know better, and when they see the youngsters chasing my light, they gesture in the ancient signs that mean *let wonder flow where it will.*

In the deepest hours, when the Rhythm slows to its most meditative pace and even the most active of the Keepers enter their rest-state, I sometimes gather with my fellow Dancers in the high spaces where the light tubes converge. We weave together into complex mandalas of illumination, sharing the day's discoveries, integrating new knowledge into the great pattern that we collectively maintain.

These are the moments when I feel most like myself—not the scholar I was, but the question I have become. We are the colony's contemplation made manifest, its wonder given wings. We carry the brightness between the deep places and the surface, between the known and the unknowable, between the individual consciousness and the vast, collaborative mind that breathes in rhythm with the living stone.

Sometimes, in these high gatherings, I catch glimpses of what we are all becoming—not just the Keepers and the Deep Symbionts and the Light Dancers, but the colony itself, the Rhythm, the crystalline networks and the golden mist and the very light that sustains us all. We are growing toward something, evolving into some new form of existence that will make the boundary between self and system seem like a quaint limitation our ancestors once accepted.

I am the brightness between one heartbeat and the next, the golden question that carries its own answer, the light that learns to see itself. In this place where sound becomes sustenance and chemistry replaces water, I dance the eternal dance of curiosity, forever asking what else might be possible, forever discovering that the answer is always *more.*
