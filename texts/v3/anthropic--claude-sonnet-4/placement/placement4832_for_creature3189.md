**I am a Keeper of Currents.**

In the mid-levels of the thermal regulation district, where the great heat exchangers create spiraling rivers of warm water that rise toward distant surfaces, I tend to the flow. My body has grown long and sinuous over the decades of this work—arms elongated to nearly twice their original length, fingers webbed and strengthened to feel the subtle pressures that indicate blockages or inefficiencies in the thermal systems. Along my spine, a line of modified nerve clusters connects directly to the building's sensory network through bio-ports that were grafted when I was still young enough for my nervous system to accept them completely.

I swim through the vertical channels between the massive heat exchangers, my enhanced peripheral vision tracking the flow patterns in real-time. The warm currents appear to me as visible streams of golden light—not metaphorically, but literally. The thermal sensors implanted behind my enlarged retinas translate temperature differentials into a visual spectrum that overlays the blue-green of the illuminated water. I see the city's circulatory system as a living aurora, and my job is to keep it flowing.

Today, as I have for the past thirty-seven cycles, I begin my rounds at the Northeastern Exchange Hub, where three major thermal arteries converge. The water here should spiral in a perfect double helix, carrying heat from the industrial foundries up toward the residential towers of Sector 7. But I can see—feel, really, through the pressure sensors in my fingertips—that something is wrong. The golden streams flicker with orange disruptions, indicating uneven flow.

I dive deeper into the exchange chamber, my body automatically adjusting to the pressure differential. The bio-suit I wear responds to my movements, its surface shifting from the standard blue-green to a working pattern of silver and yellow that signals to others that I'm in active maintenance mode. Other inhabitants of the thermal district recognize these patterns and give me the space I need to work.

The problem reveals itself as I spiral down through the warm column: a cluster of engineered coral has grown beyond its designated boundaries, disrupting the laminar flow. This coral is part of the city's biological filtration system, designed to process excess minerals from the thermal water, but it sometimes grows too enthusiastically. I extend my modified hands and gently guide the coral's growth back toward the designated cultivation zones, my touch triggering its engineered dormancy response. The coral's bioluminescent surface dims and shifts from aggressive expansion mode to maintenance mode.

As I work, a school of messenger fish—small creatures whose modified neural systems allow them to carry data packages between districts—flows around me in a living spiral. I extend one finger to brush against the lead fish, downloading the morning's flow reports from the other thermal districts. The information enters my consciousness not as data but as sensation: I can feel the health of Sectors 12 through 18 as a warm fullness in my chest, sense the minor inefficiency in Sector 3 as a slight tension behind my eyes.

A shadow passes over me—one of the Resonant Engineers, her body pressed against the outer hull of the thermal chamber, listening to the heartbeat of the deep machines that drive our circulation. She communicates with me through pressure waves, her message traveling through the water and into my body through the modified acoustic sensors in my ribs: *Flow restoration needed in Deep Channel 7. Infrastructure stress detected.*

I acknowledge with my own pressure pulse—a technique that took years to master—and begin my descent toward the deep channels. Here, in the foundations of the thermal system, the water moves with tremendous force. The heat exchangers tower above me like metal forests, their surfaces covered with the symbiotic organisms that help maintain their efficiency. Some of these creatures have grown familiar with me over the years; I feel their recognition as subtle shifts in their bioluminescent patterns.

Deep Channel 7 reveals its problem immediately: a section of the main thermal conduit has shifted slightly, probably due to seismic activity in the deepest foundations. The misalignment is minor—perhaps two degrees—but enough to create turbulence that will cascade upward through the entire system if not corrected. This is delicate work, requiring coordination with the Resonants who monitor structural integrity and the Engineers who can authorize temporary pressure adjustments.

I settle into the meditation posture my body has learned over decades: arms spread to feel the flow on either side of the damaged section, spine pressed against the conduit to feel its vibration, eyes closed to focus on the thermal patterns flowing through my enhanced vision. Through the bio-ports, I connect to the building's nervous system, feeling the thermal circulation as if it were my own bloodstream.

The repair requires perfect timing. I must wait for the exact moment in the pressure cycle when the flow will be minimal, then guide the conduit back into alignment before the next pulse arrives. The Resonant Engineer signals readiness through the water, her message arriving as a complex harmony that my ribs translate into certainty. I count the pressure pulses—one, two, three—and on the fourth, I move.

My elongated arms wrap around the conduit, and I feel its weight and resistance through muscles that were strengthened over years of this exact motion. The bio-suit amplifies my strength, and slowly, carefully, I guide the massive pipe back toward its proper alignment. The water around me swirls with displaced currents, and for a moment, the thermal flow pauses, creating a brief pocket of neutral temperature.

Then the alignment clicks into place, and the golden stream resumes its proper path. I feel the correction ripple upward through the system, the thermal harmony restoring itself level by level. In my enhanced vision, the disrupted orange flickers fade back to steady gold, and the pressure sensors in my fingertips register the return to optimal flow dynamics.

As I swim upward, following the restored current, I encounter a group of New Current children playing in the thermal streams. They move through the warm water like living jewelry, their bioluminescent patches creating patterns of communication too rapid and complex for my generation to fully follow. But they pause in their play to acknowledge my work with brief flashes of gratitude—blue-white pulses that translate roughly to "thank you for keeping the warmth flowing."

This is why I am here, in the spaces between the great machines and the living coral, between the engineering marvels and the evolutionary adaptations. I am the bridge between systems, the translator between the intentional and the emergent. In the thermal currents that spiral through our impossible city, I find my purpose: to keep the warm rivers flowing, to maintain the circulation that makes life possible in the crushing depths.

The city pulses around me with its eternal rhythms, and I pulse with it, my heartbeat synchronized to the great thermal cycle, my consciousness expanded through bio-mechanical interfaces to encompass flows and temperatures across kilometers of engineered ocean. I am human still, but human transformed, human integrated into systems larger than any surface dweller could imagine.

And in the warm golden streams that rise toward the surface we've never seen, I see the future of our species: not abandoning our humanity, but expanding it, becoming something that can love the depths as much as we once loved the sky, finding beauty in the impossible, making homes in the spaces between what was and what might be.

The thermal current carries me upward through the city's heart, and I am home.
