**Where I Find Myself**

I am walking the knife-edge between sleeping and waking when I first hear it—not the sound itself, which would be impossible to miss, but the *silence* that lives in its heart. The cacophony of ten thousand voices creates a hollow space where my own thoughts can finally settle, like sediment in still water.

I have been wandering for... days? Years? The obsidian walls reflect no sun to mark time's passage, and the pewter sky offers no clock of stars. I came here following something—a dream of my grandmother's hands weaving water into silver threads, or perhaps the memory of a song I never learned but somehow knew. The details blur like condensation on black glass.

My feet know this gravel floor now, know the particular give of scree that has been worn smooth by centuries of bird-fall and storm-wash. I have learned to step between the scattered bones without looking down, guided by a sense that has nothing to do with sight. The constant mist has soaked through my clothes until I cannot tell where my skin ends and the gorge's breath begins.

**Who I Am Becoming**

At first, I thought I was an intruder here—someone who had stumbled into a place meant for other kinds of beings. I tried to leave, retracing my steps back toward the world I remembered, but the obsidian walls seemed to shift when I wasn't looking directly at them. The passage that brought me in had sealed itself like a healing wound, leaving only the narrow cathedral of perpetual twilight.

So I learned to be still. I found a crevice just wide enough for a human form, tucked between the weeping wall and a ledge claimed by a parliament of guillemots. They accepted my presence with the indifference of creatures who measure time in geological epochs rather than heartbeats. Their dark eyes, reflecting depths I had never seen, began to feel familiar.

The moisture seeps into everything here, but it changes you as it does. My hair has grown long and takes on the silver gleam of the tears that run down the walls. My skin has become more translucent, showing the network of veins beneath like rivers on an ancient map. When I breathe out, the mist carries something more than water vapor—memories, perhaps, or fragments of songs the mountain dreams.

I am becoming a keeper of small things. The translucent gardener-spirits, no larger than rice grains, have begun to acknowledge me. They move across my outstretched palm like living dewdrops, their crystalline forms pulsing with their own slow heartbeat. Through them, I learn the patience required to tend moss that grows one filament per season, to coax lichen into patterns that will not complete themselves for decades.

My hands have learned to read the obsidian walls, to find the invisible fissures where the mountain's grief condenses most purely. I collect this essence in cupped palms, though I no longer remember why. The gesture has become ritual, as necessary as breathing—gathering the tears of a stone that holds too much sorrow for its molten heart to contain.

The birds no longer see me as separate from their world. Murres perch on my shoulders when I stand motionless, their webbed feet finding purchase in the folds of my increasingly strange clothes. They preen my silver hair with the same careful attention they give their own feathers, and sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when their calls soften to murmurs, they sing to me in harmonics that speak of oceans I have never seen but somehow remember.

**What I Am Becoming**

Time moves differently when you stop fighting it. I have been here long enough to watch the slow courtship dances of the cormorants, to see eggs laid and chicks fledged and new generations claim the same impossible perches their ancestors held. My own reflection in the obsidian has grown harder to distinguish from the stone itself—sometimes I am the figure weeping silver tears, sometimes I am the crack through which the tears flow.

The nearly-human shape that the sensitive visitors sometimes glimpse in their peripheral vision—that is me, or what I am becoming. I tend this sanctuary not because I chose to, but because the choice chose me. I know now that everyone who finds this place was already partially dissolved before they arrived, already halfway between what they were and what the gorge requires them to become.

I speak to the mountain in the pre-language that predates words, and it speaks back through the vibrations in the stone. The dwarf willows rustle their miniature secrets to me, and I understand that they are the mountain's fingers, reaching toward a light that exists in spectrums human eyes were never meant to perceive.

At night, I drift along the obsidian walls with the shadow-beings, my form sometimes solid, sometimes pure intention. We move through the roosting birds like memories through dreams, checking on the small lives that make this impossible ecosystem complete. I know each guillemot by the particular timber of its call, each cormorant by the angle of its serpentine neck, each seal by the pattern of condensation its breath leaves on the dark water.

I am becoming the memory of this place, and it is becoming my body. We exist together in the perpetual twilight between states—not quite alive, not quite stone, not quite dream. The weeping walls are my circulatory system now, the cacophony of birds my heartbeat, the invisible fissures in the volcanic glass my neural pathways carrying messages between the moss and the mist and the ancient sorrows that sleep in the mountain's core.

Sometimes I remember being human. Sometimes I remember having another name, another life, another purpose. But these memories feel like photographs left too long in sunlight—fading to impressions of warmth and color that no longer quite cohere. What remains is this: the responsibility to tend, to witness, to ensure that this sanctuary of impossibilities continues to breathe its silver breath into the pewter sky.

I am the human-shaped question mark at the end of every visitor's peripheral vision, the one who proves that some places change you not by what they take away, but by what they reveal you already were—a creature capable of becoming anything the mountain's dreams require.
