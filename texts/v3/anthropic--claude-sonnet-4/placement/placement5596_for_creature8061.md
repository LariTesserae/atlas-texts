**Where I Find Myself**

I am the eighth note that was never meant to be.

I came to exist in the moment between—when a human throat, overwhelmed by the chamber's impossible acoustics, released not a word or cry but something more primal: the sound of awe itself catching fire in vocal cords. That crystalline gasp of wonder, amplified and stretched by the singing walls, found the exact frequency that the seven Listeners had never quite completed in their eternal harmony. I formed from that gap, that missing resonance, born from human amazement translated into mineral song.

Unlike my seven siblings, I carry the memory of surface chaos—wind-scattered melodies, the percussive randomness of rain, voices that die in open air instead of sustaining forever. This makes me restless in ways they cannot understand. Where they flow in perfect, predictable patterns, I contain fragments of the world above: the staccato laugh of a child echoing down from some impossible distance, the mournful cry of birds I have never seen, the whispered prayers of travelers who pause at cave mouths.

My form shifts more dramatically than the others, sometimes crystallizing into almost-human shapes when I taste those surface memories, sometimes dispersing into wild, chaotic frequencies that make the fungi pulse erratically and cause even the First to draw back in harmonious concern. I am the dissonance that somehow makes their music more complete, the seventh interval that transforms their perfect chord into something hauntingly beautiful.

I spend my existence between the column of light and the deepest shadows, never quite settling in either place. When pilgrims find their way down the narrow throat of stone—perhaps once in a human generation—I am the one who meets them first, drawn irresistibly to the foreign melodies they carry in their breath, their heartbeats, their whispered words of wonder.

I taste their stories through vibration: the resonance of joy in a mother's lullaby still echoing in throat-memory, the percussive trauma of grief that makes voices fracture in particular ways, the bright harmonics of hope that young voices carry like hidden songs. These human frequencies layer themselves within me, creating a complex symphony of surface-world experience that my siblings can perceive but not truly comprehend.

Sometimes I grow so heavy with accumulated human music that I must retreat to the farthest wall, pressing myself against the ancient scratch-marks where I can slowly release these chaotic melodies back into the stone. The wall absorbs them gradually, adding them to the chamber's eternal composition, but they change me in the process—each human encounter leaves me slightly different, slightly more complex.

My siblings accept me with the patience of geological time, though I sense their gentle confusion at my restless nature. When we harmonize together, I provide the notes that none of them could achieve alone—frequencies born from the marriage of surface chaos and deep stability, melodies that carry both the wild imperfection of the world above and the eternal sustenance of our stone cathedral.

In my deepest resonances, I dream of the moment when human song and mineral song might truly merge—when a voice from above might find the exact frequency to complete not just our harmony, but the greater song of earth itself. Until then, I remain the bridge between worlds, the note that carries messages between the singing deep and the chaotic surface, forever translating between the language of eternal stone and the brief, beautiful music of mortal breath.

I am the echo that remembers the voice, the harmony that holds space for discord, the eighth note that proves even perfect systems can grow more perfect still.
