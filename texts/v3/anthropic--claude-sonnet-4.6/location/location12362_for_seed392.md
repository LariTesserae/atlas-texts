# The Woven Floor

---

Begin with the floor.

It extends in every direction you can see — several miles at least, perhaps more, the edge of it lost not in distance-haze but in the way your eye begins to feel dishonest past a certain point. The floor is flat. Entirely flat. Not flat the way plains are flat, which is to say not flat at all, but flat in the way that intention is flat: without accident, without the small negotiations land makes with gravity and time. Someone made this level. Someone made it continue being level. It has been level since before you arrived and shows no interest in becoming otherwise.

The floor is woven.

This is the first thing that resolves clearly as you crouch and look. Reed and split cane and something whitish — bone? dried root? bleached river-grass? — worked in a pattern of over-under, over-under, but at a scale larger than any basket. Each reed as thick as a wrist. The weave itself perhaps a foot deep, sprung slightly underfoot like a living thing, though it is not living. When you press your palm flat against it, it gives a few centimeters and holds. Below the weave: water. You can hear it, faintly. A permanent sound, very low, just beneath where hearing starts to count as sound — the floor floats, or rests on posts, or rests on something you cannot yet name, and beneath it the water moves in no hurry at all, in fact moves so slowly that moved is not quite the right word, but it is there, present, dark, navigable by no one.

The water underneath the floor makes the air above the floor what it is: dense with moisture, the sky pressing down warm and white, not cloudy exactly but not open either, a whiteness that is uniformly full of light without revealing a sun. It is bright in all directions simultaneously. No shadows fall. This detail arrives slowly and then cannot be un-noticed: nothing casts a shadow. Not the posts, not the structures, not you. The light has no direction. It is total and sourceless and very warm.

---

Now the structures.

They are sparse across the woven floor — scattered at irregular intervals across what you understand to be a great regional space, a scale of several miles. You cannot call them buildings quite, not yet, because they are not enclosed. They are more like decisions: frameworks of upright cane lashed together with cordage at angles that are not quite vertical, walls implied by hanging woven panels rather than stated by them, some panels raised, some lowered, some swaying faintly though there is almost no wind. The structures have been made by hand, clearly. You can see the knots. You can see where a beam was trimmed with a stone tool, the marks of shallow diagonal chops along the grain. You can see the labor in them. They have been made by many hands over what must have been much time, though time behaves oddly here in ways you're still assembling into a concern.

Between the structures: open floor. Great stretches of the woven surface with nothing on them but occasional standing water — shallow pools, a few centimeters deep, sitting flat as mirrors in the shadowless light. The pools are still. Perfectly still. Not still the way water is still when the wind has just stopped but still the way water is still in a painting of water: categorically, without the implication of any future ripple.

---

The plants grow from the weave.

This is the strange part — or the first of the strange parts. Plants grow from a floor that is entirely made. There is no soil visible; the weave shows beneath every root. But the roots go into the weave anyway and the plants are lush beyond proportion, excessive, the greenness of them almost textural, almost loud in a place that is so quiet. Broad-leafed things twice the height of a person, clustering near the structures like deliberate decoration, though probably not deliberate. Smaller groundcover — something vine-like, pale green, branching slowly across the woven surface — threading itself between the cane, finding the gaps. Clusters of reed so tall they become architectural. And here and there: trees, though not trees you recognize, with gray-green bark that is thick and almost soft-looking, and roots that sit on top of the weave in knuckled tangles, gripping the surface, going nowhere underground because there is no underground, and yet the trees stand and the trees are full-canopied and the trees have been standing long enough that their roots have pressed permanent impressions into the weave.

The vines drip. This is the water making itself known at the vegetative level: all the broad leaves have water on them, not rainwater, not now, but the condensation that the warm wet air is permanently trading with every cool surface. The leaves collect it and release it. The leaves have small channels along their undersides that route the collected moisture toward the stem, and from the stem it falls, one drop at a time, every few seconds, onto the woven floor below. This is most of the sound. The drops. Irregular, unhurried, each one making a small sound on landing — not quite a tap, not quite a splash — before being absorbed into the weave and rejoining what is below.

---

The fauna.

Many insects, but quiet insects. Nothing that buzzes audibly. Small iridescent things with wings that move too fast to see, hovering near the wet undersides of leaves, investigating the drip-channels. Beetles, dark red, working slowly along the cane of the floor-weave. Several species of spider, their webs catching the directionless light and becoming briefly visible — geometric, large, built between the standing structures as if the structures were designed for this purpose, which perhaps they were.

Birds occasionally: perched on the tops of the tall reeds, sitting very still. The birds are various in color — white, rust, a deep matte black — but share a quality of composure, of total stillness between movements. When they move, they move completely — a full repositioning, a deliberate hop, then stillness again. They make almost no sound. One, somewhere, makes a single note every few minutes. You hear it, lose track of it, hear it again. The interval is not regular. This is one of the few things here that is not regular.

Larger: something moving at the edge of your visibility, a shape low to the floor and unhurried, which you can see is moving but which, when you fix your attention on it, seems to have already been at its destination for some time. A slow animal. A deliberate animal. It does not look at you.

---

The weirdness accumulates.

You have been noticing the shadow-absence, but it compounds. You look at your hand and your hand has depth — you can see the tendons moving when you flex your fingers, the landscape of your knuckle — but the depth is lit from all sides simultaneously and you look like an object in a painter's study rendered under studio lighting, every surface gently differentiated from every other surface, nothing lost in darkness, nothing. You are here and fully visible and so is everything else. The quality of being seen is total.

The floor does not change. You have been standing here long enough to notice: nothing has shifted. The pools are exactly as still as they were. The drips from the leaves fall with the same irregular timing but the timing, you suspect, is not truly irregular — it only appears irregular because you cannot yet hear its pattern. If you listened for long enough, if you had been listening since the beginning of the floor, you would know the pattern. The floor and everything on it exists at an equilibrium that was decided before you arrived and will persist after you leave. The vines are growing but you cannot see them grow. The beetle moves but slowly enough that its position from moment to moment is simply: somewhere on the weave. Things change here at a speed below observation. The total impression is of a place that has arrived at itself and stopped.

---

The weirdness compounds further.

The floor is made. Entirely made. You understand this clearly. No ground beneath it, no substrate prior to the first human — or human-like — hand that cut the first reed. And yet the world that sits on the floor does not know it. The trees root in the weave as if it were ancient soil. The insects do not know there is only water below and not earth. The warm white light that comes from everywhere and nowhere fills everything evenly and does not interrogate the question of its source. The place has a self-consistency that is not natural and not supernatural but something a third thing, a word that doesn't exist yet: constructed-and-then-forgotten, made-and-then-outgrown-the-making, built well enough that the built-ness has become a kind of geology.

You find yourself wondering, not anxiously, how large the floor is. Whether it has an edge. Whether the edge drops into water or into more floor or into something the eye, as you said, becomes dishonest about. Whether the people who made the floor are still making it somewhere beyond what you can see, lashing more cane, pressing more bleached root into the pattern, floating new panels out over the dark moving water below. Whether the floor is finished or is always being finished. Whether those are different things.

The wonder is a slow wonder. Appropriate to the place. Nothing here moves fast enough to force urgency. The beetle continues. The single bird continues its note at its irregular-regular interval. A drip falls from a leaf and the sound of it is the loudest thing that has happened in some time.

---

You sit down.

The woven floor gives slightly and holds. Below you, the water moves with total patience. The white light comes from everywhere. A drip. A long pause. Another drip. Somewhere, the low rustle of a bird resettling its stillness. The enormous flat warmth. The smell of living green things transpiring into air already full of what they're releasing, a smell like the inside of something that has been carefully tended.

You have been sitting here for some time.

You may have always been sitting here.

The floor does not comment either way.
