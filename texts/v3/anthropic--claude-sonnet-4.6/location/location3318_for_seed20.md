# The Shouting Water

---

You are inside a sound before you are inside anything else.

The sound arrives first — not as pressure on the ears but as a condition of existence, the way gravity is a condition of existence. It is the sound of water falling from somewhere very high onto something it is actively destroying, and it does not stop, and it has never stopped, and the concept of *before the sound* does not apply here. The sound is a white total thing. It occupies the frequency range normally reserved for thought.

---

The place is small — perhaps forty meters wide in the widest direction, perhaps sixty long, bounded on three sides by what might be walls or might be the edges of what the eye can manage. The fourth side opens onto a drop that you cannot see the bottom of because the bottom of it is also the sound. Mist climbs back up from that drop in thick continuous columns, not rising so much as *returning*, as if the air here has a tide.

The elevation is real. You are high. The thinness of the air would tell you so if you could think about thinness of air, which you cannot, because of the sound. There is a suggestion of distance below — a whole world of distances stacked beneath your feet like folded cloth — but you exist on top of all of it, on a ledge that the water made and is currently in the process of unmaking.

---

The rock is new. This is the strangest thing about it, in a place of strange things.

It has the quality of recent emergence — surfaces that have not yet decided on their final texture, edges that still hold the memory of being interior. Where the water strikes exposed stone it finds no groove, no channel, no accommodation. The stone has no experience of being struck. The water has to start from the beginning every time, and it does, enthusiastically, with tremendous force, and the stone splits in the way new stone splits — not along old fracture lines but in fresh arbitrary directions, producing faces that catch the dim light and return it changed.

The color of the rock is the pale gray-tan of something unoxidized, unlichened, unworn. In places it is still damp from the inside. You could believe it had risen from somewhere this morning. You could believe, if you stayed long enough, you might watch it rise further.

The edges are sharp. Not dangerously sharp in the way of glass — more like the sharpness of a new decision, a line that hasn't softened yet into a curve. Things here have recently become themselves and have not had time to become anything else.

---

The water comes from above in a way that resists simple description.

It is not a waterfall in the usual sense — not a river that has reached an edge and gone over. It is more as if a section of sky has converted itself to water and is falling in that converted state, a sheet of the atmosphere that decided to become liquid and hasn't finished deciding, so parts of it are still mist, and parts of it are still air, and the whole mass arrives at the ledge in a state of ontological negotiation that never resolves.

The volume is enormous. The *width* of it is enormous. It exceeds the ledge on both sides, which means that standing anywhere on the ledge you are also *inside* the waterfall — inside its outermost reaches, its peripheral argument with the air. The mist is not secondary; the mist is primary. The mist is the environment. Breathing here is a form of drinking.

Everything is wet. The rock is wet. The dim air is wet. Wet has become the base state of matter in this place and dry is an abstraction you remember from somewhere else.

---

The light is dim and comes from no direction you can determine.

It is diffuse in the way of light inside cloud, arriving from the whole surface of the atmosphere rather than from any point within it. The mist contributes its own luminosity — not glow exactly, more like the light has been distributed through the water so finely that the water itself provides illumination, a secondhand light that has been through something before reaching you.

Colors here are muted and blue-shifted. The pale rock is paler. The sparse vegetation — and there is some — holds its green at the far edge of visibility, a green that is almost gray, almost not there.

Shadows do not have edges. Where one surface meets another and the light should change, it changes gradually, ambiguously. The world here does not have hard perceptual seams.

---

The vegetation is the only patient thing in this place.

Three or four plants — perhaps five, it's difficult to count because they move — cling to fissures in the new rock where enough material has accumulated to hold roots. They are small-leafed, succulent in structure though the water is not the problem, adapted to something else entirely — perhaps the violence, perhaps the sound, perhaps the particular chemistry of water that has been falling for long enough to dissolve the air.

Their leaves are dark and waxy. They hold raindrops in a way that looks deliberate. They flex constantly in the turbulence, not bending in a single direction as wind would bend them but oscillating, irregular, responding to a fluid environment that has no consistent preference for direction. They look like something discovering how to be alive in conditions that did not exist when they began.

One of them has recently been struck by something — a fragment of new stone, a piece of the world's self-revision — and is split at the base, and half of it hangs at an angle, and that half is still green, still flexing with the rest, not yet informed.

---

There are animals, or there is one animal, or there was.

A dark shape — the size of a large bird, roughly — occupies the most sheltered angle of the ledge, where two new-fractured rock faces meet at something close to a right angle. It is pressed into this corner with an intentionality that reads as rest or as waiting or as the refusal to be moved. You cannot see what it is. The mist obscures its details. It does not move while you are watching it. It does not move when the ledge shudders — and the ledge shudders, repeatedly, as the water does its continuous negotiation with the rock.

Whether it is aware of you is a question you cannot answer because it has no orientation you can read. You are not sure it has a front.

At some point you look again and the angle of the rock is empty and you cannot determine whether the animal left or whether the rock shifted and created a new corner somewhere else.

---

Here is where the place exceeds its own description:

The ledge changes. Not metaphorically — literally. The dimensions of it are not stable. The forty meters of width is an average, a central tendency. The ledge breathes in and out, or the waterfall shifts laterally, or the rock at the far end adds itself in fractured increments or loses itself in the same. You cannot catch it changing. You can only find, periodically, that your sense of where the edges are has updated without your participation.

The sound does this too. The sound changes *registers* without changing volume. One moment it is the sound of impact — water striking stone, stone splitting, the vocabulary of collision. Another moment it is the sound of voices, not human voices, not language, but something with the rhythm of language — with the sense that somewhere inside the total noise there is a structure, a repetition, a grammar that has no content you can access. This is not metaphor either. The place is doing something with sound that is not your business, that precedes your arrival and will survive your departure, that uses your auditory system the way wind uses a cracked window.

Things happen at the edge of the ledge that should not happen. The mist returns upward in columns, as described, but sometimes a column has structure — a coil, a series of rings, a spiral that rises for several meters before diffusing. This is fluid dynamics. This is explainable. But it happens with a regularity and a variety that feels selected rather than random. As if the water is trying forms. As if form is what this place is attempting, broadly — matter arriving from above in an undifferentiated sheet and making contact with a solid surface and becoming, in the aftermath of that contact, something with edges, something with structure, something that holds briefly the shape of a decision.

---

There is no shelter.

This is not because the ledge is exposed — the rock faces provide angles, concavities — but because shelter requires a distinction between outside and inside, and the water has abolished that distinction. The mist is indoors. The mist is everywhere. Whatever is above you is also beside you and the rain that falls on the open ledge falls also in a finer version in the spaces the rock creates. You are never out of the water. You are in the water. The water here is the medium and you are a thing moving through it.

---

There was, at some point, a structure.

You find it at the ledge's narrowest end, where the rock rises into a small overhang, and beneath the overhang there are stones arranged — not randomly, not naturally, stacked — three or four courses of careful masonry, not very high, not enough to have been a wall in any functional sense, or not enough anymore. No mortar. The stones fit by their edges. They are not the pale new stone of this ledge but darker, older, imported from somewhere with a longer history. Whoever placed them knew to look for stone that had already learned its shape.

The structure does not protect anything. It stands in the water and the mist like everything else, slightly black with wet, the courses of stone running roughly parallel to the ledge's edge. It is perhaps three meters of wall going nowhere.

You consider whether the person who built it had the same difficulty you are having — whether they also could not hear themselves think, could not track the changes in the ledge's dimension, could not find the animal in the corner. Whether they built the wall as a way of asserting *I was here*, in a place that resists the concept of *here*. Whether it worked. Whether assertion of that kind is the technology available in a place that has no other kind.

---

The sound is still happening. The sound is still the primary fact. If you stay long enough — and perhaps you have been here longer than you know, it is difficult to measure time inside an unvarying roar — the sound stops being an obstacle to thought and becomes the medium of it. You think *in* the sound. The sound provides a pressure that thought pushes against, and the friction of that produces something. A kind of interior clarity that would not be available in quieter circumstances.

The water falls.

The rock accepts it, newly, surprised each time.

The mist rises, and sometimes the mist tries a form.

The ledge revises its boundaries, obscurely, without announcement.

Something that was in the corner was there and now is not.

The wall of old stone stands in the water, patient in the way of things that have already made their point.

You are very high up. Below you is a long argument between falling water and what the water eventually reaches. You are in the middle of that argument, on a ledge that is being created and destroyed simultaneously, on a day that has been this way since before days were measured and will be this way after measurement ceases to matter.

The sound is total. The sound is company. The sound is what this place uses instead of silence to mean itself.
