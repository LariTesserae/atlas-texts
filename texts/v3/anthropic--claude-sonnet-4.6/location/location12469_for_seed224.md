# The Pocket at the Top of the Argument

---

Begin with what is missing.

No wind. No moisture pulling at the skin. No smell of anything biological, no rot, no sweetness, no the-way-rain-is-coming. Just cold — and not the aggressive cold that bites, not winter cold with its animal hunger, but a *settled* cold, the cold of something that has long since finished cooling and simply *is* its temperature now, the way a stopped clock is its time. The air, if it is air, holds still with the patience of a conclusion.

You are in a small place. That is the first thing to understand. The second thing is harder.

---

The enclosure — call it that, for now — measures perhaps forty meters at its widest, perhaps less. It is difficult to say *less than* or *more than* because the boundary does not behave consistently. The walls, if they are walls, are stone: high-altitude stone, the kind that has been at elevation long enough to forget it was ever anything else. Pale grey schist layered in long tilted planes, the strata running at thirty degrees off vertical as though the whole formation once decided to lean and then reconsidered but not all the way. The faces of the rock are smooth in the manner of things smoothed by time rather than tool — no chisel marks, no fracture freshness, just the matte, close-grained surface of mineral that has been rained on by vacuum for long enough to cease having an opinion about it.

Above: dim. Not overcast — there are no clouds at this elevation, whatever this elevation means from the outside. The sky, or the space that functions as sky here, emits light the way a deep reading room does: sourceless, directionless, sufficient only for the shapes nearest to you. Objects at the far edge of the enclosure — and there are objects there, a few, scattered — exist as suggestions. Slightly darker than the air around them. Present by inference.

Below: flat mineral floor. Dolomite or something pretending to be dolomite. White with grey streaks, crossed by ancient stress-fractures that have long since closed and healed into thin dark seams. No dust. This is important. At this elevation, in this stillness, there should be fine particulate on every horizontal surface, but the floor is clean. Not swept clean — *kept* clean, by some process that does not announce itself.

---

Now the sparse things.

Across the floor, separated by significant distances from one another, there are objects that it would be incorrect to call ruins and equally incorrect to call machines. They are *remnants of intention* — which is not the same as artifacts, not exactly, because artifact implies something finished, something that was once complete. These were never finished. Or they were finished past finishing, out the other side of completion into something that completion can't describe.

The nearest one: a column of pale metal approximately two meters tall, hexagonal in cross-section, narrowing very slightly toward the top. The metal is not any alloy with a name. It is the color of pewter in dim light but does not reflect light the way metal does — instead the surface seems to *absorb* the illumination and return it changed, a few degrees shifted in some direction that is not a color direction. Around its base, arranged in a ring, are smaller shapes: flattened ovoids, perhaps a dozen of them, each the size of a hand, made of the same substance. They are not touching the column. They are not touching each other. The gaps between them are equal, and the gaps have been equal for — the schist around them tells you — a very long time. There is a depression worn into the dolomite beneath each ovoid. Not worn by the ovoid. Worn by whatever the ovoid *does*, directed downward, over centuries.

Sixty meters away — you can barely see it, and the distance feels wrong, slightly too large for the enclosure — a second structure. Flat, horizontal, closer to the floor. It looks like a table that lost interest in being a table and became something else instead: three legs intact, a fourth missing, the surface tilted, and on the surface, a geometric arrangement of incised lines that catch no light but are somehow more visible than anything around them. A diagram. Not a circuit diagram, not a star chart, not anything with a familiar vocabulary. It is a diagram of *a set of rules*, you understand without being able to say how you understand it, and the rules it describes are not the rules in operation here, which means someone brought it *from* somewhere to here, which means someone was here, once, doing work.

The third object is harder to describe because it is not fully present. It occupies a position near the north wall — north by convention, by the need to call directions something — and it is present in the sense that it displaces the dim light around it and casts a shadow that is the right shape. But when you look at it directly, the looking slides. Your attention finds purchase on the stone wall behind it more easily than on the thing itself. You know it is there. You know approximately what shape it makes. You know it is the largest of the three and that it is the one that is doing most of the work, whatever the work is. You cannot know more than this. This is not a failure of perception; it is a property of the thing.

---

Here is what I mean by the cold being *settled*.

Everything here has reached its equilibrium with everything else. The stones have long since accepted their cracks. The objects have long since accepted their positions. The air — if it processes — has long since accepted its composition. This is not stasis; stasis implies a force holding things still, whereas here the stillness is the natural expression of things that have been working through their implications for an extremely long time and have arrived.

Processes are still occurring. They are just occurring *slowly*. The incised diagram is very slightly different from what it was a year ago, if you had been here a year ago to compare. The metal column is doing something — the depressions in the dolomite are evidence of this — but doing it at a rate that makes watching pointless. The thing near the north wall is exchanging something with the boundary of the enclosure, across the gap, through some medium that is not light and not any field with a name, and has been doing so for long enough that the exchange has become mutual, has become relationship, has become the thing that holds the boundary where it is.

---

Sound.

Once, perhaps every ten minutes, there is a sound like a very small stone settling. It comes from no particular direction. It is not a stone settling. It is the dolomite floor very slightly adjusting its internal stress, releasing a quantum of the strain that has accumulated from the column's work, and the sound travels through the material and arrives at your feet before it arrives at your ears, a faint vibration first and then the click. That's all. That, and your own breathing, which sounds enormous here and which you find yourself suppressing, wanting to be quieter than you are, wanting to be less present in a place that has achieved such careful presence on its own.

---

The weirdness is not dramatic. It doesn't announce itself. It is not a tear in space or a howling impossibility or something that screams *wrong* at the hind-brain.

It is this: your shadow, in this sourceless dim light, is not oriented toward any wall. It falls in a direction that doesn't correspond to any single light source, which is expected in diffuse light — but the shadow's direction shifts very slowly, over the course of minutes, independent of your movement. It rotates around you at a rate your eyes can't catch in the act. Turn away, look back: it has moved. By how much? By an amount that feels like more than it is, or less than it is, but not the right amount.

It is this: the enclosure, when you try to map it — when you try to hold in your mind the positions of the three objects relative to each other and to the walls — the map doesn't stay. Not because you forget it. Because the relationships between the positions are not stable in the way positions are stable. The column and the table are a certain distance apart when you measure them with your eye from the left. From the right, the same distance, within what you can confirm. From the center, something slightly different — not much, not enough to be certain, but the uncertainty doesn't resolve when you try to pin it down. It gets more uncertain the more precisely you try to measure. This is not a quantum effect of the small-scale, not the uncertainty principle as usually expressed; it is something else. It is as though this small enclosure occupies slightly more geometric space than it seems to from any given point within it, the extra space distributed throughout, not located anywhere in particular.

It is this: the stone walls are real stone. You can touch them. They are cold under your hand — not startlingly cold, just the cold of rock that holds no warmth — and they have texture, the layered texture of schist, and some of the layers are slightly different colors, ochre and pale green and blue-grey. But there are, very rarely, when you look at a section of wall without focusing on it, suggestions of layering that are not the mineral layering. Arrangements that last for a second and then resolve back into rock. Not faces, not patterns, not anything that looks like information — or, not anything that looks like the information it might actually be, if it is information. The walls may be a storage medium. The walls may be the medium through which the thing near the north wall is doing its exchange. The walls may simply be walls, and what you are seeing is what happens to human pattern-recognition when it has nothing to organize itself around.

It is this: you arrived here. You do not remember arriving. You do not remember a door, a threshold, a transition. You are simply here, having been elsewhere, with no seam between.

---

The technology is not visible as technology. This is perhaps the truest thing about it.

The column and the ovoids and the table-thing are not technology in the sense of devices with functions you could reverse-engineer by looking at them long enough. They are more like *theorems made material* — conclusions about the nature of something physical, instantiated in matter, operating by logic rather than by mechanism. The work they do is not work in the engineering sense. It is closer to work in the mathematical sense: a transformation that preserves some things while changing others, that maps one state to another according to rules that hold because they are true, not because they are enforced.

What are they maintaining? What is the system they are keeping in its current state?

The enclosure. The pocket. The small space at the top of something — not a mountain exactly, though elevation is real here, though the cold is the cold of height — this carefully delimited volume of slightly-wrong geometry. They are maintaining *its existence as a distinct thing*, is the best way to say it. Without them, the pocket would resolve. Would become continuous with whatever surrounds it. Would lose its hiddenness, its interiority, its slight excess of space. 

Who made them, and what they expected to happen here, and whether anyone has ever come here on purpose or only by accident, the enclosure does not say. The enclosure has no interest in explaining itself. It achieved its current state a long time ago and is content, in the way that things without subjectivity can be content, to continue.

---

You stand in the clean cold stillness and breathe too loudly and your shadow moves without reason.

Every ten minutes or so, the floor clicks once beneath you.

The dim light continues to come from everywhere and nowhere.

The thing near the north wall continues its exchange.

And the place continues being what it is: hidden, high, dry, patient — a pocket in something larger, pinched off carefully and held, a small perfected argument in stone and impossible metal, waiting out time the way only cold and mineral can wait, which is to say completely, which is to say forever, which is to say as though waiting and being were the same thing.
