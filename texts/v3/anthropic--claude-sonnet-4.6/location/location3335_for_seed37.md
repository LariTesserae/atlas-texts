# The Hollow Under Bright Nothing

---

You find it by going down — not spelunking, not excavating, but by some sideways slippage that doesn't quite correspond to a direction you know. One moment you are somewhere ordinary, walking on the kind of ground that accepts weight without commentary, and then the ordinary resolves into this: a pocket, a hollow, a chamber not much larger than two people could furnish into a life.

The ceiling is close but not oppressive. Four feet overhead, maybe five. It is made of something that is becoming rock — not has-become, not will-become, but *is-becoming*, continuously, visibly, the way a word becomes itself in the mouth. You can see striations forming where there were none a moment ago. Mineral ghosts resolving out of mineral suggestions. If you pressed your hand to it you might leave an impression, or you might not, or you might find your hand has always been part of what is forming there. You do not press your hand to it.

The walls have the same quality. Raw in the oldest sense — not harsh, not rough-edged, but *raw* the way a wound is before it knows what it will become. They glisten. Condensation has been arriving here, water that materialized from cool air and pressed its slow conclusions into the stone-that-is-stone-becoming. The ceiling drips, but not with urgency. One drop every forty seconds or so. You count, and then you lose count, and then the drops seem to arrive outside of counting entirely.

---

The floor is the one thing that has committed to a form, and even there you're not certain.

It is mostly dry underfoot — packed nothing, just compressed becoming, a substrate that makes your footstep sound wrong, too certain, too historical. But along the far edge, where the wall in its emerging state has not yet sealed against the floor, there is a seam of wet. A dark line of genuine moisture that pulses almost imperceptibly. Not flowing — water here does not flow so much as *accumulate by preference*, choosing this seam, this margin, finding it hospitable. Touch it with a finger and it is real and cold, cooler than the air by several degrees, the kind of cold that reminds the body that water doesn't care what temperature you are.

The air in the hollow is cool without being cold. Perhaps fifty degrees. The kind of temperature that makes you notice your own skin as a membrane, makes you aware that you are also a pocket of warmth inside something larger that is not warm.

---

There is light and it has no source.

This is the first violation of the ordinary rules and you should understand it as the entry point to the larger violations.

The light is bright — genuinely, unreasonably bright for a space below the surface, below the ground, in a chamber with no windows and no lamp and no crack large enough to admit the sun. It is the brightness of late-morning overcast, of a sky that has decided to illuminate everything equally without shadows, and it fills the hollow completely and uniformly and with something like intention.

You look for where it comes from. The walls don't glow, exactly. Or they do, but so subtly that calling it glow would be misleading — it's more that light refuses to diminish in this space. It arrives from elsewhere and stays. It does not obey the distance rule. Photons here have, perhaps, forgotten that their intensity should fall off with the square of distance from a source, and so they continue at their original brightness regardless of where they are. The shadows you cast are faint and puzzled-looking, as if they are not sure they have jurisdiction.

---

Now: the fauna.

Here is where the place most fully announces what it is.

The hollow is, in some technical sense, busy. Not with sound — sound is nearly absent, and we will come to that — but with presence, with the sense of teeming, of life arranged in every available dimension of the space.

On the walls: *things* that may be insects, may be. They are small. They have the jointed quality, the segmented consideration of insects, but they hold still in a way insects do not hold still — in a way that suggests they are not subject to the same restlessness, the same imperatives. They are pale, the color of the forming stone, which is also the color of nothing yet decided. There are dozens of them distributed across the wall's surface with the spatial logic of a careful thought — not clustered, not random, but spaced with intention, each one occupying roughly the same area of wall, as if they have agreed on something. They do not move while you watch them. When you look away — at the wet seam, at the sourceless light, at your own hands — you feel certain they have moved. But catching them at it is impossible.

On the ceiling, the becoming-rock ceiling: other things. These are less insect and more principle. They cling in defiance of what clings usually require. They have — you think — a texture like lichen but lichen taken to its logical conclusion, lichen that has outgrown its metaphor. They breathe. Small respiration, small rhythmic expansion and contraction at scales you can almost see. The drops of condensation, you notice, fall from specific points, and those points are always near one of the ceiling things. Whether they produce the moisture or simply attract it or are the moisture's preferred mode of departure from the ceiling, you cannot determine.

On the floor, careful: small things. Pale. Moving. Too many kinds to count but too few individuals to constitute mass. A beetle-shape that is not a beetle. Something like a pillbug but wrong in a specific way you cannot identify but cannot stop feeling. A worm near the wet seam that appears to be stationary until you realize it is moving at the pace of hour hands, continuously, inexorably, in a direction that loops. There is a spider. The spider is completely ordinary and it is the most frightening thing in the hollow, which says something.

---

The active quality of this place is not movement. It is *process*.

Everything here is in the middle of something. The rock is in the middle of becoming rock. The creatures are in the middle of their obscure purposes. The water is in the middle of its slow decision about where to accumulate. Even the light seems engaged in some continuous project, some brightening that has been going on since before you arrived and will continue without resolution.

You watch and nothing concludes.

The hollow is not a setting but a verb caught mid-conjugation.

---

The sparse vegetation: there is a plant.

One plant, perhaps, or a colony of one type of plant presenting as one plant, which is a meaningful distinction you cannot resolve here.

It is near the wet seam, rooted in the margin where becoming-rock meets moisture. It has the look of something that doesn't need much — a few flat leaves, the color of sage but paler, the color of sage in a dream about sage. It is cool to consider, visually cool, which is different from being green. It grows slowly in a direction that is mostly toward the sourceless light and partly toward something else, some orientation the light can't explain.

There are no other plants. The hollow does not support more than this, or has not yet gotten around to more than this, or has decided that one plant is a sufficient hypothesis.

---

Sound, or the shape sound makes when it is mostly absent:

The drip. Every forty seconds, as discussed, or outside of counting. When it lands on the wet seam it makes almost no sound at all, which is wrong — water on water should have a voice. The sound it makes is less than a sound. It is a suggestion of a sound, the vowel the sound would make if sounds could whisper their own names.

The hollow is otherwise silent in a way that has texture. This is not the silence of absence. It is the silence of something that has made a decision, that has arranged itself away from sound with some care, the way a room arranges itself around furniture.

The creatures on the wall, if they move when you look away, move without friction. The beetle-shape on the floor moves without friction. Even your own footsteps — you shuffle, just to check — are received by this place and gently refused, returned to you smaller than they should be.

---

Here is what is broken and you must sit with it:

The light. The light is the primary wrongness and everything else grows from it. This much light below the surface, with no source — that's not a phenomenon, that's a statement. The universe is making a statement here, in this pocket, in this hollow where things are just beginning to be. The statement is: *the rules you use to navigate light are local rules. This is a local exception.*

And from that exception, others:

The walls are forming. Geology doesn't work that fast. You can see it happen. The mineral content of the air is resolving — you can almost see it, almost feel it, the way you can feel static before lightning, a sense of matter deciding to matter differently. Something is depositing. Something is making up its mind to be stone and has not yet finished.

The creatures do not behave according to the treaties by which creatures usually govern themselves in relation to observers. The bugs do not flee. The things on the ceiling do not flee. Nothing in this hollow has the survival instinct you are used to reading in fauna — none of the nervous electricity of being alive in a world where other things eat you. They exist here under different terms. Their relationship to danger, to you, to each other — it has been renegotiated by whatever made this hollow available, whatever arranged the light this way.

And this: you notice, if you stay long enough, if you are not in a hurry to leave, that the wet seam pulses. Not with any rhythm you can name. Not like a heartbeat. But it *pulses*, which is a word for a thing that's alive doing something, and the seam is water in a groove in rock that is becoming rock, and it should not pulse, and it does.

---

There is something partially built here. Half-built. Pre-built.

In the corner, which isn't quite a corner — where the wall's not-quite-committed angle meets the floor's near-decision — there is what might be a structure. Small. The size of a shoebox, roughly, or of a thing made by hands that worked at a different scale than human hands. It is made of the same material as the walls because it is, or was, or is becoming, part of the walls. But something has differentiated it. Something has distinguished this volume of becoming-rock from the surrounding becoming-rock and given it edges that are too intentional, a regularity that geology doesn't usually produce.

You cannot tell if it was made from outside, by something that came into this hollow before you, or if it is the hollow making something — if the hollow's process of formation has, here, taken on a different complexity, has decided to produce not just rock but *structure*, not just stone but *shelter-shape*.

There are no tools. Nothing that was held, nothing that bore marks of handling.

The structure — call it a structure — is about two-thirds present. A third of it is still being decided.

---

How to leave this place:

You go up — not spelunking, not ascending, but by a sideways slippage that doesn't quite correspond to a direction you know. The return is as disorienting as the arrival. You re-enter the ordinary world and the ordinary world's light is significantly dimmer, which is the one wrong thing about the ordinary world that this experience has given you and which does not go away.

You are left with: a sense of having witnessed something at the moment before it understood itself. A sense that the hollow is not waiting for you to return, is not organized around your visit or your absence, but continues — processes, accumulates, forms, breathes, pulses — entirely on its own terms, in its bright impossible pocket, below.
