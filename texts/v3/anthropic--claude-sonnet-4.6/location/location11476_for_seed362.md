# The Verdant Works

---

Begin with what you notice first, which is the light.

It comes from everywhere and from nothing identifiable. A white-green luminescence that has no source you can locate — no sun overhead, no lamp arrays, no bioluminescent organ you can point to and name. The light simply *is*, suffused through the air itself as though photons are native to this atmosphere the way nitrogen is native to ours. It is bright enough to read by. Bright enough to cast shadows, though the shadows fall in directions that don't quite correspond to anything. You stand in one and it points left. You step aside and it points the same direction. The shadows here are not reflections of obstruction. They are something else. They are marks the world makes.

Under this light, everything is the color of living things.

---

The floor — if floor is the right word, and it isn't quite — is a continuous surface of compressed root-material, pale fibrous wood laid down over centuries into something like stone. It does not give under pressure. It does not creak. It has been walked and weighed and worked upon for so long that it has achieved a kind of terminal density, a vegetable concrete, and the surface is smooth in the way that rivers make stones smooth, except nothing has ever moved across it. It simply reached this state through long insistence. Through the particular stubbornness of something alive that was never interrupted.

This floor extends — in every direction that matters — farther than weather allows you to see.

Scale. You must understand the scale before anything else makes sense, and the scale is the first thing that breaks your understanding. This is not a forest. A forest has edges, has a watershed, has a relationship to something outside itself. This does not. This is a *continent of plant*. Possibly larger. Possibly the concept of *larger* needs revision here, because the place has its own physics now — its own thermal gradients, its own pressure systems, its own electromagnetic conditions generated by the sheer volume of transpiration and respiration and whatever else the mass of it is doing, which is considerable and constant and not fully describable in terms that biologists have developed, because biologists developed their terms in places where plants grew in soil, in water, in sun, and here none of those things obtain.

There is no soil. There never was. What grows here grows in itself.

---

What you are standing in — what everything is built from, built *of*, built *as* — is plant.

Not plants. *Plant*, as a singular and total substance. The distinction matters.

The structures rise around you: columns thirty meters in circumference, not trees exactly but tree-derived, compressed living wood that has grown into architectural forms over what must have been millennia of deliberate pressuring — not by intelligence necessarily, or not by any intelligence that had a plan, but by the pressure of growth itself, which in the absence of competition or limitation simply *becomes*. The columns hold up nothing visible. They terminate somewhere above in the green-lit haze where the canopy has achieved such density that it functions as a ceiling, a weather-ceiling, and clouds form beneath it, small local clouds that drift slowly between the columns like exhaled breath, depositing their minute moisture on the fibrous walls and then being reabsorbed. The humidity is therefore constant. The air is close and cold in the way that root-cellars are close and cold, pressed and still, and it smells of nothing that has a common name. It smells of scale. Of something that has been processing itself for long enough that it has moved past any smell a human nose evolved to recognize.

Along every surface: growth.

Moss that is not quite moss — denser, darker, arranged in geometric progressions that suggest either mathematics or the kind of structure that emerges when mathematics isn't needed because the underlying rules produce it automatically. Lichen in circular formations. Smaller growths nested within the circular formations. And within those, smaller still, each scale a repetition of the last, fractal not by design but by the logic of things growing on things growing on things, layer after layer through the centuries until the layers themselves are geological, until you could core this column like an ice sheet and read climate records and growth-events and catastrophes-overcome.

But no catastrophe has visited here in a very long time.

That is the particular quality of the settled, and the settled is everywhere. This place has reached its state. It is not arriving somewhere; it arrived. The balance it holds is not the dynamic balance of an ecosystem fighting itself into stability — it is the balance of something that has simply *stopped needing to fight*. The ratio of growth to decay is 1:1 and has been for longer than you can reason about. Matter cycles. Nothing accumulates beyond its capacity. Nothing is lost below replenishment. The Works — and you find yourself calling it that, though the name surfaced in you without source, without being told — is not alive in the way that animals are alive, urgent and contingent, but alive in the way that geology is alive: slowly, completely, without anxiety.

---

But it is built.

This is the thing you keep returning to, the thing that makes the light's wrongness make a different kind of sense. This is the thing that sits underneath every other observation like a root beneath roots.

It was made.

Not all of it. Or — it was not made in the way that buildings are made, with intention and materials gathered from elsewhere and a plan drawn on paper. But it was *worked*. Something — some process, some entity-or-process, some arrangement of decision-making that may have been a civilization or may have been a single organism of incomprehensible extent — shaped this. The architectural regularity of the columns: not accidental. The floor-density: not simply what happens when roots press together for millennia without intervention. Someone, something, *compressed* it. Worked it to smoothness. The lichen formations that run in spirals too consistent for wind distribution, too large for any single organism's reach, too *purposeful*-seeming in their coverage of load-bearing surfaces, as though they were applied as insulation, as sealant, as a living coating chosen for its properties rather than its aesthetics.

And the machinery.

Here. This is where the description wants to go careful and precise, because the machinery does not look like machinery in any industrial sense — no iron, no steel, no coal or diesel or electrical cable in conduit. But it is machinery. You know machinery by its *movement-with-purpose*, by the conversion of one form of force into another, by repetition in service of production.

The machinery here is also plant.

Thick vine-arrangements, some as wide as a human torso, running horizontally between columns at various heights, under constant tension — you can see the strain in the grain of them, the slight compression and extension as something moves through them in pulses. Not fluid exactly. Not sap in any normal sense. Something thicker, slower, pressurized, moving on a rhythm you can feel as vibration in the floor if you press your palm flat and hold still. The pulse is very slow. Slower than a heartbeat. Perhaps one compression every forty seconds. But it never stops. And it runs everywhere, this pulse, this distributed hydraulic heartbeat through the vine-networks — running along the ceiling, down the columns, through the floor itself — and wherever it runs, it is *doing something*. Converting. Transferring. The lichen on the surfaces glows fractionally brighter as the pulse passes through the adjacent column. The small clouds thicken and disperse in rhythm with it. Somewhere, far away and in every direction simultaneously, something is being processed, produced, maintained.

The Works works.

You just cannot determine what it produces. What it is for. What — if intent still applies here — it is *trying to do*.

---

Fauna: almost none.

This is notable. A mass this vast, this productive, should be teeming. Should have evolved or attracted a density of animal life proportionate to its vegetable density. Instead: a few things. A pale arthropod — possibly — that moves along the base of one column, tasting the root-stone with something that functions as a mouth. It is the color of the floor and nearly invisible and it does not react to your presence in any way you can detect. At great height, up where the canopy-clouds drift, something moves that might have wings, or might be a segment of the canopy itself shifting in a pressure gradient. You see it once. It does not repeat. There may be things in the spaces between column-bases that you are not equipped to see, things that occupy a scale too small or too vast for your attention to hold. The Works does not seem to need animals. Does not seem to have invited them. The few that are here are here in the way that lint is here, passively, collected by circumstance into a corner of something that was never designed with them in mind.

---

Sound.

Almost none.

This is the second thing you noticed, after the light, and it is harder to describe than the light because your brain keeps trying to fill it. There is the pulse — felt, not quite heard, a subsonic movement in the floor. There is, very occasionally, a soft sound from above: the small clouds releasing their small moisture against the surfaces, a sound like cloth pressed to cloth, like a whisper with no language in it. There is the sound of your own body, which is suddenly appalling in its loudness — your breath, your footsteps on the root-stone, the rustle of your clothing, the liquid sounds of your interior. The Works is so much quieter than you are. So much more settled in its sound-economy. It produces no noise it doesn't need to produce, and it needs to produce almost none.

Once, while you stand still enough that your body's noise reduces to something manageable, you hear — from a distance that is difficult to estimate because sound behaves strangely here, bouncing off the irregular surface of the canopy-ceiling in ways that scramble directionality — something that might be a mechanism engaging. A slow click, deep, singular. The kind of sound that in a normal factory would precede great noise and motion. Here it precedes nothing you can detect. The process it begins is too slow, or too distributed, or too alien in its nature, to register as event.

The click fades.

The silence reclaims.

---

Weather.

There is weather here, because there must be, because the scale demands it. The thermal gradient between the cool root-floor and the processing warmth of the upper canopy creates slow vertical circulation — you can feel the slight draw of air if you hold your hand at a column-gap, a cool drift moving upward. The clouds you have already noted. There are also, infrequently, pressure events: something shifts in the vine-network's tension, the pulse skips or deepens, and a wave of atmospheric change moves through the space — not a wind, but a change in the weight of air, like the feeling before lightning that never comes. These pass through you and leave no mark, except that afterward the lichen seems to have shifted orientation fractionally, and the column shadows point in a slightly different wrong direction.

---

Now the weirdness.

You have been circling it. The wrong shadows. The sourceless light. The machinery without evident product. These have been the leading edge of it, the symptoms visible from outside the phenomenon. Here is the phenomenon:

*The space does not maintain consistent size.*

Not dramatically. Not in any way that would immediately register as impossible. But if you walk from one column to another — a distance that appears to be perhaps fifteen meters — and then turn and walk back, the return trip takes a different amount of time. Not much different. Perhaps ten steps instead of twelve. Perhaps fourteen instead of twelve. The number varies. The distance between the columns varies, but the columns do not move, and the variance is not explained by your own inconsistency of stride. You have tested this. You have been testing it since you arrived without naming what you were testing.

The Works does not maintain a consistent metric.

Space here is *organic*, and because it is organic it is variable, and because the organism that is space here is ancient and large and in its settled state, the variation is subtle — not the wild spatial sickness of a place that is breaking down or reforming, but the gentle inconsistency of a living thing that breathes, that has peristalsis, that is not a machine and cannot be held to machine precision even though it is built. Even though something made it. Whatever made it made it living, made it plant-as-physics, and plant does not hold to Euclidean strictness. Plant curves toward light. Plant finds the path of least resistance. Plant grows.

This space grows.

Very slowly. Indiscernibly in any human timeframe. But it grows, and as it grows the rules of it shift with it, and the light shifts, and the shadows track something other than the geometry of obstruction-and-source, and you are standing in a place that is not quite the same place it was when you arrived, and will be not quite the same place when you leave, and this is not alarming — this is, in fact, the defining quality of the Works, the thing it most essentially *is*: it is a made thing that has exceeded its making, that has grown past its original rules into its own rules, that has been running long enough that the running *is the point now*, that the production is the process and the process is the production and what it produces is more of itself, which is to say more space, more light, more settled and bright and cool and dense and quiet growing, more verdancy as fundamental physics, more of this specific category of existing which has no name and does not need one.

---

You will not find an edge.

This is not a claim about how far you could walk. This is a claim about the topology. The Works is not a bounded region. It may not be a region at all in any sense that requires bounds. It is — possible — that to walk in any direction here long enough is to arrive back where you started, not because it is spherical in the conventional sense but because space here has the organic closure of a cell membrane: permeable at the microscale, continuous at the macro, not ending but returning. If so, the returning would take time beyond your reckoning and the you that arrived back would not recognize the starting point because the starting point would have grown.

The pale arthropod is no longer at the column-base.

The wing-or-canopy-segment has not reappeared.

The pulse continues at its forty-second interval, moving through everything, maintaining everything, asking nothing of you specifically.

The light is very bright and evenly distributed and comes from nowhere.

You stand in a shadow that points left.
