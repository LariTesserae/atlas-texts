# The Drowned Canopy

---

Begin with the water, because here the water is not a feature of the landscape — it *is* the landscape. Not ocean. Not flood. Something older than both, and stranger: an aerial sea suspended above the world's roof, a layer of cloud so saturated and so permanent that it has thickened past vapor, past mist, past fog, into something that must be called water while also being, technically, air. You move through it the way you move through a river shallows — with resistance, with pressure on your skin, with the sense that the medium has opinions about your passage. Light enters from above, through this hanging fluid, and arrives here already filtered, already softened, greenish and cool, the color of old glass found in a demolished building.

You are very high. The rock beneath everything — the actual lithic fact of the place — exists somewhere below perception, somewhere below the root systems, somewhere below the possibility of seeing it. What you stand on, if you stand at all, is plant. The substrate is alive. The floor is not stone or soil but a compression of moss so ancient and so thick that it has achieved something like geology — layers of it going down and down, each layer a previous generation of the same moss, compacted by the weight of what came after, the bottom layers no longer green but brown, no longer alive but not exactly dead, a peat of itself, an autobiography written in cellular matter, going back as far as anyone has thought to look and further. The surface layer is bright. Intensely, exhaustingly bright — the specific green of things that have been wet for ten thousand years and intend to continue.

From this floor, the trees rise.

Not densely. This is important and strange, the first strangeness you notice: for all that the vegetation here is total, for all that it constitutes *everything*, there is space between the trees. They rise from the moss-floor at intervals that feel almost chosen — not the crowding of a jungle, not a wall of trunks, but a spaced arrangement, as if each tree claimed its territory and maintained it across centuries without violence, without visible enforcement. Each one is enormous. They must be discussed individually, or not at all, because they do not read as a forest so much as a series of very large singular facts. Their trunks are wrapped and corded with growth — not parasitic, not competing with the tree itself, but *participating* in it, lichens and shelf fungi and climbing ferns and smaller mosses forming a secondary bark, a bark of living things adhered to the original bark, the whole assemblage so long-settled that it no longer looks like one thing growing on another but like a single agreed-upon object. The trunks are gray-green. They are thicker than houses. Their bark runs in long vertical channels, water-worn, water-smoothed, so that each trunk is a system of miniature rivers carrying the atmospheric water downward, always downward, always with the same sound.

The sound.

This must be addressed. The place is loud — not with intermittent noise but with continuous, volumetric, total sound. The water falling through the canopy above, striking the leaves of the canopy below, striking the shelf fungi, striking the aerial roots, striking the moss, running into the channels of the trunks, dripping from the lip of one leaf to the cup of another: it makes a sound that is not rain, technically, because there is no rain — no discrete drops falling from discrete clouds — but rather a uniform seeping of the entire atmosphere, a slow exsolution of the sky, every surface weeping steadily. At great distances this sound becomes a roar. Nearby it becomes something more complex, layered, rhythmic in ways that are not quite periodic, almost processable as music if you listen long enough and lose your sense of time. You do lose your sense of time here. There are no shadows moving. There is no wind to speak of, or rather wind is not a discrete event here but a permanent slight pressure from the west, always from the west, always the same strength, never varying, so that certain trees lean imperceptibly eastward and certain mosses grow denser on their western faces and the whole vast landscape has a subtle orientation, a lean to it, like a sentence with a grammatical direction even if you can't locate the verb.

Which brings us to the strangeness of the stillness.

---

Nothing here changes. Not nothing-much, not slowly — *nothing*. The place has arrived at a state of such complete equilibrium that time has become a formality. The moss does not appear to be growing. The trees do not appear to be growing. The atmospheric water seeps down and is taken up by roots and transpired back up through leaves into the permanent cloud above, completing a cycle so stable and so closed that the water has likely been the same water for an interval longer than ordinary geology tracks. The same molecules. Cycling. The animals —

The animals are real. They are here, and abundant, and they move, and this movement is the only evidence that time passes at all in any meaningful sense. Birds with very long legs step between the mossy hummocks. Their plumage is the gray-green of the lichen, and they are not camouflaged so much as they are *of* the place, colored by it, continuous with it. They make calls that are loud and complex and precisely the same calls, one presumes, they made long ago, calls shaped by and for this specific acoustic environment, this roaring-dripping vastness. Insects live in the channels of the tree-trunks, in the wet dark of the bark-layers, in the underside of every lifted edge of moss. Amphibians of various flatnesses press themselves to wet surfaces and may remain motionless for so long that moss begins to incorporate them at the margins, though they are alive, you can see the pulse at the throat, the blink of an eye behind a nictitating membrane, the slow and deliberate respiration of a body that has decided it is in no hurry. Among the roots, in the standing water that gathers in every basin and hollow, things move that are difficult to see and difficult to name — dark shapes, unhurried, the fauna of the permanent wet beneath.

None of it reads as predatory. None of it reads as desperate. The abundance here is not the abundance of a system in crisis, not the proliferation of a place with too much nutrient suddenly and not yet consumed. It is an old abundance. A settled one. Each creature in its niche so long that the niche has conformed to the creature and the creature to the niche in a mutual shaping that has long since reached its final shape. The birds do not seem to hunt. They seem to occupy their hunting. The amphibians do not seem to wait. They seem to inhabit waiting as a permanent condition, indistinguishable from being.

---

There is technology here.

You will not find it easily. The weirdness of the place, its specific strangeness, is this: the technology is the vegetation, or the vegetation has become the technology, or — and this is the explanation that feels truest when you stop resisting it — both of these things happened so long ago that the distinction has been composted by time, broken down, made into the same substance. The trees process. This is a word that arrives with weight. The trees *process*. Their root-systems conduct something that is not quite electrical but is not quite chemical, an exchange of signal and response moving through the mycorrhizal mesh in the permanent wet, through the interconnected root-mass, through the peat below, through the entire living-substrate-that-is-also-the-floor. It is not fast. It does not need to be fast. It has been running for long enough that speed is not a relevant metric. The outputs of this processing are the outputs of any old industrial process: heat (very slight, the moss-floor is fractionally warmer than the air), chemical product (certain seeps from between the tree-roots carry compounds that have no simple ecological explanation), and sound, sub-audible, below the frequency of the dripping, a tone that is felt before it is heard, a resonance that corresponds to nothing meteorological or biological in the conventional sense. An engine note. The engine of this place, the mechanism of its self-maintenance, its equilibrium-engine, running at a frequency it chose long ago and has not seen fit to revise.

Scaled production. Yes. On a continental scale — because the place is continental in scale, and in this scale its physics diverge from normal physics in small ways that accumulate into significant ones. The water-cycle here is not part of the planetary water-cycle. It is adjacent to it, tangled with it, but essentially a closed loop, an interior economy. The weather the place makes for itself is not weather in the usual sense, it does not travel, it does not arrive or depart, it is a standing weather, the same weather, the same cloud, perpetually regenerated. The light that reaches the moss-floor has been through more filtration than the same light elsewhere, emerging cooler, bluer, more ancient in tone, as though it too has been cycled through the system enough times to have acquired the system's qualities — patience, sufficiency, the quality of things that are exactly what they are and have been exactly this for longer than it is useful to contemplate.

---

Stand here and you will find that you are listening. Not for something specific. Listening *at* the place, as one listens at a door. The roar of the seeping. The bird-calls precisely spaced. The sub-audible tone of the root-engine. The zero wind, the same wind, the leaning permanence of the western pressure. The green-light. The gray-green of the trunks going up into the cloud-water-sky and losing themselves there, their canopies somewhere above in the saturated dimness, in the part of this place that is already water.

You will want to know what it's *for*. This is the wrong question, the question the place will not answer, not because it is withholding but because the question doesn't parse in this context. What the equilibrium is for. What the processing produces. Where the deep peat is going, over geological time, compressing toward what eventual transformation. The place does not explain itself and has never had to. It was not made for explaining. It does not distinguish between its machinery and its ecology and its geology because those distinctions require a temporal resolution that does not apply to something this stable. It is a process and it is a result. It is the factory and the thing the factory makes. It makes itself. It has been making itself for long enough that the making and the made are one thing, one entity, one extremely patient fact at elevation, wrapped in its own weather, processing in its own particular way, dripping, always dripping, the same water, down through the same leaves, into the same roots, ascending again, ascending, and the birds calling, and the whole thing *loud* with its own continuation.
