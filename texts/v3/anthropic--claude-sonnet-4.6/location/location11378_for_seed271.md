# The Yard Behind Something Forgotten

---

The ground here is packed earth, the color of old bread crust — a reddish-tan that has been this color for a long time and will remain it. The surface is not cracked. It is not soft. It has reached an agreement with its own dryness and settled there, decades ago probably, and nothing has disturbed the terms since. Walk on it and it gives nothing back; your footstep lands and is absorbed without comment.

The yard — and it is a yard, or something a yard-sized, a space with the feeling of enclosure without walls doing the enclosing — sits low in a shallow depression that may have once been the margin of something larger: a floodplain's hem, a dried tributary's last polite widening before it gave up and became soil. The surrounding land does not rise dramatically in any direction. It merely thickens, shifts register. You are at the bottom of a gentle nowhere, and the edges of this nowhere are a hundred steps in most directions, defined by a change in scrub-quality rather than any ridge or drop.

---

A few plants hold positions.

They do not cluster. They stand apart from one another the way objects left in an empty room seem to stand apart — each one complete in itself, surrounded by its own quiet, uninterested in what grows ten feet away. There is a low gray-green shrub with small stiff leaves, the kind that evolved to be inconvenient to eat. Its twigs are the color of bare wire. It has been this size for a long time; it will be this size for a long time more. A little distance away: a clump of dry grass, or what was grass, now more like a record of grass — pale stalks, the faintest amber, still upright though nothing requires them to be. Beyond that, not much. The ground between things is simply ground.

No flowers. No sign that flowers happened recently. The season, whatever it is, is one of the in-between ones — not recovering, not declining. Just resident.

---

The air is warm but not pressing. It sits against the skin at something like body temperature, so that the boundary between self and atmosphere becomes slightly unclear. This is not uncomfortable. It is more like stepping into a room where someone has been living quietly for a long time: the air is occupied but not by anything you can name. There is a faint mineral smell, something between stone and the memory of rain — the smell that condensation makes when it settles into packed earth and then mostly evaporates, leaving behind only the idea that water was here.

And it was here. Not long ago by the ground's reckoning. A brief rain, or a dew heavy enough to count for something, touched this place and then withdrew. The surface is not wet. It never got wet, exactly. But if you press your palm flat against the ground in the shadow of the shrub with the wire-colored twigs, the earth there carries a coolness that has not entirely gone. Moisture made it into the top half-inch and stopped. It will finish evaporating by the time the light changes, which is not soon.

---

The light is overcast — not dramatically, not with any visible drama of clouds piling or moving. Just a flat, even dimness, a sky the color of old paper. It casts no shadows. Objects simply are as bright as they are, which is not very bright. The shrub looks the same on all sides. The pale grass looks the same in every direction you approach from. The light gives you everything's true color without emphasis, without mood. It is the light of a place being described neutrally to itself.

It does not feel like dusk. It does not feel like morning. It feels like a kind of permanent midday that simply chose to be this quiet about it.

---

There is a wall, or most of a wall, at the far edge of the yard — maybe sixty feet away, maybe eighty. Hard to judge distances here without shadows to help. It is made of some material that was once more definitely one thing and is now becoming a slightly different thing: pale gray, rough, with the texture of something between old plaster and soft limestone. It is low — perhaps as high as a standing person's chest — and runs in a line for ten meters or so before it simply ends, not broken but concluded, as if the builder stopped there for a reason that no longer needs explaining.

No door in the wall. No gate. The wall doesn't enclose anything from this side; it just stands, oriented toward something that may no longer exist on the other side. Moss has not grown on it. The wall is too dry for moss, even accounting for the occasional moisture. Instead it has developed a faint pale skin — a mineral bloom, efflorescence, the slow migration of salts to the surface over many years of slight dampness and slight drying, slight damp and slight dry, until the wall has made something of its own out of the process. White traces on gray, no pattern, no figure. Just the record of what has slowly moved and what has slowly stayed.

No roof. No interior. Whatever was here — if there was more — is gone in the way things go when they go slowly: not collapsed, not burned, just gradually distinguished from the land until the land won.

---

The quiet here is not empty. It is a quality of the place, like the temperature — something you adjust to and then forget you've adjusted to. There is, occasionally, a sound that is not quite wind: a movement of air so slight it only announces itself by causing one of the pale grass stalks to shift minutely, once, and then return to stillness. No insect sounds. No birds. The absence of fauna is not eerie; it is simply part of the local conditions, the way certain places are simply not where animals congregate, not by violence or poison but by some subtler preference.

If you stay quiet yourself, you hear: the occasional, very faint, indeterminate sound that large quietnesses make. Whether this is air moving in small fractions, or the minute settling of warmed earth, or something the ears manufacture when given too little to work with — it doesn't matter. It occupies the same register as silence. It is silence, texturally.

---

This place does not feel abandoned so much as it feels *finished*. Not complete in the sense of having all its parts. Finished in the sense of having arrived at a form it intends to keep. The yard, the shrubs at their intervals, the wall with its salt-white tracings, the pressed earth: these have made their arrangement and stopped negotiating. They are in balance with their own erosion, which long ago slowed to a rate indistinguishable from stillness. The shallow depression holds; the plants hold their positions; the wall loses its salts at the rate it replenishes them from within.

You could come back in twenty years and need to look carefully for the differences. You might not find them.

---

Stand here long enough and the warmth of the air has a sedative quality. Nothing asks anything of you. The light doesn't shift. The scrub doesn't grow toward you or away from you. Even your own shadow, such as it is in this flat illumination, seems like a mild suggestion rather than a fact.

The place is not beautiful in any way that photographs. It is beautiful in the way that a particular quality of afternoon, in a particular unvisited part of a country you happened to be driving through, stays with you: not because anything happened there, but because the world, for a moment, seemed to be resting fully in its own form, without effort, without argument, without any particular need for you to have been there to confirm it.

You were there. The ground carries the faint cooling from the last rain. The grass stands at its angle.

This is what it has been doing.
