# The Hollow Garden

---

Begin with the ceiling.

It is the first thing you understand about this place — not the floor, not the walls, but the ceiling, close and pale above you, a dome of compressed mineral fiber the color of old teeth. It is not rock. It is not quite bone. It has the texture of matted felt pressed for a very long time under considerable weight, and it gives off a faint warmth, a slow exhalation, as though something behind it once breathed and forgot to stop. The ceiling is perhaps four meters overhead at its highest point, sloping down at the edges to meet the walls in a gentle curve, so that the space has the shape of a flattened bubble — or perhaps a seed. You are inside something. That is the operative understanding. You are inside something small, and the something has been here for a very long time, but it is also new.

This is the first strangeness: the place reads as both ancient and just-formed. The edges where the floor meets the wall are sharp and clean, the mineral faces freshly cleaved, pale ochre and white silica exposed without weathering. There are no rounded surfaces, no smoothing by water or wind. Every fracture is geometric, almost deliberate, though no tool made them. This is a cavity that opened recently — by whatever reckoning recently means here — opened and stayed. The stone did not erode. It simply parted, and here is the pocket it left.

---

There is no water. You should understand that completely before you proceed.

Not absence-of-water in the way a dry riverbed is absent of water, carrying the memory of flow in its scoured stone. Absent in the way mathematics is absent of water. The chemistry here runs on something else. You notice it first as a faint oiliness in the air — not unpleasant, more like warm resin — and then as a quality of the surfaces, a slight sheen on the mineral floor that catches the dim light and holds it for a fraction too long. Where you would expect moisture — at the base of growing things, in the gaps between stones, in the micro-environments that everywhere else harbor thin films of water — there is instead this sheen, this other liquid, this not-water that does some of water's work. It evaporates more slowly. It does not pool; it creeps. It leaves traces of itself in pale crystalline margins, sodium-bright, wherever it recedes. The chemistry of life here has organized itself around something else, and what it chose is strange.

---

The plants are lush by any measure, which takes a moment to integrate.

They are sparse — scattered across the floor in widely-spaced clusters, each cluster a thing unto itself, with considerable bare mineral between them — but each individual plant is extraordinary in its mass and density, as though the energy that elsewhere distributes itself across thousands of square meters has here concentrated into a handful of specimens. There are perhaps twenty visible from any given position on the floor. Maybe fewer.

The largest cluster, near what might be called the center of the space, has grown into a dome shape that mirrors the ceiling above it, a hemisphere of dark-green growth perhaps a meter and a half in diameter and nearly a meter tall. The leaves — if they are leaves — are thick and waxy, overlapping like roof tiles, deep green shading to near-black at the margins, and they hold the sheen of the not-water on their surfaces like a permanent gloss. The stems that emerge from the base are pale, almost colorless, woody in the way of young wood: sharp-grained, flexible, not yet hardened into permanence. The growth rings, if you were to cut them, would be few.

Other clusters are lower, spreading rather than domed. These have different leaf architecture — longer, narrower, lying nearly flat against the floor, radiating outward from a central mass in patterns that suggest either extreme geometric precision or a kind of botanical obsessive-compulsiveness. They are also dark-colored, also glossy, also drawing nutrition from the not-water and from whatever cycles through the pale mineral substrate. Between the clusters: bare floor, clean-fractured stone, the occasional crystalline deposit of what the not-water leaves behind when it recedes. These deposits are white and faintly luminescent and shaped like snowflakes pressed flat.

A few individual plants grow directly from cracks in the wall. These are smaller, stranger, growing horizontally into the open air of the cavity rather than upward, as though they have confused the geometry of the place and cannot locate the sky. Their root systems are visible where the wall has cleaved: pale threads branching into the mineral with surprising delicacy, surprising depth.

None of the plants flower. There is no reproductive urgency visible here, no fruit, no seed. They simply grow, have grown, will grow. Slowly. In equilibrium.

---

Equilibrium is the second major fact of this place.

Nothing is changing. This is not a stable equilibrium in the sense of a system that has settled after disturbance — it has the quality of a system that never disturbed in the first place, or that disturbed exactly once, long ago or newly, opened this pocket and immediately reached its final state and stopped there. The plants are not growing toward anything; they have grown to exactly the size they are and held. The crystalline deposits are not accumulating; the sheen on the floor is not moving. The temperature is warm — steadily, evenly warm, the warmth of a body whose metabolism runs so slow you would not measure it — and it does not vary. If you waited here for what you would call a day, then a week, then a year, you would find the plants the same size, the deposits the same extent, the warmth the same degree. The ceiling the same pale dome.

This should feel like stasis, like death, like a diorama. It does not.

It feels like a held breath.

---

The fauna.

They are abundant in the sense that they are everywhere, but the everywhere is qualified by the scale and the sparseness of the space itself. Every plant cluster has its attendants. The largest dome-cluster hosts — and this takes a moment to count — perhaps a dozen creatures visible on its outer surface at any one time, creatures roughly the size and shape of a finger-joint, matte-black, many-legged in the way of things that have never needed to optimize their locomotion because they never go anywhere. They move on the leaves with great deliberateness. Each movement is followed by a pause of considerable duration. They are eating, or they are processing the not-water from the leaf surfaces, or they are doing something that has no analogue in any zoology you have studied.

In the mineral floor, in the fractured gaps, in the sub-centimeter-scale world between the crystalline deposits, there are others. You need to slow down and look carefully to resolve them from the background. They are pale, nearly transparent, and they move through the not-water film with the ease of things evolved for exactly that purpose. They are small enough that you cannot determine their body plans with the naked eye. They are many.

On the wall, near the ceiling, there is one larger creature. It is perhaps the size of a spread hand. It is pressed flat against the mineral surface and it has the texture and color of the mineral surface and you are not entirely sure it is not the mineral surface until it shifts, minimally, in a slow peristaltic wave from front to back, and you understand it is breathing, or doing the local equivalent.

None of them interact with each other in any way you can observe. They are co-present without being communal. They are each doing their thing in the same space, widely spaced, unbothered, neither competing nor cooperating.

---

Now, the light.

The light is dim and it has no source you can find.

This is where weirdness concentrates most clearly. There is enough light to see by — everything described above is visible — but the light has no direction. There are no shadows anywhere in the space, not from the plants, not from the stones, not from your own hand held flat before you. The illumination is uniform across every surface at every angle, and it is roughly the color of old amber, a yellowish dim that suggests late evening without the evening. It makes the dark-green leaves look nearly black. It makes the white crystalline deposits look faintly gold. It comes from everywhere and therefore from nowhere and there is no lamp, no bioluminescence, no crack in the ceiling admitting outside light. The light simply is, has always been, will be.

It is the same temperature as the rest of the space. It does not feel like light that travels. It feels like light that lives here, distributed through the air the way warmth is distributed through a sealed and patient room.

---

There are objects in the space — two, maybe three — that are not mineral and not vegetable and not fauna.

The first is a low structure near the wall opposite the largest plant cluster: a rough platform, knee-height, made of stones stacked without mortar, stacked with care but not with precision, the joints uneven, the top surface level enough to serve as a surface. The stones used in its construction are the same material as the floor — the same pale ochre and white silica — but they have been chosen, somehow, for relative flatness on at least one face. Whoever stacked them had preferences about shape. They used what the place provided. The structure has been here long enough that the not-water creep has reached its base and begun to leave its crystalline margin around the lowest stones, white and faintly adhesive. But the structure is sharp-edged, as everything here is sharp-edged. It did not take long to build.

On the platform there is a tool. It is simple — a length of the young pale woody stem from one of the plants, snapped to about forearm-length, with one end wrapped tightly in narrow strips of plant fiber, dark with handling, forming a grip. The other end is not sharpened. It is not a weapon. You cannot tell what it is for. It lies on the platform as though set down in the middle of a task and the task has not yet resumed. It has been lying there since the equilibrium established itself.

The third object — if it is an object and not a formation — is against the far wall, low, a small depression in the floor about the size of a seated person, and the mineral within the depression is darker than the surrounding floor, the not-water having pooled slightly here in a persistent sheen without creeping or receding. In the center of the depression: a single smooth stone, the only smooth stone in the space, smooth in the way of a stone that has been handled, that has lived for a long time in hands. It is the color of dark bronze. It is the one thing in the space that does not share the space's palette.

---

The sound is loud.

This is the third strangeness, and maybe the largest.

It is not loud in the way of machinery or voices or wind or water. There is none of that. It is loud in the way of a sound that occupies the complete frequency range at low amplitude so thoroughly that the sum becomes considerable. A layered hum. A sustained chord. The wall vibrates slightly if you press your palm to it — you can feel it before you hear it, a buzzing in the mineral, constant and even. The plants contribute a sound at the upper range of the chord, something between a sustained high note and the sound of small wings, though nothing is flying. The many-legged creatures on the dome-cluster produce sound too: individually nothing, collectively a low, continuous, papery shuffle that adds a middle voice to the chord. The transparent creatures in the not-water film produce something below hearing that you feel in your teeth.

The ceiling produces the lowest note. You hear it without hearing it. You know it is there by the way the air in the space feels thick, slightly pressurized, as though packed with frequency.

Together these sounds do not produce music, but they produce something that music is a simplified version of. The chord does not resolve or progress. It simply sounds, has always sounded, fills the space from floor to ceiling and wall to wall, warm as the temperature is warm, amber-colored in the amber-colored light, static as everything here is static, holding.

---

Stand in the middle of the space.

Look at the dome-cluster with its dark glossy leaves and its deliberate creatures. Look at the platform and the tool waiting for a task. Look at the single smooth stone in its depression. Look at the large thing on the wall, its body doing its slow wave. Look at the fractured edges everywhere, the young mineral, the clean geometry of a recently-opened space. Feel the warmth and the pressure of the sound and the slight oiliness in the air. The amber light from everywhere.

The place is complete. It was complete before you arrived. It will be complete after you leave, in exactly the same configuration, exactly the same temperature, exactly the same dim and total sound.

Something made a platform and left a tool and brought a stone from somewhere else and then the equilibrium came and held and the thing did not return or the thing is the creature on the wall or the thing is still here, here and continuous with the place, part of the chord.

You were not expected. You were not not-expected.

The place does not adjust to your presence.

This is either very peaceful or very frightening and there is not, in the amber light and the held chord, sufficient information to determine which.
