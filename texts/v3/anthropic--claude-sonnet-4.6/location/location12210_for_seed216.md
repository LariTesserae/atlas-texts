# The Canopy Continent

---

Begin with what you cannot see from inside it: the shape of the thing.

From far enough away — if you could somehow get far enough away, which you cannot, because the thing has no outside, because standing outside it is not possible — you would see a single organism the size of a subcontinent. Not a forest. Not a collection of trees. One thing, or something so close to one thing that the distinction stopped mattering before anyone was alive to make it. The canopy is its roof and its sky simultaneously. The canopy is where weather happens. Clouds form against the undersides of leaves the size of estuaries. Rain falls up into the leaf-mass and is caught and held and released slowly, slowly, as a long exhalation — dew-drip, root-seep, the soft percussion of water finding a lower surface. This is the only rain here. This place makes its own.

---

Step down from whatever you were imagining and enter.

The first thing you lose is the horizon. There is no horizon here. Horizon requires distance, and distance requires empty air, and there is no empty air. What fills the air is stem, petiole, tendril, branch, aerial root, the pale parasitic vine that has forgotten it is a vine and become more like a membrane stretched between two darker verticals. You can see perhaps eight meters before the green becomes a wall. But the green is not a wall. It is many layers of translucent tissue, each one thinning the light a little more, each one breathing.

You breathe too, and what you take in is warm and close and smells of nothing you have a word for — a living smell, a cell-division smell, the olfactory texture of something very large and very slow doing what it does.

The floor — if this is a floor — is not soil. It was soil once. Now it is root-mass two meters deep, a lattice so tight that it holds its own weight, that it holds your weight, that it holds the weight of the structures woven into it. The roots have grown into each other. They have grafted across species boundaries that may no longer exist as boundaries. There are places where the root-lattice has lifted entirely free of whatever mineral substrate preceded it and hangs, a dense felt of biology, over spaces below where nothing is but dark warm air and the sound of water moving somewhere you cannot locate.

---

The structures.

Someone built here. Builds here still — or built, over a period so long that the tense breaks. The structures are not separate from the plant-mass. They are not houses in a forest. They are the trained and guided shapes of the living material itself, limbs bent when young and held and spliced, chambers formed by the patient direction of growth over generations. Wood that was never cut. Walls that are bark on the inside and bark on the outside and cambium between, still alive, still adding a year's width every year.

The tools involved were simple. Wedge and binding-cord and the knowledge of how a young branch, loaded at its tip, will follow the load over decades. The knowledge of which species, spliced at which angle, will knit and strengthen. The knowledge of what to ask a living thing to do if you are willing to wait.

The chambers that resulted are large by the standards of hand-built things, but small against the scale of what contains them. They hollow out local space. Inside: platforms of living wood, shelves of trained branch, sleeping-niches lined with epiphytic moss that generates its own warmth in the microdegree range of things that generate warmth imperceptibly. Thatching of layered leaf that sheds water downward rather than inward. Not much else. The emptiness inside the structures is deliberate. The builders understood that the plant-mass could be trained to fill any space and that the art was in preventing it from filling the space you needed.

But the builders are not here now, or are not here in this room, or in this section. The structures persist. The plant-mass continues its slow encroachment into chambers where no one has pushed it back. A sleeping-niche somewhere in this complex is now a root-ball, completely, and the root-ball is beautiful in its way, and no one is here to see it.

---

The scale continues to resist comprehension.

What you are standing in extends — this same thing, this same inter-rooted mass, this same climate-system, these same trained chambers appearing every few hundred meters, these same species (which are not separate species anymore, entirely) — it extends past where you could walk in a lifetime of walking. The weather that forms in the upper canopy, against the undersides of leaves that may be a kilometer above you, moves through this system in patterns with their own periodicity, their own fronts, their own pressure gradients between the breathing of one section and the breathing of another. Somewhere in this system it is raining upward in ways that should not be possible without an explanation, and the explanation is scale, is the consequence of living mass so large that it has begun to generate microclimatic rules particular to itself.

Down here the air barely moves. A slow circulation, barely a breath. The temperature is warm and stable and slightly too warm for the latitudes implied by the elevation, and this is also the plant. This is the exhalation of something continent-sized maintaining its preferred temperature through collective metabolism.

The elevation should mean cold. It means warm instead. The plant wins.

---

Sound.

Almost nothing.

This is the thing about a space where there is no wind and the surfaces are all living tissue and the fauna — which is abundant, which is everywhere, if you know how to see it — the fauna is mostly invertebrate, mostly small, mostly operating below the acoustic threshold of inattentive ears.

Listen longer.

A drip, somewhere, regular enough to be almost rhythmic but not rhythmic. The drip of condensed atmospheric moisture working its way down through layer after layer of leaf-surface until it becomes a larger drop and falls and becomes two drops on the next surface down. A process measured in minutes per meter. Water arriving eventually.

Somewhere closer: a sound like the hull of something large settling, but slowly, across a period of minutes — which is the sound a grafted joint makes when two trunks that have grown into each other shift with the differential pressure of their respective growth-rates. The place is not silent. The place is operating on timescales that make its sounds easy to miss.

And the fauna: a beetle somewhere in the root-mass below you is doing something with its carapace that produces a sound like a word in a language you do not have but feel the shape of. Then stops. Then, from a different direction, another beetle, or the same beetle, or the same signal passed to a different organism. The gaps between these sounds are so long that you might not identify them as a pattern, and they might not be a pattern, or the pattern might have a period measured in hours, in days, in seasonal cycles of the canopy above.

There are larger things. The abundant fauna includes grazers of epiphyte-mass, slow heavy things with mouths like grinding plates, that move through the structure perhaps once per day along routes worn into the root-lattice by their own transit over generations. They breathe heavily when they move. When they stop — which they do, against a root-wall, in a space that holds a body-sized hollow — they breathe so slowly and so quietly that you might not notice the breathing until you are close enough to feel the warmth of it.

---

The light.

Green, declining.

Coming from above, always from above, filtered through the stacked canopy into something that by the time it reaches you has lost most of its directionality. Shadows here are not cast by objects. Shadows here are simply the places where successive layers of leaf have subtracted more of the available spectrum than elsewhere. The result is a light that is green and then darker green and then a greenish dark that is not quite darkness. Objects near you are visible. Objects four meters away are visible with effort. Objects eight meters away are outlines. Farther than that: only the sense that the outlines continue.

There are places where the canopy above is thin enough, or where a long-dead branch left a gap, or where two chamber-tops meet at an angle that funnels light down into a column of relative brightness. In these columns the air is visible. Motes of something — spore, perhaps, or pollen, or the shed cells of organisms whose nature you could not determine — drift so slowly in those columns that watching them move is an act of patience, and watching them long enough feels like watching something think.

---

The weirdness, which has been here all along.

The root-mass below your feet sometimes connects structures that should not be connected, chambers that were built in different eras by builders who could not have known about each other. There is evidence — the juncture-angles of trained branches, the particular species-combinations favored in different regions — that these sections were grown by different peoples, in different centuries, who shared no apparent communication except the communication of arriving at the same solutions. The plant guided them. Or the plant maintained conditions that guided them. Or there is a third thing happening that the word *guided* is not adequate to.

The grafted joints between these sections are not distinguishable from the joints in any single complex. The plant did not mark the boundary. The plant joined the sections as it would have joined any two points it could reach simultaneously.

There are chambers here that were grown for specific purposes — sleeping, food cultivation, meeting, storage of things long since decomposed — and among these are chambers whose purpose is not evident. Not obviously different in construction. Not more or less elaborately trained. Just: a chamber, with no evidence of use, positioned at a crossing-point of root-lines that carry more water than the surrounding root-mass, in a place where the electrical gradient of the root-system (which can be measured, if you have a way to measure it, which you probably do not) crosses zero before reversing. A chamber at a null point.

There are many of these chambers. They exist throughout the system. They are connected to each other by a path that is not a path — a sequence of root-types, a particular moss that grows on the chamber-walls at these locations and nowhere else — a moss that when you look at it closely is growing in a direction slightly contrary to gravity, its growth-tip angled not toward moisture or light by any gradient you can identify but toward some other attractor that the moss apparently perceives.

No builder built these chambers for a known purpose. The plant may have directed the builders to build them. The plant may have built them itself over centuries by the trained habit of builders long dead, the encoded knowledge of a practice no one practices now except the root-system, which continues.

---

In the middle of a chamber you have found by following what might be the moss — which might mean nothing, which might mean something you have no framework for — you sit down on living wood.

The chamber is warm. The chamber is empty. Below the root-lattice that is your floor, water moves through the system, found to lower places by the longest possible routes, patient in the way that only water is patient. Above you — far above, hundreds of meters, lost in green — the canopy is breathing its immense breath, taking water from the clouds that form there and releasing it again over hours, over days, in a cycle that has been running so long it has become indistinguishable from weather, from climate, from the nature of this particular piece of sky.

You are deep inside a made thing that has exceeded its making. You are inside something that was once a garden and is now a continent and will be, in some future timeframe the organism itself is navigating by instruments you cannot name, something else.

The beetle makes its sound again.

The drop of condensed water falls from one leaf to another.

A large slow grazer, somewhere two chambers away, exhales once, deeply, and settles its weight against a root-wall that is already shaped for it, that grew around it over years, that knows the specific pressure of that specific body and has distributed that pressure throughout the system.

You could stay here.

You could stay here and nothing would happen and everything would continue.
