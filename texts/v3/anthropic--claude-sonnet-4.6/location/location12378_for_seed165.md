# The Plateau of Persistent Thunder

---

Begin with the sound, because the sound arrives before anything else does.

It is not thunder exactly, though thunder is the closest word the mind reaches for. It is a sound that lives in the chest cavity before it reaches the ears — a low, continuous detonation spread across the full width of the sky, pressed down from above as though the atmosphere has weight and the weight is vibratory. The sound does not peak and fade. It does not arrive from a direction. It is simply the condition of the air here, the way the air *is*, the same as cold is the way the air is and thin is the way the air is. The sound was making this sound before you came. It will continue after you leave. Whether you have left or not is not a consideration the sound entertains.

---

You are very high.

The numbers attached to this place suggest extremity of elevation, and the body confirms it before the mind can organize the confirmation. The breath goes short in a way that isn't panic but resembles panic's texture. The pressure around the skull feels adjusted, recalibrated to a different standard. The horizon, when you find it — and it takes time to find it, because the horizon here is not where horizons usually are — the horizon is *below* you, or at least beside you in a way that implies below, so that the edge of the world is at eye level or underneath it, and the sky begins not overhead but at your feet.

The plateau extends in all four directions and additional directions that don't have cardinal names. It is regional in scale, which means it takes days to walk across, which means most of it is permanently elsewhere, which means what you stand on is the merest suggestion of the thing. The plateau is granite and limestone in long ancient slabs, tilted by some previous catastrophe that is now so thoroughly previous that it reads as geology rather than event. The slabs are grey. They are every shade of grey that grey can be subdivided into — ash-grey, blue-grey, the grey of old iron, the grey of ice seen through fog, the grey of bone left in weather. They lie at angles to each other, these slabs, separated by cracks the width of a hand or the width of a body or the width of something between those two measurements. The cracks go down into darkness. How far down is not a question the cracks are interested in answering.

The erosion here is not active erosion, the kind with ambition, the kind still deciding what shape things should be. The erosion here is completed erosion, settled into its conclusions. Every edge that was going to be rounded has been rounded. Every weak column that was going to fall has fallen, long ago, and the rubble has itself been rounded, and the rounded rubble lies where it fell and has not moved since, will not move, rests at the angles that physics determined for it and physics is done revising. When you look at a boulder here you are not looking at a boulder mid-process. You are looking at a boulder that has achieved boulderdom and stopped.

---

The cold is not the biting cold of winter storms. It is the background cold, the cold beneath all other temperatures, the cold that exists between events. It is perhaps four degrees above some threshold you'd prefer not to breach. The air itself holds still — there should be wind at this altitude, there is no wind, the air hangs in suspension, temperature-invariant, a permanent almost-freeze that never commits to freezing because freezing would be a change and change is not the operative mode here. Your breath mists in small clouds that disperse immediately. The condensation on the rock surfaces — and there is condensation, a faint damp sheen on every horizontal face, on the upper curves of boulders, in the declensions of the cracks — was there this morning, was there last year, will be there when the concept of last year has expanded to include enormous quantities of time.

The dampness is intermittent only in the spatial sense, not the temporal. There is no intermittent rain because there is no intermittent anything — but there are pockets, low places between slabs where water has collected in quantities ranging from a dark stain to a genuine pool, finger-deep, sky-colored, perfectly still. These pools do not drain. They do not overflow. They sit at their established levels and reflect the grey above with fidelity so complete it becomes strange, so that you could stand between two pools and look at the sky in both of them and question which of the three skies is primary, and the question would not be rhetorical.

---

The vegetation is: almost not.

In the cracks, where the darkness gives way to a little accumulated soil — dark stuff, thin, more mineral than organic — there are plants of the kind that have stopped trying to be plants in any ambitious sense. Lichens in patches of orange and grey-green and black, slow as continents, indifferent as rock. Occasional mats of something mossy that survives by being mostly water itself, by committing fully to the medium. Where a crack is sheltered from the emptiness above, a low shrub might occur — low meaning almost flush with stone, meaning wind-shaped even in a place with no wind, meaning wind-shaped by memory or by instruction received from wind elsewhere. These shrubs are grey-green, leafless in their outermost tips, the leaves reduced over generations to something between a scale and a spine. They hold their forms. They have held their forms for long enough that holding form is what they are.

There is no grass. There is nothing that nods or waves or gestures toward the sky. The plants here are not reaching. They are maintaining.

---

The fauna is rare in the sense that it is nearly absent, and the absence is not the absence of animals that have left. It is the absence of animals that have never committed to presence. Occasionally — meaning once in several hours, meaning maybe twice across an entire day of walking — a bird appears: large, dark, moving against the grey sky in a long unhurried arc, wings barely flexing, reading the thermals with a patience that suggests it has all the time that time provides. The bird does not call. The bird makes no sound that competes with the sound the air is already making. It curves away and becomes a point and the point becomes nothing and the sky restores to its uniform grey.

There are other animals in the possibility-space of this place. Small things in the cracks: a beetle encountered once, black and deliberate, crossing a bare stone face with the confidence of something that has taken this route before and will take it again and the route has not changed and will not change. You are not in its consideration. You are passing through. It is not passing through. The distinction matters to the beetle in ways you can't fully access.

---

The built things are: one cairn.

It is visible from approximately two hundred meters away, which is a distance at which it is not recognizable as a cairn and is instead a shape that doesn't belong to the grammar of the plateau, a verticality amid all the horizontal-leaning sprawl of the slabs. When you get closer it resolves: stones stacked by hands, six or eight stones, each placed on the one below with some attention to balance. The stones are the same stones as all the other stones — grey, rounded, weathered to their final forms. The cairn is not new. It is not old in any dramatic sense; it is old in the sense of having been here long enough that the wind, if there were wind, would have toppled it, and it stands anyway, which means there is no wind, which confirms what the air has been telling you. Who built it is not available information. Why they built it is not available information. The stones are placed, and the placement is the whole of what remains.

Some distance from the cairn, mostly hidden by a tilted slab, there is a wall or the beginning of a wall — three or four courses of stone, dry-laid, making a right angle that implies an interior that no longer exists or perhaps never existed in a completed sense. The wall is not ruined. It is simply partial. It seems always to have been this way, which is one of the qualities this place instills: the sense that everything here has always been exactly as it is, that none of it was ever different, that the partial wall was partial from its first moment, that the rounded boulders were born rounded, that the pools were filled before there was water to fill them.

---

Here is where the description must become careful, because what happens next is not a departure from the rules of the place but rather the revelation of what the rules have been all along.

The sound is continuous and loud and the sound is the sound of something like standing waves in the atmosphere, a resonance mode that the plateau has established with the sky above it — or the sky has established with the plateau — the directionality of causation is not clear. The sound makes itself in the space between stone and cloud, using the air as its instrument, and the air is entirely willing. But the sound has a side effect, which is that it troubles the boundary between where sound is and where sound isn't, which is the same as saying it troubles the boundary between here and not-here, which is the same as saying it troubles the boundary between is and is-not.

The pools reflect the sky. This is normal for pools.

But the sky reflected in the pools is not always the sky above the pools.

In some pools — the larger ones, the ones with more depth to their darkness — the sky is the right color but wrong in its particulars. The grey is grey but it contains a light that the overhead grey does not contain: a reddish tinge, or a blue that is not the blue of anything currently present, or a darkness that exceeds what the clouds overhead are capable of producing. You look up, you look down. The sky above the pool and the sky in the pool are in conversation but the conversation is not translation, not reflection, something else — parallel commentary, two accounts of related events that share no witnesses.

This is not disturbing, exactly. It does not produce fear. It produces the specific disorientation of discovering that a rule you'd assumed universal applies only regionally, and you have left the region of its application.

---

The plateau does not change.

This is the central fact and the central strangeness. You have been here — in the description, in the imagining of being here — for what might be a day or might be longer, and nothing has moved except you and the bird and the beetle and your breath-clouds, and the breath-clouds dispersed immediately, and the bird is gone, and the beetle is elsewhere in its crack. The light has not shifted. The sound has not varied. The condensation on the stone surfaces was there when you arrived and maintains its level. The pools hold their water. The partial wall is partial. The cairn stands.

The cold does not deepen toward night because there is no deepening here, no gradient toward anything, no vector of change pressing through the moment toward another moment. The grey light is not the grey light of morning or the grey light of late afternoon. It is the grey light of a time of day that has settled on a compromise between all times of day and stopped there.

You could leave and come back in a year and find it exactly as it is. You could come back in a century. You could imagine the plateau in whatever deep past preceded human imagining and find it already established, already in this configuration, the slabs already at these angles, the water in the cracks already at this depth, the sound already sounding.

---

The weirdness does not announce itself. This is its primary characteristic. It doesn't show up against the background because it *is* the background. The world here isn't broken; it's running a different version, one that predates or postdates or simply runs alongside the version you're used to, the one with sequential time and unambiguous reflections and spaces that contain roughly what they should contain given their volume.

The space between objects here contains more than it should.

Not more in the way of hidden things — not more in the way that darkness might conceal a presence. More in the sense that the air between two boulders, a distance of perhaps three meters, takes longer to cross than three meters should take. Longer by a small amount that you might explain away as wind-resistance, as altitude-affecting-judgment, as the heaviness of the cold. But if you cross back the other way, it takes the same amount of time. The space is not stretched. The space is *full* — full of whatever space is full of when it's been alone long enough. Full of its own precedent. Full of having been this way, this empty, this quiet under the noise, for longer than anything around it has been anything.

The emptiness here is not a lack. The emptiness here is the whole point of the place. The vast space between presences — between stones, between birds, between pools, between yourself and the edge of the plateau you can see from here, which is either a kilometer away or three, the distance being unreliable — is the medium through which the rules of the place propagate. The emptiness carries the sound. The emptiness carries the strangeness of the reflections. The emptiness is where the extra time lives.

---

Stand still long enough and you will stop being sure you are changing.

Not that you will stop changing — you will stop being sure. The cold is invariant. The sound is invariant. The light is invariant. The plateau extends in all directions in the same grey stone with the same pattern of cracks and the same occasional low shrub and the same sheen of condensation. You are the only non-invariant thing present. And in a place of sufficient invariance, the non-invariant thing begins to doubt itself, begins to feel the pressure of the equilibrium, the way a pool of water placed in a still space will eventually find its level and stop moving, the way everything here has found its level and stopped.

You are not sure your breath-cloud just appeared or has always been there.

You are not sure the pool twenty meters to your left has always been on your left or if you have rotated and it is now on a different side that is also your left.

You are not sure if the sound changed just now or if you simply noticed it differently.

These uncertainties are not alarming. They are the place teaching you its grammar, showing you the tense it operates in, which is not past or present or future but something that contains all three without distinguishing between them, a tense in which the cairn is being stacked and has always been stacked and will always be stacked, in which the bird is passing overhead and has always been passing and the arc it makes is permanent, inscribed in the grey sky, a record and a current event and a prophecy simultaneously.

---

Eventually you will leave. The plateau will not register this.

The sound will continue. The pools will hold their water. The condensation will maintain its level on the upper curves of the grey boulders. The partial wall will be partial. The cold will be exactly as cold. The lichen will pursue its territorial ambitions at the rate of millimeters per decade, which is the only rate available, which is the rate it has always pursued, which is the rate that remains.

The bird — or the arc where the bird is, or the arc where the bird always is — will curve across the sky and become a point and the point will wait, at the edge of visibility, at the edge of the distance that the plateau provides, which is a long distance, which is a distance that contains significant quantities of the grey empty air in which the sound lives.

It will wait.

It will not be waiting for anything.

It will simply be the bird at the edge of its arc, present in the permanent way that everything here is present: not contingent, not temporary, not subject to before-and-after but simply *in-place*, in-place in the way that stone is in-place and sound is in-place and the reality-adjacent-to-reality that flickers in the still surfaces of the pools is in-place.

You came.

The plateau continued.

You will go.

The plateau will continue.

Between these facts there is a space, and the space is cold, and the space is loud, and the space is grey, and in the space there are pools that reflect the sky, and the sky they reflect is close but not identical to the sky above them, and neither sky is wrong, and there is no one here to adjudicate, and adjudication is not a service the plateau requires.
