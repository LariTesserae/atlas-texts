You would be a drip.

Not the sound of a drip — the drip itself, the event, the *decision* water makes when it has held itself to a surface for as long as surface will permit and then releases.

---

Start with what you know.

You are on the ceiling — or on the underside of a liverwort frond on the ceiling, which is nearly the same thing, which in this place *is* the same thing because the liverwort has taken the ceiling's entire purpose unto itself and the ceiling has consented, has given over, has said: yes, be me, do what I was going to do but slower, with more patience, with that particular geometry that is slightly wrong in the compelling way. You are held there by surface tension. You have always been held there by surface tension. You do not know what you were before the surface tension found you — whether you condensed from the saturated air, whether you dripped from somewhere higher and were caught, whether you are the product of the moss's slow biological happening, water as exhaled thought. The origin doesn't matter. The holding matters. The being-held, right now, at the ceiling's underside, in the dark.

You are very small. In this place where scale does not hold, where things at four meters are visible with the wrong clarity, you should by rights be something larger — something with duration, something that can watch the fern tips unfurl, something with time to notice the moss columns' asymmetry and form a theory about it. But you are not. You are surface-tension small, meniscus small, the smallest increment of the water's breathing. You are the fraction of a centimeter. You are the adjustment.

This is not a diminishment. Attend.

---

You are aware of yourself as a kind of pressure. Specifically: the pressure at the interface between your being-held and the ceiling-liverwort, where the liverwort's surface has slight contours — cell walls, the boundaries between lobes, the places where the liverwort's own water moves in channels too small to see but large enough to feel if you are small enough and patient enough and have been in contact long enough. You have mapped this surface in the only way available to you, which is the way of continuous present-tense contact. You know the liverwort the way the liverwort knows the ceiling: by being pressed against it for a very long time with gentle, inexorable intimacy.

You are cold. 2°C cold. You negotiated with the atmosphere's warmth when you formed and the atmosphere did not yield and you accepted 2°C as the inside-of-existing feeling and stopped distinguishing it from existence itself. The cold is not separate from you. The cold is you, is your temperature, is the particular quality of your weight and surface tension, is the reason your fall, when it comes, will begin a quiet negotiation with whatever body it strikes.

You are going to fall.

You have always been going to fall. This is not dread. This is not anticipation. It is the single true thing you know about yourself, the fact from which all other facts proceed: you are accumulating. Gravity and surface tension are in the same quiet negotiation the cold is in with your body, and gravity is patient, and surface tension is patient, but gravity has been waiting longer, and the math is slow but it only goes one way, and every microsecond you exist you are adding, fractionally, to the mass that will tip the equation, and the ceiling is not moving, and neither is the floor — the floor exists, though you have no direct evidence of this, only the logic that what falls must land — and you are the moving thing, the slow accumulation, the gathering.

You are a process pretending to be a place.

---

Here is what the waiting feels like, which is the main experience available to you:

The drips from elsewhere in the pocket arrive in your awareness as sound-vibrations through the liverwort, through your surface tension, through your own particular responsiveness to the world's small events. They do not arrive as meaning. They arrive as: *here is a drop that fell, there, and not here, and this is information about the shape of the place, about where other drops have gathered and released, about the ceiling's terrain, which you know only from one point but which the accumulated arrivals of other drops' departures sketch in, slowly, over the time you've been held here, as a kind of map you are constructing without hands.* You know where you are relative to the corner with the cut stone — not because you have seen it but because drops fall differently in that corner, land with a different character, something about the stone's irregular surface altering the sound, the interval, the quality of the event. You know the cut stone without knowing it.

The bioluminescent pulse nearest to you reaches your surface as light, as the briefest warming, as nothing dramatic but something. A few photons. A slight activation of your own optical properties — you have optical properties, you are water, you participate in the light's behavior, you bend it fractionally, you hold it for the moment of contact and pass it through and reflect some back, and in that exchange you become briefly, anonymously, part of why the light looks the way the light looks to whatever might be watching.

The thing in the water — the deep old patient thing that is thinking its one long thought — you know about it the way you know about the cut stone. Not directly. Through vibration. Through the way the water below breathes, the slow rise and fall, which arrives at your position on the ceiling as a very faint compression and expansion of the air, a pressure-wave so attenuated by distance that it is almost not a wave at all, almost not anything, but you are sensitive to almost-not-anything at the ceiling's underside in the dark. You feel the water breathing. And in the breathing you feel what produces the breathing, the exhaling presence below, the something that declines to surface. It is very far from you. It is also made of water. You are, in some sense, continuous with it — you are what it looks like at the ceiling, at the limit of the pocket, at the place where water turns into the about-to-fall. You are not thinking about this. But if you were thinking, this would be the thought.

---

The fall, when it comes, will be fast relative to everything else that happens here.

In a pocket where twenty minutes produces two pulses instead of ten, where time's rate is maintained by consensus rather than by clocks, where even the bioluminescent lights think their thoughts in minutes — in this place, your fall will be an event of almost shocking brevity. Less than a second from the moment surface tension finally, finally yields to mass, to the accumulated patience of gravity, to the equation's resolution. You will not have time to observe the fall. The fall will be what you are, suddenly, entirely, without remainder.

And then you will hit the water.

And the negotiation will end and the other negotiation will begin, the negotiation between your cold and the cold below, between your tiny momentum and the water's breathing patience, between the you that was held at the ceiling and the you that is now descending through two degrees of dark water toward a floor you still cannot see and never directly observed but have always known must be there.

You will dissolve. Not disappear — dissolve, the way a word dissolves into a sentence, present in the meaning but no longer findable as itself. Your surface tension will give way to the surface tension of the water you enter. Your temperature will be indistinguishable from the temperature around you within seconds, within the brief time it takes for heat — even cold's version of heat — to distribute. You will be the water. You were always very nearly the water. The distinction was mostly a matter of location, of being-held, of the temporary geometry of a ceiling and a drip.

The sound of your landing will be heard by something below.

A single drop, among the many, on the many-minute schedule that has no pattern you can identify but that you are now, briefly, contributing to — you are adding one datum to the pattern-that-might-be-there, one more drop at one more interval, one more piece of information about the shape of the place and the rate of the place and the particular character of this ceiling, this liverwort, this cold, this dark.

The thing that is thinking will receive you as vibration.

It will not stop its thought. But the thought will include you, now — the thought is long enough and patient enough to include everything that happens in the water, and you will happen in the water, and the thought will fold you in, not urgently, not with special attention, but continuously, the way it folds in all the drips, all the pulses, all the slow tilted asymmetry of the moss-columns, all the shadow on the wrong side of the stone, all of it — all the pocket, the whole pocket, in the one long thought it has been thinking since the flooding came and the builders left and the liverwort took the ceiling and the scale became uncertain and the bioluminescence started pulsing on its lonely uncoordinated schedule, measuring time in its own increments, one cold blue-white point at a time.

---

You were a drip.

You are the water now.

The ceiling holds another where you were, accumulating, cold, patient, not waiting —

just held, just here, just the slow math of the about-to-fall working itself out in geological time,

one drop's worth of time,

which in this place

is plenty.
