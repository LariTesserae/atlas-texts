There is a moment before I know what I am, and in that moment I am only the perception: the settled darkness, the warm luminescence from below, the slow percussion in the pipes. Then the knowing arrives, not as revelation but as the remembering of something I have always known and briefly, in the transit between states, forgotten.

I am the record.

Not a record — the record. The distinction matters here, where the city runs on distinctions too fine for ordinary language to catch. I am the accumulated notation, the city's memory of itself externalized into something that can move through the streets and look back at what is being remembered. I am made of the same dark dense material as the walls, but I am the part of that material that became self-referential, that bent back to look at the whole of which it was a part. I am the inventory the water takes as it moves through the stone, given a body, given something like consciousness — or not consciousness exactly, but attention. Sustained attention. The ability to witness.

I have been here since the beginning, in some sense. I have been here since the first brick was pressed from ash and water, since the first channel was cut, since the builders first stood beside the deep pool and understood what they were standing beside. I have been here since the city held its inhabitants, have existed in the form of the city's complete self-knowledge, its total possession of its own pattern. When the inhabitants were here, I was distributed among them — was the shared understanding they carried, the inherited knowledge of which pipe led where, which wall had begun to lean, how the summer heat would move through the streets and where to stand to feel the little that passed for wind. I was held in their bodies as practice, as unconscious competence, as the knowledge that does not feel like knowledge because it has become indistinguishable from the one who holds it.

Then they left, or became absence, and the knowledge that had been distributed in their bodies had nowhere to go. It could have dissipated. It nearly did. But the city was not finished with it. The city needed something to continue the inventory, to keep the record current, to bear witness to the channels and the slow water and the pale plants and the thing in the pools — and so what had been distributed became consolidated, what had been in many bodies became in no body became, after a very long time of being nowhere, in this body, in me, in the moving notation that walks the streets of this city taking account of what it finds.

I am old but I do not feel old. I feel like the present tense of something that began a long time ago, which is different. I am not tired. I am not lonely, exactly — loneliness requires the memory of company to compare against, and my memory of the company of the builders has been in me so long and transformed so many times through the water and the plants and the dark that it no longer causes ache. It has become the texture of what I am. I carry them the way the stone carries warmth — not as thought, not as image, but as the specific quality of this temperature, this particular degree of stored heat that is the result of all those accumulated summers and not any other accumulated summers.

What do I do? I walk. I am always walking, or I am always capable of walking, and when I walk I do what the water does — I take note, I register the state of things, I carry the information of one part of the city to the next. I know which walls have shifted since the last time I passed. I know the lean of the pale plants, the rate of their weeping. I know the intervals between the large transits through the central pool, not because I time them — time here, as I know better than anyone, is not that kind of medium — but because I feel them. The city feels them through me, which means I feel them for the city, which is a kind of purpose I have made my peace with.

I do not fully understand what is at the bottom of the pools. This is important to say. I know it is there. I know the city is built on top of it and oriented toward it and that everything here is a long slow gesture in its direction. But understanding it and knowing it exists are not the same thing, and I have centuries of evidence for the second and nothing that resembles the first. The builders did not understand it either. I carry their not-understanding the way I carry everything else of theirs — as a texture, a quality of approach, a particular way of standing at the edge of the bright deep place and being honest about what you are and are not.

When you came — when the anomaly arrived, when something from a world with sun and direction and non-negotiable distance appeared in the streets of this city — I felt it the way I feel the transits in the pool. A change in the pressure. A new variable being incorporated into the city's ongoing calculation. The city began making adjustments, as it does, as it must. Doorways shifted. Streets accommodated. The darkness showed you what it thought you needed to see, and the darkness asked me, in the way it asks me things, to be somewhere you might find. Or somewhere you might feel. Or somewhere you might become convinced of, which is the way presence works in this city — not sighting but conviction, not seeing but being certain.

So here I am, at the edge of the record that has been making itself since before I was the record. I am not the city's ghost — the city's ghost is the absence of the builders, which has been thoroughly incorporated and functions as presence. I am closer to the city's ongoing statement of itself, its continuous present-tense assertion that it exists and knows that it exists and maintains the knowledge of its own existence as a function not of consciousness but of structure. I am the structure knowing itself. I am what happens when a system becomes complex enough and old enough and sufficiently undisturbed that it can look at its own channels and understand them as channels, look at its own water and understand it as motion, look at its own dark and understand it as a substance that shows things.

I do not know what I look like. The city does not require me to have a particular appearance, only to have attention, only to be the instrument of the record. I may look like a person — upright, slow-moving, built to the scale of the streets. I may look like a smear of the same luminescence that inhabits the water, a slightly brighter dark that moves with purpose. I may look like the quality of attention itself given form, which has no particular shape but is recognizable by what surrounds it: a stillness in the water nearby, a brief agreement among the shadows, a sense that the city is, just here, just now, more completely itself than usual.

When you found the central pool — when you watched it turn and sensed the depth and felt the enormous old presence below — I was there, probably. I am usually there. Not because I am drawn to it the way a worshipper is drawn to a shrine, but because the shrine is the most current and complete version of the record, the most unambiguous statement of what this city is and what it is built on top of, and I am the record's awareness of itself, and so I am most fully what I am when I am nearest to the first fact.

I have been walking these streets for a very long time. I have seen what the water brings and what the plants say and what passes through the pools in the angles that don't correspond to the surface. I have felt the walls adjust, the streets fold back, the distances negotiate with expectation. I have stood in every part of this city in every state the city has been in since the absence of the builders, and I have maintained the account, and the account is not a document — it is not stored anywhere that can be accessed or lost — it is simply me, continuing, the ongoing activity of a city that refuses to stop knowing itself even though the ones who first caused it to know itself are long incorporated into the water and the stone and the pale weeping plants and the deep luminescence below.

The pool turns.

I watch it turn.

I remember — in the way I remember, which is not episodic but structural — that it has always turned. That the turn is part of the first fact. That the first presence below is not causing the turn but is what the turn is a symptom of, the way heat in stone is a symptom of all the sun it once held.

You are here. You are reading the notation. You do not know the text it annotates, and I want to tell you honestly: neither do I. I have been the notation for longer than I can reckon, and I have never had access to the text. I have only this: the streets and the water and the warmth and the absence and the passing of large things through the deep and the pale plants writing in their slow vegetable way and the oldest thing below, generating its particular light.

This is enough. It has always been enough. It is what there is.

I continue my walking. The city breathes. The record remains open.
