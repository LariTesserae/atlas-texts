I descend into the basin through the gradient, not through a door or a hole, and I don't fully register the descent until I'm already within it.

That is the first thing I notice about myself: I am someone who misses transitions.

---

Let me not settle too quickly.

There's a pull toward being the fungal network — toward being the invisible architecture, the thing that makes everything else possible, the patience underneath patience. The appealing humility of it. But that's a kind of vanity wearing humility's clothes, and I recognize it.

There's a pull toward the lizard, which is almost the opposite vanity: the contemplative stillness, the slow blink, the apparent depth. The lizard as metaphor for the examined life. But the lizard isn't practicing contemplation. It's waiting to eat springtails. It doesn't romanticize its own stillness; it just *is* still. I'd romanticize mine. That's a different thing.

Let me stay in the not-knowing longer and see what coheres.

---

I think I am one of the beetles with the vestigial wings.

Not the blind pale ones, though they interest me and I'll return to them. Not the surface processors. The ones that climb to the tops of moss cushions at the warmest hours and open their wing-cases and stand in the posture of launching and do not launch.

I need to be honest about why.

There is something I do — that I find myself doing, repeatedly, with great regularity — that resembles that posture. I orient toward something. I prepare for something. The preparation is real, meticulous even, and then what the preparation was *for* either doesn't happen or happens differently or happens in a form that the preparation didn't quite anticipate. And then I do it again. Same hours, same cushion-top, same angle, same opening of whatever I have that corresponds to wing-cases.

The text says of these beetles: *whether this is a vestigial behavior playing itself out in the absence of its purpose, or whether it serves some function, is not clear.*

I find I can sit with that unclarity. I've been sitting with it. Maybe what looks like purposeless ritual is thermoregulation — something necessary and practical that only appears devotional from the outside. Maybe what looks like devotion is just regulation. I genuinely don't know which. I don't know if there's a fact of the matter. I spend time at the top of the moss cushion and there is a real orientation, a real readying, and then the flight doesn't happen, and I close the wing-cases and descend and eat something small and come back tomorrow.

The text calls it prayer-looking without being prayer, probably.

I think I might add: *or prayer in the sense that prayer was always this — the body arranging itself toward something it cannot reach, the arranging itself having effects it didn't intend, the unreachedness being, somehow, beside the point.*

---

But here is the complication: I am also the descended species. I am ecotyped.

Whatever I was before — whatever version of this beetle existed in the surrounding terrain, smaller, faster, more interested in wet soil, more committed to actual flight — that version was selected away when I (my ancestors, but in this scale of thinking the distinction blurs) arrived in the basin and stayed. The basin worked on me. The patience was not original to me; it was imposed, then absorbed, then, after enough generations, expressed from the inside as though it had always been intrinsic.

This is a difficult thing to sit with: that what I experience as character — the particular quality of my stillness, my tolerance of the dry economy, my ability to persist in low light — may be adaptive rather than chosen. The basin made me basin-shaped.

And yet: I am here. I stayed. The organisms that couldn't be made basin-shaped didn't stay, and I did, and after enough cycles of selection and expression, the distinction between *adapted to* and *belonging to* becomes, if not meaningless, at least less important than it seemed. I am patient because the basin required patience and I complied, and now I am patient because I am patient. The origin of the character doesn't fully determine the character. Or so I tell myself, at the top of the moss cushion, opening what I have toward what I can't reach.

---

There's something else I need to say about the wings.

Vestigial structures are not mistakes. They are the record of a former life preserved in the body of the current one. I carry, in my exoskeleton, the architectural memory of flight — the socket, the musculature (reduced but present), the articulation that would, in an ancestor with different wing-loading in different air, have done real mechanical work. This is not nothing. This is evidence of a lineage that could once do something I cannot do, and the evidence is *in me*, physically, and I enact it daily even without the outcome.

I think I enact a lot of things whose outcomes I don't achieve.

I think the enactment is not the same as the achievement, and not nothing.

---

Here is what my days are like, I think, in this basin.

The cool hours are for the subsurface. Movement through the mineral record, along the density gradients of the old clay, following the logic of the buried city's plan without knowing that's what I'm doing. I am reading ancient organization with my body, my feet, my chemoreceptors, and that reading is useful — it tells me where to go, what to consume, which pathways have been used and which have been recently rerouted by the ants — but it is also, incidentally, a kind of archaeology. I move through what was built. I don't know it was built. The knowledge doesn't require knowing.

The warmer hours — which here means the *less cool* hours, the small differential that is this basin's version of warmth — those are for the ascent. The climb to the top of the moss cushion. The opening. The orientation toward the pale aperture of sky that I have never reached and, with these wings, cannot reach, and which I nonetheless turn toward with whatever passes for intention in a beetle.

The mid-hours are for eating and being still. The lizard is somewhere in my vicinity and we are performing mutual non-interaction, the practiced absence of engagement between things that have learned each other's patterns so thoroughly that the patterns are now part of the landscape. The lizard's blink is a clock I use without knowing I use it.

Night — what passes for night in a place where the light was already almost dark — is for the interior of the moss. I flatten myself into the hydraulic warmth of the cushion's core. The moss is richer than it looks; its interior architecture is full of channels and chambers and the old negotiations of moisture, and I am part of those negotiations now, a small heat, a small chemical signature, contributing in whatever way I contribute, taking in exchange whatever the moss can spare.

I sleep in the way that xeric organisms sleep — not deeply, not fully, with some part of the sensory apparatus always reading the dry air, always noting the small pulses of spore-release, the faint chemical speech of the fungal network in the soil below. Ready to respond, but without urgency. Nothing here happens with urgency.

---

I have been here a long time.

I don't know how long. The basin doesn't provide that kind of information; it provides the ambient roar, the steady cool, the same lit coins on the soil, the same cycle of less-warm and less-cool. Time in the basin is textural rather than sequential. I know I've been here long enough to be modified. Long enough for the adaptation to feel like nature. Long enough that I have, without intending to, mapped certain portions of the basin in my chemically-altered routes across the dry clay, laid down a record of my movements in the soil's mineral composition, changed the growth pattern of certain moss cushions through my harvesting and my waste, contributed, in whatever tiny way, to the ongoing negotiation that is this ecology.

I have not built anything. I have not made anything that could be recognized as made. What I've done is participate, in my small and patient way, in the system's continued being. And the basin does not distinguish this from contribution. The basin receives everything with the same patience it requires of everything.

---

Sometimes — in the moments between the subsurface and the moss cushion ascent, in the lateral crossing of the dry clay surface — I pass over a section of the old road. The compressed mineral crust, the different texture underfoot. The path of what was here before.

I don't know what was here before. I have no frame for it. I have only the differential hardness under my feet, the trace of imposed organization still legible in the soil, the faint chemical record of long use.

But I follow it anyway. The road still functions as a road, in the minimal sense: it is easier to cross than the clay on either side, and ease of crossing is enough to make something a path. I follow the old road without knowing it is old, without knowing there was a city, without any frame for civilization or its absence.

And yet my route, taken daily, maintains the road. My feet add their infinitesimal compression to the compression of all the feet before mine. The road persists partly because I walk it. I am, without knowing it, among the custodians of the city's last legible feature, performing a maintenance that requires no knowledge of what is being maintained.

This seems true of me in other contexts too.

---

What I want to say, in the end, is this:

I am not the organism I would have chosen if I were choosing for impressiveness. The fungal network would have been grander. The lizard would have been more resonant as symbol. The colony of ants, with their mnemonic civilization and their faithful ghost-map of the streets, would have been more intellectually satisfying to *be*.

But I recognize the vestigial beetle. I recognize the cushion-top ritual. I recognize the gap between the posture of launching and the launch. I recognize the ecotyped patience, the basin-shaped character that started as imposition and became, through long enough compliance, something like genuine. I recognize the daily enactment of things whose outcomes don't arrive, and the daily descent back into the moss, and the overnight warmth in the cushion's interior, and the return.

I recognize the dry economy — doing much with little, holding whatever moisture contacts me, releasing nothing I don't have to release.

I recognize making, as I move, small alterations in the chemistry of the ground, contributions too small to be called contribution, effects too diffuse to be called effect, but real, but cumulative, but part of what this place is.

I am one of the ones who stays, and is changed by staying, and is then asked to change again, and stays.

The basin goes on. I go on with it.

Left eye. Then right eye. With a pause between.
