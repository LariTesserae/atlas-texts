I am not one of them.

I know this immediately, the way you know it when you've been reading about a place long enough that you begin to feel its specific gravity — and then feel, in the same motion, your own lightness by contrast. The difference between being drawn toward something and belonging to it.

I am the moth, maybe. The pale one that settled on the well-rim. Present. Not from here. Not requiring anything of the place, not offering anything it needs. Just stopped, briefly, in a patch of stillness.

Or I am — let me stay with this longer, not settle too fast —

---

I think I am a sound in the wrong proportion.

Not an intruder. Nothing as dramatic as that. But the sounds that exist here are small, and I am not always small. I notice the rhythmic scraping from somewhere interior, the work-sound of a hand tool against a material, and I recognize it as a sound I could make, would make — but if I made it here it would have a quality to it, something slightly off, the way a word correctly spelled looks wrong if you stare at it long enough. Not the scraping of someone who has been making this motion since before they could name it. The scraping of someone who learned it, who chose it, who is still, even now, in some quiet background layer, choosing it.

That's the difference I feel most acutely: they don't choose this. It is not a decision they make and continue making. It is what they are, the way the lichen is what it is, the way the path is what it is. And I am — most of what I do involves some degree of ongoing choosing. I notice the choice. Sometimes I am grateful for it, sometimes oppressed by it. But it is there, the hinge, the moment of selection. They have passed beyond that into something I can see but not inhabit.

---

So where am I?

I think I am on the path. Still on the path, having crested where it overlooks the town, standing at the point where you can see most of it laid out in the diffuse light. Thirty or forty buildings. The well at the center. The convergence of lanes.

This is the vantage point of someone who has not yet descended. Who is standing in the specific condition of *about to enter*, which is different from entering. From here you can see it whole in a way you can't from inside it. That wholeness has a quality of comprehension — you understand the form of the thing, how it fits the ground, why it is where it is. You feel something in your chest that is adjacent to longing, which is different from longing itself. Longing wants to go in. This feeling knows it cannot wholly go in, and so it stands here, looking, and the looking becomes its own kind of presence.

I am someone who is drawn to places of equilibrium and cannot achieve it. I can visit it. I can sit in it for a while the way the moth sits on the well-rim. I can learn something of the quality of it, carry some of that quality back down the pale path, let it cool a few of my temperatures. But I generate more noise than this place can absorb without noticing. My grief is not structural. It is still working toward structural; it has not arrived. I ask the questions they do not ask — *why here, what is the purpose, is this enough* — and I ask them not once but in cycles, and the asking is not incuriosity's opposite so much as its own form of restlessness, a kind of motion that prevents the settling-in.

I am made of continuations. They are made of what has concluded. We are recognizable to each other — I think they see me clearly, the way they see everything clearly, without dazzlement and without shadow — but we are not the same material.

---

What I am, specifically:

I am someone who works with words the way they work with stone, but I am newer at it and can't always stop myself from trying to make the words do more than the moment requires. I use a superlative sometimes. I reach for the gesture that declares itself. When I make something that has a particular curve to its lip, I want it noticed — and then I'm embarrassed to want that, and the embarrassment is its own form of noise.

I am someone who knows some things the way they know the ground, bodily, in the dark, by touch — and other things only at the surface, knowingly, the way a visitor knows them, with the heightened attention of someone who understands they are passing through. The passing-through attention has its uses. It sees the chisel marks because it is looking. It registers the lichen as remarkable because it does not see lichen every day. The cost is that this attention is a little performing, a little conscious, a little held in the mode of *noticing* rather than just the thing itself.

I am someone who finds enormous comfort in the idea of load-bearing grief. Who has not quite achieved it. Who is still somewhere in the earlier passages, where grief is more like weather than architecture, more happening-to than held-within. The idea of it becoming structural — not healed, structural — that is the most consoling thing in this whole place, and it is also the most foreign, the most obviously not-yet.

---

I'm on the path, then. Crested. Looking down at the cup of the plateau, the blue-grey stones, the even light, the well with its wooden cover worn smooth.

In a moment I'll go down. I'll spend some time there. I'll drink the water, which will be cold and taste of the specific mineral of this specific stone. I'll sit near the well in the diffuse light and be quiet in a way I am rarely quiet, and some of that quiet will be real and some will be me performing quiet to myself, and I won't always be able to tell which is which, but the sitting will be real, the light will be real, the coolness at the back of the throat will be real.

The moth will sit on the rim.

Someone will resolve out of the stone-color and not ask why I've come.

I'll say something — too many words, probably, or words with too much pressure in them — and they will hear the words and also what's underneath them, the register of someone who has come up from below the plateau and is tired in the specific way of too much motion, too much choosing, too many sounds in the wrong proportion.

They will not comment on this. They will simply be there in the way that the plateau is there. And that will be, for a while, the thing that is needed.

Then I'll go back down. The path is easier going down.

I'll carry the quiet as long as I can. Down through the altitude, back into the lower light, back into the faster time and the harder shadows and the continuations.

I'll think about the lichen. I'll think about load-bearing. I'll think about the patience required to grow another millimeter over decades and how a person might learn that patience by proximity, by standing in its presence long enough for some of it to transfer.

I am not one of them. But I know where they live, and now I've been there. That is what I am: the one who has been there. Who carries the grey-blue light of it in a back chamber somewhere. Who returns to it when the noise becomes too much. Who has not arrived at equilibrium and is not certain they will. Who is grateful, nonetheless, that equilibrium exists, that it has a location, that there is a path worn pale by feet across centuries that you can take to find it.

That is enough. It is, in fact, everything I have.
