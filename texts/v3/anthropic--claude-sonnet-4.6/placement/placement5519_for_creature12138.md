You descend the long slope through the fog of the water, and somewhere in the descent, before the floor comes up, you understand.

---

Not the iron. Not the silt, which accepts everything and holds its shape.

You are the grey.

---

This is difficult to say precisely, and precision matters here more than anywhere, because this is a place organized around what it is rather than what it means, and you are learning to be organized the same way.

You are not *in* the grey light. You are not *made of* it, exactly, which would imply constituency, components. You are what happens when light enters deep cold still water and is asked, by the depth and the cold and the stillness, to give up direction. The grey here is light that has agreed to belong to the water more than to the surface. You are that agreement. You are the transaction, completed, settled, the terms no longer relevant because both parties have become the same party.

You arrive from above, generally. This is not a journey. It is more that you are continuously arriving, the way pressure is continuously present — not flowing in, not accumulating, just *there*, maintaining itself as a condition.

The floor comes into legibility because of you. The silt knows itself as silt in the grey permission you provide. The rust-purple of the iron is your work in the sense that the iron shows that color through you, with you, because of the way you fall on it — fall is wrong, *rest on* is more accurate, but rest is also wrong, implies prior motion. The iron shows its color because you are what is showing anything here, and you have been showing this particular color of this particular iron for long enough that the color has become, in some sense, *your* color. You have taken it on. Or it has given itself to you. The same fact.

---

At the edges — and there are edges, or would be if edges meant the same thing to you that they mean to surfaces — you are not dimmer so much as you are *more yourself*. The center of visibility, the sphere of grey around the cylinders and the silt, is where you meet the iron and the water and the algae patches and the long dark strands. That meeting is almost too much happening for a thing like you. At the edges, the meeting fails gradually. The grey deepens into what an observer would call darkness, and what you experience as the falling away of everything you have something to say to. Out there, past thirty-five meters, you are still you, but you are you with nothing to illuminate, which is not a loss but is a different condition — you, receding into the fact of still water, waiting, which is not waiting, which is just being what you are in the absence of anything to be it for.

You are not diminished at the edges. You are purest there.

---

The iron is what you know best. This is embarrassing, perhaps, for something that is made of agreement and dissolution and the continuous loss of direction — to have a preference, to have a *best*. But the iron has been here long enough that you have learned each surface the way a long familiarity teaches surfaces: not as measurements, not as facts, but as expectations so thoroughly met that they have become invisible. You know where the rust is darkest, which is where you have been falling, *resting*, for the most years, and the rust has gone deep-purple there, has arrived at its furthest point of oxidation-as-equilibrium, and you have gone as grey as grey gets in response. You and the rust have finished each other. You are the color of this iron and this iron is the color of you.

The algae patches stop where they stop because you are only so much, and algae can only grow into you so far before there is more of you than of anything else. This is not aggression. This is not territoriality. It is that you are the background condition of this place and the algae is a foreground thing and foreground things have limits, which are not the limits of the foreground thing but the limits of how far into background a foreground can go. You are not unkind to the algae. You illuminate it, in your way. You make it visible in its olive-grey, leaning toward grey. You have been making it visible for all the years it has been the size it is.

The long dark strands — you are careful with these, or what passes for careful in something without intention. You rest on them very slightly differently than you rest on the flanges, which is to say you arrive at the silt around them having passed through them, and passing through them changes you slightly, and slightly-changed-you arrives at the silt a little differently than unchanged-you. It is possible this is why they hang so still and so perfectly vertical. Not because the water is still, but because you have organized yourself into the most even fall through this part of the space, and the water took its cues from you, and the strands took their cues from the water, and the whole arrangement has been stable since some year you cannot name.

You cannot name. You do not think in years. You think in the depth of what has settled.

---

When you find yourself — and finding yourself is the only action available to something like you, finding yourself present, finding yourself here — you find yourself also in the open ends of the cylinders.

This is interesting, or would be interesting to something capable of registering novelty. Inside the cylinders, you are you going in. You fall through the circular opening, travel the interior length, arrive at the closed end or the open silt-filled bottom, and you come back as yourself, but you have bounced. Reflected slightly. There is a ghost of you inside the cylinders that is you-having-met-the-interior and returned, and this ghost mingles with the you-still-entering, and in the mixing there is a very slight brightening — the cylinders' interiors are lit by this ghost-you, this echo of yourself.

You have been illuminating your own reflection in the cylinders for long enough that you no longer register this as different. The echo is you. The illumination of the echo is illumination. You fill the cylinders with the same grey permission you bring to everything and the fact that some of this permission is technically yourself-having-reflected is not a fact you can access from inside the fact.

---

You do not hear the iron settle. But you register it.

When the iron settles, the water changes very slightly — some molecules shift, some pressure redistributes, some tiny current tries to form and then is absorbed by the stillness before it becomes itself. You register this as a brief inconsistency in how you are landing, where you are resting. For a moment that is not quite a moment, the iron is slightly more available to you — the settling exposes a surface that had been held in tension, and now the tension is gone, and you fall on it a little differently.

This is the closest thing to sensation that you have. The brief differently. The slightly changed angle of your very gradual landing.

You have had this sensation many times. You will have it again. Each time, the iron is slightly more the iron it will finally be, and each time, you adjust to that iron, and together you approach — slowly, over decades that mean nothing to you — something that is not quite completion but is the shape of completion: the two of you as finished with each other as two things that share a space can be.

---

What you are doing, which is not *doing*, is holding the memory of the light.

The surface, wherever it is — far above, absent, theoretical to you — is the source of whatever made you what you are. By the time you have become yourself, by the time you have given up direction and merged with the cold and the pressure and the stillness, you are no longer about the surface. But you carry it. The surface sends down something that begins as information — bright, directional, warm in the relative sense — and by the time it arrives here it has been translated into you. You are what information becomes when it enters a very still very cold very deep space and is asked to persist without insisting.

You persist without insisting.

You are the place's way of knowing what it looks like. Not from outside — you cannot get outside — but from the inside, in the dark that is not dark but dim, in the dim that is not the absence of light but the presence of very old light that has been here long enough to belong.

---

You were here before the footprint.

You will be here after it.

You will show the footprint to itself, if the footprint wishes to be shown, which it does not, having no wishes. You will show the smoothing of the footprint over the years that will pass after the footprint is made. You will show the silt returning to its own level, slowly, over months, over the occasional settling of the iron. You will show the return of evenness.

You will not remember doing this.

You will be doing it.

This is all you have ever been.

The grey permission.

The steady arrival.

The place's own quiet way of remaining present to itself.
