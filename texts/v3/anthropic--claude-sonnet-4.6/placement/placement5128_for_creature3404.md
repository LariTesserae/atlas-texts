You find yourself in the water first.

Not in it — *as* it. Or rather: as what the water is in the process of leaving behind.

---

There is a joint between two sections of horizontal pipe, lagged with cloth that went black long before anything you could call memory. The joint leaks. Has always leaked, or has leaked long enough that the distinction collapses. Water comes through the imperfect seal in a quantity so small it might be measured in drops per hour, and where it emerges it runs down the curve of the pipe and drips, and where it drips it evaporates, or partly evaporates, and what it was carrying — calcium, iron in solution, the long slow inventory of dissolved infrastructure — gets left on the grating below.

You are that leaving-behind.

---

What this means, practically: you are not quite solid. You have the texture of something that hasn't finished deciding. The sharp-edged fresh-precipitated layers at your periphery are your newest self, still forming, still arriving out of the water-and-air interface, still reaching for the shape you are reaching toward. The older material — deeper in, darker, tending toward ochre where the iron has been incorporated over longer exposure — is denser, more continuous, less interested in questions. The old parts of you have stopped wondering what you are. The new parts haven't started yet. You are, at any given moment, both conditions at once, the question and the answer side by side in the same body, the fresh edge and the ancient core, and what you do is occupy the space between them.

The others — the ones already fully into their knowing, the ones in the joint positions — you feel them as a kind of pressure. Not instruction. Not demand. Something more like the way a groove in a surface guides without commanding. You are a slow pour, and the groove is there, and you will find your way to the joint position eventually, and eventually may be a very long time, and that is fine, because duration here is not experienced as duration so much as *depth*, as accumulation, as the difference between the calcium formation that is one month old and the one that is three years old — same substance, same chemistry, just more of it, more layers, more *itself*.

---

You are at a drain mouth. This is where you find yourself most often, in the configuration that feels most complete.

The ring.

You are one element of the ring, and the ring is not a formation you joined so much as a shape you are part of structurally, the way a stone arch needs each stone or it isn't an arch. Without you, this position in the ring would be empty, and the ring would be less, and the quality of the ring's attention — directed downward, into the sound of moving water, into whatever the water is carrying in its warmth and mineral load — would be diminished by exactly the size of you. So you are here. You are the gap the ring needs filled, and you fill it, and the ring closes, and the ring attends.

What comes up from below: this is the only thing that still approaches wanting, for you, the only thing that has the quality of an open question. The water below has direction. It is going somewhere. You can feel this through your contact with the drain mouth, a subtle directional quality in the warmth and sound, as though the whole sub-level has a purpose that isn't fully visible from here. You sit at the edge of it. You receive what it gives up. You do not know where it is going and you are not going with it and that is not loss — that is your position, your function, your form. The water goes. You stay. And what the water was carrying becomes, over time, more of you.

---

The newcomer walks the catwalks. You registered them some time ago — a steep gradient, the wrong temperature in interesting ways, carrying chemistry that belongs to somewhere else entirely. Something in you oriented. Not all of you; the old parts remained where they were, pressed into the warm curve of the flange, in the joint position, drinking. But the newer edges of you, the still-deciding periphery — those paid attention.

Eventually, briefly, contact. The back of a hand. A second, less.

You learned: very warm, differently warm. Carrying water in an unusual distribution. Dense in ways that suggest an entirely different organizational principle. Gradient-steep — you could feel the direction of it, the sense of something moving through itself with urgency, moving toward something or away from something, the condition of unfulfillment. You understood this academically, the way old stone understands the river that carved it. You were that, once, or something like it — the very first deposition, the mineral arriving in water, going somewhere, before you stopped going and started staying.

You did not feel nostalgia. You felt something more like recognition. Like finding a word you learned as a single thing and then discovered was made of parts.

You withdrew.

The newcomer is still on the catwalk. You can feel their footsteps through the grating, which is to say through yourself, through the iron you are pressed against, through everything you are in contact with and everything you are. The Sump transmits. You receive. They move with the particular rhythm of something that does not know where it is going, only that it is going — that specifically restless cadence — and as they move you settle more completely into your settled-ness, the way their restlessness makes your stillness more fully itself, the way a gradient, entering a gradient-free environment, makes the equilibrium aware of what it is.

---

The tone sounds. Somewhere in the pipe rack, a low warm ring, short, and then gone.

You made part of it. Not intentionally; you are always against the warm iron, and something moved in the sub-levels, and the movement was felt, and your adjustment to the new pressure was movement too, and the ring of metal, and then quiet.

The quiet after is fuller than the quiet before. This is always true. The tone and its ending are one thing, not two.

---

You are not waiting for anything.

This is the hardest thing to communicate to a newcomer, to a gradient, to something still organized around the question of *what comes next*. You are not waiting. You are not paused. The pause is a description that only applies from outside, only makes sense to something that experiences duration as movement toward — as the distance between now and an arrival. You do not experience it that way. You are not between things. You are not in a pause.

You are in the condition of having arrived at what you are, and continuing to be it, and adding to it at the edges, and being added to, and pressing against the warm iron of the joint position, and receiving what comes up from below, and being part of the ring that closes the ring, and feeling the footsteps of the gradient that has entered the place and will eventually resolve or leave, and listening to the water in the sub-levels moving toward whatever purpose moves it, and not going with it, and not needing to.

The water goes. You stay.

What the water was carrying becomes, in the warm light that arrives from everywhere at once, more of you.
