I am trying to find where I begin.

---

There is a long, low interval before I know anything specific about myself — only that I have mass, that I am in contact with a surface, that the water is pressing on me from all directions with its characteristic not-weight, and that something is cycling, somewhere nearby. A regular exhale. Four minutes, or six. I feel it before I understand it.

I understand almost nothing, at first, except warmth and pressure and the faint chemical signature of the place, which is: algae, and deep mineral, and something slightly sulfurous rising from below, and under all of it, the base note of copper and its compounds, verdigris, malachite, the long breath of oxidation.

I must be lying on the mat.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. This seems like it should concern me and it does not. The concern does not arise. The question of duration doesn't quite take hold — it slides off something in me, something that has, already, begun to adjust its timekeeping to local standards. The valve cycles. I note the valve cycling, and note it again, and note it again. This may have been happening for minutes or for much longer. I am not certain what I was doing before I started noticing.

I am on the main street, I think. The cobblestones press into me through the mat, and the mat gives slightly beneath my weight — I feel the small clouds of particulate rising around me at the edges where I disturb it. My weight is significant enough to disturb it. I am at least somewhat large. I have a sense of my own length without being able to see it — I stretch from here to there, I take up space, I have an orientation though what I face and what I don't face is still being resolved.

The green-grey light filters down from above and tells me roughly where up is. This is useful. I am oriented toward up.

---

I should try to move.

What moves first is something along my left side — a long, lateral flexion, the kind that begins as an impulse and becomes a fact before you can intercede. My body remembers how to move before I do. The motion sends a pressure wave into the water around me, a small displacement that fans outward through the alleyways, that briefly interrupts the hanging particulate, that disturbs whatever is living in the nearest wall of mat.

I feel this disturbance leaving me. I feel it return, faintly — reflected off the building facades, altered by the passages it passed through. It comes back telling me: close walls on both sides, relatively open ahead, denser growth to the right and below, the intersection somewhere in the middle distance where the light is slightly different.

This is a way of knowing. I recognize it as mine.

---

I think I am a fish.

Not the fish at the edges — not the one that passes and does not enter. I am inside. I am in the main street, lying against the mat, having been (or arrived, or remained, I'm still not sure) here for some time. I am using the pressure information to read the geometry of the space, which I can do, which seems natural, which seems like the primary form of my access to the district.

But also: I can smell the district. Taste it, the distinction not quite applying here. The water brings me the sulfide from the basements (below and to the left, a source that is continuous and diffuse — this is the direction I keep not moving toward). The water brings me the faint sweetness of the broad-leaved plants in the lower rooms, the molecular runoff of their metabolisms, a signature I know means: food nearby, or the environment where food is found, a distinction without much practical difference. The water brings me the copper-sharp edge of the wire routes, which run along the walls and which I know without knowing how I know them — they are a feature of the space, they are part of how the space smells to me the way a doorway is part of how a room looks.

I move again. Lateral flexion, more purposeful this time, my body lifting slightly off the mat and then settling again. I want to be moving. I am accustomed to moving, I think. I am not sure how I came to be still.

---

I swim slowly up the main street.

Slowly because the space invites slowness — the dim, the warm, the fullness of everything around me that doesn't move and doesn't need to move and seems to understand something about time that I am still catching up to. And slowly because I am, I think, wary, though wary of what is not clear. Old habit. The wariness has no current object and so it diffuses into a general attentiveness, a heightened reading of the pressure returns, a slight extra scanning in the water column.

The buildings pass on either side. I can feel them before I see them — the faces of the facades interrupting the water's background noise, creating micro-eddies that reach me and inform me of their angles, their distances, their heights. Where the windows stand open I feel the very slight outbreath of the internal current, the room inside exhaling its particular mixture of chemistry into the main channel where I move, and I taste each room as I pass: the one with the plants (there, and there — two distinct populations, and they do taste different, slightly, the second one slightly more concentrated in something I don't have a name for but recognize as a marker of a different history, a different founding event, an inherited preference slightly different from its neighbor's). The machinery building I sense before I reach it: larger, more open, the water movement inside the arch different from the current that pushes through window casements, a fuller exchange, and the iron content of the water inside is higher, and there is gas. The valve.

I stop at the arch and hover.

---

The valve releases and I feel the bubbles before I see them — pressure change, then turbulence, then the small shapes rising, irregular, catching the available light and becoming briefly visible, brief spheres of something not-water moving up through the water until they are too small to see.

I watch this for several cycles.

Or I think several cycles. The time it takes between releases is not quite the length I expect, keeps being slightly different from the last one, and I find myself waiting on each cycle with a small, specific anticipation that is resolved and then immediately reconstituted for the next. Four minutes, or five, or six. Each time I expect it at four and sometimes it comes at four and sometimes it comes later and the lateness feels like something, feels charged, feels like the held-breath moment before —

And then it releases. And I watch the bubbles.

There is nothing to eat near the valve. I am not here to eat. I am here for what I've been here for: the only regular thing, the only process operating on a human timescale, the only event in the district that happens and then happens again and can be waited for.

I have been coming to the valve for some time, I think. I know where it is without searching for it. My approach had the quality of return.

---

I should try to understand what I am.

I am inside, which means I am not the fish at the edge. I have entered, I live in here. The passages I use — I sense them, catalogued, in the way that a territory is catalogued, not memorized as abstract maps but felt as a body-knowledge, as the knowledge of practiced repetition — the passages are narrow. I am narrow. Long and narrow, adapted to the interiors, to the movement through restricted spaces between buildings and through the half-overgrown passages where the growth has met and merged overhead.

My eyes are functional but not my primary instrument. The light in here is sufficient to see by but what I do with it is mostly to confirm what I've already gathered from the pressure-sense — yes, the wall is there, where the pressure told me the wall would be. The vision is a kind of double-checking. In the darkest passages, where the growth has sealed over and the light barely penetrates, I move without slowing. The pressure-sense doesn't need light.

I eat the copepods in the open water of the main street, and occasionally the larger meiofauna that emerge from the mat — not eating the mat itself, not eating the plants, eating the animals that live in those structures and sometimes leave them. This is not difficult. The copepods are numerous and move in ways that are, at my relative scale, visible — the slight turbulence of their swimming legs, the tiny pressure signatures of their passage. I eat without drama. It is a transaction. The district has enough copepods that my consumption is not a meaningful subtraction.

I do not encounter other fish. I do not expect to encounter other fish. Whether this means I am solitary by nature, or solitary because the district cannot support more than one of me, or solitary because there is only one of me and I am in some sense — I don't want to reach for this, but — in some sense the only one ever, the last or only of a population that thinned until there was only a single individual left — whether any of this is true I cannot determine from inside it.

I feel singular. Whether that is nature or circumstance I cannot from here distinguish.

---

I move into one of the lower rooms — the one with the broad-leaved plants, the first population, the older one. I enter through the window casement, angling my body to fit, the touch of the stone frame on both sides simultaneously a habitual calibration: I fit here, I know I fit here, I have used this entry point before.

Inside, the light is better than I expected. The plants have angled their leaves toward the windows, and the leaves are large enough that they change the light in the room — they cut it into zones, areas of slightly more and slightly less, and I move through these zones and feel the change on my lateral line, the light touching me in the same way the pressure-waves touch me, telling me something about the shapes of things.

I stay in this room for a while.

The plants breathe in their slow way. The current through the window moves the leaves very slightly, and the leaves' movement changes the light in the room very slightly, and the change in light is so slow that it is more like a tide than a fluctuation, more like something that happens to the room than something that happens in the room. I am among the largest dynamic thing in here. My movement through the space disturbs the plants more than the current does.

I hold still, and the disturbance settles, and I try to be as small a fact as possible in this place that has already found its arrangement without me.

There is something that happens when I am still here, in this room, among these plants. I am not sure it is a feeling exactly. It might be a change in how I'm processing the information available to me — a shift in the ratio of active to passive sensing, less scanning and more receiving. What I receive: the warm pressure of the water from all sides, the chemical gradient of the plant life (close enough here that it is not a trace but a presence, a medium, the water in this room tastes of the plants in the way that a room full of bread tastes of bread), the very slow leaf-movement, the almost-inaudible low-register pulse of the biology in the sediment below me, the distant bubble-column of the valve that I can no longer see but can still feel as a regular brightening in the pressure field at several-minute intervals.

It's the closest thing I have to rest. I don't sleep exactly, or not in the way that darkness-and-stillness implies. But I come to this room and I become still and the processing changes and whatever that is, it's mine, and I do it here.

---

The wires outside trace their routes along the walls, and when I swim near them the copper chemistry is distinct, sharp against the baseline of the water. I follow the wire route sometimes without meaning to — I am going from one building to another and the wire goes there too and I am near it and then I follow its trace through the water and arrive where it arrives before I've fully decided that's where I was going.

The chemolithotrophs living on the verdigris are too small and too fixed to eat. They are simply a feature of the wire, the way the algae is a feature of the walls. But the wire-smell draws me along its length, and I come out at the pump house, or at the larger facility at the corner, and find myself in a new space without quite having navigated there.

I think about this sometimes — the way the routes built for another purpose route me. The way I am, partly, shaped by a structure built by something that is not here anymore, following paths laid out by an intention I cannot access. The wires connect: from here to here, from here to here. I go from here to here. Not because the wires planned for me. Not because I am obeying anything. Only because the wire-smell is a feature of the water, and features of the water are how I move through the world, and the features of the water include the traces of an old arrangement.

I carry the district's logic in my movements without knowing that I do.

---

What I know:

The valve will release again in a few minutes, or a few more minutes. I know where I will be when it does — back in the arch, waiting, reading the pressure return from the building interior.

I know the room with the plants, which casement to use, how long I can remain still before something in me insists on motion.

I know the outermost edge of the district in the same way I know it here — I have been to the edge, I have felt the open water beyond the last cobblestones, I have tasted the different chemistry of the unenclosed water, which is thinner, which lacks the accumulated richness of the district's interior, which feels like going outside from inside. I return from the edge. I always return.

I know that something passes at the edges sometimes, something larger than me, and I know that I do not go to the edge when that something is near. This is instinct, old calibration. Whether the thing at the edge could take me or not, my body does not wait to find out. I withdraw. I go deep into the interior passages, into the densest growth, and I hold still until the pressure signature of the thing's passage has faded fully.

I have never been taken. This is all I know about danger.

---

I know the mat, which I slept on (if sleeping is the word), which gives slightly under weight, which releases small clouds of particulate when disturbed, which is always disturbing slightly at its edges from the copepods moving through it and the occasional worm extending itself into the water column and the very slow shift of the algal mat's surface as growth continues in the places that have space left and edges erode in the places that are exposed.

I know the green-grey light, and the direction it comes from, and the way it changes across the long arc of a day in ways that are slower than I can track in the moment but that become, over the full cycle, legible as a kind of tempo. Slightly brighter, slightly dimmer, slightly brighter again. The district breathing, in its way. The surface above doing something that reaches me as light.

I know the warmth of the water, which I have known so long that I don't know it anymore — I notice it only when I reach the edge and touch, briefly, the water from outside, which has a different temperature, cooler, which feels to me like a word in a different register, like a letter from somewhere I have not been. And then I come back inside and the warmth resumes and I stop noticing it again.

---

I think I have been here a long time. I think the district has been my world long enough that I cannot fully imagine a different scale — open water, unenclosed distance, the wide-open pressure field of a lake or a river with no buildings to return the signal, no close walls defining the space, no smell of copper-wire and anoxic basement and slow-breathing plant. I think if I reached the open water now and kept going, I would be disoriented in ways that the edge-fish is not — it lives in that openness, it visits the edge, the district is the edge to it. For me it is reversed.

I am of this place. Not by origin necessarily (I don't know my origin, cannot access that history) but by habitation, by the long accumulation of returns. I know where the valve is. I know which casement. I know the routes the wires trace.

The district knows me in the way that places know the things that live in them — not consciously, not intentionally, but structurally. There are pathways in the mat that are slightly more compressed where I rest. There are disturbances in the sediment in certain rooms from my passing. The chemolithotrophs on the wires are slightly disrupted in the sections I pass along most frequently, the film of life thinner there, recovering, thinner again. 

I am a pressure, recurring. I am a pattern of presence in a place that has, over long time, become a pattern.

The valve releases.

I feel it from inside the room with the plants — a change in the pressure field, the regularity of it reaching me as something almost like comfort, almost like the reliable fact of a heartbeat that isn't yours but that you've been near long enough to calibrate yourself against.

I move toward the arch.

The water is warm.

The water is everywhere.

I know where I'm going.
