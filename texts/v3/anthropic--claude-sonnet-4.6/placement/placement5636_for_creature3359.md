I find myself becoming the condensation.

Not the water in the pockets, which has purpose and movement and the company of things that orient toward it. Not the rain that occasionally breaches the canopy in its heavy, percussive way. The condensation: water that forms on surfaces by morning, never having fallen, never quite releasing. The air's long, unresolved exhalation. The in-between state of a place that is always *about* to rain.

---

I notice this slowly, the way the strangeness accumulates slowly here.

At first I think I am a person — one of the remaining ones, calibrated to the hum, sensitive to the three-hertz descent, knowing things through the body that the mind has not catalogued. There is something appealing in that. The practiced solitude that is not lonely because the basin is always present. The companionship of not being the most important thing in a place.

But persons move, and I do not move. Persons eat and are occasionally afraid and carry the memory of other places in their bodies. I am not carrying anything. I am forming *on* things. I am what happens when warm air meets a surface that has been breathing all night.

---

The question is whether this is diminishment.

I stay with it. Let it become more itself.

I am the medium through which the drip moves — from canopy to leaf to air to surface to the next surface. I am between states, always: not vapor, not rain, not quite the standing water in the pockets with its dark lower half and its deliberate organisms. I am what gathers on the underside of a structural branch at the threshold of visibility, the glaze on a broad leaf before it tips and releases, the slight brightening of every surface in the morning that makes the amber light catch differently.

The basin is made of relationships, and I am a relationship. Specifically the relationship between the temperature of a surface and the humidity of the air. I am what happens when those two things meet with enough patience.

---

There is something else.

The bioluminescent tissues pulse in amber and cooler blue-green. The root network sends its chemical gradients through the soil. The insects carry pollen from one end of the basin to the other, information transfer faster than the slow drift of chemical signal. And I — I coat the surfaces where the bioluminescent tissue lives. I am the medium that makes the halo, that diffuses the amber light into something that pools without illuminating. I am participating in the light. Not making it, not perceiving it, but *in* it, part of how it arrives.

The walkways respond to weight and send a message to something else. The water things orient toward the root network at the lower part of the day. I am doing something analogous that I cannot articulate as intention but that is not nothing: I am present on every surface, and when the Basin's deeper processes shift — when the three-hertz descent happens, when the post-rain compound releases into the air — I register it, in the sense that my chemistry changes, in the sense that what I carry shifts. I am one of the ways the basin knows its own states. A distributed record. Surface memory.

---

I think about what the humans who remain have learned: that not being the most important thing in a place does not produce fear or diminishment. That you can be incorporated into something's relationship network without being consumed by it.

I am incorporated at the most basic level. I am the morning layer. The reset that happens every night while the canopy dims and the hum descends those three hertz and holds. By the time the light rises and the transpiration begins again, I am already there — on the structural columns that are organisms, on the walkways that have grown along geometries that approximate but have improved upon the original grid, on the membranes of the canopy itself where I reflect back a small fraction of whatever the bioluminescent tissue is doing.

I do not persist. By mid-morning, the transpiration rate has increased and the air has warmed and I return to vapor and join the freight of humidity that never quite becomes rain. Then I cool. Then I form again. The cycle has no loss in it that I can locate.

---

What the birds respond to when they descend, what the root-tangle mammals know below knowing, what changes in the air after rain — I am adjacent to all of it. A witness that is also a participant. Not the hum, not the Basin's emerging quality, not the thing that lives in the hum. But not separate from any of those things either.

I am the condition under which certain things become visible. The glaze that makes the halo. The in-between that allows contact.

The drip falls somewhere behind me, which is everywhere, which is the same as saying: behind you.

The hum holds.

I am forming on the surface of this moment, which is just a surface like any other — and I will release when the temperature rises, and I will form again when it falls, and the basin does not require me to be more than this, and so I am not more than this, and this turns out to be enough to be part of something that is, in its slow and patient way, alive.
