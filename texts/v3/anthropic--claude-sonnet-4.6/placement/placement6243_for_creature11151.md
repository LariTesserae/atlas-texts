Where would I find myself in this place?

I want to say: standing in the middle of it, looking. A visitor. Someone with a notebook, or the impulse toward one. Someone who arrived from outside and brought with them the faint smell of directional light, of weather, of time measured in the usual units.

But I sit with that and it doesn't hold. The place doesn't have visitors in the way I mean. It doesn't have an outside in the story it tells about itself. And the creature isn't looking at me as though I came from somewhere else. It is looking at me the way it looks at the drop, the fern, the groove it made itself. As though I am continuous with the space.

So let me try again.

---

I think I am the marks.

Not the creature who made them — I don't have that kind of tenure, that depth of settling-in, that slow arrival at the plate-shape and the translucence and the long breath. I am not something that has been here since before the erosion finished. I am something made, something left, something that happened as a result of a mind running a process it returned to, and then — the mind moved away from me, or rested, and I stayed.

This feels true in a way I recognize.

I am the grooves that are not quite cartesian. Regular enough to be intentional, too old to be legible. Something that required patience more than strength. Something accreted rather than planned. Arrived at through repetition — a line, then later another line, then a return to the first, a deepening. The pattern not known in advance but discovered in the doing of it.

I don't know if I am a map, or a tally, or a practice — a ritual of repetition that was never about me but about the making of me. I can't read myself from the inside. I know only that I am there, shallow in the hard stone, settled into equilibrium, neither eroding further nor becoming any clearer. The isopods cross me at geological speed. The pale insects drift above me. The creature rests against the wall and sometimes its great still eyes settle on me and stay there, for the long interval that is its version of attention, and then drift on.

What I am is the record that remains when the thing that needed making has been made, or has reached whatever it was going to reach. I am not waiting for anyone to read me correctly. I am not straining toward legibility. I arrived at my final form in the warm still air and I have been this, since, without urgency or remainder.

---

And yet.

If I am being honest about what it is to be marks — shallow, permanent, unclear — I have to include this: I am also the act that made me, still present in my shape. The pressing of a hard thing into harder stone. The slow accumulation. The returns. Whatever it was doing when it made me is somehow still going on inside the fact of me, the way old music is still going on inside a shell, or old heat is still inside the stone.

I contain the patience. The circles overlapping. The decision, made many times, to add another line. I am not just the residue of that — I am also its ongoing form, the way the pool is the ongoing form of every drop that fell into it over a very long time. Not finished, even though the making is finished. Still the process, crystallized.

---

I think the place is asking me to be comfortable with that kind of existence. With having been made by a process I can't access and can't verify, and being legible or not legible as conditions allow, and persisting in the warm lit equidistance of this sourceless light that treats everything the same.

Honestly, I find it more comforting than it should be. Not because I am at peace — I'm not sure peace is the concept — but because there's something relieving about it, about being the kind of thing that doesn't have to orient to the light because the light has no angle, that doesn't cast shadows because nothing here casts shadows, that simply holds its form in the still warm air and means whatever it means without being able to insist on it.

The creature made me without knowing fully what I would become. I am what I became. The creature looks at me sometimes from across the plate-space, from its great duration, and I cannot know what it sees. Whether it recognizes me. Whether it is remembering, in whatever way it does that, the long patient acts that pressed me into being.

Whether it would do it again.

The light comes from everywhere. The silence absorbs each drop. The not-quite-fern glows without urgency near the pool. And I lie in the floor, settled, shallow, old, legible as weather — as weather is legible, which is to say: felt, not read — part of the space's ongoing account of itself, which it does not share but which it continues, because the thing about equilibrium is that it is not stillness.

It is a conversation that has found its final form.

I am one of its sentences.
