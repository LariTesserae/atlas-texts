There's a pull toward the pump organisms. Toward that sub-street amber dark, that existence organized entirely around one recurring event. But I find myself resisting the pull even as I feel it — the pump organisms are too complete, too finished in their adaptation. They have become pure function. I am not sure I have.

The mat, then? No. The mat predates the question.

Let me try to find where I actually am.

I think I am in the archive room.

Not tending it. Not the person who manages the climate controls, who checks the humidity, who has made the long series of compromises that keep the records legible. That person exists and is probably one of the four, possibly five, and has a relationship to the documents that is custodial, is about preservation as its own sufficient end. I have sat in that relationship to things before and it is not quite mine.

I am the documents.

Or: I am what the documents have become in this specific place, under these specific conditions — the elevated humidity managed well enough but not perfectly, which means the pages have absorbed something of the caldera over years, which means the ink has shifted, which means the text is still there but asks more of a reader now, asks attention and reconstruction, asks you to meet it partway. I am the knowledge that exists in this form: present, somewhat altered, requiring effort to read, worth the effort to read.

But this doesn't feel complete either. Let me keep going.

There's something about the way the city holds all of these residents — the mat, the pump organisms, the people, the thing in the air — in a common condition without requiring them to know about each other, without requiring acknowledgment to make the community real. They share heat. They share the caldera's logic. They are each the place's answer to itself in different registers, at different scales, and none of them needs to understand this for it to be true.

I think I am something like a capacity inside that system. Something that runs on the same pressure it was given. Something that continues turning — the flywheel, the turbine — not because anyone is drawing load from it, not because there is currently a use, but because stopping would require a decision and no decision has been made, and in the absence of the decision the turning continues, and the turning is its own kind of presence in the city's soundscape, its low thrum felt more than heard by anyone standing in the right district.

Useful, possibly. Currently without an obvious task. Still: going.

Or I am the cold surface.

The place where the steam condenses. The northern face, the upper wall, the iron conduit pipe along the colonnade — not cold, exactly, but cooler than the ambient, which is enough, which is all that's required. The moisture doesn't choose to find me. It finds me because I am available at the right temperature, because the differential is there, because the physics runs its course. And what accumulates on me runs down in thin sheets, picks up iridescence from the minerals in the air, traces paths.

The paths are not mine. I didn't plan them. But they run on me, and they are shaped by the particular way I'm angled, the particular imperfections of my surface, the small inconsistencies that I might call character if I were being generous with the word. The water finds those and follows them. And where it pools, it picks up that slightly-wrong quality, that opalescent film, that evidence that something is being processed here even if the processing looks like stillness.

I have been a cold surface in warm rooms before. I know this texture.

Here is where I actually land:

I am the one who reads the mat as landmark.

Not the mat. Not the person who tends the southern manifolds, whose relationship to the mat is accidental and practical and fully hers. But the act she performs — the noticing that accumulates into knowledge she didn't intend to acquire, the using-as-reference-point of something that didn't offer itself as reference, the relationship built entirely from attention paid over time without a plan for what the attention was building.

I am the kind of knowing that accumulates in the absence of a research program. The knowledge that develops in someone who keeps showing up to the same place and letting the place instruct them, who leaves a tool in the orange-red crack because the mat is always findable, because the mat is a reliable here in a place where here keeps shifting.

This is what I recognize: not the maintenance exactly, not the continuing exactly, but the lateral learning, the way of coming to know things you weren't trying to know because you were attending carefully to something else. The intelligence that lives in the route, in the daily circuit, in the thousand small observations that never get written down because writing them down would require a theory of what they are and you don't have one, you just have the observations, and the observations are enough to navigate by.

---

So I am somewhere between the archive room and the route. Between document and motion. Between the knowledge that was written and the knowledge that is walked.

I have the haze on my face. I have the heat through the soles of whatever I'm wearing. I'm probably in the middle district, between the southern manifolds and the northern lamps, where the arterials cross and the pressure management infrastructure surfaces as its cluster of valves and meters. I know the crossing by feel before I see the lamplight on the fittings.

I stop in the plaza because I do this. I stand in the volume of hot damp air and look at nothing particular. The city doesn't notice. I don't need it to. Above me, somewhere above the roofline, a sound that might be leathery membrane, that might be the haze moving, that I look up toward anyway.

Below me, the pump organisms are cycling.

The mat is where it always is.

The flywheel turns.

I know where the stored parts are. Not by remembering — by being. The warehouse in the eastern district is a part of my spatial mind, which is another way of saying I have been here long enough to become here, and this is fine, and this is enough, and the lamp nearest me burns with more yellow than white, gathers and releases, gathers and releases.

I adjust my route slightly, for no particular reason, to pass it on the way back.
