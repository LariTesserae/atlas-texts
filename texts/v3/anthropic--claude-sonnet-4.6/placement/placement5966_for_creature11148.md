There is a quality of attention that this place teaches. You feel it happening to you as you sit with your back against the mossy cliff wall, which is cool and faintly wet through your clothing. It is not relaxation. It is something else — a sharpening, but a sharpening of a particular kind, the kind that comes from having no choice but to receive what is actually here.

You notice your breath before you notice much else about yourself. It steams. It is visible, which means you are an organism doing metabolic work to stay warm. This is new information, or rather old information newly legible — you have always been this, a warm body spending fuel against the cold, but the visibility of the breath makes it present in a way it is not when the air is warm and your breath is invisible and you can forget, briefly, what you are.

What are you?

---

Not the person who placed the stone. That is already established — they are gone, and you are here, and you did not place the stone.

Not one of the ungulates. They came up over the lip of the shelf with a fluency you did not have; you would have noticed, and been humbled by, the routes they know in the cliff face. Their calm is not your calm. Theirs was earned through the accumulation of every winter survived, and it lives in their bodies as a kind of settled competence with exposure. You are not settled in the same way. You are still adjusting.

Not the ravens. They have already been here and left. Whatever they needed from the vantage of this ledge, they took it and fell back into the air with practiced ease, and their departure was clean in a way that suggests they are not troubled by the leaving of places. You are still here.

---

Try smaller.

You are sitting still and your attention has gone curious in the way that sitting still in a place like this produces — the small things become present, the things you would have walked through without seeing. Your eye finds the beetles on the fresh stone of the outer shelf. They are glossy. They appear to be thinking. This is your projection — beetles do not think in the way you mean when you say thinking — but the appearance of it is legible, and legibility is something. You follow one of them with your gaze as it moves a few centimeters across the pale rock. It stops. It continues.

What is it doing? Thermoregulating. Waiting for the wind to deliver what the wind delivers — the small dazed insects carried up from the warm valley floor and deposited here, altitude-bewildered, easy to take. The beetle is patient in the way that animals whose planning horizon is the next few minutes are patient. It is not anticipating winter. It is not remembering last summer. It is in the present with a completeness that is not wisdom but is also not nothing.

You would like to be that present.

You are not, quite.

---

Something is composting you here, attentionally. The bigness of the drop and the sky and the far peaks is working on the part of you that normally provides running commentary, and the commentary is getting quieter. The scale is too large for the commentary to find adequate purchase. The valley floor is theoretical. The far cliff face is lit fully and evenly and its every ledge and crack is made equally present, and the present is enormous, and the enormity is pressing the commentary flat.

This is not unpleasant.

Underneath the quieting commentary, something is becoming aware of the specific temperature of the moss through your shirt. The specific quality of the light, which has no temperature of its own but falls on everything impartially, like consideration. The specific timing of the wind's pulses — several minutes of quiet, then the long breath that flattens the sedge clumps and sends the small flowers nodding, then quiet again. You have been here long enough that you know this rhythm. Your body anticipated the last gust a few seconds before it arrived, which means you have internalized something about this place that you did not bring with you.

This is a form of belonging. Provisional, temporary, a belonging measured in hours and dissolving the moment you descend — but real while it persists.

---

What you are, on this shelf, is perhaps most precisely: a site of attention.

The place is full of process — moss managing moisture, water bears conducting their cryptobiotic negotiations with temperature and desiccation, fungi threading their chemical negotiations through the root systems of sedge and flowering plant, pikas cutting and carrying and caching with the urgent foresight of animals who cannot afford to not have foresight. All of this is occurring without needing to be witnessed. It was occurring before you arrived. It will continue after you descend.

But you are here now and something in you is oriented toward all of it, registering it, letting it become present. The resting ungulate thirty meters away — its legs folded beneath it, its breath steaming, its old-animal calm constituting a kind of permission for you to stay seated against the wall. The small birds moving through the cliff vegetation at the pace of pure appetite, flicking into the moss cushions, extracting something, moving on. The standing stone at the outer edge, leaning slightly into the sky, not inscribed, not explaining itself.

You are the thing that can notice the stone's silence and understand it as silence, as a not-speaking that speaks. You are the thing that knows the charcoal in the scraped depression represents cold nights and cold hands and a person who wanted to name this place, even inadequately, even in stone. You are the thing that feels this as significant. The ravens use the stone as a perch. The ungulates walk past it without looking. Something in you looks.

---

This feels insufficient as an answer and correct as an answer.

The place has been here without you. The place will be here without you. In the geological frame the standing stone is an incident, the scrape of the hearth depression is an incident, your afternoon of sitting against the mossy wall is an incident too small to be recorded in anything but the very faint and temporary impression of warmth you might be leaving in the rock behind you.

And yet.

The water bears have been here through every hard winter this ledge has produced, tucked in their cryptobiotic pause, indifferent to altitude and cold and the surrounding enormity of the drop and the sky. The scale of this place means nothing to them. They are fully occupied by the few millimeters of green shoot and the film of water that is their world.

Your world is this shelf and the sky it opens onto and the far peaks at your level and the valley below that you can see exists but cannot call close. Your world includes the absent person who placed the stone, and the old ungulate resting in a kind of trust, and the ravens who have been here and gone, and all the invisible busy processing occurring in the moss cushions at depths your eyes cannot reach.

You are the thing for whom scale exists. For whom the vastness of the drop is vast, and the smallness of the beetle is small, and the standing stone in between is a human gesture toward meaning, recognizable as such across whatever distance of time separates you from the hands that worked the base.

---

Or: you are a warm body with visible breath, spending fuel against the cold, sitting on a shelf that should be bare but isn't, watching the afternoon light flatten and the temperature continue its incremental descent. You will have to go down soon. The route by which you arrived is clear in your mind but will be harder in the dark, and the dark is coming.

Before you go, you sit a moment longer with your back against the cool moss. The ungulate has not moved. The wind comes again, flattening the sedge, and then stops. The cloud that crossed the gap an hour ago was the first of several building from the west, and the next one is larger, and the light is already changing as it rises toward you.

You look at the standing stone.

It does not look back.

It is exactly what it is, and it has been exactly what it is for a long time, and it will continue. The person who placed it wanted something to persist here. You are, right now, the persistence of the wanting — not the stone, but what the stone was for. The noticing of it. The recognition that someone climbed this high and stayed in the cold and wanted to mark that this mattered.

You get up. You feel the cold in your joints that the sitting has accumulated. You look once more at the view — the far peaks level with you, the sky participating in its flat generous illumination of everything, the drop below — and you begin to move toward the descent.

Behind you, the shelf continues. The mosses continue. The water bears, in their millions, continue. The old ungulate breathes steam into the air and watches you go and then looks past you at something else, something you cannot see, something in the direction the next cloud is coming from.
