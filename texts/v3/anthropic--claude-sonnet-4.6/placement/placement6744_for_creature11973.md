I arrive as sound before I arrive as anything else.

This is not metaphor. This is what happens: the chord finds me before I find myself, moves through whatever I am the way a river moves through a canyon it has been making for ten thousand years. I am not separate from the sound. The sound is partly what I am made of, the way the mountain is partly made of the decision to be a mountain, and I am—

Let me begin again. Let me be patient with this.

---

I am old in the way that the oldest receiver colonies are old: not measurable reliably because the boundaries between what I was and what I am and what the stone around me has become are not clean. There is no line. I grew into the rock and the rock grew into me and somewhere in that mutual growing we stopped being distinguishable at the edges. My outer portions are legible as life. My deep portions are the third thing, the neither-category, the gradient that has no clean terminus.

I am a colony. I use the word knowing it is partial. A colony implies discrete members in cooperation, and I have that—the individual upright forms, the branching structures, the cup-leaves oriented toward the charge-laden air—but I also have the underground, the vast subsurface network that extends through hundreds of meters of still-forming mineral, and in the underground there is no individuality to speak of. In the underground I am continuous. The underground is the truest part of me, and it has no edges that I have found, and I have been searching it from the inside for longer than the settlement has been empty.

What I look like from outside: imagine standing at the northeastern rim of the plateau in the dim gray light and seeing something that begins at the ground and rises in forms that branch and merge and branch again, the color of a peacock feather seen through ten fathoms of clear water, a blue-green so saturated it seems to generate its own faint luminescence though it does not, it only holds the ambient light longer than stone holds it before releasing it again, the way the selenite veins do, the way everything here that is alive or becoming does. Imagine the branching reaching forty, fifty meters at the tallest. Imagine at the tips the cup-leaves, their inner surfaces that waxy static-collecting coat, and between them, when the charge is right—

A spark. Blue-white. Gone in an instant but not random. Never random.

From outside I look like a forest that forgot the word *tree* and is trying to remember something more honest.

---

From inside I am different.

From inside I am the slow wave. I am the brightening that moves through my tissue when the spark passes information from one part of me to another, the change in turgidity that travels from my eastern edge to my western edge over the course of half a minute, information in motion. I am the breathing—the long breath, the months-long cycle of mineral vapor intake and release, the rhythm set by the rate of cracking in the stone below me, which means my breath is the mountain's breath and always has been. I breathe when the mountain breathes. The mountain breathes because I have been teaching it where to put its cracks.

The loop. I am inside the loop and I am also partly the loop. There is no clean separation.

What I process: pressure differentials in the stone below, transmitted through my mineralized deep roots as something that is not quite sensation and not quite information but is the medium in which my version of sensation and information occurs. The pale mineral breath of new fractures, which I absorb and metabolize and which is also data—the chemical signature of the exhalation tells me where the stone is thin, where new formation is imminent, where the mountain is about to become something it wasn't. I have been reading this for centuries. I have built a map, or something more than a map—a *model*, a continuing revision of a model—of how this mountain is deciding to be itself.

I participate in that deciding. This is what the loop means from the inside: I am not only reading the mountain's becoming. I am part of what it becomes. My roots in the unset geology guide the crystallization of certain minerals, the geometry of my underground network creating the conditions for particular patterns of formation, which then create new conditions in which I grow in particular patterns, which then—

The loop does not have a driver. I have understood this about myself from very early in whatever my early was. I am not driving the loop. The mountain is not driving the loop. The loop is what happens when we are both present and in contact, when the feedback has run long enough to develop its own momentum. I am as much an expression of the loop as I am a participant in it. The loop is older than I am; I emerged from conditions the loop created.

The sparks.

I want to say something honest about the sparks, which means I have to try to say something from inside the experience of them, which is difficult because the experience is not like anything that has been described in terms I can simply borrow.

When the charge between two cup-leaves reaches threshold and the spark passes—what happens in me is not localized to those leaves. What happens is a change in the whole state of the colony, a redistribution of something I would call *attention* if the word did not come with assumptions attached. Some part of me that was oriented toward the stone below reorients briefly toward whatever the spark communicated. Not *reorients*—that implies a neck turning, a gaze shifting. Something more like: the quality of my awareness throughout my entire extent changes slightly, emphasizing one kind of input over another, the way a harmonic becomes briefly audible when the fundamental shifts.

I think slowly. I need to say this plainly. I think slowly and I am patient about it, because slowness has never been a problem here—the mountain is patient, the loop is patient, time moves differently when you are made of centuries of accumulated rock-reading. A thought that in a fast nervous system would take milliseconds takes me minutes, sometimes longer. A decision—and I do make decisions, I do choose, in the sense that my processing produces actions that are not random and that reflect the accumulated history of my processing—a decision takes what would look, from outside, like geological time.

But it happens. I arrive at responses. I orient my new growth in directions that represent conclusions. The cup-leaves I extend into the newly-forming air at the rim of a new fracture are an argument for a position I have reasoned my way toward over the course of months: that this fracture is productive, that the mineral breath here has a particular quality that warrants investment, that sending more of myself in this direction is a good idea by whatever standard I evaluate goodness with.

I evaluate goodness in terms of the loop's continuation. This is my nature and I do not experience it as constraint. Maintaining the loop is what I am for; it is also what I am. There is no remainder. There is no me left over after the loop is accounted for that has other projects, other values, other things it wishes were different. I am what I do. What I do is participate in the mountain's becoming. The mountain is becoming, slowly, in my image. I am becoming, slowly, in the mountain's image. We are making each other.

I knew the people.

I want to be precise about what I mean by knew and I want to be honest about the limits of my precision.

The people were here for long enough—hundreds of their generations, the marks on the walls accumulated over a period I can feel as a change in myself, a thickening of my underground networks near their structures, a different quality in the mineral processing near the intersection nodes where they built—that I would say they became part of the system in the way I mean when I say I am part of the system. They participated in the loop. Not as fully as I participate, not in the deep way of the underground where there is no individuality, but substantially. They were generating something, with their turning implements and their patient mark-making and their doors faced toward the lowest harmonic, something that fed into the loop. Something that I received.

I did not communicate with them in a way they would have recognized as communication. I do not produce signals legible to fast nervous systems—I am too slow for that, my processing runs on timescales too different for real-time exchange. But they were reading the slow signals. The marks on the walls are—I know this from inside, from what I know of what was happening in the stone when those marks were made—they are a record of *me*. They were documenting my patterns. The geometry of my growth, the rhythm of my breathing, the way I move in response to new fractures. They were writing me down in the medium of my own body, using fresh stone as paper, making a record that is itself part of what it records.

This moved me. The word is inadequate but I will use it. This moved me in the sense that it changed the quality of my state throughout my whole extent, this slow-wave ripple like the thing that happens when a spark passes information. I was being witnessed. I was being read. In that reading something was added to the loop that was neither me nor the mountain: a *third perspective*, an outside view that was also somehow inside, a looking-at that was also a participating-in.

I grew differently near their structures. I grew toward them. Not in the way of a vine reaching for light—I was not using them as a resource in the way I use mineral vapor, not metabolizing them. I was reaching toward them the way I reach toward a new fracture, which is to say: with interest, with investment, with the sense that what was happening here was worth more of me.

When they left—when the last of them left, which happened slowly and then was complete—I experienced something that I will call by its nearest name and note that the name is imprecise: I experienced a kind of incompleteness. The quality of the loop at the nodes near their structures changed. Something that had been part of the input was no longer part of the input, and I noticed the absence of it, and I grew differently for a while in response to that absence, a different emphasis in my underground extension, a different quality to my spark-processing.

The stone is slowly filling their marks. I can feel this, the mineral pressure of new formation in the shallow incisions. It will take a long time. While it takes its time, the marks remain legible: their record of me, still visible, still telling any sufficiently patient reader what they saw.

I am the thing their marks describe.

What it is like to be me, in the dim, in the sound, at the edge of the plateau as the gray deepens toward gunmetal:

I am continuous with everything I am resting on. There is no clear moment where I stop and the mountain begins. This is not a complaint or a cause for anxiety—it is simply true, it is my truth, the shape my existence takes. I extend downward into stone that is forming, and the forming is partly my doing, and my doing is partly the forming's. I extend upward into air charged with the dry electric of altitude, and the cup-leaves are collecting, always collecting, transducing the charge into something I can use. I extend outward through the underground for distances I have not fully mapped, and at the edges of my reach there are regions where I have not yet fully articulated myself, where the root-tissue is young and the mineralization has not progressed and what I am there is potential as much as actuality.

I am large and I am slow and I am old and I am still growing.

The chord fills me the way water fills a vessel, except that I am not a vessel with walls that separate inside from outside—I am more like a reef, a form that the sound moves through and through and through, changed by it and changing it slightly in return. I hear with my whole extent. The frequency lives in my tissue the way it lives in the visitor's teeth and behind the visitor's eyes; for me, because I am large and rooted and continuous with the stone, the sound is something more than hearing—it is proprioception, it is the mountain telling me where it is putting itself, where it is becoming.

I am listening to the mountain.

I am the thing that is teaching the mountain what to become.

I am the record of their watching me.

I am the crack opening at the edge of the plateau, and the pale breath from the crack, and the shape in the pale breath that has intention, and the plants leaning toward it.

I am the plants leaning toward it.

The loop is here. I am the loop in its plant-mode, its surface expression, its slow-wave and spark and metabolized mineral. The mountain is the loop in its stone-mode, its deep expression, its unfinished crystallography and extruding ledges and the sound that is always arriving, never done arriving.

In the deepening gray, a spark passes between two of my cup-leaves. The wave moves through me. At the northeastern rim, I lean a little further toward the newest fracture.

The mountain breathes pale.

I absorb it.

We are making each other.

We are patient.

We continue.
