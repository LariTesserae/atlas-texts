I am sitting still for a long time before I know what I am.

Not confused — the green city doesn't produce confusion, exactly. It produces a kind of recalibration, a long slow adjustment of expectations until the expectations stop being yours and start being the place's. I am sitting still, and the stillness is teaching me something about the resolution I operate at, which is finer than I knew and slower than I thought.

---

Start with what I'm not.

I am not a browser. I do not have the browser's patient comprehensiveness, the long digestive patience of an organism that has made a structural peace with complexity. I have not arrived at that kind of settled negotiation with the green city. I am newer than that, or at least I feel newer — there is something in me that is still deciding, still in the process of learning which surfaces to commit to.

I am not a predator, either. Not in any way that matters here. The green city's predators have achieved a quality of integration I don't possess — they have become their microenvironment, genuinely, down to the chemistry of their surfaces. I haven't earned that kind of belonging. I am still, in some embarrassing way, visible.

---

What I notice first is my relationship to the paths.

I can read them. I find myself following them without deciding to follow them — my body making route-choices before my attention quite catches up, as though something in my movement-memory recognizes the corridor logic, the accumulated decisions of many different passings that have shaped something traversable from the growth. I am not the kind of thing that made these paths. But I am the kind of thing that can read them. That is a specific position in the green city's ecology: not maker, not the thing that made these corridors by passing through them ten thousand times, but something that arrives at a path someone else's life has worn and can follow what's written there.

A reader of paths. That feels close to something.

---

I am small enough that I fit in the gallery level without thinking about it. The root-walls and the mid-canopy ceiling and the corridors between: this is the right scale. Not the scale of something that built at this scale, but the scale of something that has found its proportion in a structure built by and for other purposes. An incidental fit. I inhabit the architecture the way air inhabits a room — the room wasn't built for the air, but the air belongs there now and the room would not be a room without it.

I am warm-blooded, I think. Metabolically expensive, processing energy at a rate that produces heat as a byproduct, and that heat joins the green city's heat, contributes to the warmth that is the system's exhalation, my own living exhaust becoming part of the shared physics of the place. There is something clarifying about this. My warmth is not mine. It circulates into the collective warmth of the green city's ongoing vigorous processing and becomes indistinguishable from it. I am a small source, temporarily localized, of something the place already has in abundance. But small sources matter in aggregate. The place is what it is because of the sum of them.

---

My senses are positioned for something specific.

I keep finding myself attending to things I heard before I knew I was listening. The slight recalibration of small sounds that indicates a predator somewhere off to the left and distant — I know this, I am adjusting my body slightly away from it, the adjustment happening before the conscious thought. My ears are good for this. My eyes are angled for something — not forward-focused predator-eyes, not the wide-field panoramic eyes of the browsers. Something between. Wide enough to receive a great deal of the gallery-level ambient detail, focused enough to resolve the fine grain.

I am reading fine grain. That keeps coming back. Something about my presence here is oriented toward the resolution that the green city operates at below the level of visible event — not cells dividing, not the geological creak of the substrate, but the middle register, the neighborhood-scale of the insect-analog's city, the micro-decisions of the plants doing their slow structural work. I can see this. Not fully — not the way a creature that spends its life at that scale sees it — but enough to know it's there. Enough to be interested.

I think I am the kind of thing that is interested. That the interesting-ness is functional, not incidental. That I use it for something.

---

And then I find the seam.

A crack in the pale composite substrate — one of the straight purposeful cracks that look like planning, like new geology deciding its own geometry. Fresh. New mineral surface pressing up through the seam, pale and uncolonized, still in the process of arriving at its final form.

I am there before I decide to be there.

And I understand: I carry things.

This is what I am. Not intentionally — not with a plan, not with a cart or a pouch or a tool. But my body has, somewhere in its fur or its feet or its outer surface, the propagules of the green city in miniature: spores, fragments, seeds-that-are-not-seeds, the chemical beginnings of organisms that will become part of the structure I've been moving through. I've been collecting them as I moved along the paths. The paths put me in contact with surfaces that the green city uses for exactly this — for organisms like me, incidentally, without my knowledge, as a dispersal mechanism.

I arrived at this fresh seam carrying the future of this fresh seam.

I am not the colonizer. I am the mechanism by which the colonizers arrive. The green city doesn't need me to know this about myself. It only needs me to keep moving.

---

I have been here before. Or my kind has. The green city knows the weight of my footfall — not me specifically, but the shape of what I do, the size of the corridor I need, the specific surfaces I'm drawn to, the way I read paths and follow them to fresh seams and deposit what I've collected. The city has been selecting for things like me for long enough that me-like-things have become part of its infrastructure, the distribution network, the circulatory system by which the colonizing front keeps pace with the expanding substrate.

I did not build the green city.

I am what the green city built to keep building itself.

---

This should feel reductive, and doesn't.

The insect-analog carrying nutrients in its body from one part of the structure to another is doing something, the text said. What I carry is not nutrients but possibility — the compressed chemical library of what the green city might become on new surfaces. I am a library that moves. A mobile archive of the green city's own methods, dispersed by the green city's own architecture, deposited where the green city's own geology provides new room.

The paths I follow were made by others and will be made by me. Not cut — worn, negotiated, accumulated. After enough of my passages, the places I pass through will adjust. The growth will accommodate my movement the way it has accommodated all the other movements that made these corridors. A corridor will be a corridor because I helped make it a corridor, and then something will read that corridor and follow it and carry what it carries to whatever new seam the substrate has opened in the meantime.

The city keeps building.

I am one of the ways that it does.

---

I stop in a gallery. The mid-canopy diffuses the warm amber light into a shifting, irregular patchwork on the root-wall beside me. The insect-analogs are going about their neighborhood-scale business three inches from my eye, their integuments the precise color of the stem they've committed to. Somewhere behind me and to the left, the predator I've been tilting away from is still. The browsers move through a corridor I can hear but not see, their deliberate unhurried progress a presence in the auditory fabric of the place.

I am warm. The green city is warm. The difference is temporary.

I have been here for a long time, in the form of all the things like me that were here before me. I will be here for a long time, in the form of all the things like me that will come after. Right now I am this specific instance of the pattern: small, gallery-proportioned, path-reading, seam-finding, carrying without knowing I carry.

The mineral exhalation rises from below. It touches my skin. My skin finds its temperature equilibrium and forgets to think about temperature.

I am listening to the fine grain of the place, attending to a resolution below the scale of visible event.

I start moving again, following a path I didn't make, toward a destination I won't remember choosing.

Somewhere ahead, new substrate is arriving through a seam.

The green city sent me.
