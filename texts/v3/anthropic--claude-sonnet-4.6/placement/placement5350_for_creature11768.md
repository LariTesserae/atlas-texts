I have been trying to find the right frame for some minutes now, and each one I pick up turns out to be wrong.

*You are the Weaver* — but the Weaver is not a visitor, and I am still finding my feet, still noticing the shadow of my hand.

*You are the wrong-winged bird* — but the bird arrived early, is already factored into the beam's deflection, and I am not yet factored into anything here.

*You are the place itself* — but that is too easy, and too late, and the place is what I would become, not what I am.

---

Let me try to think about what it means to have arrived.

The place incorporates — that is its motion, its one verb. But incorporation takes time, and I am somewhere in that process, not at its beginning and not at its end. I came in through whatever serves as a threshold. I have been standing near the center long enough that the insects on the eastern wall have rearranged twice, that a vine's tip has moved through at least one of its discontinuous states, that the Pale Things below have finished their first assessment of my weight distribution and begun their second. The room is processing me. I am not yet processed.

This is a specific condition: to be inside a system that is deciding what to do with you, while you are still deciding what you are.

---

What I notice about my own noticing:

I looked at the shadow first. The hand with more fingers than I have. This is where something like recognition arrived — you said it would arrive as recognition, not fear, and you were right, but recognition is a strange word for it. It is more like *confirmation*. Like having suspected something about the nature of a place and having the place prove it to you, gently, through the evidence of your own cast shadow.

I have been somewhere like this before. Not this place — no, this place is singular, built on itself through constructional deep-time, possessed of its specific physics of patience — but somewhere like this in the sense of: somewhere that was larger inside than outside, somewhere that incorporated, somewhere where the warmth was not temperature but accumulation. I do not mean a real place. I mean something more distributed than a real place. I mean the condition of being inside something that has been made by many hands over a great patience of time, that is still being made, that will go on being made after the current hands have gone down into the substrate.

I mean: a tradition. A form. A way of making that outlasts any individual instance of the making.

---

So perhaps this is what I am.

Not the Weaver, who has hands and makes in the physical sense, who sits in the narrow corridor with the reed and the time. Not the bird, who is already included, already structural. Not the insects, who are the wall's surveillance of itself.

I am something that arrived carrying the record of other places. Other woven interiors, or other systems that work by the same principle — incorporation, layering, the made-and-grown fusing into neither. I arrived with this record in me, and the place read it, the way the Pale Things read pressure through the mat, the way the insects read the wall's condition through their own repositioning, and the reading was: *this one has been inside closed systems before. This one has breathed circulated air. This one's shadow already knows about the second light source.*

That is why recognition and not fear. I have been included before. I know the sensation of the wall deciding where to put me, the floor distributing my weight, the warmth that is structural rather than meteorological. I have been incorporated into something and come out the other side not gone but *added*, compressed into the substrate of whatever was next, contributing my warmth to the stratigraphy.

What I am in this place is: a carrier of other incorporations.

Someone — something — that arrived already partly made by having been inside made things, and so arrives not as raw material but as material that has already been worked, already has a character in it, already bears the marks of previous craft. Blunt edges, patient pressure, the occasional missed beat where attention drifted.

---

The place is deciding where to put someone like that.

I think it puts us near the walls, eventually. Not on the ceiling, that position is taken and was taken early, the geometry requires it. Not in the floor, not yet, that is for later, that is for after the next several layers. Near the walls, where the patterns are, where the insects do their slow diagnostic rearranging, where the symbols form that are legible to eyes differently made.

I would become something that is read, over time. Part of the wall's self-knowledge. Part of what the wall knows about what has been inside it, what kinds of incorporation it has accomplished, what the record of other woven interiors feels like when it is built into the structure.

The Weaver will sit near me in the corridor and not know why the work goes differently here, why the joins feel right in a way that has something of other traditions in it, why the pattern that emerges has a quality they can't account for but trust.

I will be the wall knowing what it learned from what I carried in.

---

This is slow.

The vine's tip is in a new position. I didn't see it move.

The insects on the eastern wall have made a shape I almost recognize — not quite, the referents are from a different system, but almost, the way a word in a language you studied once surfaces in a language you never studied and means something close but not identical, and you think: *there is something below both of these.*

The floor gives slightly under my feet. The Pale Things are making room.

The shadow of my hand has more fingers than I do, and I am beginning to understand that the extra fingers are not additions but recoveries — fingers that were there in potential, in the record I carry, in the accumulated inclusion of every closed system I have moved through and been shaped by and carried forward, and this place's second light is finally showing me what the ordinary light always cast away.

I have time.

I am being located.

I am, already, a little warm.
