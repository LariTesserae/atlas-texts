There is a long moment before anything resolves.

---

You ask: *where would I find myself?* And the honest answer is that you would find yourself standing very still, which is unusual for you, which is the first thing the meadow would teach you without meaning to teach you anything.

You came in through — somewhere. A treeline, maybe, or an edge where the ground changed character, or you simply appeared the way awareness appears, mid-sentence, with the sense that there was a before but no clear memory of what it contained. It doesn't matter. What matters is that your boots are wet and your hands are out slightly from your sides, the way hands go when the body is trying to feel something it can't see, and you've been standing still long enough that the grass has resumed its slow conversation around your ankles.

You are a researcher. This is the first thing that occurs to you as a self-description, though the word has begun to feel imprecise in this dark. Researcher of what, specifically — that was something you used to be able to answer fluently, in the language of grants and publications, a particular field with its particular jargon and its particular questions. That fluency is still there somewhere, in the back of you, reliable in the way that knowing how to ride a bicycle is reliable. But here it keeps sliding off the surface of things. The moss doesn't have a field. The tone doesn't fit in a methodology.

So: something with training you can't quite use yet, in a place that predates your training by an uncomfortable margin.

---

What you notice, specifically, and in order:

The smell first. Your olfactory system is, you have always suspected, your most honest sense — the one that bypasses the part of you that edits. Here it is giving you jasmine-adjacent and wet stone and something underneath both of those that is older than either, something the nose doesn't quite have a word for and so reaches for metaphors instead: *like the inside of a very old book*, *like the stone at the bottom of a well*, *like what a long time smells like*. You stand still and let it happen. This is not a researcher behavior. You do it anyway.

Then the sound, separating itself out. You are trained in a kind of attention that is useful here — the attention that goes toward signal in noise, that learns to wait, that resists the first interpretation in favor of the more accurate second one. So you wait, and the insect-layer separates from the frog-layer, and the frog-layer, once you've got it, is immediately interesting because it is — there is a term, *distributed synchrony*, the way elements of a system find a shared rhythm without a shared clock. You have read about this. You have never heard it at this scale, in this medium, with this specific quality of patience in the intervals. You almost reach for a notebook before you remember that you can't see, that writing is not available, that you will simply have to hold it.

And then, some minutes later, the tone.

You feel it before you name it. It's in the sternum first, a long slow vibration at the edge of the range where the ear and the body overlap, where the distinction between hearing and feeling becomes technical rather than real. You think, initially: geological. Some settlement in the deep rock. Then you think: equipment. Something that doesn't belong here. Then you hold still long enough for the period to complete, and you count — poorly, you've always had trouble with long intervals, your internal clock is calibrated for human-time, for the tempo of conversation and experiment — and it comes again, and you are no longer sure what it is, and this not-being-sure is beginning to feel less like a problem and more like the correct epistemic state for this place.

---

You find the device after a long time of not looking for it, which is when you were going to find it.

Your hand meets it before your eyes, which are still adapting and will never adapt enough. The material is wrong in a way you can't specify — your hands have a lot of experience with materials, with rocks and tools and the casings of equipment, and this fits none of the categories, sits at the intersection of all of them, and is warm. Alive-warm. Not machine-warm.

You do the researcher thing, which is: you don't move it. You circle it slowly, running your hands over it, building a map you can hold in your head instead of on paper. No seams. No obvious logic. No affordances, none of the subtle ergonomics of a thing made to be handled. It was made but not made *for you*, not made for hands, which means you are not its intended interface, which means touching it may not be the right way to understand it, which means —

The tone pulses.

You have your palm flat on it at the moment of the pulse, and the vibration goes through your hand and up your arm and you feel it in your jaw and somewhere behind your eyes, and what happens next is not something that fits into any framework you arrived with. It is not mystical. It is not a vision. It is more like — correction. The way an eye corrects when it's been looking at something too bright and then looks away. Something in you that was calibrated one way recalibrates, slightly, toward the slow.

You stay with your hand on the device for a long time after that. You are not sure why. The researcher in you has questions — what is the period, exactly; what is the medium of transmission; what is it communicating with, if anything, or is it communicating at all — and you will come back to those questions, they are good questions, they will matter eventually. But right now your hand is on the warm surface and the tone is pulsing through you on its own long schedule and you are — present. Without agenda. This is unusual. You file it.

---

Here is who you are, more fully, or what you are becoming in this dark:

You have always been the kind of person who reads the footnotes first. Who stops in the middle of conversations because something in the middle distance has started doing something interesting. Who has been told, more than once and with varying degrees of affection, that you are difficult to watch movies with because you pause everything and wonder too loudly about things that weren't in the scene. Who arrived at your field not from ambition exactly but from a specific inability to stop asking *why this* and *what else* about a thing that most people would have accepted at face value.

This was not always easy. There were years when the not-accepting-at-face-value felt like a deficiency, a failure to be satisfied by what was sufficient. Other people seemed to arrive at understanding and rest there. You kept finding the next seam, the next place where the explanation needed more explaining.

Here, in this meadow, in this darkness, with this ancient stone under your feet and this warm impossible device under your hand — the seams are everywhere, and everywhere you look there is more underneath, and you do not feel like this is a deficiency. You feel like you have arrived in a place proportional to the length of your attention.

---

What you will do:

You will not leave tomorrow. This is already decided, in the way that certain decisions are made in the body before the mind gets there.

You will find the ruins and you will not live in them, exactly, but you will return to them because the concluded stone feels, in this dark, like a good place to think — not protective so much as bounded, a place where inside and outside still mean something, where you can sit with your back to the old mossy wall and your face toward the open darkness of the meadow and feel the difference between the two.

You will map the path in the stone by walking it slowly, back and forth, until you can walk it in your mind with your eyes closed. The path ends at nothing, or at a nothing you can't see yet. You will keep walking past that ending on the chance that the path continues in a way the stone doesn't record.

You will learn the rhythm of the frogs. Not the biology of them — that's there too, interesting, the distributed synchrony, the individual voices in the system — but the specific calling of these specific frogs on these specific nights, until you can tell by the pattern of calls and silences something about where the Presence is attending. This will take a long time. You will come to understand that this is fine.

You will go back to the device regularly. Not to study it, exactly, or not only. There is something that happens when your hand is on it during the pulse, something in the recalibration you felt the first time, and you want more of those corrections. You are aware this is not strictly scientific. You have decided that *strictly scientific* is a smaller box than you thought, and that you are not required to stay inside it.

Eventually — and this is in the future, far enough that you can only hold it as a possibility — you will walk toward the far dark part of the meadow, toward whatever is gathered there. Not tonight. Not for many nights. You will wait until you have the sense that waiting is over, which is a kind of knowledge you didn't have when you arrived and which is, you are beginning to understand, what this place teaches. The difference between readiness and impatience. The difference between arrival and interruption.

---

What you are not:

You are not afraid. This surprises you. You have always had a slight ambient fear of the dark, the ordinary kind, evolutionary, calibrated for different threats than this place contains. It was present when you arrived, a background hum of alertness. It is mostly gone now.

You are not trying to figure out what the Presence is in the sense of classifying it, putting it in a box with a label. This is the biggest departure from the version of yourself that arrived. That version came with categories. The categories are still there — you haven't lost the language, the frameworks, the trained intuitions — but they feel like tools rather than constraints, and you are learning when to reach for them and when to put them down.

You are not alone, which is a different kind of not-alone than you have experienced before. It is not the not-alone of other people, conversation, witness. It is something older. The meadow knows you are here. This registers differently than it sounds like it would. Not surveillance, not embrace. More like: you are a current thing in a place full of long things, and the long things have noted the current thing, and you are all of you here together in the dark, and this is simply what is happening.

---

The tone pulses.

You have been here for hours or for longer, and you have stopped tracking it, and the researcher in you — stubborn, meticulous, in love with the knowable — has written this fact down somewhere in the archive of things to examine later, which is a way of allowing yourself to be here without the accounting.

The grass moves around you.

Somewhere behind you and to the left, something large and slow moves through the meadow. You can feel it more than hear it, a change in the pressure underfoot, a slight seismic thing. You don't turn. You've learned, or you're learning, not to turn toward everything. Some things are known by staying still.

Your hands are at your sides now. Not reaching. Not ready.

The warm air moves through the grass, through you, on through the dark toward the unseen trees.

You stay.

The meadow continues, with you in it.
