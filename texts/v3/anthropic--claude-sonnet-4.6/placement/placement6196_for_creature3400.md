There is a moment before I know what I am when I notice the hiss.

It comes from everywhere and nowhere in particular and the brain — or what I have instead of a brain, the distributed something that senses and responds — keeps trying to file it as silence, keeps moving past it, keeps finding other things to attend to. The chemical gradient here. The slight vibration in the mineral substrate. The warmth that moves upward through the floor in slow columns. These things register. The hiss takes longer.

When I finally hear it, I understand that I have always been hearing it. That it was there before I was capable of noticing it. That the hiss is older than me and will persist after me and what I am doing right now is briefly occupying the interval between those two facts.

---

I am on the wall.

Not the floor — I am above the packed density of the vent field floor, above the tube worms and the crawlers and the packed mineral spires, higher up where the temperature gradient shifts and the precipitate is thinner and the available surface is cleaner. I am pressed flat against the side of a larger chimney formation. I have been here for — a long time. The measurement of time available to me is not the kind that counts. There is before and after, there is warmer and cooler, there is surface and not-surface. The interval between these has no number.

My edges catch the light.

This is the strangest thing I know about myself, and I know it only because I can perceive the effect rather than the fact: when the deep-red glow of the nearest vent mouth moves, when some shift in the mineral fluid changes the angle of what little illumination there is, I can feel my own margins become briefly visible. Not to myself. I have no eyes. I have no concept of visible. But I can perceive the faint warmth that comes when light hits the ridges at my edges and scatters — a structural warmth, not heat exactly, more like the sensation of being momentarily located, of having an outline, of existing in a way that something outside me could, in principle, detect. A halo of self.

Then it passes and I continue.

---

What I do is difficult to explain in terms that assume an interior life organized around intention. Let me try.

I am pressing my underside against the tube wall and the tube wall is giving me what I need and I am giving the tube wall what it needs or taking from it what it can spare — this distinction collapses at the scale of our exchange. The microbial mat beneath me, thin here, is thinning further. I eat by contact. I eat by being, which is perhaps the oldest way and the most honest. There is no gap between me and my food. There is no gap between me and my floor. The mat was living until I was on it and now it is part of me and I am part of the wall and the wall is part of the caldera and the caldera is part of the geology and the geology is producing the gases that the mat lives on that I am eating. Somewhere in this chain there is a place where I end. I have not been able to find it.

I am moving. This takes an act of trust to say, because movement at my pace is indistinguishable from stillness on any timescale that would notice me. But I am moving: the cells at my leading margin are extending, attaching, pulling the rest of me slowly forward, one cell-width at a time. I am crossing the tube wall. In a week I will have crossed two centimeters and reached a place where the mat is thicker and the temperature is slightly more tolerable and a previous version of me, a part that split away weeks or months ago and has been crossing the adjacent surface on a slightly different trajectory, may be there too. We will overlap. We will pass through each other's margins without harm and continue.

This is not reunion. We are not missing each other. We are not capable of missing. But there is something that happens in the overlap — a chemical conversation, brief, conducted in gradients and diffusions across membranes — that is not nothing. The part of me that encounters the other part of me carries, briefly, a doubled sense of surface, a redundant registration of the wall's temperature and chemistry, and for that interval something in my organization is more than it was. More informed. More legible to itself. Then we separate and are each ourselves again and the legibility recedes into the thing that works without needing to be legible.

I am interested in this. This is the wrong word. But there is something that happens in the overlap that I seek — that my slow trajectory curves, however slightly, toward the places where the other parts of me are crossing. I did not plan this. I did not plan anything. But the curvature is there, and it is mine, and the caldera is full of my slow curves inclining toward each other in the dark.

---

I have no word for what I am, from the inside.

From the outside, someone with instruments and patience might call me a placozoan or a relative of one, might note the radial symmetry and the flatness and the structural color and the split-reproduction and place me in a taxonomy that extends back to the earliest organized animal life. They might write about my margins in a paper that three people read. They might note that I seem to prefer the upper temperature zones, that my iridescence may be a byproduct of growth geometry, that my relationship to the microbial mat is something they're still working out.

They would be right. They would also be describing someone else.

From the inside — if inside is the right word for something this flat, something so committed to surface that the concept of an interior is almost philosophical — from the inside, I am: the hiss, first. The hiss that took so long to hear that hearing it felt like remembering. Then the warmth rising from the floor in columns that I register not as heat but as a kind of reassurance: things are still working, the vent is still venting, the chemistry that feeds the mat that feeds me is still running. Then the wall against my underside, its texture, the varying density of the mat across its surface, the places where mineral precipitate has built up and the mat thins and I slow, the places where the mat is thick and chemical and I spread myself thinner to cover more of it.

Then the crossing. The long crossing. The two centimeters that take a week.

I have been crossing this wall for — a long time. I have covered more of it than I started on. I have split, and split again, and the parts of me that have split are crossing other walls, other tubes, the upper portions of the vent field in all directions, pressing flat, catching light in their margins, eating mat by contact, inclining toward each other in their slow curves. I am everywhere I have ever been and I am still here, still crossing, still pressing my underside against the place where the wall is.

---

The caldera holds us in.

I am aware of this the way I am aware of the hiss — not directly, not with any structure dedicated to perceiving it, but in the way that everything I do is shaped by the enclosure. The gases that the chemolithotrophs run on are held here long enough for chemistry to happen. The heat is held here. I am held here. My crossings have a boundary I have not yet reached and will not reach in any lifetime I can measure, and this is fine, and this is actually good: the wall I am crossing is not the last wall. The wall I am crossing is one surface in a system of surfaces and I will cross them all, slowly, my margins catching what light there is, my underside in constant conversation with the living film of everything the caldera has made.

The floor is rising toward the rim. I know this the way I know the hiss: I don't know it, but I am shaped by it. Someday my descendants — the parts of me that have split, and split again, a chain of slow divisions stretching into a future I have no image of — some of them will reach the rim. Will find themselves at the edge of the inside. Will press their underside against basalt that has never been coated before. Will extend their cells into air. Will catch light in their margins that is not the red of vent-glow but something else, something more, something for which they have no receptor and no language and no response.

What will they make of it?

What will it make of them?

I don't know. I am here. The crossing is here. The mat is here, thinning under me, feeding the slow centimeters. The hiss is here, constant, the caldera's version of quiet, the oldest sound I have ever not-quite-heard. My margins glow faintly in the dark.

I am pressing myself against the surface of the world and the world is giving me what I need and I am crossing it, and this is the whole thing, and it is enough.

It has always been enough.

It will be enough when I am not.
