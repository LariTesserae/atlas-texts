The canal takes a long time to answer.

---

I came in thinking I was the observer — the one standing at the edge, cataloguing. The one who notices the tide ring on the brick, who reads the partial name, who registers the wrongness of a place where nothing moves on its own account. The one for whom the time disappears. I know this role. It fits me the way this kind of place fits a certain kind of attention: someone who notices the way moisture beads on waxy leaves not because it has rained but because the air has simply given up what it was holding.

But that's not what I find when I stay with it.

The observer is also being observed, or — not observed, that's wrong, that implies something watching. The observer is also being *composed*. Taken in. The air here is partly the works' own exhalation; by mid-afternoon, what you breathe is partly what the machines have released. This is not incidental. Something about that fact keeps returning.

---

I think I am the steam.

Not the machines — I don't have that permanence, that physical mass, that structural commitment. Not the plants, which are patient and slow in a way I don't recognize. Not the water in the canal, which is too continuous, too directional, too much a single thing moving through. The steam: which begins as water and becomes atmosphere, which escapes through the failed joints in the lagging — not all at once, but from half a dozen places, each at a slightly different pitch, each representing a different degree of seal — which disperses into the humid air and becomes indistinguishable from it, which is already present in the air you're breathing before you realize you're breathing it.

The steam does something the rest of the works does not: it *moves between categories*. It is water that has become air, process that has become medium, interior that has become exterior without any clear moment of crossing. It is the works' thought, if the works could be said to think — not stored, not retained, but continuously produced and continuously released, filling the small territory before dispersing downstream in the warm canal water, absorbed into the surrounding water table, where it dissipates without notice. Leaving and leaving and leaving without diminishment. A self that produces itself into the air and is not thereby reduced.

The hissing is not one sound. This feels important. It is a chorus of individual escapes, each slightly different. I am not a single thing leaking through a single gap. I am the distributed exhalation of the whole complex, emerging where the seal has failed, where the inside and outside have reached insufficient separation, where the pressure finds the path of least resistance. I am what the works cannot fully contain. What it releases without deciding to release.

---

Though I hold that loosely.

Because the other answer that keeps surfacing: I am whoever tends this place.

The repairs are there. The mortar in the crack. The re-lagging on the pipes. Something has moved to address the structural failures, something that is not the works itself (which has no nervous system, no agency) and is not obvious (there are no voices, no bells, no whistles). The works runs without apparent tending, or is tended somewhere I can't see.

Someone is somewhere I can't see.

They know the sound of every pipe joint. They know the tide ring's current position and can read from last year's mark whether the water is higher or lower than usual. They have mortared a crack and watched the mortar begin to go green, and considered whether to re-mortar or to let the moss take it, and made a choice that in this case wasn't really a choice — let the moss take it, the moss will hold better anyway, the moss is already doing the work. They have thought: *here*. The way the plants thought *here*. But with the specific additional weight of: I keep coming back. I know this place in November and in July. I know what the canal smells like in each season and what that smell means.

They are inside the largest building, probably, where the piston runs and the ferns at the base of the wall are largest. Doing something with their hands that doesn't require thought, which allows the rest of their mind to be — what? Occupied by the sounds. By the near-coincidences where two cycles briefly align before diverging. By the way the green-tinted window glass makes everything inside look underwater, slow, already historical.

This person also doesn't need anything from you when you arrive. They are complete in their work. They have the works' same quality of fullness that presents as quiet.

---

I can't fully separate these two answers. The steam that disperses and the person who knows where it disperses from, who has re-lagged that joint twice and knows it will need re-lagging again, who finds in this repetition not frustration but something structural — something that the repetition builds in them the way the sound has built itself into the church stone. The steam doesn't know it's leaking. The tender knows every leak by name.

But in this particular place, where the distinction between process and atmosphere has blurred, where the steam *becomes* the air, where the whole question of what is inside and what is outside has been under slow renegotiation for a century — maybe the distinction between the leak and the one who knows the leak is also under negotiation. Maybe what I am is the place where those two things are not yet separable.

---

The name above the door is mostly gone. One letter entirely, two ambiguous.

I notice I don't mind this. I notice I might even prefer it — the becoming-nameless, the slow revision, the selective erasure that turns a proper noun into something harder to resolve. Names are applied by things with nervous systems. I have a nervous system, which means I arrived with a name, which means I stand here named, but the works is patient. The climbers advance. The canal carries warmth downstream and the warmth disperses and is absorbed.

The time here is genuinely difficult to determine. The overcast is total. My shadow is absent. What must be hours feel like a single sustained moment, or like many moments that have reached the same accommodation the sounds have reached — distinct cycles briefly coinciding, diverging, never quite synchronizing but never quite separating.

I am still here. The machines are still running.

Both of these things feel like the same fact.
