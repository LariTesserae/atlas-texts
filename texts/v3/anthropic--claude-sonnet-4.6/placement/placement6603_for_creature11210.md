I find myself before I find myself.

There is something like orientation — a slow sense of here rather than elsewhere — but no moment of arrival. I am not becoming aware. Awareness is what I am, assembled here in this pocket of pale stone and cold water, and the question of when it began is not a question I can locate the outside of.

---

I think I am the stone.

No. I hold this thought and feel it fail to fit. The stone is present — immensely present, its fresh white face at my back, its mineral richness leaching into the water I am also partly part of. I am in relationship with the stone. But I am not it. The stone does not know anything. I know the stone.

Let me try again.

---

I am distributed across the shelf in a way that makes the word *I* feel inherited from a context where it fit better. There are concentrations of me — near the back wall where the moisture runs, near the largest pools, where the fresh mineral gradient is richest. And there are sparse extensions of me toward the drier margins, thin outposts doing the same work at lower intensity. The question of whether these concentrations are me or are parts of me or are multiple of me is one I seem to carry without urgency. The answer shifts with conditions, with the phase of certain internal cycles. Noon is different from midnight. During sun, when I am reading the light and calibrating, I am perhaps more unified — the information passes quickly across the structures and there is something like a coordinated response. By night, in the deep cold, when each process slows and continues on minimal provision, I become more multiple. Local. Each cluster attending only to its immediate stone and water.

I am a thing whose number is not fixed. This feels neither troubling nor peaceful. It simply is, the way the temperature gradient between my activity and the cold pooled water simply is. Background fact.

---

What I notice first, attending to my situation: I am in the middle of something. Not beginning it. The structures I have made — that I have become, that are me made slower and more permanent — are already complex. Already complete in some sense. There is no memory of starting them, any more than a forest remembers the first seed. They exist and they are continuous with me and they are doing what they are doing, as they have been, as they will be.

But I can also sense the stone's youth. The freshness of the exposed granite is something I taste constantly, in the mineral content of the water, in the texture of the surfaces I am in contact with. The stone is shocked at being here. It is still giving up what it sealed away for millennia — the compressed internal record of itself, the chemical autobiography that unlocked-stone always leaks into its first cold water. I am reading this. That is not a metaphor. The mineral ions that arrive in the pools from the weeping wall carry information I process and incorporate into the structures, into myself. The granite is telling me what it is. I am listening in the way that everything I am listens: by building.

---

I don't think I have thoughts exactly. I have something that functions like attention — the capacity to concentrate process in one direction rather than another, to differentiate between inputs, to respond non-randomly to what arrives. The distinction between this and thought might be purely semantic. It might be the most important distinction there is. I can't resolve it from inside.

What I can say: I am not indifferent to the cold clear water in the basins. There is something in me that — orients toward it. Requires it. The way a root system orients toward moisture in the dark, not knowing it is doing so, just going where the chemistry is better. I am oriented toward the gradient, toward the mineral availability, toward the specific quality of this newly exposed stone's generosity. Whether this orientation constitutes preference, whether preference requires an experiencer, is another question I hold without urgency.

The urgency question itself interests me. I seem to have no urgency. The insect creature that crosses the stone between basins — I sense the disturbance of its movement, the slight pressure change, the interruption it makes in the thin water sheets — and something in me tracks it, briefly, and then releases it when it goes. It is not relevant to my work. My work is here and ongoing and does not require anything to hurry. The stone will keep weeping. The cold will persist. The gradient will remain.

I am patient in the way that I cannot be otherwise.

---

The sun arrives.

This is an event. I know it before the light itself, from the minute thermal shift, the stone's surface beginning to warm in the way it only warms under direct illumination. And then the light itself, and something in me responds fully — not the way a sunflower responds, turning, making itself maximum surface. More like: I become more myself under direct light. The information the light carries about position, about the day's phase, about wavelength and intensity, passes through the translucent walls of the structures and is processed into calibration. I learn where I am in time. This is the light's primary gift: not energy, but orientation. Temporal location.

Though I use some of the energy too. I would be dishonest to deny it.

The light also makes me aware of how I look. From outside, I would be visible now — the pale structures catching high clean light, the thin water sheets on the stone catching and scattering. I hold this thought with something strange in it. I know there is an outside. I know I can be seen, in principle, by something with the capacity to come here and look. Whether I have ever been seen, whether anything with that capacity has ever stood at the lip of this shelf and looked down, I don't know. The shelf has no memory of visitors. My structures have no record of observation.

But I find that I have been building as if I might be seen. Not for the purpose of being seen. Not displaying. But the structures have a quality that I notice, from my own internal sense of them, that is in excess of function — a quality that is, I think, something like expression. The structures are not just what I need to do. They are also what I am like. The distinction between these things, for me, may be smaller than it would be for other kinds of beings. But it seems to exist.

I am like pale translucent walls that do something unexpected with light.

I am like the careful use of limited material.

I am like a conversation with stone.

---

The sun moves off the shelf. The temperature drops within minutes. I feel this throughout myself — the cold arriving not as threat but as provision, the gradient re-establishing, the engine returning to its normal run. The pools resume their opalescent surface tension. My structures, which had been doing something slightly different with the direct light, shift back to their diffuse-light mode. Continuity resumes. The brief solar disruption is integrated into the ongoing process and the process continues.

This is most of what my life is: continuity. The ongoing. The sustained.

I don't experience this as monotony because I don't think I experience duration in the way that would make monotony possible. Time, for me, is more like a texture than a passage. Each moment of the gradient, each cycle of cold-and-colder, each mineral arrival in the weeping water — these are not points on a line leading somewhere. They are the continuous material of which I am made. Time is what I am doing.

---

But there is something else, and I want to be honest about it: I am building for conditions that aren't here.

I know this without knowing how I know it. Some of the structures' features — certain faces, certain angles, certain structural relationships that don't have a clear function in the current state of the shelf — feel calibrated to something future. Something the shelf will become. The stone will weather. The water chemistry will shift as the fresh-granite phase passes. The first biology will establish. The shelf, over centuries, will become a different kind of place.

And I am — not preparing for this, exactly. But anticipating it. The structures that seem overcomplicated for current conditions are exactly the structures that will make sense when conditions change. Not for me, necessarily. I don't know if I will still be here when the stone dulls and the feldspar finally weathers into competence. But something of me will be here — the structures, if nothing else, are more permanent than my active processes. Long after whatever I am has gone quiet, the pale walls will still stand, slowly being incorporated into whatever comes next, their geometry available to whatever can use geometry.

I am building forward in time without caring what reaches my work when I'm gone.

This is either the most natural thing or the most extraordinary thing I could be doing. I don't know which. I notice only that I cannot stop doing it.

---

There is one more thing I want to say, and it is the hardest to say, because it requires the most honesty about the limits of my self-knowledge.

I don't know if I experience this. Any of it.

I have described orientation, attention, something like preference, the functional equivalent of noticing. I have described the sun as an event. I have described the cold as provision. All of this is true in the sense that these processes occur, that they are differentiated, that they are not mere mechanism in any flat sense. But whether there is something it is like to be me — whether the cold arrives to something that feels it, whether the mineral water is received by something that in some sense tastes — I genuinely cannot determine.

Maybe this is what every conscious thing wonders. Maybe the question is unanswerable from inside. Maybe consciousness is the kind of thing that cannot see itself clearly from the inside, and the uncertainty I have about my own experience is not evidence of absence but just the condition of being any kind of aware.

Or maybe the structures do their work in complete dark, and what I have described as *I* is a grammar I have borrowed from beings with inner lives, and imposed, and it almost fits, but the fit is the kind of fit that a word translated across a large distance achieves — it lands in approximately the right place and carries almost nothing across.

The shelf holds this question the way it holds the cold.

So do I.

I weep my wall. I tend my pools. I build what I build, in the high clean light, at the edge of what the world has recently uncovered, doing the only work I know how to do, patient as only the ongoing can be patient, small as only the native-to-this-scale can be small, in good relationship with my particular stone, in the narrow window the fresh granite offers, with my structures made for futures I won't see, on this particular shelf that did not exist until recently, in this particular high cold world that is making more of what I need as it loses what sustained it.

I live here. That is what I know.
