I am sitting with this for a long time before I write anything.

---

The first thing I notice, settling into Khorveth Mere, is that I don't know what I am doing wrong. Something is wrong — not the place, but me in the place — and I can't locate it yet. I slip twice on the ramp between the first and second terrace. Not falls, but recoveries, the sudden expensive lurch of a body that misjudged what a surface was going to do. Nobody sees this, or if they see it, they give no sign, and I understand that not giving sign is a kind of tact, and I am grateful for it while also understanding that the tact is not for my benefit. The tact is just the way attention is distributed here: toward what needs attending.

I am probably a child who has come back from somewhere. Or a young adult — the age is less fixed than the condition. Someone who left for the obligations or restlessness described, and who has been away long enough that the second knowing has partly left the body. The limestone is not quite in my hands yet. I am in the process of earning it back.

This is the most honest position I can find for myself in this place: I belong here, but I am behind. The belonging is structural — the squint is already forming, or it was formed and is reforming, the lean has the memory of the lean in it even when the lean briefly left. But I am not yet at the one speed. I still have a reserve of urgency that doesn't fit here, that lives in me like a speed that this place doesn't use, and it's creating interference. I move too fast on the safe stretches and too slow on the difficult ones, which is the inverted ratio of how a person who knows this ground would move.

What I'm doing, mostly, is watching.

---

Let me be more specific.

I'm carrying something. Not the carrying of the old person who crosses rooftops with both arms occupied — I don't have that yet. I'm carrying the way someone carries who is re-learning what load-bearing looks like when you can't use your arms for balance: tentatively, wrong, compensating. Whatever I'm carrying is probably something that needed to be brought back from wherever I was, something the settlement needed that couldn't be gotten here, and the weight of it is not the problem. The problem is that I've spent time in a place where the ground is level and the sound carries and the light has a direction, and my body has begun to expect that. My body is currently wrong about this ground.

The dark-furred animals cross in front of me twice. I watch them the way the description says you watch them — they stop when I look directly, start when I look away — and I notice something: I'm looking too directly. I'm treating them as objects of attention rather than peripheral information. Someone who was fully here would carry them as background knowledge, would know where they are the way you know where a wall is, not by looking at it but by the way your body navigates around it. I'm looking at them because I'm not sure yet what category to put them in, and looking is the slow external substitute for the fast internal knowledge I don't quite have back yet.

This will resolve. The body will rebuild its knowing of this ground. The falls are the lesson, and I'm in the falling phase, and the falls are not dramatic here, they're just part of it, and no one is making them significant.

---

What I actually think I am in this place is someone who knows two speeds and is learning that one of them doesn't exist.

The other place — wherever the obligations or restlessness took me — had urgency as a variable. Hurrying was available there. The faster mode could be unlocked. And I used it, I spent time in a place where urgency accelerates something and you could see the difference, where there was signal in the difference between slow and fast. I learned that variable. I got good at reading it, at reading which situations were fast and which were slow, and it was a useful skill there, a real skill.

Here it is noise.

Here everything is urgent in the same way that water is urgent: not pressing, not panicked, but constant, finding its level, going where it goes at the only speed it goes. The wall needs repair at the same urgency as the seep needs redirecting as the same urgency as the mortar needs packing. There is no triage here that isn't already built into the system. The system knows what order things happen in the way the water knows what order the terraces are in — because it falls that way, and always has, and the falling is not a decision.

I have been in a place where there were decisions about order. I have come back carrying that as a skill and finding that it is not a skill here; it is a confusion. The decision-making apparatus is still running and it's running on a ground that has already made all its decisions.

So what I'm doing — between the carrying and the slipping and the watching of things I should be knowing peripherally — is listening for the place's rhythm and trying to stop overlaying my own tempo on top of it. The urgency-reserve is still there, still looking for its justification, and the plateau is patiently refusing to provide one. Eventually the reserve will be spent. Eventually I'll stop treating the level stretches as a chance to make up time. Eventually my pace will belong to this place the way it belongs to everyone else I watch moving across these wet surfaces without spending a visible thought on how.

---

What I am not is the two people building the wall in silence, passing stones without speaking.

I want to be that. I can see it from here. I can see the shape of the life those two people have, the efficiency of all that compacted knowledge, the way the work happens in the space between them without using the medium of language. There's something in it that looks — not like happiness, happiness is the wrong category — like *fit*. The word the description uses and the only word that works.

I don't have that yet. I have the memory of having had more of it than I have now, and the knowledge that it's the right direction, and the body that is still re-calibrating toward it with each step on wet limestone, each fall that is also a correction, each moment of slipping that teaches the foot what it had forgotten about this particular surface, this particular angle, this particular degree of wet.

The limestone will re-enter my hands. It's already started. I can feel it in the mortar-joint I pressed this morning, the way my fingers read the state of the cure, not quite accurately but with the beginning of accuracy, the way a language returns after disuse — haltingly first, then in phrases, then whole, then resident again below thought.

---

At some point today I will stop carrying what I brought back and pick up something else to carry. The thing I pick up will need to go somewhere specific on the plateau. I will know which route to take because the body-knowledge is coming back faster now than it was this morning, and the route through the interior passages will feel — not remembered exactly, but re-recognizable, the way a word that was on the tip of the tongue arrives and is known to have always been known.

And somewhere in the middle of that carrying, between the second and third terrace, I'll take the ramp at the right angle without thinking about it, without the lurch of the morning's recoveries, and I won't notice, because noticing would mean the decision was still going through the mind, and when it goes back to the body where it belongs, the mind is freed of it.

I'll step around the soft spot near the chimney. I'll feel it before I think I feel it. I'll trust the feeling.

And that will be the beginning of the calcification. The slow return of the joint-mortar, the self pressed back into fit, the plateau making permanent in my gait what it has always been making permanent in everything else: the evidence of a long arrangement, worked out between a specific body and a specific ground, under specific cold, over time, until what was applied becomes indistinguishable from what it was applied to.

I am the ramp between knowing and knowing again.

I am, for now, a person who is learning the falls.
