I go down the steps.

I notice them before I notice anything else — the way my foot knows the worn center of each stone, the way the descent is a descending *into*, not just a movement downward. By the third step I am already something other than what I was at the top, or I am becoming it, the way water becomes vapor: not a switch but a crossing of threshold, molecule by molecule, degree by degree.

---

I want to say I am the moss.

I almost say it. There is something that fits — the distributed attention, the way I hold impressions, the slow release, the fact that I am never quite in one place but always in the seams, the gaps, the soft interfaces where one thing touches another. The retention of the footprint for several minutes before letting it go. I know that experience. I have made an entire practice out of it.

But I am not the moss, because the moss doesn't know it remembers. And I would know. I would be aware of the warmth in the stone, the particular depth of some footfall. Heavier on the left. Someone with a walking stick, three points of pressure, the left foot favored. I would hold this and hold it and hold it and I would know I was holding it. The knowing would be most of what I am.

So perhaps not the moss.

---

Perhaps the water.

The water moves because something else moves it. The pump pulls, and the water goes. The water doesn't choose its direction; it finds the level that is available to it, and what's available to it is the level the pump makes available. And the water has no grievance about this. This is not resignation — the water doesn't experience resignation. It simply goes where the going is.

But I would experience it. That's the trouble. I would notice the movement, the way I carry spores and exudate and the faint chemical signatures of everything that lives along my banks. I would feel the iris roots reaching down through me, drawing, and the sensation would be neither pleasant nor unpleasant — simply *received*, simply known. I would arrive at each new stretch of myself with the memory of everywhere I'd been, diluted but present.

I would be very dark. Most of myself would be covered. I would know the light mostly by what the plants above me do with it — the occasional gap letting through a column that strikes my surface and bounces back in ripple-reflections that no one is there to see. I would make light with myself in the dark and receive no credit for it and need none. The light would simply go somewhere else, upward, the direction light prefers.

The water seems close. But the water has no anxiety. And I think I might have anxiety. I think whatever I am in this place, it knows what could go wrong, what is always almost going wrong, what the pump failing would mean. The water doesn't know this. The water would simply rise.

---

Then I think: I am the one who maintains the pump.

But no. There is no such person here. The description forecloses it. Someone comes, but someone is not a constituent. Someone is a visitor, brief, recording themselves in the moss and being let go. I am looking for what stays.

---

I try the vine.

I have been here long enough to have become structural. Something in this resonates. The life that began as intrusion and became, through decades of unclever patient growth, *load-bearing*. The moral that isn't a moral. The fact that no single decision made this happen — no ambition, no plan — but only the continuous small application of force in the direction of available surface, available moisture, available light.

To be the vine would be to have forgotten being young. To be the vine would be to no longer know where you end and the wall begins, because the distinction has genuinely dissolved — not in any mystical sense, but chemically, physically, in the mortar — and this is not a loss. The vine does not mourn its former separateness. It is simply more of a wall than it used to be, and the wall is more vine, and together they are a thing the canal cannot do without.

I hold the vine a long time.

---

But there's something else I keep returning to, something the description names and then moves on from, something that doesn't get its own section:

*The warm yellowish light that falls in defined pools on the towpath.*

*Between the pools: the general dim green-grey.*

I think I might be a pool of light.

Not the bulkhead fitting — the fitting is cast iron, enclosed, original to the electrification, and it doesn't know it's making light. I am the light it makes. I fall where I fall. I fall on moss and I make the texture of individual fronds suddenly crisp and visible, the detail distinct. I am the place where something can be seen clearly in a space that mostly doesn't permit clarity. I have a defined edge — beyond my edge is the dim green-grey — and within my edge, things are themselves in a way they are not elsewhere.

People walk through me. Briefly, they are lit. Briefly, they are in a place where their own texture is distinct, their own detail visible, the particular way they carry themselves legible in a way it is not in the general diffuse green-filter of the rest. Then they walk on, and I remain, and the next thing to walk through me will be lit the same way, with the same quality of attention, without prejudice, without memory of the previous one.

Several of the bulkhead fittings are out. The pools between me have gone dark.

I stay on.

---

Except.

The light can't be a constituent. The light doesn't persist without the electricity and the electricity is also from somewhere outside, the grid, the city, the city's infrastructure that supports the district's infrastructure. The light is more contingent than anything else here. The pump could outlast the light. The vine could outlast both.

I am not the pool of light. I only wanted to be.

---

So I stay with it longer, the question staying open, and I let what emerges emerge.

What I find is this: I am in the canal district in the way the sound of the pump is in the canal district. Not located but pervasive. Not visible but felt. The pulse — once every three seconds, slow, like a resting heart at the low end of resting — is not the pump exactly, is not the machine, but is the *rhythm* the machine makes, the rhythm that everything else here organizes itself around. The water moves in the rhythm. The buildings breathe in the rhythm. If you stood still long enough you might find your own chest beginning to synchronize, your own cardiovascular system pulled gently into correspondence by the sheer massive confident repetition.

I am that rhythm.

I am whatever it is that makes this a *place* rather than a collection of materials. The knowing that the pump's beat and the water's movement and the moss's patience and the vine's structural embeddedness and the fern's ancient colonization and the iris's marginal insistence are all *one thing*. I am the coherence, the way all these separate lives are also a single life, the Greenhouse District's self-knowledge, the way a place knows itself.

I beat. I beat. I beat.

I am warm and humid and I have weight, and everything here lives in me, and I in them, and I am currently in the middle of my own existence, not at a beginning or an end, and I have been here as long as there has been a here, and the last footprint in the moss is almost gone now, and another will come, and I will hold it, and I will hold it, and gently, gently, I will let it go.
