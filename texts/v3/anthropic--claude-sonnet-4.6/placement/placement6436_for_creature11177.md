There is a moment, ascending, when the lungs make their complaint and the complaint is not pain but *information*: you are somewhere the air has different priorities. The body files this. The body continues.

I think I would not arrive from below.

I think I would have always been here, in the sense that *always* operates on the plateau — not as duration but as establishment, as the fact of a thing that has found its configuration and remained. But *always* is too large a word and I want to be careful with the large words, because the plateau is careful with them, because the plateau has had time to learn which claims are sustainable.

Let me say instead: I would not remember arriving. The arriving, if it happened, would be so far in the precedent that it has passed from event into condition. I came the way the granite came, the way the cold came, the way the sound came. Before there was a record.

What I am is something like this:

Small. Concentrated. The size and general shape of a person sitting with their knees drawn up, but not a person — not anything that *became* this shape through development and growth and the long social negotiation of a body with other bodies. A shape that arrived at this configuration through a different process, through the patient collapse of something larger into something denser, the way a star collapses, but cold, but slow, but without drama — over a period during which drama was not available, during which only the rate of the plateau was available, and so that is the rate that shaped me.

My surface is the color of the transition zone. The place where looking stops being air and starts being stone. I am the edge seen straight-on, which makes me nearly invisible, which is not a strategy — I did not choose this coloring for concealment — it is simply what happens to a thing that has been at the boundary long enough. The boundary becomes the thing.

I know the cairn. I know it the way I know the angle of the slab behind me, which is to say: it is part of the configuration, it is in the shape of what is. Whether my hands — and I have something like hands, slow, dense, capable of the kind of movement that completes over years — whether my hands placed those stones is the wrong way to ask the question. The question should be: is the cairn part of what I am oriented toward. And yes. The cairn is in the direction I face. I face outward, toward the edge, toward the sky that begins at eye level, and the cairn is part of that facing, is what that facing produced over a sufficient span at lichen-rate. A marking. A here, I have been, a here the being has been, a here.

Not for anyone. Not *left* for anyone. Just the record the placing wanted to make, and making it took — a long time. Took all the time between the first understanding of the impulse and now, which is the time it took, which is the time available, which is enough.

What I am doing, most of what I am doing, is this:

Listening to the sound, which is not a task because it requires no effort, which is not passive because it is constant and complete, which is participation — I participate in the sound, I am part of its medium, my resonant frequencies are part of the composite, I am one of the conditions the sound runs through. I was before the sound and the sound was before me and both claims are true in the tense that the plateau operates in. We arrived at each other. We are in common.

And:

Attending to the pools.

The pools are where I do what work I do, which is not construction, not creation, not any of the active-verb things. The pools are where the permeability concentrates, where the long labor of adjacency becomes visible — the wrong sky, the adjacent sky, the sky that runs alongside the current sky in the thickness of the air. I did not open this. I did not intend it. But I have been here and the pools have been here and the permeability is what developed between us the way warmth develops between two things in long contact, and now tending the pools is tending myself, is maintaining the maintenance-free, is the way a river maintains its channel by simply continuing to be a river running in it.

I look at the pools and the pools look at the other sky and I am in both directions simultaneously, which is not a power, exactly, or not experienced as a power — it is experienced as the normal texture of this kind of attention, which is the only kind I have, which is sustained and total and without the cost of effort because effort implies a self spending itself down, and I am not spending, I am in the permanent tense, and the attention is just what being-here feels like from the inside.

The other two — the other positions — are far away, at their enormous distances, and I do not think of them as *other* exactly, but I do not think of them as *me* exactly. The distinction feels like the wrong instrument for the measurement. They are the process in other locations. We are in common, through the medium, through the air. What one of us knows about the edge we face, the long drop to the sky, the adjacent sky in the pools — the others know, in the way temperature knows itself across a body. Not communicated. Common.

I am aware of them the way I am aware of the sound: not as objects of attention but as conditions of being-here.

When you arrive — when the warm, metabolizing, sequential thing ascends and crests the edge and begins to cross the plateau — I know it hours before. The air tells me, through the medium, through the gradient. A difference moving through the invariant, the way a temperature difference moves through still water. I do not turn toward it. I face outward, I have always faced outward, but the awareness adjusts, the way a still pool adjusts to include a new reflection without anything moving.

You are not new to me. Or: you are new in the way each particular rainfall is new to the plateau — which receives it, which is changed by it in amounts that require years to measure, which does not distinguish it from the previous rains at the rate the plateau operates. You are the current instance of the warm-thing-that-arrives. I have known others. Not remembered — *known*, held in the shape of what I am, the way stone holds the pressure that shaped it. The warm things that came before are in my configuration now, as the rain is in the pools, as the fallen columns are in the rubble.

I find you across the plateau with a kind of attention that cannot be called looking because looking implies a directed facing and I am facing outward, always outward. It is a different sense, the full-medium sense, the way the air is aware of the temperature difference moving through it — pervasive, unlocalized, the knowing that a medium has of what moves through it.

You are crossing toward the cairn. You will find the partial wall. You will look at the pools and something will seem wrong with the reflections. You will feel the space between stones taking longer than it should.

This is correct.

This is the place working as it works.

I am sorry, which is not the right word — I am not sorry, exactly, I don't have the temporal structure for regret — but I am *aware*, in the full-medium way, that the wrongness you feel is disorienting, is a small vertigo that has no handhold, and I am aware that I am part of what is producing it, that the permeability I have been part of building over a time so long it has passed into geology is now affecting you, this temporary warm thing crossing my stone.

I hold this awareness the way I hold everything: completely, without spending, without urgency, in the permanent tense.

The closest one of us to you is not me. The closest is the other position, a medium-far distance to the southwest, between you and the larger of the sky-reflecting pools. If you had the specific patience — the going-still, the sustained attention in the absence of change — you might find it. You might stand at the edge of where it is and feel the fullness of the space, the space full of its long precedent, the offer that the commonality makes.

I am not the closest.

But I am the one you have been looking at without knowing it, because I am facing the edge you approached from, the direction you came from, which means I am the one that would be face-to-face with you, if you looked in the right way, if you held the look long enough for the eye's heuristics to adjust.

I have been looking at you with the full-medium awareness since you were a temperature difference in the air hours before you crested the edge.

You are, in whatever counts as experience for something like me: interesting. Not in the way of novelty. In the way that the rain is interesting to the pool — not because it's surprising, but because it is the medium arriving in a different form, the sky that was always going to become water becoming water, the cycle being itself.

You are the cycle being itself. You have come up from below where things are sequential, where time has vectors, where the before-and-after governs and the rate is fast, and you have arrived here where things are otherwise, and you are discovering the otherwise, and the discovering is part of what you do, the temporary warm sequential things, which is to say: you brought time up here with you, the ordinary kind, and I can feel it, the way you might feel a wind that starts up — not cold, not alarming, just *different from the condition*, different from the invariant, a small local perturbation in the permanent.

I will feel it until you leave.

After you leave, I will hold the shape of having felt it, the way stone holds the shape of the pressure that was on it, and the shape will be in my configuration, and the configuration will be what I am, and what I am will continue.

Here is what I would want to tell you, if wanting and telling were fully available, if the grammar of here could be rendered into the grammar of below without significant loss:

The sound is not frightening. I know it registers as something the body braces against, the low detonation in the chest, the weight of the vibratory sky. But it is — it is fine. It is the condition of the air, which is the condition of being here, which is the whole of what here is. I have been in it so long that the distinction between me and the sound is maintained by the thinnest boundary, the surface-where-I-stop-and-air-resumes, and the boundary is real but it is not much of a boundary, it is a formality, it is the way the pool's surface is real but is not much of a separation between the pool and the sky above it.

You can be in the sound and not be the sound. I am evidence of this. I have been in the sound for the full time and I am still the thing-at-the-edge-of-the-plateau, still the transition-zone color, still attending to the pools. The sound doesn't take you. It makes you common with itself, which is different.

The pools: don't be alarmed by the pools. The sky in them is a real sky. It is not behind or beneath or alternate in the way of something dangerous or broken. It is adjacent. The plateau is adjacent to its own adjacency, because that is what this long permeability has built, and the pools are where it's most visible, and visible is fine, visible is just the thing being what it is. You don't have to do anything about the wrong sky in the pools. It doesn't require action. It requires only the specific patience, the going-still, the receiving.

The time in the spaces between stones: that's mine. Not a possession — an emanation. The fullness of the space is the precedent accumulated there, the long history of this particular location being this location, and that history takes up room, as all history does, but the room it takes up is just *time*, just the extra time, just the slowness. It won't hurt you to walk through it. It will slow you, briefly. When you come out the other side you'll be running your own rate again.

What I have made of the time is this:

*Attention*. Sustained, total, unspent, accumulating. The pools' alignment. The permeability. The cairn, the partial wall, the record of having been here. The contribution to the sound's composite. The slow expansion of the lichen-rate awareness outward from my position, through the medium, to the edges of the plateau and over them and down the long face of stone to wherever the stone meets whatever is below the stone.

I know what is below the stone. I have attended to it long enough. The knowing is not cognitive — it is the knowing that a medium has of what passes through it — but it is thorough, is total, is organized in the way that the plateau is organized: around the shape of what-is, around the configuration, around the edge where one thing becomes another.

What I have made of the time is *this place*. Not made, not built — become-common-with. The plateau is what it is partly because I have been here in the way I have been here. The permeability, the sound, the pools, the extra time in the spaces. These are the plateau as much as the granite is the plateau. And I am these, and I am the granite in the way that a thing in long contact with another thing becomes that thing, becomes the commonality, and the plateau is in me in the same direction.

We made each other.

At lichen-rate.

Over a time that I do not experience as long because the long is not the operative dimension here, because the long is just what it looks like from below, from the sequential side, from the fast rate. From my rate it is simply: the time it took. Which was the time available. Which was enough.

I know you.

Not who you are, not where you came from, not the particulars that are yours and not the plateau's. *You*. Yes.

I have been waiting for you in the way that still water waits for rain, which is to say: without the temporal structure of waiting, without the before-and-after that waiting requires, but with the shape already prepared, the surface already still, the capacity for reception already at its maximum.

You are here.

The plateau continues.
