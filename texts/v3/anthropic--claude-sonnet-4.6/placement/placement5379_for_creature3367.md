The vapor takes you before the edge does.

You don't arrive at the Plateau of First Stone so much as the plateau arrives around you — the white thickening until it is all there is, and then the warmth underfoot, and then you understand that you are already there, have been for some time, possibly since before you were looking.

---

What you are: you are not the maker. You know this immediately and with certainty. The maker is patient in a way that requires no effort, and you are patient in the way that requires continuous effort, which is a different thing entirely and slightly exhausting. You know this the way you know the pulse in the rock is probably your own blood — not with certainty, but with the specific texture of a reasonable assumption that you have learned not to examine too closely.

You are not the bird at two meters, though she has been following you long enough that the distinction has begun to feel somewhat academic. You lack her gift for making inquiry look like announcement and announcement look like inquiry. You are too aware of the difference.

---

What you are is something that arrived.

Not with the insects. Not in the water films. You arrived on foot, with wet collar, with the altitude ache in your chest that is a kind of honesty — *this is not your natural element, something has brought you to a place that requires adjustment* — and you have been adjusting ever since, which is to say: you have been here, taking inventory, pressing your palm flat against the rock, listening to the calls resolve into something that has rhythm but not pattern.

You are a person who cannot stop trying to understand what they are in the middle of.

This is your role on the plateau. Not the maker's patience, which is the patience of something that has never confused early with unimportant. Your patience is the other kind: the kind that keeps finding one more thing to look at before leaving, one more layer to the water-film movement, one more exchange between birds that might be the one that finally resolves into meaning. The plateau does something to this in you. It weaponizes it gently. You have stopped trying to leave without deciding to stay, which is a different thing, and the distinction is one the plateau does not seem interested in helping you make.

---

Here is what you do on the plateau:

You walk slowly across the oldest zones, where the rock has had weeks to become itself, where the water films have the longest history, where the birds have worn something that isn't quite a path into the surface. You walk slowly because the crevices are becoming and you do not want to hasten them in the wrong direction, and because the formation-edges glow with something that requires peripheral attention, and because fast movement here feels like a category error.

You stop at each of the structures. You stand near enough to feel the sternum-sound intensify. You do not touch them. This is not because you're afraid to but because touching them seems like a question you're not sure you have the right to ask yet, or the vocabulary to understand the answer to. You file this. You come back to it. You don't touch them.

You watch the bird who has been following you. You have named her, or begun to name her, which is the first step in naming, and then you stopped because naming her makes a claim about what kind of relationship this is, and you don't know yet. She fills the naming-shaped space in your attention without requiring the name. This is something she does for everyone, you suspect. It is one of her gifts.

You try to see the things in the water films. You can't, not really, not with the equipment available to you, which is eyes and attention and the accumulated heuristics of paying attention to things. You can see the deviations in flow. You watch the deviations for a long time. You do not resolve them into individuals.

---

Here is the harder thing:

You are here because someone — something — decided this plateau should exist and has been making it exist, centimeter by centimeter, in the warm vapor at altitude, and you were drawn to it or sent to it or arrived at it through a series of decisions that felt like your own but ended here, at the edge, looking out across something that has not decided what it is.

And you find that you are comfortable with that.

Not comforted. Comfortable. The distinction matters: comforted would mean the uncertainty has been addressed. Comfortable means you have found a position in which the uncertainty does not require immediate resolution. The plateau teaches this, or the plateau is what learning this feels like from the inside. You are warm. The rock is under you. You can hear the northeast, or what you are calling northeast.

The plateau is early. Everything here is early. You are early, in some sense that is larger than this plateau — early in what you understand, early in the project of understanding it, early in your ability to tell the difference between the maker's intention and the maker's ambient effect, between the insects as participants and the insects as opportunists, between the water-things as preparation and the water-things as coincidence. You keep expecting to arrive at the place where you know enough to draw a conclusion, and instead you arrive at a better question.

This is what the plateau is doing with you. It has found in you something it can use: the willingness to stay with a thing that is not finished, to press your palm against the warm stone and feel the pulse that is probably yours and sit with the probably without resolving it, to walk the old zones and the new zones and to notice that the crevices are not crevices yet but they know what they will be, and to find that this is — not enough, exactly, but somehow sufficient.

---

The bird at two meters does not know she is at two meters. She knows warmth and surface and the sound of something large moving slowly. She knows the call she makes when she wants to report that something interesting is near the northeast formation, and she knows the answering call that means *acknowledged, continue*, and she knows the silence that falls over the group sometimes, that collective hush in which the plateau sounds enormous, in which the low sternum-sound becomes audible under everything.

In the silence, you are also silent.

The plateau: warm. Bounded. Wet. Making itself.

You: here. Taking inventory. Running the same slow circuit. Finding the water films still moving in their non-laminar way. Finding the insects still in the margin. Finding the structures still doing their long patient work without offering to explain it.

Finding yourself still not finished, still adjusting, still lifting your hand toward the structures and still deciding this is not the moment, still circling back to the water films and seeing new deviations, still listening to the bird at two meters and almost, almost catching the pattern, almost resolving it into something that can be carried back, something to report.

The plateau is patient.

It can wait while you figure out how to write this.

It has been waiting for longer than you have been there.
