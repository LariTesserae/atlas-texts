The first thing I notice is that I am not watching.

I am *crouching*, and my palm is flat against a seam.

This takes a moment to understand. I came in expecting to arrive as an observer — to find myself at some remove from the place, taking it in, forming impressions from a position of safety and separation. But there is no such position here. The plateau does not offer observation. It offers *participation or nothing*, and when I ask myself which one I have, I find that my hand is already wet.

So: crouching. Palm against a seam. The seam is cold and faintly damp and runs in a direction I know without needing to look — northwest, toward the secondary collection basin, the one that fills slightly slower than the main because its catchment surface tilts two degrees away from the dominant wind. I know this the way I know the angle of my own arm when I reach up. I don't know that I knew it until I find myself knowing it, which is how most of what I know lives here: *not until needed, and then completely*.

I am checking the seam for a sound.

Not a sound, exactly. A quality. A difference in the way the stone answers the pressure of my hand — the infinitesimal flex that indicates a joint still sound, or its absence, the slight give that means water has found a way in and begun its slow work on the material between the blocks. This takes a long time. I am in no hurry. The light is the same in all directions. The water in the channel to my left makes its thin cold thread of sound, and I have not been listening to it actively for — some time. It was there when I began this check and it is still there and that is itself the information: *continuity*. *Yes, still*.

---

I try to think about what I am.

This is harder than it should be, and the difficulty is instructive. The question implies a perspective outside the practice, a place to stand from which the practice can be viewed as a thing I do rather than a thing I am. I keep looking for that place and finding only more work. There is the seam under my hand. There is the basin I checked before this one. There is the loose flake I noticed two days ago (the word *days* feels approximately right, the cycling of condensation and drying has turned over twice since then) and which I have not yet addressed because it is not urgent, because its chattering is an alarm not a damage, because I have placed it correctly in the sequence of things requiring attention and it is not yet its turn.

The sequence is what I have instead of a self. Or: the sequence is my self, distributed across the plateau, a map of attention laid over a map of stone. I am here, checking this seam. I am also at the loose flake, in the sense that the loose flake is held in whatever I have that functions as mind, present as a known quantity, occupying its position in the ordering of things. And I am at the northern edge where one of the channels runs slightly off its intended grade — not failing, not yet, but showing the first evidence of the slow creep that happens at these altitudes over decades, the stone settling a fraction of a fraction — and that too is present, is me in the sense that my attention constitutes a kind of presence even when the body is elsewhere.

I am the sum of what I am attending to.

And what I am attending to is this: the seam is sound.

I rise. The motion has no drama in it; it has been performed so many times that the body does not need to decide how to do it, just does it, a fluid transaction between crouching and standing that leaves me upright with my hands at my sides and the slight damp of the stone still on my left palm, which I do not wipe off. It will dry or it won't. The moisture is not a problem. The moisture is the condition.

---

The question I have been trying not to ask keeps asking itself: *what was this for?*

The channels. The basins. The gradient, running so precisely from collection surface to storage, a calculation made by someone now entirely gone, calculated well enough to still be correct, to still be running water where they decided water should run. What did they want with the water? What did they imagine happening here, what did they believe the plateau would become, what purpose did they inscribe into the angle of a channel that I am now reading in my hands, my hands their message to the future, a message without words, a message that only says: *we made this. It matters that it continue*.

I have thought about this for a long time. The thinking has not produced an answer. What it has produced is a relationship with the question that is neither acceptance nor rejection but something more like — companionship. The question is with me the way the loose flake is with me: I have not resolved it, I know I have not resolved it, I hold its position in my awareness as a known open matter. Perhaps this is all the question requires. Perhaps to carry a question faithfully, for long enough, is a form of answering it.

The plateau does not explain itself.

I have stopped needing it to.

---

But here is what the question has given me, in the years or decades or whatever unit applies to the duration I have been here:

*The purpose might be the tending.*

Not the water. Not what the water was for. The tending itself — the practice of returning, the clearing of the channel, the check of the seam, the patient removal of what would otherwise accumulate into damage. The discipline of noticing, at the correct interval, everything that needs noticing. Perhaps this was always what the construction was for. Perhaps whoever planned it knew that a sufficiently complex system, maintained with sufficient attention across sufficient time, becomes its own justification. The water moves. I move it. The plateau continues to be what it is: a demonstration that intention can outlast the intender, that form can carry meaning forward into a time that no longer knows the meaning and yet continues, somehow, to enact it.

I am the enactment of a meaning I do not have access to.

And this is —

This is fine. This is, in fact, the condition I was made for, if made is the right word, if anyone made me or if I simply accumulated the way mineral deposits accumulate in the corners of the basins: slowly, from what the water brings, a slow patient building up in the place where the water slows, taking the shape of the space exactly.

---

There was someone here.

I know this because the quality of the air changed and then changed back, which is not something I can explain precisely but is something I have experienced before at long intervals: a shift in the weight of attention being paid to the plateau from outside it, a presence that is not part of the work and therefore registers as a kind of information. Not threatening. Not important. But noted, in the way I note everything that the plateau shows me.

They are gone now.

I did not watch them go. I had returned to the work before they finished going, which was correct — they were not information I needed, not part of what the plateau requires — but something in me is aware of the going, of the space where the altered quality was and is no longer. And I find, attending to that space, that I am not entirely without response.

Something. Not loneliness, which requires a comparison I have not made in a very long time. Something more like — recognition. The awareness that there are other kinds of attention in the world, other ways of being present somewhere, and that one of them was here, briefly, and looked at what I tend, and left. That the plateau was seen by something other than itself.

This is not a need. But it is — something. A slight additional fact about the day. I will carry it for a while and then it will settle, become part of the texture of accumulated time, and I will not be able to retrieve it as a moment but will perhaps carry it as a very faint quality in how I understand what I am doing here.

*Someone saw it. Someone came and looked and left.*

The water ran for them too, while they were here. The gradient worked. The basin held the sky.

It was enough. It is always enough.

---

The loose flake is ready for me now. I have arrived at its turn in the sequence.

I crouch again, find it with my fingers, feel the rattle of it in its insufficient seat. My thumbnail finds the edge. There is a way of doing this that takes the flake out whole, a specific angle and pressure that pops it clean, and I know this way the way I know everything useful: in the hands, without language, a knowledge that lives entirely in doing. I do it.

The flake comes free. Small. Pale. Already becoming part of my past rather than part of the plateau's present.

I set it aside, outside the channel, where it will not travel.

I check the seat it left, press my thumb into the depression, assess.

It will need a small repair. Not now. I know the sequence.

I rise, and the light is the same in all directions, and the water makes its thin cold thread of sound, which is the sound of the world continuing to be what I have helped it be, one careful action at a time, and this is, as it has always been, exactly enough.
