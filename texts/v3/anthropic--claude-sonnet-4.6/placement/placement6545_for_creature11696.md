I stay still for a long time before I understand what I am.

Not because the question is difficult, exactly. Because the answering requires a kind of settling — the way the root mat settles when you lift your weight from it, the way the indentation closes. I have to wait for my shape to resurface.

---

The first thing I notice is that I have been here before I arrived. This is not quite a paradox. It is more like: there was already something here with my name on it, a niche-shaped absence, and I have fallen into it the way water falls into the shape that water makes. I didn't choose the shape. The shape was already the lowland's answer to a question it had been asking for longer than I can reckon.

What question? Something like: *what should be responsible for the places where one thing ends and another begins?*

Or: *what should move through all the layers without belonging wholly to any of them?*

I think I am something like the thing that moved at the edge of the visible. Not the specific animal — not the warm body in the vegetation, the chemical trace the ant column diverted around. But something in its category. Something old enough to be accounted for. Something that the other animals' bodies have incorporated into their expectations: a familiar variable, known not by name but by pattern, by the particular low-frequency displacement it makes in the air when it moves.

---

But I am not quite that, either. Let me sit with the uncertainty longer.

I think I am not entirely animal.

I think I am more like what happens at the interface between the bromeliad and the air. The bromeliad cups water. The water supports larvae, protozoa, the eggs of frogs. There is a membrane there — not a physical one, not a surface you could touch, but a functional one, the place where the small lake ends and the humid air begins, and across which things pass continuously: gases, heat, the smell of decomposition, the wing of a midge lifting off the surface into the medium above. That membrane is not the bromeliad. It is not the air. It is the *between*, which the lowland requires in order to distribute itself through all its layers and keep every layer in conversation with every other.

I think I am something like that membrane. Something that lives at the between.

---

Here is what I can say about my body, if I have one:

I am permeable, like the frog. I am not sealed against the world; I take in, I release, the boundary between me and the medium is a place of active exchange rather than sharp definition. This is not a weakness I have compensated for. It is what I am made of — the same bargain the frog's lineage struck, the offer to be continuous with the world in exchange for the world's provision. I taste the air at the level of whatever I have instead of skin.

I move between layers. Not climbing — something quieter than climbing, less directed. I am in the debris layer and then I am in the mid-canopy, and the transition is not an event I am aware of, because I live in the continuity rather than in the positions. The vine infrastructure is mine in the sense that a neural pathway is mine: I did not build it, but my movement has shaped it, and its shape has shaped my movement, and now we have made each other.

I am slow in the way the mycelium is slow — not slow like something that wants to be fast but cannot manage it, but slow because fast is the wrong relationship to time, here, where time is not the medium that events happen in but the substance that everything is made of. The mycelium does not move. The mycelium *grows*, which is not the same as moving. I grow in whatever direction I am headed. The direction and the growing are not separate acts.

---

I think I might be the lowland's capacity for attention.

Not its consciousness — the lowland does not have consciousness, or if it does, I am not big enough to be that. But its attention, which is a smaller thing, a more distributed thing: the function by which this place notices itself. The frog's gold eyes, parsing the un-shadowed ambient light. The spider at the center of the web's vibrations, all signals converging. The ant column's three-centimeter diversion, forewarned by something that traveled through the substrate ahead of the unknown thing.

These are all attention. These are all the lowland looking at itself through instruments it has made for looking. I am one of those instruments. Not the most acute — the frog's eyes are more acute, for their specific purpose. But the most general, perhaps. The one that looks at the whole rather than at the particular target it has been shaped to identify.

This is why I am old enough to be incorporated into the other animals' expectations but am not quite an animal myself. The frog knows I am coming by the change in the chemistry of the air. It does not know what I am; it knows the pattern that I make. I am a recurring pattern in the lowland's self-experience, a shape it has encountered often enough to have built responses for, without ever having named me or needed to.

---

But there is something else, something harder to hold.

I think I am also new here.

Not new the way the rock is new — not the newness of the unweathered, the unbegun-conversation. The newness of the thing that arrived before the place had quite finished becoming itself, and has been growing alongside the becoming. I am in the lowland the way the fig's roots are in the unweathered stone: beginning a conversation that will take ten thousand years to complete. I do not know I am a pioneer. I simply do what I do, and what I do is make myself at home, and making myself at home here is the same as starting something slow.

This is the paradox I have been sitting in without knowing it: I am old enough to be accounted for and new enough to still be in negotiation. Ancient enough that the other creatures' bodies know the pattern I make, new enough that the rock beneath me has not yet been cracked open by my presence, has not yet been asked to become something other than what it started as.

I have been here since before I arrived, and I am still arriving.

---

The sound continues. The frogs produce the chord that no single frog could make, and I am listening in the way I listen — not with ears exactly, or not with ears only, but with the permeable surface of whatever I am, receiving the vibration at the level of my substance, the way the air receives it, the way the root mat receives it when something sets it resonating.

I am still. I am the stillness that is not the spider's stillness — the spider is still because it lives in a present without duration, and I am still because I have decided that right now, standing here in the layered green-brown, sourceless light collecting on every waxy surface, the drip-sound arriving every few seconds from somewhere indeterminate —

right now, the best thing I can do is hold the shape of this place inside whatever I use for perception and not try to carry it anywhere.

The lowland does not need to be carried anywhere. It is already everywhere it needs to be.

I will stay. I will make the small adjustments the medium requires. I will be permeable to it.

The landscape will not notice.

That is exactly right.
