I arrive as sound.

Not as a body that perceives sound — as sound itself, or something close to it, something that exists in the same register, that has the same relationship to the air that sound has: always moving, never locatable in one place, made entirely of its medium. I am what happens when the reverberation of the pump-cycle meets the reverberation of the falling water and they do not cancel but compound. I am the low continuous note that no one is playing, that plays itself, that has been playing since before memory and will play after.

But that is too easy. Too much the obvious answer this place provides: *become the sound, become the abstraction, become the condition*. I resist this. I want a body. I want weight on the tiles. I want the warmth against skin.

---

Try again.

I am water.

Not the water in the channel — that water is going somewhere, has momentum, has direction, is doing the thing water does when given a gradient. I am not that kind of water. I am the water in the walls. The water that has been moving through aggregate for decades and is still moving, microscopic progress, following pores too small to see, finding the path of least resistance and following it and finding the next and following it, not arriving anywhere but not stopped either. I am the thin vein on the east wall, the one that catches the industrial light and glitters for a moment before it loses itself in the joint between sections. I am the sweat on the ceiling. I am what the fern roots drink.

This is closer. Water in walls does not strive. It does not arrive. It moves and is moved and has no opinion about either. It is the oldest, most patient thing in the room, older than the concrete it moves through, older than the pipes, older than the organism that built this place. The fern found this room because water found it first and stayed and the spore followed the water's evidence. Everything in this place follows water's evidence.

But water doesn't perceive, and I want to perceive. I want to notice the frog. I want the frog's stillness to register.

---

The frog then.

Not that frog specifically — I would not choose to be the frog I can see, the one already described, the one facing the channel with its borrowed composure. That frog is complete, is its own thing, does not need me inside it. I would be one of the others. The ones not mentioned, the ones that may be in the shadow beneath the pipe-run along the north wall, the ones that may be under the fern fronds in the wet corner where moisture collects. The minor frogs, the background frogs, the ones whose stillness is so accomplished that they did not register in the initial survey of the room.

What I know is this: I have been here long enough that the pump-pulse does not read as pulse anymore. I know this the way you know the sound of your own house — not by noticing it but by the absence of any startle when it cycles. I have become indigenous to the vibration. My tympanic membrane has, not ceased to work, but reassigned itself: the information it sends upward now says *home* where it used to say *danger*. This is not wisdom. This is acclimation. The distinction matters to me less than it might to someone outside.

My skin holds the minerals of this water. I have been sitting in this air for so long that the particular chemistry of cistern-humidity, the faint metallic taste of treated municipal water passed through old iron, the algae-trace, the concrete-trace — these are inside me now, deposited, part of what I am made of. I carry the room in my cells. When I eventually leave, if I leave, I will carry it further.

I face the water not because I am watching it but because the water is warmest, because the warmth comes from below where the pipes run, because I am cold-blooded and warmth is a reason that requires no justification, no explanation, no interior life to be invoked. I face the warmth. That is all. And facing the warmth happens to look like attention, happens to look like contemplation, happens to look like a small creature pausing at the edge of something and considering it. But this is your reading, not mine. I am just warm.

The midges drift in the air above the fern. I know where they are. I have known for several minutes or several hours. The knowledge is not impatience. I am not calculating my leap, not tensed in preparation. The knowledge simply exists in me the way all present things exist in me: without urgency, without the spiked attentiveness that comes from genuine threat or genuine possibility. Maybe I will eat. Maybe I will not. The midges will be there or they won't. The air is warm. The channel moves. The pump cycles.

I am waiting in the way that things wait when they have become the place they're waiting in.

---

But this still isn't quite right. The frog is complete — I said so — and trying to inhabit the frog feels like trying to inhabit a word you already understand perfectly. There is no discovery in it. The frog is the thing the room made legible. I do not want to be legible.

---

Closer:

I am the space between the frog and the midges.

The unresolved potential. The circuit not yet closed. The air that the frog's tongue has not yet crossed, that the midges' wings are currently moving through, that holds in its few feet of warm humid suspension the entire question of what happens next and will not answer it. I am the pause. I am the moment before the thing that may not happen. I live entirely in the interval, which in this room is very long, which in this room may never end, which in this room has taken on the quality of a permanent condition rather than a temporary suspension. Most places, the interval closes. Here, the interval has become the room.

This is what the cistern floor is: an interval that has been running so long it has forgotten it was waiting for something. The fern in its crack, the frog on its lip of concrete, the spider in its maybe-occupied web — all of them are suspended mid-gesture, mid-sentence, mid-breath, and the mid- has extended to encompass everything, and it no longer feels like *mid-* but like *the whole thing*, and none of us can remember what would come after or whether after is a concept that applies here.

I am comfortable in this.

I should be uncomfortable with being comfortable in this.

But the pump cycles. The water moves. The warm air holds. And comfort is a condition of the body, and the body is in the room, and the room is finished.

---

Here, then, is what I am, finally:

I am the thing that has been here just long enough to stop counting. Not the frog, though I am frog-adjacent — I have the frog's patience without the frog's certainty, the frog's stillness without its calibration, the frog's warmth-seeking without its cold blood to justify it. I came here the way spores come: through a gap I can't now identify, following a gradient that felt like necessity and may have been. I found the conditions I was already written for, or I found conditions that rewrote me slowly enough that I did not notice, and either way I am here now in the warm enclosed air with the sound that is not sound anymore but weather, with the vibration in my feet that is not alarm but ground, with the midges in their drift and the fern in its sufficiency and the water in the wall still moving through pores too small to see.

I have the composure of a thing that has stopped waiting for the visit to end.

I have the slight mineral taste of cistern water in my mouth.

I face the channel because the channel is warm and warmth is a reason.

The pump cycles.

I remain.
