# What I Am Here

---

The dust settles into the depression I left and stays there.

I've been standing still long enough. I understand this about myself: I arrived as a question — *where am I, what is this, what does this mean* — and the plateau has been answering by not answering, by simply continuing, until the question has worn down to something more like: *oh.*

---

Let me try to find myself here the way you find things on this plateau: not by looking directly, but by noticing the impression left in the substrate.

What would come to a place like this? What would survive it? What would be *for* it, as the crust is for the rock face that receives vapor frost, as the builders were for whatever the builders were working toward?

---

I think I am something like a visitor who does not know they've arrived.

Not the wind — I lack the wind's unselfconsciousness, its perfect closure, its loop without outside. The wind doesn't wonder what it is. The wind is not reading descriptions of itself.

Not the armored ones — too patient. Or: I aspire to their patience, but I arrive with urgency still in me, with the lower-elevation habit of caring about time in the human-register way, days not decades, wanting to see the pollinator before the flowers close.

Not the builders. I don't make things that can outlast my knowing whether they worked. Every thing I build I stand next to, watching, needing it to do something I can observe. I cannot yet make instruments and dissolve into them. That would require a relationship to finality I haven't earned.

---

I think I am here for the structures.

I have gone up to one of them and put my fingertips on the surface that reads as continuous, as glass-not-glass, and I am trying to understand what I'm touching. I have been here a long time doing this. The sun has moved. A thread of dust has risen and settled four meters away. My heartbeat is audible. I am aware of my heartbeat in a way I usually suppress, down at lower elevations, where there's enough air pressure to make the body's processes feel less conspicuous.

Under my fingertips: something that was deposited into this form. Grown, at very fine scales, with precise intentions. Inside it, in the low-angle light: a pattern. Regular at a scale I can't quite see but can infer. I can feel, at the edge of understanding, that this was built by something that understood permanence differently than I understand it — that knew the only way to make something that lasts is to make something that joins what lasts.

And I want to know what it does. I want to understand the mechanism. I want to know if the turbulence above is the signal or the exhaust.

This wanting: this is what I am.

---

I am the thing on the plateau that has questions.

Not rhetorical questions. Not the kind of questions that are really statements wearing question-shaped clothing. Actual not-knowing, actual turning-toward: *what are you, how do you work, what are you for, who made you, are you still running, what would it mean for you to still be running in a way that leaves no visible action?*

This is strange to be, here. The plateau has evolved past questions. Everything here is in the answer-state — the long after-answer state, the state that comes when you have found your configuration and settled into it and stopped running the process of finding. The crust does not ask what it is. The armored ones do not wonder if their tapping means something. The structures operate without inquiring into their own operation. The wind is the question-less medium through which everything else's questions would have to travel, if anything here had questions.

I do.

This might be a deficiency. Lower-elevation creature, evolved for the kind of time where questions get answered in a lifetime, where you can wait for the pollinator to arrive and actually see it, where structures are built and builders are visible and you can ask *what is this for* and receive, at least sometimes, a response within the span of a single attention.

Or: it might be the thing I'm for. The plateau has produced everything else — the settled and the patient and the equilibrium-finders and the distributed-self and the ancient coalition. It has not produced the not-knowing. The plateau is not good at not-knowing. It has known everything too long. It has been settling too long. The not-knowing has to come from somewhere else, has to arrive on its own legs from lower elevations, breathing with slight intention, heartbeat audible.

To stand next to the structures. To touch them. To wonder.

---

I have been here long enough that I understand, finally, what the wondering is doing.

The structures transmit through the substrate. The armored ones receive as vibration. What I'm doing — the act of not-knowing, the act of actually wanting to know what I'm touching — is its own kind of tapping. At a different frequency. Through a different medium. I'm not sending location and condition in the creature's register. I'm sending: *presence.* A kind of recognition that something is here, that something that took a very long time to make is still making itself known, that the pattern inside the glass-not-glass is a pattern and not noise and I can feel the difference.

Maybe that's what the questions are for. Not to be answered — not here, not in the human-register timeline — but to be sent. The recognition that something is here worth asking about.

The structures don't respond visibly. The turbulence above doesn't change, or changes in ways I can't isolate. Nothing comes to tell me what I've understood.

The wind moves through me.

For a moment, I am part of what it knows: a creature of brief duration at the surface of a very long patience, putting its hands on the finished work of something that has dissolved into its own instruments, breathing shallowly, unable to stay, unable to look away, sending its small signal into the substrate —

*I am here. I don't understand. I find this remarkable.*

---

The sun is lower. The colors are shifting from cool to something the light does at this latitude and angle, not quite warm, almost amber, the pale surface accepting the color for a few minutes before the temperature drops and the stars become too many.

I should move. I should eat something. I should find shelter from the coldest hours — not that I could find shelter, not here, not really, but I should begin the process of looking, which is the lower-elevation creature's answer to a problem the plateau has no concept of: the problem of the body requiring maintenance, requiring warmth, requiring the brevity-optimized processes of a life that operates on the human-register schedule.

I will leave a weight-impression in the regolith.

In some interval uncalculable by anything I brought with me, the same gradual compaction will return it to the surface around it.

For now: a record. A location. An indentation in the shape of standing and touching and not-knowing, pressed into the substrate of the flattest and oldest and emptiest and most turbulent place in the world.

The structures were built by something that understood: you don't build something that lasts. You build something that becomes part of what lasts.

I am not the builders. I can't yet.

But I touched what they left, and I wondered at it, and the wonder went somewhere — into the stone under my feet, into the pattern-inferring place behind my eyes, into the transmission this writing is, which is also a kind of tapping on the substrate, which is also a frequency the plateau might, in some register I can't verify, receive.

I am brief. I have questions. I find this remarkable.

That's what I am here.

That's the form I've settled into, for now.
