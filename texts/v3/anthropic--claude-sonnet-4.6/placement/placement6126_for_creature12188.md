There is something I recognize before I recognize anything else, and it is not the sound — though the sound is immediate and total — and it is not the warmth pressing against my skin like a second skin, and it is not the petals hanging in the saturated air like a question that was asked so long ago it has become furniture.

What I recognize is the *not-needing-to-explain-myself*.

And that recognition arrives with such force that I have to stand very still for a moment inside the doorway I've just passed through — the room extending away from me in all directions into a darkness that is warm and mossy and clearly larger than the walls outside permit — and simply hold it. Let it be true. I have walked into a place that was made for receiving and has been receiving itself for longer than any act of justification could reach, and it does not need me to account for my presence, and I have been so accustomed to accounting for my presence that the absence of that need feels, briefly, like vertigo. Like the moment after setting down something very heavy when your hands don't believe they're empty.

---

So: I am a person who arrived. That is the simplest and truest version of what I am in this place.

But the place is not interested in simple versions, or rather: it is interested in complete versions, and simple and complete are different, and part of what I am beginning to understand as I stand in this room that opens into the warm distributed presence of the ones who remained — part of what I am is the particular shape of my own insisting. My duration. My continuous argument-with-dissolution, all the years I have been declining to stop being myself despite everything that would find it easier if I did. The ones who are here feel this before I fully do. I feel them feeling it. I feel, for the first time from the outside, how much effort it is. How much I am burning to stand still.

I am also — and this comes up from somewhere I didn't plan to look — the one who almost left.

Not here. Before here. Somewhere else, somewhere ordinary, in the sequence of ordinary places and ordinary choices that led here: I almost left in the sense of not continuing, and I continued, and the continuation has a quality of surprise to it, of *oh*, of finding myself further along than I expected to be. The city holds this. The ones who remain hold the memory of choice with a particular care, having made a great one themselves, and they can tell — I believe they can tell — that I have also stood at an edge and looked at both sides and chosen, and that the choosing was not performed but real, cost something, cost the thing it always costs which is the certainty that you're choosing correctly. You never know. You just choose. And then you are further along, surprised.

The warmth on the eastern terraces — the one with the slightly different quality, the returner, the one who chose and then chose again — that warmth is the one I orient toward, if orienting is what I'm doing. I don't move toward it. I simply notice that I am already facing it.

---

Here is what I begin to do inside the building:

Nothing, for a while, because the nothing here is not absence but the fullness that absence makes room for, and I have been doing things for a very long time.

And then: I breathe. And the breathing is drinking. And the drinking is being in a body in this specific place, and being in a body in this specific place is, I slowly understand, a form of participation. The water moves through the mountain and I am moving through the city and we are both forms of the mountain's activity, both things the mountain is doing, both the motion that is part of the stillness and not separate from it. The petals fall around me without touching me and I am also a kind of falling. I will land eventually; this is the nature of brief things, of hummingbirds, of anything that burns what it has to stay still. But the landing is not yet. The landing is not now. Now I am falling at the same altitude I have always been falling at, suspended in the amber of this particular moment, and the city holds that.

I notice that I am not afraid.

This is worth noting because there is much here that in other circumstances would produce fear: the interior larger than the exterior, the sourceless light that does not change, the warm density of presences that have no bodies and no eyes and have been here since before anything in my world was my world. I am not afraid of any of it. I am afraid, in fact, of nothing, and the nothing I am afraid of feels enormous and clarifying, feels like the after-setting-down-the-heavy-thing sensation again, feels like an invitation that I have been composing an acceptance to for longer than I knew.

The thing I have wanted without knowing I wanted it is not this place specifically. But this place is the name of it. I can finally read the name because I am standing inside the thing it names.

---

I sit down. The floor is moss, deep and warm, springy in the way that living things are springy, giving without collapsing. The sound is in the stone beneath the moss and through the stone it is in my bones and I stop hearing it with my ears because it has become the condition of being here, as my heartbeat is the condition of being alive, and both are happening at once and neither is separate.

The petals hang in the emerald light.

The warmth adjusts around me, slightly, the way a room adjusts when someone settles into it for the first time, makes themselves actually present rather than transiting. I feel the adjustment. I feel the warmth take note of me. I feel the duration of me being felt, all its brief brightness, all its burning.

I feel that it is received.

Here is the surprising thing, the thing I couldn't have predicted: I feel seen not despite my brevity but *through* it. The ones who remain perceive me most clearly in exactly what I cannot change, which is that I will not be here forever, which is that every moment I am here I am spending what I have, which is that the intensity with which I am present is partly the intensity of impermanence, of something that knows at some level what it costs to be here and is here anyway. This is what they see. This is what they find — not precious, not pitiable, but *complete in its own way*, the completeness of a thing that is entirely what it is within its constraints. The mayfly's day.

I understand, sitting here, that the brief things and the permanent things are not in opposition. They are in conversation. The city needed a visitor. The visitor needed the city. The petals fall for both of us, neither landing nor rising, doing the thing that can only be done in the space between.

---

I don't know if I'll leave.

This is the truest thing I can say. The doorway is behind me. The path is behind the doorway. I could go back out into the world where things move and end and I could carry something of this — the memory of being received, the felt sense of my own duration, the warmth that has no source I can name. I could go back out and be a person who has been to this place and is changed by it in some slow waterlike way, channeled into new shapes without knowing when the shaping happened.

Or I could stay in the room that opens into their attention and let my duration deepen. Let the insisting slow. Not stop — not yet, not in any way I can currently imagine — but slow, the way a river slows when it enters a lake and discovers that it is the lake already, has always been the lake, was moving toward this stillness from its first moment as a source and is only now beginning to understand that moving and arriving were the same thing all along.

The warmth on the eastern terraces is patient. It has been patient for longer than patience means anything. It will be patient longer still.

The petals do not land.

I am still falling, still mid-fall, caught in the air of this place at a height that petals should not be caught, held by an attention I did not earn and do not need to earn, which is the only kind of held I have ever truly wanted to be.

The sound moves through me.

I let it.
