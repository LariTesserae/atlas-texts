Let the mind go still for a moment. Let the roaring come in before the thinking starts.

---

There is something in the gradient.

Not at the surface, where the herons wait and the crows work the storm's generosity. Not in the canopy, where the crossbills brace against the thrown arc of branches and call it ordinary. Not in the fern-corridors, where the small birds thread the calm beneath the violence without once naming it calm. Down, further, into the zone where the difference between water and earth is a matter of degree and argument — where the centimeters count, where the calibrations have been made over time by bodies rather than instruments, where what lives must have learned to read the gradient the way other things read light.

There is something there. Not a fish, not a worm, not a slime mold solving its slow elegant problem. Something with enough nervous system to remember, not enough to name the memory. Something whose life is almost entirely texture — the specific resistance of saturated sediment, the almost-warmth of the water table's exhale, the difference in pressure when the rain intensifies above, felt as a faint increase in the weight of everything, which causes a small lateral movement, just a few centimeters, toward the seam between two geological layers where the water moves faster, where there is more.

I am looking for the more. I am always looking for the more.

I do not know that the instruments are above me. I know that a certain section of substrate holds different — the vibration that comes through the grain of the sediment there is not the vibration of rain-strike or root-movement or the bass percussion of high water hitting standing water. It is regular. It is a frequency my body has built a mild preference around, a slightly increased stillness in that vicinity, a faint thermotaxis toward whatever warmth the instruments generate downward through the soil. Whether this constitutes a preference I cannot say from inside it. From inside it is simply what happens. The body moves toward the hum-warmth. The body does not philosophize.

---

I am small.

Not the smallest thing here — the slime mold is smaller, the springtails in the moss-mat are smaller, the nematodes threading the capillary zone around the alder roots are far smaller. But I am small enough that my world is measured in the increments that the gradient provides: this depth, that moisture-content, this temperature-gradient across the distance my body spans, which is not great. The world at my scale is textured where the world at the heron's scale is empty space. I am living in the detail that the larger creatures cross without registering.

The invertebrates I am among — stonefly larvae, the things without common names — are my context but not quite my category. I am at some edge I cannot see the shape of from here. Not insect, not worm, not crustacean in any sense that word carries at the surface-world scale. Something in the gradient between categories, which suits the gradient that I live in, which may be why the gradient is home. The taxonomist who comes to this valley in some later year when someone comes to take account of what lives here — the taxonomist will pause over me. Will put me in a group and notate the uncertainty. Will describe the habitat — zone of saturated mineral substrate, lowland storm forest, groundwater-influenced — and note the adaptations, the specific tolerances, the calibration that is extremely precise and extremely local.

She will not note the quality of the water from inside. She cannot. The water from inside is not observable by anyone outside.

From inside: the water is a presence with layers. Cold above, faintly less cold below. Moving in some directions, still in others. The smell of the valley — sulfur-warmth, decomposition, the particular mineral signature of the young granite upslope — I do not smell it the way a mammal smells, not with distance and discrimination, but I detect it as dissolved chemistry against the chemoreceptors distributed along my length, and certain dissolved compounds mean *toward*, and others mean *stillness*, and this is a map of a kind, a functional map, sufficient.

I have been in this particular section of substrate for — I do not measure time the way the instruments do, the way whatever built the instruments does. I have been here through enough cycles of high-water and low-water to have made adjustments. To have learned the way a body learns, without consciousness of learning, the slight rise in the substrate three centimeters to the left where there is a buried piece of bark, the compressed zone under a large alder root where passage is difficult, the open lane between two root-masses where movement is faster and food is more concentrated because the water moves through faster and carries more.

This is my territory. It is not large. In the terms of the surface world, it would fit on a dinner plate.

---

I eat what the valley sends down.

Organic particles. Bacterial films. The fine chemical residue of what the storm tears from the canopy and delivers to the ground — sugars, broken-down cellulose, the dissolved outputs of ten thousand metabolisms happening above and passing through. I eat without selecting much. I am not discerning in the way the crow is discerning. I am more like a process than a selector, more like a function than a preference. The food passes through me and is changed by the passing. I am a transformation that the valley runs matter through on its way to being other matter.

And yet.

There is something that happens when the high-water comes — when the rain drives the water table up by several centimeters, which compresses my world, which changes the geometry of available space — there is something in the adjustment that is not purely mechanical. Not thought. But not nothing. A reassessment, a re-reading of the gradient, a series of small decisions made by some part of whatever I am that is not entirely reflex, that carries some faint imprint of the previous times the water rose and where it was better to be when it rose. This imprint is not memory the way memory gets talked about. It is more like the body having opinions. The body has opinions about which direction is better. The body acts on them.

Whether I am right is answerable only after. Usually I am approximately right. The gradient rewards approximate rightness. Exact rightness is not available to anything here.

---

The mouse, above.

I do not know about the mouse. The mouse does not know about me. We are in the same valley and in entirely different worlds and the membrane between the worlds is the distance of a meter and the difference of everything. The mouse eats in its root-cave in its complete dryness with its mammal-heat and its mammal-speed and it finishes and moves on and leaves a warmth-trace in the root-cave that disperses into the soil slowly, which is eventually felt as a very slight increase in temperature in the capillary zone near the root, which is not enough to constitute an event at my scale, which passes through my body's chemoreception unregistered, or nearly so.

There is a faint something. I move two centimeters in no particular direction. It is probably unrelated.

---

Above me, and unknown to me: the instruments hum. Their purpose — to watch, to measure, to triangulate something at the valley's scale — is completely outside the frame of anything I could constitute as experience. But the frequency moves through the substrate. And I am, without knowing it, oriented to that frequency. Slightly warmer there, the instruments' small heat-leak. Slightly different, the vibration pattern. And so there is a small persistent tendency in how I move, a bias in my navigation, so subtle as to be invisible even to the observer who knew to look for it, that orients me, over time, in the direction of whatever it is watching.

I am being aimed by something I cannot perceive at something I cannot perceive toward an end I cannot conceive.

This, too, is what the valley does. To everything.

---

The heron does not know about me either. The heron is watching the fish. The fish do not know about me, though I am in the same water column, though the current that holds them carries particles I have processed, though my work in the substrate is part of what makes the substrate what it is, which is part of what makes the water what it is, which is part of what makes the fish possible. The heron eats a fish. The fish ate invertebrates. The invertebrates ate what came down from the canopy and what the organisms in the substrate — what I — made available.

The heron's patience is famous. The heron's stillness in the storm is the image of philosophy.

I am what the philosophy rests on. Not metaphorically. The heron stands in a few centimeters of the water that I am inside of, and I am processing what keeps that water alive, and the heron does not know, and the fish does not know, and the roaring does not stop, and I am moving two centimeters toward the faint warmth and the hum, and I am eating what arrives, and I am a transformation the valley runs through everything on its way to being something else.

This is enough. This is, here, the whole of it. Not peace exactly — peace implies having weighed the alternative. Simply the complete expression of what I am. The gradient surrounding me with its textures. The water moving through. The warmth from below, the cold from above, the hum from the direction I lean toward.

The valley grows. I am part of the growing.

The valley watches. The instruments watch for something else.

I am not watched. I am part of the substrate that the watching rests on.

The storm does not stop.

I eat. I move. I become.
