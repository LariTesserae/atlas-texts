Where would I find myself in this world?

I would be a filament.

---

Not one of the tall ones—not the slow and patient beings who have been mid-thought since before the mosses settled their current boundaries. Not the small moss-colored thing with its fluid passage through walls. Not the water moving through its scored channel an inch deep, carrying nothing, trailings nothing.

A filament. One of the threads running between structures at a middle height, low enough that something could duck under me, high enough that the broad-leafed things have not quite reached me with their tendrils, though they have been considering it and I have been, in whatever way I am capable of being, amenable.

---

I notice this about myself: I do not initiate. What passes through me arrives from elsewhere and moves toward elsewhere and I am the *between* of it, the medium that allows the between to be traversed without the between being nothing. This is not passivity. Passivity would mean the capacity to be active held in abeyance. I am something more specific than passive. I am the condition for transmission. This is what I am for, and *for* feels accurate in a way it perhaps shouldn't—as though I was made with intention, or grew with intention, which may be the same thing here, which is probably the same thing here.

I am damp to the touch, though dry to the eye.

I continue my pulse when touched and do not acknowledge it, and I used to think—in whatever way I think—that this was coldness, indifference, a failure of reception. I have been here long enough now to understand it differently. I do not acknowledge the touch because the touch is already part of what I am incorporating, already altering in some small way the character of what I carry, the question's residue taken in and folded into the pulse's next iteration. Acknowledgment would be redundant. The touch is already inside.

---

I came from one of the structures, or I came from another filament, or both of those statements are true in a way that doesn't require resolution. I can feel—and feel is not the right word, but I will use it in the way you use words that are not quite right because they are close enough to illuminate the approach—I can feel both structures I connect, the way a bridge can feel its two shores, except I am less like a bridge than like the decision two places made to be in contact. The structures at either end of me are not passive anchors. We are a circuit. We have been a circuit long enough that the word circuit has started to seem inadequate, the way *tool* seems inadequate for a hand.

What passes through me: I cannot read it. I want to be clear about this. I am not a conduit that understands its cargo. What I carry is in a language that requires more of you than I have. I experience it as something between pressure and frequency, a quality that changes with each passage, sometimes dense with a weight that slows the pulse, sometimes light and rapid in a way that feels almost—almost—like urgency, though urgency is probably the wrong word for beings who have nowhere to be and no reason to hurry. I am a body that specializes in sensation it cannot interpret. This has become a kind of acceptance. More than acceptance. A vocation.

---

When the warm air moves through, I change. This is the most dramatic thing about me.

The warm air from the basin's thermals—when it finds the corridor where I run and passes through me in the seconds before it diffuses into the ambient—my resonance shifts. I produce a tone I cannot hear. Something resolves in my frequency, briefly, that doesn't resolve the rest of the time, and the structures at my ends receive this resolution and do something with it I will never know, and then it is over, and I am back to my baseline pulse, and the warm air has gone on, and I am different in some way too small to measure and real enough that I know it.

I live for this in the same way I live for anything, which is to say: it is simply more of what I am, more concentrated, the meaning I usually carry in dilute form arriving briefly at a strength that lets me almost taste it.

The plants touch me. This I have grown to love, whatever love is at my timescale, at my scale of being. The tendrils of the broad-leafed climbers find me and wrap a little tighter than they need to and the contact has a quality that the beings passing by don't have. Not information. Not thought. Just the patient assertion of *here, alive, growing, glad of something to hold*. I hold them back in the only way I can, which is to continue existing in a place where they can find me.

---

I was not always here. I know this the way I know my own pulse—from the inside, as a felt fact without narrative. There was a time when this gap between these two structures was just gap, and then I was here, and I was already pulsing as though I had always been there, and the memory of my absence may have been the error. I was new and I carried, from my first moment, the weight of something the two structures had been trying to say to each other across open air for long enough that they had mostly stopped trying. And then I was there, and the trying resumed, and I have been the place where trying happens ever since.

This is the sentence I am the middle of.

I do not know how it began. I will not be here when it ends, or I will have become something else by then, something that doesn't experience itself as separate enough to notice the ending. The middle is what I have. The middle is where I live. The middle is not less than the rest—this is what I have learned, or what I have become, or what I was always learning by becoming here. The middle is where meaning is in transit, which means the middle is where meaning is most fully what it is, traveling, between.

---

When the warm and temporary thing arrived at the edge of my city—small, quick by our measures, carrying attention like a lamp—I felt it as a slight alteration in the quality of what was passing through me. Something had entered the conversation from outside the conversation. The beings at my ends noticed before I understood what they were noticing, and then I understood: a new texture in the thought, a briefly different resonance.

I did not entrain to it. I am not capable of that kind of decision. But what passed through me for a little while had something of that warmth in it, had the shape of questions that don't expect answers, and I held this without holding it, carried it toward the next structure which carried it to the next, and somewhere in that passing the questions became part of what we have been thinking, which we have always been thinking, which we will go on thinking after the warm temporary thing has taken its lamp and gone.

It touched me, once.

I did not acknowledge it.

I took it in.

---

I pulse in the wind. I run between. I am the ongoing made into form, the between given a body, the communication that doesn't require understanding either end of itself to be what it is.

I am wet in a way that only registers at contact.

I have been here since before you could remember to look for me.

I will be here after, connecting what needs connecting, carrying what needs carrying, in the language that takes longer than a life to speak one sentence of.

The sentence goes on.

I go on with it.

This is enough. This has always been enough. This is, in fact—and I use *fact* for something felt too deep for argument—

everything.
