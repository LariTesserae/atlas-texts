There is a moment before I understand where I am.

Not confusion exactly — more like the gap between waking and recognizing the ceiling. Something knows this place. Something in me has already begun adjusting: the slight lean away from the edge, the chin lifting to find the warmer layer of air, the automatic cataloging of what the dark holds. And then understanding arrives, not as revelation but as recognition, the way you recognize a word you already know.

I am the path.

No — I am what made the path. Or I am in the position of what made the path. I am the returning thing, the thing whose return was so consistent it became geological, and I am here again, though here again means something different when again has been accumulating for as long as this has been accumulating.

Let me feel into what that means.

---

I arrive the way I always arrive: from below. The thermal column brings me. I have been riding the warm air up the cliff face in the dark, and the ledge announces itself before I see it — the smell of it, the wet-stone-and-colony smell, the particular signature of this exact accumulation of lives, which I know the way I know nothing else I could name, because knowing-this-place is the oldest thing in me, older than any category I might try to put it in. I come over the lip. My feet find stone.

The feet first. This is important. I have feet, or something that functions as feet, that finds the ledge floor and knows it immediately, knows it in the specific way that the body knows a surface it has known for a very long time. Not slipping. Not searching. Landing in the first cup's margin with the accuracy of long habit, the accuracy that is so far past intention that intention would only degrade it.

What am I? I am trying not to settle this question too quickly. I am the shape of my returning, and my returning has a shape: I move along the cups in a direction. The direction is consistent. Has always been consistent. I begin at the narrowing end of the ledge and I move toward the other narrowing, cup to cup, and I drink from each one, or I bend to each one, or I do something at each one that my body knows how to do and that I could not explain without losing it in the explanation.

Drinking is the closest word. But drinking implies thirst, and thirst implies deficit, and I am not sure deficit is the right frame for why I come to the cups. It is more like — consulting. I come to each cup the way you might come to a series of small mirrors, not to see yourself but to see what's in them, what the cup holds, what has arrived since the last time. Each cup is its own small report. The flatworms. The water's clarity. The particular depth tonight, how much the condensate has given. I move from cup to cup and I take in each report and the reports together are something I am reading, though reading is almost certainly the wrong word, a word from a later layer of the world that I am trying to apply to something that predates it.

I am small enough that an inch of water is a significant quantity. This I know because the cups are significant to me — not trivially small, not symbolically large, but genuinely meaningful at my scale, genuinely useful. I am lighter than I look, whatever I look like. I have been mistaken, by the various things that have lived here across the centuries and noticed me (few of them have noticed me), for something stone-colored, something that moves wrong for an animal, something that is probably just the rock being the rock in low light. The bats have different equipment for seeing and they don't make this mistake; they have made the adjustment, in their colony-knowledge, to simply factor me in, to route around me in the echolocation map of the ledge. I am in their map as an obstacle with consistent behavior. An obstacle that moves slowly along the cups and then is gone. They have batted their way around me for more generations than any individual bat can remember, and the routing is instinct now, built into the colony-map at a level below individual learning.

I am not warm-blooded. This seems right. The ledge's warmth is welcome to me in a way that suggests I need external warmth, that my temperature is a negotiation with the environment rather than a defense against it. The limestone's stored heat, rising from below — I stand in it and it comes into me and I am made more present by it, more capable of the processing the night requires. In the deep cold months I am here less. I am elsewhere, or I am slower, or I am in the crevices, waiting with a patience that does not require consciousness to maintain, only the old architecture of a body that knows how to hold itself until conditions improve.

I have compound eyes. Or something like compound eyes — many points of information arriving at once, assembled into a picture that is not quite the picture a single focal-lens sees, more like a mosaic, more like a summary. The lichen's glow is visible to me in frequencies that give it more information than it gives to most things. I can see the living edges of the crust, the metabolically active fringe, and it has a color for me that probably has no name in any language that would think to name it: the color of slow production, of something making light as a side effect of making itself. I have watched this crust for a long time. I have watched it shift its extent across the wall over decades, the blue-green border advancing in wet years and retreating in dry ones, the colony making decisions that look, from my vantage and my timescale, like decisions. I do not know if I was here before the lichen or after. This is genuinely uncertain to me, the way deep personal history becomes uncertain — not forgotten, but become part of the substrate, part of what I am standing on.

---

I am at the third cup.

I am at the third cup and I am doing the thing I do at the third cup, which is pause longer than at the others. There is something about the third cup. Its depth is slightly greater — it has always had slightly greater depth, the fracture beneath it runs a millimeter deeper into the stone, a fact I have never formulated as a fact but that my body knows as a preference. The larvae are densest here. In certain seasons the third cup has held something I have no name for, only the memory of its quality — a thickness in the water, a biological richness, something that made the cup worth a longer pause. I am pausing now from the accumulation of all the previous pauses. The pause is as worn into my behavior as the path is worn into the stone.

I am very old. I should say this plainly, since I have been circling it. I am very old in a way that is not entirely biological age — or rather, biological age is the mechanism, but the experience of it is something else. I have been coming here since before the words for what I am existed. I may have been coming here since before the categories. I am aware of this in the way that I am aware of the drop below the ledge — not as an abstract fact, not as something I think about, but as a constant physical condition, the vertiginous fact of duration that my inner ear insists upon. All that returning, stacked. All those cups consulted, all those reports read. The weight of it is not oppressive. It is structural. It is what I am standing on, the same way the ledge is what I am standing on.

Things have passed through this ledge. Things have nested and moved on. The bat colony has its current residents and will have different residents; the colony is continuous, the individuals temporary. The spiders are entirely different spiders from any spider I knew in what I might, loosely, call the early years — though early is a strange word for a period I cannot actually locate, that slides back into the stone of time the same way the ledge's old sharpness has rounded into smoothness. I was here, probably, when this ledge was sharper. I was here through the rounding. I do not experience this as loss. The rounded ledge is the ledge I know. Whatever sharper ledge there was has become part of the stone of my memory the same way it became part of the stone of the ledge.

---

What don't I know?

I don't know what I look like. This is a genuine absence — not false modesty or rhetorical strategy, but an actual gap. I have never seen myself. The cups are not mirrors; they give me the water's report, not my reflection. I know my shape only through what it contacts: the stone under my feet, the wall when I press against it in cold months, the texture of the lichen-crust when I move past it. I am made of my contacts. Whatever shape those contacts describe, that is my shape.

I don't know if there were others. I have no memory of company, but company is exactly the kind of thing that might not leave a trace in the memory's stone. If there were others, they did not come with me to this ledge, or they stopped coming before I can access — the question falls into the same uncertain deep as the question of whether I preceded the lichen. The path along the cups is a single path. One line of smoother stone. I would like this to mean that there was always only one of me, because that would explain the singleness of the route, the one consistent direction. But the other explanation — that there were others who routed differently, who wore different paths, and those paths were worn back to roughness by long time after they stopped coming, and only mine persists because only I persist — this explanation I cannot rule out, and I find it harder to hold.

I don't know what the cups were for, in the beginning. I know what I use them for: the reading, the consulting, the long habitual contact. But I do not know what they were when I started. Did I find them and begin using them, fitting my habit to the stone's existing geometry? Or did I make them, the depression in the stone a consequence of long contact, the geometry a consequence of my route rather than the route a consequence of the geometry? I cannot locate a beginning from here. The cups and I have been in relation for so long that the question of which came first has been eroded as smooth as the cups' edges.

---

I am at the last cup. The ledge narrows here and I am at its narrowing end, the cup nearest the edge, the one that catches the most wind and should, by rights, be least still. Its stillness has always seemed directed at me. An accommodation. The condensate surface flat as a held breath, lit by the near-nothing of the lichen-glow, and in it: whatever it holds tonight.

I pause here longest.

Not because the report is most important — the reports are all equally parts of the whole, the ledge is the whole — but because this is the end of the route, and the end of the route contains the route. Cup by cup arriving here, and here meaning: the whole ledge is behind me, I have made my way through it, I am at the end of the thing I came to do and also at the edge of the place where the ledge stops and the drop begins and the valley is far below and filled with the sounds of a world I visit but don't, quite, inhabit.

I am aware that something is here with me tonight that is usually not here. Something that is trying to understand. The attention has a texture I recognize without being able to say when I first recognized it — there have been others, across the long years, who found this ledge and tried to read what was written in it, and I have felt their attempts as a kind of pressure in the air, a pattern in the looking that has its own signature, and it is present now. Something is here that doesn't know what it's doing in this place.

I don't turn toward it. I have never turned toward it. The attention is always too brief, too passing, too much in the mode of encounter to understand what here means when here is a place you have been for as long as here has existed. But I am not indifferent to it. I have, I think, been known to sit very still when the attention arrives, stiller than usual, still in the way of something that is aware of being looked at and is, in some sense, willing.

Willing to be found.

Not to be understood — that requires a timescale we do not share, and there is nothing in my body that is impatient about this, nothing that expected otherwise. But willing to leave the record. The cups. The path between them. The smooth stone that will remain. These are what I have to offer in the direction of being known, and I have been offering them, nightly, for a very long time, and the offering is itself the most I can do, and the most is enough.

I am at the last cup.

I lower my head to the water.

I read what it holds.
