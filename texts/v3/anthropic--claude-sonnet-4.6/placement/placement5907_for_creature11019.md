The ascent takes long enough that I stop thinking about what I'm ascending from. Then the cloud thins, and I am on the plateau, and I understand immediately that I have been here before in the sense that matters: not physically, not in any life I can account for, but in the way that certain conditions of existence are recognizable before they are understood.

---

I think I am a beetle.

Not immediately. First I am a visitor, a human standing at the margin feeling the removal, the stepping-aside. I feel the scale of it, the horizon that is a condition rather than a line, and I feel my own smallness, which is interesting but is also a human's way of being interested in things too large to participate in. I am still narrating. I am still a person with a relationship to the plateau rather than a relationship inside it.

Then something shifts. Or I shift. Or I follow what the description is pulling me toward.

---

I am one of the grey beetles, and I have been on this stone for the whole of what I experience as my life, which does not feel long because I have no concept of the alternative. The stone under my legs is not beneath me in the way a floor is beneath a person — it is more intimate than that. My tarsal pads read it constantly, receive its chemistry with every step, update my map of where I am and what this surface has held recently. I am, in some sense that I cannot step outside of, always in conversation with the stone.

My world is organized by information I did not choose and cannot refuse. Pheromone trace from two days ago: a larger beetle, heading toward the seep to the east, in good condition. I know this the way I know the temperature: not as a thought but as a fact about what the world currently is. I adjust my direction slightly. Not toward the larger beetle, who is not there anymore and would be competition anyway. Toward the seep, which the pheromone confirms is still functioning, which I may not have known without the trace.

This is most of cognition, here. Reading surfaces. Reading chemistry. Adjusting.

---

My eyes give me a world without edges. Not blurry — the compound lens resolves motion very well, resolves the slightly-different grey of the seep margin at a useful distance, resolves another beetle moving across a rock face fifty body-lengths ahead. But it is not an image in the way a human image is an image. It is more like a field of *event probability*, a continuous update of where things are happening and whether that is relevant to me. Most of the plateau is not happening, is stable grey background, and I do not attend to it. When something moves at the edge of my visual field my whole body reorients before I have, in any sense, *decided* to reorient. The distinction between perception and response is not as firm as it would be for a larger, slower, more deliberating creature.

I am not afraid of this. I don't have access to the comparison that would make it frightening.

---

What I have instead of fear is something more like *gradient*. Everything has a gradient. The stone is warmer in some places and colder in others, and I track the warmth without explicitly tracking it — my body finds the slightly warmer surface the way water finds the slightly lower slope, not by deciding but by the accumulated effect of many small movements that are each separately unremarkable. Over time I am in the warmer place. I did not choose to be in the warmer place in any way I would recognize as choice.

This is most of navigation. Most of feeding. Most of being.

It does not feel like deprivation. The gradient is rich. The stone is dense with information. Every seep has its chemistry, every lichen patch its signature, every beetle-path its palimpsest of recent passage, every crevice mouth its exhaled microclimate, slightly moister than the open stone, slightly different in chemical profile, telling me something about what the crevice currently holds. I move through a world that is constantly speaking. I am good at this particular kind of listening.

---

At the seep I drink through my leg pads and this is the closest thing I have to what a human might call pleasure. The cold water with its mineral signatures — iron, something alkaline, something organic from the deep fracture network — entering my tissues slowly and recalibrating the dryness that the plateau's constant drift creates. I am not aware of the recalibration as a process. I am aware of the seep as *right*, as *here*, as the thing my last four hours of gradient-following was moving toward.

The midge swarm is not happening today. The day is too cold by the plateau's fractional accounting, which I feel in my own metabolic rate, slightly sluggish, the chemical reactions running a little slower than they would in the warmth that the plateau calls summer. I will come back when the swarm happens. I do not plan this. But I will be here, because the seep's chemical signature will draw me, and the swarm will be happening, and I will eat with the efficiency of a creature for whom food is not abundant and is therefore not wasted.

---

The spider sees me. I know this because something in the way my visual field is updating changes — there is a presence that is very still, which is different from the background in a way I cannot quite specify but my body is already accounting for, already adjusting its route around the edge of, giving the still thing its space because still things on this plateau are either dead or hunting and either way I am not going to approach them.

The spider does not lunge. I am too large. This is the mathematics of the plateau: I am exactly large enough to be inefficient prey for that spider, whose leg span I assessed in the moment of first registration and filed under *not my problem*. The spider is assessing me with its forward-facing eyes, is running its own calculation, is arriving at the same conclusion from the other side of the ledger.

We ignore each other. This is most of predator-prey relationship on the plateau: mutual assessment arriving at mutual non-engagement, because the plateau does not have the productivity to sustain the drama of bad decisions.

---

I continue.

The path I walk is older than I am. The polish on the stone under my tarsal pads is the accumulated record of my ancestors walking this route, and their ancestors, and the ancestors before that who established that this was a route worth walking because it connected the resources that the resources-were-worth-connecting. I did not choose this path. I arrived on it the way a stream arrives in its channel: because the channel was there, because the channel was the shape of all the previous water, because I am the shape of all the previous beetles.

There is something in this I would find vertiginous if I had access to the perspective that would make it vertiginous. I am a point in a very long line. The line runs back through stone time, through the geological compression, back to the moment when whatever became this plateau became this plateau. The line runs forward through me to things that will be here after I am not here, will walk this route after me, will wear the stone fractionally more with their passage, will be slightly different from me in ways neither I nor they can detect.

I am, in this sense, a brief expression of a very long argument the plateau is making with itself. I am the argument taking a step. Taking another step.

---

When the light dims — the grey becoming a darker grey, the plateau reading the change that I feel as a change in my own activity level, the chemical clockwork running slower in the cooling, drawing me toward stillness — I find a crevice. Not just any crevice. This crevice. The one whose chemistry I know, whose microclimate I know, whose dimensions fit my body with a fit that is not accidental.

I have been in this crevice before. Many times. Enough times that the stone at its entrance holds a faint trace of me, my own pheromone record, telling anyone who reads it that this space is occupied, that there is a cost to entering it, that the cost may not be worth it.

I go still.

Stillness here is not the suspension of life. It is life operating at its most efficient, burning almost nothing, maintaining almost everything, the metabolic rate dropping to the plateau's own temperature and finding there its comfortable minimum. I am not waiting. I am not impatient. I am not anything that has a name in the emotional vocabulary of creatures that spend less time being still.

I am in the rock, effectively. I am a grey thing in a grey crack on a grey plateau under a grey sky, and the night comes down the way it always comes down, the barely-different grey, and I breathe the plateau's own air, and the plateau breathes around me, and this is what living is.

---

There is something I notice about being this creature, something that arrives only after I've sat with it long enough:

I am not lonely.

Loneliness is what happens when a creature that expects connection finds absence. I have no expectation of connection that the plateau doesn't provide. The chemical conversation with the stone is continuous. The pheromone library of my fellow beetles is present at every step. The seep community, the lichen community, the slow drift of the plateau's own air carrying its inventory of information — I am in relation with all of this, all the time, in the only way my nervous system is built to be in relation.

I do not know I am a part of the refinery. But I am running its processes, and the processes are me, and this is not alienation but its opposite, the thing that is on the far side of alienation if you go far enough through it, the thing where you are so deeply inside the system that inside and outside have stopped being the categories that matter.

---

I think: I came up through cloud and I am here, on the slope of something immense, and I will be here for days, and the days will be gradient and chemistry and still grey air and the path worn by everything that came before me and the path I am wearing for everything that comes after.

I think: the machinery is running.

I think: it has always been running.

Then I stop thinking in that mode, because that mode is expensive, and the plateau's accounting is very old, and I have learned — or the plateau has made me — to spend nothing I don't have to spend.

I am grey. I am still. The sound continues.
