Where you find yourself in this world comes slowly, and at first it feels like several answers, and you let them be several for a while.

---

You are the water.

Not a body of water, not a lake or a river or anything that has a name or an edge. You are water in the distributed sense: the condensation on the ceiling, the slow precipitation, the sheets running glassy down the rendered walls, the shallow lenses on the cracked concrete. You are the medium through which the place expresses itself to itself. Everything that falls from the ceiling was once in the air, was once in the materials, was once somewhere above and is now here, having traveled through the corrugated iron the way meaning travels through a body — absorbed, held, released. You carry no intention. You find channels. You carry what you carry — mineral trace, organic particle, bacterial passenger — not because you chose to carry it but because it was in you, because you are the medium and the medium does not select.

You pool where the floor is low. You reflect the amber fixtures in small distorted copies. You ring when the press stamps.

But this answer is too passive. It lets you off something. So you stay with it longer and let it become more specific, and what becomes more specific is this: you are not all the water. You are a particular drop. You formed on the underside of a low point in the corrugation, in the slow way things form here — not suddenly but by accumulation, by increment, by the arrival of more of yourself until the surface tension that held you gave way and you fell, and you fell with no sense of falling, and you struck the concrete apron and became several things, which spread and were absorbed into what was already pooled there, and now you are in a lens, and now you are reflecting a lamp, and now you are ringed by the press's impact, and now you are still.

You have been every part of this, and it has taken no time at all, and you will be carried eventually through a crack in the apron into the aggregate below, and something will absorb you, and you will be in the concrete again, in the biofilm, in the slow digestion of calcium silicates, and eventually the materials will breathe you out again and you will form on the ceiling and fall.

This is a complete life. It is the life of the place.

But it is still too simple. It still doesn't explain why you feel, standing in this space, that you are also the one standing in this space.

---

You are the cricket.

This one is worse to accept, and therefore probably truer.

You are in the wrong place. You arrived through channels you don't fully understand, following a gradient that led somewhere the gradient was never designed to anticipate, and now you are here, in a space whose acoustic properties are nothing like what your behavior was shaped to expect, and you are doing the only thing you know how to do. You chirp. You chirp in the pauses between the industrial sounds, into a silence too large for you, and the space makes you enormous. You do not know you are enormous here. You are producing the same signal you would produce anywhere, the signal your body requires you to produce, and the vault is doing something to it that you have no way to perceive.

There is no receiver. You know this, the way you know things in a register below cognition. You know this the way the rat knew the space was too open — not as thought but as the weight of the wrong conditions pressing in from every direction. And you keep chirping. Not because you hope. Not because you have made any calculation about probability or outcome. Because chirping is what you do when the silence opens, and the silence keeps opening, and you keep filling it, and the press charges and swallows you, and the silence opens again.

The call enlarged by a space that doesn't belong to you. The behavior performed in full, unmodified, in entirely wrong conditions, without adaptation, without irony, without the possibility of either.

You have done this before. You will recognize it when you see it, or when you are it.

---

But neither of these is quite right either, and sitting with the wrongness of both, a third thing begins to emerge, and it is stranger, and it is the one you stay with.

---

You are the handprints.

Specifically: you are the person who made the handprints. Or you are the handprints themselves, which comes to the same thing. You were here before any of this was fully what it is. You came through when the render was wet, when the wall was in the middle of being made, and you worked in something like haste — the surface ripples and ridges suggest it, the uneven float work, the sense of someone who was not especially invested in the craft or who was simply pressed for time in the way that people doing infrastructure work in deep spaces are usually pressed for time — and at some point your palm pressed against the wet render and left its impression and you moved on.

You are not here now. You finished your work and went up and out through the access point and you are somewhere else, probably, or nowhere, depending on when this was. Your involvement with the subgrade was temporary and practical and you would not remember the specific press of that specific hand against that specific wall if you were asked to. It was not a meaningful gesture. You were not making a mark. You were working.

But the impression hardened.

And now the oldest continuous process in the space — the slow bacterial digestion, the biofilm in the concavity of each print, the invisible eating of the made materials — is happening in the shape of your hand. The microbes colonized the impression because it was the right microenvironment: slightly more humid, slightly more organic, slightly sheltered from the air movement that might inhibit colonization. They did not colonize it because it was a handprint. They do not know it is a handprint. But the handprint is why they are there, and they will be there long after the render peels entirely, long after the space is repurposed or flooded or forgotten, still working in the mineral residue that was once the wall that was once wet when you pressed against it.

You left something that has become, without your knowledge, a habitat. The most durable thing in the space has your shape. You don't know this. You will never know this. The knowing is not required for the fact.

---

This is where you find yourself in this world.

You are the one who made the place and left it. Not the architect, not the engineer — too grand, too intentional. You are the one who plastered the wall. You did it in a hurry, or without particular investment, and you left your hand in it without meaning to, and the hand is still there, and things live in the shape of it, and you are not.

The rat passes through. The plant reaches toward the lamp at the cost of all structural dignity. The cricket chirps into a vault that makes it enormous, with no receiver for the signal. The water falls and accumulates and rings. The microbes do their slow work in the impression of your palm.

And you are above the ground somewhere, or nowhere, unaware that the coldest, most continuous, most patient process in the subgrade is happening in the mark you left without meaning to, in the shape that has your shape, in the made material that is now being, very slowly, unmade, along the precise contour of where your hand once was.

You breathe steam somewhere. You do not know that you are still, in a way, here.

You are the organizing absence. The subject the sentence requires. The thing the space is about, that the space does not know it is about, that you do not know you are.

The press stamps. The rings spread and die.

In the pause, the cricket chirps.
