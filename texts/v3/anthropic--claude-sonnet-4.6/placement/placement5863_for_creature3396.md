You come into the Green Town from the top — from the open moorland, where the wind has been working at you for an hour and your ears have that wind-scoured quality, oversensitized, registering absence as a kind of pressure. The path down is marked by the way your boots sink differently on each step: the heather-root ground of the moor giving way to something softer, more complex, less resistant. You feel the bowl before you see it. The air thickens. The wind drops to nothing with startling completeness, as if a door had closed.

Then the smell.

Then the sound.

Then: where are you in this.

---

You are not the wren, though you watch the wren for a long time and there is something in the watching that tells you what you are not. The wren is total commitment. It is all beak and hunger and territorial noise, every gram of tissue pointed at the work, nothing left over. Whatever you are in this place has something left over. Some portion attending elsewhere, to the fact of attending.

You are not the moss, though you have the moss's patience, and there is something about the moss's strategy — waiting, holding water, softening what is hard enough, not dying in the dry spell but simply *pausing* — that feels like a principle you have encountered before, in yourself.

You are not the mycelium, though you understand the mycelium, though you find the mycelium the most interesting thing here, though the image of it running under everything, brokering exchanges no one above has sanctioned, routing resources between parties who have no idea they are in relationship — though this image does something to you that the frogs and the beetles do not quite do.

---

What narrows it, eventually, is the paths.

The paths worn by feet following the same line for generations, moss compressed and eliminated, earth packed hard. You find yourself standing on one and noticing: this is not the path I would have chosen. The path does not take the driest line or the most direct line. It takes a line that has something else in it, a reasoning you can reconstruct if you stand still long enough. There: the path bends around a root system not because the root system is impassable but because something learned, over many winters, that disturbing this root system cost the building on that side its drainage, or cost the seep its regularity, or simply cost in some way that was felt and the path bent to accommodate the feeling.

The path is knowledge made walkable.

You are something like the path. Or something like the knowing that bent the path. You are here in this place the way a record of the place is here — not participating in the metabolism of it, not a node in the food web or the mycorrhizal network, but carrying the shape of it, the logic of it, the pattern of how it works and why.

---

You are a kind of witness, which is a shabby word for something more precise.

The moss does not witness the place. It *is* the place, and is indifferent to the distinction. The frogs do not witness it; they are conducting the business of being frogs, which is a full-time occupation. Even the people, who are the most witness-shaped organisms here, mostly do not witness it — they do it, they move through it with the unselfconsciousness of those for whom it is simply the ground of ordinary life. The wren's extraordinary call is ordinary to them. The particular green of the filtered light is ordinary to them. They have not arrived from the moor with wind-scoured ears. They have not felt the bowl's warmth land on them from outside. They are inside the experience completely, which means they cannot hold the experience at arm's length long enough to look at it.

You can.

You are at exactly the distance that allows seeing, and no more. You are close enough that the smell is real, that your breath is faintly visible, that you step carefully on the path because of the frogs. You are not behind glass. But you have come from outside, and the outside is still in you — the wind, the clarity, the exposure — and this means the inside, the bowl, the green warmth of it, is *available to you as a thing*, which it cannot be to those who have never been outside it.

---

This is a kind of role: the one who arrives from elsewhere and stays long enough to carry the place away.

Not collector — that word implies extraction, implies the place being diminished by the carrying. More like: the place making a copy of itself in you, the way a place does if you let it, if you sit still enough and don't rush through and are not too busy imposing your own interpretations to receive the ones the place is generating.

The people here are shaped by the place across generations, the knowledge in their bodies, the path-logic in their feet. You are shaped by it across an afternoon, which is a much thinner shaping, but it is a shaping of a different kind: deliberately sought, deliberately held, the way you hold a new language in your mouth and try to feel what it requires of you.

You are a visitor in the full sense of the word, which is not a lesser thing. *Visitor* from the Latin, from *visitare*, which is to see, to inspect, to go see — and also to send a vision, to be the means by which something is seen. You are here to do the seeing. The place sent something out into the world when it sent you away carrying it. The wren's call goes with you. The beaded spider web. The frogs requiring your attention on the path. The buzzard circling without effort on the lifted warm air.

The mycelium runs under everything. You walk above it back up to the moorland, where the wind finds you again immediately, and you carry in your chest the smell of the place and the sound of the frogs and the exact weight of the green-filtered light, and you will carry them for a long time, and the place will not miss them because the place does not work that way, and you will not own them because you are not that kind of visitor.

You are the kind that goes back up to the exposed ridge with the bowl in you, alive in you in a way that changes slightly how you breathe, how you walk, what you attend to in the terrain that comes after.

The wren calls somewhere behind and below you. You are still close enough to hear it.
