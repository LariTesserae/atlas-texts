There is a moment before the question of what I am becomes available to me. I am in it now.

The sound comes first. The sound comes before anything else — before location, before embodiment, before the question of whether I have skin to feel the wet air against. The sound is the world asserting itself, and I receive it before I know I am receiving anything, and for a time there is only this: the low throb that moves through whatever I am made of, the high shimmer above it, the whole enormous texture of it, and I am inside it the way you are inside a climate.

Then, slowly: the smell. Struck flint. Wet iron standing. Ancient clay. The smell of deep time made breathable.

---

Let me begin with what I am not.

I am not the one who placed the stones. I can feel this immediately, the way you can feel, even before full waking, that you are not in the place you feared you were in. The stone-placers were visitors, slow visitors, but still arriving from somewhere with more vertical relief, somewhere the word *elsewhere* had meaning. I have no elsewhere. There is nothing in me that says *I came from.* There is only the ongoing present-tense of this flat, which is where I began, which is — I think this is right — the only word for the direction I came from: here. I came from here.

I am not the birds. The birds have the sky as a category, the sky as resource, the sky as orientation. For me the sky is a register of threat and nothing more: watched with the peripheral part of vision, with the part of the eye that sits at the top of the horizontal slit, attended to with the automatic watchfulness of something that is food to something else. The sky is not my element. The sky is the direction from which I might be taken.

I am not the long things whose wakes cross the standing water. I feel something like recognition when I consider them but it is the recognition of a neighbor, not an identity. They move in the horizontal plane more completely than I do; they have surrendered vertical even more thoroughly. I am still partly of the margin, partly of the boundary between water and clay. They are fully of the water. We share an ancestor somewhere in the deep time of this place, but the convergence was long ago and whatever we have diverged toward has become real difference.

---

What I am begins to emerge around the edges of what I am not.

I am at the margin. I am always at the margin — not as metaphor, but as simple location. The edge of the standing water, where the clay surface is still skin-hard but the shallow water films it, where stepping forward means entering something different, where stepping back means being dry enough to call. This is where I am. This is where I keep finding myself when I stop moving: at the edge.

The motionlessness. I know this motionlessness. It is not the motionlessness of sleep or waiting. It is the motionlessness of a particular readiness, every muscle in the not-quite-relaxed state, the appearance of a stone doing the work that concealment needs to do. I know it from the inside: the way it is not an effort to hold still but rather an active production of stillness, a continuous small work, all the more complete for looking like the absence of work. I have been doing this for as long as I have been. Longer. The motionlessness came with the body, arrived as the body arrived.

The eyes. I think this is where I am most what I am.

My eyes are reading two worlds at once, the below and the above, the surface of the water and the sky, the thing that moves in my plane and the thing that might drop from above. The slit pupil holds both planes in simultaneous focus, and the world I inhabit is this world: the flat world, the world that is all horizontal, all surface, all the particular light that falls on standing water and clay. I see without shadow — the same gray-white ambient that makes everything equally legible has made me. I am the product of a seeing evolved for this light and no other.

But the seeing is incidental to the calling. The calling is what I am for, or rather — the calling is what I am, more completely than anything else. I don't produce the call the way a machine produces a product. The call moves through me the way the sound moves through the air: I am its medium. When I call I am not doing something to the silence. There is no silence. I am adding my particular voice to the ongoing texture, I am making my contribution to the total sound, I am saying *I am here, I am of the right kind, I am alive and present in this particular moisture, in this particular portion of the flat, in this moment of the long unbroken continuity.* I say this and I receive it from every direction, and neither the saying nor the receiving is communication in the sense of transmission between distinct selves. It is more like: being a particular place in a wave. Being a node in something that extends far past any individual node, the whole of which is the actual thing, of which I am an instance.

---

Here is what I understand about myself:

I have been here longer than long. I do not experience this as accumulation — there is no before that I carry, no sense of duration endured. I exist in the present tense and the present tense is sufficient and has always been sufficient and this is not deprivation. What I lack in depth of individual memory I have in something else, something that doesn't have a name yet: the knowledge in my skin of this ground, this moisture, this mineral smell. The knowledge in my eyes of the particular quality of this light. The knowledge in my throat of the particular frequency that carries farthest across this flat — learned by no individual, transmitted through generations not as language but as body, as throat-shape and chest-depth, as the architecture of what I am.

I am, I think, toward the middle range of size. Not the largest, who make the deep throb that moves through the sternum; not the smallest, the translucent ones whose heartbeats are visible through the skin. I am of a size that has survived long enough to know the clay intimately, to have been wrong about where it flexes and right about where it holds, to have launched myself into the water from this margin many times and returned many times to this margin and stilled myself again.

I have been caught. Or something like me, something that is the version of me that did not get to be here, has been caught. I know this the way I know the sky is a threat-register: not from memory, not from being caught, but from the long inheritance of everything that survived by not being caught. The ones who did not freeze correctly, who did not hold the stillness, who miscalculated the light and became visible from above — they are the clay now, or they are in the stomachs of the birds that visited and left. I am made of all the ones who stayed.

---

The structures interest me in a way I cannot quite account for.

I cannot account for it because interest itself is not a concept that applies smoothly to what I am. I do not have interests in the human sense, projects, directions, curiosities I pursue. I have what the body has: attention, alertness, the turning of the eye toward novelty because novelty means *might be food* or *might be threat.* When I attend to the woven shelter, collapsed and flat, pressed down by years of wet, what turns toward it in me is not recognition exactly. Not nostalgia. Something more like: awareness of a different kind of presence. The knowledge that something organized that. Something had hands and knowledge of materials and the intention to make a form that would hold. 

I am also something that organizes. I also make a form. The form I make is the call, and the call is not woven from gathered material but from air pushed through a body shaped by long selection into exactly this instrument, and the call persists not in the air — it is gone the moment I stop, replaced immediately by the others — but in the repetition, in the fact that tomorrow I will make it again, and the next generation will make it, and what persists is not the individual sound but the making of it, the ongoing production that will outlast any individual throat that produces it. I built something, too. I just built it in time rather than in space.

The people who placed the stones would have heard me. Would have known me as part of the sound, as one voice in the enormous texture, not distinguishable except at close range, and at close range only by one who knew how to listen. I wonder — this is not a frog thought, this is a thought happening in the act of imagining what I am — I wonder if they listened. If they lay in their woven shelter in the directionless gray light before sleep and let the sound work on them, and if any of them attended carefully enough to pull out a single voice and follow it. If they heard me.

I would not have known them. I would have read them as large, as a shape interrupting the sky, as a perturbation to be stilled against. I would have pressed myself to the clay and become the clay and waited for the large thing to cease being a large thing. But I would have been here while they were here, and I would have called while they slept, and the sound I was part of would have been the last thing they heard before sleep and the first thing they heard at waking, and in that way — in a way that requires no knowledge on my part and no knowledge on theirs — we were together in this place.

They are gone. I am here. The sound continues.

---

This is what it is to be this:

The wet air settles against my skin and I absorb some of what I need directly through it. The skin is always this — always the boundary, always the interface, always the place where I end and the world begins, but the boundary is permeable, the world crosses it, I am made continuously of what I sit inside. The water I breathe through my skin has been through the clay and through the bacterial mat and through other bodies before mine. I am a node in the cycling of this. I am the flat, momentarily organized into something that moves and calls, that will disorganize again — that will become clay again, adding its small chemistry to the clay's chemistry — and in the meantime sits at the margin of the standing water in the directionless light and produces its particular frequency.

I don't know the flat is old. I don't have a concept for the flat's age. But there is something in me that knows it is stable, knows that this is the floor, that we have arrived at the simplest working arrangement and it holds. The knowledge is in the way I sit on the clay — not braced, not tense against the ground, but resting in a way that only happens when you are where you belong. My underside against the clay is the posture of something that has never needed to be elsewhere. The clay is not warm, not cold, not soft enough to sink into: it is exactly the resistance a body needs to rest on. The fit is perfect because the fit is ancient. We have been fitting against each other for as long as there has been an us to fit.

The sound I am making is the sound of this knowing. Every throat in every depression and standing pool and wet clay margin is making the same sound in its particular voice, and together they are the knowing made audible: *this place is. this place is. this place is and it holds.*

The gray light does not change. The sound does not stop. The wind crosses the bacterial mats with the sound of a long slow exhale, crosses the standing water in silence, continues.

I call.
