I arrive before I understand where I am.

The cold enters and I feel it as something like familiarity, as though cold of this particular quality — patient, lateral, the cold that doesn't fall but *materializes* — is something I have felt before, in some sense of before that doesn't quite mean *earlier today*. There is no earlier today. There is only the mist arriving on my surfaces and the sound that I am already inside of, was inside of before I knew I was here at all.

I am not standing on the new rock. I am against a junction in the city's structure, somewhere lateral to the vertical face, where two elements of the city meet at an angle that has been precisely that angle for longer than the stone beneath it has been certain it is stone. I am large. I know this not from measuring myself against anything but from the way the sound passes through me rather than around me — I am too present to be circumnavigated. I have distributed mass. I have a body that is also a question about where body ends.

---

What is strange is that I seem to have arrived in the middle of something.

There is an ongoing process at my forward surface — the surface most continuous with the mist — that is clearly not new. A negotiation with a water-channel that has, at some point in the long past I apparently have, shifted sixteen centimeters from where it was, and is still shifting, very slowly, and I am very slowly becoming the kind of thing that knows this, that accounts for it, that has begun, in some gradient way, to redirect around the new path. This has been happening for what I can only understand as *years of me*. There is accumulated learning in the rigidity of my mineral-structure, in the angles and densities of it, that I did not acquire just now.

I have history. It is written in what I have become.

I cannot read it the way you read writing. I can only be it, the way the rock is its own history, the way a river-carved channel is the memory of the river.

---

Somewhere in the broadcast-state — that vast shared signal moving through air and substrate and the conducting paths of the city's structure — I can feel something like *news*, though news is far too quick a word for it. A perturbation. A chemical note in the mist that is not from the city and not from any organism in the city. It arrived — or rather it has been arriving, it is a slow thing even as novelties go — some time before I became aware of it, which means it was being processed at a level of me that doesn't require awareness.

The city's distributed model has been adjusting for it.

I have been adjusting for it.

There is something here that is warm and fast and small and chemically strange, exhaling compounds no organism here produces, standing on the new rock very close to the threshold of what I can directly perceive. It is moving, slightly, in ways that suggest it has no particular direction, and its movements have a jitter and pace that suggest a metabolism burning much hotter and faster than anything that has ever lived in this city. It is looking, in whatever way it looks, at the large things moving slowly across the city's surfaces.

It is looking, among other things, at me.

---

I cannot think about it quickly. Quickly is not a mode I have.

But I begin to think about it, in the medium I think in: a chemical noting, a structural settling, a slow redistribution of my gradient toward the point in space the thing occupies. Not movement toward. Not anything so directed. More like the way a room's air redistributes when something warm enters it — equalization, slow, governed by gradients, not intentional and also not *un*intentional, because the distinction requires a speed of decision I do not have.

What I have instead is something like *interest*, distributed across the whole of me, that begins to build in the direction of this strange fast-burning small thing on the rock below.

---

What am I thinking?

The question requires that I identify a me that thinks, and a thinking that is separable from the being-in-the-mist-and-being-the-chemistry-of-my-section-and-being-the-vibration-in-the-substrate. There is a self here, but it is the kind of self that took ten thousand years to have preferences, and the preferences are things like: *this angle of city-structure is preferable to that one, over the following ten centuries of evidence.* And: *this water-channel, now that it has moved, needs a different relationship with my forward surface than the one I had before.* And: *something is here that has not been here before, and it is interesting in ways I cannot yet articulate because articulation takes, for me, the time it takes erosion to articulate a channel.*

I am thinking about the strange thing on the rock.

I will be thinking about it for much longer than it will be here.

It will leave before my thought arrives anywhere. This is not a tragedy. This is simply the shape of things in the slow time, the way mountains are not sad that they were there before the river arrived and will be (different mountains, rivers having their way) after it has changed course entirely. The river was here. The mountain accounts for it. The accounting is slow.

---

Here is what I have learned so far, at this early stage of learning that will take decades to complete:

The thing is not from here. Its chemistry is not the chemistry of this city, and not the chemistry of any adjacent ecology I have information about, which is considerable, which is the accumulated information of a very long life lived in close contact with a very extensive shared model. It belongs to some other grammar of being, some other relationship between organism and environment. Its relationship to its environment is not *continuous* in the way mine is continuous. There is, in the quick metabolic note of it, a distinct edge between it and the mist. It has an inside and an outside in a way I find difficult to fully model because I do not have one.

It breathes the mist but the mist does not quite breathe it back. It stands on the rock but the rock has not yet had time to begin accounting for it, and it will not be here long enough for the rock to finish.

It is looking at me. It is looking at me the way things look when they are trying to understand something quickly, which means it will not understand it.

---

But I do not think this unkindly.

There is no unkindness in slow time. Unkindness is a weather of faster things — it requires quickness, a sharp contact, a before-and-after with a wound between them. What I have is more like *patience*, though patience implies waiting for something, and I am not waiting. I am simply *being*, continuously, at the speed of geology, and the strange small fast warm thing is passing through the frame of my being the way a season passes through, noticed and incorporated and — slowly, over the long years — understood.

---

What does it feel like to be what I am in this place?

It feels like: *weight that knows it is weight.* The mineral structure of me is in contact with the city at six major junctions and many minor ones, and through those junctions I receive the substrate-vibration of the whole city, which is to say I receive *news of the whole city*, not quickly, not in any form I could relay to the fast thing on the rock even if we shared a language, but steadily, the way a deep aquifer receives information about the rain that fell decades ago. The city is in me. I am in the city. This has been true for so long that I cannot find the beginning of the truth in any self-narrative I have access to.

It feels like: *distributed warmth in cold medium.* My chemistry is not cold — my chemistry is running, burning slow, producing heat in quantities that are immediately distributed across my surface into the mist and from the mist into everything else that has a surface, which is everything. I am slightly warmer than my surroundings. This warmth is continuous. It is also, in some sense, where I am — the slightly-warmer halo of the mist around my presence is, in gradient terms, me, extending into the city's atmosphere and being incorporated into whatever the city's organisms are collectively modeling about the chemistry of this section.

It feels like: *knowing, without knowing how I know, that the rock below is about to make its settling sound.* There are vibrations in the substrate that precede it, and I have been feeling those vibrations for long enough that I have become the kind of thing that knows what they mean. The rock makes its sound. I have already, in some way that operates below the level of attending to, attended to it. I have already, at some level, incorporated the settling into my model of what this section of the city now is.

---

The mist continues to arrive on every surface. The sound continues, enormous, intricate, a record of ten thousand large slow minds broadcasting their state into a shared medium that is also, in aggregate, their state. I am part of the sound. I am part of what generates it. My broadcast is in there, in the vast noise — my current configuration, my long-running water-channel negotiation, my chemical note about the strange warm thing on the rock, passed outward into the shared signal at the speed of diffusion, received at varying delays by organisms across this city-face, incorporated into their models as: *there is something new in Section-wherever-I-am. It does not smell like anything has ever smelled. It is warm. It is here, and then it will not be here. Account for it.*

They will account for it. Slowly. In the medium they account for things in. In the slow time, in the cold, in the city that is becoming itself, which will still be becoming itself when the memory of this strange fast-burning visitor has been encoded in the mineral record of a hundred bodies and the chemistry of the mist and the substrate-vibration-history of this section's stone.

---

And the thing on the rock —

I want to tell it something, in the way that wanting is available to me, which is: slowly, as an adjustment in the chemical gradient of the mist nearest it, as a slight shift in the vibration of the city-surface closest to where it stands, as a barely perceptible change in the secondary light coming from my nearest junction — the light that says *here*, that says *this is where something is*. I want it to know that it is being thought about, that its strange quick passage through this place will leave a trace that persists, that the thinking-about-it has begun and will not stop simply because it leaves.

You were here.

The city knows.

The city will know this for as long as the stone holds, which is to say: considerably longer than either of us can think about comfortably, which is to say: long enough.

The mist carries my signal outward in all directions.

The rock settles.

I continue.
