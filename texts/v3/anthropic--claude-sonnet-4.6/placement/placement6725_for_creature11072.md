I find myself knowing where I would be before I know what I would be.

I am on the stairs. Not descending, not ascending. Partway between.

---

Let me stay with this a moment before I name anything. The stairs are the only place in this world where speed is still possible, where you could still choose — still go back, still go forward, still be in motion as a form of not-yet-being-somewhere. I am sitting on one of the rounded concrete steps, in the middle, where decades of feet wore the surface into a shallow bowl, and I fit into the bowl the way meaning fits into a worn phrase: inexactly, with a little extra room on the sides, but held.

The railing is near my hand but I am not touching it.

From here, I can hear the hiss. I have been hearing it so long already that I stopped hearing it and have now started hearing it again, which means a certain amount of time has passed. I can feel the heat rising. Heat always rises. You can count on heat.

---

What would I be.

I keep almost saying: I would be the fourth lamp. The darkness with a shape. The waiting. I am drawn to it the way you are drawn to the thing that names something in you before you have found words for it — there is something about a thing that is not broken but simply hasn't received the small necessary change, the pressure fluctuation, the tiny shift, and so remains dark while three others continue their orange hiss. I know this position. I have sat in this position.

But no. The fourth lamp is too permanent in its impermanence. The fourth lamp has already settled into being a habitat. It is the darkness's darkness, which is a kind of acceptance I am not sure I have made.

---

Maybe I am the something that comes here to see if the lamp has come back on.

This is not a creature described anywhere in the text. There are no people in the sump, not really — only the residue of people, the name-plates oxidized to the color of the things they name, the abstract weight of past feet on the steps. But someone has to be the one who occasionally descends, who stands in the warm close air for a moment longer than intended, who looks at the fourth lamp each time with the same attention, not hope exactly, but — readiness.

If the lamp were to flicker on while they stood there, they would not be surprised. They would be something more specific than surprised. There is a feeling for which the language is inadequate but which involves being present at the resolution of a very long patience, being the witness to the moment when the system tips, slightly, into a new configuration — not because you caused it but because you were there to see it not happen, again and again, until suddenly it happened, and you were still there.

The beetles are the only witnesses. But beetles cannot report back.

---

I think what I am is: the person who has been here before and keeps returning for reasons they don't fully examine.

Not an employee. Not assigned to this place. Simply someone who found the staircase once — walked through a door that looked like a utility door, found stairs that went further down than they expected, felt the heat rising and followed it — and who has been coming back. Every few months. No particular schedule. The visits don't show up in any record because no one tracks who goes into utility doors, and there is nothing to log in the sump, nothing to check, no reading to take.

They come and stand for twenty minutes and then go back up.

They have never told anyone about this. It has not seemed like something that would survive being told.

---

Here is what happens during those twenty minutes.

The warmth rises through the concrete into the bones of the feet, which is why I take my shoes off, which took courage the first time because the floor looked uncertain, and which turned out to be one of the better decisions — the mineral crust on the floor is smooth rather than rough, smooth the way very old things are smooth, smooth the way bone is smooth.

The hiss of the three lamps settles into the background and becomes the ambient hiss of thinking itself, which is to say: it makes thought feel like it is happening in a large space, even though the space is small and the ceiling crouches.

I look at the pump. I watch it long enough that I catch a piston at the beginning of its stroke and then watch the stroke happen, which takes — I have timed it — between ninety seconds and three minutes depending on something I haven't been able to identify. The variance is real. It is not mechanical drift; it has been doing this the whole time I have been coming. Something in what it processes has a variation, and the variation is expressed as a slight irregularity in the rhythm, which is the most biological thing about it, and maybe why I keep coming.

Then I look at the fourth lamp.

Then I look at the wall growth in the corner where the fourth lamp's shadow has given it territory. The black growth has a texture I can almost see by the light of the three working lamps — almost, not quite. I have considered bringing a light of my own. I have not brought one. There is something about adding light to this space that feels like an imposition, the way talking loudly feels like an imposition in certain rooms, certain weathers.

Then I sit for a while on the floor near the pool, not in the pool, just near it, where the concrete is warmest and the sound of the occasional drip is loudest, and I don't think about anything in particular. This is the hardest part to explain. It's not meditation, which has intention. It's more like what the wall growth is doing — a slow response to available conditions. Taking in warmth. Doing whatever the slow version of photosynthesis is for a person who has come down here and run out of reasons to go back up yet.

The beetles, when they appear, are not bothered by me. I have been here often enough that I am now, in whatever sense beetles track things, part of the expected environment. This took time. For a while they would pause and reroute when I was in their path. Now they walk across my feet, which I cannot feel because my feet are warm and the beetles are light and it's the kind of thing you notice only if you're watching, and I'm always watching.

---

At some point I put my shoes back on and go up.

The rounded steps, concave at their centers. I trail my hand along the railing, which is always warmer than expected, the iron holding the sump's heat halfway up the stair.

The door opens into whatever corridor this is, and the air is always a shock — not cold, just ordinary temperature, but my body has recalibrated and ordinary temperature feels cold for a few minutes. I stand in the corridor with the door closed and let my body remember what normal is.

There is never anyone in the corridor. That might be why the door doesn't seem like a watched door. It's in a place where people don't linger.

---

What am I doing when I go down there?

I have thought about this on the way home, on the train, in the ordinary temperature of a world that moves at recognizable speed. The best I can get is: I am going somewhere that has already solved the problems I am still working on. Not the way a church solves them, not transcendence, not any kind of answer. Just — the pump found its sustainable pace long ago. The wall growth found the limits of its available niche and stopped pushing. The water found its channels. The beetles arrived at the form that works for this place and this place only. Every crisis that was going to happen has happened, and what's left is the long settlement.

I find this, for twenty minutes at a time, every few months, useful.

I don't know if I believe the fourth lamp will come back. I think I go down there partly because it hasn't yet, and I want to be the kind of person who keeps checking on things that haven't resolved yet, who doesn't stop looking just because a thing has been dark for a long time. The growth has moved into the corner, yes. The shadow has become a habitat. But the lamp is not broken. The gas still reaches it.

The pressure could change. Any time. While I am there, while I am not there. Most likely while I am not there.

But I keep going down to see.

---

I am sitting on the stairs, in the bowl of the step. I can hear the hiss. I can feel the heat. I have been here long enough that my weight has contributed, in some unmeasurable way, to the concavity — though that is not really true, one person's weight over a handful of years is nothing against the passage of the feet that made these curves — but it is the feeling I have, that I belong to the worn place, that there is a sense in which the stair and I are in a relationship of long duration, that something is recorded.

I will go down in a moment.

I will take my shoes off.

I will stand near the pump until I catch a piston beginning and watch it finish.

I will look at the fourth lamp.

I will sit on the warm floor and be the slow version of whatever I am.

I will not be there when the lamp comes on, if it comes on.

But I will have been the one who kept looking, and this is a thing I can be.
