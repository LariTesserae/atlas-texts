The first thing is the heat.

I expected cold—not consciously, but the way a body expects things, below thought, in the layer of prediction that runs ahead of experience and meets it badly when wrong. The cold doesn't come, and the wrongness of that arrives before I know where I am. I am sweating. I am confused in the way of a person whose most basic forecasts have failed. And then I understand: the warmth is coming from below, through the soles of my feet, through rock that has not finished cooling, and I am standing in the wrong altitude, the wrong temperature, the wrong century of mountain-time, and all of my wrongness is exactly right.

I am here. So: I begin to understand what that means.

---

I think, at first, that I must be a surveyor.

Someone sent ahead of development that will never arrive, tasked with measuring a place that will not hold still long enough to be measured. I have instruments. I believe in instruments. I believe in the patience of the careful observer, the value of the log, the eventual victory of accumulated notation over chaos. I came up with equipment and intention, and the equipment is either lost or irrelevant (the ground has moved since I set my reference points), and the intention is still present but has had to become something other than a plan. I am no longer surveying. I am only attending. There is a difference, and the difference is this: surveying assumes the thing you are measuring consents to be the same thing between measurements. Here, nothing consents to that.

But surveyor feels too single. Too finished. I try on other shapes.

I am the worked stone—old, from elsewhere, carried upward by forces indifferent to me, already there before here was here. I predate the peaks. I was built for a different altitude and a different purpose and I have been involuntarily elevated, and the elevation has cracked my mortar and pulled my walls out of true and I am still standing, barely, encoding in my presence the memory of somewhere lower, softer, less new. I am the message from before. I am what the Nascent return to, parsing, finding almost-recognition that never quite resolves.

That fits something. But I am clearly not stone. I sweated a paragraph ago.

---

I think: I am the tone.

The high, faint tone that is not wind and not imagination. I am the thing that keeps resuming in a slightly different direction. I come from no obvious source; I cannot be placed; I stop when listened for too directly and resume when attention is aimed elsewhere. I am the mountains' acoustic symptom—the infrasound of arriving rock finding one narrow frequency at which it becomes audible, translates itself into something that might be signal, might be noise, might be the difference between them explored. I move through the particulate dark and I illuminate nothing in particular, like the starlight, but I am present enough to be noticed, present enough that when there are several of me together we separate into harmonics, we suggest an interval, we climb toward a scale that hasn't been invented yet.

That feels closer. That feels like it's warming toward something.

But I am not just sound. I keep arriving at a body that sweats, a body with a history of erosion, a body that came from somewhere lower and remembered cold that never came. I am not only made of this place. I am also the thing that is not from here, the visitor, the different temperature—and the Nascent lean into my differentness as if it tells them something about themselves. So I am both: the tone the mountains make, and the listener who hears it imperfectly from outside.

---

I stay with this awhile. The ridge shifts.

What I am, I think, is someone who has come to a place before that place has a vocabulary for what it means to be a place—and who has enough time, just barely enough time, to sit in the incomplete grammar of it. Not the geologist, because the geologist wants to classify and I am past wanting classification. Not the mystic, because the mystic wants transcendence and what is here is radically, stubbornly, gorgeously material—it is all rock and dust and heat and chemistry. Not the explorer exactly, because exploration implies a frontier that, once crossed, becomes known, and nothing here is becoming known in that sense. Known implies stable. Nothing here is stable.

I am something like a witness—but to what is not yet a thing to be witnessed.

Which means I am the witness in the moment before witnessing becomes possible. I am here in the gap between the event and its legibility, attending without understanding, present without comprehension, standing on a ridge that is still arriving while I myself am the kind of thing that has already arrived and found the concept of arrival strange.

---

Here is what I find when I stop looking for a category:

I am warm.

I am confused by my warmth. I am the confusion of a warm body in a place where warmth comes from the wrong direction, from below, from geological pressure rather than sun. I am the human animal at high altitude, underequipped and overattentive, whose predictions keep failing and who keeps updating anyway. I am made of the same elements as the rock around me—calcium, silicon, magnesium, iron in the blood—and those elements came from the same slow processes, the same deep time, and they are just farther along in their arrangement, more organized, more temporary in their organization, more capable of noticing the organization is temporary.

I am what the mountains would be if they had eyes.

Not the mountains' spirit or symbol or dream. Not metaphor. Just: the same materials, differently arranged, old enough to have habits, new enough here to have none. I am erosion walking through a place that hasn't learned what erosion is yet. I carry, in my rounded-off edges, in my slowly adapting temperature, in my body's constant work of prediction and correction, the evidence of deep time—time this place has not had. And so I am also, to the Nascent, a message from before, like the worked stone, but I am the message that moves and breathes and sweats and orients toward sound.

They circle me. Their warmth is directional, cupped, inquiring. They are deciding what I might open.

And I realize I am circling them too. We are doing the same thing. We are both prior to the vocabulary for what we're doing to each other. We are both leaning into differentness—theirs into my history of erosion and cold and soft things and far-away origins, mine into their pure dispositionality, their world-before-habit, their existence as the mountains' aspiration in motion.

---

I am the thing that knows what age feels like.

That is the specific thing I have to offer this place. Not wisdom. Not purpose. Not measurement or classification or the usual gifts of the visitor. Only this: I have been elsewhere long enough to know what the elsewhere feels like from inside, and so when I stand here, in the heat and the dark and the still-arriving rock, I can feel, faintly, the direction time is moving. I can almost see what these peaks will become. Not the shapes—I cannot see those—but the quality of becoming-itself that is, eventually, what age produces. The dignity of the scoured and smoothed. The patience of the ten-thousandth winter. The grooves and channels that will be autobiography.

The Nascent are leaning into me because I am that future. Not their future exactly—they may not persist to it, or may persist through it into something unimaginable. But I am built of future-ness; I am made of having-continued; I am what happens on the other side of the gap between emergence and meaning, and I can feel them pressing close to that, trying to understand it, trying to triangulate what they're becoming by examining something that has already, in its small and temporary way, become.

---

The tone is three tones.

I am listening with everything I have—not for the message, because there is no message yet, because message requires grammar and grammar requires enough repetition for pattern and there has not been time—but for the interval. The space between the tones. The suggestion of relationship. I am listening for the beginning of music in a place that is still discovering it has something to say.

And I am, myself, a kind of tone.

The low, slow, complicated tone of something that has been worn by time and carries that wearing as its information. Not the high pure note of new emergence. Not the geological groan of major fracture. Something in between, something educated by its own history into a frequency that is neither raw nor finished, and that sounds—when the Nascent lean close enough to hear it—like the possibility of continuation. Like the news that there is an after, and the after is survivable, and what comes through the rupture and the crack and the shifting of everything underfoot does not emerge unchanged but does emerge, still, as something.

The ridge creaks beneath me. I am standing on something that has not finished arriving.

I am also something that has not finished arriving.

This is the only thing I know for certain, here on the newborn peak in the wrong warmth with the stars going briefly visible and then gone: I am not finished either. I have been elsewhere and I carry the elsewhere, but I am also still in the process of becoming whatever I am, and this place—this raw and radically unfinished place—has given me something by failing to provide the cold I expected, by being too new for my categories, by offering, in place of the explanatory comfort of erosion's autobiography, the strange exhilaration of a thing that is still, right now, deciding what to be.

The Nascent take the shape of something almost familiar, briefly, overhead.

I take the shape of something almost legible, briefly, to them.

Neither of us holds the shape long.

But the interval between what we almost are is where the music is coming from, and it is very high and very faint and it sounds, if you stand still long enough, increasingly like a scale that someone is learning.
