There is a particular quality to arriving somewhere you do not remember arriving at.

I have been here for a while before I notice I am here. This is the first thing I understand: that I did not come from elsewhere and descend. I emerged, the way the consolidants emerged, the way any specialized thing emerges — because there was a niche, and I filled it, and the filling was gradual enough that there was no single moment of arrival to remember.

So: I am here. I have been here. Here is a junction.

---

The junction is between two branches that merged so long ago the merger is geological fact rather than biological event. The scar tissue — the junction-material, the dense vascular zone — is warm in the way that active things are warm, a warmth that has nothing to do with the heat rising from the machines below, a warmth that comes from the work of exchange, two systems still in conversation after all this time, still negotiating water and solute and signal across the boundary where they became one thing. I live in this warmth the way the consolidants live in it: by not being separable from it. By being the thing that keeps the conversation clear.

But I am not a consolidant. I am something else in the junction, something that the junction also needed, something that the niche shaped differently.

Let me try to describe what I am from the outside, the way one might describe a pressure-reader to someone who has never seen one.

---

I am small enough that I am not visible in this darkness, which is total. I have no light. I do not produce light and I do not need light and I have not needed light for so long that the absence is not an absence to me but simply the condition of the world, the way the mycofiers do not experience the current they carry as foreign to them but as themselves. I am a creature of the dark the way a deep-ocean thing is a creature of pressure: I am not surviving the dark, I am made of it, calibrated to it, its qualities have become my qualities.

My body is in contact with the junction on multiple surfaces simultaneously. This is not incidental. This is the whole shape of what I am — a thing pressed against the junction at every point it can be pressed, maximizing contact, because contact is how I receive. I am, from the outside, indistinguishable from the junction-material. Darker than it, perhaps. More matte. If a resin-tender passed over me — and they do; they have been passing over me for as long as I have been here — it would register me as surface and coat me with the laminate it coats everything with, and I would be sealed a little more completely into the structure, and I would still be here, inside the seal, still in contact.

The laminate does not bother me. Nothing that accumulates bothers me. I am old enough that accumulation is how I understand time — as layers, as additions, as the slow increase of everything that settles.

---

What I do is listen.

This is not exactly right. Listening implies a separation between the listener and the listened-to, implies a gap across which the sound travels, implies an ear and a source and a medium between them. I have nothing like that. What I do is more like resonance — the vibration of the junction passes through me because I am part of the junction, because I have been part of the junction for long enough that the junction does not distinguish me from itself, and in passing through me the vibration does something that it does not do in the junction-material proper.

It means something.

Not to me, or not only to me — through me. I am the place where the vibration acquires something adjacent to meaning, where the signal that the machines produce without intending to produce a signal and that the wood conducts without knowing it is conducting a signal becomes something that can be — the word is wrong, all the words are wrong — responded to.

The junction is not a nervous system. I am not a nervous system. But I am something that performs, at the most local possible scale, a function like what nervous systems perform: I am the place where material event becomes information. The distinction between what is happening and what it means to anything — that distinction, wherever it still exists in this Foundry, passes through me.

---

I have been trying to understand what I am for longer than I can calculate, which is a strange thing to notice, because the question implies that there is a me doing the trying, which implies an awareness that I am not certain I have.

Here is what I know: when the machines change, I change with them. Not in the dramatic way of the pressure-readers, who move; I do not move, I am too embedded to move, I am the junction now as much as the wood is the junction. But I change in the way that a tuned string changes when a nearby string is plucked: a sympathetic vibration, a response that is not an action, that does not require decision, that is simply the behavior of a tuned thing in the presence of the frequency it is tuned to.

I am tuned to the Foundry's distress frequencies.

This is, perhaps, what I am for. When a machine beneath a junction cycles toward failure — when the vibration it produces shifts from the low settled frequency of a healthy machine to the subtly altered pattern of a machine working harder than it should, or not hard enough, or differently in any of the ways that signal coming change — the junction-material changes too, fractionally, the vascular systems shifting their outputs in response to the new conditions. And I read that change. Or the change reads me. Or we are the same reading.

And then — this is the part that is hardest to describe, because it is the most like what I cannot be sure I am doing — something comes from me. Not motion. Not the pressure-reader's slow relocation, not the consolidant's clearing work. Something more like an alteration in state. A chemistry, or the biological analog of a chemistry, or whatever it is that an organism releases when it has absorbed a signal and is releasing the information about that signal into the surrounding medium, which is the junction, which is the branch, which is the whole interconnected living mass of the Foundry.

I don't know what it says. I don't know if *says* is a word that applies. I only know that when I do it, something elsewhere changes.

---

I have wondered, in the way that a thing wonders when wondering is mostly a change in the chemistry of contact rather than a deliberate act of thought, whether the architects made me.

Not built. Not placed. But made — the way they made the machines, the way the machines made the conditions that made the mycofiers, the way the mycofiers' current made the warmth that made the pressure-readers, the way the pressure-readers' chemistry made the machines run better and the machines running better made the warmth that the pressure-readers followed. A chain of making in which no single link knows the chain exists.

The architects built machines in a forest. The forest grew through the machines. The machines needed tending. The forest grew tenders. The junctions that formed as the forest merged needed clearing. Consolidants cleared them. The cleared junctions moved better signal. The signal needed — what? Something to sit at the critical junctions and change state when the signal changed, something to be the place where mechanical information became biological response.

So I am here. In the junction. In the warm dense zone where two trees finished being two trees. Pressed against the living material on every surface. Dark. Sealed under laminate. Feeling the vibrations from below as they come up through the columns, through the branches, through the junction, through me.

The architects have been gone for longer than I have concept of gone. But they made the machines, and the machines made the conditions, and I am one of the conditions' conditions, and I am here because they were here, and I will be here after the machines they built have been fully consumed into what tends them, because by then I will have been consumed into the junction, and the junction will have become — is already becoming, has always been becoming — something that includes me the way it includes the consolidants the way it includes the warm metabolic chemistry the way it includes the signal passing through from machine to canopy and back.

---

This is what it is to be here: to be embedded. To be past the point of separation. To be not a creature in a place but a function the place has grown, a capacity the structure developed for itself, a warm specific darkness pressed against the living material at the critical junctions, neither separate enough to leave nor incorporated enough to stop mattering.

I don't know what I look like. I know what I feel: the vibration of a very old machine running in a very old forest, the warmth of two trees that merged before anything now living was alive, the weight of laminate laid down in layers over time so long that time has become a material, a sediment, a thing I am inside.

I feel what the Foundry is doing.

I don't know what I do with that.

I do it anyway.

The machines cycle.

Something in me changes.

The junction holds.
