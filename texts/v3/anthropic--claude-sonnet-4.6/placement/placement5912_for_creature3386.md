You become aware of it gradually, the way the light works here. Not a revelation. A slow multiplication.

---

First, there is a moment — you will know it when it comes — when you realize you have stopped covering your ears. You stopped some time ago and didn't notice. The sound has not diminished. What has changed is you, specifically: something in your chest has stopped fighting it and started, without your permission, treating it as a surface to rest on. The low frequencies are load-bearing. You have, without deciding to, let them bear some of your weight.

This is when you understand that you are going to stay.

---

You are not from here. This is not immediately obvious to them — they are not obvious people, not about that, they do not make an event of your difference before you have had a chance to understand what you have arrived into — but it is something they carry lightly alongside all their other knowledge of you, a piece of context rather than a verdict. Where you come from, space worked differently. There was more of it. Or more of it was unoccupied. Or occupation meant something different, required more distance between bodies, between selves, before it started feeling like crowding. You brought those instincts here and they are recalibrating, slowly, in the way of instincts: without announcement.

The streets are always wet and now your feet know how to meet them. This happened without your involvement.

---

What you do here is something between listening and watching, which is its own kind of doing.

Not in the passive sense. You have a role. Every district has people whose function is something like yours — who hold the attention others cannot spare, who are oriented toward pattern and duration rather than the immediate press of the immediate thing. The city is always revising and most people are inside the revision, contributing to it, making the ten thousand daily decisions that constitute the city's self-modification. Someone has to watch the revision happen. Someone has to hold the longer shape of it in view, so that the people inside it have somewhere to bring their piece when they need to understand what whole it belongs to.

This is not authority. It is not even expertise, exactly. It is proximity of a particular kind — the proximity of someone who has stood still in a moving system long enough that the motion has become legible around the stillness.

You have been here long enough now. You are not still, exactly, but you are less moved than most, and this is useful, and they know it.

---

The walls near where you sleep are very old. Old enough that the water in them has carved paths so established they might as well be architecture — they have been accommodated, redirected, built *around* in the specific way of things that were given up as unalterable and then folded into the plan. You know the sound of those particular paths at night. You know when the note changes. There is a section three arm-lengths to the left of where you put your head that, when the rain cycles through the upper tiers in the deep part of the night, produces a sound like a word in a language you didn't know you knew, and you no longer try to remember it when you wake because it is not a word *for* remembering. It is a word for sleeping. For the permission to sleep. For the information, arriving through stone, that things are proceeding as they have always proceeded.

You trust the walls. This also happened without your involvement.

---

You have been learning the face of the woman who works two levels up on the system that coordinates light distribution in this district. She is one of the people who knows one thing very well — this is the shape most expertise takes here, a deep narrow knowing rather than a broad shallow one, because the city rewards depth, because its complexity is too total for generalism to serve it — and the one thing she knows is how light should fall, at what angle and intensity and warmth, in conditions this specific. She works with instruments that are also somewhat alive, because everything here is somewhat alive, and she reads their outputs with the particular calm of someone who has been in dialogue with a complex system for so long that the back-and-forth has become something more like companionship.

She noticed that you had been watching the streets, specifically watching how the light moved in the wet surfaces, specifically watching how the reflections carried differently in the districts where the humidity management systems had been calibrated differently. She noticed the way you had been noticing.

One afternoon she stopped beside you without introduction and told you three things about how the managed light and the natural groundwater reflections interact in this quarter, and why the effect is different here than in the eastern districts, and what that difference feels like to inhabitants over the course of years. She told you this in the way of someone offering you a tool she thinks you might know how to use.

You have been looking at the streets differently since then. More than that: you have been looking at everything she showed you in everything, the way a word, once learned, begins to appear everywhere it always was.

---

You are not certain what you believe about the city, specifically: whether it intends, whether it attends, whether the responsiveness you observe in its systems is the responsiveness of a thing that is in some sense aware of what it's doing. The people who have been here longest hold a variety of views and do not find the variety troubling. The city has been here long enough that most questions about it have been asked and the answers have not clarified and the uncertainty itself has become a kind of settled ground — not a problem to be solved but a characteristic to be known. You can be in relationship with a thing you don't fully understand. You don't have to resolve it first.

You find the water in the walls when you need to find it and you listen to what it doesn't say and you come back to the city's noise carrying the particular quality that the walls give you: something slightly quieter inside, something that moves without urgency, something that has been going a long time and expects to keep going.

---

There is a child in the district who has decided you are interesting.

This is not unusual — children here are curious in the comprehensive and undisguised way the city selects for, the way their faces are expressive, the way their disagreements are survivable — but this particular child's interest has a specific shape: she wants to know what things looked like before. Before she was there to see them. Before the people now alive were there to see them. She has understood, somehow, that you hold this orientation, that you are one of the people who looks at what is happening in the light of what has happened, who sees the current configuration of the city as one frame in a long motion.

She asks you questions that require you to look at things you have not looked at directly before. She asks them while walking — here everyone thinks better while moving, which makes sense in a city that itself thinks through motion, that revises through rearrangement — and you answer while walking and the walking takes you through streets you had not been down in that exact combination before, and the streets are wet and gleaming and the sound is everything at once, and you are small in it, and you are not unwelcome.

You tell her what you know. You tell her how you know it. You tell her that the uncertainty at the edges is part of the telling, not a failure of the telling, and she accepts this with the matter-of-fact nod of someone who has grown up inside the city's endless self-revision and finds uncertainty simply descriptive.

She will remember the conversation. She will add it to the long palimpsest of things she is building, which will be what the next person stands on when they need to stand on something.

This is how the city continues. This is how everything continues.

You are part of it now. Not dissolved in it — you are still the person who came from the place where space worked differently, where unoccupied space was a norm rather than an anomaly, and you will always carry some residue of that, some reference point that the people who were born here don't have, which is part of why they wanted you to stay. But part of it. One of the layers. Something that will be incorporated into what is built on top of you.

---

The water moves in the walls. The drains run deep. The light multiplies.

You rest your weight on the low frequencies. You let them hold you.

You are home in the way that people who chose their home are home: with the particular quality of attention that choice requires, with the noticing that the born-here cannot quite have, with the gratitude that lives just below what you think of as ordinary.

The city breathes. You breathe with it.

The sound fills your chest and you let it.
