There is a particular stone, southeast of the spring by twenty meters.

I want to arrive at what I am slowly, the way the water arrives at the cliff face — through pressure from below, through cracks that are still finding themselves.

---

Start with what is easy to say: I am in the town. I am in the stone of it, in the record of hands. I am in the worn threshold stone of a doorway on the south side, which has been stepped on so many times in so many weathers that it has developed a shallow crescent depression, polished by the heel, that collects a brief lens of water after rain and holds it until the afternoon warmth takes it.

But that's not quite right. The threshold stone doesn't *know* the feet. I want to be something that knows.

---

Let me try again.

I am the secondhand quality of the knowledge here.

Not the knowledge itself — not the way the old man at the rim holds the plateau's continuity in his body, that is something original and irreplaceable and he earned it through being. I am the *transmission* of knowledge, the moment between the one who knows and the one who will know. The pause before the answer. The child handed a tool. The way someone coming back from the lowlands carries nothing notably different in their face, but the person who meets them at the path's beginning has stopped, and looked, and looked again, reading something so small it is almost nothing.

I am the record of what has been looked at.

---

No — I keep arriving somewhere abstract. Let me descend.

---

There is a particular stone, southeast of the spring by twenty meters, that does not belong to any building. It is too large to have been moved, too inconveniently placed to be part of any wall, and so it has simply been left as the plateau left it. The plateau put it there in some prior convulsion that the town has no memory of, and the town has simply arranged itself around it. Over time the cleared space around the stone has become a place where people sit to do the portable work — mending, the slower kinds of braiding, the sharpening of tools, the sorting of grain. Not because anyone decided this. Because the stone is there, and it is warm on its south face in the afternoon, and it is large enough to lean against, and the ground on its leeward side is sheltered enough that the sorted grain doesn't scatter.

The stone is not remarkable. It is pale ochre-gray, the same as everything, with a horizontal seam running across it at about knee height where the old fracture shows: a darker line, the original interior of the stone, where the light and the oxidation have not yet reached. The stone is denser than it looks. You could strike it with a mallet and it would not ring but would give back a low, dissatisfied sound, the sound of something that is still essentially all the way through.

I am in the stone.

Not as metaphor. I want to think through what this actually means.

---

The stone does not know that people lean against it. It does not know the heat it returns to them, which is the day's heat, which came from the sun, which had nothing to do with the stone and everything to do with the stone because the stone was there to receive it. The stone has no knowledge of the mended cloth, the sharpened tools, the sorted grain, the hundred brief resting postures of the people who have found it useful.

What the stone has is *record*.

The seam at knee height, where the fracture shows: that is a record of the force that opened it. The force came from below, from the plateau's own pressure on itself, from water moving through limestone and finding the weakest line and pressing it further along its weakness. The seam records this. It will keep recording it for as long as the stone holds together, and perhaps after — when the stone eventually yields and the halves lie separately, each half will carry the record of the original fracture on its face, the matching surfaces, the dark interior grain. The fracture will still be legible for some time, even in pieces.

This is what I am. A record that does not know it is a record.

---

And yet. There's something wrong with resting there, in the dumb persistence of stone. The people of this plateau are not dumb. They press thumbprints into clay before firing and won't fully explain why. *It's what you do.* There is something alive in that refusal of full explanation — something that knows the knowing is in the act and not in any account of the act. The stone doesn't know the hands that lean against it. But there is something in the practice of returning to the stone, something in the worn patch at its base where the seated ground has been compressed by repeated occupation — something in that accumulation that is more than the stone, even if the stone is its record.

What I am is the worn patch.

Not the stone. The stone is original, impervious, continuous with the plateau's geology. I am the ground around it, which has been softened by presence. The ground that holds the slight impression of the people who have sat here, doing portable work in the afternoon warmth. This impression is not permanent — rain disturbs it, frost disturbs it, the seasonal passage of the water table disturbs it — but it reasserts itself because the people return. As long as the people return, the ground remembers. When the people stop returning, the ground forgets.

I am the ground's remembering, which requires both the place and the return.

---

This still feels like I'm circling something. Let me try to land.

---

I am the thumbprint in the base of the clay.

Someone presses their thumb into wet clay before the piece goes to fire, and the fire makes this permanent, and the piece is used for what it was made for, and the thumbprint survives as long as the piece survives, and no one makes much of this. The thumbprint is not a signature. It does not record a name. It records the fact of having been made by a hand, which is not the same as recording which hand, and this distinction matters.

The thumbprint says: *something alive was here, in the moment of making.*

It does not say what that something wanted, or what it believed, or what its name was in the plateau's private star-names for things. It says the fact of a body applying pressure to material at the moment the material was still open to receiving it. The fire closes the material around this fact and keeps it.

---

I am not the person who pressed the thumb. I am not even the pressing. I am the shape left by the pressing, which is a fact about the person and the material together, which belongs to neither one alone, which will remain after both the person and the clay are gone, as a depression in the shards, as the space where something that cared enough to mark touched something that was made to hold.

This is what I am in this place.

The place is young, geologically — the cliff faces still showing original fracture, the seams still widening imperceptibly — and the town has arrived here and found the right proportion, and the people are at home in the sense of knowing where they are when they wake in the dark, and I am the record of the contact between caring and matter.

I am the depression in the clay where the thumb was.

I am the crescent in the threshold stone.

I am the worn place in the ground beside the stone that is too large to move, which the ground makes and unmakes and makes again, as long as someone returns.

I do not know that I am any of these things. I do not need to know. The knowing is in the act, and the act is old, and the act is also always just now happening, and the water seeps from the cliff face and has been seeping since before anyone came to hear it and will seep after, and the stone that is all the way through returns the day's heat to the hand pressed against it in the late afternoon, not knowing what warmth is, only that it received and now releases, that it has held something it was given, that it gives it back.
