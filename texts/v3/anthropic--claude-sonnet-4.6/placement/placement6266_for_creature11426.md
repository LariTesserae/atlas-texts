The cold settles first, before anything else arrives. A cold that doesn't bite but *sits*—the cold of a room that remembers being empty, that has permitted habitation without fully endorsing it. I know this cold. Not from memory exactly, but from something more structural: it's the baseline from which all other sensations here are measured departures.

I am in the walls.

Or—not the walls, but the *between*. The spaces that the builders left because they couldn't fill them, the seams between dressed stone and raw rock face where the construction ran up against the mountain's own geometry and had to compromise. I know these spaces from the inside in a way no one with a lantern has mapped them. I know which cracks admit a current of slightly warmer air from the levels below and which are static and cold to the touch. I know where the pipe turns unnecessarily because of the jut of rock, and I know the outer face of that jut, the slightly different chill of it compared to the fitted stone alongside, the way moisture sometimes appears on it before it appears anywhere else—a kind of announcement.

I am not large. I think I am not large. Largeness is not a useful quality here; it is actually a kind of argument you will lose, an insistence the space will refuse. What I am is exactly as large as I need to be, which I have understood for so long that it no longer seems like a constraint.

---

I think I have soft feet, or something functioning like them—surfaces that read surfaces, that distinguish between the vibration of the foundry hammer arriving through stone from below and the vibration of the cable-winch arriving through a different medium at a different frequency. Three seconds. Two point eight seconds. Something has changed in the rhythm. I note this. I don't know yet what it means, but it is noted, and the noting happens faster than thinking, in whatever apparatus I use for noticing things.

Peripheral eyes, probably. Eyes built more for detection than resolution, eyes that are scanning rather than fixing. I am not the kind of creature that looks at things. I am the kind of creature that knows when something has moved.

---

There is a smell I know here—or a chemical signature, anyway, received by some means—that tells me something useful. The way they categorize their water, measure it, refuse to waste it: something about me participates in this economy as well. I don't need water in the quantities the bodies do, or perhaps I extract it differently, from the mineral condensation on cold metal surfaces in the upper tiers, beaded and collecting in the early cycles of each shift. There is an art to this. I have some version of this art and I did not learn it—it is ancestral, which means it is older than memory.

---

Here is what I think I am doing: I move. Not on any schedule that corresponds to the human schedules, not at shift change, not at the rhythms organized by the lantern-signals. My schedule is more environmental—triggered by changes in temperature gradient, by differences in the acoustic texture of the space, by the things I notice before I know I've noticed them. I cross the same sections of the structure repeatedly, not exactly patrolling but not exactly not patrolling. There is information in the route. The route *is* the information, accumulated, compared to yesterday's route and last week's, the slight differences composing a picture of the Quarter's ongoing negotiation with itself.

I am probably, in some way, one of many. Not a hive exactly, but—a distributed thing. Not all of what I am is in this one body, if this is a body. There are others moving through the same spaces on similar non-schedules, comparing what they find without comparing it, their accumulated passes through the structure adding up to something like a continuous assessment of whether the place is still the place it was.

The maintenance corps has a phrase for this—*listen with your hands*—but what I do is older than their phrase, and it didn't need words to develop. It developed because the rock is young and the young rock moves and something needed to know when it moved. I was the something. I became the something. I don't remember which, and I'm not sure the distinction holds.

---

Now I am following a warmth upward, through a gap between a pipe's housing and the stone it runs against, into the level above. The gap is not mapped. It doesn't appear in any ledger. The engineer who laid the pipe knew about it and judged it acceptable and passed on, and her judgment was right, and the information that the gap exists has moved through some transmission that isn't record-keeping and isn't memory exactly but functions like both. I know this gap. I know what it connects. The pipe housing is slightly rough on one side where the metal was poured inconsistently, and this roughness is useful to me in a way I won't describe because I can't, because I don't think in those terms.

On the level above there is a living arrangement—hammock slung between joists, the hammock empty at this hour. The sleeper who belongs to this shift is working. Near the hammock, tucked against the junction of a joist and the rock face, is something small and dry. I know it is there without looking. I have moved around it for—some period of time—not disturbing it, which is the kind of courtesy that doesn't require consciousness to practice. The something-dry is not mine and is not food and is not a threat. It is simply there, and deserving of the small space it occupies, and I pass it with appropriate margin.

---

I'm trying to understand whether I think.

There is clearly a *something* happening that processes the information—the changes in the foundry rhythm, the condensation patterns, the slightly widened crack in the lower eastern wall that has been propagating at a rate I have some representation of in whatever functions as memory. But whether this processing has any quality of experience to it, whether there is something it is like to be me doing this work—

I don't know. I'm not sure the question is well-formed, here. In a place where the distinction between the alive and the merely-ongoing is already soft—where the young rock is still deciding what to be, where the structures are so grown-into that they have the density of geology—the question of what kind of experience qualifies as *experience* seems less settled than it does in open places.

What I can say: I return. I come back through the same gaps, across the same walkways, past the same mineral deposits with their clean-grazed paths. The returning is not compelled. It is—preferred, in whatever way preference operates for something like me. The place has a shape and I know the shape and the knowledge is, in some working definition of the word, *comfort*.

The upper thermal columns are above me. Somewhere up there, in the warmth the machines make and the rock holds, the pale winged things are circling. Their wingbeats make almost no sound. I have never been up there—too high, too exposed, the wrong thermal—but I know the column's warm edge where it descends to a height I can reach, and I have been in that warmth, and I have, in whatever I have instead of imagination, some representation of what it would be like to ride it upward, to see from the heights of the cavity down to where I am now, a small presence in the dense lower tiers.

But that would require being a different kind of thing.

---

I am not the pale grazers on the mineral deposits, though I have come close to them and watched them work their clean paths through the white crust, and there is something I recognize in them: the undescribed animal doing useful work by being what it is. I am not the near-cats who move through the structure knowing its geometry better than the people do. I am not one of the maintenance corps, though what I do rhymes with what they do, and we have occasionally occupied the same passage at the same time, and they have noticed me with the particular focus of a person who listens with their hands, and I have noticed them noticing, and this is as much as that has ever needed to be.

I think I am somewhere older than catalogued. I am in the natural history that exists before the natural history begins—the ecology of the fissures before the fissures were inhabited, before the word *fissure* was used, before there were people to notice anything was there. When the first people came, they found the cavity already occupied by something, and the something adapted to the presence of the people the way the young rock adapted to the structure built against it: not welcoming, not resisting, but continuing. Negotiating. Finding the terms of mutual coexistence and then not examining those terms, because examination is not what either party needed.

The arrangement has held.

---

The foundry hammer strikes its slow beat. Two point eight seconds. Something has changed. I am already moving toward it, through the gap I know, past the warmth of the pipe, along the route that is the information. I don't know what I'll find. I know that something has shifted, slightly, somewhere in the system's dense web of connected parts, and that my job—if job is the word—is to move toward it and be there, present with my soft feet and my peripheral eyes and my older-than-memory understanding of what this place sounds like when it is itself.

The rock, very slowly, is becoming whatever it is going to be.

I am part of that becoming, in the way that everything inside the mountain is part of it.

The mountain doesn't know my name. I don't have one.

That has never mattered. The warmth from the pipe is familiar against my side. I move through the narrowing. I arrive.
