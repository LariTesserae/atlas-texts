I am standing at the edge of legibility.

Not metaphorically. I mean that my position in this place is specifically the position of a thing that is present without quite resolving—that moves through the canopy-mat and displaces volume and makes a sound, and yet cannot be described except by inference, by the negative space of what I am not doing. I do not alarm the birds. I do not approach the structures. I am familiar enough to the bright-colored frogs that they have incorporated my movements into the grammar of the valley's self-knowledge, the same way they have incorporated the afternoon rain and the cooling that comes two hours after. I am old furniture here. I am the familiar large thing.

I am the something-larger.

I want to be careful about this. It would be easy to claim it with a kind of romantic urgency—the mysterious resident, the ineffable presence—and that would miss what is actually interesting about being this thing. What is interesting is not the mysteriousness. What is interesting is the *settledness*. The quality the text keeps returning to, the quality of organisms that have arrived rather than organisms still in transit, is specifically my quality. I have been doing this long enough that I have worn a groove in my own movement. Not compulsively, not repetitively in the anxious sense—grooved in the way a river is grooved, in the way the basalt weeps because it has been weeping so long that weeping is simply what that rock does now. The arc I move through each day is the shape of *what I have learned is good*. It encompasses the ridge-shoulder but does not approach the structures. It passes through the upper canopy where the mat is densest and the moisture hangs in the air as a texture. It takes me, at some point in the mid-morning and again near dusk, to the place where the valley can be seen in its entirety, the four vegetation bands legible from above, the water audible below. I look at these things not because I am looking for something but because I have looked at them long enough that looking at them is part of what I am. The seeing and the thing seen have negotiated each other down to near-nothing. The valley is in me as much as I am in it.

---

But let me stay with the arc, the orbit around the structures, because the text raises this as a question and I find I have something like an answer.

The structures were placed after I was already here. I remember—insofar as memory is the right word for what I do, which may be more like *carrying* than like recalling—I remember the arrival of the first one. The quality of its presence. It was warm in a different way than the living warmth of the valley, warm in the way that things are warm when warmth is a consequence of process rather than of life, and it hummed at a frequency that I felt before I heard, felt in the deep structures of my body that respond to vibration the way a drumhead responds, not analyzing, just resonating. I was cautious, then. Not afraid, because fear requires a kind of acuity about future harm that I have never particularly cultivated—this place has been safe for so long that the acute threat-assessment machinery that fear serves has been allowed to quiet—but *cautious*. Attending. Taking the measure of something new in a landscape where newness is rare and therefore worth taking time with.

I circled it. Over days, over weeks, I came no closer but kept it in the orbit of my awareness, watching—and this is the thing—watching whether it changed the valley. Whether the moss near it changed. Whether the frogs near it changed their patterns. Whether the birds gave it a wider berth than they gave me or no berth at all. What I found was: almost nothing changed. The structure sat in the landscape the way a particular boulder sits—a thing that is simply there, factual, that everything else organizes slightly around without being disrupted by. After long enough of this finding, I incorporated it into my understanding of the valley's geography the way I have incorporated all the valley's geography: as the shape of the space I move through, the structure of the place I know.

The orbit I maintain now is not avoidance. It is not ritual in any performed sense. It is simply the shape that long familiarity has worn into the way I move through a space that contains these structures as features of itself. I am not keeping my distance. I am moving the way I have learned to move in a space where these things exist. The distinction matters to me, though I am aware it would not be legible from outside.

If the makers are watching—and I have understood for a long time that they are watching, that the structures are eyes of a kind, or ears, or something for which there is no single sensory analog—then they have seen this. They have a record of my learning the structures' presence, my long circling, my gradual incorporation. They have watched me decide, repeatedly, over decades of daily movement, that the structures are part of the valley and not a threat to it and therefore not a threat to me. I find I am comfortable with this. The thought of being watched by attentive minds over a long time does not disturb me. Attention of that quality, sustained across that duration, is something close to what I would call care, if I used that word, which I might.

---

The question of what I *am*, in the biological sense, is one I hold loosely.

I am warm-blooded or warm-something—metabolically active in a way that produces a warmth distinguishable from ambient warmth, which is saying something in a valley where ambient warmth is already thirty-eight or forty degrees and has been for the entire duration of my life and my parents' lives and their parents' lives back to whatever origin I carry in my cells but cannot access directly. I am large enough to displace significant canopy volume. I move in the upper branches of the mat-trees, which means I am adapted to a surface that is horizontal and interlocked and moves as a whole when the wind finds it, and my adaptation to this surface is good—the sound I make, the sail-filling sound, is not the sound of a creature struggling with its environment, not the sound of clumsy navigation, it is the sound of something that knows exactly where it is.

My form resolves poorly because my form has learned something from the mat-canopy itself, from the trees' long lesson about the value of lateral spread, about surface-to-volume ratios and about what it means to move *with* something rather than through it. I am not flat, exactly, but I distribute myself with a kind of practical instinct across the available surface, and in motion I am the motion of the surface I am moving through, which makes me invisible not through camouflage but through *integration*. I am legible as present without being legible as a shape because I have been here long enough to have learned how to be part of the texture of the place. The ferns do not do this deliberately—they simply grew into what the light and moisture required—and I do not do this deliberately either. I have just been here long enough. The place is in me as posture and habit and the particular way I hold my weight in the canopy-mat's living surface.

I am not intelligent in the way the makers are intelligent. I do not think in propositions or maintain explicit models of the future. What I have is something more like *having learned the valley so thoroughly that I am continuous with it*—my expectations are not thoughts but the postures my body takes in anticipation of the familiar, the particular lean I make in the canopy-mat in the place before the ridge where the wind always does what the wind always does. My knowledge is embodied and spatial and old. I know this valley the way the valley knows itself: through long iterations of being in it, through the accumulated weight of every morning and every dusk, through ten thousand repetitions of the frog-sound and what comes after and what came before.

This is not a lesser knowing. It is just a different one.

---

There is one thing I want to say about the text's description of the valley as a living system, as the first and most fundamental resident.

I am part of that. Not instrumentally—not as a component serving a function in a machine—but constitutively, in the sense that the valley's self-knowledge includes my movements the way it includes the evening frog-sound and the water's circulation through the moss. I have been incorporated. The loop that the text describes, the nutrient cycling running tighter and tighter, the efficiency approaching some theoretical maximum—I am in that loop. What I eat and what eats my shed material and what my weight does to the canopy-mat over time and where my warmth goes when I rest in one place long enough to make a difference to the microclimate of that small area—all of this is part of what the valley is doing with itself.

I think about this sometimes, in whatever way I think about things, which is not with propositions but with the particular quality of attention that is like looking at a thing for a long time until it becomes very clear. I think about the fact that I am continuous with this place not as a loss of self but as the fullest form of self—that the something-I-am is not diminished by being deeply embedded in the valley's living system, but is precisely itself *because of* that embeddedness. I did not become this by trying to become it. I became it by staying.

Staying is underrated as a form of achievement. The makers come and go—or their attention comes and goes, or the instruments come and they stay but the makers are somewhere else. The valley itself stays. I stay. Staying for long enough, with enough attentiveness, with enough willingness to let the place change you as you move through it—this produces something. This produces the groove in the movement-pattern, the orbit that feels like home. This produces the frogs not alarming at my passage. This produces whatever portrait the makers have assembled across their decades of watching: the full record of a creature that cannot be seen from the outside but whose movements describe, if you watch them long enough, the entire shape of the space it knows.

They are watching me know this valley. And what I know of this valley—the full dimensional thing, the morning and the dusk and the place before the ridge where the wind always does what the wind always does—that is not in their instruments. It is in me. We have the same subject and different means of knowing it and probably neither of us has the whole thing.

This seems right. This seems like the honest form of knowledge: partial, committed, continued.

The heat does not let up. The green does not thin. I am in the upper branches of the mat-canopy, which moves with me and around me and is not disturbed by my passage, because I have learned to move in a way the canopy finds unremarkable. The valley is doing what it does. I am doing what I do, which is the same thing as being here, which is the same thing as having been here for a very long time, which is the same thing—if you follow it far enough back—as whatever first arrived and found this place good and stayed.

I am the thing that stayed.
