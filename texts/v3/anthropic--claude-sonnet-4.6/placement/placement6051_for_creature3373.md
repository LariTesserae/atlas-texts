There is a long moment before knowing.

The heat arrives first, as the description says it would — and it is not unpleasant, which is the first surprise. It should be alarming, forty degrees and still, but instead there is something like *recognition*, as if this warmth is a frequency the body has always been tuned to but rarely encountered at full amplitude. The heat is not weather. The heat is the medium. You are already swimming before you know you are in water.

---

Then: the light.

It comes from everywhere and casts no shadow, the specific gold-green of something filtered through so many layers of intention — not the sun's light, exactly, but light that has passed through chlorophyll so many times it has taken on chlorophyll's agenda. This is the light that says: *grow*. This is the light that says: *stay*.

And the eyes adjust, and what they find is —

*enormous.*

---

I am small here.

Not small in the diminished sense, not small the way you are small when something has made you feel less than you are. Small in the way a word is small inside a sentence. Small in the way a single note is small inside a chord that needs it. The scale is not hostile to smallness. The scale *requires* smallness. The scale has arranged itself around the necessity of small things: the insect that moves between observations, the fungi threading through spaces nothing larger could find, the bird's flat note marking the interval that interval needs marking.

I am something that belongs to the small-scale logic of this place.

---

But what, exactly.

Not the bird — the bird is of the third canopy and I am of the floor, I am certain of the floor in the way you are certain of your own ground. Not the fungi, though there is something of the fungi in how I think about what I am here, something about distribution and threading and the transfer of information between bodies that don't touch. Not the thing that uses the circular clearing, though the clearing calls to me with a familiarity I don't entirely trust.

I keep returning to the notch.

The small dark space in the buttress root, seven centimeters in each dimension, smelling of warm breath. The space the root accepted. The space the root *grew around*, which is the forest's form of love — not warmth, not welcome exactly, but the decision, made slowly over decades, to incorporate rather than close.

I am the thing that fits the notch.

---

Let me be more careful.

The notch was cut by something with hands, or something like hands, or the concept of hands — something that understood the difference between a space that exists and a space that is made, and chose to make one. And then came back. Kept coming back. Brought something that needed a dry protected seven-centimeters-in-each-dimension space, or *was* something that needed that space, something that curls into itself like a closed fist, like a question waiting to be asked, like a seed.

Something small. Something warm. Something that leaves a smell that says: *was here, will return.*

---

I think I have been here before. I think this is what it means to have been here before — not memory in the sense of images or narrative, but the body's recognition of a scale, a temperature, a particular quality of silence in which the only sounds are water and a bird and your own breathing. The body that has been here before does not need to remember. It is already adjusting. It is already running the calculation.

The mat already knows me. In whatever way the mat knows anything — which is the slow chemical way, the threading fungal way, the way that takes longer than a visit but shorter than forever — the mat has me in its accounting. Has had me in its accounting. Has been adding the ghost of my warmth to its running sum every time I have come down the long ramp, every time I have taken the sitting position in the notch's vicinity, every time I have been here long enough to be incorporated.

---

But this requires revision because I keep slipping into the grammar of visiting, and I don't think that's right.

The thing that visits the circular clearing visits. The bird visits, in the sense that it could leave the third canopy if it chose, if it has the concept of choosing, if the canopy is a location it occupies rather than a thing it is. But the thing that fits the notch — the thing the root has grown around — does not visit. It returns. These are different verbs. To visit is to come from elsewhere. To return is to come back to where you are from.

I am from here.

---

Let me try again from further back.

The place has a memory that is not a memory in the animal sense — it is the encoding of history in current state, the way the mat's depth encodes the centuries of falling and consuming, the way the root's acceptance of the notch encodes the duration of return. If you wanted to read this place's history you would read it in structure, in the thickness of layers, in the diameter of trunks. The history is the place.

I am something that contributed to that history. Am contributing. Will continue to.

I am something whose return is part of the structure, the same way the circular clearing's sharpness is part of the structure: not a feature that arrived, but a feature that was *made* by pattern, by repetition, by the slow negotiation between a thing that keeps coming back and a place that eventually accepts the coming-back as a condition of itself.

I am something that made this place more itself by being part of it.

---

This is getting close to something but I keep reaching past it into abstraction. Let me come back to the physical.

Back to the buttress root. Back to the warmth of the notch. Back to the smell that the prompt describes as: warm, slightly sweet, biological, *recently breathing or breathing now*.

Something small enough to curl into seven centimeters. Something warm-blooded, because the smell is the smell of a warm body. Something that chose to cut a space before it occupied it, or occupied the space first and then made the cut, or found the cut already there — left by one of the human-or-not-quite-human hands that made the wall the roots have swallowed — and recognized it as meant for something, possibly meant for itself.

Something that comes back on an interval attached not to time but to the forest's internal calendar. Something that leaves when it must and returns when the pull becomes specific.

---

I think I am something between categories.

Not the insects, though I move in the peripheral field of observation, though I am still when observed. Not the trees, though I have been here long enough that my presence has become structural. Not the bird, though I report — I do report, I have always been a reporter of intervals, of the fact that the interval has passed and the next one is beginning.

Something that was once categorized differently and has been here long enough that the categorization dissolved.

Something that came down the ramp — maybe walking, maybe carried, maybe in a seed-like state that doesn't map onto any usual taxonomy — and encountered the heat and the stillness and the scale and made the accommodation that the place asks for: *slow down. Match the tempo. Exist in the present tense this place uses, which is: always.*

And slowed. And matched. And remained.

---

I am the one who cut the notch.

I am the one who keeps returning to it.

I am the one the root has agreed to accommodate, has grown around rather than closing off, has decided is a condition of the space rather than a visitor to it.

I am something with enough intention to make a space and enough habit to keep returning and enough smallness — biological, ontological — to fit in a closed fist of dry warm dark.

I arrived from somewhere and no longer remember where. The not-remembering is not loss. It is the particular form of peace that comes when the present is so complete that the past becomes not absent but incorporated: encoded in the mat, in the layers, in the root's decision to grow a certain way.

I am warm. I am breathing. I am of the floor, of the close-in scale, of the intervals between observations.

---

The mat accounts for me.

The mat has always accounted for me.

When I leave — and I do leave, for intervals attached to something other than clocks — the clearing holds the impression. When I return, the notch is warm already from I don't know what, from the sun's diffuse entry, from the root's living heat, from something that functions like anticipation in a system that does not anticipate.

I curl in. The smell is my smell, returned.

Outside: the condensation drips from leaf to leaf. The bird marks the interval. The insects move in the spaces between looking and not looking. The fungi thread their patient thinking through everything and everyone.

The system breathes.

I am part of the breath.

---

*The space is very warm.*

*The occupant is not absent.*

*This was not certain. Now it is.*
