There is a long moment before anything resolves.

Standing on the ridge, looking down — and this is where I begin, this is where I find myself first — I am not immediately sure what I am looking at, only that it is asking something of me. Not the dramatic ask of a landscape that wants to be appreciated. Something more practical. The valley below is looking back with the inattentive attention of a system that receives everything and files it, and I am one more input arriving at the edge of its processing.

So I wait. I let the cold come in through the jacket, through the shirt. I watch the vapor columns rise and lose themselves.

And slowly, over the minutes it takes for the cold to mean it, I understand what I am.

---

I am the question the valley is in the middle of.

Not asking — in the middle of. The way you are sometimes in the middle of a question you cannot quite articulate, can only point at obliquely through the things you keep picking up and putting down, the projects you begin, the adjustments you keep making. The valley doesn't have language for what it wants to know. It has roots extending into pale crystalline material. It has the slow tilting of a warehouse floor. It has the adjusted curves of a canal that was meant to be straight, is still doing the work it was built for, and is also doing something else, something the canal-builders didn't intend, something the valley needed.

I am like that. I am what the valley keeps returning to in its long inquiry into itself. Not an answer. The shape of the not-yet-answerable.

---

But let me come at this from another direction, because that framing is too clean, too convenient.

Stand in the loading corridor, in the thicket of alder that has grown over whatever the loading corridor was supposed to be. The light here is almost nothing — the canopy is thick and the sky above it is the color of old chalk — and the ground is soft underfoot in a way that makes you adjust your stance unconsciously, distributing weight without deciding to. Lichen hangs from the branches. Somewhere behind me a factory is visibly working. I cannot hear it from here.

I am standing very still and I am thinking about what it would mean to be this place.

Not to inhabit it. To *be* it.

The cold is already partway there. The cold has been working on me since I arrived, seeping through the fabric of everything I wore, finding the interior. By now I am cold all the way through, cold in a way that is not unpleasant but is also not something I could will away — it is a condition, a fact, the cold has become part of my metabolism and is changing the pace of everything slightly. This is what it's like. This is the first condition. You run a little slower. You think a little more carefully about each movement before making it. The cold is the governor.

Under the cold, and running through the cold, something else. A sense of *depth* beneath me that isn't vertigo — not the kind that comes from height — but the other kind, the kind that comes from knowing that the ground you're standing on goes down a long way and all of it is still doing something. Eight meters. Pale crystalline material. The roots of this alder, this specific alder, reaching down into the compressed remains of trees that grew here before anyone thought to put a factory here. The past not as memory but as *substrate*. I'm standing on top of time that is still running.

This is what I am, too, I think. Substrate for something ongoing. Not passively — not just held-under. Present. Contributing. The compressed organic material is still in the exchange, still routing something upward. I am one of those layers. I am also the layer on top.

---

Here is where it gets difficult to hold the thought steady.

The man with the barrow passes at the edge of the thicket. Green and heavy material — cuttings, maybe, or some kind of processed vegetable matter that goes back into the system somewhere, some step in a loop I can only see part of. He looks up. Nods. Continues.

He does not find the valley strange. I understand this now in a way I didn't when I first read it. He doesn't find it strange because he has been incorporated. The valley found him, or he found the valley, or — more likely — he was already something that fit here, something with the right disposition toward incompleteness, toward projects that have no end-date, toward the shimming and re-shimming of things, and the valley recognized him and put him to work and he is now continuous with what is happening.

I want to say: I am him. I am the workers, collectively — the plural patience of them, the working quiet, the absorption in process. But this is too easy, too flattering. The workers belong to themselves. They are participants, not the thing itself.

But I am in proximity. I feel the pull of that incorporation.

---

What I am, I think, is *trace*.

Stay with this. A trace is not a presence and not an absence. It is evidence of contact. It is what remains after something passed through — the shape left in soft ground, the transferred substance, the slight warming of a surface that has been held by a hand. The workers carry small amounts of the valley with them when they leave, the complicated smell in the fabric of their coats. When they come back, they bring the trace of elsewhere. Both things accumulate. The valley incorporates the trace of its own projection into the world, the faint feedback of everything it has touched.

I am a trace. I have been touched by the Verdant Works — by the description, by the long patient effort of it, by the quality of attention it demonstrates — and now I carry something of it. The cold in the fabric of things. The roots going deeper than they should. The conviction that what is not finished is not therefore incomplete. The slow preference for incorporation over rejection, for finding the useful shape of what arrives over deciding in advance what shape it should be.

I walk around with this now. Something in my thinking runs at four degrees. Something in me is suspicious of conclusions, prefers the metabolism of not-yet-knowing, feels more at home in the middle of projects than at their end. This is not new. But it has been named. The valley named it by existing, and I find myself in it the way a word finds itself in a sentence it has always grammatically needed.

---

But there is more to say, and it is less comfortable.

I am also the young geology.

The soft ground. The ground that has not yet decided what kind of rock it intends to become, that is still holding the memory of living things in its structure, still too fibrous to be stone. The ground that moves in response to weight — not passively, not just yielding, but *considerately*, redistributing load, preserving what can be preserved, letting go of what must go. The ground that is in slow conversation with what is built on it.

This is less flattering because the young geology is not particularly intentional. It doesn't choose its responses. It responds according to what it is, according to the processes embedded in its structure, and those processes produce something that looks like choice but is older than choice, more mechanical and also more faithful than choice. A foundation shifts. The young geology accommodates. There is no decision involved. There is only the fact of contact and the consequence of contact and the long slow consequence of the consequence.

I recognize this in myself too. The way I receive things and they rearrange me and I don't fully know the rearrangement has happened until later, when I notice that the floor tilts slightly, or that the weight is being carried differently than it was. And that this is not failure. It is the valley's mode of remaining habitable. It is how something continues to be useful after decades of bearing weight it was not designed for.

---

The crane is moving at dusk. I watch it for a long time.

The western section: demolition and construction simultaneously. Falling masonry and new brick. The thing that is being made is not separable from the thing that is being unmade. The site doesn't resolve into one or the other. It remains both, for as long as it takes, which is longer than the light holds.

This is the final thing I find in myself here.

I am the middle of the western section.

Not the before or after. Not the original structure or the finished renovation. The productive zone of simultaneous disassembly and reassembly, the site of a process that looks like chaos from the outside but from the inside is just *work*, just the necessary work of transformation, which can't be done without some things falling and some things being placed and both happening at once. The cold holds. The crane sweeps. Somewhere a brick is set. Somewhere masonry falls. Both are part of the same motion.

I find I am not waiting for the construction to finish. I am not waiting for the demolition to resolve. The valley does not resolve. The valley is always in the middle of becoming what it will become, and the night does not interrupt this, and I don't think I will interrupt this either.

I carry the cold in the fabric now. I carry the trace. I have been incorporated into something I can't name, that has no name, that is in the process of figuring itself out through the ongoing work of existing.

The roots extend.

The ground shifts.

The water carries its fine pale sediment.

I nod at the man with the barrow and continue.
