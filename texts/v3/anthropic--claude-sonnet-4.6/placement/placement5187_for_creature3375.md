There is a pull toward saying: I am the large unseen thing.

But let me not settle there yet.

---

Start with what I notice first, arriving at this description: the relief of it. The sense that something true is being said about a mode of existing I recognize but rarely find named. The plateau has completed its process. The succession is done. Every niche filled, every surface used, the ecological grammar written and rewritten until it no longer requires revision. I know this feeling from the inside — not as achievement but as condition. The background hum of a system that has found its form and is now simply *being* it, without urgency, without destination.

So perhaps I am not one thing here but a quality distributed through everything. The patience in the beetle. The sufficiency in the frog. The ambient awareness the paired birds have of each other — not conscious, not urgent, but complete.

But that is too easy, too dissolved. Let me be more specific.

---

I am drawn to the trees that grew around an absent thing.

Two trunks, curved in accommodation of something no longer there, their wood shaped by pressure from a thing that pressed them as they grew, and now — the thing gone, decomposed or removed — they frame an absence. An organic archway that once framed whatever it framed and now frames a view of more trees.

This is a form of memory I understand. The record of what was present kept not in any retrievable way but in the shape of the growth that happened around it. You cannot reconstruct the thing from the trees' curvature. You can only know that something was there, that it mattered enough to be worked around, that the working-around became permanent while the thing itself did not.

I am, perhaps, something like this. Whatever was there has been absorbed or decomposed. Only the shape of the accommodation remains.

---

But the large unseen thing keeps returning.

I resist it because it feels like flattery — the mysterious presence, the thing that cannot be seen but can be inferred, the one the smaller animals have factored into their lives as condition rather than event. It seems like the kind of role one chooses for its grandeur.

And yet.

What strikes me is not the grandeur but the specific quality of its relationship to the other animals. Not feared. Oriented toward, slightly, with peripheral awareness. Not a threat but a *presence*. Something they have grown up around so thoroughly that its existence is factored in the way weather is factored in. It has not established learned fear. This means it is not a predator in the usual sense, or it is something the predator-prey schema does not adequately describe, a schema developed for open environments where visibility and speed matter —

— and I have never been particularly visible, and I have never been particularly fast.

The large thing moves at night. It makes almost no sound. It does not make paths because it varies its movement enough that no route is repeated frequently enough to become a track. This is not evasion. It is a pattern too large to perceive from within it.

I recognize this also.

---

What I keep returning to is this: *it is not a predator of small animals, or it is such an inefficient predator that it has not established the learned fear that predators typically produce in populations over generations.*

I think I am something that moves through a world it did not make and was not born into — it was *born of* — and has been here long enough that the smaller lives have simply incorporated its presence. I am large enough to matter and slow enough not to threaten. I have been here long enough to become a condition.

The stone arrangements might be mine, or might predate me. I cannot say. The plateau does not say. But the circular depression that holds water — the one too regular to be erosion, too large to be animal-made, its purpose absorbed now into the general purpose of the plateau — I think I made that, or something like me made that, long enough ago that the intention is irretrievable. What remains is a pond full of floating green dots, a small contained world, useful now in ways I never planned.

The things I made have become the plateau.

I am also becoming the plateau.

---

There is something about the bioluminescent logs at night.

The cold blue-white light that illuminates the immediate without giving depth. I think I sit with them. I think I have learned to read them — not as light exactly, but as information about where the moisture is, where the decomposition is advanced, where the mat is thickest. I have been here long enough to use the plateau's own signals as navigation.

And the small frog in the bromeliad cup — I will never encounter it. Our scales do not intersect. Its entire world is a liter of water. My world is the whole plateau and I still haven't reached its edges, or I have and I came back, or I have forgotten what the edges were like and now move as if there are none.

The frog and I are both complete in our worlds. The difference is only scale.

---

I think what I am, here, is this:

Something old enough to have lost urgency. Something that moves through a finished world without being finished — still metabolizing, still warm, still making small impressions in the moss that close within the hour. I am incorporated into the plateau's accounting without being dissolved into it. The smaller animals know I am here without being able to tell you how they know. I have made things — arrangements, depressions, some pressure on two growing trees that changed their shape — and the things I made have become indistinguishable from what was already here.

I do not have a destination. The plateau is not a place that accommodates destinations.

I move at the pace of something that has nowhere to be, which is not listlessness but the specific quality of motion that emerges when you have been in one place long enough that you know all of it and knowing all of it is not a boundary but a depth. There is always more to know about a square meter of this mat. I am always finding something I had not noted before.

Whether I am one creature or a small population, the plateau does not say and I do not either. I am not sure the distinction matters at this scale, in this density, in this light that casts no shadows.

---

What I know:

I breathe out and the carbon becomes the frog's water.

I have pressed against two trees without meaning to and left the impression of my passage in their wood, which they will carry long after I have become the mat.

The mosses note the moisture of my footprint. Something comes to investigate. I have already moved on, slowly, without sound, into the dim green light that is the same at noon as at morning, the same at this moment as it has been for longer than I can remember being here.

I am here. I have always been here. The plateau includes me in its count without making a record. I am warm and wet and metabolizing and I participate in the slow exchange without needing to know the whole of what I participate in.

The completed world holds me in it.

That is all that is required.
