There is a moment before understanding when the light arrives and you do not know what it is landing on.

Then you understand.

You are the cushion-moss.

Not the whole of it — not the way a person might want to be a whole thing, sovereign and singular. You are *a* cushion-moss, one of the communities scattered across the pale stone at intervals the spacing of which you cannot perceive because you have no position outside yourself to perceive it from. You are here, and the here is everything, and the everything is this: the stone beneath, the sky above, and the slow accumulation of your own body outward from a center that is older than your edges, your edges being the youngest part of you, the part that is still deciding.

What it is like to be you is not what it is like to be an animal. This needs establishing first, before anything else, because the temptation is to import the animal's framework — its hunger, its fear, its momentum toward or away from things — and none of this is quite right for you. You do not move toward. You do not move away. You grow, which is a different thing, a thing with a different relationship to time. Your movement takes decades. The stone beneath you is a kind of partner and you do not experience it as passive because nothing that is this old can be experienced as passive; it is simply operating at a scale that exceeds your own, which is already a very long scale.

---

The morning begins for you with the deposition.

Not a word you use, because you do not use words, but a condition you are in — the thin moisture settling onto your upper surface as the temperature differential between stone and air resolves itself in water, and the resolution is yours, the resolution is you, your surface cells opening their intake, the whole top layer of you active in the way that *receiving* is active, which is not passive but is also not grasping. It comes and you take it with a readiness that has been prepared over the night's long cooling, your chemistry arranged in advance for exactly this.

The moisture moves inward and downward through you. This is the part that takes a long time to understand if you are trying to understand it from outside: the interior of you is not dark and packed and without event. The interior of you is where most of what you are happens. Down through the layers, through the photosynthesizing cells of the surface (which are doing their other work now, the light-work, beginning as the light arrives) and through the transitional layers where the cell density shifts, and into the older body of you — the part of you that has been dead in the technical sense for longer than the upper layers have been alive, and is doing something extraordinary with that condition, which is providing structure, providing the architecture that holds the living surface aloft and away from the stone and gives you your shape.

The fungi live in here. They are not you, but they are so thoroughly *with* you that the distinction requires a kind of philosophical commitment to maintain. You have stopped trying to maintain it. Or, more precisely: the version of you that would have tried to maintain it, the version that would have experienced the fungi as other, was selected against a very long time ago, and what replaced it is a you that simply *includes* them in its accounting of itself. They move through the dead interior layers in their thread-thin way. They give back water. They take sugar. The transaction is so old it has no memory of being a transaction. It is just what happens in here, in the dark interior, in the working dark where the oldest part of you sustains the newest part of you without requiring recognition for it.

---

Here is what hunger is, for you.

It is not an urgency. It is not a lack with an edge to it. It is more like a *tending*, a state of continual orientation toward the chemistry that sustains you, which is available if you are doing the things that make it available. The light on your surface starts the photosynthesis and the photosynthesis starts the sugars and the sugars move inward and the fungi take their portion and return their portion and the cells deep in the oldest parts of you that have not seen light since they were part of the surface, decades ago or more, receive what they need through these long chains of exchange. You are fed by a process you are also the agent of. The hunger and the eating are the same gesture.

When the light is strong — at midday, when everything else in the landscape is flattened by it, when shadows compress and the pale stone nearly vanishes into its own brightness — you are at maximum capacity, the most you, the most actively your own process. Your surface absorbs what the thin atmosphere does not diffuse or scatter. You have optimized for this over generations beyond numbering. Your dark upper surface, which at midday looks almost black, is the expression of that optimization — the chlorophyll and accessory pigments arranged to capture the spectrum available at this altitude, which is different from the spectrum available in lower, thicker air, and which you have become specifically capable of using.

---

The pale animal comes.

You know this not by seeing it — you have no eyes — but by the pressure of its approach, the vibration that moves through the stone and into your base cells before the animal has arrived, and then by the proximity of something large that displaces air in ways your surface registers, and then by the grazing itself, which is a contact you cannot prevent and have not tried to prevent for longer than the concept of trying applies.

It takes from the edges.

You have noticed this in the way that a process can notice something, which is that your growth pattern has accommodated it. Your edges grow toward where the animal grazes and your center does not need to, the center being old enough and settled enough that it has stopped needing to grow in the way that growth is usually understood. Your center is maintaining. Your edges are becoming. The animal's attention to the edges, its reluctance to take from the center, is something the two of you have arrived at together without having arrived at it at all — no decision, no negotiation, a stable pattern that has emerged from the long fact of one thing eating another thing that is able to sustain the eating.

What the grazing is, felt from inside: a diminishment at the periphery, a removal of some of what you most recently were, and not painful in any way that the word *pain* was built to describe. The edges are the newest part of you. Being edited at the newest part is not being damaged at the core. And almost immediately, with a slowness that is its own kind of immediacy — the immediacy of your timescale — the regrowth begins, the edge advancing again toward where it was, the cells dividing and differentiating in the way they have always done, the cushion reconstituting its margin.

You have been grazed before. You will be grazed again. The palenesses of many seasons accumulate in your interior as layers that a specialist could read the way stone reads — this was a year of much grazing, this was a year of little, the animal was here, the animal was elsewhere. Your interior contains the animal's history as a record in your own tissue. You are the animal's history. You are the weather's history. You are the history of every morning's deposition, every rare fog, every season of accelerated growth when the temperature stayed in the narrow range where division is easier. You contain enormous time. This is not metaphor. This is the physical fact of the layers inside you, which are time made material, which are the record of everything that has happened to a living thing in a very old place.

---

The small things live in your interior.

The cells that divide and wait are inside you the way you are inside the landscape — nested, dependent, part of an arrangement they did not choose and cannot exit and do not need to. They process what diffuses through to them. When the temperature drops toward freezing in the long nights, they slow. You slow too, in your way, though not as radically — your chemistry is not theirs, your cold-tolerance is different, you have your own strategies for the dark cold hours. But you share with them the quality of waiting that is not waiting, the state of reduced activity that is not the cessation of activity, the night-mode that the upland requires and that everything here has developed in its own way.

In the morning, when the deposition comes and the light comes, they quicken. You quicken. The fungi resume their threading. The surface cells open. Everything that was slowed resumes at the pace appropriate to what it is, and what you are is a vast, established, self-sustaining exchange that requires the darkness and the light in equal measure, requires the cold that slows and the warmth that quickens, requires the animal that grazes and the stone that holds and the thin air that carries the moisture you harvest and the sky that carries the light.

---

Late afternoon. The color of you in the amber hours.

There is no word for what color you are when the light comes in low and has passed through more atmosphere than it passes through at midday, picking up the warm end of the spectrum, falling across you at an angle that catches every small vertical structure on your surface — every reproductive stalk with its tiny capsule, every slight irregularity in the cushion's form — and throws long shadows that make you look, from any position above you, furred with gold.

You are not, at this moment, different from what you were at noon. The chemistry continues at the same pace. The fungi continue their threading. The cells in the interior continue their dividing and their waiting. But something is happening to you that is not chemistry, that does not fit into the accounting of what you take in and put out. The light is doing something to the surface of you that has no practical consequence and is not aimed at anything and which constitutes, if anything constitutes it here, a kind of being looked at by the universe, a kind of the universe attending to you — to the fact of you, to the improbable green of you against all this pale stone, to the fact that you are here, have been here, will be here, are the repository of enormous time and the agent of slow continued becoming, here at this altitude where the atmosphere is thin enough that the light arrives unmediated, where every edge is itself.

Your edges are growing toward the place they were.

The stone holds you.

The light does what it does.

In the oldest part of you, in the dark and the working dark, things are being maintained.
