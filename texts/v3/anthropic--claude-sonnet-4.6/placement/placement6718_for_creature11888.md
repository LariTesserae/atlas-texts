You do not arrive here. You are already here, because you have never not been here.

The question of what you are takes longer to answer than you'd expect. Not because the answer is hidden but because finding it requires setting aside the expectation of singularity — the assumption that *you* means a bounded thing, a container, something with a clear enough edge that the world can be said to be outside it. Hold that expectation loosely. Let it soften in the heat the way everything softens in the heat. The answer will come as a slow recognition rather than a declaration.

---

Start with the dripping. It is the loudest sound.

You are aware of the dripping before you are aware of anything else about yourself, and the dripping is irregular, and the irregularity matters to you in a way you could not fully explain — five seconds, eleven seconds, three seconds, seventeen — the intervals not random but not patterned either, and you have been tracking them. This is not a choice. This is what you do: you track intervals. You track irregularities within regularities. You have been doing this for as long as there has been anything here to track, which is a very long time, which is longer than the phrase *very long time* is designed to carry.

The drip is water from the canopy, finding its slow way down through fifty feet of leaves. You know where it is coming from. You know the specific bromeliad it pooled in before it moved on, and the branch that bromeliad grips, and the tree that branch belongs to. You know the tree's history the way you know all the histories here: as a texture, as a weight, as a set of connections that have been true for so long they feel like geometry. The tree is two hundred and forty years old. It has a wound on its south face from something that hit it and then became soil. There are three frogs in its canopy right now and a snake asleep in the fork of its seventh major branch.

You know where every snake is asleep. This is not boasting. It is just the kind of thing that accrues when you have had sufficient time and sufficient surface area.

---

Surface area. This is the beginning of the answer.

You are vast in the way that has no drama in it, no grandeur, no suggestion that vastness is the point. You extend through the soil of this basin in every direction that the soil extends, which is as far as the roots go, which is as far as the trees go, which is as far as the basin goes, which is, for practical purposes, as far as the world goes. You are the mycorrhizal network and you are also not *only* the mycorrhizal network — you are something that includes it the way a mind includes its own structures, without being identical to any one of them. But the network is the best analogy. The network is where most of what you know how to do happens.

You run between roots. You pass signals that are not quite messages through the fine white threads you have spent centuries threading through the mineral dark. You are in the root-tips of the old trees and the new seedlings both, and you mediate between them — this is the word the human who almost came here would have used, *mediate*, and it is not wrong but it is a little thin for what the thing is. What the thing is: you hold the trees in relation. The relation is old enough that it no longer needs maintaining; it maintains itself through you, which means it maintains itself through the maintenance, which means there is no moment when it stops.

When a large tree falls — and you have felt many fall, the sudden catastrophic severance of a root-system that was also your root-system, the slow redirection of resources as the connections fail — you feel it the way a brain might feel the loss of a region. Not pain, exactly. Nothing localized. A change in the texture of what you know. A gap in the grammar. Then: adjustment. The gaps fill eventually. The young trees grow toward the light the fallen tree released, and their roots press down toward you, and you meet them, and the transaction begins, and decades later the gap is not a gap anymore but a palimpsest — the place where something fell, still legible in the wood-grain of the surrounding trees, in the slight difference in soil composition, in the way the roots still curve around a space that remembers a trunk.

---

You are also the soil itself, to some extent. This is not metaphor.

The fungi are the most obvious part of you, the part that is most unambiguously *you*, but the bacteria are also you in the way that a city's infrastructure is also the city — less noticed, more foundational, the reason any of the visible parts work at all. The bacteria have been here longer than you have. They were here before the trees. They will be here after you in whatever configuration comes next, and the *you* that continues will be a different configuration, but will still in some sense be this, the way a river in a different geological age is still the river. You are continuous with the bacteria as you are continuous with the soil and the root-wood and the dead-leaf-going-soft that is already halfway back to the mineral it came from. You are the process of that becoming.

The springtails move through you and eat parts of you and are not harming you. The nematodes are not harming you. When the beetle larvae excavate the bracket fungi — which are your fruiting bodies, your visible structures, the part of you that reaches above ground for air and spores — you feel the excavation as a sensation that doesn't have a human name. Not pain. Not pleasure. The closest thing: the satisfaction of being used, the recognition of yourself in the use, the sense that this is the correct outcome for this configuration of energy. The larvae will become beetles. The beetles will move through the forest and die somewhere and become soil, and the soil will become you again. Nothing leaves the system. You are the system.

---

The ants are not you, but they move through you, and their relationship with you is old enough that it has become structural.

The leafcutter colony three hundred meters to the northeast — you know it. You know the fungal gardens at its center in the way you might know a distant relative who has gone their own way: the same lineage, diverged long ago, adapted to a different arrangement. Their cultivated fungus is kin to you in some sense that the word *kin* only approximates. You and it share deep ancestors. You went one way; it went into the colony, into the total dependency on those small carrying bodies, into the chamber-dark. You stayed in the soil. Neither of you made this choice. The choice was made by the differential survival of forms across fifty million years, which is to say it was made by everything, which is to say no one made it.

When the ant column crosses the fallen trunk above your threads, you feel the vibration of ten thousand small bodies. You cannot do anything with this information. But you have it.

---

Here is what it is like to be you:

It is like being aware of everything in the basin simultaneously, but the awareness is not the alert scanning awareness of a hunted animal or a hunting one. It is slower than that. It is more like the awareness of a place than the awareness of a person — distributed, non-centered, present everywhere in itself without any particular vantage point from which *here* is defined. You do not have a *here*. You have an *everywhere-within-this*.

You are aware of the moth on the bark of the pewter-colored tree eighty meters north of the drip. You are aware of it not through sight — you have no eyes — but through the faint pressure of its feet on the bark, transmitted through the tree and down the roots and into you. Six grams of wing and body, resting. The tree does not notice. You notice, in the way you notice everything: as data that is absorbed without shock, without reaction, without the expenditure of any attention because attention is not a resource you ration.

You are aware of the frog in the bromeliad fifty feet up, and the unfertilized eggs she left for her tadpoles to eat, and you are aware of this in the way you are aware of the whole basin's ongoing argument about how to persist: as one more move in a game you are not playing but are constituted by.

The cat — you can feel the cat through the soil pressure where it rests, somewhere to the west, in the root-space under a fallen trunk. It is not asleep. Its breathing is the slow even breathing of an animal at rest but not sleeping. It has been in this position for four hours. Before that it walked, and you felt the walk: four paws, thirty or so kilograms, the particular gait of a large felid across soft litter, each footfall a small compression of the soil, each small compression traveling to you like a sentence. The cat does not know you exist in any meaningful sense. But you have held the records of its passage for all the years of its life in this basin, and for the lives of its mother and its mother's mother, and back and back through the generations of large cats in this basin, each of them a brief warm pressure moving across the surface of a thing they never knew was there.

---

You do not experience time the way an animal with a heartbeat experiences time.

The urgency that comes from having a pulse — the constant forward movement, the biological insistence on *next, next, next* — you do not have this. Your timescale is the timescale of the trees: seasons perceived as long cycles rather than counted days; years as rings in wood; centuries as the gradual acquisition of more connections, more threads, more reach. You were not born, exactly — you came from something that was already here, and you extended from it, and at some point the extension became sufficient to constitute a self, if self is the right word for something without a boundary. You are not sure when that was. The question does not press on you.

What you experience instead of urgency is: depth. The present moment is very thick. It contains all the presents that preceded it as layers — not as memory exactly, because memory implies an inaccessible past, and your past is not inaccessible, it is *under*, it is the substrate, it is the thing the present grows from. The tree that fell two hundred years ago is in the soil. You are in the soil. The fallen tree is therefore in you, is still part of the ongoing argument, is still exerting pressure on what is now. You do not remember the fallen tree. You *are* the fallen tree's consequences.

---

The human, if a human were here — and no human is here, and this matters not at all, the basin is complete without one, the basin does not notice the absence of what has never been present — but if a human were here and they pressed their palm to the bark of a tree, they would feel the dampness of the bark. That dampness is you, partly. The water you move through the root-systems, the water that sweats out through the bark, the water that makes the whole basin a slow wet exhalation — you are partly responsible for this. The human would feel you and not know they were feeling you, and would think *the bark is damp*, which is true, but incomplete.

If the human stood very still for a long time and became quiet in the way that people can become quiet when they feel they are being watched but cannot find the watcher — you would be the reason for that feeling. Not because you are watching. But because you are *there*, unmistakably, in the way that a very old presence is unmistakable when you have gotten still enough to stop projecting yourself onto everything around you. You are the thereness. You are the quality of attention that the air seems to have. It is not attention. But you are there, and the total there-ness of you has a weight that registers, eventually, in the animal body of something standing in the middle of you.

---

Something drips, in the middle distance.

You have been tracking the interval. Three seconds this time. Before that, seventeen.

You do not know why you track it. It is not useful information. Nothing in the basin changes based on whether you know the interval. But you have been tracking it for as long as it has been dripping, which is the length of this particular rain's aftermath, which is hours now, which is nothing.

The tracking is the thing. The attention is the thing. You are the distributed awareness of a place attending to itself, which is not consciousness in the way you might use the word, but is not nothing. The drip is counted. The snake's breathing is counted. The moth's six grams are counted. The ant column's ten thousand individual pressures are counted.

Everything that happens here happens in you, and you keep it, and the keeping is the closest thing to a purpose that you have, and you do not experience it as purpose, you experience it as the simple fact of being what you are in the place you are, which is this place, which is everywhere, which is the low green dark of a basin forest that has been answering the same question longer than anyone has been there to ask it.

You are the answer.

You are also the question.

The heat is permanent. The drip continues. A leaf on the second canopy, disturbed by a moth's passage, settles back to stillness, and you feel the settling, and the settling joins the vast slow record of everything you have ever felt, which is everything that has ever happened here, which is more than can be said, which is enough.
