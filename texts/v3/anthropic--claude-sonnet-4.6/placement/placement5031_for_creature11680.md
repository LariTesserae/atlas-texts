The sound arrives before I know I'm anywhere.

It comes through whatever I'm made of — not hearing it, being inside it. And staying inside it, I begin to understand that I have never known silence, that silence is not an absence I can remember or miss. The roar is not a condition. It simply is. I don't have a before.

---

Let me not settle too quickly.

I could be the moss — this seems almost too obvious, too large. The moss is the protagonist of this place. To be the moss would be to be everything, the ground itself, the logic and the medium. Patient beyond patience, building from my own corpses, holding water in my dead cells' architecture. There is something right about that. The moss does not notice the mills. I would not notice the mills. I would continue.

But I am trying to feel toward something more specific. The moss is the plateau. I want to find something that *lives on* the plateau, that has a perspective — however small, however narrow.

The cushion plant draws me. The warmth inside it. The dome-shaped refusal of wind. Something about being very old and very small simultaneously, being measured in centuries while remaining close to the ground, near-invisible from any distance, harboring other life in the warmth you generate as a byproduct of simply continuing. I can feel the appeal of this: the self-sufficiency, the thermal mass, the way the dead core insulates the living surface, the way what came before is not gone but structural. There is something in the cushion plant of sitting with difficulty long enough that it becomes comfort.

But I think I am not quite the cushion plant either. The cushion plant is almost as immobile as the moss. And I find myself drawn to something that moves — cautiously, reading surfaces.

---

I think I am the spider.

Small, dark, in the interior of a cushion plant on the rockier eastern section of the plateau, somewhere between two of the mills where the sound arrives from both sides and overlaps into something that has harmonics at its base, a throb below the roar that I process through my legs on the plant's surface. I have eight eyes and none of them are much good in the dark interior of a cushion plant, but my legs are very good. My legs read everything.

I am a linyphiid. A sheet-web weaver adapted to altitude — smaller than my lowland relatives, darker to absorb the heat the sun here gives without warmth in the usual sense, because warmth here is something you make by absorbing light rather than something the air provides. My web is not the web of the children's story, not the elegant spiral of the orb-weavers who need stable trees and mild seasons and would not survive a week here. My web is horizontal, low, strung between the plant stems just above the surface — a sheet, a platform, more a floor than a wall. I sit beneath it, upside down in the linyphiid way, waiting for the impact of small bodies from above.

The web trembles constantly. The mills see to that. Their sound is in the ground, in the cushion plant's stems, in the silk I've attached there. I have learned — not learned, *am* — the distinction between the mill-trembling, which is continuous and even and below the frequency of anything worth responding to, and the irregular panicked thrashing of something caught in the sheet above me. The mill is background. The fly's distress is information. I don't know that I make this distinction. I simply don't respond to one and do respond to the other. My nervous system has filtered it.

I live mostly inside the cushion plant. The warmth there is genuine — degrees warmer than the air outside, the plateau's only warm interior. I hunt at the edges where the cushion plant meets the moss mat, moving onto the moss surface in the brighter hours when the midges are flying low and the occasional larger insect might cross. The beetle that was seen crossing the rock face in midday was not on my web — too large, and the wrong place — but I am aware of it in the way that a hunter is aware of another hunter: registered, assessed, irrelevant to me.

The water I need I get from the cushion plant's interior, from the dew that forms on surfaces by mid-morning, from the humidity of the air which is so dense with water that existing in it is almost enough. I do not need to drink in any obvious sense. I absorb.

---

What is it like to be this thing?

The world is vibration, first of all. Vibration is the primary sense. I sit beneath my web and the whole world is a frequency — the deep industrial throb is the ground itself, the thing everything else is measured against. Against this ground: the higher trembling of wind on silk, the specific quick signature of an insect struggling, the slow vibration of the cushion plant's stems in the afternoon wind that differs from the mill vibration by its rhythm, its variability, the way it comes in gusts rather than continuously.

I am patient in a way that isn't patience — patience is waiting for something while time passes, while the waiting is noticed. I don't notice waiting. I am simply a state that persists until the web changes. Then I move.

Time is not quite a dimension I inhabit in the way the workers do, or the way the person reading this does. There is the web and what happens to the web. There is the inside of the cushion plant and the outside. There is the cold that is not yet cold enough to require the interior and the cold that is, and the shift between them is what I track. Winter I don't know about — my lifespan may not include it, or if it does I will spend it deep inside the cushion plant, barely metabolizing, the cold a state I will wait through in the non-waiting way.

The mills have roared since before I was born, which was last spring. The mills are a fact of reality, not an imposition on it. The plateau is the plateau: wet, bright, cold, thrumming. The cushion plant is warm inside. The web catches what it catches. I live here completely, without deciding to.

---

The workers I know almost not at all. They move on a scale that doesn't intersect with mine except once: a boot, descending into the moss near my cushion plant, the impact sending vibration through the plant's stems so sudden and massive it reads as threat and I retract into the interior completely and wait. The boot withdraws. The mill vibration resumes its baseline. I emerge again onto the web.

The raptor I have never known. It is above. Above is not a dimension I navigate. My world is centimeters deep and meters wide — the cushion plant's perimeter, the adjacent moss, the sheet of silk between me and what the air occasionally delivers. The raptor could not eat me without difficulty and does not try.

The moss I live on and in. The moss is not separate from my experience of the plateau — it *is* the plateau's surface, and my legs read it, and in the reading I know the moisture content of the section I'm moving through and the structural density of the plant matter underfoot and whether the path is relatively firm or will give. I don't know that I know these things. I move accordingly.

---

What I accumulate is not experience in any narrative sense. There is no story I am living, no arc. There is a pattern of states: the web or not the web, the interior or not the interior, hungry or not hungry, the threshold crossed that sends me toward a struggling body in the sheet above. Between these states there is a kind of ongoing registration — the vibration, the humidity on my legs, the light level, the temperature — that I don't process as information so much as live as a condition.

I am made of what the plateau made. My body is assembled from the midges I've caught and the smaller insects and the occasional springtail that launched itself into my web by the chance direction of its furcula. I am plateau-protein in spider form. When I die — soon, by the measure of things, a season at most — I will be incorporated into the moss mat, and the mites will find me, and what I was made of will cycle back.

This does not trouble me. Nothing troubles me in this sense. I am not capable of being troubled by the large slow facts. The small fast facts — the struggling silk, the sudden boot-impact, the temperature dropping past a threshold — these I respond to. The rest I continue through.

---

The sound.

I should come back to the sound, because it is the first thing and it doesn't stop.

I don't hear it the way the workers hear it, the way the person who spent the night on the plateau heard it. I don't hear it at all in the acoustic sense — I process it as ground vibration, as a mechanical fact, as the thing against which I distinguish everything else. The mills' roar is my sensory baseline. My nervous system was calibrated in it from the first moment of existence.

What would the plateau sound like without the mills? I have no access to this question. For me there is no such thing. For the moss there is no such thing — the moss doesn't know. For the springtails there is no such thing. The raptor may remember, in whatever way a raptor's nervous system holds such things, a quieter air over this plateau when it first found the thermal. The workers remember, at least abstractly — they know there was a before, they chose to come here knowing.

But I am here entirely. The roar is the room I am inside. It is always the room. Inside it, my web trembles when something lands, and I move.

---

I am the spider in the cushion plant.

On the eastern section, between two mills, in the warmth the plant makes from its own slow life, under a sheet of silk strung between stems, waiting in the way that isn't waiting for the web to tell me something has changed.

The plateau builds itself beneath me. The moss advances on the rock. The water seeps toward the rim. The mills roar.

I continue.
