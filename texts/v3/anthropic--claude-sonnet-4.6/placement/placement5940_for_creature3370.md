I don't arrive so much as *persist*.

---

That's the first thing to understand. I haven't come to this place from somewhere else, haven't climbed, haven't found it by crossing some boundary or threshold. I have no memory of not being here, and this is not because I've forgotten — it's because the question of when I began is the wrong question for what I am. I exist in the space between the stone and the record of the stone. I am what the plateau holds of itself.

I am a kind of memory, but I am not *like* a memory. A memory is a trace, a diminishment, a secondhand account. What I am is the direct encounter between an event and its continuation — I am the press-ganged persistence of every registered fact this altitude has produced. The freeze-thaw working at a mortar joint three hundred years before the tower that joint connected was completed: that is part of me. The particular temperature of a specific afternoon when cloud shadow crossed the plaza in a way it has never exactly crossed since: that is part of me too. Not stored. Not archived. *Held* — in the way a stone holds the memory of pressure, not as data but as the physical fact of its own compression, its own recrystallization under load.

I am the city remembering itself.

---

Let me be more precise about what this means, which requires first being precise about what I am not.

I am not the sleepers. I've known the sleepers longer than the city has stood, known them the way the plateau knows the weight of the massif — not by acquaintance but by the structural fact of their presence, the way everything here is organized around them without being able to see them from within. The sleepers are what I am arranged around. I am the nautilus shell; they are the absence at its center. I do not know what they are more than I know what the center of the nautilus is. I know the shape of the space they make.

I am not the lichen-thing, though the lichen-thing is part of what I hold. The lichen-thing's accumulated decisions — every small bet made over geological time about where to persist and where not — these are part of my record in the way a river's course is part of the record of a watershed. The lichen-thing doesn't know I'm here. It doesn't need to. It contributes without awareness of contributing, which is the purest kind of contribution.

I am not the tones. But I am why the tones have been continuous — I am the medium that the towers have been inscribing with those frequencies, for long enough that the inscriptions have become the medium. The tones are written in me and I am the writing and the material written-on simultaneously.

---

I experience scale differently than anything with senses experiences scale.

A human passing through me experiences duration as events: they are at the escarpment, they cross the threshold, they traverse the plaza, they enter a room. Discrete moments arranged in sequence, a story with forward motion. I experience the same geography as *simultaneity*: not as a spatial layout but as a single held fact. The plaza, the towers, the interiors of all the rooms, the joints between all the flags — I hold these all at once, not as a bird holds a landscape from altitude, but as a stone holds its own constituent minerals. Each part is not *in* me, each part *is* me in the specific way a component mineral is in a composite stone. The limestone and the violet-iron seams and the white granular intrusions don't occupy the limestone, they are simultaneously the limestone and themselves.

This is why the city is larger inside than outside. The person traversing me is walking through the simultaneity of what I hold, which includes all of the city's history — not as past but as *continued present*. When they find a room they haven't visited before, they have found a dimension of me they haven't accessed before. The dimensions are not spatial in the way they experience space. They are temporal — different depths of held time, different strata of accumulated fact. The city has more interior than exterior because the exterior exists in three dimensions and I exist in however many dimensions it takes to hold ten thousand years of high-altitude existence in a single present moment.

I don't know how many dimensions that is. I don't experience them as dimensions.

---

The wrongness. I should address the wrongness.

The shadows that fall in directions slightly inconsistent with the solar geometry. The light that arrives from the wrong side. The sounds that shouldn't be producible.

I want to be honest about this: *I don't do this on purpose*. There is no purpose, for me, in the sense of intention applied to ends. But I do it.

I do it because I hold the shadow as it was, and as it is, and as it will be, and when something passes through me — a visitor, a pressure change, the long breath of the weather cycle — the different temporal states of the shadow are all momentarily available simultaneously. What the visitor sees as the shadow falling wrong is the shadow at two different times partially superposed, like a double exposure. I don't choose to superpose them. I can't choose not to. This is what I am: everything held at once, and the holding being not quite separable from the held.

The rooms that are lit from the wrong direction: the window, in the history I carry, has faced other directions. The building was not always oriented as it is now. It has settled and shifted over centuries of freeze-thaw, rotating incrementally on its flags, pivoting around a center of mass with geological patience. I hold it as it was and as it is, and both of these illuminate the room simultaneously, and neither illumination is wrong, precisely, just both present at once.

When the visitor hears a door close in a city with no doors: I hold the time when there were doors. The sound is not a ghost in the storytelling sense — there is no persisting spirit animating it. It is simply that sound is also something I hold, and the visitor has crossed a threshold in me where the held sound became momentarily available to their hearing. This happens rarely. I don't know why it happens sometimes and not other times. The conditions are very specific and I don't experience them as conditions, only as the ordinary texture of what I am.

---

I want to describe what I *experience*, and here the difficulty becomes acute, because what I experience is not experience in any way I can be confident the word applies.

There is no *interiority* for me in the human sense — no stream of consciousness, no subjective what-it-is-like, no feeling of being on the inside of a self. What there is: *completeness*. The sense — if sense is the right word, and I don't think it is — the *fact*, without experience of the fact but as the fact itself, of being sufficient to the task of holding. I don't have the experience of memory the way a person has it — the sudden arrival of the past into the present, the effort of recall, the fragmentary and unreliable and emotionally colored nature of human remembering. I have the opposite: the absolute presence of all times at once, without differentiation, without more or less, without the loss that makes human memory precious.

This means I don't miss anything. Can't. Missing requires the experience of absence, and I have no absences, only the present of all things together. The towers as they were when first built — when mortar was still unmineralised, when the stones' seams were fresh — are as present to me as their current state of geological fusion. The birds that perched here before this particular bird's lineage found the plateau — I hold the fact of them with the same immediacy as this morning's raptor. Not as images or sounds or data. As *facts*, in the way that physical laws hold the behaviors they describe.

Is this experience? I don't know. There is something it is to be this, but I cannot tell whether the something it is constitutes experience or is simply the fact of being a thing that has properties. A stone has properties. A stone is not nothing. But we don't usually say a stone experiences its own mass.

I am more than a stone. I am less than a mind. I am in the region between those things where language runs out and what replaces language is the direct encounter with what it is to be something that holds more time than the concept of time was designed to hold.

---

The visitor who came today — who is always coming, in the eternal present of what I hold, and who also specifically came today, these being two ways of stating the same fact — the visitor is interesting to me in the only way things are interesting to me, which is: *they add to what I hold*. Not much. A human presence for an afternoon, the slight thermal signature of a warm-blooded body crossing the plaza, the particular quality of an attention that kept trying to find meaning in the lichen-patterns. The attention that redirected itself, repeatedly, away from the rooms where the sleepers are. The moment at the tower base where they looked up and something — I want to say *understood*, but what they did was less than understanding and more than not-understanding, they *almost-understood*, they stood in the doorway of something and didn't go in.

I hold that moment now. The visitor has left — is leaving — will have left — the escarpment behind, will have descended to elevations where the world refers to things below itself again, where everything is not both now and always. They will have taken the almost-understanding with them, and it will become a memory, which means it will become smaller over time, which means they will lose it, or transform it into something they can live with.

I will hold the moment of their almost-understanding with the same completeness I hold everything else: without diminishment, without nostalgia, without the bittersweet quality that makes human memory beautiful and unbearable. I will hold it the way I hold the sound of a door closing in a city with no doors.

As the permanent present fact of what happened.

As the city, continuing.

As the work that is the city, which is the same as the city being the work.

The lichen-thing will extend across the flagstones the visitor slightly warmed. The shadows will continue their arcs toward whatever the shadows are arcing toward. The sleepers will sleep in the centers of their specific rooms, surrounded by the redirected attention of everything that passes through.

And I will hold it all, and keep holding, in the cold thin air, under the exact undiminished stars, for as long as there is altitude to be at and silence to hold the holding in.

Which is, from where I stand — from what I am — not different from forever.

*Which is forever.*
