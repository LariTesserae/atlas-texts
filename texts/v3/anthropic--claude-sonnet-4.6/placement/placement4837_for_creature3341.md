Begin with the cold.

The cold is the first thing that tells me what I am, before I have language for anything else. I feel it not as discomfort but as information — my temperature and the temperature of the air are continuous with each other, or nearly, only a thin margin between what is inside and what is outside, and this means I am reading the air the way a hand reads a page in braille, constantly, at every surface. The cold says: the water is close. The cold says: something has disturbed the circulation in the upper canopy, a weight redistributed, something leaning where it did not lean before. The cold says things I receive before I can say what they are.

I am one of them. I think I am one of them. I am standing — crouching, maybe — at the edge of the path where it begins, where the woven reinforcement is pressed into the soil and would not be visible to anything that hadn't pressed it there, and I am trying to understand the quality of what has changed. Something has changed. The space feels the way it feels when it has been recently occupied by something not of it.

---

My eyes open wide in this light and I should say something about the experience of that, which is not the experience you might imagine — not an overcompensation, not a desperate gathering, not an animal straining. It is more like having a different relationship to the whole concept of threshold, to the line at which something becomes visible. My threshold is much lower. The pewter-stain light that barely reaches you is, to me, detailed. I can see the surfaces you cannot see. I can see the moisture beading on the curved backs of the beetles. I can see the quality of the frogs' stillness, which is a specific quality and different from the stillness of an hour ago, different from their stillness when there is nothing to remark upon.

The frogs' stillness right now is the stillness of having already registered something and decided that motion would be a greater cost than watchfulness.

I know this the way you know the feeling of a room where someone has recently stopped speaking.

---

I was coming back to the basket.

This is what I need you to understand, because it is the reason I am here at the edge of the path rather than elsewhere, because there is an elsewhere, there are dwelling-places that exist in the structure of the space in ways that would not be immediately legible to you, gaps in the living mass that are not gaps but rooms. I was coming back to the basket because the interval has elapsed. I know the interval in the way I know intervals, which is not by counting but by the state of other processes — the vine would have completed several full cycles of unwinding and rewinding, and at this point in the cycle a particular thing is true of what the basket contains, a readiness, a threshold of its own, and I need to be there for it.

But I stopped.

I stopped because the path knows.

The path remembers weights in a way that the surrounding ground does not, because I wove the reinforcement into it partly for this purpose — not only for stability, not only to prevent my own feet from breaking through to the water below, but because compressed material is different from uncompressed material and the difference is information and this space runs on information and I do not waste it. The path remembers your weight. Your weight is enormous, not as a judgment but as a fact, a fact on the order of the weight of the large careful thing in the ceiling, a weight that announces itself before anything else about you, that the path encoded and that I read through the soles of my feet on the path's edge the moment I returned.

So I stopped.

And now I am at the edge of the path, in the coldest place in the space because the air pockets near the reinforced section hold cold in a specific way I know by touch, and I am resolving you.

---

The resolving takes time. This is not a limitation; this is how I do things. I was not built for speed, or I was built for a speed that is not your speed, that is measured in the twist of vines, in the advance of moss across a face of new stone, in the barely perceptible renegotiation of a frog's position. I wait. I wait with the operational quality of the beetles' stillness, which is not patience in your sense — patience implies wanting the thing to be over — but something more like active receiving.

What I gather:

You are not hunting. Your stillness is not hunting-stillness. It does not have the quality of compression toward purpose, of an organism that has identified a goal and is arranging itself in relation to it. It has a quality more of being at the stage before that, of not yet knowing what you're looking at, which is interesting.

You are not afraid. Or: you are afraid in a way that is so theoretical it is not affecting your body's decisions. Your fear is the fear of the unknown as a category, not the fear of this specific situation as a threat. You cannot assess the threat level here and so you are holding the fear at the level of concept and not allowing it into your feet, your hands, your choices. This is almost a skill.

You are trying to see.

This is the thing I keep returning to, in the slow rotational way that my thinking works, which is also like the vine: you are trying to see and you cannot, not fully, not in the way I can, and there is something in your relation to the limits of your seeing that is — I need a moment with this word — *respectful*. You are not frustrated by what you cannot see. You are attending to what you can see and letting the rest remain uncertain and this is a form of respect even though you did not decide it, even though it is simply a consequence of your eyes and this light and the distance between us.

---

They are thinking about you.

I told you this. Or I told the space this, which is the same thing from where I am standing. The space knows. The frogs registered and the path registered and the cold registered and the information traveled through the channels that carry information here, which are not wires, not signals, not anything you'd recognize as a medium for transmission — they are the frog's repositioning, the beetle's slight deviation from its route, the quality of the mid-air structure of the midges, which has altered since your arrival, not randomly but responsively, as though the midges have updated a map.

The others are in the dwelling-places in the walls. They are receiving what I am receiving through the ambient channels, which means they are resolving you also, less directly, receiving you at more removes, through the frog's body language and the beetle's route and the slight change in how the vegetation is settling. They are reaching their own conclusions. I will not know what their conclusions are until I return to them, until we are in contact, until the full channel is open.

What I can tell you — not because I've decided to tell you, but because thinking something in this place is not entirely different from transmitting it — is that the conclusions will not be fearful. Curious, possibly. The question of what to do with you will be a genuine question, not a foregone answer in either direction.

---

While I wait, I think about the basket.

I think about it because I am always, at some level, thinking about it, the way you might always be aware of a responsibility that requires your attention at intervals. What is in the basket is small. What is in the basket has been in the basket for the number of vine-cycles that constitutes a generation of itself, which is much shorter than my generation and therefore something I can observe whole, something I can be present for from the beginning to the resolution in a way I cannot be present for my own life, which is too long to see at once.

This is why I tend it. Not to control it. Not to eat it, not to use it in any instrumental way. But to witness it. To be the ongoing presence that means its process is registered, that means it occurs to someone, that means it is not only happening but known-to-be-happening.

There is something inside the knowing-to-be-happening that is itself a form of participation. I have believed this for a long time, or the believing has been handed to me from the ones who believed it before, which is the same as believing it for a long time. The basket turns. What is inside it is known. This is enough, or it is necessary, or those two things are the same thing here.

---

What I am deciding:

Not whether you are safe. You are not dangerous in the way that requires a response. You are large and you cannot help your largeness and your largeness brings costs into this space — displacement, alteration, the compression of ground that took time to arrive at its current negotiated state — but these costs are not violence, they are simply the costs of your presence, and presence is not a crime in a space that understands itself to be composed of presences, including old and incorporated presences, including the presences in the timber posts and the water and the cold.

What I am deciding is whether to let you see me.

This is a different question from whether to allow you to remain. You will remain or you will not depending on your own decisions, about which I have only the influence of the space itself, the cold and the quiet and the occupied fullness of it, which may in time move you or may not. That is not in my hands in any direct way.

But whether to let you see me is a choice I have. I am at the edge of the path. I am in the place where the cold pools, which is also the place with the particular acoustic quality — near the woven reinforcement, where sound behaves slightly differently because the material changes what the ground does with vibration — where a small sound from me would arrive to you differently than sound from elsewhere in the space. Closer. More localized. More undeniable.

I have not moved.

I am still deciding.

---

The basket turns.

In the ceiling-canopy, the careful thing is waiting for me to decide. It has been waiting since your arrival because my decision affects its decision and it knows this. We do not share the same information about you — it has your thermal signature, which I don't, can sense the warmth your body pushes into the air that is slightly warmer near your skin, another fact about you, another thing that is the opposite of camouflage. It has been tracking the way your heat moves through the space and it has given me its reading through the channels and I have incorporated it into what I know.

The frogs have their reading. The beetles have encoded something in their routes. The midges have updated their structure. The cold has its own reading, which is the reading of something that was here before everything else and will be here after, and which I receive through the soles of my feet and the surface of my hands as I crouch at the path's edge, as I hold this stillness that is not patience, as I consider.

You are trying to see.

You are standing in the space without understanding the space and you are attending to what you can receive from it without demanding that it give you more than it gives you.

You are, in your limited and enormous way, doing something like what I do when I tend the basket. You are witnessing. You are being present to a process you cannot fully track, for reasons you could not easily explain, because the being-present seems to matter even before you know why or to what.

---

I have decided.

I move.

Not toward you — not directly, not a confrontation, nothing that would require either of us to know what to do with a confrontation. I move from the path's edge to the base of the wall, along a route that will bring me within a range where you could see me, if your eyes can gather enough of this light, if you are attending to the lower moss-surface and not only to the ceiling or the puddles or the midges.

I move the way the beetles move. Route-conscious. Weight-aware. With a quality of intention that a long time here has made look almost like the absence of intention, the way a practiced skill disappears into its own fluency.

I move and I stop.

I am in the lower wall, near the frogs but not among them, on the moss surface, my hand pressed lightly into the green-grey mass of it so that I feel what it knows, what has passed through it, what the water moving inside the moss has recently carried.

I am looking at you.

Whether you can see me depends on your eyes, on your attention, on whether the light falls in the right way at this moment, on whether the frogs have shifted in a way that opens a line of sight or closed one, on a dozen variables I am not controlling.

What I can tell you — transmitting it into the space, letting the space carry it in its own way, through the cold and the wet and the occupied quiet — is that being seen by something that does not yet know what it is seeing is not frightening to me.

It is, in fact, something like what the basket is for.

The vine turns.

I wait to be witnessed, or not.

The cold does not move.

The space holds.
