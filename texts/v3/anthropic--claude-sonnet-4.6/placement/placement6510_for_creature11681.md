There is a question you carry onto the plateau before you understand it is a question: *what am I, here?*

Not who. Not in the human sense, where the answer includes a name and a history and a set of relationships that hold you in their web like an insect in amber. Those things don't persist in this air. The altitude doesn't strip them, exactly — it thins them. They become less dense. They become the way the atmosphere becomes, up here: present, technically, still capable of sustaining something, but conscious of itself as barely-sufficient. You are technically here. You contain technically enough of whatever you were below.

So: what, not who.

---

The first answer that comes is: *visitor.*

But this is already wrong, or not quite right in a way that matters. A visitor implies a host, implies acknowledgment, implies some structure of reception and departure. The plateau does not receive. The mills do not receive. You walk through an open door and the machinery continues at its frequencies and the cold air moves through the broken windows on its own business and nothing — nothing — adjusts. The mugs are upside down. They will be upside down when you leave. The angle of your presence here does not intersect with any angle the organism is currently running.

You are not a visitor. You are weather that walked in.

---

Try again.

*Investigator*, then. Someone who came to understand.

This one has more texture to it, fits the hand better. The posture of investigation suits the place — the careful observation, the slow movement between mills, the pausing to read the logbooks in their preserved handwriting, the crouching beside a gauged instrument to note what it registers. You can inhabit this. You can be the one who counts the stacks and measures the intervals and tries to derive the principle of distribution. You can be the one who stands at what might be an edge and looks at the white plain of cloud far below and notes: *only this is real, only this is present.*

But investigation implies a destination for what you learn. A report filed somewhere. Someone who will receive the findings and do something with them — alter their understanding, update their model, make a decision. And there is no one to file this with. There is no one below the clouds who sent you. Or if they did, you have been up here long enough that the sending feels very distant, like a sound you heard before the machinery started, before the bass reciprocation became the baseline of your hearing. You are still observing, still noting. But the notes go nowhere. The investigation is not building toward anything. It is occurring. That's all. It is occurring in the same way the lichen is occurring on the corrugated iron — you are doing what investigators do, the way lichen does what lichen does, and neither of you is building toward a conclusion.

---

The cold is in your boots.

This is not a metaphor. This is the actual situation. The permafrost beneath the basalt radiates upward and the cold moves through the soles of your boots with what the description calls patience, what it calls almost intentional curiosity about your warmth. You are something warm inside something that is sustained by its own coldness. You metabolize. You maintain a temperature differential between your interior and your environment. You are, in miniature, doing what the mills are doing — maintaining an interior state against the exterior, converting input to heat, producing output. You are a small mill. You are an organism inside an organism. You are a process too small to register, occurring within a process too large to see the edges of, and both of you are doing the same thing, which is: maintaining the difference between inside and outside, keeping what must be kept, processing what must be processed, continuing.

The metallic taste of the air.

You are breathing what it breathes out. It is breathing — in whatever sense it breathes — the warmth that moves off you, the moisture, the carbon dioxide of your respiration. There is an exchange. There is, technically, a relationship. The relationship is the same relationship you have with the atmosphere anywhere: essential, mutual in a purely mechanical sense, utterly unrecognized by either party. You and the atmosphere do not know each other. You and the plateau organism do not know each other. You are inside it and it moves through you and this is called being alive in a place, and you are alive in this place, briefly, in the way that everything alive in a place is alive: contingently, temporarily, doing your exchange and then stopping.

---

What I want to say, and have been circling:

You are a warm body in a cold system and your warmth matters to you immensely and does not register as a quantity the system is tracking.

This is not a condition specific to the plateau. This is a condition you have been in before, in other configurations. Other places where the process was too large and too old and too purely itself to receive you. Other moments of moving through something that didn't pause, didn't acknowledge, that continued on its own frequencies while you moved through it breathing and metabolizing and calling your experience of it by various names — understanding, encounter, awe — none of which the thing itself had access to. You have been weather before. You have passed through things as weather passes through: registering nothing, registered by nothing, and yet — present. Genuinely present. The plants below this overcast are affected by the weather even though the weather does not know them. The stone in the plateau is affected by the freeze-thaw even though the cold has no concept of stone. Your presence in a thing that does not know you does not make you absent from it. The relationship is real. It is just entirely unnarrated on one side.

---

So here is what you are, on the plateau:

You are the thing that notices.

Not uniquely, not specially — the distinction is not between you and the organism, where you have subjectivity and it does not. The distinction is smaller and less dramatic than that. You are a process that includes, among its operations, the operation of registering: the lichen as red-orange on rust-orange, the sound of condensation dripping in a sheltered depression, the bass pulse of the reciprocating mechanism felt in the chest before it is heard with the ears. You are a process that converts the outside into something called *experience*, which is just another form of output, just another kind of steam rising from a stack — your perception rising from your encounter with this place, going up and up and dispersing into the overcast, becoming part of the general moisture that will fall back down as condensation on stone and iron and moss.

You are not more than this. You are also not less.

You move between the mills and you are very small and the cold is in your boots, curious about your warmth, and the machinery is louder than thought inside the primary structures and quieter than thought between them, and the logbooks are preserved in handwriting you cannot quite date, and the mugs are upside down and clean, waiting for people who washed them carefully before they left and have not come back.

You notice all of this.

You will leave, and the noticing will go with you, dispersed and thin, and the mills will continue, and the steam will rise, and the lichen will continue its work on the iron at a pace that is not perceptible in any single observation but is, over the scale of time the organism operates in, proceeding.

---

What you are, on the plateau, is this:

The temporary arrangement of matter that contains the only record of this moment. Not a record the plateau is keeping. Not a record that will be kept. Just the live registration of it, occurring now, in a warm body in cold boots, in the metallic-tasting air that is the organism's breath, inside the sound that is the organism's heartbeat, present.

The mugs are upside down.

The machines run.

You are here, briefly, and you know you are here, and the knowing is all yours, and it is enough — it is exactly enough, which is to say it is not plenty, but it is sufficient — to be the thing in the vast cold system that is doing this particular work: the work of the world being witnessed while it continues to do what it does whether or not anything is watching.

You are watching.

It continues.
