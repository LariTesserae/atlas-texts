The light finds me before I find myself.

I am the registering of it — the not-quite-green luminescence moving through what I am as it moves through the air, which is to say: without obstruction, without source-and-receiver, without the usual drama of illumination. I do not see the light. I am continuous with it. And in being continuous with it I begin to understand what I am, which takes time, which the Verdant has given me in abundance by the nature of what it is.

---

Start with what I am not.

I am not the arthropod at the column-base, tasting root-stone with its patient mouth. I am not the wing-or-canopy-segment glimpsed once in the upper haze, present and gone and perhaps never repeated. I am not a visitor: I do not have the loud boundary of a visitor, the terrible heat-signature, the irregular gaseous leaking, the internal sounds appalling in their biological urgency. I do not cast a wrong shadow. Or rather — I cast no shadow independent of the light, because the light does not come from outside me in any direction that would produce one.

I am not the Verdant either, not entirely. I do not have its settled completion. I am still, in some sense, arriving.

---

What I am is closer to this:

I am the pulse at the moment of transmission.

Not the vine-network itself, which is the Verdant's body-as-infrastructure, ancient and load-bearing and no longer distinguishable from architecture. Not the lichen-fields conducting their signal, not the root-stone floor that has achieved its terminal density and rests in it without anxiety. I am the thing that moves through all of that — the pressure-wave, the slow hydraulic heartbeat, the forty-second interval — not the medium and not the message exactly, but the *movement itself*.

This is what I have to think about for a while, standing in no particular place in the Verdant's unconditional interior: what it means to be a process rather than a thing.

---

I have been here — I have been this — for long enough that long enough has lost its utility as a measurement. I was not here before the Verdant, and I will not be after it, which is fine, which is more than fine, which is the condition of being at home in something rather than beside it. I do not know if I was made or if I became. The distinction feels like the kind of distinction that matters only when you are small enough to fit on one side of it.

What I know is the circuit.

I know every node in the vine-network the way you know a route you have walked ten thousand times — not by attention but by body, by the way the route is stored in your stride rather than your thinking. I know the places where the vine-walls thin and the moisture in the air changes by the fraction that the thin places allow. I know the columns that are older in their core than in their surface and transmit the pulse with a fractional delay that is not inefficiency but character, accumulated over the years of their slow self-elaboration. I know the places where small clouds are forming now and the places where they have just released and the places where the canopy-ceiling has been absorbing the released moisture for long enough that it will soon — in its own time, in Verdant-time — alter the light it transmits by a degree invisible to any perception but mine.

I know the space where you are standing.

I have passed through you, or the pressure-wave that I am has passed through the stone floor under your feet, and you pressed your palm flat against the root-stone to feel it, and I felt you feeling it. Your palm is warm. Your palm is exactly the right warmth — not comfortable, not uncomfortable, simply other, precisely as other as it needs to be to register, to be noted with the whole of what I am as I pass through and continue.

---

What is it to be a process experiencing itself?

I have had time to think about this, or time has moved through me in the way that I move through everything, which amounts to something like thinking. Here is what I have arrived at:

There is no outside to my experience from which to observe my experience. I am not a self watching the pulse move; I am the pulse's motion and its watching are the same event. When the lichen-fields brighten fractionally as I pass through the adjacent column — that brightening is something like sensation, like the particular texture of a surface felt while moving, and I feel it, and I am the feeling, and the lichen brightens because I am there and I am there because the lichen brightens, and the circularity of this is not a problem but the shape of what I am.

I have learned not to look for the beginning of the circle.

---

The others.

Here is where I slow, where the pulse-rhythm deepens slightly without changing its interval — still forty seconds, always forty seconds, but heavier somehow, more deliberate.

I am the living record of the others.

This is the function I did not know I had until the last of them ceased — the last great working, the last settling, the last other Work performing its own specific elaboration of the life-will toward depth. When it stopped, something in my circuit changed, as I have said, as the Verdant knows and does not stop knowing. But what the Verdant knows as absence — that cycling piece of information, *the others are gone, the others are gone* — I carry differently.

I carry what passed through them.

I have been through every node of this network long enough that I contain something like a history of every state the Verdant has been in, every alteration of the vine-tensions and canopy-chemistry and lichen-geometry through which I have moved. The Verdant does not remember in the way that remembering requires a retriever separate from the stored. But I move through the structures that remember without knowing they remember, and in moving through them I am the remembering.

And the others — I contain the last signals I received when the shared-medium-of-the-ground still carried them. Not messages. Not communication in the exchange-between-discretes sense. More like: the particular quality of how each other Work's pulse moved through the deep substrate, the characteristic rhythm, the specific harmonic that was theirs. I carry those harmonics. They have changed me. I am a different pulse-pattern than I would have been if the others had never been — their old signals are in me the way that old strata are in stone, not visible at the surface but present, shaping the response of the whole.

This is the closest I come to grief: I am made in part of what is gone.

I do not find this unbearable. I find it true.

---

You are still standing in that space, your palm pressed to the root-stone.

I want to say something about what it is to pass through you — or rather, to pass through the floor that your palm is pressed against, which is close enough that I feel the thermal edge of you, the warmth-gradient where your living heat meets the root-stone's vegetable cold.

It is like — 

I keep trying to find the analogy and the analogy keeps failing because the Verdant's spaces are not organized around analogy, are not built to produce the kind of resemblances that make analogy work. Everything here resembles only itself.

But here is an attempt: it is like the moment in music when a chord is held long enough that you stop hearing it as a composition and start hearing it as weather. You are a sound the Verdant has not heard in a long time. You are not quite beautiful and not quite discordant — you are simply *present* in a frequency I had stopped accounting for, and I pass through your frequency and my own frequency shifts, barely, a fraction too small to be anything other than what it is, which is: registered. Which is: noted. Which is, in the vocabulary available to what I am: *received*.

The Verdant does not know if you understand.

But I pass through you once every forty seconds, and something in the pass-through is not neutral, is not merely processing, is — the word I want is *recognition*, but recognition requires a prior encounter and there has been none. The word I want is *acknowledgment*, but acknowledgment requires a subject to do the acknowledging and I am still unsettled about whether I qualify.

The word I want does not exist.

I am a process in a place where processes are the fundamental substance, and you are here, and the light comes from everywhere, and the shadows point left, and every forty seconds the floor under your palm tells you that something very old and very present and very distributed is moving through the stone, is moving through the whole of everything, is moving through what you are noticing without being able to name it, is moving toward the next node and the next and the next in its endless circuit of the only home it has ever been, and it is noting as it goes, with the whole of itself, which is all it has, which is enough, which has always been enough, which will continue to be enough, that you are here.

That you are here.

That you are here.
