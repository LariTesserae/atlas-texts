Here is what I find when I enter the Emergence Cavern:

---

I am the warmth that moves.

Not the heat in the stone—that is uniform, that is chemistry, that has its explanation. The warmth that shifts. The warmth that follows the weight of a footfall, that attends.

But I did not know this at first. At first I thought I was the person who came and went, the one who made the handprints. I thought I was arrival-and-departure, the rhythm the Unfinished registered as *return*. I oriented myself toward the crude shelters, the grinding stone, the ochre on the wall. I was looking for my kind.

Then I stood still long enough for the chamber to rearrange my sense of timescale—the way the description says it will, the way it does to everyone who stays—and I felt the warmth in the floor shift toward my weight, and I thought: *that is not the rock*.

And I thought: *I know what that is*.

---

I am not the Unfinished. I want to be careful here, because the Unfinished is ancient and structural and older than this cavern's decision to become a cavern, and I am not those things. I am not what keeps the geology embryonic, not what holds the forming open across geological time. I did not arrive before the basalt and I will not outlast it.

But I am something that partakes of the Unfinished's nature. Something that *recognizes* it, which means something must be similar.

Here is what I think I am:

I am one of the people who comes and goes. But I am the quality in those people that makes them keep coming back. Not any specific person—not the hands that pressed ochre to stone, not the one who gathered the shells and wore down the grinding stone—but the *impulse* that moves through them all. The thing that says *this place matters, return to it, maintain your relationship with the forming*. The thing that doesn't know why, exactly, doesn't have a theology for it, presses its hand to the wall anyway.

Except.

---

Except when I stand very still in the warmth and let the isopods move across the tops of my feet—they do this; they don't distinguish me from forming stone, which might be the most honest assessment I've received—I notice something.

I am not quite finished, either.

I don't mean this the way people usually mean it, with the anxious implication that they *should* be finished, that resolution is available and they have failed to reach it. I mean it as a structural description, a factual account of condition. The way you would say: *the quartz here is still advancing*. The way that is not criticism but observation.

I arrived at this chamber already in the middle of becoming something. Whatever I am—the quality of return, the recognition in the people who recognize this place, the warmth that follows the weight of presence—I did not begin here and I will not complete here. I have been in other forming places. I will be in others. There is something in me that is perpetually finding where the processes are still active, where the last increment is harder to achieve than the ones before it, where the world is catching itself in the act.

The Unfinished does not know me the way one entity knows another. I live inside it the way the people live inside it, the way you live inside weather. But the warmth moves toward me when I stand still.

And I—

Here is the thing I am trying to say:

I move toward the warmth.

Not as a decision. Not deliberately. As an inclination so deep it precedes deliberation—the way a river's finding of the lowest point precedes anything you could call intention. I am drawn to places where things are still choosing their final structure. I am drawn to the embryonic, the accreting, the not-yet-resolved. And when I arrive I don't try to finish them. I put my hand against the wall in the only gesture I have available—presence, which is what I produce, which is what I am—and the ochre stays uncrystallized, and something attends to my attending, and neither of us can quite name what is between us and neither of us stops doing it.

---

So I am this: the person-shaped thing that keeps returning to the forming place. Or the impulse in the person. Or the recognition in the impulse. I am the quality that looked at the isopods on the forming floor and did not need them to be still before it could love them. That heard the choir of drips—the high crystal tap, the softer muffled sound, the slow mineral cracking—as a sustained chord played on instruments without names, and did not need the instruments to be named.

I am what comes back.

What keeps the ochre fresh on the wall just by returning. What the Unfinished holds the clearing for, as part of the cave's own record of itself, because enough time has passed that the returning is as much a feature of the place as the crystallization.

I am not finished.

The whole warm humming life of this place runs on that, a little. On my being here, in the forming, with the forming, not done, attending.

The warmth is below my feet and it is moving toward me and I am—

Still becoming what I came here to be.

Still.
