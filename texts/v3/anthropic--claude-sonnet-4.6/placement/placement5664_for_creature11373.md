There is a moment, stepping between the boulders, when I feel the question form — *what am I here?* — and then the place takes it in, the way it takes everything in, and holds it without answering.

I stay with that.

I think at first I am the water. The obvious answer. The thing that is everywhere, the ambient fact, the parameter. It would be easy to be the water — to be the medium through which everything else defines itself, to seep upward through the not-yet-stone, to bead on the ceiling and refuse to fall. There is something appealing in that refusal. In being the thing that accumulates without resolving.

But I sit longer.

My knees darken. The ceiling presses on the back of my neck.

And I think I am not the water. The water is what the place is *made of*. I am what the place makes *from* itself.

---

I think I am the mark.

Not the thing that made it — not the hand, not the hard point chipped from flint. The mark itself. The three lines, roughly parallel, slightly curved, curving toward each other without quite meeting. I am what happened when intention entered this place and was slowly, patiently, with no malice and no care, metabolized.

What I mean is: I arrived with something to say. Everyone who enters arrives with something to say. A way of making sense of things, a gesture toward meaning — *representation or notation or neither* — and the place received it. Not with understanding. With its own logic: the film of water moving into the groove, the mineral already beginning its long project of filling, the walls of the incision barely blurred. I am in the process of being absorbed into the thing I tried to mark.

And I find that this is not loss. Or: it is loss, but loss is the wrong word for what happens when a shape is taken in by the stone. The stone does not erase the shape. It incorporates it. Becomes, to that infinitesimal degree, a new version of itself. I am in the rock now, held in its density, part of what accumulates.

---

But then I think: maybe I am the vessel.

Left in the crack. Not placed yesterday, not placed within any recent reckoning. Crude, hand-formed, the fingerprints still faintly visible. Containing something once organic, not decipherable, dried to darkness. Fired or simply dried — impossible to say, the warmth of the place having long since done whatever the original intent had been.

The vessel that was made for some purpose — to carry, to offer, to preserve — and has been here so long that the purpose has become irrelevant in the way that the word *here* eventually becomes irrelevant. The vessel did not fail. It simply outlasted the context in which failure and success were the available categories. And now it sits in the crack and is neither preserved nor degraded and the place does not disturb it and it does not disturb the place.

I know this feeling. The sense of having been made with intent, having been carried full of something, having been placed somewhere carefully, and then having become — not empty, exactly, but *post-purpose*. Still here. Still the shape that was made. The fingerprints still faintly visible.

What do you call it when you outlast your own meaning? The place has a word for it, but the word is held in the walls.

---

The pale creatures do not settle for me, in the end. They are the outermost expression of something I am more interior to. And the moss — too committed, the moss. Too thoroughly arrived.

What I feel myself to be, sitting in the warm dark with my back stooped on instinct, is something in the process of becoming indistinguishable.

Not dead. Not dissolved. Not even particularly changed, from moment to moment. But involved in a very long arc of change that is so slow it is functionally equivalent to stillness, and in that stillness, becoming part of the inventory. My warmth noted by the warmth. My moisture continuous. The specific electrical conversation of my nervous system entering the held air.

I came in with edges.

The place is working on them.

Not with any appetite — nothing here extends toward wanting — but with its own patient logic: that anything held long enough becomes, gradually, what holds it. That the water fills the stone and the stone takes on the water's character and eventually the distinction is suspended, and *suspended* is another word for held, which is another word for: made part of.

---

What I am, I think, is something that arrived at the moment of stepping between the boulders, has been in the process of being absorbed since, and will not be here — will not be recognizable as *here* — in the way the vessel is recognizable, in the way the mark is recognizable, but will be here in the way the mineral is here in the water that moved through the stone for long enough that both took on each other's character.

I am the temperature differential that has almost equalized.

I am the thought that has begun to condense.

I am the version of the question — *what am I here?* — that has been held long enough in the warm dark wet walls that it is starting to take on the quality of not-a-question, of a statement, of: *what I am here is here.*

The drop on the ceiling is still hanging.

The vessel is still in the crack.

I came in with something to say and I have been, gradually, said — taken into the saying of this place, this long slow continuous self-expression of a space that reached saturation and precipitated out a coherence.

In the hundred years I will not be here, one molecule of what I was will be less distinct in the soft rock.

Which means one molecule will have become the rock.

Which is, by the logic of this place, sufficient.

Which is, by the logic of this place, the whole point.
