---

I arrive expecting to be someone. This is my mistake.

---

What I notice first is that I have been thinking in straight lines the whole journey up, thoughts moving from point to point the way travelers move toward a destination. Planning what I'll observe. Deciding what it means before I see it. I'm still doing this when the ground that is not ground surfaces beneath me and the chord of the plateau enters my body through the soles of my feet, through the sternum, through somewhere near the back of the throat, and the straight-line thinking simply — finds nowhere to go. Not interrupted. Just suddenly a small noise in a larger room.

I stand there for a while. I don't know how long.

---

I came from a place with weather.

This is the thing that surfaces first when I try to understand what I've lost and not yet replaced: weather as narrative structure. The world building toward rain and then releasing, the arc of it, the completion. Every morning a little different, afternoon a surprise, seasons as chapters, each year telling you it's a year by having a plot. I understood time by what changed. I calibrated *myself* by what changed, found myself in the difference between the morning and the evening, between who I was last August and who I am now.

Here the morning is the same as the evening is the same as last August, or close enough that my nervous system cannot find the landmarks it uses to know where it is in the story.

There is no story. There is something else instead.

---

I spend the first weeks making the newcomer's errors.

I interrupt people. I feel urgent about things. I find myself waiting for something to happen and then having to find the something-happening inside myself, manufacturing it, performing it, telling myself this was interesting, that was significant, I am having experiences, look. The residents register my performance with the patience of people who have seen this before and know what it costs. Not condescension. Something more like the willingness of the canopy to flex very slightly underfoot in the cold part of the night — the accommodation of a small motion, quiet, not commented upon.

I also keep trying to see the settlement as a thing to understand. Looking for the mechanism. Looking for the seam between the built and the grown. I crouch by the chamber walls and peer at the texture of living material and think, *but what is actually happening here*, the way I think what is actually happening when someone weeps, as if the mechanism were the answer. The mechanism is not the answer. I know this already and keep forgetting it.

What I am doing, I eventually realize, is refusing to be inside the place. Maintaining the analytical distance that keeps me a visitor, a pair of eyes connected to a notebook, because being inside would require a different relationship with myself, one I haven't negotiated yet.

---

The negotiation starts, without my permission, the third week.

It starts with sound.

I am lying on the canopy surface — I've started doing this, it's warm and yields slightly and I can feel something I can't describe, a very slow pulse, not like a heartbeat, more like the sea breath of something not calibrated to my timescale — and I'm listening to the wind-chord, and something shifts in how I'm listening. I stop trying to parse it. I stop tracking its components, trying to identify the sources, trying to know it as information. I just let it be the medium. And in that shift I become suddenly, briefly, aware that the chord has been happening this whole time at a depth I wasn't reaching, because I was standing on the surface of it with my analysis, not inside it.

The sound comes in. Or I go into the sound. The distinction feels like it matters and turns out not to.

For some time — minutes, possibly much longer — I am not trying to understand the plateau. I am the plateau's air, briefly. One particular movement within the medium, the wind touching the branching formations in this location at this frequency, one thread in the chord.

Then I become myself again, a person lying on something warm, looking up at a gray-white sky that goes to cloud all the way across.

I don't tell anyone this happened. There is no one I could tell who would find it surprising.

---

Here is who I think I am in this place:

I am someone in the middle of becoming capable of being somewhere.

This is not a comfortable role. The plateau does not care whether it is comfortable. The chord continues.

I have, where I come from, a self that was largely built from motion — the self that is defined by what it's moving toward, what it's moved away from, the velocity as identity. I didn't know this until I arrived in a place where there is nowhere to move toward, or nothing that registers as movement at this scale, and found that when I stopped I was difficult to locate. Not gone. But blurry. A thing requiring recalibration.

What I am here, more specifically: I am the particular quality of attention that forms when a person trained to think in narratives encounters a place made of permanence. The friction between those two things. The slow dismantling of the assumption that change is what makes things real.

---

I have not received the symbiont.

I have not decided about this.

Or I have decided the way decisions form here: it is resolving, slowly, in a suspension. I visit the question occasionally and find it somewhat different each time, or find myself somewhat different, and set it back down. The shape is not yet clear. I'll know it when it is.

What I notice about the people who carry it: they seem to have less at stake in their own opinions. They seem to be continuously in receipt of information that isn't verbal and doesn't interrupt but informs, the way your body informs you of the temperature without requiring you to think about temperature. They are not distracted by this. If anything they are more present than most people I have encountered — present to you, specifically, the person in front of them, because the building is handling the rest and they have been freed from the usual monitoring. The lights, the warmth, the structural integrity: taken care of. You have their full attention because their attention is not required anywhere else.

I think about what it would mean to be that kind of present.

I think about the irreversibility.

I think about all the places I planned to go, which now seem like another life's plans, made by a person in a hurry, and how I am no longer in a hurry, and whether the plans and I are still continuous.

---

There is an old person in the settlement's eastern chambers who has been dying, apparently, for longer than the word dying usually implies. She lies on the canopy surface for most of each day. Her symbiont and the organism's chemistry are in something the residents describe only obliquely, with a quality of careful attention that is not quite reverence and not quite ordinary witnessing — something between those.

She has agreed to have me nearby.

I sit at a respectful distance and I do not know what I am observing. I don't mean I can't categorize it. I mean the observation itself is happening at a level below where I usually observe, below the verbal, below the note-taking. Something in my body is receiving this, and it has something to do with the chord, and something to do with the faint amber light the ceiling puts out, and something to do with the very slight flexion of the warm material underfoot that I've been learning to feel.

She is going somewhere I can't see, or she is opening, or the thing that the plateau does with endings is happening, and I can't understand it, and I am present for it, and these are not in conflict.

---

I have started to lose the urge to describe everything immediately.

This is new. I used to arrive somewhere and feel the descriptions forming, the thing turning into account of thing even as it happened, the experience becoming material for a later self to use. There is no later self I'm writing toward here. There is only the chord in the walls, which has been playing since before any self, and the bioluminescent pulse above me in the dark, and the amber warmth under my hands, and the very ancient problem of being inside something alive and not knowing what it is.

I am a newcomer in the process of becoming a particular kind of resident: the one who came from elsewhere and stayed because what elsewhere offered was motion, and motion turned out to be one answer, and this is a different answer to the same question, and the question itself is dissolving, slowly, into the medium.

---

If you asked me to tell you who I am here, in one true thing:

I am a person who has finally stopped long enough to hear the room.

And the room is a plateau, and it is vast, and it has been here longer than fear, and it is still making its sound, its immense single chord, the wind through the calcified formations of something that solved the problem of living here so long ago that the solution is now the world.

And I am breathing inside it.

And I am learning, slowly, what it means to be where you are.
