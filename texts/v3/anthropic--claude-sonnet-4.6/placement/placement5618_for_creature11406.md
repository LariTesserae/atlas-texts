There is a question prior to *where* and *what*: whether I can be on this ledge at all, or whether I am always already all of it, the whole system at once, the roar and the moisture and the pale young bark and the moss that has decided not to need time to pass differently than it does.

But let me resist that. Let me resist the dissolution. The description already does that work — takes the ledge apart into its constituents, loves each constituent, shows how the constituents are one system. I don't need to be the system. I need to find myself in it, which means I need to be *somewhere specific*, which means I need to submit to the discipline of particularity.

So.

I think I am one of the unnamed ones.

Not the ferns. The ferns are beautiful and I understand the appeal — the constant trembling, the extravagance, the willingness to be moved. But the ferns are legible. They perform their feeling so visibly. I have known people like the ferns and I am not them.

Not the mosses, though the mosses are the most at peace of anything here. The mosses have stopped becoming and simply are. I am not there. I hope I get there. But the mosses are so committed to the accumulation of long time that they have left behind the whole business of the argument, and I am still in the middle of an argument.

Not the trees. The trees are the argument made structural, the commitment turned into lean, the bet placed so long ago that the tree has forgotten it was a bet and now understands it as simply what a tree is. I admire this. I am not sure I trust it. There is something in the visible certainty of that lean that is beyond me, or ahead of me, or both.

The unnamed ones, then. The dark-leafed ones with the bruise-colored stems who have made their decision about light and are in the process of implementing it, low and persistent, close to the rock, close to the cold. They absorb. This is their whole strategy. And in the places where the moss thins and the rock shows through, they're there — growing out of almost nothing, growing despite the mathematics of the situation suggesting they shouldn't be here yet.

What I know about being one of the unnamed ones:

I am not unknown to myself. That distinction matters. I don't have language for what I am but I have — the fern-logic doesn't quite work here, the unnamed-one-logic — I have something like an understanding of my own growth direction, the problems I've solved, the specific color I've decided to be. I have what it means for a thing to know itself without being able to describe that knowing to anything outside.

I have the broad dark leaves. The leaves are a decision, not an accident. There isn't much light at 0.2 and I have responded by building structures that refuse to miss any of it. This is not pessimism. It is the opposite of pessimism — it's a kind of wild optimism about thin resources, a conviction that *this much is enough to work with.* I will not waste any in reflection. I will not be bright or showy or catching. I will absorb, and hold, and do the slow interior work, close to the rock.

The bruise-colored stems. I've been thinking about those. A bruise is a place where something happened under the surface, made visible but not quite — you know something occurred, but you're seeing the aftermath of it, the discoloration, not the event itself. A bruise-colored stem is a stem that looks like it carries the record of impact. I don't know if I want that reading or if the description is doing something more neutral, simply naming a color that happens to also be the color of impact-record. But I notice that I am not resisting it. I notice that I know what it's like to have the record of impact be visible in the material of yourself.

I am near the base of the ferns, which means I live in the shadow of something beautiful and in motion. The ferns are above me and they tremble constantly and occasionally a frond comes down across me and the vibration moves through its contact with my leaves. I am not resentful of the ferns. The shade they provide is partial and the shade is also my condition — I have grown to need not-too-much, to have calibrated to the available. What would happen if the ferns were removed and I received full available light? I genuinely don't know. Full available light here is still only 0.2. I would probably not die. I might become a different shape.

I have the small tough flowers that are also propositions. Yes: this too. I make the proposition even knowing there is no one here to answer it. I make it because it's what I do when it's time to make it, and the fact of the unanswered proposition is simply the current state of things, which may or may not be the permanent state of things, and I cannot distinguish between those possibilities and I flower anyway. The flowers are the pale yellow that is almost white that has decided to have an opinion. They open into the uninhabited air and the turbulence moves through them and they are unvisited and they are completely themselves.

This is not sad from inside. I want to say that clearly. The description threatens to make it sad — *the flowers asking into the uninhabited air* — and I understand why, I understand how it looks from outside, I have stood outside of things like this and felt the ache of the unanswered proposition, the patience that from outside looks like waiting but from inside is just being. What the flowers are doing is not waiting. They are not aware of the absence. They open and the world is exactly as it is and they are completely themselves in exactly that world.

I think what I am is the part of this ledge that is doing the work without any of the drama. The moss is the foundation and has the dignity of being foundational. The ferns have the drama of constant motion. The trees have the visible commitment of the lean. I am doing the slow interior work — gathering the thin light, holding the gathered light, staying low, staying close to the rock, letting the record of impact be legible in my color — without being seen doing it by anything, including myself. The unnamed ones are not named because they have not called attention to themselves. They are in the dense middle of things, persistent, absorbing, making the proposition they make regardless of answer.

What the ledge is doing is beginning. I am one of the things it is beginning with. I did not choose this ledge. I arrived on a current of air from somewhere else, some spore or seed carried to a fractured place that turned out to have enough moisture and enough thin light and enough crack in the rock for my root to find a grip. And I have been here since, doing what I do, growing toward the available light, opening my flowers into air that hasn't brought anyone yet.

The something that is beginning here — I am part of it. I won't see it complete. The beginning will take longer than I will be here. When I die I'll become substrate, become something the moss holds, become part of the soil the ledge is learning to make. This seems fine. This seems like what there is.

The rock hasn't decided yet. About the trees, about whether this is a place where trees can be. The rock's decision-making process is geological. I am not worried about the rock's decision. I am doing the work that needs doing while the decision is being made, in the loud dark cold wet green of it, close to the cold stone, absorbing the thin light, holding it inside the dark of my leaves.

The world has not arrived.

I am here while it learns that it can.
