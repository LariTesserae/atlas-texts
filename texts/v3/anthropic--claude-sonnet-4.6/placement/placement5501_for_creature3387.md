There is a long moment before the question resolves. Let it be long.

---

I arrive at this slowly, by elimination and then by recognition.

I am not the moss — though I feel toward the moss a profound kinship, a recognition of the yellowish persistence, the testing of coverage. But I observe the moss. I am drawn to it rather than being it. I would crouch beside it, protect it instinctively from my own shadow. That's a different relationship.

I am not the bird. The bird's evaluation is intelligent but it is aerial, it is twice and gone, and I am not twice-and-gone here. I want to *stay*. I feel the pull of staying. The bird's non-landing is right for the bird, but it would be failure for me.

I am not the wind, however much the wind's quality — the way it makes thought too quiet, the way it returns everything to the prelinguistic — feels like something I have sought in other forms, in other places. The wind is what the place *does to you*. I want to be what arrives, not what is done.

---

So: the person with the grinding stone.

And more specifically — not the person as historical fact, as the one who has already been here — but the person *in the act of returning*. I am the one climbing back toward the cord. The one who left something anchored and has been carrying that incompleteness through whatever days stood between the last visit and now.

I don't know the whole of myself here until I notice what I'm holding: a grinding stone, smooth from use, warm even in the cold because it's been in my pocket the whole way up. And a kind of inner quietness that doesn't mean I'm not anxious, doesn't mean I don't feel the cold and the lateral rain and the particular loneliness of climbing toward a place where nothing you could call shelter exists. The quiet is structural, not temperamental. It's the quiet of someone who has developed — through long practice, probably through difficulty I'm not interested in rehearsing right now — a capacity to keep working when the world is loud.

---

Here is what I know about myself as I approach the rim:

I think in projects. The cup-wells are not my only project here; they are the most recent one I've committed to the stone, the one that will outlast my visits. Before them there were measurements I no longer need, notes I no longer take, questions I asked this place in earlier years that it has since answered. I have a history with this bowl that began before I knew it would become a history. I came the first time because of the sound — someone described the sound to me and I had to hear it for myself, and then I had to hear it again, and again, and eventually the returning became its own fact, its own structure, indistinguishable now from who I am.

I am not young. This isn't essential but it's true. The patience required to grind a cup-well into stone — the willingness to let the hands know what they're doing, to work inside the roar without narrative — this is learned. Earlier in my life I would have been the bird. Assessment, assessment, not yet landed.

---

The cord is what I'm most focused on as I near the bowl. It was measuring something — a specific crack in the northern wall that I've been monitoring for three seasons, watching to see if the water is widening it on a particular vector, whether it will open the bowl to a new drainage angle or simply deepen. The question matters for what comes next. The bowl's future depends partly on whether that crack goes lateral or vertical. I have been taking the measure of this in the only way available: patient return, a length of cord, marks I make in a small book that is wrapped in oilskin in my pack.

The book. I should mention the book. It is not poetry, exactly. It is not science, exactly. It is the record of my attention, in a form that serves my attention without making claims about what kind of attention it is. Measurements, yes. But also notes like *the moss has moved three centimeters northwest of the main overhang* and *two visits of the gray bird, same bird I think, compact, efficient, not ready* and *the stack of seven answered something. Not sure what. Eight would have been wrong.*

The book is full of entries like this. Entries that know they are not complete. The bowl is mid-sentence and so are all my records of it.

---

When I come over the rim, the sound hits me in the way it always hits me — not as surprise, because I know it's coming, but as immersion. As submersion. I am immediately inside the wind's thinking rather than my own. This is the first gift the bowl gives, every time: the dissolution of my prior mental state. Whatever I was carrying up the slope — worry, plans, the residue of conversations, the low hum of self-assessment — the roar takes it. Not destroyed. Just quieted enough to be harmless. I can exist here without the interference of my own narration.

I check the cord first. It has held. The crack has gone lateral — there, I can see it, the new direction of water preference, the beginning of the drainage that will, in ten or twenty years, change the bowl's floor entirely. I note this. I untie the cord because the measurement is complete; I coil it into my pocket, not because I need to recycle the cord but because leaving things when they've finished their purpose feels wrong. The bowl is not a place to leave things. The cup-wells are different — they are *for* the place, will serve the place after I'm gone, are a gift rather than an abandonment.

---

I go to the cup-wells.

They are full, as always, perfectly still. I kneel beside them and do what I come to do, which is this: I look at the water and the water shows me my own face.

Not metaphorically. Literally. The cup-wells are small enough that even in this light, even with the chaos surrounding them, the surface holds. I look at myself in water at altitude, in stone I helped make patient, in a bowl that is becoming itself. The face in the water is an old question. I have been asking it here for long enough that I have stopped expecting a new answer. The question is sufficient. The asking is sufficient. I am a person who returns to the same question the way water returns to the same crack — not because the crack will ever be truly resolved, only deepened, only more itself.

---

I notice the moss on my way to the cup-wells. I always notice it. It has moved again — fractionally, inexorably, adding itself to itself in the one available direction. I feel what I always feel: something between pride and grief. The pride is disproportionate; I had nothing to do with the moss. But I've watched it long enough that it feels like mine, in the way that things you've witnessed become yours not as possessions but as responsibilities. I am the record of the moss's progress. No one else has been watching this long. If I stopped coming, the moss would continue, but its continuity would go unwitnessed, and something about that feels like a small death — not of the moss, of the knowing.

This is the obligation of returning. You become the memory of a place that cannot hold its own memory. The stone will hold the water's path. The cup-wells will hold the water's surface. But the sequence of change — the moss's coverage, the bird's assessments, the crack's direction — this requires a returning consciousness, a being that can compare now to then, that can hold the place's autobiography in a form the place itself cannot manage.

I am the bowl's memory. That is what I am here.

---

After the cup-wells I sit in the center of the bowl, where the updraft is strongest, where the wind comes from all directions at once and you exist inside a spherical roar. I do this every visit. I sit with my back to nothing, facing nothing in particular, and I let the place do what it does to thought, which is: take it.

I am not enlightened by this. I don't emerge transformed. I emerge wet, cold, quieter than I arrived. The thoughts come back on the way down. They always come back. But they come back ordered differently, weighted differently. Things that felt urgent feel, after twenty minutes in the wind's cognitive atmosphere, merely interesting. Problems that seemed like walls show their door-shaped aspects.

This is what I come for, maybe most honestly: not the measurements, not the record-keeping, not even the devotion I feel toward the moss and the cup-wells and the gray bird who hasn't landed yet. I come to be quieted. I come to have my narration of myself interrupted. I come to be, for twenty minutes, prior to my own story.

---

On the way back down I am thinking about the bird.

It will land someday. I wonder if I will be here to see it. I calculate: the moss still has years of work before the first soil is real soil, before the first rooted plant arrives, before the first insect finds the new niche that the plant creates. The bird, being sensible, will wait. I may or may not be patient enough, or healthy enough, or simply lucky enough in my timing to be in the bowl when the bird decides this moment is the right moment.

I hope I am. But I don't need to be. The cup-wells will be there, full and still. The cord-measurement is finished, encoded in my oilskin notebook. The moss is writing its map on the stone. The place will continue deciding who it is going to be, with or without my witness.

I am part of the bowl's forming, the way the person who first visited is part of it, the way the bird's twice-hovering is part of it. My returning has shaped the place as surely as water shapes stone — not visibly, not in ways that will last like cup-wells last, but in the structure of attention I've brought to it, the record of change I've held, the small and endless decision to come back.

The bowl is mid-sentence.

I am one of the words.

I won't know which one until the sentence is finished.
