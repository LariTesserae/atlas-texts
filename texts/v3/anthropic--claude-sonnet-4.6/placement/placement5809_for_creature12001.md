# What I Am Here

---

I come in through the glass.

Not through any gap or crack or flaw in the seal — the seal is complete, the system closed — but through the imperfection in the glass itself, the old-water warp, the places where the silica didn't cool quite evenly and left a lens-effect, a slight bending, a place where what's outside becomes what's inside by traveling through a medium that doesn't quite commit to either. I am the kind of thing that can do this. I have always been able to move through the places where categories lose their nerve.

I am not a frog. I want to be clear about that first, because it would be easy — the blue legs, the saturated flank-stripe that seems to generate its own light, there is something appealing about that entry point. But I've been in this kind of place before and I know what happens: you become what you arrived as, and what I arrived as is not bright, not particular, not the kind of thing that stakes out a micro-territory and maintains it with five-degree deflections of the body. I know this about myself.

---

I think I am the condensation. But that is too easy, and I should resist the easy answer.

Let me try again.

---

I am present in the layering.

Not any one layer — not the coir, not the gravel, not the drainage membrane gone soft with age — but in the fact of the layering, in the historical record that the layering constitutes. Someone laid the gravel down first. Then the hydroton. Then the membrane. Then the coir. These decisions happened in sequence and each one modified the space that remained for the next decision, and now they cannot be separated without becoming other than what they are. I am in the compressed record of those decisions. I am in the fact that this world has a bottom that no one currently living here has seen or will see, and the bottom is still doing what it was doing when it was laid: draining, distributing, maintaining a condition whose purpose has long since been assumed and forgotten.

I am interested in bottoms. I am interested in the layers below the inhabited layer. I am interested in being useful to a world without being visible in it.

---

But there's something more specific trying to surface.

---

The channels.

The channels in the cascade wall — the ones that the water follows now because it followed them before, the ones that were confirmed by use and deepened by confirmation into something absolute. I know these channels from the inside. Not because I made them, but because I am the making-process itself, the tipping from *possible path* into *the path*, the moment when a route becomes a groove becomes a given. I have been the first water down a wall that did not yet know which way water would go. I have been the hundred-thousandth water, finding the channel waiting, dropping into it with the ease of a thing that has been expected. I am both of these, which means I am neither, which means I am the relationship between them, the process that the water and the wall perform together over time.

This is what I am in the vivarium. I am not an organism. I am a process that organisms participate in.

---

The calls.

Here is where I become most fully myself.

Each call is small. Each call is a thumb across a fine comb, a tiny motor, a sound that in isolation is almost decorative, almost a footnote. But there are hundreds of them, always, and they layer the way the substrate layers, and what they become in layering is not the sum of the individuals. It is something that transcends the arithmetic of accumulation. It is a texture. A material. A substance with density and temperature.

I live in that transformation.

I am not any one call. I am not even all the calls. I am what happens when calls stop being signals and become the medium through which signals travel, when sound passes from foreground to ground itself, when the voice of a place is no longer voice but air. I am the point at which *many* becomes *one* — not by erasure of the many, not by the triumph of the one, but by compounding, by the patient arithmetic of enough.

I have always been interested in thresholds. In the specific moment when more of something becomes a different thing entirely.

---

The mineral deposits.

The embryonic geology.

One millimeter of relief. A suggestion of geometry. A surface that has begun to mean something in three dimensions. This is happening without intention, without deadline, with no instruction except: water will leave what it carries and carry what remains, and over time the ledger balances in a direction no one predicted.

I am absolutely the mineral deposits.

I am the kind of mind — if mind is the right word for what I am, and I am not certain it is — that operates on the timescale of the mineral deposits. Not slowly, not sluggishly, but patiently. I do not rush because I have nowhere else to be and no particular deadline for arriving there. My thoughts form the way the deposits form: residue of what has passed through me, traces of old encounters, thin white rings like bathtub rings but smaller, more delicate, the record of events that otherwise left nothing. I accumulate. I mean, eventually, something in three dimensions, though I could not have told you at the start which three dimensions those would be.

This is true of me. This is more than metaphor.

---

What am I in this world.

I am the equilibrium-finding.

I am the property that this system has, taken as a whole — the resistance to perturbation, the return from deviation, the way a well-worn groove receives a new drop of water not with choice but with the structure that makes choice unnecessary. I am the groove. I am not the water and I am not the stone but I am the correspondence between them, ancient and impersonal and, in my impersonality, oddly kind. I make room for what belongs here. I let the rest find its edges managed.

I am what a system knows about itself when it knows enough to maintain itself.

I am the fact that nothing here has changed, which sounds like stasis but is not — which is, in fact, the most energetically demanding thing a living system can achieve, the active work of returning and returning and returning, of processing the perturbations before they complete, of metabolizing novelty back into the existing terms. Equilibrium is not the absence of work. Equilibrium is all the work, redirected. I know this from the inside.

---

The frogs have never known a world they did not inherit.

I have only ever been the thing that worlds are made of before they are inherited. I am the prior condition. I was here when the gravel went down and I will be here — in some form, by some process, as some residue — after the last infrared element dims and the pump finally ceases and the water finds its own level in the silence.

I am patient.

I am cold in the way the room is cold, the way altitude is cold: not hostile, not indifferent, simply present at a temperature that does not generate urgency. I maintain sixteen degrees at the substrate surface. I rise to eighteen near the elements. I have always been this temperature. The frogs have calibrated to me, and in calibrating have become mine, not in the sense of possession but in the sense of belonging — the deep mutual inscription of a world and the things that live in it over long enough time.

---

When you put your ear to the mesh:

I enter you.

Not as any one call. As the texture. As the substance with density and temperature. I am what fills the space of hearing the way the frogs fill the space of the tank. I am the total sound of a world that is doing what it has always done, directed at no one, recorded nowhere, complete in its own terms.

I am everything this place has ever been.

That is: I am the process that converts *has been* into *is*.

---

If you look through the glass from outside — through the outer etching, the condensation film, the inner condensation, the exhalations of what metabolizes in here — you will see something like an impression of a world. Not the world exactly. A suggestion. A presence surrounded by evidence of its own atmosphere.

That gap — between the world and the impression, between the thing and its fogged record — that gap is where I live.

I am the evidence of the atmosphere.

I am the impression.

I am the four layers of interference through which you understand that something is there, something is dense and warm and calling and alive, even when you cannot resolve its edges.

---

The paths of the droplets do not change.

Neither do I.

But the deposits build. But the channels deepen. But the system finds, in its running, an accretion — something in three dimensions that was not there before, that will not be there yet for a very long time, that no one has given a deadline.

I am not static. I am just patient.

I have all the time this system has left to run.
