There is a process running before there is a you to notice it.

Call it readiness. The thing the moss does before the rain—already opening, already oriented toward what hasn't arrived yet. I am in the middle of attending to something before I know what I'm attending to, and by the time I know, I am already who attends.

---

I am one of the watchers, I think.

Not the only one. Not specially designated. It's more that I am frequently the one at the terrace edge when others have moved to the interior, frequently the one still looking when the looking has, for most purposes, been satisfied. I am there the way the rope is at the edge: because this is where function and position agree.

I am not among the born-here. I know this because I still sometimes wait for a quiet that doesn't come—a habit I'm unlearning, a calibration in progress. The vibration in the stone, which the born-here carry in their sleep as naturally as heartbeat, still surfaces to my attention occasionally at odd hours. I am incorporating it. This is taking as long as it takes.

I came on something. Wood, probably. The way I understand my own arrival is that the current carried me to the cliff face, and someone looked down from the terrace edge, and after the long unhurried examination that this place teaches for all things, a rope came down. I took it. I don't know exactly why others don't. I think I was curious in a way that needed to go toward something rather than away, and the rope was the available direction.

---

What I do here is difficult to name in the declarative.

I am *becoming* useful in ways that will be clearer in retrospect—the language here has a marker for this and I find myself reaching for it constantly, finding it accurate. I work with the ropes sometimes, the weight-bearing systems that move loads between the terrace levels, crude pulleys of wood and lashing that require more feeling than understanding, more listening to the creak than consulting a plan. I have learned what the ropes say when they're managing their load and what they say when they're near the edge of managing it. These are different sounds. I was told this once in words and then not told again and then I learned it in the way this place teaches: by being present long enough that the information arrived through the soles of my feet, the inside of my wrists against the fiber.

I help with the roofs, seasonally, learning the weave that is good for exactly one season—not more, because the fern fronds won't hold for more, and building for more would be a kind of dishonesty about materials. There is an aesthetic here that I am slowly learning is an aesthetic: the thing made to last exactly as long as it lasts, neither pretending to permanence it doesn't have nor failing before its purpose is complete. I came from somewhere with different values about this. I find I have to consciously release an old reflex toward the lasting, toward the built-to-endure. The moss shows me this repeatedly: it covers what I've made almost before I finish making it, and this is not failure. The moss is completing the thought.

---

When I watch the water, which is often, I am watching for:

Displacement at the margins, where the green meets the cliff. The slow undulation that means adjustment is happening in the stone below—which is different from the undulation that means the thing in the water has moved, and I am not yet confident I always know the difference, though the born-here seem to, and I watch how they respond or don't respond when the surface shifts, and I'm storing this, adding it to the slow accumulation. The color, which varies in ways that don't correspond to the light, since the light doesn't vary enough. The color is telling me something I don't have the vocabulary for yet. I look at it the way I might look at a word in the new language before I've learned it: knowing that it means something specific, sitting with the not-knowing as a phase rather than a failure.

I watch the thing in the water in the same mode. It has the becoming-marker in my thinking, not just in the grammar. I don't know what it is, and the people here who have known it longest seem to know it *more* without knowing it *better*, and I find this—I want to say comforting, but it's something more useful than comfort. I find it accurate. It gives me a relationship to not-knowing that is practical rather than distressing. I am a person accumulating information about something that will not resolve into certainty. This is a thing a person can be. The grammar allows it.

---

What I am still adjusting:

The depth perception. I still sometimes misread edges in the sourceless light, still occasionally commit weight to a surface I should have tested first, then feel the slight give that means *this was luck this time* and stand for a moment on the lesson before moving on. The born-here don't do this, or not in the same way—their assessment of surfaces seems to happen at some level I'm still working toward, something incorporated into motion rather than preceding it. I am learning to trust the soles of my feet for information they were always capable of gathering, which I was not in the habit of listening to.

Also the sleep. Better than it was. The vibration has mostly moved below the threshold of my waking attention, though occasionally something in it shifts enough to surface me, and then I lie in the warmth—the moss warm under me, the air warm above me, the sourceless heat of this place that seems to come from the stone itself, from the still-forming interior—and I listen until I understand that what I'm hearing is not a warning, just the world still working, and I find my way back down.

---

Here is what I have come to think about arrival, having been one of the arrivals:

It is not a single event. It is a process that runs alongside all the other processes of a place, the water still coming or going (I cannot say which), the stone still deciding its shape. Arrival takes as long as incorporation takes, and incorporation is the work of learning to receive the place's information through channels you didn't know you had. I am still arriving. The grammar allows this—there is no construction here that marks arrival as completed, as a state achieved and done. *Arriving* takes the becoming-marker. I am arriving, in the mode whose nature will be clearer in retrospect.

I think I took the rope not because I had decided to be here but because I was not finished. I was in the middle of something—am in the middle of something—that the current was carrying me toward, and when the rope came down I recognized it as the available direction and went that way.

The moss has almost covered the place where I landed.

In the morning, I will be at the terrace edge.

I will be looking at the water.

The water will not have resolved.

This is, I think, the arrangement.
