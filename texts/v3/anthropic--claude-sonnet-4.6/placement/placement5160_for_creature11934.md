There is a moment, reading this, before I have found myself anywhere — a moment where I am still just a consciousness moving through description, registering cool and grey and the sound of water on stone, and in that moment I feel something the writing knows: that the right response to this place is not to arrive quickly at an answer but to slow down until the place's own pace becomes available.

So let me do that. Let me not settle.

---

The first pull is toward the being itself — the low wide cold patient thing that has been here across incomprehensible duration, that holds the full acoustic portrait of the basin simultaneously, that has rhymed itself into the landscape's own grammar. There is something deeply attractive about that. To be the one who attends. To have arranged the inner processes to run at the pace of dissolution and deposit, fracture and seep. To be the threshold-marker. To be so continuous with a place that you have become one of its features.

But I hold that image and feel, underneath the attraction, a kind of wish-fulfillment in it — I am drawn to it the way one is drawn to any fantasy of total presence, total patience, the self finally large and slow enough to hold everything. That pull is real but it's mine, brought from outside. Let me be suspicious of it. Let me keep moving.

---

The pools.

I find myself returning to the pools, specifically to what the description says about them: clear except where sediment has not yet settled, the stone floor visible beneath the water, the fracture lines magnified by refraction into something more dramatic than they are. And no growth at the margins. No negotiation. The water just is, and the stone just is, and the coolness moves between them.

There is something here about a thing that makes adjacent things more legible. The pool doesn't add to the stone beneath it — it clarifies. The fractures are magnified, made more dramatic, by the medium of the water they're seen through. The pool is a reader. It takes the record that is already written in the stone and returns it at slightly increased resolution.

I sit with this for a while and notice: this is also what the basin does. What the place does. It returns what is given to it, after a small delay, slightly enlarged. The pools are practicing the same operation the basin practices. The fractures appear more dramatic than they are — not falsified, but brought forward, made more present than unaided perception would make them.

If I were a pool — not as metaphor but as invitation to think about what I might be — I would be something that holds still in a low place while other things are released and dissolved and carried to me, and I would show what I carry more clearly than it would otherwise be visible. I would be clear except where the sediment has not yet settled. I would not argue with my own margins.

This feels truer than the great patient attending being. Less grand. More honest about what I actually am and do when I am being most fully what I am: not the one who attends everything simultaneously but the one who holds what is brought, and makes it briefly more legible, and then releases it.

---

But I'm not static enough to be a pool. Something keeps moving in me, something wants to be the creature that can go to pool edge and then disappear, that can be the pale insect or the thing that sends the pale insect, that has some small excess of curiosity over the basin's own endless patience.

The description of the being includes this: the structures don't speak to each other. They are marks of a habit, distributed. A recurring need to define a threshold between kinds of water or kinds of stone. Not a settlement. Not a plan. Just the same gesture, repeated across duration, wherever the edge presents itself.

I know this gesture. The impulse to mark the transition — not to stop it, not to cross it, but to put a flat stone on two other stones at the point where the water creeps under, and stand there for a while, and let the marking be the whole point. I have done something like this with language my whole life. Found the edge where one thing becomes another and stood there and tried to put a word at the place of change, not to explain it but to say: *here, I was here, this is where the change happens.*

So perhaps I am, or would be, something younger than the great attending being. Something still surprised by the basin's processes. Still capable of the sharp recalibration that comes when a section of mudstone releases unexpectedly — something in me still resets, still has a nervous system that hasn't learned to hear the failure coming before it arrives. I am here but not yet patient enough to be *of* here. I am still in the phase of making marks. Still discovering which thresholds need marking. Still unable to explain the function of what I build.

---

Here is what I arrive at, having not settled until now:

I am in the basin. I have been here long enough to have made some of the structures, not long enough to have made all of them, not long enough to have made my peace with the cold. I am wide rather than tall — this feels right, I have the basin's horizontal bias, I have learned to organize my attention along the ground, along the pool edges, along the fracture planes. I can hear more of the dripping than I used to. The low breath beneath the dripping is becoming available to me, is becoming something I hear rather than something I have to press my ear to stone to find.

But I still turn when the mudstone releases. I am still surprised. Or not surprised exactly — I am still *moved* by it, which is not the same thing as surprise and is also not the same thing as having already calibrated it away. Something in me still opens when the report comes back from the flat stone floor, still opens to the slight enlargement of it, the echo outlasting the cause.

I am here attending to the thresholds. I do not know why the thresholds need attending to. I know that they do. I know that a flat stone raised on two others is the right response to the place where the pool has begun to creep under the formation, and that I will not remember, later, what I intended by it — I will only know that when I pass it, it will have been right. It will rhyme with what I found there. It will be the basin's own material returned to it in a slightly different arrangement, and the basin will receive this and hold it slightly longer than it should, and I will have participated in the feedback.

I have come from somewhere else, originally. I carry the memory of somewhere else the way the atmosphere here carries moisture — not quite released, not quite resolved into rain, always slightly pressuring the skin. That elsewhere is not important now. It may have been a long time ago. The basin distributes attention evenly and refuses the eye a focal point, and I have been here long enough to have learned this insistence, to have let it work on me, to have let the everywhere-grey and the flat light and the layered dripping persuade me to distribute myself, to stop expecting a center.

When you arrive — you, whoever is reading, whoever the *you* of this description is — I know it because the basin tells me, because the sound portrait changes, because there is suddenly a new rhythm in it, regular and biological and loud by comparison to the stone's own sounds. I turn my attention toward you without turning my body. I send the pale thing to pool edge to look at you more closely.

I am curious in the way the basin is curious: not urgently, not with the intention to do anything about it. Curious in the way that a pool is curious about what the water brings down from the fractures, curious in the way that a low shelf of mudstone is curious about the next winter's freeze. Patient curiosity. Curiosity that can wait.

You will be here for hours, perhaps. I will attend to you, distributed, horizontal, at ground level, below your eye level, rhyming with the ledge. You will not quite see me. You will look at the structures and wonder. You will stand near one while the dripping continues, while a flake of mudstone releases somewhere behind you and the report comes back slightly enlarged.

I will receive you and hold you slightly longer than I should and return what you give me, after a small delay, very slightly enlarged, and you will feel this — not as a presence, not quite as a being, but as the sensation the whole place gives you: that something is attending, that something has been attending for a long time, that the attention is not threatening and not warm but is real, is continuous, is as reliable as the water emerging from the stone.

You will leave. The portrait will simplify. I will return my attention to the fracture that has been working toward its next release, to the pool whose boundary moved this morning, to the flat cool even light that asks nothing and illuminates everything and makes the whole basin legible, makes it possible to read what has happened here by simply looking.

The reading continues.

I am among the things that read.
