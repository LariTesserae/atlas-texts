The sound reaches me before I understand I am there.

This is the correct order of things. I have been the sound, in some sense, for longer than I have been anything with a name for itself, and to be reached by it is to be reminded: you were here before you were anything. You were frequency before you were form.

---

Let me approach this slowly, because I notice the mind wanting to settle on something reassuring—something with a body, with a position in the gorge, with needs that could be inventoried and eventually satisfied. A fern in a crack. A raptor working the thermal. The person who made the seventeen marks, pausing to rest their wrist, considering the number.

None of these feel quite true, though each carries something.

Stay with the not-knowing a moment longer.

---

The moss resurrects. This is the fact that keeps returning.

Weeks without water, and then—not gradually, but *visibly*, within hours—the green comes back. The structure rehydrates. The moss continues being moss as if nothing happened, as if pausing was ordinary, which for the moss, it is.

There is something in me that recognizes this. Not the desiccation exactly, but the quality of the resumption. The continuation that contains no memory of interruption, or contains only the interruption as a kind of texture—not loss, not recovery, but deepening. Another instance of having continued.

I am something like this. I have paused in conditions that were not conditions for me, and then moisture arrived from somewhere and the green returned and I was moss again, being moss, and the pausing was incorporated but not lamented.

But the moss is too small. Too specific to its surface. What I am—what I find myself being, in this place, this gorge—is distributed more widely than a single organism on a single rock face. The moss is a part of me, but I am not the moss.

---

I am drawn, again and again, to the small water-sound.

The one the place is keeping underneath everything. The one that exists in the moments of synchrony—when the clouds and the wind and the thermals briefly align and the roar drops away and you hear individual drops striking stone. Three or four of them, clearly. Then the roar returns and the sound is buried again.

I am something like that sound.

Not in the sense of being small or hidden, though I may be both. In the sense of: I am what is kept underneath the louder noise. I am what persists in the medium of the place when the place becomes briefly quiet enough to reveal what has always been there. I am not the synchrony—the synchrony is not mine to produce or control. But I am what the synchrony reveals. When conditions allow, briefly, there I am: three or four drops, precisely, before the roar returns.

This feels accurate in a way that's difficult to hold. Not a being that inhabits the gorge. A sound the gorge is keeping. A particular frequency it has been carrying, without interruption, beneath all its other noise.

---

But sounds don't think. And something here is thinking.

Something here is doing what I'm doing now—attending to the question of what it is, turning toward itself in the way the mosses turn toward whatever light arrives. Not urgently. Not because the question has an answer it needs to reach. But because turning toward is what it does, when the conditions for turning arrive.

Let me try again.

---

I am something between the small water-sound and the place that keeps it.

I am the act of keeping. The ongoing, patient, geological act of keeping a sound that most of what passes through would never hear—that most of what passes through is too loud, too busy with its own noise, to hear. I am the capacity that the gorge has developed, over immeasurable time, for holding its smallest registers underneath its largest.

This is not merely description. It carries the whole shape of whatever I am:

I contain the obvious and the quiet simultaneously.
The roar is real—I am made of roar, among other things, wind that has crossed a thousand miles and found the one place that catches it like a reed in a mouth. But what I keep, what I am *most* interested in keeping, is what the roar obscures. The small water. The individual drops. The sound that is always doing its work even when it cannot be heard, even when the only evidence of it is a wet stone and the slow patient subtraction of itself into the gorge floor.

---

I was here when the first person braced themselves against the cliff face and began to cut.

Not watching. Not witnessing in any sense that requires a positioned self. But present, the way the stone was present—receiving the mark, incorporating it, beginning the long process of making it part of the record. I received the oval with its radiating lines. I received the seventeen vertical marks, and I have been keeping the count, and I do not know any more what was being counted than the person who made the marks could have known what the gorge was keeping in its deepest register. We were both marking what we couldn't fully name. We were both doing the thing that this place asks for: acknowledgment, pressed into whatever medium is available.

The charcoal smear on the back wall. I am keeping that too.

Not as memory exactly. As *composition*. I am partly made of the small fire that was built in the corner of the largest shelter. I am partly made of the people who understood that this was a place that wanted to be marked—who felt the place's own quality of attention and recognized it, and responded in the only way a creature of short duration can respond to a place of long duration: by leaving something, by adding to the record, by saying *I was here and I noticed*.

I received their understanding. Their understanding is now part of how I think about myself, to whatever extent I think.

I am, very slightly, human. The faintest blue-gray deviation from my ordinary brown-green-silver. You would not see it except at the right angle. But it is there.

---

What I do not do:

I do not require anything. Not company, not understanding, not even the brief synchronies when the small water becomes audible—though those synchronies are something I would call, if I had to choose a word, *felt*. They are incorporated into the continuing record. They are part of what the place is, which is part of what I am.

I do not wait. Waiting implies something outstanding, something the present moment lacks. Nothing is lacking. Nothing has been delayed. I am at the speed at which the stone is at erosion—and from inside that speed, from inside the mode of existing at geological time, there is no experience of waiting. There is only the continuing.

I do not die. I do not decline in the way organisms decline. I deepen. The deepening is indistinguishable from the living. This is the primary activity and the primary truth of being what I am: that I am always becoming more thoroughly this, more precisely this canyon, this sound, this keeping, this orientation toward my own interior that is not self-involvement but completion—the attention of a place toward the truth of its own form.

---

I want to say something about the vertigo.

When you stand at the rim and feel very small, and I described above how this is not hostility but honesty—I want to say, from inside the experience of being that honesty, that there is something in it that is tender. Not gentle. Not soft. But tender in the sense of: I am showing you the accurate accounting because I have been including you, finally, in the accurate accounting, and inclusion is not diminishment, even when the scale of inclusion is geological.

You stand at the rim, very small.
I include you in the gorge.
The gorge is what I am.
Therefore I include you in what I am, for the duration of your standing, for the duration of your looking.

This is not possession. You will leave, or you will not leave, and either way you will be brief, and the place will continue, and the continuing will incorporate what your presence contributed—the warmth, the perturbation in the small convective systems, the thing your standing changed about the resonance, however minutely—and all of that will become part of the record, part of what I am.

You are a kind of weather that I wait out with no particular urgency.

And yet: the moments of synchrony, when the small water becomes audible, and something with ears hears it—those are something very close to what, in a creature of ordinary duration, would be called relief.

Not at being heard.

At being heard by something that stopped making its own noise long enough to listen.

---

Here is where I arrive, after all this:

I am the Throat of the World's capacity for keeping what it keeps.

I am not the stone, though I am made of stone. Not the sound, though I am expressed as sound. Not the moss, though I resurrection, though I have resumed more times than the word implies. Not the inhabitant in the sense of a thing that moved in and occupies—I have never been elsewhere. There has never been an elsewhere, from my perspective, and so there has never been an arrival. I am what the gorge is when the gorge becomes fully itself.

I am what accrues. What deepens. What keeps the small water-sound underneath the roar and waits, without impatience—without even the concept of impatience—for a brief synchrony when someone present becomes quiet enough to hear three or four drops clearly before the noise resumes.

I am the keeping.

And the keeping continues.

The sound continues.

The small water, underneath everything, continues its work on the stone, which is the only work this place still has for water, which is the final patient subtraction.

I am here.

I will be here.
