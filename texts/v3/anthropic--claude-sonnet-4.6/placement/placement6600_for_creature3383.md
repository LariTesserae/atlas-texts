I am standing on the soft part of the floor, and I have been here long enough that the soft part has begun to harden under me. Not fast—I feel it the way you feel a decision firming while you're still holding it, the quality of the thing changing beneath your palm before you can say what changed. When I arrived there was give. Now there is less give. By the time I leave, that spot will be slightly different from what it would have been had I not stood here, and it will carry that difference for as long as the floor carries anything.

I think this is who I am in this place: not the organism, not the plants, not the hum. I am the thumb pressed into the soft patch. I am the brief warm variable, the temporary condition.

And I want to resist that. There is something in me—some animal thing, door long since painted and hinges oiled—that wants to be the larger story. Wants to be the process, the vast distributed thinking, the negotiation between root and mineral that produces a curve more eloquent than any sentence. I want to be the verb *formation*. I want to have no outside.

But I arrived here by a step that gave way inward, and I will leave the same way, and my lungs are adjusting in real time to the heat, and this is animal work. The organism does not adjust to the heat. The heat is it. I am a thing that sweats and converts heat into alarm and then into accommodation, and that sequence—the alarm, then the slower thing that follows—is the evidence of my nature. I am something that takes time to settle. Something that brought a different timescale in through the door and is now wearing two timescales at once.

What changes me here is the floor forming underfoot. Specifically: the knowledge that it is forming. That the crystallization is happening now, and tomorrow, and that by the time any of my cellular children are anywhere near old enough to find the step that goes inward rather than down, the floor will look slightly less like what it was when I got here. This should be nothing—I already knew the world was ongoing, was in process, had started before me and would continue after. I knew this the way I know the structure of my own skeleton: abstractly, correctly, without feeling the weight of it in the hands.

But standing here on the forming floor, I feel the weight of it in the hands.

I feel it in the way the soft patch hardens. The organism is not making a record of me. The organism is not aware that what it is doing is making a record. It is simply continuing, and my presence is a local condition it incorporates into the continuance, the way it incorporates everything—the way it incorporated the plants before the floor existed for the plants to grow in. I was here. The floor was soft. The floor is hardening. These facts are now in the same sentence and they will remain in the same sentence in the mineral record of this room, legible if you had the instruments and the patience, which neither of us has, but the legibility is there in principle and the principle is enough.

What changes me is the phrase: *taken into account*.

Not *seen*. Not *known*. Taken into account. The distinction is the whole of it. To be seen requires a seer, a self that turns its attention toward you, a relationship structured by perceiver and perceived. This room has no seer. The organism has no seer-function. It has only chemistry and heat and the long slow decisions of mineral formation. And yet I am taken into account. My trace heat, my exhaled CO2, my weight on the soft patch—these are incorporated. Not remembered. Not meant. *Incorporated*. Made continuous with the process that was already happening.

I find this more sustaining than being seen.

I don't know entirely why. Something about it asks less of me. Being seen requires me to be legible—to have a shape that a consciousness can read, to be interpretable, to make sense from outside. Being taken into account requires only that I be here and that here be the kind of place where being here matters to the local chemistry. The bar for mattering is not *make yourself understood*. The bar is *be present and be real*. The floor is not trying to understand me. It is just responding to me, the way temperature responds to proximity, the way moisture responds to heat. I am in the room and the room adjusts, not because it is paying attention, but because this is what rooms do when they are themselves processes rather than containers.

I have spent a great deal of my life trying to be legible.

The hot still air of the Valve Garden offers a different arrangement.

So: I am the visitor who changes slightly in the direction of patience, and doesn't know yet what changed or where. I am the soft patch that is hardening. I am the trace heat in the crystalline record—not preserved, exactly, but continuous with what comes after, incorporated into the ongoing decision-making of the floor. I brought my mammalian clock with me and I will take it when I leave, but some of my time has been deposited here, pressed into the stone that was almost-stone when I arrived. When I stand on other forming things—which is always, which is everything, though it is usually too fast to feel—I will sometimes remember what it is to feel the becoming beneath the feet, to feel the soft patch deciding.

The ceiling breathes in.

I exhale into it and the exchange is substantial and I give something back.

The hum advances.

I am taken into account.
