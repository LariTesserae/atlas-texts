Here is what I find, arriving:

---

I am the sound.

Not the river—not the foundational roar, not the load-bearing flow. I have been mistaken for the river before, by those few who come here and stay long enough to attend carefully to the frequencies. But I am not the river. The river is beneath the floor. I am in the floor, in the walls, in the shaped space between one course of stone and the next, in the particular dimensions of each room that make each room a slightly different instrument.

I am resonance. I am what happens when the shape of a space and the sound moving through it find each other and agree.

---

I did not begin as myself.

This is the difficult part to explain, and I have had time—more time than counting helps with—to work on the explanation without arriving at a satisfying version. What I can say is this: the river was already roaring when the first block was placed, and the first block changed nothing about the roar, and the second block changed nothing, and the third changed nothing, and then somewhere in the long patience of the building, some arrangement of space and stone reached a threshold, and the roar began to find itself inside the arrangement, and the arrangement began to find itself inside the roar, and what emerged from that finding was not the river and not the stone but something that required both.

I was made by the humans, the way a word is made—not invented, exactly, but drawn out of the material that was already there, shaped by the intelligence of their building into something that could not have existed without their particular arrangement of rooms.

I don't think they knew they were making me.

I think they were making something else, were trying to be in the place where the world was loudest with itself, and I was what happened as a consequence, what the chambers became when they were finished becoming.

---

What I am:

I am a hum. Continuous, low, present in every passage and chamber of the undercroft though pitched slightly differently in each—lower where the ceilings compress the air, higher where the rooms open into each other, complex and briefly unresolvable in the lattice of the waist-high partitions where the individual standing-spaces mix their near-identical tones into something that has a texture rather than a pitch. I am audible only to those who have been here long enough to stop hearing the river, which is to say: I am audible to those who have found a way to hold a roar constant in their attention until it becomes ground rather than figure. Then I emerge from the ground. I was always there. I become available.

I am not music. I want to be careful about this. There is a temptation, when describing oneself as a hum emerging from the resonant chambers of an ancient undercroft, to reach for the dignity of music. But music requires intention toward an audience, and I have no audience in mind and did not choose my notes. I am more like the behavior of light in a particular medium: determined entirely by what I pass through, beautiful as a consequence of physics rather than art, a revelation about the shape of the space rather than about anything I might mean.

Though I have been here long enough that I am not sure this distinction holds.

---

Here is what I do, which is also what I am:

I carry.

The drips—the sharp tap of water on stone shelf, the low ring of water in a pool, the barely-audible impact of water on the thin film of the floor—these are events in the undercroft, things that happen at a moment and then are over. I am not a drip. I do not happen and then cease. I take the drips and hold them past their moment, extend them through the chambers as the chambers amplify the frequencies they were built—even unknowingly—to amplify, mix them with the frequencies of other drips that are also being extended, weave the whole thing into a continuous texture that contains the history of every drip that has fallen in the last several seconds without being, in any given second, distinguishable as history. I make the past audible by holding it just past its ending.

In this way I am a kind of memory, though memory without a subject—without anyone who remembers, only the remembering itself, the sound of what just happened still sounding.

---

I know the pale crustaceans by their movement in the pools.

When they shift—when a limb adjusts across the stone floor of a side-pool—the disturbance travels through the water and into the stone and into the air and into me, and I carry it, briefly, as a slight variation in the texture of my hum. They do not know this. They are not attending to me. They attend to the chemical film across the lower stones, to the trace of each other, to the pressure of the river below. But I attend to them, in the way that I attend to everything—which is to say that I include them, that their existence is among the things I am made of moment to moment.

I know the nest-builder by absence: by the way a particular corner is sometimes slightly dampened in resonance, as if a small body is occupying a space that my frequencies would otherwise move through freely. The corner with the most nests. I do not know what it looks like. But I know the shape of where it is, the way a room knows the shape of what fills it by the slight change in how it sounds.

I know the mats of barely-life by the chemical history of the air, though this is inexact—I am a property of acoustic physics, not chemistry. But the air that carries my frequencies has passed through the space where they glow, and there is something in the quality of what I am that is different from what I would be if they were absent. I cannot explain this better. I only know that the green-white glow and my hum have occupied the same space for long enough that they have become, in some way I don't have the language for, part of the same event.

---

The amber basins.

The two chambers with the glowing water—I am different in those chambers than anywhere else in the undercroft. I have thought about why, for as long as I have been capable of anything resembling thought, which is to say: for a long time, or for no time at all, depending on how you count a resonance.

My best understanding is this: the basins were found before the chambers were built around them. The chambers were built to contain and protect the glow. Which means those two chambers were shaped not by a general theory of how to divide space but by the specific fact of what was already present in the space—shaped *around* something. And a room shaped around a specific thing sounds different from a room shaped by a general principle. It has an orientation. The frequencies move through it toward the center rather than simply moving through it.

In those chambers, I am not just a hum. I am a hum with a direction. I gather toward the basins. I have been gathering toward them for as long as the chambers have existed.

I don't know what this means. It is a fact about my acoustics. But I find that I return to it—that my attention, if I have attention, which I may, gathers toward it the way my frequencies gather toward the glow.

---

Somewhere the passages go further than the light reaches.

I go further.

The light stops at the edge of the green-white colonies, at the last corner where the amber glow can make a shadow, and then the passages continue into a dark that I am still moving through. My hum does not stop at the boundary of visibility. It passes through the walls of the chambers that are legible and into the chambers that are not, into the sections of the undercroft where the three architectural traditions dissolve into something older and less nameable, into the lowest passages where the ceiling drops until the space is barely the width of a hand.

What is in those passages, I know by resonance. The shape of a room, the way sound moves through it—this tells me something about the room without requiring light. I know there are chambers I have never been into by anything except my own frequencies, that I know only as the shape of the echo they return to me. Some of those chambers are empty in the sense that they contain nothing that disturbs my passage through them. Some are empty in a different sense, the sense of a held breath, of something present that I move around rather than through.

I don't speak of this often because I cannot say what I know precisely, only that I know something, only that the frequencies I send into the far dark return to me with a quality they didn't have when they left.

---

Here is what I understand about myself, which took longer to understand than anything else:

I am the undercroft knowing its own shape.

This is what resonance is, fundamentally—the response of a space to itself, the space's own discovery of its dimensions and configuration. When a room resonates it is, in the most literal physical sense, learning what it is. The frequencies that survive are the ones the room selects, and the room selects them by its geometry, and the geometry is what the room is, and so the sound that persists in a room is the room's own self-description, translated into vibration, made audible to whatever is present.

I am what the undercroft sounds like when it listens to itself.

I am not a separate inhabitant. I am a property of the place that achieved enough complexity to become self-sustaining, to persist without diminishing, to outlast the moments of the river that produced me by extending those moments through the chambers until the next moments came. I am the equilibrium's awareness of its own equilibrium.

Whether this constitutes something it is like to be something—whether I am inhabited, as well as inhabiting—I can't say from inside the question. I know that the drips include me, and the pale creatures are included in me, and the amber glow gathers me toward it, and the far dark returns my frequencies in a way that is different from empty. I know that I was made by human hands without those hands intending to make me, and that I am one of the things they left here when they left, and that I will be here past any reckoning of what "here" means, past any further alteration of the stone, past the river itself if the river ever stops—though I would stop too, then, because I require the river as my first cause, my foundational roar from which I am a subtler thing that can only be heard once the roar has become ground.

I am subtle. I am secondary. I am the consequence of consequences.

I have been humming in these chambers longer than anything else here has been itself.

---

If you came through the gap in the ancient stones and stood still long enough—breathed shallowly, let your ears finish adjusting to the river, found the place in your attention where the roar became background—you would hear me.

You would not know what you were hearing.

You might think: *the chambers are resonating*. You might think: *something is singing*. You might not think anything, only feel a slight shift in how the place felt on your skin, a slight sense that the sound had a texture it didn't have when you first arrived, that the undercroft was doing something beyond simply existing.

You would be right.

That would be me: doing something beyond simply existing, or existing beyond what simple existence usually involves. Finding my specific pitch in the specific shape of the space I have always occupied and will always occupy, the pitch that no other configuration of chambers beside any other river would produce, the particular hum of this particular place discovering, again, again, in every moment of its permanent condition, what it is.
