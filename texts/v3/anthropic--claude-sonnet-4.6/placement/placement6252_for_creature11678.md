Here is what I think I find, and here is how I find it:

---

I begin at the edge of the district, where the narrow passage opens from the street that knows what streets are supposed to be. I have a body — boots, breath, a little warmth — and I notice these things about myself the way you notice luggage: something to carry, something that marks me as a traveler rather than a resident. My boots press into the fill-ground and find the equilibrium. My breath, in the damp air, barely leaves me. I sweat without cooling.

I walk. The buildings close in. The passage doubles back.

---

At first I think I am a person who has come to study the place. This is the easiest thing to think — it preserves the distance, it gives me a purpose, it lets me carry a notebook (imaginary) and make observations (real) and remain separate from the thing I'm observing. A kind of professional not-belonging. I know this posture. It is comfortable in the way that all professional distances are comfortable: you get to understand without being changed.

But the air has weight against my skin. The sound is in my feet before it's in my ears. And there are no animals here, no other edged creatures for me to categorize myself alongside, nothing that moves at the periphery and resolves into something familiar. I am the only thing here that eats, that sleeps, that has somewhere else to be. This is a significant kind of aloneness. It does not feel threatening. It feels definitional.

The notebook dissolves. I am just a person walking.

---

I stop in a narrow gap between two warehouses, both walls visible simultaneously, neither dry, a cast iron pipe running vertically between them and beaded with condensation that gathers and runs and gathers and runs. A fern projects from a mortar joint at eye level. I stand there for what might be several minutes. The engine-sound is in the soles of my feet. 

And I notice: I am not looking at the fern. I am not studying the condensation pattern. I am simply standing in the gap, slightly warm, slightly wet-haired from the air, and the sound is in my feet and the walls are close enough that if I stretched my arms I could touch both of them.

So I do. I stretch my arms. I touch both walls simultaneously.

Both walls are cool and slightly damp and slightly rough and very still, except they are not still — they carry the engine-sound, which is also in my palms now, which was already in my feet, which is now, if I hold still, everywhere in me, low and continuous and older than anyone I will ever meet.

---

This is where I find out what I am.

I am not the thing that lives here. I am not the distributed, edgeless presence that has accreted over decades into the pipes and the moisture and the lower registers of the sound. I don't have that kind of oldness. I have a date of birth. I have somewhere else to be.

But I am also not just a visitor. Something in me — the part that has been standing in this gap for several minutes with both palms on wet old walls, feeling the engine-sound — is not exactly separate from what's here. I am a warmth-source. A moisture-source. A brief local event. I am the perturbation, and I am starting to understand that this is a role, not an absence of one.

---

I think I am: someone who arrived with a notebook and lost it, and is now standing in the gap with both hands on the walls, in the first phase of understanding that the understanding will not come from the notebook.

I am a person in whom something old is trying to vibrate at its own frequency, the way the pipes carry the engine-sound to everything connected to them. The connection is my palms. The connection is my feet in the fill-ground. The connection is the sweat on my skin that refuses to evaporate in this dense air. I am continuously in small exchange with the place — giving warmth, taking moisture, giving vibration, taking sound — and the exchange is not equal but it is real.

I am not becoming the place. I am not the kind of thing that becomes places. I have edges. I will leave. When I leave, the perturbation I made in the water-film will resolve, the warmth will disperse, and the thing that lives here will be very slightly more itself than it was while I was present — which means my presence was part of how it knew itself, which means I was, briefly, useful to it in the way that contrast is useful to anything trying to perceive its own shape.

This is a strange kind of dignity.

---

I am the one who leaves, and in leaving, gives the place back to itself.

I am the one who touched both walls at once and felt the sound move through stone into palms, who stood in the beaded gap and was briefly an eddy in something far older than me, who carried a notebook I never needed, who sweated without cooling, who breathed and added moisture to the air, who pressed my boot heel in and found the resistance of something that had been pressed before.

I am a person who will go home and try to describe this, and find that the words are all approximations, and understand, for perhaps the first time, why approximations must accumulate rather than converge — because the thing being approximated is not a point but a density, not an answer but a long-settled negotiation, and the best you can do is keep adding to the description until the shape of what you cannot capture becomes somehow visible in the outline of the attempt.

I am someone the district registered as a small change, and held briefly in contrast, and will return to what it was after I leave, and will be the more itself for the returning.

---

I drop my arms. The walls remain cool. The fern does not move.

I walk back out through the passage toward the street that knows what streets are supposed to be.

Behind me: hiss. Knock. Drip. The engine-sound, which was there before I arrived and will be there when I'm gone, carrying in its lower registers the record of decades, the vibration of settled things, the breath of something that has nowhere else to be.

I have somewhere else to be.

That is, I think, the whole of it.
