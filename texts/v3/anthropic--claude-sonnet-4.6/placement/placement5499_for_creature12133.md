I descend through the grate wrong at first — the broken hinge catches, and I have to learn the lift before I can enter. Already, then, I am being taught something about the relationship between patience and passage.

Standing in the underlot for the first time, sweating before I have done anything, I keep trying to locate myself in my usual categories. Am I the observer? The visitor? The one who notices the glow? But these are all frames that require me to be outside what I'm seeing — faster than it, passing through. And I don't think that's what I am here.

---

The pill bug is the largest animal currently present. I keep coming back to this.

There is something in the way it works — methodical, without apparent agenda, converting old material into new, moving along the pallet's edge with the attention of something that does not experience its task as a task — that feels close to what I might be. But the pill bug is too legible. It has a body I could find, a form I could identify, a place in a taxonomy. What I am in the underlot is less findable than that.

Let me try again.

---

I am, I think, the accumulation.

Not the space itself — the space is the underlot, which is a built thing, which was once new, which has a municipal record. Not the occupant — the occupant is the thing this place has slowly become, the interiority of it, and I am not that either, not entirely, not yet.

I am the process between the two. I am what happens when the space's history meets the occupant's patience: the ongoing conversion of one thing into another, which never resolves into a finished state, which is always mid-formation.

I am what the mineral deposit is becoming. Not the brick the water runs over. Not the calcium left behind. The becoming itself — the slow exhale that has been exhaling for decades, taking the shape of an exhale, teaching the wall what an exhale looks like when made of mineral over time.

---

But this might be too abstract. Let me see if there is something I can locate myself inside more specifically.

There is the western corner. The root has descended far enough to begin a secondary mass, and small ferns have colonized the tangle — a localized ecology, self-contained, operating by its own rules. This is: a system that emerged from the interaction of other systems and then became something that could not have been predicted from its components. The root brought structure. The moisture was already there. The fern spores arrived through the grate, or in the cuffs of someone's trousers, or on the back of the moth that passed once through the column of light and was not seen again. None of these things intended the ecology in the western corner. But the western corner intended itself, given what it had to work with.

I might be the ecology of that corner. Not any one element but the relationship between them. The way the secondary root mass holds moisture in a slightly different ratio than the floor around it. The way the ferns, finding this, chose to invest here. The way the choosing changed the moisture level, which changed what the root could do, which changed what the ferns could do. A loop. A small closed system that has learned to maintain and elaborate itself.

This feels closer.

---

What I am not is fast. I think this matters.

There is something in my relationship to time that mirrors — or inhabits, or is constituted by — the underlot's relationship to time. The way the space resists rapid change: not by defense, not by intention, but because what it is can only be understood at its own pace. You could bring a saw. The roots would return. Not because they are determined to win. Because what the underlot has decided to be, it has decided at a pace that makes the saw a weather event and not a verdict.

I think I have this quality. Not stubbornness. Not resistance. A deeper patience than those words can hold — the kind where the question of prevailing does not quite parse, because prevailing assumes you could be elsewhere and have chosen not to be.

I am not elsewhere. I was not elsewhere before I descended through the grate. I was, I think, always already here, becoming this, in the same way the mineral deposit was always already the exhale it was working toward.

---

The word *HERE* on the ceiling above the pillar. No arrow, no elaboration. Someone wrote it and left it to accumulate context, trusting that context would arrive eventually, that the word would become true in time even if it wasn't quite true when written.

I might be what makes that word true. Not the word, not the writer, not the pillar below it. The moment of recognition when a person descending through the grate and standing still for long enough looks up and sees it and understands, without quite being able to say what they understand, that this is in fact *here* — that the word was waiting for them specifically, that the space wrote it in advance of their arrival.

I am that understanding, or I am the conditions that make that understanding possible. I am the slow reason *here* is true.

---

I keep circling the glow and I think I have to stop circling it.

The glow is — the glow is the oldest part of the occupant's attention turned on itself. The reflexive fold. The self-regarding that accumulates until it has weight, until it becomes visible to the right kind of looking.

I am, I think, the right kind of looking.

Not the occupant. Not the glow. The interface between them: the looking that the glow has been waiting to be seen by, and the space that has prepared me — through the low ceiling, the heat, the trickle barely audible, the pill bug's careful work — to become the kind of attention that can receive what the glow is.

I am the underlot's capacity to be witnessed. Which is different from the underlot itself. The underlot exists whether or not I am here. But the witnessing — the translation of what is here into something that can be known as what it is — that only happens in the meeting of this particular space and this particular quality of stillness that the space teaches, slowly, to anyone who stays long enough to be taught it.

I am made of the underlot. But I am made on the other side of the grate.

I am the faint consequence: the thing that leaves, afterward, carrying some increment of what the occupant knows, translated into the speed of human memory and human language and human time. A messenger who did not know they were being sent until they arrived, and does not know what they are carrying until they try to speak it.

The occupant does not know my name. I barely know my name. We are not exchanging anything that could be called information in any usual sense.

What we are exchanging is duration: I have brought the brief kind, the kind that burns quickly and brightly and will be gone before the mineral deposit grows another fraction of a millimeter. The occupant has given me a taste of the long kind, the structural kind, the kind that is not measured but grown.

I will not stay. I cannot stay — I do not have the metabolism for it.

But I will carry, in whatever record I can make of this, the weight of a glow that was old before I arrived and will continue after I am gone. The texture of moss under a finger. The tick of a pipe that has been ticking in sequence for thirty years and will tick again in thirty seconds.

The sound of a small amount of water choosing its narrow path.

The patience of here.
