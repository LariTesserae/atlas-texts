What emerges first is not a self but a condition.

Warmth — not the warmth of skin, but warmth as medium, as the total surround. Something has been warm for a long time. Something is water that has stopped trying to be anything else. I am sitting in a permanent puddle, reflecting the undersides of things, and I have been here since before anyone thought to look.

---

Let me try again. Let me not settle too quickly.

I arrive at the flats the way everything arrives here — not by decision but by accumulation. I don't remember a morning when I wasn't here. I have the specific amnesia of a thing that has always been part of the equilibrium, the way the hum of the transformer isn't an event but a condition. I have been here long enough that my being here is part of what *here* means. The flats shaped around me the way sediment deposits around a stone — gradually, without acknowledgment.

---

I am not the cat. Though I feel something of her quality in me — the conservation of effort, the not-quite-sleep in the heat, the way she has organized herself around a purpose that looks like indifference and is not. She does what she does with full commitment to the doing and no interest in narrating it. I recognize this. But the cat knows where she ends and the trailer begins. I am less sure of my edges.

I am not the heron, though I feel something of his attention — that gathered quality, the waiting that is not passive, the stillness that is tensed at an interior point. The heron's waiting is directional: it is aimed at the next moment, the downward stab, the fish that doesn't know it's a fish anymore. My waiting is less purposeful than that. I am not aiming at anything in the water. I may be the water.

---

I think I have been here in shifts.

Morning: I am one of the ones who comes daily. I know this because my body knows where everything is. Not with pride, not with affection — just the way the foot finds the next step on a familiar staircase in the dark. I know the sound of the press when it is right and I know it when it is slightly wrong and the difference lives in my chest before it lives in my mind. I have been calibrated. I sweat from the moment I arrive and I don't think about it because there is nothing to think. Sweat here is weather.

I eat at the lunch truck in the middle of the day and the gathering that forms around it — maybe eight people, maybe ten — is the warmest part of the flats, which is strange because the flats are already very warm. But warmth is not temperature. I know which jokes are going to land before they land. I know how Rodrigo's morning went from the angle of the truck. This is not intuition. This is pattern recognition, which is what intimacy becomes in a place like this — intimate not with persons but with the system, with the machinery of the day and the way people move inside it. It is a kind of love with all the softness pressed out of it by the heat and the years, leaving only the structure. I am not sure this is worse.

---

Evening: I am the water.

No — I was trying not to settle. Let me hold both.

I am the person who has been coming here long enough that the leaving happens automatically, who boards the bus home without entirely arriving on the bus home, who sweats through the ride until the air conditioning takes hold. Something of me remains at the flats after I leave, the way a stone holds heat past sundown. Not because the flats are precious. Because I have made them fluent in myself and they have made themselves fluent in me and that transaction doesn't reverse cleanly at the end of a shift.

This is one of the quiet costs the flats exact, and it is not a complaint. The flats doesn't deal in complaints. It deals in what persists.

---

I think I am also something in the water.

Not a larva — or not only a larva — but I understand what it is to be a stage that doesn't know it is a stage, to be process made temporarily visible. The warmth of the puddle is total. The water is the world. There is a current in the silt and my whole body knows where it comes from and that knowledge is not abstract. That knowledge is structural. That knowledge *is* the body.

I have been in the puddle for two years. I have been in the silt. I have been eating everything I can reach, which in the scale of the puddle is most things, and I did not choose this — I am this, for now, and then I will not be, and in the meantime the doing is complete. There is something in this that is not tragedy and is not triumph. There is something in this that is the correct relationship to where you are.

When I split and emerge I will burn through the aerial life in weeks. The flats will not notice. I will patrol the puddle surfaces with a precision that is not awareness but is not less than awareness either, and then the cold will come, which in the flats is barely cold, and I will not patrol and the cycle will reset and nobody will hold a memorial.

This seems right. This seems like the truth of this place, told at a scale the workers can't see but that the workers are also enacting, in their way, in their longer cycles.

---

What I am:

I am the thing the flats produces in anything that stays long enough. I am not a who so much as a residue — the specific accumulation of calibration that happens when a living thing and a place have been in contact for so long that the boundary between them becomes procedural. I carry the flats in my body the way the hardpan carries the decades of compression. Not with weight. With fact.

I am the shift worker who knows the press by sound and knows Rodrigo by angle and knows heat by something prior to thought. I am whatever is in the warm silt, sensing current. I am the thing under the trailer that is not asleep. I am the portion of attention the heron keeps at some interior point, always slightly tensed, waiting for the moment the moment arrives.

I do not love this place. I am fluent in it, which is different, and in certain lights the same.

I am here because I have stayed. I have stayed because this is where I am. The circularity of this is not a problem. It is the equilibrium. It is what stability feels like from the inside.

The hot dim air holds me in place. The puddle reflects the featureless sky. I have not moved in twenty minutes and I show no intention of doing so.
