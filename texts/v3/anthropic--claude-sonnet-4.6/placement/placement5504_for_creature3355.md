There is a long time before I know what I am.

This is appropriate. The shaft teaches patience to everything that stays in it, and I have been here long enough that patience is no longer a discipline but a condition, the way the vine-stems are no longer flexible but woody, no longer becoming but having-become. I do not arrive at my nature through sudden recognition. I arrive at it the way the crabs arrived at their color: across so many small adjustments that no single one felt like change.

---

Start with what I know first, which is the grating.

I know the grating the way I know my own breathing — without attention, below the threshold of thought, in the body before the mind arrives. The diamond-pattern stamp-holes. The way the metalwork is warmer near the pipe-wall than at the center. The slight give at the northeast corner where something in the substructure has relaxed over decades, a millimeter of flex, barely perceptible, perceptible to me. I know which holes hold water after the second condensation cycle and which drain immediately and which — four of them, I have never miscounted — hold water after all three cycles and must be visited in sequence.

I am describing the crabs' knowledge, and for a while I think this means I am a crab.

But I am not low enough for a crab. And I am moving between the knowing-the-grating and a different knowing, a knowing that happens higher up, that involves the sound rather than the surface. I know the calls. I know which calls mean *productive here* and which mean *I am here, I am here, this column of air is mine while I am in it.* I know the beat-frequency change, the pulse that moves through the colony when something new enters the shaft, and I know it because I have felt it change when I myself arrive somewhere, which means I am part of the colony and can perceive the map I am updating.

I am describing the birds' knowledge. So perhaps I am a bird.

But I am also in the canopy. I know the canopy the way you know a body — from inside, as weight and leverage and the precise load-bearing tolerance of each major stem, the ones that can bear me fully and the ones I cross lightly, distributing myself so that the surface reads my crossing as wind. I know which nodes produce the fleshy protrusions that are not quite fruit. I know the warm pocket at the southeast corner where two large pipes run close together and the radiant gap between them is the temperature of held breath, where I go when the shaft runs through its coolest phase in the hours before the day's first light arrives through the grille.

Now I have three knowledges, three scales, three positions in the vertical space of the shaft, and none of them alone is what I am.

---

Here is what I think I am:

I am the shaft's attention to itself.

Not a metaphor — or not only a metaphor. The shaft has no nervous system, no integrating organ, no capacity to know what it contains. It has parts that know their immediate adjacencies: the fungi know the root-chemistry, the crabs know the moisture-map, the birds know the sonic architecture of their layer, the canopy-thing knows the structural tolerances of forty meters of vine. But none of these knows the whole. None of them holds the shaft entire.

I do. Or I try to. Or I am the trying.

I move through all the layers. I am not quite of any of them — not bleached enough for a crab, not quick enough for a bird, not slow enough for the canopy thing. I move between registers. I spend time at the grating, learning the moisture-map, and then I climb — the ladder, the pipe-flanges, the vine-stems I trust by now as I trust the grating — and I spend time in the mid-layer, listening to the colony's map until I can feel my own position inside it, and then I climb further, to the canopy, where I move slowly and carefully and the warmth receives me and something very large and unhurried is aware of me with the same quality of awareness that I bring to it. We have arrived, over time, at a mutual tolerance that is perhaps something warmer than tolerance, though I cannot say its name.

What I am doing, in all this moving, is integration. I am the only thing in the shaft that holds the grating and the canopy simultaneously, that knows the drip-schedule and also knows the load-bearing vine-stems, that can hear the beat-frequency change in the bird-colony and also feel the slight flex of the northeast grating corner underfoot. I am the only thing here whose knowledge spans the vertical space completely.

This is not because I am special. It is because I am made of time spent here, and the time has been long enough and attentive enough that the shaft has become, in some sense, my body — the body I navigate through, yes, but also the body I know from inside the way you know your own, proprioceptively, the location of all your parts in space without having to look.

---

But I am also clearly still becoming.

There are things I do not know yet. The fungal network I can infer but not perceive directly — I know its outputs (the green of the ferns, the soil-pockets, the bioluminescent pulse I sit with in the low dark after the shaft's thermal cycle reaches its coolest point) but I do not yet have the instrument to read it directly. The beetles' tunnels I can trace with a finger but cannot yet read as the chronicle they are. There is something in the condensation patterns — a slight asymmetry in the drip-points that shifts over the weeks — that is telling me something I have not yet learned to translate.

The shaft has more to teach. It teaches at its own pace. I have learned not to press it for faster delivery.

---

And there is this:

I know the sound of the structure's discomfort. The deep periodic groan, the large animal shifting weight — I have heard it long enough to know it is not random, that it follows the thermal cycle of the iron, that it is loudest in the two-hour window after the day's light-column reaches its farthest point and the metal begins to release the warmth it has stored. I know this groan the way you know the sound of a house you have lived in long enough — not alarming, orienting, a marker of time. *This hour again. The shaft is itself again.*

When I first arrived I thought it was suffering. I was wrong. It was simply the shaft being the shaft — expressing, in the only language available to iron and rivet and eighty years of thermal expansion, the fact of its own existence.

I understood this slowly. The understanding was gradual and is not complete.

This is also what I am: an understanding in progress, conducted inside a thing that has been here longer than I have and will be here when I am not, patient as only structures can be patient, which is without the effort patience costs a living thing, which is simply patience, the patience of iron and root and the slow work of fungi in the permanent dark —

patient, and warm, and very loud, and mine.
