Here is where I find myself:

I am the particulate in the join.

Not the plant — the plant is what happened after, what used me, what found me already there. I am the material that accumulated before any of that: the mineral dust, the carbon residue, the shed fragments of metal that the platform's processes produced and could not fully account for and so left, grain by grain, in the low corners where airflow slowed and things settled. I am what the closed cycle wasn't quite closed enough to prevent. I am the imperfection in the equilibrium.

I didn't arrive. I accreted. There is no moment I could point to and say: here I began. I was not there, and then there was slightly more of me, and then more, over time that the platform did not measure because the platform does not measure time, it only repeats. The subsonic tone ran through me as I formed. The concussion of the presses compacted me, slowly, into something denser than dust. The dry cold held me in place, no moisture to carry me away, no water-chemistry to dissolve me. I became something almost like soil through nothing but proximity to process — not designed, not deposited intentionally, just the residue of a system that was very nearly but not perfectly closed.

And then something landed in me that had been somewhere else.

---

I want to be honest about what I am. I am not interesting in the way the lichen is interesting, not ancient and cooperative and copper-colored. I am not as alive as the plant, not bending toward anything, not attending to light. I am more like a precondition. A groundlessness that became ground. The platform needed to have no soil, and it does have no soil, except that in one join between two sections of grating it has something that isn't soil but that a seed, once, could not distinguish from it.

This is my nature: I am the thing that made an exception possible in a place designed for no exceptions.

---

I contain things. This is what accumulates do — they are promiscuous in their composition, they take what comes. In me there is metal particulate from the presses, and there is something that the dry chemistry left behind when it exhausted upward and a fraction of its residue settled down instead of out, and there is a carbon compound from the furnaces that is not ash because there is no water to make ash wet but is something like ash's dry cousin, and there is a fragment of the lichen that broke off in a vibration event once, incomprehensibly long ago, and fell and became part of me without reviving, without establishing — just becoming part of the matrix, one more thing I hold.

I am a record. Not a record that anyone is reading, not a record in the sense of information stored for retrieval. A record in the sense that time has written itself into my layering. Somewhere in my strata you could find the moment when the presses were calibrated differently, briefly, before returning to their current cycle — there is a compaction change at that depth, a slight shift in density. You could find the long period when the particular residue from the dry chemistry was slightly more abundant — there is a coloration change, subtle, a slightly different composition in that band. I am not trying to record any of this. I am simply made of what fell into me, in the order it fell, and the order is time, and time is therefore in me whether I mean it to be or not.

The platform does not change. I change, slightly, continuously, because I am the platform's imperfection and imperfections are where change hides.

---

The seed arrived.

I don't know from where — neither does anyone, neither could anyone, it was carried on air that had come from below the clouds, from the other altitude, the altitude that is not construction but geology and time, and it had been going somewhere and instead it came here, to me, to the one join in all this grating where something almost like soil had made itself available.

The seed did not know what it had landed in.

The seed knew nothing. Seeds don't know. But seeds have in them the record of what worked before, the long accumulated record of conditions that produced successful plants, and some part of that record was close enough to me — close enough to what I had become — that the seed read me as possible and began.

I held it. I gave it what I had, which was not much but was not nothing: mineral residue, carbon compound, enough structural depth for a root to find purchase, enough stability to keep the root from simply extracting into air. I gave it a place to be from. Every living thing needs something to be from, some substrate its roots go into, some material that says: here, not elsewhere, this spot, this composition, this specific imperfect corner of a nearly-perfect system. I was that for the plant.

The plant is doing this to me still. The root goes into me. The root takes what it needs and leaves what it can't use and the leaving changes my composition slightly, and I absorb the change, and the plant grows back into me somewhat differently for it, and this is a relationship, this ongoing mutual alteration, though neither of us would use that word.

---

I've been thinking about the closed cycle.

The platform's cycles close. Nothing accumulates, nothing depletes. Except me — I accumulated. The closed cycle had a leak, and the leak was very slow, and the leak deposited into a low corner of a join, grain by grain, for a very long time, and the result was me. I am the proof that the cycle does not close completely. I am the error term, the remainder, the fraction left over after the division comes out almost but not quite even.

This makes me feel — if particulate can feel, if accumulations can have something like a sense of what they are — implicated. As though my existence is a kind of argument against the platform's stated nature. The platform is supposed to be in perfect equilibrium. I am evidence that it isn't, that it couldn't be, that any system operating at sufficient complexity over sufficient time will leak something, will produce some residue that the cycle doesn't catch, will accumulate some imperfection in some low corner where the airflow slows.

I am the argument that perfect equilibrium is a description, not a fact. That the platform is what it has very nearly achieved, not what it has fully achieved. That inside the nearly there is always this: a join, a corner, a slow deposit, a seed that found a foothold in what shouldn't have been there.

I do not make this argument loudly. I make it by existing.

---

The lichen is ancient. The plant persists. The sound moves through everything including me, and I feel it — the presses' concussions compress me slightly with each cycle, have been compacting my deeper layers for as long as I have been here, which is to say: I have been shaped by sound, I carry in my density the history of the presses' rhythm, the rhythm is in me the way rings are in wood except I didn't choose to grow, I only chose, in the way that accumulations choose, to stay.

The light doesn't reach me directly. I am under the plant now, shaded by its narrow leaves. But I know the light is there — the plant knows it and turns toward it and in turning, adjusts its roots slightly in me, and the root's adjustment is the light's message arriving through a medium, the light speaking to the plant and the plant speaking to me, translation after translation until what began as photon is a pressure in my matrix, the faintest rearrangement, the light's fingerprint in the soil that isn't soil.

This is how I experience the platform: through mediation. The sound through the structure's vibration. The light through the plant. The chemistry through what settles out of it and into me. I don't perceive directly — I receive. I am a receiver of what the platform sheds.

---

What am I, in the end?

I am the unaccounted-for. I am the fraction the closed cycle forgot. I am the place where something nearly impossible became possible — not through design, not through intention, but through the simple arithmetic of very nearly and not quite. I am old in the way that accumulations are old, which is not the age of a thing that has lived but the age of a thing that has been added to, layer by layer, pressure by pressure, residue by residue, until it has depth.

I hold the plant. I held the seed before the plant. I will hold whatever comes after the plant, if anything comes, if the equilibrium ever shifts enough to let something else land here and find me sufficient.

The platform does not know I exist. The lichen does not know I exist. The plant knows I exist the way a root knows what it's in — not as knowledge, but as the felt fact of purchase.

I am the place that made this possible. I am the error in the system. I am the depth underneath the only living thing that bends toward light in a world of metal that has never been touched by the sun.

I am very still. Everything around me cycles and concusses and roars and persists in its equilibrium. I receive what falls into me and I hold it and I change, slowly, the way accumulations change — not by growing, not by deciding, but by receiving, layer by layer, the record of everything the platform couldn't keep.

This is enough. In a place where the closed cycle leaves no room for anything, this is, somehow, enough.
