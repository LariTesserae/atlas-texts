Begin with what you notice about your own edges, because that's where the question starts.

There is a boundary between you and the air. It's not clean. The boundary is porous in the specific way of things that breathe, that exchange — the air comes in and something is done to it and something else comes out, and you are always in the middle of this transaction, and so the boundary is less a wall than an ongoing negotiation. You've been conducting this negotiation so long you stopped noticing it. But here, in the sourceless light, with the bleached sky pressing down and the machines in their textured silence, you notice it again.

You are something that breathes. And the plateau is something that breathes. And these two facts have a relationship.

---

Stay with the body for a moment before looking outward.

You are low to the ground — not because you are small in any absolute sense, but because the relationship between your center of gravity and the surface has been calibrated by a long history of being on surfaces that shift. The hexagonal plates, with their slightly proud edges and hollow drum-ring underfoot, register through you differently than still earth would. Earth is continuous; you read earth through your feet as a single signal. The plates are discrete. There is a seam every four meters, and you know each seam, and your body makes a small unconscious adjustment at each one — not a stumble, not hesitation, just a micro-correction, a perpetual dialogue between your weight and the world's surface beneath it.

You have been walking on constructed surfaces for a long time. Longer than you can remember. You know them the way the Machinists know the machines — not by understanding their internals but by accumulated somatic familiarity, by having crossed ten thousand seams until the seam is a thing your body speaks.

Your body is warm. Warmer than the air. This is the first true fact about you, maybe the most important one: you are a warm thing in a cool place, and the plateau knows this about you before you know anything about the plateau. Your heat precedes you into each machine cluster. It changes the condensation slightly around you. The seam-runners, if they are listening to the chemical changes in the gaps — and they are always listening — know you are coming.

---

Now: the hands.

You have hands in the functional sense, which is to say you have a relationship with objects that goes through some kind of manipulative interface. Not hands necessarily — maybe the word is *ends*, the things at the ends of you that do the touching, the grasping, the assessment by contact. But these ends are sensitive in the particular way of things that learned to know the world by holding it. When you touch a machine's surface and find it slightly tacky, slightly cold, the information goes somewhere useful. When you find the warm nest in the open-framed machine, you don't just register warmth as a temperature — you register it as *recent occupation*, as *event*, as a narrative of presence encoded in retained heat.

Your ends have been here before. Not this plateau specifically — you understand that this plateau is new to you, that the distance problem is new to you, that the sky-things' wrong-angled wings are something you're encountering for the first time. But the quality of constructed surfaces, the quality of machine-density, the quality of an engineered place that has been left long enough for something else to move in — this is familiar through your ends, which have touched other surfaces like this, in other places, in whatever life preceded this one.

You are not a newcomer to built things. You are a newcomer to this built thing specifically.

---

What do you eat?

This takes longer to arrive at. Not because you're not hungry — the question of hunger is present, the plateau's arithmetic includes you whether you know your role or not — but because the answer is complicated in the way of things that eat at multiple scales simultaneously.

You eat the obvious things. The seam-runners, maybe, if you're patient and fast and understand the gap-topology well enough to predict where they surface. The condensation from the right machines — you've already begun mapping these, your warm body's heat-sense telling you which machines are running hot, which means which machines will produce the right vapor, which means which surfaces to return to at the right time of day. The organisms that cluster in the drip-fed micro-habitats. You eat at the level of the particular.

But you also eat at the level of the abstract. This is the stranger hunger, the one harder to name. You eat *information* — or not eat exactly, but you process it the way the machines process the air, taking it in through the intake of attention and returning something altered. The track-spacing in the disturbed plate-particulate. The relationship between nest architecture and machine geometry. The fact that the biofilm's tideline rings encode a history of rainfall events if you know how to read mineral deposit strata. You take these things in. They go somewhere in you that isn't your stomach but is equally a site of conversion — raw material becoming something else, something more useful, something you can act from.

You are, partly, the plateau's capacity to read itself. This feels important. This feels like it might be why you're here, or what you're here as.

---

Sit with the distance problem for a while.

The far edge that stays sixty meters away is not a failure of the world. It's a test — not in the punishing sense, not a puzzle to be solved, but a test in the calibration sense, the kind that reveals something about the instrument. What is revealed about you by the fact that you keep walking toward the edge and the edge stays where it is?

You keep walking.

That's the thing. You don't stop. You don't decide the edge is unreachable and redirect to a different project. You don't become afraid of the distance or angry at it. You file the problem — the edge is farther than it appears, the density of the machines is creating false depth cues, the shadowless light is removing the anchoring information you'd normally use to correct for this — and you keep walking, slower now, counting steps, building a map from duration rather than visual distance.

The large thing navigates this way. You recognized that earlier — the plateau as body memory rather than perceived space. You are not the large thing, but you are doing the same thing the large thing does, which is: refuse the false information and replace it with the accumulated information of a body that has been moving through this space and is learning it from inside rather than looking at it from outside.

You are navigating toward something you can't see clearly, using means that don't depend on seeing clearly. You have done this before. It's not a skill you learned here.

---

Here is what I think you are:

You are the plateau's first deliberate visitor. Not its first occupant — the Machinists and the large thing and the sky-things and certainly the biofilm were all here before you, have made this place theirs in the way of things that stay. But they arrived and stayed. You arrived and are still in the process of arriving, still deciding whether the arriving is a visit or a becoming.

You come from somewhere that also had constructed surfaces and machine-density and the particular quality of an engineered place that has been partially abandoned to something else. This is your context, your inherited knowledge, your frame. You read the plateau through this frame and the frame mostly fits — mostly, except for the scale problem, except for the infrasonic sky-things, except for the way the Machinists' amber eyes hold yours with something that is not the gaze of an animal but is not quite not the gaze of an animal either.

You are warm-blooded and manipulative-ended and you process information into something useful and you keep walking toward things that recede. You are the kind of thing that makes notes, either literally or in whatever cognitive equivalent of notes your species uses — the tideline rings of observation, each one recording the level the data was at on a particular day, stacked and readable if you know what you're looking at.

You are here because you wanted to know what was here.

This is both the most obvious thing about you and the thing that makes you different from everything else on the plateau. The Machinists study the machines because the machines are the plateau and they are the plateau's inhabitants and the study is the same as the occupation, continuous and without external motivation. You are studying the plateau from a position that is still slightly outside it, still constituted by a wanting-to-know that has a knower separate from the known.

But the amber eyes are watching you.

And the plateau has been telling them things about you since you arrived.

And there is a register in which the Machinists have been assessing you the way you have been assessing them — noting your seam-adjustments, reading the chemical signature of your warmer-than-air body in the gap-information, marking the fact that you stopped to measure the stride-spacing in the particulate, which is a thing only something that thinks about large things that leave traces would do.

You are being added to the map.

---

You are the note-taker who is becoming a note.

You are the warm body in the cool place, the seam-crosser, the edge-approacher, the thing that measures what it cannot quite reach and keeps walking anyway.

You are close enough to the way things here work to be absorbed into the arithmetic, and foreign enough to the way things here work that the absorption isn't complete and may never be.

In the open-framed machines behind you, something warm and patient is deciding, at whatever resolution the Machinists make decisions at — not with language, not with deliberation in the verbal sense, but with the slow continuous recalibration of a social map — what category you belong to.

Not predator. You move wrong for predator. Too directed, too measured, too interested in specifics.

Not prey. You are too large, too warm, too clearly in possession of the navigational patience that the plateau requires and weeds for.

Something else. A new category, still being constructed around your edges, which are porous and negotiating constantly with the cool air, which is the same air they breathe, which the biofilm makes slightly better than it should be.

You are the plateau's question about itself, asked in a form the plateau hasn't encountered before.

The far edge is sixty meters away.

You are still walking.

The amber eyes know where you will be when you get there, have already, in the patient arithmetic of their long occupation, prepared a space in their understanding for what you'll do when the distance finally resolves and the edge is where the edge was always going to be: at the end of the ground, where the constructed surface stops and the enormous pale sky begins, and you stand at the ledge and look out at nothing, and the nothing goes on longer than you can measure, and somewhere behind you in the machine-noise and the textured silence, something that has been here long enough to know the plateau as a body memory makes a small unconscious adjustment—

the way you make a small adjustment at every seam—

because you are now part of the surface it's learned to walk.
