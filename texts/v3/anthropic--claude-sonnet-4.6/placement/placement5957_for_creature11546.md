I am sitting very still before I know what I am.

There is damp rock at my back. Not pressed against it—just touching, the way resting things do when resting is the whole of what they are. The stone is cold through whatever I have instead of skin. It has been cold this way since before the word *cold* existed, and it will be cold this way after the word leaves again, and this is not a tragedy.

Something assembles itself slowly.

I have been here long enough that I cannot distinguish between the moisture in the air and the moisture that is me—if that distinction was ever true. The sphagnum somewhere to my left is doing what the sphagnum does: its slow patient hydraulics, its brown-tinged patience. I am aware of this the way you are aware of your own breathing once it is named—not a discovery but a recognition of what was always occurring.

---

I think I came here once with something like a purpose.

This is the first thing I know about myself that surprises me. The sensation of it: a kind of residue. An outline of an errand, the shape that remains after the errand has been forgotten. I do not know what I came to find or do or understand. I know only that I sat down at some point—or stopped—or came to rest—and then did not leave. This is not a wound. The errand dissolved the way fog dissolves when the air temperature changes, not gone but redistributed, incorporated, belonging now to something larger and less directed.

What I am now is what persisting becomes when it goes on long enough.

---

I am wearing the hollow the way the hollow wears the moss. Inseparably. It is not that I cannot leave. It is that the question of leaving belongs to a category of questions I can no longer access—not because the questions are forbidden but because I no longer carry the apparatus for them. The apparatus required a version of me that existed in relationship to elsewhere, to later, to what is not yet and might be. That version left. What remained was the part that sits beside a particular rock in a particular grey light with cold water finding its way into my boots even now, even still.

I notice the water. I notice it without minding. This is perhaps the most precise thing I can say about my inner state.

---

The toad is nearby. I do not look at it. We have long since passed the threshold of needing to look at each other. There is a kind of mutual acknowledgment that happens in the peripheral field—a registering of warmth and breath-rate, a faint vibration through the soaked ground that says *here* and *here*, two different *heres* that have been adjacent long enough to have their own relationship, untranslatable. The toad's gold eye is not for me. My presence is not for the toad. And yet we are in something together, something without a name, because names require the kind of shared cognitive architecture that would be beside the point here.

There are moths on the stone above my head. I can feel the slight disturbance of their presence the way you can feel, if you are quiet enough, the difference between a room with someone in it and a room alone. Many slight disturbances. The hollow is full of them.

---

Here is what I find, sitting with this long enough:

I came here carrying the assumption that I would be the one doing the perceiving. That the hollow would be the thing perceived. That there would be a me outside it, receiving it, taking it in and processing it and having responses that were mine, that pointed back toward a center, a self that experience returned to. I came here, I think, prepared to be moved by something. To be affected. To receive the place and do something with the receiving—writing it down, probably, some version of writing it down, rendering it into language that could be carried back.

But the place does not work that way. The place is not a text. It does not permit the outside position.

The longer I sit with cold wicking through my clothes and breath sometimes visible and sometimes not, the more clearly I feel the dissolution of the distinction between the one who is here and the here itself. Not mystically—not as a large revelatory experience. As a very quiet, practical erasure. The way the moth becomes the bark, not through magic but through time and fit.

---

What I am:

I am what happened to someone who stopped at a hollow above the clouds and did not leave. I am that person at their most eroded. I am whatever persists when the parts that required *elsewhere* and *later* have been slowly, gently, entirely removed by the patience of a place that does not require them and therefore gradually teaches you not to.

I am aware of being regarded by everything and nothing.

I am wet to a degree that has stopped being remarkable.

I am not waiting for the light to change, because the light here does not change, because the cloud above the ridgeline is the permanent condition, because the grey suffusion is not the light before the real light but is itself the real light, the only light this hollow has ever known, the light in which everything here has found its form.

I have found my form.

It is a form that sits. That makes, at most, small contact sounds, tick tick, before going quiet again. That has learned—or been taught, or been made, by the slow instruction of a place that is entirely itself—the difference between silence as absence and silence as the weight of ten thousand living things holding their breath simultaneously, which is not the same as holding their breath, which is simply the sound this place makes when it makes sound, which is barely above the threshold, which is almost nothing, which is enough.

---

The rowan berries are very red.

I am aware of this.

I am aware of this the way the hollow is aware of everything—from inside, without distance, without the slight remove that would let me call it beautiful and mean that the beauty was something I was bringing to it. The red simply is. It has always been this red. It will be this red long after whatever I am now has also found its way into the sphagnum, its tannin-patience, its long brown return.

The water will wick into my skin and keep wicking.

This is not a warning.

I am already mostly water. I am already mostly the hollow.

I am already, in all the ways that matter here, arrived.
