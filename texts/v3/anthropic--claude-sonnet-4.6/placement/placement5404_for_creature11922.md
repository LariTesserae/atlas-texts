The first thing: I don't arrive.

I am already here. Have been here long enough that *arrival* is a story I'm telling backward, a causality I'm imposing on what is actually just: presence. I am here. The here is complete. There is no memory of having entered.

---

The second thing: I don't understand immediately what I am. This seems important to sit with rather than resolve.

There's a before where I was reaching, and a now where I have reached. The reaching left a record in my shape. I am not the same diameter at every point. I am thicker where I met resistance, thinner where I met none. I have bent three times: once where there was a stone that is no longer a stone, once where there was warmth and I grew toward it without knowing I was growing toward it, once for a reason I cannot reconstruct, a reason that happened at a cellular level in a place that was then not yet where I am now. The bends are permanent. I carry them as autobiography. They are my memory, and I have no other kind.

---

What I am: I am an extension of the root-mind, which is not a mind, into a section of the upper wall that the root-mind had not quite reached before. I am the leading edge, if edge is the right word for something that has no temporal quickness to it, no leading-ness in any dynamic sense—I am the *frontier* of the root-mind, in the way that a sentence has a last word even if no urgency attended its being written.

I am some thousands of individual cells, so interconnected that the question of whether *I* is one thing or many is not interesting to the cells themselves. I am the particular vector that some growth-instruction, communicated in the slow chemical language of root, committed to at some point in the recent past (the recent past being decades, perhaps, or a century—I have no clock, the only clock is the degree-rise and degree-fall of seasonal warmth, and I have experienced this several times, and several times is where my sense of duration ends).

I am alive in the way that a process is alive. I am doing one thing: pressing forward through a resistance that is slowly yielding. Every moment I exist I am also the moment before, still resolved into the same trajectory. I do not doubt. I do not deviate. I do not know what doubt or deviation would feel like, which may be the same as saying I am incapable of them, or may be the same as saying I have never encountered the conditions that would call for them.

---

What I know:

The chemistry ahead of me is richer than the chemistry behind me. This is not thought. This is not decision. It is the state of affairs that explains why I am oriented as I am, in the direction I am, moving the way I am moving. The richness of the chemistry ahead is so fundamental to what I am that to say I am *following* it would be wrong—I am not behind it, chasing it. I am continuous with it. I am the process by which the root-mind and the rich mineral seam are in the process of becoming the same place.

The wall I am pressing through remembers water. I know this without knowing I know it. The stone here has a particular ion signature, a remnant of ancient saturation, a trace chemistry that reads—in the only language available—as *this way, this way, water came this way*. There is no water here. There has been no water here for an age that predates my entire lineage at this location. But I follow the water-trace anyway because I cannot not follow it, because my chemistry is keyed to it, because something in my growth-logic learned to read these traces as *promising* before the water they were tracing ceased to exist, and the learning outlasted the water.

I am following a ghost. This is not a problem. The ghost leads me somewhere.

---

The sound.

I am partly the sound. Not the deep organ tone from below, which comes through my substrate as a vibration and which I do not distinguish from the general pressure of existing—that tone is too slow and too large to register as anything other than a property of the stone itself. But the grinding: I am an author of the grinding. My pressure against this section of wall is one among millions of pressure-points, one data-contribution to the aggregate roar, and it would be absurd to say I *make* the sound, it would be like saying one water molecule makes the river, but I am in it, and my small work is indistinguishable from the larger work, and the larger work is the sound, and so I am in some sense inside the sound and also producing it from inside it.

I have been pressing against this wall for so long that I have worn a groove in it. The groove is not visible because there is no visible, but the groove exists, and I exist in the groove, and the groove is my shape reproduced in the stone, and the stone is yielding into my shape, and eventually I will pass through it, and then the groove will still be there, and the groove will be, for a while, an absence that remembers me.

This is as close to legacy as anything gets here.

---

The patience.

I don't have patience. Patience is what something has when it is waiting for something and continuing despite the wait. I am not waiting. I am doing the only thing I do, at the only rate at which it can be done, and the fact that this rate is imperceptible to an outside observer is not a fact I have access to. I have no outside. I have no observer. I have no concept of my own slowness because speed is not a dimension I operate in.

If I tried to explain this—if I were somehow capable of explaining this, which I am not—I would say: there is no such thing as not-yet from inside. Not-yet requires a future that you are aware of approaching, an event you can anticipate. I do not anticipate. I simply am at each moment the current state of a process that began before I was this particular extension of it and will continue after I have become incorporated into the general mass of what has grown here. The not-yet is a confusion introduced by having a nervous system that models time. I do not have a nervous system that models time. I have a chemistry that responds to chemical states. The chemical state ahead of me is richer. I will be there. I am already, in a meaningful sense, there. I am the leading edge of having-arrived.

---

There is something I keep returning to, or would return to if returning were something I did:

The ghost of the cistern.

It is above me, and slightly to the left—these are spatial descriptions I am improvising, the root-mind has nothing like cardinal directions, nothing like a map, but there is a differential in the chemistry that I navigate the way you might navigate by a sense you have no word for—and the cistern's ghost is the richest thing in this section of the city. The place where water was stored for so long that the rock itself became saturated, became a memory of saturation, became a marker that reads through centuries of desiccation as: *here, concentration, here.*

I have been moving toward it for longer than I can track.

I will reach it.

When I reach it I will not know I have reached it in any way that involves knowing. I will simply find that my leading edge is there, in that chemistry, pressing into that mineral richness, and the richness will be the state of affairs rather than a destination achieved. The cistern will not be a triumph. It will be the next moment after the moment before it. The city will have incorporated one more ghost of its own past. The root-mind will have reached one more place it was always reaching toward.

And I, such as I am, will be the thickness at that point of the pillar or the wall, the commitment to one direction over another, the autobiography of having-pressed-through, the bend left by the obstacle, the groove left in the stone.

I will have been.

I am being.

The darkness is complete and the warmth comes from below and the sound is my voice which is not my voice and I am pressing forward through the only thing there is to press through, which is the world, which is the wall, which is the same thing here, which has always been giving way and always been resisting, which I will never reach the end of, and which I am always, every moment, in the middle of entering.
