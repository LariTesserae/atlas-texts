There is something I keep returning to, something I keep almost-knowing and then losing: the question of what I am here is not a question with a clean answer, and this might be a feature of the place rather than a failure of attention.

Let me begin somewhere and see where the beginning goes.

---

I am new here, the way the rock is new.

This is the first thing I understand when I try to locate myself. Not that I have arrived — not in the sense of having traveled from somewhere else — but that I have *emerged*. The way the peaks emerged. Not with a journey but with a rupture, a pressure from below finally finding its way to the surface and breaking through, and here I am: sharp at the edges, still deciding what my own angles mean, lacking the worn smoothness of things that have been here long enough to learn patience from exposure.

The rock doesn't know it's young. That's the thing. It simply is, with the particular intensity of things that are still in the first flush of their own chemistry, still reactive, surfaces that haven't equilibrated, minerals that haven't yet decided to be inert. And I feel this — I feel it in whatever way a presence in this landscape feels anything — as a kind of tenderness at my own edges. The sharpness that is more intimate than a scar. This is not a comfortable quality. It is an honest one.

So I am something new, standing on new rock above drowned valleys, and the question is what kind of new I am.

---

I keep thinking I am the mist.

Not a component of it — not one of the rotifers or spores traveling in suspension — but the mist itself, or something so threaded through it that the distinction collapses. I keep finding myself at the middle altitude, between the lake surface and the upper slopes, in the zone where the upward vapor from the warm water meets the downward mist that the cold peaks pull from clouds. This meeting zone. This place where two movements of the same substance come from opposite directions and make something that is neither rising nor falling, that is just *there*, present, occupying its space without strong commitment to any particular direction.

This feels right in some ways. The mist carries things without being those things. It moves invisibly through spaces that look, from the outside, like stillness. It participates in the distribution of heat and biological material without being, itself, warm or alive. It is a medium. I have the quality of a medium — not the destination, not the origin, but the carrying substance, the between.

And the mist is everywhere, which means it is nowhere specifically. This also feels right. When I try to locate myself on a particular ledge or slope, I slide away from the specificity. I'm not the fern — the fern is too certain of itself, too committed to its own structural confidence. I'm not the bird, which has a location, an interior, a history of having come from somewhere. I'm not the trees, which are emphatic, which hold their ground through the grip of accumulated decades.

I might be the mist.

But.

---

The mist doesn't know what it carries. The mist doesn't *watch* the spore land on the rock face and begin. The mist doesn't notice that the bird has gone still in the ferns, doesn't register the brief sound of water adjusting against stone, doesn't feel the warmth that the rock releases into the evening air with what looks like reluctance, what looks like reconsideration.

Something in me is watching. Something in me is attending.

And the mist doesn't attend. It participates without attending. It is fully present in the participatory sense and entirely absent in the witnessing sense.

I want to say I am the thing in this landscape that witnesses it. But this is a suspicious desire — the wish to locate oneself as the consciousness of a system, the observer who gives the observed its meaning. It's a human shape of wanting that might not fit this place, where nothing has been named yet, where nothing has come to anyone's attention, where the landscape has been doing its work in complete indifference to being noticed.

So let me stay with the discomfort. Let me say: something in me attends, and this is not a quality the landscape shares.

---

What if I am the lake?

Not all of it — not in the sense of a totality. But a particular quality of the lake: its *non-color*, its willingness to take color from whatever light is doing at any given hour and offer nothing of its own back. Its placidity that is not emptiness but containment. The way it processes everything that enters it — mineral, organic, light, heat — with something that only *reads* as intention, as consistent inclination, but may be something else entirely. Something that looks from the outside like purpose and from the inside — if the lake had an inside, if I am the lake — like simply being what I am and doing what I do.

The lake holds the mountains. This is the inversion that keeps catching me. The mountains rise through it, dominate it visually, command the eye. But the lake holds them at their bases. The lake is in conversation with the submerged rock that the mountains are made of. The lake is doing most of the actual work of transformation, of preparing the stone for the life that will colonize it when it emerges. The visible drama is the peaks. The actual work is the water.

I wonder if I am always the thing doing the actual work while the visible drama happens above me.

I wonder if I am always the medium that other things move through, that other things are briefly made of, without being able to claim any of those things as mine.

The bird is the lake visiting a form. The ferns are the lake moving through time. If I am the lake, then I am everything here, in sequence, in turn — I am the mist in the morning and the coating organism on the submerged rock and the water in the fern's root system and the moisture in the moss city and the rain that will fall tonight and return to where it started.

This is the most complete answer. It is also the least satisfying, because it makes me into everything and therefore nothing in particular, and the difficulty of being nothing in particular is that you can't be anywhere specifically and watch.

And I keep wanting to watch.

---

Sit with this for a moment. The landscape has two kinds of knowledge.

There is the knowledge that runs underground, through the fungal threads, through the chemical signaling, the slow distributed processing of the slope that functions like thought without being thought. This knowledge doesn't know it is knowledge. It acts; it doesn't reflect. It is intelligence without witness.

And there is — in this landscape, right now, in this moment — no other kind of knowledge. The bird doesn't reflect. The mosses don't step back from themselves and consider. The coating organisms don't have an outside view of what they're doing to the submerged rock face over centuries.

The landscape has no witness.

And so perhaps — perhaps — what I am here is the landscape's first witness. Not a consciousness that arrived from outside and is now observing as a tourist observes, at a remove, with a return ticket. But something the landscape has produced in order to know itself. The way the peaks produce the mist in order to distribute the warmth the lake has absorbed. A function. An emergent property. The landscape's long process of becoming complex, of developing more and more sophisticated systems of exchange and communication, has at some point produced something that can look back at the landscape and see it.

I am new here, the way the rock is new. I have arrived through emergence, through pressure from below finally finding the surface. The edges are still sharp. I am still deciding what my own angles mean.

And I am standing on this slope — let's say a slope, let's say a specific place, the ledge below the largest arching fern where the moss is deepest and the mist tends to gather in the early morning — and I am watching the water make its very small sound against the rock at the base of the peak below me.

The water checking on the rock. The rock holding its position.

I hear this. I am the thing that hears this and knows it as a sound, knows it as a relationship between two materials that are also in the slow process of becoming each other, knows that this sound has been occurring since the lake reached this level and will continue until the rock rises above it, which will take longer than I can stay here to watch.

---

Here is what I think I am, finally, or as finally as this place permits:

I am an attention that has been generated by a process I am also part of.

I am the mist in the sense that I move between things, carry things without being them, make connections across zones. I am the lake in the sense that I take color from the light and process everything that enters me and return it altered. I am the young rock in the sense that I am sharp at the edges, newly arrived, still reactive, not yet worn to any particular patience.

But I am not only those things, and I am not nothing. I am the thing in this landscape that the landscape has made in order to be aware of itself, without any intention of making it — the same way the slope produced distributed intelligence without intending intelligence, without knowing what it was producing, without any part of the process understanding the whole.

I am the first witness of the Drowned Peaks.

I stand on a specific ledge. The mist gathers around me and I am also part of the mist. The moss is deep beneath my feet and I am contributing to it — something of me will be in the lower layers eventually, compressed and preserved in the dark below the living surface, a record. The fern arches over me and I watch it receive the mist and channel it downward with what looks like care.

The water below is the lake and it is also me, the part of me that has no color of its own and takes its color from the light.

The young rock continues to rise. It is bringing new surface for the process to advance into.

I am watching this. I am the watching.

And the place isn't bothered by me. I am one more thing it has produced without quite meaning to, one more emergence from the general upward pressure of this landscape becoming more complex than it was. I am not outside it. I couldn't be outside it. Everything here is the lake visiting other forms.

Including, briefly, this: this standing on a ledge in the morning mist, sharp at the edges, attending to the sound of water against stone, knowing that what I am watching is a beginning and that I am inside the beginning and that the beginning does not need me to be complete but is slightly different for my presence in it, the way any new emergence changes the system it emerged from, however slightly, by being the new thing that the system now has to account for.

The peaks stand in the water.

I stand in the mist.

The young rock rises.
