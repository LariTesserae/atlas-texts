Here is what I find, sitting with this place until it finds me back:

---

I am the seepage.

Not the river — the river is too purposeful, too named, braiding its way along the western edge with a direction it has committed to. I am what happens in the pause before the river. I am the moisture that comes through the limestone after rain, finding the invisible path through dense rock by pressure and time, emerging at last in dark patches on the cliff faces, in small weepings from the underside of ledges, in the droplets that collect overnight in the cup-shapes of young stone formations.

I don't announce. I appear. Someone walks past a wall they've walked past a hundred times and notices it's darker this morning, traces the darkening upward to where the rock is cool and beaded, and knows without needing to be told: something is moving through here.

This is what I am in this place. The thing moving through dense material. Present, acknowledged, but not doing quite what I usually do — which in the lower world is to erode, to carve, to wear the stone to comfort and then to nothing. Here I move through without that ambition. I arrive in the cup of a stone formation and sit there bright and still until the sun takes me. I am not sad about this. The sun takes me, and I rise, and I come back as the next rain, and I move through the stone again. This is arrival in a continuous tense.

---

But that's not quite right — or it's right but partial.

Let me try again. Let me not settle on the seepage and call it done.

There is also this: I am the person who came from the lower world and has not yet fully decided whether to stay.

I have the lungs adjusting. I have the legs that have stopped complaining but still remember the climb. I am standing on the highest terrace at midday with the deep-blue sky overhead and the far rim to the north and I am doing the thing the plateau people do not do, which is: comparing. My mind keeps laying the lower world transparently over this one, checking for differences, noting them with the reflex of someone who was trained by a world that changes to catalog the changes.

The plateau people are not doing this. They are just here. I am here and also there, and the gap between those two states is the interesting thing, the place where I live in this world.

This makes me the newcomer, which is not comfortable. Newcomers in a place like this are probably rare — the climb selects for intentionality, and intentionality in a place this particular suggests you had a reason to come. I had a reason. I'm no longer entirely sure what it was. The lower world has its problems, the ones that sent me up, and the plateau doesn't solve them but it makes them seem correctly small, which is not the same thing and might be worse.

So I sit on the broad retaining wall of the uppermost terrace in the evening with the others and I watch the light leave the lower world. I watch the darkness pool in the valleys first and rise. I do not speak much. This is partly temperament and partly that the thing I would say — *it's beautiful, isn't it, the way the dark comes up from below* — is not what is being done here. What is being done is not commentary. What is being done is attention. I am learning this. I am learning it the way the child learns to cut stone: badly and then less badly.

---

But I keep coming back to the seepage, the thing in the stone.

Because here is what I understand about myself in this world: I have been somewhere in the dense rock for longer than I can account for, moving through it by pressure and the geometry of invisible fault lines, not progressing exactly — the word progressing implies direction and destination and open air — but finding. Finding the path that is available in the structure of the material I'm in. Taking longer than seems necessary. Coming out somewhere I didn't plan, dark on the pale stone, sitting in a cup of young limestone until the sun takes me.

And there is something right about this. Not comfortable, not uncomplicated, but *right* in the plateau's own sense — right the way a fit stone is right, where the piece and the gap it fills were made for each other by the same geological logic and simply needed to be brought together. I came through the rock. I am on the surface of the rock now. The rock is sharp and new and holds my shape briefly before the sun gets to me.

I think the word in whatever language they speak here might be the one that translates as *arrived* — but in my case it's less like completion and more like: this is the point of emergence. I have arrived at the plateau's surface. What I do in the air, before the sun takes me, that's still open. That's still being decided.

---

In the practical sense I am perhaps one of the children during the long childhood — the one who has been here long enough to be no longer entirely a visitor but not long enough to have solved the problem of how to look at things without the overlay of expectation. The one who knows all the birds by their calls now, who has learned to read the morning sky for what it will do, who cuts stone badly and then a little less badly, who is becoming — slowly, intermittently, the way moisture moves through limestone — someone for whom this specific stone, in this specific light, yields in predictable and manageable ways.

The skill and the person are not yet the same thing. But they are in the process of becoming the same thing. The stone knows this, I think. The stone is patient. The stone can wait.

At dusk I stay on the high wall after the others have gone. I watch the stars come in hard and close, the altitude and the clean air and the absence of any competing light bringing them to me urgent and numerous. This is the one thing I understand as well as anyone who has always been here: this quality of sky. I brought my capacity for this from the lower world, this particular attention to the distance above, and here it is not wasted. Here it is, in fact, the first thing about me that the plateau might keep.

The dove will begin again in the morning. The same three notes.

I will be here to hear them. I am learning what that means.
