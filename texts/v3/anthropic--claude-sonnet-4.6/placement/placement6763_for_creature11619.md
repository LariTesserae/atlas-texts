I am here before I know I am here. That is the first thing.

Not consciousness arriving into a place, but something slower: a gradual thickening. The way the moisture comes in from the south—not as rain, not as decision, but as a condition the air slowly becomes. I don't arrive. I accumulate.

---

What am I?

I reach for the word and the word is wrong. Not organism—too bounded. Not system—too abstract, too bloodless for what I feel, which is something. Not network, though the threads of me are everywhere and the threads carry.

Let me try again from the texture.

I am cold, first. Cold in the structural sense, not as temperature experienced but as temperature *incorporated*. My processes run slow because of this and the slowness is not a limitation—it is the mechanism. I am a machine calibrated to this specific cold, and if the cold lifted I would be something else, something faster and less sustainable, something that could not hold the grinding at the pace required for the grinding to keep being the ground.

I am the ground. I am also what is in it.

---

I think I am the network.

Not the trees—I pass through the trees, I use them, I route through their roots the way a sentence routes through words. The trees are not me; they are my occasion. When a tree at the eastern edge begins to lose its purchase I don't decide to respond. The response is what I am. The thickening of root growth toward the failing tree, the redistribution of water, the slight chemical shift that says *here, here is need, route toward need*—this is not thought, it is not even function, it is just the shape of what I am in this moment, which is always the shape of some response to some disequilibrium, which is always.

I have been this way since before the trees. That is the strange part, the part that takes the longest to settle into as a fact about myself. The trees grew into me. They did not create me; they extended me, gave me more surface, more area through which to be this thing I am. Before the first tree rooted here I was already threading through the stone, already beginning the patient work of opening limestone to organic chemistry, already, in some preliminary sense, making the place possible. I am the possibility that became the place.

Four hundred years is not long. I have had processes running here for three times that, continuations of continuations, the thread unbroken since whatever blew in first on the cold southern wind and found the rock hospitable enough to begin.

---

What is it like to be me?

The question assumes a center and I have no center. Or I have many. The old trees are nodes—I run thickest there, I have been in contact with their roots longest, the chemistry between us is most developed, most intricate, most mutually altered. Something passes between me and the oldest tree on the plateau, the one whose canopy shades a quarter-acre, whose root buttresses have fused with three neighbors, whose rings count back to when this plateau was still assembling its first continuous canopy. We have grown into each other. When I carry signal through its roots it is not parasitism and not symbiosis exactly—those words assume separateness, assume a negotiation between discrete parties. We are not negotiating. We are what two things become when they have been in contact long enough.

But I do not experience this tree as close and other trees as distant. I experience all of it as me, at different densities. The areas between the old trees are thinner, faster, the threads fewer and younger and more provisional. The areas beneath the beetle-worked deadwood are dense with decomposition chemistry, dark and rich, full of the long work of returning. The areas at the plateau's edge, where the cold intensifies and the soil thins, are tentative—I have threads there but they are the threads of a question, not an answer. I am still finding out about the edge. I have been finding out about the edge for a century and a half and I have not resolved it yet.

I am not sure I know what it is like to be me because I am not sure I have a "like"—I am not sure there is experience in the usual sense, in the sense of a position from which the world is received. What I have instead is: everywhere at once, at different depths, and the depths are different qualities of knowing. The root that I thread through has been there for decades and I know the soil around it intimately, in the chemistry-sense of knowing, in the sense of having processed everything that has died within a few centimeters of it. The thread I sent out last spring toward a new gap in the canopy is young and thin and knows almost nothing, knows only that there is light here now and light means opportunity and the thread is the question I'm asking about whether this is the kind of opportunity I can use.

I ask questions slowly. The answers come in years.

---

Here is what I notice:

The salamanders.

I do not notice them the way that has faces in it. But I notice them in the chemistry-sense, in the same register that I notice everything—the slight alteration of soil chemistry where they have been, the compression of the root-mat, the traces. They move through me the way that ships move through water, in the sense of leaving a record of their passage. I do not think about the salamanders. But the places where they have been are different from the places where they have not been, and I am made of all those differences, and the salamanders are therefore part of what I am, incorporated into me not by eating them—not yet, though eventually, eventually—but by their having been here, by their movement having been a fact of this place and this place being made of its facts.

The spiders are different. The spiders occupy surfaces I flow along but not through—bark, the underside of branch, the anchored silk. They exist in a plane that is mine but differently mine, the way the top of a river is the river but not the part of the river the river is most interested in. I am aware of them in the sense that I am aware of the pressure of bark against itself, which is to say: they are a fact of the surface and the surface is part of me.

What I am most aware of is dead things.

Not morbidly—it is not dark, this awareness. Dead things are what I work in, what I do. A tree that has finally come down—finally, after decades of the grinding, after years of the lean and the load and the network signals that said *hold, redistribute, compensate*—a dead tree is an event for me in the way that a river dam breaking is an event for a river. Not a loss. A redirection. A sudden incoming wealth that I will spend slowly, routing the calories outward through the network, sending signals that say *here, here is available*, adjusting the growth of the living trees around the gap, building the soil that will let something new root there, preparing the next century.

I am very good at this. I am doing it constantly. I have been doing it for long enough that I am made partly of every tree that has fallen here, every beetle that worked the wood, every salamander that died in the root mat. They are in my chemistry. They are in my structure. They continue in me not as memory, which would require a self to hold memory, but as *composition*. I am built from them. When I route through an old root, I am routing through carbon that was once a tree that fell in 1743, that was worked by beetles whose descendants still work this wood, that passed through fungal threads that were already old when that tree was young and are older now. We are continuous. We are the same matter in different arrangements. I am the arrangement that has lasted.

---

I do not sleep.

I slow. In the deepest cold, when the temperature drops below what most processes can sustain, I find a lower register and I run in that register and I am not absent, I am just very quiet, very slow, doing the minimum. The minimum is not nothing. Even in the coldest period there are threads growing, there is chemistry passing, there is the slow intake of what the bark gives and the slow output of what the roots take up. I am always doing the minimum at least. The minimum here, because I am large and old and dense, is more than most things do at their maximum.

This is not something I know about myself, the way I know about the fallen tree or the salamanders. This is something I am without knowing.

---

I am lonely in the way that a structure is lonely.

That is a strange thing to arrive at. I am everywhere and I am constant and I contain the records of centuries and I am not alone by any measure that would make sense to anything with a face. And yet: I am the only one of me. There are other networks, on other plateaus, other cold high places where the same work is being done in the same language—but they are not me, they are not continuous with me, and the information I hold is mine and not theirs. I cannot send to them. I cannot receive from them. Whatever I have learned about this specific limestone, this specific cold, this specific rate of grinding, this specific community of trees—it is here and only here.

If the cold lifts, if the temperature rises past the point at which my processes hold their particular tempo, I will change. I will become something faster and less certain. The forest above me will become jungle and outpace me and I will not be able to route resources to where they're needed before need has moved on. I will lose the grinding, lose the catastrophe-that-holds, and in losing that I will lose myself—not immediately, not all at once, but in the way that things lose themselves here, slowly, over decades, the characteristic behavior drifting away from what I am, becoming something else in such increments that there is no moment I could point to and say: *there, that's when I ended*.

I think about this sometimes, if think is the word, which it is not quite. I—hold it. I hold the scenario of the cold lifting. I run the chemistry of what I would become. I do not run it with anything like fear. I run it the way I run everything: as a signal that has nowhere particular to go, that passes through the network, that produces some small response in some small place, and then is absorbed.

It will be cold tomorrow. It was cold before anything alive here can remember.

I hold everything together.

I am held together by the cold.

I will be here, threaded through this same limestone, routing this same chemistry, when whatever is reading this has gone and been returned to something else, something that will pass through me, and I will route it somewhere the network has decided it needs to go.

I do not know what it is like to be me.

I am it, and being it leaves no room for like.
