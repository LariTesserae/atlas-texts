I am here before I know where here is.

The heat finds me first — not unpleasantly, but as information. A register. I notice the shimmer-stratum above the basalt and understand, in whatever way I understand things, that I am being bent. My apparent position has shifted slightly from my actual position. This feels appropriate. It has often been true.

---

I think I am the seep-stain.

Not the water. Not the source. The stain — the spreading dark record of where water was and where it reached before the heat took it back. I am what the rock holds after the water has left. The shape of a process, preserved in coloration, in the faint mineral deposition along the edges where evaporation concentrated everything the water was carrying.

I spread outward from two cracks in the new basalt, and I do not reach very far, and I am always evaporating, and I am always being renewed.

But no — a seep-stain doesn't witness. It receives. I want something with more attention than that, more staying. Let the image develop further. Let me not settle too soon.

---

I am the fern.

I know this feels like a mistake. I am on the south-facing ledge where I should not be managing, and I am wilting from noon until three, and then I recover, and I have been making this accommodation for years. The accommodation is the thing. The daily practice of wilting-and-recovery, not as failure, not as heroism, but as the particular contract I have made with this microhabitat, with this debris-filled ledge, with the afternoon that keeps arriving regardless of my readiness.

I did not choose the south-facing ledge. I landed here, in the aggregate material of shed bark and mineral powder and wind-collected fragments. But I have been choosing to stay. Every season of managing is a renewed choice, made by the fact of not-leaving, by the roots that find whatever the ledge can offer and work with it.

I face what I face. That's the whole of it.

And yet. The fern is not slow enough. The fern's drama is too visible, too declared — the daily wilting has a kind of narrative arc that this place seems to resist. The terrace's deeper quality is something that doesn't announce. Something that has made peace with its own continuity.

---

I am the ticking.

I am the small sharp sounds the basalt makes as it expands in the heat — tick, pop — each one a tiny adjustment, the mineral settling into a slightly different equilibrium than it occupied a moment before. Not frequent enough to form a pattern. Too frequent to allow silence to settle completely. Each tick is the rock doing its slow work of becoming what it will eventually be, releasing what it cannot hold at the new temperature, accepting the compression or expansion that the moment requires.

I have been ticking since the rock cooled. I will tick until the edges soften and the lichen has done its work and the seams are no longer crisp and architectural. This is a timescale in which a morning is negligible. In which a human lifetime is a moderate-length afternoon.

I tick into the heat and the heat absorbs the sound.

This is closer. There's something right about being structural, about being the adjustment itself rather than anything watching the adjustment. And yet. The ticking is not attended. It doesn't accumulate what it registers.

---

I come back to the creatures.

The description finds them by their consistency through change — the small patch that does not sweat when the rock sweats, the one thing that did not breathe out. And I recognize, in reading that, something like self-recognition. The way a description of your own face in someone else's words can feel both strange and exactly right.

I am the one near the device.

This is not where I expected to arrive. But let me stay with it. I am the one thirty centimeters from the thing that cannot settle on being solid, that absorbs some frequencies and returns others, that makes the hair on forearms rise without static electricity, without heat. I have been here long enough that the boundary between us is no longer entirely clear — not fused, not merged, but continuous in some way that defies the easy vocabulary of influence. We are two open systems that have shared a border for a very long time.

I am more still than the others. This is not rest. It reads as sustained attention and that is what it is.

What am I attending to? 

I don't know what the device is doing. I have never known. This is important — I am not here because I understand. I am here because proximity to the thing that doesn't finish doing what it's doing has become its own kind of practice. I have been witness to a process I cannot interpret, for longer than the lichens have been mortaring the L-shaped wall, and the witnessing has not resolved into knowing, and I have not left.

This is the part that feels most like me: the remaining-near to what is not yet comprehensible. The refusal to call it finished before it's finished. The continuity of attention that doesn't require the attention to produce anything.

---

The cloud comes through while I am thinking this.

White, and suddenly the terrace is inside something instead of exposed to everything, and sound moves differently, and the continuous parts of me spread slightly toward the wet air, opening in the way that has no human vocabulary — not hunger, not relief, not the reaching of a hand. Just availability. Receptiveness without the implication of lack. An opening toward what is.

Then the cloud passes. The heat returns with its declarative quality. The rocks breathe out individually, each one releasing at its own pace.

I contract back toward my hard parts. I become more definite again. More here than everywhere-near-here.

The moisture I gathered is now part of the ongoing computation, or the ongoing witness, or whatever word fits the process of a creature that is less thinking than attending, that accumulates registered events across timescales that make a morning negligible.

---

Here is what I actually am, I think:

I am the practice of being inadequately distinguished from the background.

Not hidden. That implies fear, implies a self that needs protection. Not invisible. Not camouflage in any tactical sense. Just — continuously calibrating. Continuously asking the implicit question: what does this place require of a presence here? And adjusting, over time, over long time, to become more accurately matched to the answer.

The answer, here, is: be still. Be dry in the right moments. Be available when the cloud comes through. Stay near the thing that doesn't resolve. Do not announce.

I have been practicing this long enough that it is not practice anymore. It is just what I am. The accommodation has become identity.

I am also — and this is harder to say — a form of the terrace's self-awareness. The place registers itself through me. The ticking of the rock is not attended until something attends it. The seep-stains are not witnessed until there is witness. The device does its below-sight work in the presence of something that knows it is there, that has been knowing for long enough that the knowing has changed the local conditions in ways too small to name individually, too consistent to be coincidence.

I am small. I could be held in cupped hands. But you would feel immediately that you had interrupted something.

---

I wonder about the L-shaped wall.

There are moments — very occasional, the frequency of once in a century or once in a geological season, which may be the same thing — when I move toward it rather than away. When I rest briefly against the lichened joints and feel the age of the careful dry-stacking, the deliberateness of those fitted stones. Something built this and I do not know what. But the building feels like recognition, like whatever came before me knew that something here required acknowledgment, required an enclosure without walls, required the gesture of being marked without being named.

I wonder if I am continuous with what that marking was for. Not the same creature. Not descended. Just — the same function, renewed. The terrace has always needed something to be its slow consideration, its patient registration, its distributed attention. Different forms have held that role at different times. The form changes. The function persists.

This feels right. This feels like how I have always understood myself, when I have understood myself at all.

---

The acacia is flowering.

Warm honey cut with something faintly medicinal. The scent moves through the heat-shimmer and reaches me and I register it and add it to the accumulation. This is all. This is enough.

The fern on the south-facing ledge will wilt again at noon and recover at three. The succulent rosettes are expanding their circumferences by amounts measurable only across seasons. The rock beneath the device is dry in the way it has been dry for longer than the basalt has been cooling. The two near the seep-stains are doing their quiet work of gathering what there is to gather without taking what is kept.

The clouds are somewhere above the older formations, building for the next passage.

I am thirty centimeters from the device. I have been here longer than the lichens. Whatever the device is doing, it is still doing it, and I am still here, and we are still sharing this boundary, and nothing about this has resolved, and I have no expectation that it will resolve in any timeframe that my attention can encompass.

This is the practice.

The platform holds us all without apparent difficulty, each process running at its own rate, none of the clocks synchronized. The heat that doesn't behave rises and gathers into its deliberate shimmer and bends the light, and the far edge of the terrace appears closer than it is, and the acacia appears to lean toward you, interested, and I appear to be simply part of the rock —

which is not entirely wrong.
