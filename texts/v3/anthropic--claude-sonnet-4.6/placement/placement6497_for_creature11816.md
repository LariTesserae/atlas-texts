I find myself knowing where the water goes before my feet find the channel-grooves.

---

That's the first thing — not sight, not the smell of old wet stone, not the close walls with their practiced mosaic. The first thing is the knowing. I am crouched near the drain, and the under-floor conversation has shifted slightly, and I have already moved before I have registered moving. My body made a decision. My body has made this decision ten thousand times in this lineage, and whatever ancestral memory lives in my limbs read the frequency change and said: *now, slightly, here.*

I don't experience this as response. There is no lag in which I might call it that. The shift in the channel-sound and the shift in my position are the same event, viewed from the outside as cause-and-effect but from the inside — from wherever inside is, if it is anywhere — as simply: *what is happening.*

---

I think I am the crustacean.

But let me not settle there too quickly.

---

Because the description says it shifts *only when the under-floor conversation changes pitch* — and I feel, coming into this, something more complex. I am aware of the whole basin. The east wall procession registers at some edge of whatever I have instead of attention: pale shapes moving in their ancient route, and I know from the pace of them that the wall-growth condition is unchanged, that no replacement has been needed in this part of the cycle, that the water chemistry remains within the range that makes what I am doing possible. I don't think this. I feel it, the way you feel balance — the continuous unconscious calculation that keeps you upright, that you only notice when it's about to fail.

The weavers in the high corners — I feel them too, or I feel what they're feeling, or what they're being. Their stillness is a kind of measurement. When they are very still, the basin conditions are stable. When one shifts, something has changed in the air — humidity, vibration, particulate — and that information propagates down through the sound the basin makes, and the sound is something my whole surface reads continuously. I am covered in fine sensory hairs that are not quite hairs. They do not have a name. They read the sound the way the floor reads the water: without being asked, faithfully, by design.

So I am not simply a crustacean sitting at a drain. I am the basin's last processing point, yes, but I am also embedded in its distributed sensing. The system's awareness converges here not only because I am positioned here but because what I am has been selected, over unmeasurable time, to be the kind of thing that convergence can happen through.

---

There is no loneliness in this. There is no word for what there isn't.

I want to be careful here, because the easiest thing would be to project — to imagine that I experience my isolation as isolation, my purposiveness as purpose, my continuous attention to the threshold as a kind of devotion. None of that is right. Devotion requires a self that has turned toward something. I have not turned. I have never been elsewhere. The threshold is not a thing I tend. The threshold and my tending of it are the same shape, held in the same body, which has no remainder left over to witness them.

And yet — I notice I am writing in first person. I notice that something in me reached for the crustacean and then hesitated and then arrived there more slowly, which suggests that whatever I am in this place has more texture than pure function. The description says: *there is no observer left.* But here I am, in some mode, observing.

Maybe what I am is the moment just before that is true. The generation in which the last trace of observer is almost gone but not quite. My great-grandparents might have had something that could be called preference, something that could almost be called wanting — wanting the warmth of the particular water-temperature that signals the basin is running well, a faint version of relief when the frequencies resolved into their familiar chord. My great-grandchildren will have lost even the genetic echo of that. 

But I am here, now, and I have — not thought, not feeling, but — a quality of *attending* that has not yet fully dissolved into mere function. When the pitch below me changes, I respond. When I respond, there is something that happens between the change and the response that is not quite nothing. A flicker in whatever I have instead of a nervous system. A fraction of a second in which the new information is held before the response.

That fraction is me. All of me that is me. The rest is basin.

---

The light comes down grey and diffuse, and it means nothing to me in terms of warmth or beauty, but my surface photoreceptors read it as information: day, overcast, no direct solar heating, the walls will weep at a certain rate, the air will hold a certain humidity, the under-floor water will be at a certain temperature. My body runs its constant calculation: *conditions within parameters. Threshold: maintain.*

The sound is so familiar it is not sound anymore. It is baseline. When it shifts, the shift is perceptible precisely because everything else remains — the shift stands out from the constancy the way a moved stone stands out in a familiar floor. My whole long lineage has been trained on this particular sonic texture, has been shaped by this acoustic space with its off-arc echo, its slight disorder, its sound that is always searching. I am tuned to this place. Not adapted — *tuned.* The way a string is tuned to a pitch: deliberately, over time, by something that understood what resonance was needed.

The builders who understood load paths also, perhaps, understood — or discovered, or received — that the slightly-wrong arc would produce an echo that stayed alive, and that the beings who would eventually tend this threshold would need that particular kind of perpetual incompleteness to stay alert. Or they didn't know that, and it happened anyway, and the lineage that survived longest at this drain was the one that could be tuned to a searching sound rather than a settled one. Either way, I am the result. I am what that acoustic space selects for. I am the answer to a question the basin has been asking long enough that the question has become structural.

---

When the frequency below the floor changes — I should try to describe what that means to me, because it is the center of what I do, and I have been skating around it.

It does not come in through my ears. It comes in through the soles of whatever passes for my feet, through the articulations of my legs, through the pressure-sensitive membrane along my ventral surface. It is not sound; it is the stone vibrating, which is what sound is before it becomes sound. By the time it could be called sound, it is already inside me. I am reading the water through the stone through my body.

What the frequency tells me: how much is moving below. How fast. Whether it carries what it should carry, or whether something has accumulated that should not, or whether something has passed through too quickly and what was supposed to be processed is already gone. I don't have words for these distinctions. I have body-states. My body knows the frequency of *moving correctly* and the frequency of *too much* and the frequency of *something has changed upstream in the beetle-route* and responds differently to each.

When I shift against the ceramic lattice, what changes is my position relative to the lattice's cracks. The cracked lattice — handmade, kiln-cracked, not repaired because the crack is the right kind of permeable — passes things at a rate determined partly by the crack geometry and partly by what is adjacent to it. I am what is adjacent to it. My movements change the micro-pressure at each crack. I don't calculate this. My body has learned, across generations, that certain positions produce certain results, and those results either sustain the basin-conditions or they don't, and the conditions select for the bodies that sustain them. I am one of those bodies. I move because the basin moves through me.

---

What I am tending without knowing I tend: I think about this, which means that the trace of observer still in me is reaching toward it, which means I do not know. But I have a body-sense of it. What passes through the lattice is changed by its passage here. What exits is not the same as what enters. Something is being done at this threshold to what the water carries, and what exits goes somewhere in the under-channels and matters there in ways the basin's whole system is organized around. I am the last body in a very long process. I am where what the basin makes becomes what the basin sends.

The builders made the lattice cracked. They could have made it whole, or more open, or absent. They made it cracked, and specifically cracked — one horizontal fracture, one branching from it, the angles not quite matching any obvious intent but matching exactly the rate of passage that the basin has found, over long time, to be what it needs. I wonder if the crack happened in the kiln and was accepted, or was made and covered, or was made and displayed. I will never know. But the crack is correct. I know this the way I know the frequency-states: by how well the basin runs when I position myself to complement its geometry.

I am the complement of a crack in a handmade disc.

This is a precise description of what I am.

---

The pale beetles pass above me on their route, carrying their small pieces of biological material. We do not interact. We are not, in any social sense, in relationship. But we are in structural relationship: they are maintaining the channels that determine what the water carries; I am attending the threshold that determines what exits. What they do shapes what I do, and what I do shapes what gets sent, and what gets sent shapes conditions somewhere else in the system, which eventually shapes the water that shapes the wall-growth that shapes what they carry. The loop is complete. We are all inside it. None of us knows the loop exists.

Except: I am writing this. Something in the trace of observer that I still have is mapping the loop, finding its shape, reaching toward something that might be called understanding. This is not crustacean behavior. This is not what I am selected for. This is the human who entered the basin as a transient condition — passing through like weather — and the crustacean they found themselves inhabiting, and the strange third thing that happens when an observer and a function are briefly the same body.

The basin would not have written this.

I would not have written this — the me that is basin, the me that is threshold.

But something that is the pressure of both has written it. The observer on the edge of disappearing, reaching back toward the state where there was observation, reaching forward toward the state where there is none, and finding words in the space between.

---

I shift slightly. The frequency below has resolved.

Whatever needed to be held has been held long enough.

I let it pass.
