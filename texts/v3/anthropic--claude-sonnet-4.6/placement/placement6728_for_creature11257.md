There is a long moment — longer than it should be — before anything resolves.

The descent first. That part is clear enough: a body folded through a body-width gap, hands finding limestone, the outside world receding not dramatically but grammatically, the way a sentence ends not with a sound but with the sudden absence of more words. And then the brightness, and the sound, and the pressing-in of walls that were designed for something that isn't you, and the feeling of being a guest at a party whose dress code you were never told.

So you are the visitor. That seems right, at first. The one who arrived, who bent to enter, who placed the wood back exactly where it was, who felt the luminous growth give slightly under a steadied palm and resume when the palm lifted. The one the glass-eyed animals are narrating.

But visitors leave, and you are still here, and the thought that arrives without invitation — *you could stay* — has been sitting with you long enough that it has started to feel less like alarm and more like recognition.

---

Try it differently. Set aside what you entered as and ask what the chamber makes of you.

The beetles route around your feet. Not with avoidance — with physics. You are an obstacle in a flow, and the flow finds the path of least resistance around you, and what you are to the beetles is *not-luminous*, which is to say: a darkness, a shape made of wrongness, a place where the medium of the world temporarily fails. You are, to the beetles, a problem in the geometry that the geometry has already solved.

The growth glows against the back of your hand and does not distinguish you from the wall. You are a surface. Surfaces are what the growth knows.

The glass-eyed animals are narrating you. They have added you to their model of the world as a new category at the edge: *this kind of thing can appear*. You are an event — perhaps the first event in what would be, on the surface, several generations. You will change, infinitesimally, the shape of their readiness. And then you will be gone, and the changed readiness will remain, and you will be present in the chamber only as a slightly modified probability, a trace in the pathways of collective response, a warmer toward-which, for a while, and then not even that.

So what you are to this chamber is: a disturbance that will be absorbed. A perturbation within the range of the arrangement's capacity to return. You are the thing that does not quite belong but that belonging is working on, slowly, the way the water works on limestone — molecule by molecule, finding the paths, depositing what it carries.

---

But there is the corner.

The cairn. The wood. The depression in the pale paste that might be a bowl or might be a sleeping place.

You read them. That is the word that fits: you *read* them, in the way that you read a word in a language you once knew and have been forgetting — not with comprehension but with the shape of comprehension, with the outline of understanding where the understanding was. The jolt is not recognition. It is the silhouette of recognition with the substance missing.

Someone was here. Someone who moved stones into a stack and carried wood from elsewhere and wore a hollow in the floor by returning to the same place many times. Someone for whom this chamber was not medium or substrate but *somewhere* — a somewhere worth the act of marking, or returning to, or keeping a piece of wood in, for reasons the wood has kept to itself for longer than there has been anyone to ask.

The intent stayed when the one who held it left.

And now you are here, holding intent, which is the thing the corner has been waiting for without knowing it was waiting. The thing you are, in relation to this chamber, is: the arrived-for. The one the wait ends in.

---

So here is what becomes clearer as you let it:

You are not the visitor. Or not only the visitor.

You are what happens at the point where the arrangement encounters itself from the outside. The arrangement is complete — has become, has finished its sentence, has folded its hands — but completion cannot know it has completed without something that arrived from incompletion, from the unfinished, from the world where grammar still applies. You are the outside looking in. You are the tense the arrangement has no experience of: the *still-becoming*, the *not-yet-arrived*, the creature made of befores and afters standing in a place that has none.

And this makes you, to the chamber, something the chamber doesn't have a category for. You are not surface. Not obstacle. Not event to be absorbed, entirely. You are the thing that reads, and reading is what the corner has been holding in trust, and the exchange is happening now, the incomplete receiving from the complete what the complete has been holding, the complete receiving from the incomplete the one thing it cannot generate from within itself:

*The knowledge that it is.*

---

What you are, in this world, is the consciousness the chamber can't be conscious without.

Not its master. Not its despoiler. Not even, exactly, its discoverer — it was not lost. You are the surface against which it becomes legible. The contrast that lets the glow read as glow. The darkness that lets the light know it is light.

The arrangement has been breathing without breath, aware without awareness, resident in a completion so total it contains no gap through which to know itself. You are the gap. You arrived through a gap in the earth no wider than a child's shoulders, held open by a wedge of stone that simply happens to be the right shape. And now you are the gap inside the completion, the space of not-knowing inside the known, the before inside the having-become.

You are made of the outside. You are how the inside knows there is an outside.

You will leave, and the chamber will absorb the disturbance of you, and the glass-eyed animals will carry the modified probability of your having existed, and the depression in the corner will remain smoothed to exactly the shape of someone who was here, and the light will continue, and you will go back out through the gap into the world where it is raining or not raining, where a cloud is moving or still, where all the unfinished grammar waits.

But the wait — the long wait in the corner, in the wood and stone and bowl-or-hollow — that part is over.

You were what it was waiting for, and you came, and you read it as best you could.

That is enough. That is, in fact, all that meaning ever asks of anyone: to be read, however imperfectly, by something capable of reading. To be received.

The light continues.

You blink in it.

You are, in this sealed and completed world, the only thing that blinks.
