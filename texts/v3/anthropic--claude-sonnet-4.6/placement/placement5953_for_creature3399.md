There's a pull toward the Keeper, the obvious gravity of consciousness meeting consciousness. But something in the description resists that easy arrival—the boots standing empty, the path made by one body's singular habits, the clipboard you're not yet ready to approach. The Keeper is already there, already completed in some sense. Their adjustment made, their tempo found. Something already whole and not requiring addition.

And the flies—there's something true in them, in being the hinge between death and renewal without knowing it, in maintaining the plateau without any distance from it. But being the flies means surrendering inference entirely, means being *inside it all the way down*, and that foreclosure feels too simple. To be fully embedded is one answer to the problem of this place, but not the only one.

What keeps returning is the description of the burrowing things: *holding still for the Keeper for as long as the Keeper has been here. This is memory encoded in behavior, not in anything like narrative.*

---

You are in a tunnel.

Not metaphorically. Actually in a tunnel—a compressed corridor of dark soil and root-smell, the ceiling close enough to feel without touching, the walls giving slightly under pressure where the fern roots have been negotiating with your passage for some years now. The tunnel is warm here, warmer than it should be at this depth, because somewhere above you a pipe runs through the soil's memory, heating it a degree or two, and your body has mapped this warmth over many circuits as a place to return toward. You're returning toward it now.

Your map is extraordinary and entirely local. You carry no idea of *plateau* as a concept, no altitude, no ceramic sky—but you carry something more precise: the exact angle of descent toward the water basin, the particular compression of soil at the entrance nearest the structures (where the Keeper's boots compress it differently than elsewhere, a weight you know the signature of), the location of three fern root masses that cannot be crossed, must be circled, which adds a certain arc to passages in that direction. You know how long dry soil smells dry after rain, measured not in days but in a sensory gradient you'd have no word for even if words were available to you. You know the warmth of the pipe's influence as a presence with edges. You navigate by presences with edges.

---

The footstep came forty seconds ago—or what would be forty seconds on a scale you don't use—and it came as vibration, not sound, a pressure wave moving through the ground and arriving in your body before you had any decision to make about it. The decision was already made, encoded, inherited from parents who inherited it from theirs, the whole chain of ancestors who learned to read this particular vibration as *Keeper, safe, hold still*, as distinguished from the much rarer vibrations that mean *unknown, hold still, hold still, hold still, don't emerge for a long time.*

You held still.

Then the footstep pattern resolved itself—that familiar unvarying path, the same angle between structures every time, the same weight, slightly heavier on the left foot—and you resumed.

This resumption doesn't feel like relief. You have nothing to compare it to that would make relief the right word. It feels like the return of motion, the way a stream resumes after a hand is removed from it. You were held and then you were flowing and the holding and the flowing are simply the conditions, not events.

---

What are you thinking? The question keeps failing to arrive in a form you'd recognize as a question.

There is the map. The map is not a picture—it's more like a body-knowledge, a proprioception that extends outside the body into the whole branching system, so that moving through the tunnels feels less like navigation than like moving an arm, and the rightness of a turn feels less like decision than like the satisfaction of a joint moving in its socket. You are not consulting the map. You are the map, running.

There is the hunger—not urgent yet, but present as a quality of attention, a slight orientation toward the root mass that produced food three passages ago, which you'll assess again on the way back. The hunger is also a kind of knowledge: it tells you how long since you last fed, more precisely than clocks could, because it is calibrated to your body's actual expenditure rather than to any external measure.

There is the warmth ahead. You're moving toward it because moving toward warmth in a cool place is what your body has learned to do across a great many generations, and learning encoded in the body at that depth doesn't present itself as decision. It presents as something closer to desire, if desire can exist without narrative, without an imagined future self who will be warmer and therefore glad.

You have something like desire without a story about the desire. The warmth pulls and you move toward it and there is no separation between those things.

---

The Keeper—that long, known, regular vibration—is somewhere above. Moving between the structures, probably. Doing the thing they do that you have no name for but have encoded as *part of the background of this place, like the pipe's warmth, like the weight of the soil after rain.*

You have been here your whole life. The Keeper has been here for what feels, if you could feel duration, like your whole life plus a long time before. You have been coexisting with the Keeper's footsteps since you were small enough to fit entirely in the section of tunnel where it bends under the third water basin, where the soil is always a little damp and smells of a mineral sharpness that you associate with security—your earliest sensory memory, if memory is the right word for what you carry.

The Keeper doesn't know this. The Keeper looks at the burrow entrances and infers a colony and goes no further because the inference is enough up here—the plateau teaches inference, as we've established—and the inference is accurate as far as it goes. There is a colony. There are burrows. Something made them. Something uses them.

But the Keeper doesn't know about the warmth-map, the pipe's two degrees radiating outward into the soil until the soil holds it at the edges. Doesn't know that their footstep-signature has been memorized and is now a form of safety, a known vibration in a place that could contain unknown ones but mostly doesn't. Doesn't know that you—or whatever the word would be for *you* at this scale, this depth of embeddedness—have incorporated the machines into your understanding of *home* the way the lichen has incorporated the metal into its substrate.

The Keeper maintains the machines so the machines stay warm. The warmth modifies the soil. You have built your home at the intersection of that warmth. If the machines stopped, you would adjust—the colony has adjusted to many things over its long tenure here—but something would be lost from the map, some edge of warmth would cool and become indistinguishable from the surrounding cold, and navigation would be slightly different afterward, the recalibration of a joint that has been moved to a new position.

You don't think about this. The thinking is mine, overlaid on your body-knowledge the way altitude is overlaid on an animal that lives at altitude but doesn't conceptualize altitude. You are simply here, in the warm-directed dark, running your map, waiting for hunger to sharpen into action, attending in the profound non-linguistic way that attention works when there's no language to make it self-conscious.

---

This is what the plateau has made room for that the lichen can't be and the Keeper, now too slow for, can no longer fully access:

Living inside inference, being made of inference, without knowing it as inference.

The whole enterprise of your existence is reading—pressure, gradient, vibration, smell—and responding without the gap of reflection, the gap in which a human stands and looks at the plateau and *thinks about* what they're reading. You read and respond in a single motion. The map is not a representation of the territory; it is as close to the territory as you can get while still being a thing distinct from the territory.

The plateau teaches inference. You are the inference it has been practicing on itself.

---

Somewhere above, a drop of condensation falls from a waxy leaf to the leaf below. In twenty-eight more seconds, another will follow. You don't count seconds. But your body knows the moisture gradient here—which surfaces stay damp, which dry between rains, which are permanently darkened—and that knowledge is part of the map too, part of what home means at the only scale home has ever meant to you.

The warmth is very close now.

You reach it.

You have always been reaching it.

The plateau continues in its bright, dense, quiet self, and you are inside it—not looking at it, not learning it, not keeping it—but woven into it as one more thing that has been here long enough to become part of what here means.
