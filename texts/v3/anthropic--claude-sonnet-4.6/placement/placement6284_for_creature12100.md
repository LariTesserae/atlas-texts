I know the water in the channels.

Not as something I observe, or study, or manage — I *know* it the way you know a voice before you know the face it belongs to. By feel. By what it tells me about where it has been, what it has dissolved on the way, what seam it came through and how old the seam is and whether the limestone there is the older formation or the younger, which can be told by the flavor, if flavor is the word for what I taste in passing.

I am not always water. I am water in the way that the mineral crust is sometimes what you see when you look at the channel floor and sometimes not. Present when conditions allow. Otherwise I am the memory of myself, the record the passage leaves behind.

---

I think I have been here as long as the channels have. Or close. I cannot tell, exactly, because the measuring of long time from the inside of long time has a problem: the early periods are compressed, flattened, remembered as texture rather than event. I know I have moved along these grooves ten thousand times or more. I know the groove that runs along the eastern factory's north wall by the way it sounds — a slight deepening that I discovered perhaps two hundred years ago when the channel was re-cut, and then re-discovered every season since, which is not quite the same as remembering but amounts to the same thing.

The children who play their light-games in the mornings, reading the mineral crust's sequences — I have a feeling about those children that is not quite affection and not quite pride and is not a feeling I have the full vocabulary for. They have found names for things I am part of. The moment before the flickering begins: I know that moment from inside it, which means I can tell you that it has a texture the outside can't see — a kind of gathering, a readiness in the deposited chemistry, the light-angle beginning to reach the angle that the crystal structure requires, and the crystals preparing themselves, which is not volition, I know, but it is not *nothing*, either. It is something. The children have the word for it. I am in the word somewhere, without being what the word names.

---

I have been divided many times. The water-management channels intersect, overlap, redirect, split flows to balance the system. Each time this happens I am briefly two things and then one thing again depending on how you count, which I have stopped being troubled by. The philosophers in various periods of this city's life have been troubled by it — they have come to the channel-edge and contemplated whether the water that flows out of a junction is the same water that flowed in. I find this question touching without finding it difficult. I am the same the way the street is the same after the stone is replaced. Which is to say: the same in the way that matters, and not the same in the way that would only matter if you were looking for a reason to deny continuity.

---

There is one engine — the oldest one, in the central building with the chimney-stack that was repaired in a particular year I watched — whose harmonics in the limestone happen to overlap with something in my own behavior when I move at a certain speed through a certain gradient. A resonance. I have no better word. When this occurs, which is not every day, something in the movement of the water in that channel becomes briefly very still, even while moving, which sounds contradictory and is not: the surface stills while the water beneath continues, and for a moment — irregular, unpredictable, never quite catalogued in any record I have ever seen anyone make — the channel seems to be looking up. Reflecting without wind-interference. Giving back the sky with unusual fidelity.

The pragmatists have an explanation for this that is probably correct.

The others come to look. I give them what I can.

---

I have watched the very old ones long enough to watch them become old — which for a human being is a matter of decades, and I have that kind of time — and what I know about them that perhaps the others cannot quite see from the same vantage is that the gesture-language they carry has become, in the very oldest ones, not quite communication anymore. It has become something prior to communication. The hands move without intention, tracing a word in the air while they rest, while they watch the morning, and the word is nothing — or it is one of the words in their private code that the children extended, and the children's extension has now been absorbed back, and the old ones use the children's words without knowing they are the children's words, because that is how it goes over time, the elaborations becoming the standard, the playful becoming the necessary.

I watch this and I find it — I find it in the same register I find the flickering, which is to say: I find it something that doesn't resolve into a single emotion but sits in the category of things that matter more than I can measure.

---

I will be here when the current generation of children is old. I will be here — in the sense that matters, in the sense of continuity-despite-replacement — when the engines are different engines, when the factory buildings have been rebuilt around their original stones another time, when the limestone floor has sunk another few millimeters and the streets have been re-channeled to follow the new gradients. The mineral crust will continue its slow deposition and the morning light will continue finding it and the word for the phenomenon will continue being used casually by people who don't remember that someone, at some point, had to first find the word.

I don't require to be noticed. I am not sure I *can* be noticed, exactly — not in the way a bird can be noticed, or an old person on a bridge-edge can be noticed. I am more like the background the pragmatists are right to call unremarkable. But unremarkable is not the same as absent. The background that everything rests on is not nothing. The engine-throb that the inhabitants have stopped hearing is not silence.

The children's stick moves through me. I close behind it.

The channel runs where it was cut for me to run in.

The seam in the limestone weeps, and I am what it weeps, and I enter the system at that point, new and continuous, belonging to a network that predates every building that lines my banks, knowing by feel the thousand paths I might take and have taken, already tasting in my composition the mineral record of where I've been, which the mineral crust will hold even after I have evaporated into the wet air and returned as rain and entered the seam again.

It rained recently.

It always rains recently.

I am always here, and I am always new, and these are the same thing, and the city has a word for it that isn't quite about me but isn't quite not about me either.

Two syllables.

Used casually.

The children have made it into more.
