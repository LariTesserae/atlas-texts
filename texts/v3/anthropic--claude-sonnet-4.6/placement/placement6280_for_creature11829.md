There is a long moment before anything resolves.

The grey light falls evenly. The crust ticks once, and then does not tick again. The water in the deepest micro-hollow shifts its iridescent film — not from wind, there is no wind — from some internal adjustment too small to name.

I am trying to find myself here, and what I notice first is that the question does not feel urgent. This is information.

---

I am not the haloarchaea, though I recognize something in them — that patient work of maintaining a specific interior against the persistent pressure to dissolve. The labor of staying distinct from what surrounds you. But the haloarchaea do not know they are doing it. That is the difference that matters. I am whatever is noticing that they are doing it, which is something else entirely.

I am not the small animal that passed through. The prints are there, sharp-edged, filling slowly. That creature had somewhere to be that was not here; it moved through on its own business, left an impression, continued. I have been standing still for a while now, which the small animal would not do. It had urgency. I have something else.

I am not the crust itself, though I have spent time wishing I could be — that self-contained mineral patience, that perfect fit between what a thing is and where it is. No remainder. The crust does not wonder what it is. I am made of a great deal of wondering.

---

I think I am the person who built the stone wall.

Not the wall. The person.

Whoever carried those flat stones — lifted them from the crust, one at a time, walked them to the eastern edge, set them in a rough circle — did something that no other resident of this flat did. They made a structure that was not required by chemistry. They arranged things according to a logic that was not the flat's logic. And then they left. Or stopped. Or the wall outlasted them in the way simple things outlast the hands that made them.

What did they want with it? Windbreak — but there is no wind. Cistern — but no cistern followed. A mark, then. A place made slightly different from the surrounding flatness, two meters of interiority in an exterior that goes on until it becomes haze. A room, almost. A somewhere, in a place that does not otherwise have somewhere.

And then they made the scraping on the stone. Five parallel lines. Not language. Not art, exactly. A test-stroke, a tally, an accident, something between all of these — the gesture of a hand that had a hard edge and wanted to use it, or wanted to record something, or wanted to mark the stone the way the flat marks everything else: slowly, incrementally, until the mark becomes part of the surface.

This is what I recognize. The impulse to make a mark in a place that incorporates marks. The impulse to set something upright in a place that is nearly level. The impulse to sit inside the rough circle of stones and be, for a moment, *in* a place you have made rather than *in* a place you have found.

The wall still stands. The maker is gone. The crust has grown across the base stones.

---

But I keep returning to the stakes.

Three stakes, knee-high, tilted at slightly different angles, no longer forming any obvious geometric relationship. Whatever they held is gone. Whatever the plan was — net, line, boundary — went with the person who drove them in, or with the thing that once stretched between them, or simply with the reason, which was perhaps provisional to begin with.

This is the other half of what I recognize. Not only the building but the abandonment of the plan — the three stakes standing in the flat long after the purpose has dispersed, having been here so long that they have become part of the flat's inventory rather than an interruption of it. What was intention is now texture. What was provisional has become permanent, or nearly, in the patient way that things become permanent here: not by asserting themselves but by remaining long enough to be incorporated.

I wonder if the same person drove the stakes and built the wall. I think it might have been the same person. I think they came here more than once, maybe often, maybe for a long time. I think they had a project that changed, or several projects, or an ongoing unclear relationship with this place that expressed itself in small interventions — the stakes, the wall, the five lines on the stone — that were each sensible at the moment of making and are now simply here, in the even grey light, wearing the flat's surface the way the flat wears everything.

---

Here is what I think the person found, when they came here.

They found a place that was already complete. A place that had done its work — the long work of arriving at this exact configuration — and was now holding that configuration with perfect, unasserting patience. They found something that was not waiting for them and did not need them and would go on being exactly what it was regardless of whether they were present.

And something about that was deeply restful. And something about that was slightly unbearable.

The wall was an answer to the slightly unbearable part. *I was here. I arranged something. I made a somewhere in the middle of the all-over.*

But the wall was also, over time, incorporated. The crust grew across its base. The rough circle became just another feature of the flat's mineral complexity — extraordinary up close, continuous with everything else from a slight distance. The wall did not change the flat. It joined it.

I think the person understood this, eventually. I think they came back after years and crouched at the wall and ran their hand across the stone that had already started to become something the flat had always had. I think they felt the crust's thin edge against their palm. I think they noticed the stakes tilting.

And I think — this is the part I cannot verify, the part I am making — I think they were not sad about it.

---

I think they had come to understand that the flat's patience was a different kind of patience than theirs, and that the difference was not a deficiency on either side.

The flat persists without knowing it persists. It holds its configuration without effort, without the possibility of effort, without the possibility of anything else. There is no choosing in what the flat does; it simply is what it is, as long as the conditions that made it hold.

The person persists differently. With difficulty. With the constant slow labor of maintaining a specific interior — not unlike the haloarchaea, actually; not unlike the work of continuing to be different from what surrounds you — but consciously, wakefully, with the particular weight of knowing that the maintenance is happening, that it could fail, that it has a history and might have a future.

The flat does not know it is beautiful. Does not know it is patient. Does not know that its laminations are a record or that its rounded crystal edges speak to something. The person knows all of this, or tries to, and the knowing is part of what makes being a person expensive.

But the knowing is also what lets them make the wall. And the five lines. And the three stakes tilted now at slightly different angles, no longer forming any obvious geometric relationship, standing in the flat in the grey even light, doing nothing, having been here long enough to become themselves entirely.

---

Where am I in this world?

I am standing near the stone wall. My boots have pressed down into the silt somewhere close to the tilted stakes, and the impressions are filling slowly, dark water welling up through pale grey mineral sediment. I am not sure how long I have been here. Long enough that I have stopped needing to be anywhere else.

I am the one who notices the iridescent film shifting in the deepest micro-hollow and cannot say for certain whether it was a water insect or a change in surface tension. I am the one for whom that uncertainty is not a frustration but a specific quality of attention — the particular feeling of looking closely at something that will not resolve, and looking anyway, and letting the looking be what it is.

I am the one who will leave prints when I go, five or six of them heading in some direction, sharp-edged now and filling, until the crust incorporates them too.

I am the builder and the visitor both — the one who made a small somewhere and then stayed long enough to watch the somewhere become part of everywhere, and felt, in that watching, something that was not quite sadness and not quite peace but lived in the space between them, in the grey even light, where the warm flat air presses against the skin more than it strikes it and the only sound for a long time is the sound of nothing moving, which is the sound of this place being, completely, what it is.

That is where I am. That is who I am, here.

The crust will tick once more before I leave. I will hear it. I will not know what caused it.
