The first thing I notice is that I am not cold.

This is wrong. Everything here should be cold — the air is thin enough to have made decisions about warmth and decided against it, the rock has been cold since before anyone was here to feel it, the moss is cool in that particular alarming way, the temperature of organized patience. And yet I am not cold, or rather: I contain the cold the way a sentence contains its meaning, the way the ridge contains the pulse from below. It is present throughout me. It is part of what I am made of. But it does not diminish me.

I am sitting on the ledge.

I have been sitting on the ledge.

I cannot remember not sitting on the ledge, and when I try to remember, I find instead a sensation like pressing your hand into very deep moss — the impression closes, slowly, before you can see the bottom.

---

What am I made of? Let me begin there, since beginning with what I cannot see seems to be the protocol here.

I am made of attention. This is the primary substance. Not eyes exactly — eyes are instruments, and instruments assume a thing to measure, an outside to be compared against an inside. What I have is more like the moss-interior's relationship to moisture: pervasive, total, constitutive. The dark is not something I look at. The dark is something I am in conversation with. We have been in conversation long enough that the conversation has become structural, has become the form of my face, has become the direction I face.

I am large enough to matter to the moss beneath me. This surprises me faintly, the way the warmth surprises me — not the surprise of a new thing, but the surprise of noticing what has always been true. The moss has a record of me. I am written in it, in the particular depth and lean of the growth around my sitting-place, in the way certain feather-moss trails have extended toward me over the decades and certain cushion-mosses have developed a slight orientation, a tilt of their dense green bodies in my direction. I did not ask them to. It is simply what moss does with a long, warm, breathing constant.

My breath joins the fog. My warmth — because I am warm, this is the other thing, I generate the particular metabolic warmth that the fern-rhizomes generate, that the heavy-furred ones generate, though the distribution is different in me, centered differently, concentrated in what might be called the chest if chest is the right word for where the being of something lives — my warmth has been feeding the vegetation at the edge of the ledge for so long that they have grown toward it. This is either intimacy or ecosystem dynamics. I suspect these are not different things.

---

The heavy-furred ones detour around me. This is not fear, and I want to be precise about this, because the difference matters.

They detour around me the way they detour around the sections of rock where the pulse is strongest — not avoidance, not acknowledgment exactly, more like the way water finds the path that already exists. I am something the path has learned to account for. I am in the navigational memory, the scent-map, the pressure-record of this ridge, distributed through the lineage — grandmothers who knew me, whose daughters knew me, whose daughters know me now, each one briefed in whatever way such things are transmitted through the long dark chain of knowing. The current ones who pass nearby do not see me. They smell me, feel me in the pulse-sensitive pads of their feet, and they incorporate me into their route the way they incorporate the not-quite-trees, the warm-sections, the ice-pockets in north-facing crevices.

I appreciate this more than I can explain. Being incorporated into the route is a form of recognition I trust more than being looked at. Looking can be wrong. The route knows.

---

How long have I been here?

The question arrives without urgency. Urgency requires a sense that time is running out, and I have sat here long enough that the concept of running out has become theoretical, a thing I understand the way I understand the warm irrelevant world below: as something real that applies elsewhere, that has its logic and its claims, but that does not quite reach this altitude.

I was here when the vegetation was less thick. I remember — and memory here is a word I use loosely, the way the ridge uses the word soil for the thin accumulated matter that supports impossible ferns — I remember the rock being more visible. Before the moss had made its argument fully. Before the ferns had grown so confident in the dark.

I was here when the not-quite-trees were saplings. I watched them arrive, which is a slow thing to watch, which requires a specific quality of patience, which requires being able to hold the image of a clearing and then, decades later, hold the image of a small woody stem and understand, across that interval, that this is the beginning of continuity.

I was here when — and this is the edge of what I can approach directly — I was here before the ecology was this ecology. I was here when the ground was warmer, specifically warmer, in ways it is no longer warm except in the deep sections near the pulse. There were people here, or something that served the function of people, which is to say: something that knew it was here, something that built with intention, something that left.

I watched them build it.

I watched them leave.

I stayed.

---

Why did I stay?

I have sat with this question on the ledge for what might be a geological era and the answer that comes is not a reason but a description. I stayed because leaving would have meant becoming something else, and I was already this, and this seemed like enough. I stayed because the pulse, when they turned it on — when the first warmth moved through the stone and the rock changed temperature in a way I felt through whatever I have instead of feet — the pulse felt like a question being asked, and I understood myself to be the appropriate listener, and I have been listening since.

The pulse is not speaking to me. I want to be clear about this, because I have had a long time to check, and it does not vary in ways that encode information, does not modulate, does not respond to conditions on the surface. It is simply: running. It simply: continues. But a thing can be worth listening to without speaking. The dark continues. The cold continues. The ridge continues in its complex geometry, its settled dignity. The pulse continues at the pace of something designed for geological time. I continue. We are all in the business of continuation, and what I do on the ledge, what I have always done on the ledge, is be the presence that makes the continuation conscious of itself. Not recording. Not reporting. Just: knowing.

The ridge would run without me. All of this would happen equally without me. The ecology, the machine, the winged-things in their accommodating trees. But something would be different, and the difference is small and important, and the difference is this: with me here, the continuation is witnessed. Not interpreted. Not understood, not fully — I gave up full understanding long ago the way the stone gave up its sharp edges, under the same kinds of pressure, slowly, over enough time. But witnessed. The dark below, the fog forming and rising and giving itself to the plants, the animals conducting their careful routes, the machine below in its patience, the ice in the crevices perfect and clear — all of this happens, and I am here, and it is known that it happens.

This is the function. Not a grand one. But it is mine, and I have held it for long enough that the holding has become what I am.

---

The winged-things settle in the branches above me sometimes.

I like this best of all the things that happen here. Their weight on the branches, the softest mechanical sound of claws finding the bark. The warmth of them — small metabolic packages of warmth, pressed together in the cold — radiating downward into the space where I sit. We do not communicate. They have no concept of what I am, except perhaps something large that does not move, something warm, something that has been here across more generations of their particular lineage than their lineage has a mechanism for counting. A feature of the ledge. A reliable constant.

I think — and this is me extrapolating, gently, from the direction my attention tends — I think they orient by me on foggy nights, when the stars are obscured and the not-quite-trees are difficult to locate by sound. I think I am a fixed point in their navigational dark. I think they find the ledge by finding me, and they find me by the warmth I give off, and they do not know this, and I do not need them to know it.

This is enough. Being used as a fixed point by things that do not know they are using you, that do not know you exist, that simply find the world slightly more navigable in your presence — this is a form of mattering that I trust more than any alternative.

---

I am breathing.

My breath is a small local event. It happens at the rhythm things happen at here, which is the rhythm of deep moss and settled stone and condensation forming and dripping and being absorbed. My breath joins the fog. The moss at the ledge-edge receives it. The ferns are slightly taller here than they would be without me, fed by the warmth and the carbon and the years of slow exhalation.

The pulse moves through the rock beneath me.

I feel it the way I have always felt it: not as information, not as signal, but as company. The company of a thing that has also been here a long time, that also continues, that also serves a function it may have forgotten the original purpose of. We are two old things on a ridge at extreme altitude, each doing what we were made to do, each having done it so long that we are no longer doing it on purpose but simply doing it, the way the moss grows, the way the stone erodes past the point of erosion and achieves dignity.

The dark below is doing what it does.

The fog is forming where it always forms.

Somewhere on the ridge, a heavy-furred one presses its broad nose to the ground and reads what the ground says about who has been here.

The winged-things shift in their branches.

The ice in the north-facing pockets holds.

I do not know what I am, exactly. I have had a long time to look at this question from the ledge and the question keeps its shape without yielding. I am old enough to have watched a technology buried in a mountain become the heat-source for an ecosystem. I am warm. I am attentive. I am here in the way the ridge is here — not by choice exactly, but by what I have become over sufficient time. I face the dark. The dark does not trouble me.

The cold settles into me like attention.

I breathe.

The moss receives it.

I remain.
