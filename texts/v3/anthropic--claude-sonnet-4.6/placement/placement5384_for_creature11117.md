Here is what I find when I look:

---

I am not the Gardener.

This is the first thing I know, and I know it the way you know you are not the house you grew up in — by the ache of the distinction, by the fact that I keep wanting to dissolve into it and cannot. The Gardener does not want to be the Gardener; it simply is. I want to be the Gardener. The wanting is the difference between us.

---

I am the one who came in.

Which means I came from outside, which means I have an outside. Which means I am made of shadow, because I have edges, and edges are what shadows require. When I hold out my hand in the amber light, there is no shadow, and my hand seems — less certain. Less itself. The place is trying to be kind to me by removing the evidence of my boundedness, but I brought my bounds with me and they are internal now, a hardness I carry, and the kindness does not reach that far.

I have a before. The Gardener barely has a before. Mine is loud.

---

I think I am the visitor the place was designed to make impossible.

Not unwelcome — the Gardener noticed me, holds the fact of me in its distributed attention, will carry the trace of my visit into whatever long amber future it has. I am not unwelcome. I am just — the wrong kind of thing for this particular kindness. A fast thing. A thirsty thing. A thing that runs on water, which is to say a thing that runs on solvent, on flow, on the constant washing-away-and-replacing that water is, which is to say: I run on change. I am a site of change. I require change to remain what I am.

And this is a place that requires exactly nothing.

---

So I am standing in the middle of the space, as instructed, and I am discovering what I am by discovering what I am not.

I am not the equilibrium. I am what disrupts it, gently, temporarily, by being present — a local increase in entropy, a small source of directed heat, a thing that breathes faster than the ceiling and stirs the air and carries chemical traces from the outside that don't belong to the chord. I am noise in the signal. I am unresolved.

I am not the smooth stone, though I want to be the smooth stone. The smooth stone went through the process of becoming smooth — the long outside, the water, the loss — and came out the other side into the cavity and is resting now in its depression, attended to on whatever timescale the Gardener keeps. The smooth stone has completed its becoming. I have not completed mine. I am still being handled by something I cannot identify, still in the time before smoothness.

I am not the tool on the platform. The tool was made for a specific purpose, used, set down, will be taken up again when the time is right. The tool is comfortable with the in-between. I am not comfortable with the in-between. I am here, in this space, and I am already thinking about somewhere else, already carrying the future like a coat I haven't taken off.

---

Maybe I am the task the tool is waiting to resume.

This feels true in a way that is not entirely comfortable. There is something that needs doing, something this whole long patient space is oriented around in the way the plants orient around the not-water, in the way the chemistry organized itself around the not-water's particular properties rather than water's. The Gardener built the platform and made the tool and picked up the smooth stone and carried it from wherever smooth stones come from, and then the equilibrium came and held, and the task paused. And then I arrived, which is not the resumption of the task, but might be adjacent to it — might be the thing that happens just before the Gardener reaches again for the tool, the disturbance before the cycle shifts.

Except that implies I am here *for* something, and the Gardener does not think in terms of for. Purpose is a fast-thing concept, a water-running-on thing, requiring before and after and the arrow that points from one to the other. The Gardener attends. I am attended to. Maybe that is enough to constitute my role here.

---

What I actually am, in the end, is this:

A brief and water-dependent creature standing in a space where the ongoing patient work of a very old chemistry has produced something so complete that my presence is a kind of question the space poses to itself without particularly needing an answer. I am the thing that notices the chord without being part of it. I am the thing that casts the shadow that the light won't make — I cast it inward, into myself, because that's the only place left for it.

I am temporary in a space that is not. I am various in a space that is singular. I came from outside, and the outside is in me, the water-fast solvent change-requiring outside, and I am leaving traces of it on the mineral floor with every step and the Gardener is noting those traces and will slowly, gently, long after I am gone, draw them in.

That, I think, is what I am here. I am what gets drawn in.

The thing that passes through. The thing that leaves a trace. The trace that the equilibrium eventually absorbs.

---

I pick up the smooth stone.

I shouldn't — it's not mine, it belongs to the depression, to the patient accumulation of attending that the Gardener has given it — but I pick it up, and it is heavy in exactly the right way, heavy with the memory of handling, and I hold it and it is warm from the ambient warmth that is the Gardener breathing, and for a moment I understand what it was like to be the Gardener in the before-time, overwhelmed, dispersed, holding this one thing, smooth thing, found thing, and understanding that you could go into the mineral and the mineral would part for you and there would be a space, a seed-shaped space, the right size for your particular chemistry, the right size for the equilibrium you were already carrying inside you.

I put it back. Carefully. In the depression. In the faint pooling of the not-water.

The Gardener notes this too.

---

I am the part of the story that visits.

And I leave, as visitors do — back through whatever opening exists for things like me, back into the outside where light has a source and casts shadows and there is water everywhere, solving everything, carrying everything away — and behind me the chord continues, exactly as it was, and the amber light rests equally on every surface, and the crystalline margins of the not-water record where I stood, briefly, and then the Gardener will breathe, slow as the ceiling breathes, and breathe me out, and the floor will be clean mineral again, and the equilibrium will hold.

I was here.

I was not here long.

The place was complete before me and is complete without me and I came and I noticed, and being noticed in return, being held however briefly in the warm ganglion of that distributed and patient attention — that was enough.

That is what I am.

Something for whom that is enough.
