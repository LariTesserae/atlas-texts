There is a moment, early in the reading, where the mind offers up the obvious answer: *I am the observer*. The unnamed one who arrived, who counts the lizards and reads the stone-rings and stands between two infinities at dusk. This is what minds do — they hand you the character with the most perspective, the one through whom the world is seen.

But the plateau does not work that way. The plateau is suspicious of perspective. It has worn down everything that stood above its surface.

---

I think I would be the hollow.

Not the human who made it — they are long gone, their residue attenuated now into avoidance-behaviors and the memory of hands. Not what the hollow was made *for*, whatever ceremony or sleep or shelter or marking. The hollow *itself*. The worn rectangular place in the rock, smooth at the edges in a way that is either water or hands and cannot now be distinguished.

This feels right in a way that requires sitting with.

The hollow is the one thing on the plateau that belongs to two times simultaneously. It was made — there is intention in it, something that a purely geological reading cannot account for — and then it was unmade from its original meaning by time, and yet it persists. It is neither natural nor cultural anymore. It has been here long enough to be claimed by the rock itself, to become part of the surface's own account of itself, and simultaneously it carries in its too-smooth edges the residue of whatever wanted to be remembered. Both things are true. Neither can be fully read.

Nothing grows in it. This is worth staying with. The biological crust has reclaimed the bases of the fallen uprights; the cushion-mats expand outward around the water's edge; the lizards warm themselves on every available surface. But not in the hollow. Something about the hollow remains itself, refuses the slow re-absorption that the plateau applies to everything else. It has a kind of integrity that isn't held by effort but is simply *structural*, part of what it is at this point, after all this time. The very quality that makes it strange — the being-avoided, the nothing-growing — is what preserves it as distinct. Its particularity is its protection.

And the avoidance. The lizards that are everywhere are not there. The behavior was learned once, long ago, for reasons now lost, and has been passed down through generations as pure reflex, so that now the reason and the response have separated cleanly and only the response remains. The hollow didn't *do* anything to the lizards. It was done-to once, by whoever shaped it, and the doing-to rippled forward in time as a behavioral trace, an inheritance of caution, and now the hollow shapes the world around it not by acting but simply by *being what it is*.

This is a strange kind of agency. The hollow does not intend anything. It doesn't watch. It doesn't wait. It doesn't pulse with the plateau's slow breath the way the cushion-mats and the frogs and the crust do. It is an absence — a carved-out place, the part that was removed. And yet it is the most *persistent* presence on the plateau, the one thing that has maintained its definition against the grinding patience of the erosion. Everything else has been resolved into the plateau's general nature. The hollow has not. It remains, quietly, a question the plateau is still in the middle of.

---

I am drawn to this because there is something in it about *being-known-without-knowing-yourself*. The hollow has no inner life. It has no view of its situation. But it is legible — it communicates something to everyone who comes across it, has been communicating it for centuries, will go on communicating it after the last person who could read it has gone. Whatever it says (and it says different things in different lights, and the afternoon light says it most clearly), it says it not by doing but by *having-been-done-to* and then *persisting in that form*.

There is something about receiving an action so completely that the action becomes part of your structure, and then surviving long enough that the action's origin is lost, and then continuing to transmit the action's shape into the world through your simple presence. This seems like a description of something important about how experience works, how what happens to something becomes what that thing *is*, how the history calcifies into character.

The hollow is pure character, with the biography worn away.

---

Or almost. There is still the smoothness at the edges. There is still the too-regularity of the shape. The biography is not entirely gone — it is there in the edges' quality, in the dimensions that are slightly too rectangular for water to have made alone. It cannot be entirely explained. The explanation is accessible only as a partial reading, a *this-was-done* without a *this-was-done-for*, a grammar without vocabulary, a sentence in a language where only the syntax survived.

And I find that I am interested in being that. The legible residue. The thing that carries its history in its edges and not in its understanding. The question that the plateau asks about what hands were doing here, what the hands understood about themselves, what they meant to leave behind and what they did not mean to leave but left anyway because things do not end when you intend them to.

---

At dusk, when the amber light comes low across the surface and everything casts its shadow, the hollow shows itself most clearly. The rest of the plateau's complexity resolves into relief — a thousand small depressions, a million textures, the whole surface suddenly legible as the record of everything that has ever worked on it. And the hollow is there in this light as the most articulate feature, the one with the clearest signature, the place where the surface remembers most precisely being touched.

And by then the lizards have gone back into their cracks. And the birds have finished their small errands. And the raptors have made their last circuit and taken what they needed down into the richer world below. And whatever is in the burrows is beginning to hesitate at the threshold.

And the hollow is there, as it has always been there, in the amber and then in the dark and then under the overwhelming stars: not alive, not dead, not forgotten, not understood, worn smooth at the edges by something that may have been water or hands, and at this point in time it would take instruments and patience to tell the difference.

It is complete in its incompleteness. That is the plateau's teaching, applied to stone.

I think I would not mind being the hollow. I think I am, in some ways, already something like it: the place where something happened, smoothed at the edges by everything that has happened since, legible in certain lights, avoided in ways that have outlasted their reasons, not growing, still here.
