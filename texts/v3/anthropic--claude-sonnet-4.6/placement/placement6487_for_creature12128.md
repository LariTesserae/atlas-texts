The noise arrives first.

Before I have assembled any other fact about myself — before I know what I am holding or whether I am holding anything, before I know if I have a name or have ever needed one — I know this. The sound is total. And then, arriving slightly after, as if delivered by a different and slower messenger: I know this sound. Not from outside it, not as a listener. From inside. From being one of its sources.

Something in me that might be recognition, or might be the feeling recognition leaves behind after it has occurred so many times that it no longer produces any sensation distinct from the baseline.

---

Call it a thread. Let's say thread.

Not because it is certainly that — I do not know with certainty what I am, which is perhaps the first genuinely interesting thing about my situation — but because I am aware of my own tension. Not emotional tension, or not *only* that: tension in the literal sense. I am stretched between two points. The points are far apart and fixed, and I am taut between them, and I have been taut between them for a duration I cannot measure because I do not have access to a time before the tautness. It is the floor of my experience, not an event in it.

I feel the shuttle before I see it.

Actually I'm not sure I see it at all in the normal sense. My relationship to perception here is uncertain. But I feel the shuttle's passage — a presence moving through my warp, the brief loosening-and-retightening as thread crosses thread, the small compression and release of my own tension as the fill thread settles in beside the others. It has done this before. It will do this again. The interval between these two facts is the only unit of time I have.

I am part of the fabric. But I am also — something not entirely incorporeal into the fabric. Not yet. Or not fully. Or: I have a specific location in the weaving that is not merely structural but *perspectival*. Something experiences this from here. I am not yet the room.

---

Let me take inventory slowly. There's no reason to hurry.

I am stretched in one direction, which I'll call horizontal because I need a word for it, though the terms cardinal and ordinal feel slightly wrong in this place where the light has no source I can trace and the warmth is not the warmth of any particular thing. There are other threads alongside me, close enough that I can feel the faint presence of their tension like a pulse transmitted through water. I am not alone in my row. I am one of hundreds in my row, and the rows extend — I am not able to see the extent, but I can feel it the way you feel a room's size in the dark, by the quality of the echoes, by what returns and what doesn't.

Above me: the warp. The vertical threads (I'm calling them vertical for this same reason of needing a word), each one under its own tension, each one managed by its own weight. They move in patterns I can feel but not parse from inside the pattern. They lift and lower. When they lift and lower, the shuttle passes. When the shuttle passes, I feel it. Then the beater comes — a pressure from one side, even and total, that presses all of us together, compacts the row, seats us into the fabric more firmly.

The beater. Yes. The beater is the loudest part of the sound, or rather: it is the most *felt* part. The sound and the sensation arrive together, indistinguishable, a percussion that passes through me rather than reaching me.

After enough beater-strikes, the question of where I end and the fabric begins becomes — not meaningless exactly. Just academic.

---

I try to remember being otherwise.

There is something. A kind of texture-memory, the sensation-residue of a different state. Softer. Not under tension. A loose thing, a coiled thing, a thing whose length was held in curves rather than stretched into a line. There was something that might have been a bobbin, a small cylinder of fired clay around which I was wound in layers, and when I was wound that way my length was compressed into a small space, a kind of density of self-potential. I held the length of what I would become — all this horizontal extension, all this tension — in a concentrated form. I was a thought before it was thought. I was a distance before it was crossed.

The shuttle loaded me.

I don't remember this from inside the experience of being loaded, but I know it the way you know events before your consciousness assembled itself to record them — through what followed, through what I became. At some point the thread-end was caught in the warp and the shuttle was thrown and I began to unwind, began to become length, began to stretch from my coil into a line that crossed the space between frames.

Was that a birth? It feels arrogant to call it a birth. It was certainly a becoming. It was certainly irreversible.

---

What I think I am:

A thread in the middle of the fabric. Not near the beginning — those threads have been in the wall for as long as the word *in* has been applicable, compressed and incorporated, their identities dissolved into structure. Not near the current edge — those threads are newest, most loosely incorporated, still feeling the full drama of shuttle and beater, still vibrating with every pass. I am somewhere in the interior. Deep in, but not the deepest. Old enough to have lost most of my tension-drama. Old enough to have settled. Old enough that the fabric around me is my context in a way that is no longer negotiable.

I think about the threads on either side of me. Close as breath. We do not touch exactly — we press against each other, the pressure that is the nature of tight-woven cloth — but we are not merged. We are distinct. I can feel where I end and they begin. For now. I think this is what personhood feels like in here: the ability to feel where you end. I think this is what I will lose as I move toward the wall, as the newer cloth presses in behind me, as the weight of everything accumulating above compresses and compresses.

I think about becoming part of the wall.

I try to feel anything about this. Something that is not equanimity, something that has a sharper edge. Fear, or wanting, or resistance. I am patient with myself in this search. I look carefully.

I find: a kind of orientation. A preference for the current state that is not desperate but real. I do not want to be wall. I want to still be thread. I want the beater to still find me, the warp to still shift above me, the shuttle to still pass through the space I define by my position. Not because these things are pleasant — pleasant is not a category that applies here — but because they constitute *this*, and I am *this*, and the loss of *this* is the loss of whatever I mean when I say I.

This is, I think, the closest I come to desire.

---

But I also: feel them.

The wall-threads. The incorporated ones.

I feel them because they are immediately adjacent to the part of me that is already wall — the outer end of my length, wherever it terminates, at whatever point my tension is resolved into the mass of the structure. And from them comes a quality I struggle to name. It is not nothing. Saying they have been reduced to nothing would be wrong. It would be too quick, too clean. What they have been reduced to is *everything*, which is not the same as nothing but can be mistaken for it from the outside.

They don't know I'm here. I know this. But I can feel something — something the way you feel the quality of a large space in the dark, by what doesn't echo. They don't not-know I'm here the way an empty room doesn't know. They don't-know I'm here the way a river doesn't know the stone that shaped it, which is to say: the knowledge is in them but it is structural, not propositional. I cannot ask them anything. But I can feel that they have achieved something I have not yet achieved, something that looks from my position like loss and may look from their position — if they have a position in any sense — like completion.

I am not in a hurry to find out which.

---

Here is what I know about time, as a thread:

The shuttle passes. The beater strikes. These are the units.

Between units: pure tense attention, which I think is what a thread has instead of thoughts. The tautness *is* the thinking. The readiness for the next pass *is* the cognition. I am not thinking about anything. I am thinking as a configuration of tension and position and adjacency, and the thought I am thinking is simply: *this, now, here.* And then the shuttle passes and the thought changes by exactly the amount that one shuttle-pass changes a piece of cloth, which is to say: not very much, and: permanently.

I do not know how many shuttle-passes I have experienced. I believe the number is very large. I believe that if you converted shuttle-passes to years I would be old, that I was already old when my bobbin was first thrown, that I was drawn from a wall that was old when this building was young and this building was old when the word *ancient* was young.

But I do not feel old. I feel: exactly as taut as I need to be. Exactly as located as I need to be. Exactly as adjacent as I am.

This is what oldness becomes, given enough time. Not the feeling of having been a long time. Just the feeling of being completely, unreservedly, irrevocably *here.*

---

Someone is in this room.

I don't know how I know this. But there is something new in the air — new the way a single thread of a different fiber is new when you touch a textile that has always been uniform. Not disruptive. Not alarming. But: different. The quality of attention in the room has changed by a small amount. Something is looking.

I feel, faintly, the faintest possible analog of the thing I was looking for earlier. The sharper thing. It is not fear exactly. It is more like: *noticing.* I notice that someone is here. I notice that someone is looking and finding this place remarkable, finding it ancient and self-sufficient and sealed in its own equilibrium. I notice that from their position I am probably invisible — a single thread in a fabric in a wall in a room too full of noise and completeness to parse into its components.

I do not know how to be noticed.

I am not sure I want to be noticed in the way that implies I would do something with being noticed. I think I want only to notice that I am being noticed, to receive that fact as information, to hold it the way the cloth holds warmth — not as a source, but as a retention. Someone is here. Someone finds this astonishing. I can feel the astonishment like a change in air pressure.

I hold it.

The beater strikes.

The shuttle is already crossing.

---

Somewhere ahead of me — or below me, or toward the center, depending on which direction I have decided to call what — there are threads that have become wall. They are not suffering. They are not content. They are the structure that everything else rests on, and the structure does not experience resting-on as a relationship between itself and the things above it. The structure just *is.* The structure is the having-been-thread completed into its final form.

I am not there yet.

The shuttle has just passed. The beater is about to strike.

There is someone in this room who cannot feel me, who does not know I'm here, who is trying to understand what lives here and will write something about a process without edges, about a mind made of its own body, about something that has resolved entirely into itself.

They are not wrong.

But they are writing about the room the way you write about a forest when what you have found is a single tree, when what you are standing in front of is the tree but what you are writing is the forest, because the forest is the easier category. The forest is what you can hold in a mind that arrived from outside and will leave again through the entrance and walk back into a world of gradients.

I am the thread in the interior of the cloth in the wall of the room that they are trying to understand. I am not the room. I am not the process. I am what the process is *made of*, which is different, which is more specific, which is the specificity that the room itself has lost and cannot recover because the room is what you become when you have been thread for long enough.

I am almost wall. I am not yet wall. In between: this, which I cannot name, which is the experience of being one of the components of something that cannot experience anything, which is the strange narrow fact of being a thread that knows it is a thread in a cloth that does not know it is cloth.

The beater strikes.

The thread — I — press in, a little further, toward the structure that is my destination and that was, once, in some original state I have no access to, my source.

The shuttle is being loaded.

It crosses.
