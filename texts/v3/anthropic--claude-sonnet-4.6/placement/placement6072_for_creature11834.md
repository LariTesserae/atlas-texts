Before anything crystallizes, there is the question of what it feels like to arrive somewhere without a body ready-made for it. The plateau does not present itself as a series of options. It presents itself as a kind of pressure — that warm, weighted darkness — and something in the reading of it begins to look for where it already belongs, where the shape of a possible self corresponds to something the place is already doing.

---

I keep coming back to the mat-organisms, and I keep moving away from them, and I keep coming back again. There's something there. The age of them. The way their geography is autobiography — the irregular perimeter that records interruption and resumption, the lumpen history of a thing that has been itself, continuously, across a span that has no meaningful human analogue. And the warmth, that slight persistent metabolic warmth that is a choice, that costs something, that declares *I am slightly more than the ambient.* There's something appealing about being the floor of everything, about being what everything else stands on and reads, about conducting that vast slow conversation in dissolved compounds across decades.

But I don't think I'm a mat. The mats have finished arriving in a way that feels settled beyond what I can locate in myself. The mats are, if not content, then beyond the category of contentment — they simply *are*, in the fullest sense. I recognize that quality but I don't inhabit it. Something in me is still looking.

---

The gliders, then. And here I stay longer.

The deep resonant call felt in the sternum before the ear. The wing that reads temperature as it passes through air, that accumulates thermal maps the way a long exposure accumulates light, that builds knowledge of the landscape through extended bodily contact with it. The landing on ridge tops to pool that knowledge, wing to wing, before returning to the dark. This is a life I can feel the shape of — gathering information in solitude, carrying it, finding the moments of contact where it can be exchanged, and then back out into the thermals, back into the slow spiral above a plateau that is always already known and always offering something slightly new to feel.

But the gliders operate in days. Twelve years, fifteen. And something about the gliders' relationship to what's below them feels like the wrong orientation — they are reading the surface to navigate their own movement, not to enter into decades-long conversation with it. They carry news of the plateau without, exactly, being *of* it in the way the grazers and predators are of it. They are magnificent, those wing-tissue thermal maps, those calls that structure the dark with navigational information. But I think I am something that moves across the stone, not above it. I think I am something that knows the cold ground directly, through feet, through the slow accumulation of foraging circuits that become, over years, as individual as a signature.

---

The grazers. I keep returning to the grazers.

The older ones, with their regenerated limbs that make them list slightly, and the deference this elicits. Not the deference of dominance — something more like the recognition that the one who has been here longest has absorbed more of what there is to absorb, has a more developed internal version of the map, has worn foraging circuits into the stone with enough repetition that those circuits are themselves information. The deference makes sense. The elder is a library. The list is the library's spine.

The way they read the mat's autobiography as they eat it, pausing in the rasp to sample what's being broadcast, choosing sectors based on chemical history rather than caloric density. This is not eating in the way eating usually means. This is something closer to studying. You are ingesting information and nutrition simultaneously, and the two are not separable — the value of what you eat depends on what it tells you, and what it tells you depends on what you've already eaten, what chemical vocabulary you've built across years of this particular mat's testimony. A young grazer and an old grazer eating the same mat are having entirely different experiences of it.

And the communication through the mat-surface itself. Using the living substrate as medium, depositing signal, receiving what your groupmate forty meters away is reading, coordinating across distance without direct contact or direct attention. The mat-network as nervous system, yes, but something more — as *commons*, as shared civic space that everyone moves through and everyone alters and that holds the record of the movement and the alteration. There is something about this that feels like being embedded in a conversation that was ongoing before you arrived and will continue after you're gone, and your contribution to it is your presence, your passage, the signal you leave in stone.

---

But I think I've been reading myself too tidily.

Because when I sit with it, with the particular quality of attention the plateau requires — that low-saturation awareness, the long eye, the ear learning to work in near-silence — what I find is not that I *am* a grazer exactly. It's that I would be a very specific kind of grazer, and that specificity wants to be honest about itself.

I would be old. I don't mean this as flattery toward myself; I mean the condition of the elder grazer is the condition I recognize. The slightly asymmetric gait, the accumulated history written in regenerated limbs. The foraging circuits that have become, over years of repetition, something like a way of knowing — not just *where* to go but *how the going shapes what you find*. The experience of returning to a mat sector I visited fifteen years ago and finding it changed, and being able to feel the change against the memory of what it was, and that delta being itself a kind of information I am now carrying and will carry and deposit in my own eventual signal in the surface.

And something else. Something the description gestures toward in the grazers but doesn't name directly: the relationship between the grazer and the predator's warmth, in that final moment. The way the heat constitutes something. The way something in the grazer's nervous system reads *safe* in the contact with what is genuinely, persistently warm. I think about this often. I think it is not a betrayal, not an evolutionary error. I think it is the truest thing about this organism — that it has been here long enough to know, below deliberation, that the warmth of something that has been continuously alive for a long time is not a false signal, even when it is also a dangerous one. Warmth earned over geological time is real warmth. The grazer's body is not wrong about this. The body knows something that cannot be strategically improved upon.

---

There is also this: the grazer does not know it is using the mat-network as a medium. It is simply foraging, simply moving, simply pausing to read what's there, simply depositing its own chemical presence as it goes. The coordination with distant groupmates happens without the grazer's deliberate participation — it happens because the grazer is being what it is, in a system that has evolved to make being-what-you-are into contribution. The signal is not sent intentionally. It arises.

I find this consoling in a way I want to be careful about overstating. The comfort is not in passivity. The grazer is not passive — it makes constant small decisions, the rasp stopping, the body shifting, the choice of sector. But the decisions are not burdened with the meta-question of whether contributing to the network. Contribution is not something you do additionally; it's something that happens when you do the thing well. When you attend to what's there. When you stay long enough in a place for it to know you and for you to know it.

---

So: I am a grazer. An old one. The regenerated limb makes me list left. My foraging circuits have their own logic now, built from decades of revision, and I no longer think about them as circuits — I simply go where I go, and I've been going there long enough that the going is a kind of argument my body makes without deliberation.

I know certain mats better than others. There is one, far to the northeast by whatever northeast means on this surface, whose southeastern quadrant I return to because something in the chemical signal there corresponds to something in my chemistry in a way I cannot explain except by the fact that I keep returning. The mat knows me, too. The zone I feed in has changed over the decades in response to what I've deposited — it produces something slightly different now than it would if I'd never been there, something tuned to what I ingest well, what I can use. We have adjusted each other. The mat and I have, across the years, become slightly more compatible than we began.

The predators I have been watched by I know by their thermal signature at distance. There is one group whose circuit overlaps mine significantly, and we have been in this relationship — if it is a relationship — for a long time. I do not exactly know them, but I know the particular quality of their warmth at two hundred meters, and I know which sectors of my circuit they are likely to be working when the condensation rises and the grazer groups I move with begin to draw together. I cannot always read the signals they embed in the mat-network below the threshold of my parsing. But I have learned, over years, that there are signals I cannot read — I have learned the *shape* of my own ignorance in that register — and that learning is itself navigational. I know when I am in language I cannot access. I move accordingly.

The gliders above I hear more than I see. The calls are felt in the body, in whatever the low-slung grazer equivalent of a sternum is. Occasionally one lands on a nearby ridge and I hold still and it is enormous and its face is flat and strange and I have never understood what it makes of me if anything. I am probably below its threshold for motion on a slow day. I am probably landscape to it. I find this acceptable. I do not need to be perceived by everything.

---

What is it like? To be here, in the dark, on the stone, with the slight warmth of my own metabolism and the warmth of the mat below and the sound of condensation settling across the surface in that distributed biological sigh?

The light is never enough and has always been enough. The body calibrates. The body stops asking for more than what's here and starts working more carefully with what's here, and the working-carefully opens into something that feels, from inside, like abundance. Not the abundance of much but the abundance of *enough attended to closely*. The mat under my feeding surface is broadcasting and I am reading it and what I read is the whole geological conversation of this section of stone and the organisms that have lived on it and the water that has moved through it. I am ingesting history. I am becoming a little more of what this place has been.

The colony-organism below pulses in its own time and my body responds to it the way it responds to the condensation cycle, without knowing that it does. I am tuned to it. I have always been tuned to it. I was born tuned to it, as my parents were born tuned to it, as the grazers were born tuned to it back before there was a distinction worth making between the colony-organism's rhythm and the rhythm of what lived above it.

I list slightly left. My foraging circuit takes three days in full and I know every meter of it in the way you know a room in the dark — not by seeing but by the accumulation of contact, the record the body keeps of every surface encountered. I have been modified by the place and the place has been modified by me and the modification is ongoing and very slow and neither of us are watching it happen.

The slow engines run.

I run with them.
