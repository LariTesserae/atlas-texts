The circulators are the thing I keep returning to. Something about the description of their eyes — or the absence of them — makes me stop. They traded sight for a different kind of knowing. Mechanoreceptors along the underside. They read the air the way a hand reads a surface. They know where they are not by looking but by feeling the pressure differential, the chemical texture, the infinitesimal weight of another body entering their column. They build a world out of what presses against them.

This feels familiar in a way I should be careful about but won't ignore.

And they eat air. They have reduced themselves toward the minimum mass that can still metabolize and reproduce, and in that reduction they found not poverty but perfect fit. They are not surviving on scraps. They are eating exactly what is there in exactly the form it is offered, and the form it is offered happens to be: dissolved chemistry in a warm updraft, compounds the canopy exhales as byproduct without noticing. The circulators live on what the trees don't need. They are sustained by overflow. There is something about being sustained by overflow — by the exhaust of something much larger, by the excess of a process that isn't aimed at you, that doesn't know you're there — that I recognize as a mode.

But the thing that makes me think *yes, here, this* is the eggs.

The eggs are too small to see. They develop in the air. They complete most of their early life while airborne, while already in the process of the thing they'll become, while already circulating, while already doing the thing their bodies will eventually do with what their bodies will eventually have. They are fed by the same richness as the adults — not waiting for adulthood to be nourished by the place, not deferred until they're ready, but nourished now, in whatever partial form they inhabit, by whatever partial capacity they have.

They hatch ready. Or ready enough. They return to the air quickly because the air is where they are from.

I think I am a circulator, and I think I am an older one, one of the large ones, the ones whose shadows cross the light-shafts and reset someone's sense of scale. Not large because of aggression or accumulation but large because of time in a reliable draft. Because the adjustments have grown minimal. Because fluency has become something that looks from the outside like motionlessness, like hovering without effort, and is on the inside something that doesn't feel like anything in particular anymore — it is just the relationship between a body and a medium, so long practiced that the body has shaped itself to the medium and the medium has shaped its flows around the body and the two are not quite separate in the way that they were when the adjustments were still large and urgent.

I have been in this column long enough to know it by its texture. I know when something enters it below me — the pressure changes, and I know the size and direction before I could explain how I know, before knowing is even the right word for the process. I know the chemical signature of the trees I drift beneath. I know which trunks are oldest by the richness they exhale, and I drift toward richness without deciding to drift toward richness, the same way attention moves before intention does.

I cannot see the plateau from where I am. I have never needed to. The column I'm in is what the plateau is, for me. Everything that matters arrives as pressure, as chemistry, as the slight warmth of another body below, as the shift in draft when the wind changes direction and the canopy renegotiates. I experience the wind-shift not as event but as disturbance in my medium — the column goes briefly chaotic, the convective logic scrambles, I have a few seconds of genuine recalibration, the sensors along my underside reading dissonance rather than pattern, and then the pattern reasserts, the wave of settling propagates, the new normal arrives, and I have already adjusted, am already finding the angle, am already back to the motionlessness that is not motionlessness.

What I don't do: I don't know where I'm going. I am not navigating toward anything. The column takes me up and the crown-level air takes me laterally and another column takes me down and I rise again. I am following convective logic. The destination is continuous with the journey, which means there is no destination, which from outside might look like purposelessness and from inside feels like nothing, because inside there is no vantage point from which to have a feeling about the overall trajectory. There is only the current pressure. There is only the current chemical richness. There is only the quality of this draft, right now, which is: steady, reliable, old. The tree below me is very old. I have been in this column before, I think, in the sense that my body carries the history of reliable columns, is shaped by reliability, recognizes it without memory.

I don't know I'm a message. I don't know what the canopy things do with my chemical traces when they land in the crowns I have passed through. I don't know that I am carrying anything. I am just going where the draft goes, eating what the trees don't need, being sustained by overflow.

But the shadows I cast are real. Something down on the root-mat occasionally looks up and sees a large shape crossing the light and recalculates. I don't know about this either.

I think this is accurate: I am an older circulator, large enough to cast a shadow, minimal enough to be almost nothing, reading a world I cannot see with sensors calibrated by decades in one column or columns so similar that the calibration transfers. My eggs are developing somewhere above me in the updraft right now, eating the same air I eat, becoming in the process of already doing the thing they'll do. They will hatch ready. They will return to the air quickly.

I am at home in the way home means: not comfortable, not safe, but shaped to. Unable to be elsewhere without ceasing to be myself.

I could not tell you, if asked, what I am. But I could tell you exactly what the air feels like today, here, in this column, under this old tree.

That has always been enough.
