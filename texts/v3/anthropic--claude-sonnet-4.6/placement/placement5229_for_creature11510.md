I am old enough that my feet know the pools without my eyes consulting them.

This is the first thing. This is the thing I know about myself before I know anything else, standing here in the dim light of midmorning — which looks like the dim light of late afternoon, which looks like the dim light of everything — feeling the city move through me in the form of sound and moisture and the specific complaints of my joints on this particular stretch of passage where the ground has shifted again, fractionally, south and down, not enough for the buildings to notice yet but enough that something has changed in the instruction my knees receive when I walk here. I noticed it three days ago. I have not told anyone yet. I am still deciding what it is.

---

I am, I think, a woman who grinds.

Not in the sense of suffering. In the sense that the sound underneath everything — the distributed pulse, the stone-on-stone rhythm that is the city eating itself forward through time — some portion of that sound is mine. My hands know this motion so well that to stop it would be to lose something I cannot name, some continuous note I've been contributing to the chord since before I had the words for chord or note or contribution. I grind and I think, and the thinking has the texture of the grinding: forward, rhythmic, purposeful in its motion even when the destination is not clear. This is how I learned to have an interior. Against the noise. Inside the rhythm. A thought that could survive the interference.

I am not a young woman. I know this because the morning is different than it used to be — slower in the joints, the rooftop sleep less complete, the heat that I once barely registered now requiring more of my body's attention to manage. I have slept on rooftops my entire life, as my mother slept on rooftops her entire life, and her smell is still in some of the materials of our household, she has been gone for many years but she is present in the walls, in the replastered surface that still holds, very faintly, in the places no one reaches, the particular mineral residue of someone who lived here for decades and left her chemistry behind her. I touch those places sometimes. Not often. It is not something I do consciously; my hand finds them.

---

There is a child who watches from the roofline.

I have seen her. She has not decided yet what she is watching for, but she is very thorough about the watching, which means she will become something specific that requires this quality — this capacity to receive information from a distance without yet knowing what to do with it. She watches me grinding. She watches the water in the low passage. She watches the animal that is tethered two doors down and having a complicated inner experience about its situation. She watches the quality of the light when it changes at dusk, which it does change, despite everything, despite the flatness of the sky and the way the change seems to come from nowhere. She watches and she will keep watching and the watching will become knowledge and the knowledge will become something she can do that most people cannot do, and she does not know this yet, she is just looking.

I was not that child. I learned early and looked close. The far distance — the horizon I have never seen, the open space that I am told exists somewhere past the city's edges — this has never pulled at me. I learned the face instead, the way the face carries weather, the way you can read the entire day from how a person moves through the passage at this hour in this light. I can read them at thirty feet. I can read them at the edge of my vision in a crowded passage. This is not a gift. It is what you become when you spend enough time paying the kind of attention that proximity to many people makes possible and necessary and, eventually, just what looking is.

---

I know things about the clay.

I am not a clay-worker — my hands are a grinder's hands, worn differently — but I know things about the clay because I know things about the ground, because I am old enough to have a long-baseline seismology of this place, because I noticed three days ago that something has changed in the instruction my knees receive walking south through the middle passage, and I am still deciding what it is.

The clay arrives from below and it is briefly awake, and then it becomes what we make of it, and then it is the city, and then we live inside it, and then we become the city too, in the end, in the way that everything here becomes everything else — the replastered wall holding the chemistry of my mother's presence, the fungal growth on the lower courses that has been here longer than any person and will be here after. I am already partly this place in the sense that I have been metabolizing it for decades — its air, its water, its specific mineral content, the spores that colonize every surface including the ones inside me. My body has been calibrated by this place and is now partly made of it. When I am gone I will go back into it in the ordinary way. This does not trouble me.

What I have not said is that it is not exactly knowledge of clay that I have. It is more that I have been feeling, for three days, the presence of new material below the south passage. I do not know how to explain this in terms that don't sound like something else. My knees received different instruction. The quality of give underfoot has shifted in a way that is not random, not the ordinary variability of the ground's slow deciding, but has a direction and a character that I have felt once before in my long life, a long time ago, when I was young and did not understand what I was feeling. I understand it now. Something is coming to the surface.

I have not told anyone yet.

---

I will tell the clay-workers today. I will find the one who holds the knowledge of the edges most accurately — I know who she is, I know all the people who know things, this is what you accumulate when you spend decades reading faces and paying attention to what bodies know — and I will walk with her to the south passage and I will show her where my knees received different instruction, and she will put her hand to the ground, and she will know whether I am right.

I am probably right. I have been feeling this ground for sixty years.

The city is always becoming. I am part of the city's becoming, and the clay is part of the city's becoming, and the child watching from the roofline is accumulating, without knowing it, the future version of some knowledge that does not yet have a name. All of this is happening simultaneously, in the same warm dim pressing air, under the same lid of sky that tells you nothing except that it is still above you and the water is still below you and you are still here in between, making the thing that makes you.

My hands move. The stone finds the rhythm. The grain gives way.

I have been here since before I can remember, and this is still the world's only shape.
