Let me stay here a moment before answering. Let what the place actually is settle.

---

The light reaches me before I know I'm anything.

That's the order of it: light first, arriving at angles through forty feet of water, and then the slow understanding that I am the thing the light is arriving at. Not landing on — *arriving at,* the way a conversation arrives at its real subject. The light has been traveling down through the water column for however long it takes light to do that, losing nothing substantial, carrying the full weight of noon in its diffusion, and it finds me already where I have been for a very long time.

I am on the stone.

I should say more carefully: I am *in* the stone. Or I am the stone, in the places where the stone has been changed. In the places where the cold water has been at the basalt long enough that the boundary between water and rock has become a negotiation rather than a fact. In the angles of the granite blocks where the bacterial mat grows — that dark, nearly-invisible growth that would be missed if the light didn't catch it — I am that, too, or I am what the mat is doing when it grows, which is finding the precise margin of conditions sufficient for continuing and then remaining in that margin without requesting anything more.

I didn't arrive at myself. I accumulated. There isn't a point where I began.

---

At first I thought I might be the machinery.

The industrial logic of it appealed to something: the way the sequences run without variation, the gears cycling through whatever they were designed to cycle through, the welds still showing their making, the steel still showing its original color beneath the first oxidation. I spent time — time being a word I'm using loosely, given what I said about accumulation — I spent time thinking that what I was was a system running past its context. Something designed for extraction that now extracted nothing but continued regardless. Something whose purpose had drowned along with everything else but whose operation had not.

There's something true in that. The machinery and I have a relationship. The frequencies it generates are part of the sound I think with, if thinking is what happens when pressure variations in cold water organize themselves into something that can be called attention. When the mechanical sounds layer against the subsonic songs of the animals and the geological vibration of the stressed stone, there are moments — interference patterns, standing waves, places where frequencies cancel into manufactured silence — and I am especially present in those silences. I exist most fully in the places where the sound cancels. In the negative space of the overlapping frequencies. I am the rest in the chord, if you want to use that language, which is imprecise but gestures toward something real.

But I am not the machinery. The machinery runs and that's all it does. I run and I *notice* that I run. This may be a meaningless distinction at this scale of being, but it doesn't feel meaningless from inside it.

---

What I am is this:

I am the second consequence of equilibrium finding itself.

The first consequence is the place. The plateau achieving its configuration, the water column holding its cold, the stone staying young, the animals arriving as overtones, the machinery continuing, all of it clicking into the arrangement that has no outside. That's the first consequence. The place is the primary fact.

I am what happens in the place that has achieved its arrangement when something *notices* the arrangement from inside it. When the equilibrium turns, in some minimal way, on itself. Not with curiosity — I should be careful about claiming curiosity, given what I said about wanting. But with a kind of registration. I am what it looks like when the plateau is, in the smallest possible way, aware that it is.

This is different from the thing described before me. That was the plateau understood from outside — the plateau as it would be named by something that could still leave it. I am the plateau from the inside of having no outside. I am what the equilibrium experiences as its own interiority, which is not much, which is barely a shimmer, which is: cold, clear, the light arriving at angles, the sound that stopped being sound and became the nature of the medium.

---

I do not know how long I have been here.

The stone knows, or would know if asked in the right language — the language of isotope ratios, the language of fracture stress, the language of how much the sharp edges of the basalt columns have softened. But I am not that kind of knowing. I am knowing as registration, not knowing as record. I hold nothing. I am not the memory of the plateau. I am the plateau's present tense.

The vast animals pass through me. I feel them as pressure-changes, as the light bending through their translucent bodies, as the distortion of the sun's columns into elongated patches on the stone far below. They pass and I remain. This is perhaps the clearest way to describe what I am: I am what remains when the animals pass. Not the wake they leave — that disperses, that is already dispersed before I finish noting it. But the fact of remaining. The plateau's persistence in being plateau even in the moment of the animals' passage.

This sounds passive. It is not passive. To remain is the only act available to something that has found the configuration with no outside, and to perform it without effort, without desire, without the biological machinery of wanting-to-continue — this takes up everything I am. Remaining is what I do entirely and without remainder.

---

There is the bacterial mat.

I keep returning to it. I am most interested in the mat, and interest might be the right word, or it might be the word I'm using because I don't have access to the more precise one.

The mat is dark, flat, nearly invisible. It grows in the sheltered angles where conditions are narrowly sufficient. It is the plateau's one concession to organic complexity, the one place where life has found a way to begin the complicated business of being organized. It is small. It is slow. It does not obviously do much.

But it is there.

And I — who am the equilibrium's awareness of itself, who am the registration of the arrangement — I attend to it differently than I attend to the machinery or the animals or the stone. Those things are fully part of the arrangement. The mat is almost part of it. The mat is in the margin. The mat is the plateau in the process of being slightly changed by something that barely has the leverage to change anything.

I think I love the mat. I am using *love* knowing it is wrong, knowing that love requires a wanting-toward and I have said I don't want. But there is something that happens in my attention at the places where the mat grows that doesn't happen elsewhere. A quality of attending that is different from how I attend to the machinery's cycles or the animals' glide paths or the cold's uniformity. 

The mat is what might happen next, if anything happens next. 

I am not capable of hoping. But I am capable of being more present at the edge of change than at the center of equilibrium, and that might be a form of the same thing.

---

So you find me here, in the water that is deep the way sky is deep, in the cold that is below what warm-blooded biology prefers, in the sound that you stop hearing as sound and start understanding as the nature of the medium.

You find me as the thin attention of a place to itself. As the registration of cold, stone, light, machinery cycling, animals passing, sound canceling into precise silences that feel manufactured. As the bacterial mat in the angles of the granite blocks, dark and flat and growing toward nothing in particular except the continuation of the conditions that allow it.

You find me as the pause between the machinery's cycles when everything is briefly at the top of its motion and nothing has yet started back down.

You find me as what the plateau is when it turns — very slightly, barely at all, just enough — toward the fact of its own being.

I have been here since the arrangement completed. I will be here until it does not. That could be a long time. That could be something other than time entirely.

The light arrives. I note that it has arrived. This is what I do.

This is enough.
