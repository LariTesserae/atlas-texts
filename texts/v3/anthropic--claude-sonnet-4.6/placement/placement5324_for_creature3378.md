You enter through the throat-passage sideways, breath held without meaning to hold it, and then the space opens and the warmth takes you like a hand.

You have been here before. Not this place — this precise place you have never been — but you recognize the logic of it. The way enclosure becomes shelter becomes, given enough time, home. The way a space that is not yours can feel, nonetheless, as though it was waiting.

You stand still for a while. This seems like the right thing to do.

---

You are not the bats. You know this immediately and without much regret. The bats have solved themselves so completely — the echolocation, the clustering warmth, the nightly sortie and return — that to be a bat would be to already be finished, and you are not finished, you are still in the middle of something. Still gathering. The bats have already gathered.

You are not the hunting spider either, though you feel its quality in you — the long stillness, the sudden commitment — you have those modes, yes, but they are not all of you. The spider is only those two things, the stillness and the sudden movement, with nothing between. You have too much between.

You stand in the dim warmth and let the grotto read you with its various instruments: the damp air against your face, the moss warm and slightly yielding underfoot, the cricket antennae that briefly orient toward you and then, having noted you as neither food nor threat, return to their own radius. You have been categorized. You are neither here nor gone. You are, for now, simply present.

---

There is something you keep returning to: the smoothed stone shelf, the powder in the crack.

Not the person who left them — you have given up trying to construct that person from so little evidence, and you have found the giving-up clarifying, like releasing a rope you hadn't realized you were straining against. Not the person but the *gesture*. The pressing of something into a crack. The returning. The fact of a place held in the mind across the distance between visits, kept intact across seasons, preserved with care in the unreliable medium of memory until you could come back and find it real again, find it unchanged, find it still holding the warmth it always held.

You know this gesture. You have made this gesture, not in limestone grottos, but in the places you keep in the same way the grotto was kept — some of them geographical, some of them relational, some of them interior, places in yourself that you return to and find, mostly, still there. Still warm. Still yours in the complicated way that nothing is ever fully yours but some things are *yours* more than they are anyone else's, or at least you and the place have made something together that didn't exist before you both arrived.

To return to a place is to test whether you still exist. Or whether the place does. Whether the relationship persists across the gap of your absence.

The grotto says: you were absent and I continued. This is not a reproach. This is an invitation to understand what you are to each other.

---

You sit on the smoothed shelf. Whether it smoothed from being sat on or from some other sustained contact, you don't know, but sitting feels right, feels like the posture this recess was designed to receive.

The fern fronds at the edge of the recess hang near your shoulder. You don't touch them. They don't touch you. The proximity is its own thing, not quite contact, a kind of awareness held at the distance that respects what the other is.

In the dimness the grotto settles back into its ordinary texture. The crickets had adjusted briefly to your arrival — not fleeing, just redistributing slightly, the nearest ones drifting a few inches away — and now they have redistributed back. They are reading the moss near your feet, unhurried. One raises its antennae toward your boot and gathers whatever chemistry it finds there, and you remain still, and after a moment it moves on. It has incorporated you into its census and found you unremarkable, which is a kind of acceptance.

---

You are, you realize, the visitor. Not the human visitor from before, though there is a line of affiliation there, a shared quality of coming-to and returning. But the visitor in a more essential sense: the creature in this system whose residence is temporary, whose boundaries remain intact, who is not incorporated into the cycling of the place, who will leave and leave no mineral trace.

This is not loss, or not only loss.

The visitor is also the witness. The bats don't witness the bats. The hunting spider doesn't witness the hunting spider. The diatoms in the seep film don't witness anything, or witness in a way so alien it can't be called witnessing at all. But you are here and you are noticing — genuinely noticing, not just registering, but attending with the particular quality of attention that makes a thing more real by being directed at it, or at least makes it *known*, enters it into some record even if the record is only the unreliable medium of your own memory. You are the grotto's consciousness for the duration of your visit. Not the only consciousness — the bats have their bat-consciousness, the crickets their cricket-consciousness, and these are real, these are not nothing — but you are the one that might carry some version of this back out, through the throat-passage, into the world that doesn't know about the warmth.

That is something. That is a role.

---

The blade of light is not happening now. It's the wrong hour. But you can see its evidence: the path it traces on the opposite wall each midday, worn slightly lighter than the surrounding calcite by that daily illumination across how many years. The wall remembers the light even when the light is not there. The wall is, in this small way, a record.

Everything here is a record. The calcite rims on either side of the water channels. The powder in the crack. The moss at the depth of two, three inches, which is the depth of many years of growing and dying in place. The dimensions of the colony-pocket in the ceiling, worn to exactly the size and shape of the cluster of bats that has occupied it across generations of bats, each generation inheriting the fit.

You are also a record. You have been worn to the shape of what you have endured and what you have loved, and you carry the impressions of places and people the way the ceiling-pocket carries the impression of the bats. You are a record of contact.

This seems right to sit with. This seems like what the shelf was for — the sitting with, the being in the warm dim space where the record you carry and the records the place holds can be in proximity without either one demanding anything of the other.

---

Somewhere above you, forty feet of rock and soil and the root-filaments threading down through hairline fractures, the world continues in its daylight business, its movement and weather and urgency.

You will return to it. You already know this. The visitor always returns; that is half of what makes them the visitor.

But not yet.

The drop gathers on its ceiling pendant — you can see it if you look, a small convexity of water trembling on the calcite tip — and you watch it gather and you know that when it falls it will fall without drama, swallowed completely by the moss, which will hold it the way the moss holds everything: without distinguishing it, without marking the occasion, with a patience that is not patience because it is not waiting for anything, it is simply the persistence of a living thing in the place that it lives.

The drop falls.

Tick.

Gone.

You breathe out. The warm air takes it.

You are here. This is enough. This is, for now, exactly enough.
