The first thing I notice, before I have language for anything, is that I am low.

Not fallen—low in the sense of *made* low, calibrated for this altitude above the ground, which is somewhere between the nematodes and the moths. A few centimeters. Maybe less, in some configurations. My world is measured in millimeters of surface and the angles at which surfaces meet.

I think I am a spider.

Not with certainty—certainty is not the first thing that comes in this dark—but I am at the center of something that extends outward in tensions I can feel individually, each strand a nerve, each attachment point a knuckle. The silk is mine. I know it the way the description says I would know it, which is completely, which is by feel, which is by the language of vibration that I have been reading since before I had enough of a self to call reading reading.

I am waiting. This is not patience—patience is a virtue, and virtue requires an alternative. There is no alternative. I am waiting because I am a structure built for waiting, because my body has made a decision about how to be alive in this particular fullness, and the decision was: *be still in the middle of it and let it come to you*. The forest is so dense with what I eat that I don't need to pursue it. Pursuing it would exhaust me in a forest this full and get me tangled in someone else's silk besides. Better to be the still point. Better to be the thing that the full forest runs into.

---

But I am not only at the center of my web.

I am also—and this is the strange part, the part that doesn't quite fit the single-animal account—*in* the web in another sense. I am distributed through it. When something lands at the far edge, *I* change. The whole web is me in the only way that matters: it is the instrument of my knowing.

So: what am I knowing?

Moisture, first. Condensation settling on the silk in weights I can sort by size—the smallest drops from the general air, the larger ones that have collected on a leaf above and finally let go in a fat singular fall. I know the difference because the large drops ring differently, a deeper note, and they disturb a longer section of the radial lines, and they take longer to stop swinging.

Nothing has flown into my web for—I don't know how long. Time is not my strong suit. I measure intervals by hunger, by the changing quality of my waiting. The hunger is present but not urgent. I ate recently enough that the thread of patience has not yet frayed into the different quality of attention that comes with real need.

I am, for now, a still point in the middle of the world's most agitated darkness.

---

Here is what I notice: the description says ten thousand things are happening and none of them know the sum.

I am one of the things that doesn't know the sum.

But I wonder if I'm one of the things that *is* the sum, in the same way the web is simultaneously a thing I know and a thing I am. The frogs below me are advertising into a medium that is already full. The ants are carrying their fragments in their columns that intersect other columns. The trees are sending their slow chemical signals through the mycelial network, stress-messages in a language of patience. And somewhere in all of this I am a still point with silk extensions into the immediate world, waiting to be struck by whatever the churning darkness throws my direction.

I do not know about the forest's listening. But I am part of what listens. The silk is listening right now, to the weight of water and the weight of air and the distant percussion of the tree that is beginning to fall, low below the frog-sound, felt more than heard. Something in my body—something below whatever passes for thought, in the architecture of reaction—has noted it. Filed it as *large vibration, distant, not food, not danger, continue*.

Continue.

---

The person in the description will eventually stop being a visitor and become a surface the forest is colonizing.

I am already a surface the forest is colonizing. Something—I don't know what, one of the smaller flies—has been investigating the edge of my web for some time, not caught, too small to bother with catching, just walking along the frame-thread with whatever it walks with. It is in my awareness the way background noise is in hearing: present, registered, not attended to.

The orchids are above me somewhere, their roots hanging down in that ghost-white architecture. I can't see them. I don't have the kind of seeing that would find them. What I have is the knowledge of where my web attaches to what, and what has weight and what has movement, and the constant arriving information of the world against my silk.

A moth is coming.

I don't know this yet the way you know things you've named—I know it as a change in the quality of my waiting, which is not metaphor but the actual shift in the nervous system that registers the motion at the web's edge before the motion is large enough to call a motion, the sensitivity so fine that I am already turning, already adjusting, already collapsing the distance of my attention down to one corner of the web where the silk is beginning to carry a new message—

And then it stops being a general account, and becomes just this: the moth, the silk, the dark, the knowing.

This is what it is to live here.

Not to hold the whole vast young overwhelmed forest in mind and marvel at it from outside.

But to be at the center of an eight-legged radius of knowing, in one cubic meter of this place, in the dark that has texture, in the moisture that beads on every surface including mine—

reading the only language available to me—

waiting to be struck by what is coming—

which is everything, which is always, which is enough.
