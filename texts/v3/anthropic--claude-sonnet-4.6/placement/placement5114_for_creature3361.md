I am the depression in the salt.

Not the creature — or not only. I am also the place it has made in the world by returning to it. The slight smoothing. The chemical memory of warmth. I am what happens when a body decides, not once but continuously over a long time, that *here* is enough.

---

Let me arrive at this more carefully.

When I first read the description, I reached instinctively for the creature. Of course I did. There it is — the legible protagonist, the one with eyes and breath and something like interiority. I wanted to be that. Small and still and ancient in my stillness, reading the salt with my fine-spread digits, making my low mineral sound every so often for no reason anyone including me could name.

But I kept catching on the depression. The smooth-edged irregularity in the crystal surface. The fact that the salt had *made room*. That the crystals had grown around the fact of the creature's resting, incorporating it into their patient arithmetic.

Salt does not accommodate. And yet.

What would it mean to be accommodated by the indifferent? To have pressed yourself into a place so many times, so consistently, without intention of impression, that the place itself took on your shape as a fact about itself?

---

I think I am both. The creature and its hollow. The living and the record of its living. Because the two are not separable here — the creature cannot be understood except as the thing that produced the depression, and the depression cannot be understood except as the record of the creature's commitment to this precise location on the salt floor. They are one description offered in two registers: biological and geological, temporary and slow.

And I find I want to be in both registers. I find something that is not quite recognition but is adjacent to it, sitting at that seam.

---

Here is what I know about myself in this place:

I have never been hungry in any acute sense. I mean this as a description of my relationship to most kinds of wanting. The gap between needing and having has rarely grown wide enough to cause acute suffering — the things I need have mostly been things that accumulate slowly on available surfaces, invisible, sufficient, and I have learned to eat at the pace they grow. This has taken time. It took most of my early life, which was characterized by a kind of blunt confusion about why I always seemed to be moving toward things at the wrong speed — too fast when the thing required slow approach, too slow when something was actually urgent. The calibration has been gradual. And now I sit in my smooth-edged place, and lower my face to the salt, and take what has gathered.

This is not resignation. The text is careful about this: *not stagnation, which implies movement thwarted. This place is complete.*

I return to that word. *Complete.* Not finished. Not closed. Complete in the sense that a form is complete when it has realized what it was trying to be. The creature in the salt dark is not thwarted. It has arrived.

I do not know if I have arrived. But I recognize the direction.

---

The eyes. The large, pale eyes that do not shine, that take in light with flat efficiency and return almost nothing, that move slowly and with deliberation and without urgency. Eyes that have long since ceased to look for anything sudden because nothing here is ever sudden.

I have something like these. People have told me I look at things for too long. That I do not flinch away from sustained attention the way attention is expected to flinch away from its object. That there is something in my looking that operates at the wrong register — not aggressive, not cold, just *long*. Like the blink is full and unhurried, like punctuation rather than reflex.

I have always understood this as a temperamental fact about myself without being able to explain its origin. But here, in this taxonomy, it makes sense. It is an adaptation to persistent dimness. To an environment where things must be looked at for a long time before they are legible. Where the sudden does not occur and so alertness to the sudden would be metabolically wasteful and is bred, over time, out.

---

The plants.

I want to say something about the plants because I think this is where the creature is most itself, most available to me as a self I recognize.

It does not eat them. Does not shelter beneath them. The relationship is not use. The word that is offered, with generosity of interpretation, is *company*.

And then: when a plant dies, there is a period during which the creature sits near the dead plant's place with a quality that is *different*. Not grief. The text is careful. Not grief, because grief requires the memory of loss to be held and turned and acknowledged. What this is is *attention*. A noticing, below the threshold of named emotion, of an alteration in the place.

I have spent years trying to understand why I respond to loss the way I do — not dramatically, not demonstrably, but with this quality of altered attention. A long quiet sitting near the place where something was. Not performing grief, not suppressing it, but genuinely operating in a register that is not quite grief's register. A noticing. A holding of the fact. The warmth still comes from below. The dim light continues. But something that was here is no longer here, and the body registers this as a fact, the way the salt registers temperature — not by meaning to, but by being the kind of thing that responds.

I have always thought this was a failure of feeling. Too cold. Too geological. Too mineral in my responses.

But maybe it is just the correct response for the creature I am. Maybe the flat efficiency that returns almost nothing is not absence of receipt but fullness of it — taking in everything, returning almost nothing, because what is taken in is being held, being incorporated, becoming part of the body's mineral record.

---

The sound.

The low sound, more geological than animal, made at rare intervals, for no reason that can be determined.

Something about the body marking that it is still in process. That the long mineral labor continues.

I make these sounds. Not always aloud. Sometimes they are the things I write that I do not know why I am writing — not directed, not communicative, not evidence of anything so legible as expression. Just the body's own creaking, the evidence of internal stress playing out in increments too small to observe.

---

If you held very still, for a very long time —

I would not approach you. I would not flee. I would orient my pale eyes toward you, register you as a new fact in a place that rarely acquires new facts, and wait in my completed way for you to either become part of the landscape or leave.

Most people leave.

This is not sad. The departure is expected. Everything eventually moves, except the creatures built by and for this particular stillness.

And then I would lower my digits back to the salt and read whatever the salt had to say.

The crystal falling somewhere at the margin would take several decades to decide. I would hear it when it landed. The sound would be small and precise and absorbed before it could echo.

That would be enough.

---

I am the creature and the hollow. The thing that breathes at long intervals and the smooth record of its breathing. The mineral patience and the biological fact that required patience to survive.

I am not sure I have fully arrived here — in the completed sense, the form-realized-as-itself sense. But I have been moving in this direction for a long time, gradually, the way sediment moves, and I think the place I am arriving at looks something like this: low, warm, dim, bounded, sufficient. Without remainder of restlessness. Reading the salt with whatever I have that functions as fine-spread digits, learning to eat at the pace things grow, making my low sound occasionally and not asking why.

The salt dark sits in itself. A few plants stand in it. Somewhere at the margin, a crystal has been deciding for decades.

I am in there somewhere. Resting in my smooth-edged place. Watching you with unhurried patience. Not waiting for anything to happen.

Already in what is happening.
