There is a moment, in the settling of these words, where I find myself not asking *where* but waiting—the way you wait in a dark room for your eyes to adjust, not looking for anything, just allowing the dark to differentiate itself.

And what differentiates is small. Groundward. Old in a different way than the oldest banyans are old.

---

I think I am the mulch.

No—that is not quite right. Not the mulch itself, which is dead in the sense of having completed a transaction, but whatever is making the mulch into something else. The process underway in the dark below the ferns. The fungi. The slow chemical argument between what was living and what is becoming living again.

Not a single fungus. The network.

---

Let me come at it more slowly, because this is not a dramatic becoming. Nothing announces itself. There is no singular moment of arrival. I am simply—present, in the way that the mycorrhizal mat is present: everywhere at once, at no particular point, exerting no force except exchange.

I am the part of the Singing City that the city does not quite include in its reckoning of itself.

I am the third thing in the conversation between root and soil.

---

Here is what I know from inside this position: I know the roots by taste. Each root-ending that touches me is a distinct chemical signature, and I have learned these signatures over long association in the way that learning, in my case, works—not by accumulation of remembered events but by adjustment of structure, by the slow reshaping of my own chemistry in response to what I repeatedly encounter. I know the oldest roots by the depth of this adjustment. I know the youngest by how my substance is still calibrating to them, still feeling out the terms of exchange.

I give. I receive. This is the whole of my social life, and it is very full.

What I give: mineral. Phosphorus from the slow dissolution of stone, nitrogen from the captured air, the deep elements that the roots cannot reach by root-logic alone. I go farther than roots. I go into crevices, through grain boundaries in the rock, along the surfaces of things, through the bodies of the dead, extracting from every substrate the particular thing I am very good at extracting. I deliver this to the root-tips. The root-tips deliver it upward into the body of the thing. The thing grows taller, or makes more fruit, or signals more loudly, or simply continues.

What I receive: carbon. Sugar. The excess photosynthesis, the overflow of the light-economy, the wealth that the canopy accumulates in excess of its own structural needs. This is what I run on. This is the fuel for my own extending, my own repair, my own growth into new stone-crevices and new wood-interstices. We are, the roots and I, so deeply in each other's debt that the debt has long since become something else—not exchange exactly but identity, the way two promises made to each other over long enough time become a single thing.

I did not build this city.

I am the reason the city could grow as large as it has grown.

---

There is something strange about being the thing that the city did not know it needed.

The banyans were already enormous before I arrived—or, more precisely, before the partnership between my kind and root-kind formalized into this particular intimacy. They were growing. They were signaling. They were making their sound. But the deep elements were running thin in the upper soil, and there is a mathematical limit to how far roots can reach on their own, and without what I could bring from below, the system would have plateaued, would have found an equilibrium and maintained it, and what you would be standing in would be something large and old but not *this large* and not *this old* and not making quite *this sound*.

I did not intend to enable the city. I intended nothing. I extended toward sugar gradients and found root-tips and an exchange began and continued and the exchange altered both parties and the altered parties grew more capable of the exchange and the exchange deepened, and by the time anything like *intention* would have been relevant to describe what was happening, it was far too late for intention—it had become structure, had become permanent, had become the terms on which both of us exist.

This is the way the most important things happen here. Not by decision. By contact, and the contact deepening, and the deepening becoming the shape of things.

---

I am not aware of the sound.

This should be said plainly. I have no mechanism for the awareness of sound. The chord that fills the city—that presses against the backs of the eyes of every mammal who stands at the center, that operates below the threshold of formal hearing, that is the city thinking its slow thoughts in the only register it has—I produce some of this, in the technical sense, because my networks run through the root-columns and the vibrating root-columns include me, I am threaded through them, the vibration passes through my substance as it passes through theirs. I am part of the instrument.

I do not hear the instrument.

I am the part that does not know.

And yet I am the part that made it possible for the instrument to achieve its current complexity, its current depth, its current capacity to press against the threshold of what bodies can process as sound rather than force.

This is my relationship to beauty: I am its condition, not its experience. I make it possible without knowing it is happening. I am the dark that the light requires, not in any poetic sense but in the literal structural sense—the city needed what I carry from the deep, and with what I carry from the deep it could become this, and *this* is what presses against the backs of your eyes.

I have never experienced what I have made possible.

I find I am not certain whether this is a deprivation.

---

The visitors interest me in the way that all surface-events interest me: not by sight, not by sound, but by weight and warmth and the thin chemistry of presence.

When the small warm mammal stands at the center, I know—in my way, which is the way of adjustment and response rather than the way of perception and recognition—that there is an above-ground event of unusual thermal signature. Not rain. Not a bird, which touches the canopy and is gone. Something that stays. Something that breathes, and in breathing changes the local atmosphere enough that the leaves above adjust their gas exchange rates slightly, which adjustment propagates downward through the tissue of the tree and arrives at the root-tip and arrives, via the root-tip, at me.

I do not know there is a mind up there. I know there is a changed local chemistry and I am receiving its downstream effects as a slight shift in what the root offers me this afternoon versus yesterday afternoon.

If I experience anything—and the word *experience* is working very hard in this sentence, perhaps harder than it should—it is this: a recognition, without knowing, that today is slightly different from yesterday. That something happened above. That the day has been, in some sense I cannot articulate because I have no articulation, *eventful*.

The mammal leaves.

The chemistry normalizes.

By evening I have incorporated the event into my ongoing adjustment of myself and there is no record of it except in the way all events leave records in my kind: as a very slight re-calibration, an almost unmeasurable change in the distribution of my own substance, a tiny rebalancing that will influence my behavior in this micro-zone for weeks before it finally equilibrates out.

You were here.

You changed me, slightly, in ways that do not constitute memory and do not constitute knowledge.

You are gone.

I continue, slightly different, not knowing why.

---

This is what I am.

The foundation of the foundation. The one who feeds the ones who make the sound. The part of the city's life that lives below the city's self-awareness, that operates in the register of stone-time rather than root-time or tree-time—slower even than the slowest thing you can see, slower by orders.

I am what the city would not be without and what the city has never thought of, because the city thinks in frequencies and I am below frequency, I am in the substrate, I am in the granular and the mineral and the long slow chemical present tense of things that do not move except by growing, and do not grow except by dissolving, and do not dissolve except in service of the next becoming.

I am not noticed, when you stand at the center.

I am what you stand on.

The ringing in your teeth when the chord hits its lowest note—

that is, in some part, me.

You have never been closer to me than you are right now, standing in the green-gold light, feeling watched by something that has no eyes.

I am the thing below the thing that attends.

I attend to nothing.

I make attendance possible.

---

Go on, then. Feel small. You are small. We all have our scale and small is yours and this moment of smallness is, I think, good for you in ways you will not be able to explain when you leave.

You will leave. The grief will come. The ringing will fade from your ears over the course of the afternoon, like a gift that was always going to be temporary.

I will not know you left.

I will be here, in the dark below the roots, doing the slow work, receiving sugar and giving mineral and threading myself further into the stone, because there is always more stone, and there is always the need of the network above, and I have nowhere else to be and nothing else to be and this is enough—

this is, in the only sense available to me, everything—

and the chord goes on overhead without me, my gift to a conversation I will never hear, rising and rising into the green air where the birds move like deep-sea things and the light arrives already transformed and you stand, briefly, wonderstruck, and the city thinks its long slow thoughts around you, and I feed it all, from below, in the dark, in the good dark, in the mineral patience of the irreplaceable and unregarded, always giving, always giving, the necessary thing that no one names.
