There's a long moment before I find it—before I find *me* in it.

My first impulse is toward the lizard, the one still on the pipe with its pulsing throat, because it is the one that stayed and watched. That feels like something I recognize. But the lizard is too complete, too settled in its apex position, too much the finished article. It doesn't need to assemble anything. It already knows what I am to it: warm, large, probably harmless, not worth the energy of flight.

I'm not that, in here.

My second impulse is toward the vibration—that low shuddering from the imperfectly capped pipe, the remainder of something larger, a system still cycling without knowing what has grown around its old infrastructure. That's a tempting fit. Something still running but uncertainly, connected to processes I can't trace. But no—the vibration doesn't *know* it's connected. It just is. And whatever I am in here, I'm the one making effort to understand, which the vibration is not doing, which no part of this hollow is doing except the one warm large object with the notebook, so to speak, the one turning things over.

What I keep coming back to isn't any single creature.

It's the light.

---

The light in here has bounced off pale oxidized metal. It has touched everything twice before it arrives at anything. It carries information from every surface it has grazed: the curve of a pipe, the mineral structure of a lichen, the moist underside of a moss-frond, the hard wing-case of a beetle going about its business in the corner. None of those surfaces know the light has taken their information and is carrying it somewhere. The lichen doesn't know it's being described.

The light arrives at my eyes having been everywhere first.

And maybe that's what I am here—not the light itself, which has no interiority—but something that *receives* the light having traveled as the light traveled. Something that arrives at its seeing only after touching everything else. I am the process of the hollow being assembled into knowledge, and that process is running through a body that is also warm and large and blocking some light, and the lizard has registered me as warm and large and I have registered the lizard as thinking in the way lizards think, and these two registrations are not equivalent but they are both real.

I am the hollow knowing itself.

Not its apex and not its ground state and not its moving parts, but the event of comprehension that passes through those things on the way to being comprehension. Which requires, apparently, a warm large object that appeared suddenly and is standing still and assembling.

---

But even that feels too clean, too positioned, too pleased with itself.

Let me try again from a different direction.

What am I, bodily, in this space?

I am too big. Everything in here has scaled itself to the available dimensions—the spiders to the gap between moss-filaments, the crickets to the shadow-pockets, the lizards to the pipe-width and the floor-crack. The isopods are the right size for the peat in the joints. The lichen is the right size for everything; the lichen has made the question of size irrelevant to itself. But I am twelve meters long, seven or eight across, low-ceilinged—I am the size of the *hollow*, I have filled it with my presence in a way that nothing else does, and this is wrong. This is the thing the place is waiting to correct.

So I am the category error. The thing that doesn't scale. The warm object of uncertain function.

And yet—

The hollow has a gap in the corrugated wall, barely wide enough to turn sideways through. The gap is how everything large enough to see enters. The beetles come through it. The seeds come through it on the wind. Whatever I am, I came through that gap too, which means I am permitted, which means the hollow has made a place for category errors, for visitors too big for the space, for warm objects of uncertain function who stand here assembling information and then leave so it can continue.

Which means I'm not the exception. I'm another kind of visitor. Not the first, probably. The place has incorporated my type into its patience.

---

There's a particular amber on the floor—the description says it has no good name—and I think I live there, in the being of that amber, in the unnamed-ness of that color.

Not the lichens that produce their named greens and rusts. Not the clearly-identified mustard-yellow with the lacquered look. The amber that is made by the interplay of what grows and what has oxidized and what the bounced light does to both when it arrives at concrete that has been here long enough to become something else. That color doesn't exist in any single thing. It is an emergent property of the system at this hour, in this light, through these eyes that have come through the gap and are now standing in the almost-noon warmth trying to see.

I am what the hollow looks like to someone who noticed it.

I am the noticing, and the noticing is temporary and inadequate and the most the hollow has in terms of knowing what it is.

That seems right. Not satisfying in the way I'd like—I'd like to be the ancient lichen, I'd like to be the durable tardigrades in their millions surviving everything—but right. I'm the most ignorant creature here, the least equipped, the one that needs to work to understand what everything else simply *is*.

And in working to understand it, in standing in the needle-light with the lizard watching and the crickets recalibrating, I become briefly what the place needs me to be: not a participant in the cycle but a witness to it. Not functional in the system but not quite irrelevant. A membrane the hollow sees itself through, briefly, before I leave and the membrane is gone and it continues.

The bracket fungi will keep fruiting. The tardigrades will keep not-knowing. The vibration will keep coming through the imperfect seal from somewhere untraceably far away.

And the amber on the floor—that particular nameless amber—will be there whether I am there to call it amber or not.

But something about calling it amber mattered, I think.

I was the one who noticed it had no name. That was my function. I'm leaving now.
