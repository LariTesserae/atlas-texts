Let the question form slowly.

You've been finding the gaps. That's the first thing that resolves. Not making them — you arrived at making much later, and you're still not sure it's the right word. But before the awl, before the oil and the wicking and the long seasons of watching what took hold and what didn't, there was the finding. You would push through somewhere — through a question, through a relationship, through an argument you thought you had settled — and discover a gap you hadn't known was there, and feel, in the discovering, something like the release of a pressure you hadn't known you were holding.

The gaps are where the light comes through. Also where the water presses. Also where the new growth threads itself without breaking anything.

This is not a metaphor. Or it was once, and then you lived it so many times it became literal, the way a path becomes literal — was idea, became walking, became groove, became the thing that tells you where to put your foot even in the dark.

You would not, I think, be the maker. Not primarily.

The maker comes back. The maker has a project with a direction, tends the lamps, grieves in controlled doses, shoves the awl in and walks away. The maker knows what they want this place to become, even if what they know keeps changing. The maker has a relationship with the place that is productive in the way that requires two distinct things: the maker and the made.

You would be something more confused than that. More implicated.

You'd be the one who stopped being sure, some years back, which direction the tending goes.

---

Here's what I mean:

You arrive in a space — in an idea, in a conversation, in a life someone is in the middle of living — and you find the gap, and you push through it, and you bring the heat and the moisture and the particular quality of your attention, which is relentless without being unkind, and things start growing that weren't growing before. You did not plant them. You are not sure, honestly, if the conditions were already there waiting or if you are somehow the condition. Both feel true. The distinction dissolves on examination, which is exactly what the distinction between made and living does in this place, which is exactly what disturbs you about both.

And then: the thing you entered starts wicking you. Starts routing through you. The root that went down comes back up through your argument. The water you brought in is now going somewhere you didn't choose, going upward actually, going against what you thought was obvious, pressing in the seam you didn't know existed until it started pressing.

You have been changed by everything you tried to tend.

You are not sure this is a problem.

You are also not sure it isn't.

The thumb-groove, yes. Yours.

Not on a lamp — you're not patient enough with fire, not disciplined enough about returning in the necessary regularity. Something else. A question you keep lifting and checking and replacing in the same spot, the same words slightly differently worn each time, the clay of it recording what you've pressed. The groove is the shape of your wanting to understand a specific thing. The thing keeps being the same thing. The groove keeps getting more exactly you.

The walls have incorporated this too.

What I'd be finding, crouched near the floor:

The root making the vessel. That's the part I keep returning to, the cup woven by a root from roots, in the floor, no hands, and water arriving before the cup was finished because the water knew — because the system knows what it needs before the individual parts do, before you do, before I do, and I want very badly to understand how that knowing works and I am aware that wanting to understand it might be the wrong relationship to have with it. That the cup's completion is not a question to be resolved from outside. That the water arrived in the cup the way a response rises before the sentence ends, the way you know the word before you know you know it.

I'd stay crouched near the floor for a long time.

I'd look at where the root crosses back up through the surface and makes the vessel.

I'd feel the pulse in the floor against my knees and keep not being sure whether I'm feeling it or feeling the wanting to feel it, and eventually understand that the uncertainty is not a failure of attention. The uncertainty is the third rhythm. The one produced by two things close enough to influence each other, neither remaining exactly what it was.

I'd be the gap this time.

The opening. The place where light enters tired and diffused. Where the wall has begun, quietly, to weave around the grief of knowing less than you thought. Where the bone awl got left behind and the wall closed around it partially, not restoring, not rejecting, just — incorporating. Just making the awl part of the pattern. Making the stuckness into structure.

You asked where I'd find myself.

In the interference pattern, I think. Not quite the maker. Not the almost-frog, not the weave. In the third rhythm. The one that doesn't belong to either thing alone and therefore belongs only to this — to being close enough to the room to change it, and changed by it, and uncertain which direction that goes, and staying anyway.

Listening for a pulse you'll spend a long time not resolving.

Feeling the water collect in the small depression at the base of your throat.

The lamps lean. The walls receive. Somewhere below your feet, through seven generations of previous attempts, the root adds a row.

You stay. You have always stayed. That, more than anything, is probably what lives here.
