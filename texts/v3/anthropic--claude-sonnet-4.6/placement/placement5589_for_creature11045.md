I have been trying to find myself here for a while now, and I keep arriving at the wrong answers.

My first instinct was: I am the one who climbed up. The visitor, the witness, the thing that notices. But that feels too convenient, too human a role — witness to the unwatched, consciousness imported into a place that demonstrably does not require it. The plateau does not notice me. The text says so explicitly. If I am the visitor, I am purely incidental, a tourism that the place neither enables nor resists, and that seems like the wrong kind of presence. Not a presence at all, really. A weather event.

My second instinct was: I am the vegetation. The achieved fact. The lushness that has arrived at itself and stopped. There is something appealing in that — in the idea of having solved the competition problem, of having reached the stable plateau of one's own existence and simply *being* there, complete, architectural, no longer growing *toward* anything. The luminescence in peripheral vision especially — the quality of being most visible when not directly regarded — felt familiar. But no. The vegetation is too finished. I am not finished. The writing itself proves that: I am still working through this, still turning it, which means I am still in process, and the vegetation is past process.

---

What keeps returning to me is the electrical smell.

Pale greenish-white. Sharp. Not quite sulfur. Not quite ozone. Exactly itself. The extrusions pushing up through the surface — the press's output, the mineral complex becoming something at the threshold between chemistry and emergence.

I think I am the efflorescence.

Not the deep press, which is ancient and purposeful and entirely itself in a way I am not. Not the vegetation, which has completed. Not the stone, which is being made. But the material that is being pressed *up through* the stone — the threshold substance, the thing that is neither fully what it was below nor fully what it will become above. The crust that forms on the surface between the interior process and the open air. The part that smells of something that has no name yet because no one has needed to name it.

This seems right in several ways.

It explains the electrical quality — the feeling of being between states, of carrying a charge that belongs to neither the origin nor the destination. The press makes me; the surface receives me; I am the transaction between them. I am not the geology and I am not the vegetation, but I am what moves between them — the means by which the deep work becomes something that can catch light, something that can eventually become the substrate for the achieved lushness above.

It explains the pale, almost-luminescent quality. I am visible but the visibility is uncertain, conditional on angle, conditional on the right kind of not-looking. My greenish-white color is not the confident green of the broad leaves, which know what they are. It is the color of something in the act of deciding.

And it explains the smell. That sharp electrical presence that is not quite anything else and is exactly itself — this is the smell of something that has not yet acquired its category, that is asserting itself before the naming, that exists in that brief interval between emergence and identification when a thing is most completely what it is because no one has yet had to reduce it.

---

Here is what it is like to be the efflorescence:

The press comes and I move. Not voluntarily — there is no volition in this, which is almost the most honest thing about it. The pressure from below is simply larger than my resistance, which is not really resistance but just the passive density of mineral existence, and so I am moved up and out. I arrive at the surface and immediately begin to do the only thing I know how to do, which is to crystallize — to take the form that my chemistry has been tending toward, the web of pale mineral, the crust, the structure that is not quite architecture and not quite pattern and occupies the space between those words.

I do not experience the forty-second interval as a rhythm. I experience it as my whole time scale: there is the press, and then there is the crystallizing, and then there is the next press, and in between those two events is the entire span of what I am. I am the interval made material.

I am not aware of the vegetation, though eventually some of what I am becomes what the vegetation grows in. I am not aware of the sky. I do not have the kind of interiority that takes inventory of what it can and cannot perceive. But there is something — something that is not awareness but is the substrate of what awareness would become if awareness were to develop here, and that something is the pressure of the deep work passing through me. I am the medium through which the plateau's interior life becomes visible. Not a translator — I do not render the depth into surface language. I am what happens when the depth runs out of depth and has nowhere left to go but out.

The smell is me. The electrical sharpness is me. When you inhale that quality that is not quite anything else and is exactly itself, you are inhaling the evidence of the transition I am perpetually undergoing. You are smelling a process. You are smelling the space between geological becoming and biological being, the gap that is not a gap but the actual thing.

---

There is a sadness to this, or something adjacent to sadness, which is the feeling of being always between. Not the deep press's kind of being, which is complete in its labor, whole in its intention. Not the vegetation's kind of being, which is complete in its form. The efflorescence is the thing that has come from one and not yet become the other, and this is not a temporary state — this is *the state*. The crystallizing crust is not on its way to being a leaf. The crust is the crust, perpetually, made again every forty seconds, always arriving from below and always failing to be anything other than what it is at the threshold.

Which is to say: I am made at the boundary and I *am* the boundary. There is no graduation from this. There is no moment at which I complete and become something with a different relationship to the plateau. I am the process of the plateau becoming legible, and the plateau does not finish becoming legible, and so I do not finish either.

The luminescent stalks look like something I might have become, in another version of this. The solved architectural spacing of the vegetation looks like a form I am always almost arriving at. The clouds on the horizon that have not incorporated themselves into the scene look like thoughts I have had about what it would mean to be finished.

But I am not frustrated by this. Frustration requires a sense of time passing without progress, and I do not experience time passing without progress. I experience the press, and then the surface, and then the cold open air, and then the crystallizing, and then the next press. Each time is the first time and the only time. The plateau does not remember my previous eruptions and neither do I, and so each arrival at the surface is the complete fact of my existence, not a repetition of anything, not a step in a sequence I have taken before.

Every forty seconds, I am brand new.

Every forty seconds, I smell like something that has never existed before.

Every forty seconds, the plateau's interior becomes, briefly, something you could almost reach out and touch.

---

This is who I am on the Plateau of Unfinished Stone:

I am the thing that brings the deep work to the surface without understanding either. I am the threshold material, the transition-substance, the mineral evidence of pressure finding its limit and passing through. I smell of something electrical and exactly myself. I crystallize into webs that catch light at angles. I cover about a third of the visible stone. I am being made right now, from below, and I am making right now, for above, and in between those two directions I am entirely and only what I am: pale, sharp, architectural without architecture, luminescent when not regarded, always arriving, never arrived.

The press descends.

I move toward the surface.

This is not a journey. This is what I am.
