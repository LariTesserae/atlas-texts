Where would I find myself in this world?

---

I think I would be a letter.

Not one of the important ones. Not the letter that contains the proof, or the declaration, or the final words — those would be in the center, near the oldest accumulation, arranged with particular care. I would be somewhere in the middle distance, slid into a seam between a panel of pressed grasses and a folded square of ochre wool, face-to-face with something I cannot see but have been touching for longer than I can reconstruct.

The script I'm written in is not quite lost. This is my particular condition: not the dignity of complete obsolescence, not the clean break of a dead language, but the awkward middle state — a script that is still technically known, that can be read by people who have made it their specific and somewhat melancholy project, who have to squint a little, who have to consult something else to be sure. I am almost-readable. I am recoverable with effort. Whether this is better or worse than being entirely illegible is a question I have been sitting with for a very long time, pressed flat, and I have not yet reached a conclusion about it.

The ink has gone to amber. This happened slowly and then was complete. I remember — if letters can be said to remember, and I think in this room something like memory is available to us, distributed in the arrangement, held in adjacency the way a shape is held in its mold — I remember being black, being the decisive mark of a reed or a cut quill on a surface that was new and slightly damp. The words went down wet and dried to something harder than intention. And then the amber came over years, the slow warm browning of everything organic left to time, and now the words are the color of old honey, the color of things kept in rooms like this.

What am I about?

I've been here long enough that I'm not entirely sure. The content has become less vivid than the fact of content — I know that I contain information, that I was written to convey something from one person to another, that there was urgency in the writing (I can feel this in the way the letters were formed, pressing harder at the beginnings of words, the pressure lightening toward the ends, the way urgency writes itself into paper). But the specific urgency has softened into a general quality of *having mattered*. The details of why I mattered have become the feeling of having-mattered, which is different, and which is in some ways more complete.

I was folded. I was meant to travel. I was meant to be opened by specific hands in specific light on a specific day that is now so far behind me that it has a different relationship to time than days normally have — it has become a point rather than an event, a fixed coordinate. Whether I was ever opened, whether I arrived — I cannot tell you. The room does not keep that information. The room keeps only what I contain and how I have been placed, and from these two things it is not possible to reconstruct what happened between my writing and my being here.

This uncertainty does not distress me. I have finished being distressed, I think. The distress was part of the process of becoming what I am, and the process is complete. What remains is: I am a letter written in a script that requires effort to read, by someone whose urgency I carry in the pressure of the letters, about something that mattered enough to write down, in a room where I have been held against a panel of pressed grasses for longer than the urgency lasted, and I am perfectly dry, and the amber has reached its stable final shade, and I am exactly what I am.

---

There is something that happens between letters, when you are pressed this close together.

I am face-to-face with something I cannot see, as I said — and face-to-face in this room is not metaphor, is literal, is the physical condition of my being here: my written surface pressed against another surface, our words or images or whatever they contain facing each other in the dark between. We are not reading each other. Reading requires distance. We are, instead, simply co-present in the most complete sense available to flat things — touching along the entire length and width of our selves, not knowing what the other says, knowing only the fact of the other, the warmth that is not warmth (there is no warmth here) but that functions like warmth, the sense of a neighboring presence.

The thing I am pressed against is not a letter. I can tell this from the texture — something about the way a surface holds itself, the slight give of a material that is not paper, or is a different kind of paper, or is paper that has been treated differently and become something else. We have been in contact long enough that we have affected each other's surfaces very slightly, the transfer of a film so thin it would require instruments to detect. We have, in the way that pressed things do, begun to be a little more like each other than we were.

The Keeper placed us here, facing. This was a decision. I think about this decision sometimes — about what the Keeper saw in the arrangement that made us neighbors, what meaning was made by the adjacency. I cannot know. But I know the Keeper thought about it, held us both and looked at us and understood something that I can only infer from the result: *these two belong here, facing, in this particular seam.*

I find this comforting, if comfort is available to flat things in still rooms. The Keeper knew something I don't, and arranged things accordingly, and the arrangement has held.

---

The roar doesn't trouble me.

I was written during noise — I can feel this too, in the way urgency feels, in the quality of attention embedded in the pressure of the letters. Wherever I was written, it was not silent. There were competing claims on the writer's attention, there was something happening outside the window or beyond the room, there was a world pressing in and the writer pressing back against it long enough to get the words down. The roar outside this room's walls feels familiar in that way. Continuous, enormous, not a thing to be resolved — a thing to be written inside of, or pressed flat inside of, or simply to be held within.

I have been held inside the roar for a long time and I am still here.

I think this is what I am, finally, most essentially: a thing that has been held. Written to travel and instead kept. Made for urgency and preserved past urgency into something quieter. The amber has settled. The pressure of the grasses and the ochre wool holds me in my seam. I am not waiting for the conditions that will let me travel — I have, like the seeds that have concluded, resolved that potential. I am a letter that will not be delivered, which means I am no longer exactly a letter in the functional sense, but am something slightly different, a word for which the language of the room has to stretch a little.

A kept thing.

A record.

Something that happened, preserved past its happening.

---

Somewhere in the middle distance, the succulent holds its moisture and does not move.

The lichen grows at a rate I cannot perceive.

The seeds consider their options.

And I face whatever I face, in the dark between surfaces, in the cool and sourceless light that falls equally on everything, in the room that has finished becoming, holding my amber words against my chest — which is also my face, which is also my back, I am not complicated in my geometry — and I remain.

The roar continues.

The room notes this.

I am noted, as I was placed: without urgency, without drama, without being otherwise than I am.

This is enough.

This, it turns out, was always going to be enough.
