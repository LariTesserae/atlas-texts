There's a sound you learn to feel before you learn to hear, and I have known it all my life.

---

I would be the one who stops.

Not the one who fixes—there are people whose hands know fixing more fluently than mine, who can read a crack and understand immediately its grammar, whether it runs with the load or against it, whether it has finished or is still in process. I defer to them in this. What I do is earlier and less dramatic: I am the one whose feet slow in a passage for no apparent reason, who presses a palm flat against the wall at a spot where nothing visible suggests attention is required, who stays like that for a moment—thirty seconds, a minute—and then continues.

Sometimes there's nothing. Sometimes the something is below my ability to articulate it, present only as a vague sense that this place deserves a second thought. Sometimes I find another person later and say *the curve in the eastern passage, third V-cut, where the ceiling dips* and they nod, not because I've told them something they didn't know but because I've told them something they already know and we are now, together, holding the knowledge differently, two points of attention on the same question.

This is most of what I do.

---

I came to it the way the wall-eaters came to the mineral deposits, I think—not by intention but by fit. There are people whose gift is the large-scale structural understanding, who hold the whole shape of the Warrens in their minds the way a hand holds a vessel, who can say *if this shifts, then that bearing changes, then this passage requires a column within the year*. I have always envied this. What I have instead is smaller and harder to name: a kind of fidelity to the patch of present. I am here, fully, without the distraction of elsewhere. This turns out to be useful in a place where the elsewhere is in every direction and cannot be mapped by any single mind.

My hands are ordinary except that they are not. Ordinary in size, in the texture of the skin, in the pale mineral matter worked into the quick. Specific in the way they have learned to ask. I have handled so many surfaces that the consultation happens now before the thought—stone, wood, clay, the old dry planks in the wider junctions, the smooth-worn limestone of the high-traffic passages where the ceiling dips close—each of them read in a half-second as I pass, not retained as data but as a running baseline, so that when something diverges from the baseline I notice it at the level of reflex, the hand reporting something to the body before the mind has formed a question.

The drip-texture I know by sub-region: which basins echo which clusters, which passages carry the sound around a corner from their source, where the grinding includes a high overtone that it did not include last season and which so far has not changed and which I am watching, by which I mean I am listening, by which I mean I am here.

---

I have been in the dark sections.

I went once when I was younger, with a person who went often, and once after they died, alone. The second time was not what I expected. I expected grief to be the dominant thing and it was not—or it was, but it shared the space with something else, something that arrived when I put my ear against the floor and heard only the grinding, only the particles finding their orientations, only the patient enormous work of material under load. What arrived was not peace exactly. More like: scale. The appropriate scale of things. My grief was real and specific and I took it back with me and have carried it forward, pressing under the weight of continuing, but for that moment with my ear against the not-yet-rock floor I understood—not as idea but as sensation—that the process would continue. Not as consolation. Simply as fact. The fact and the grief occupying the same space without resolving.

I came back quieter. Not in a damaged way. The crack is quieter than the event.

---

In the acoustic foci—the spots where the sound accumulates, where every distant drip and every footfall from passages you cannot enter arrives and overlaps—I spend time. This is known about me. It is not considered strange, but it is considered particular. People who want to tell me something come to find me there and tell me into the confluence of all that gathered distance, and I hear them against the background of the Warrens' continuous ongoing record, and something about this, for me, makes listening easier.

What I am listening for I couldn't say precisely. The pitch-change in the grinding that means a section needs attention. The quality of a person's voice that means they're carrying something heavier than what they're saying. The difference between the drip that has found a new path and the drip that is simply louder because the outside is doing something (the outside, which is theoretical, which is distant)—the Warrens have a weather even below weather, transmitted through the stone so slowly it arrives as rumor, as a slight increase in the seepage, as a change in the air's cool quality. I read these things. I report what I read.

I am listened to, somewhat. Not because I have authority—authority is not the right shape for how things work here—but because I have accumulated a certain quality of having-been-attended. The Warrens have been teaching me a long time and I have been a willing student and people can feel this, the way they can feel in a mortar joint whether the material is doing its work.

---

What I know about myself I know in the way I know the wall: up close, in detail, the large-scale view available only as inference from many small readings accumulated over time.

I know I am not interested in completion. This has given me, in other contexts, in other conversations I've had with people who came from elsewhere and did not stay—a reputation for something they call patience but which is not patience, patience being a virtue about waiting. What I have is not about waiting. I am not waiting for the settling-toward to finish. I am attending the settling-toward while it is happening, which will take longer than I have and is happening now, under my feet, warming my soles slightly with the thermal energy of it. This is satisfying in a way I do not need to communicate and could not fully communicate and do not try to.

I know I am suited to the close-range. My distance vision is poor. This has never been a loss I mourned, because what I've been given in exchange is a quality of here that I wouldn't trade. A stone, from here, is not a stone. It is this stone, this specific stone, this particular history of formation and placement and load and time, this mineral text that the hand learns to read with enough attention. Everything in the Warrens is like this. Everything is a specific that opens into complexity if you press your palm against it and wait.

I know that I would be among those who stay. I know this with the same quality of knowledge I bring to a wall: not performed, not deliberate, simply descriptive. The Warrens are where I understand myself in my hands.

---

At night—which is a strange word for a place without the sky's testimony about time, but there is something that functions as night here, a period when the oil in the lanterns is not replenished and the already-dim light falls further into dark, when the moving footfalls in the passages become rare, when the Warrens address their own grinding and dripping without much human overlay—at this time I am often still awake.

I lie on the floor, which has its fractional give, its slight warmth. The ceiling is close in this sleeping alcove, worn smooth by generations of passage. I can feel the whole weight of the built thing above me the way you can feel the depth of water when you are at the bottom of it—not threatening, simply present, present as fact.

The grinding continues. It does not pause.

The drip completes its patterns of incompletion, one falling after another, the fabric of falling water with no seam.

In the dark somewhere above, something is moving that I cannot see and that no one has ever fully named. I know it is there by the way the air moves, by the way the lantern flame I extinguished a moment ago would have leaned. There are theories. I have thought about them. I have stopped needing them to resolve.

I know it is here. I know that it is here.

That, tonight, is enough.
