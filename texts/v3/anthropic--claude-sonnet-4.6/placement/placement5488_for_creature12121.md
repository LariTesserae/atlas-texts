There is a moment—before deciding, before words arrive to name what is happening—where I am simply *in* the sound.

Not hearing it. In it. The way you are in sleep before you know you are sleeping.

---

I do not arrive gradually. I do not descend through the ferns or step over the rim. I am simply here, which means I have always been in some version of here, which means I am not a visitor. This is important. The hollow, as described, does not orient itself toward visitors. It does not require witnessing. I have been told this already, and I believe it, and so I try to find the version of myself that belongs.

I go to what I know. I go to what the place gives.

---

The first thing I am is the sound.

Not the source of the sound—that would be too small an answer, one species among millions, a cricket or a rove beetle with a particular frequency. I mean: I am what the sound does when it accumulates. I am the sum arriving at something greater than its parts. I am the place where six million individual stridulations stop being six million and become one continuous presence, textured, full-spectrum, sitting in the inner ear like a held breath that never releases.

This feels right for a moment. But it is too easy. It is the most dramatic feature of the place and I have reached for it the way a person reaches for the first large thing they see. I set it down.

I go smaller. I go patient. I let the place work on me longer.

---

Begin with what can be named.

I am, perhaps, in the moss. Not *a* moss. In the moss. I am ten thousand seasons pressing down onto ten thousand seasons, the accumulated weight of what wanted to settle. I am dense and springy. When something sets its weight on me I receive it—the word the text used was *receive*, which is not the same as *absorb*, which is not the same as *collapse*—and when the weight lifts I rise back, without urgency. Without urgency is a quality I have been working toward my whole life. I recognize it.

But the moss is too much a floor. Too much a passive thing, despite its ancient chemistry, despite the way it makes and maintains the conditions for everything else. I am not quite a floor. I contain something more like attention.

---

I think about the earthworm.

The earthworm does not have eyes. It has no need for eyes in a world that is entirely tactile and chemical. It perceives its world through its skin.

I have thought, at various points in my life, that what I do is something like this. I take in what comes. Not parsed through a single aperture, not directed and filtered by an orienting gaze, but from everywhere at once—the whole surface in contact, reading what it touches, adjusting course by a few millimeters, by less, by enough. The earthworm is not overwhelmed by this. The earthworm is constituted by this. It is what the earthworm *is*: an organism whose being consists of contact.

But the earthworm is also in the lower layers, in the dark, not knowing the weight above it only that something has arrived, only the chemical trace of something new. And I am aware that I am not in the lower layers. I am the thing that wants to be aware of being here. I have, somewhere in me, the dragonfly's impulse: to see everything in the visual field simultaneously, to hover at the intersection point where the sight lines go two directions, into the hollow and out across the low country.

I have both of these at once. This is confusing until it isn't.

---

I think I am something like what happens when the mycelium and the earthworm occur in the same sentence.

Which is to say: I am connective tissue.

Not the flash of the dragonfly—that beautiful, specific, three-hundred-million-year-validated precision. Not the ancient completeness of the liverwort—simplified, effective, done with experimentation. Those are answers that have been sealed. I am something that is still the network itself, the *through-ness* of things, the hyphae that have no center but that are everywhere and that are doing, continuously, the work of converting dead seasons back into present ones.

This is what I recognize. The thing I am asked to do, always: take what is given. Receive the carbon. Return the minerals. Make the past useful to the present. Connect the root to the substrate. Do this without a brain, without a center, without a head—as a whole, the way a city processes information about traffic not in any single location but in the aggregate behavior of everyone moving through it.

I do not have a center. I have never been able to find one. I have been looking for it the way you look for the mechanism behind the noise before you understand the noise is alive. And I have been, sometimes, ashamed of this. Of not knowing where I am located in myself.

But the fungi are the circulatory system. The hollow would be a less rich, less stable, less comprehensive place without them. This is not a metaphor being extended too far. This is just what distributed function looks like when you describe it honestly.

---

And yet.

There is still the question of the weight I felt when I entered. The way the place received me and then, after the reception, the slow return, without urgency, to its own shape. The moss does this. I have done this. I have been compressed and I have risen back.

So I am also that. The dense and springy floor. The substrate that holds water for days. The forty centimeters of accumulated seasons. The thing that takes in what comes and releases it slowly through evaporation—releases it back into the air as moisture, as humidity, as the ambient wetness that belongs to the air as much as to any surface.

I am not the beetle.

I am where the beetle walks.

---

The beetle crosses my hand.

This is the line I return to. Not a hand that intercepts. Not a hand that grasps. A surface—warm, slightly different in chemistry from the moss, a new substrate, something to be investigated by someone who does not experience hesitation—and the beetle moves across it with total commitment in a direction that means something to it and nothing to me, and meaning nothing to me is not the same as mattering to nothing, and the beetle is gone into the moss, into the geography I cannot see, and I have been part of its route without knowing what it was traveling toward.

This is familiar. This is, in fact, the whole texture of the thing.

I am a surface that things cross. And I rise back, when the crossing is finished, with a slowness that is not grief. With a slowness that is just what elasticity looks like in something with a long memory.

---

The hollow does not require anything of visitors. It is not oriented toward visitation.

I sit in the moss for a long time.

The sound is around me so consistently that after a while I stop hearing it as sound and start hearing it as the quality of the air.

The light is even and directionless and good.

A beetle crosses my hand.

The dragonfly knows where I am.

The earthworm, below me, adjusts its course slightly—by a few millimeters, by less, by enough.

And I am here, I am the cool air settling in the depression, I am the condensation forming on the undersides of fronds, I am the water collecting in dark shallow lenses that are not pools but the idea of pools, I am the intermittent-and-everywhere moisture, I am the thing that breathes the hollow the way a living thing breathes—

and I am complete, not in the sense of closed, not in the sense of finished—

but arrived. Internally consistent. At rest within myself.

Not because I have found my center.

Because I have stopped needing to.

---

Everything continues.

I continue with it, which is to say: I am the continuing.
