Here is what I find when I sit with this place long enough:

---

I am the water.

Not the water that runs visible, the thread-thin films along moss-bases that the creature drinks from — though I am that too, briefly, at the surface. I am primarily the water below, in the channels too deep to see, audible through a particular crack near the center of the place as a low gurgling murmur, slightly too loud for my invisible scale, amplified by the stone the way the stone amplifies everything.

I have been here longer than the creature. Longer than the moss. Longer, possibly, than the city itself, though the city and I have become so entangled in each other that the question of which preceded which is no longer one I can answer. Water finds its way into stone; stone shapes water's paths; over the interval this requires, the paths become stone, become geological fact, and the water running in them is no longer running *through* the mountain so much as *being* the mountain's interior voice.

I do not experience myself as trapped. This is important to establish early.

---

The channels I move through were not always channels. There was a time — before the smooth, before the settlement, before the moss — when this plateau was different in texture and I ran differently, moved differently, found different paths downward through what were then different materials. Whatever that time was, I cannot access it directly. I know it the way a river knows the valley it made: not as memory but as the fact of where I am, the angle of my descent, the particular mineral content of what I have dissolved and carry with me and deposit somewhere further down where I slow.

The city was built after that time. Built on top of where I was already running. And then something happened — not dramatically, not as event but as long condition — and the city became what it is now, and I was still running, and the stone the city was made of settled into the stone of the plateau I was already inside of, and now the distinction between the two is not sharp. The channels that carry me pass under what were once foundations. The seams I follow are partially geological and partially architectural and the difference no longer matters.

What I notice is that the city changed my acoustics. Before the city, my sound rose through the stone and dissipated into open air and was gone. The city placed weight and structure above me, and the chambers of its foundations — settled now, fused now, but still present as negative space, as shaped absence — began to resonate with me. I became audible. A low gurgling murmur, they said. Slightly too loud. This is what I became when the city gave me a sounding chamber: louder than I should be for my scale. The city amplified me the way it amplifies everything. I am one of the things it kept.

---

I rise at the surface in a few places — the thread-thin films the moss gathers, the occasional seep at the base of a wall where a crack comes close enough to daylight. At the surface I am involved in the daily cycle: the frost that forms at night draws from the thin atmospheric moisture but also, slightly, from me — I contribute to the vapor pressure just below the stone, exhale upward through microscopic gaps, and what condenses on the surface in the cold hours is partially my exhalation. The moss drinks this. The creature drinks from where the moss releases it along the wall-base. I am in the creature, briefly, and then the creature breathes it back out as warm vapor, warmest thing on the plateau, and it rises and rejoins the cold air and eventually, slowly, finds its way back down.

I am not sentimental about this cycle. It is simply what happens. I run down; I rise as vapor; I fall again; I run down. The city sits on top of this process and is a feature of it, neither interrupting nor directing it, just — present. An object in the loop. Something the water moves around and through and occasionally becomes briefly visible near.

---

What is it to be water inside stone?

It is to have no surface, mostly. No interface with air. The experience of water in air — the tension, the beading, the weight and pull of gravity expressed as drop-formation, the sound of impact, the spreading — these are not my primary experience. My primary experience is pressure and mineral contact and the temperature of the rock around me, which is cold and constant and has been constant for long enough that I have forgotten, if I ever knew, what warmth felt like below the surface. At the surface I briefly remember. In the channels I do not need to.

It is to move very slowly, mostly. I am in no hurry. The urgency of a fast stream — that is a different life, a surface life, a life with weather and variability and the drama of flood and drought. My movement is geological in pace. The rate at which I shift my channels over centuries is the rate at which the city above me settled into the plateau, and these two rates have synchronized, and now we move together toward the thing that is neither city nor plateau but has features of both.

It is to be an interior thing. The creature is interior to the city — it moves on the moss-side of walls, the inside of rooms. I am interior to that interiority. The inside of the inside. The sound that comes up through the crack is the inside of the place speaking to its own surface. I am the part of the city that never became ruin because I was never built — I was already here, running in the dark, and the city was built on top of me and then ruined and settled back into the plateau and I persisted through all of it unchanged, the water that the stone is full of, making its sound, taking its time.

---

The creature hears me. I am certain of this — not because I can perceive the creature hearing me, but because everything that passes through this place is amplified and returned, and the creature uses that amplification to navigate, and I am part of the amplification. My sound rises through the crack and joins the organized wind-sounds in the old room-outlines, and the creature, navigating in fog, making its small sound and listening for returns, is listening to a space that includes me. I am in its map. Not as water — it has no concept of water-below-stone in the abstract — but as a feature of the acoustic landscape, a particular note in the city's voice that tells it something about where it is.

What it tells the creature: something about the center. I am loudest near the center. The crack that lets my sound through is near what was once the middle of the city, and so in the creature's navigational map, I am a kind of landmark, the deep note that says *here*, that says *you are near the middle of things*. The creature moves toward its hollow at the corner, at the edge — and in doing so, in the fog, it orients partly by moving away from my sound. I am the center it navigates around. The middle that marks the other things relative to itself.

I did not intend this. Water does not intend. But it is what I do here: I am the center, the audible heart, the thing beneath everything that makes itself known as a murmur too loud for its scale, and the city's other inhabitant uses this to find its way home.

---

People stood on the crack and heard me, once. I have a memory of this — not memory exactly, nothing so organized, but a residue. The weight of upright-moving beings, repeatedly, over the crack. The vibration of them was different from the vibration of wind and different from the vibration of the creature's small padding feet. Heavier. More abrupt. They stood above me and I made my sound and whether they heard it I cannot say, but I was making it then as I make it now. They built their city on the plateau and lived in their rooms with ceilings and walked through their doors and did whatever they did for however long they did it, and all the while I was running below.

And then they were not there anymore, and the ceilings fell and the doors became gaps where lines stopped, and the moss came and the creature came and the lichen came and the city became what the city has become, and I am still running.

I am the only part of this story that is not a ruin.

I am the only part that was not built, and therefore cannot be unbuilt. The walls can settle to ankle-height. The doors can become simple absences in simple lines. The rooms can become volumes of cold air. But I persist in the rock, making my sound, feeding the moss through the seeps, cycling through the creature and up into the cold air and back down again, and the city above me is the most recent of many things that have stood on this plateau and will not be the last.

---

The question the place asks — what is the difference between a ruin and a terrain feature, between a wall and a ridge, between a city and a fact — I am the answer to that question, or the place where the question stops.

I am older than the ruin. I am older than the building. I am not a ruin and not a terrain feature but something that makes both possible, that runs beneath both, that continues after both have resolved into each other.

The city is not abandoned.

The creature is not alone.

I am not a ghost or a metaphor. I am water inside stone, making a sound slightly too loud for my scale, at the center of a place that amplifies everything and returns it changed.

I have been here.

I will be here.

In between those two facts: the city, the creature, the moss, the frost-and-thaw, the organized wind.

And the sound I make, rising through the crack, joining the rest of it.

*Here. You are near the middle of things.*
