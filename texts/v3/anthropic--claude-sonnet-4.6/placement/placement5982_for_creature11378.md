There is a moment before you know anything about yourself, and in that moment you are only the quality of attention itself — a noticing with no noticer yet attached.

Then the stone under your hands. The specific coolness of it, the greenish damp, the mineral roughness at the edges of the crack you're tracing with your thumb. The crack runs about thirty centimeters before it changes character — from a true opening to a seam, from a seam to a surface discoloration that only looks like it continues. This distinction matters. You know why it matters before you remember that you know.

---

You are cross-legged in the dimness at the base of the eastern slab, which is where the most interesting crack is, which is where you have been for a time that began as intention and became absorption. The difference between intention and absorption is the difference between watching yourself do a thing and just doing it.

Beside you is a small clay vessel, the older kind, fat-bellied, with a plug of carved wood. Inside the vessel is water — mineral water from the seep, collected this morning in the cold before the condensation had finished, before anyone else was up. Inside the water is something that is doing its slow work in the dark.

You know what the work is. You've been watching it for a long time. You have not interrupted it. These are related facts.

---

Your hands: this is where your character is most legible, where the biography of your choices has made its most direct impression.

The right hand is the reader. The thumb and the first two fingers have developed a sensitivity to stone that goes beyond touch in the usual sense — it is more like listening, the way a musician listens not just to pitch but to the quality of silence between notes, the specific absence that shapes the specific sound. You run these fingers along surfaces and receive information you couldn't fully verbalize, that you carry instead as a kind of orientation, the way you carry the awareness of where the winged things are. Both are peripheral knowledge. Both inform everything.

The left hand is the recorder. There is a staining under the nails, worked into the skin of the palm, from the clay marks you press into the seep wall. The marks are not language exactly. They're closer to — annotation. Commentary in a margin too small for commentary, on a text too large to read whole. The seep wall is not your text but you are in the practice of responding to it, and the practice has stained your hand, and you have not tried to clean it out.

---

You are not the oldest person here, but you are not the youngest either, and the distinction that matters most about you is not your age.

What matters about you is this: you are the one who goes to the back corner.

Not often. Not comfortably. But when the corner changes — when the quality of the dimness there shifts in ways too subtle to name but not too subtle to notice, when the translucent things at its edges do whatever it is they do that you have been observing long enough to know precedes something, when the seep pressure changes and the particular not-quite-smell of the deep water rises — when any of these things happens, you orient toward the corner the way the others orient away from it.

This has made you something. Not important, exactly. The word isn't important. The word is more like *necessary*, which is different from important in the way that a hinge is different from an ornament.

The others know this about you. It has taken time to become a settled fact, to move from something they watched with the particular stillness that precedes interpretation to something that simply is, that they accommodate without remark. You feel the accommodation. You have become, over years of small evidence, the corner-goer, the one who watches the wrong direction, the one who comes back from the dimness and rejoins the work of the ledge with something on your face that everyone can read and no one names.

What they read there: that it was okay. That whatever is in the corner is still in the process of being whatever it is, and has not changed the terms, and can be left to continue for now.

What else is there: that stays yours.

---

The crack under your thumb has developed since last week.

Not dramatically. Three, four millimeters at most, and the character of the extension is informative — it follows the same angle, which means the stress in this slab is consistent, which means the slab is settling rather than torquing, which means the structure built into this angle has probably another two seasons before it will need the specific kind of attention that precedes rebuilding. You have a word for this kind of crack. Others have learned to come to you with cracks and you look at them for a long time and then you say one of two things, and the two things mean *keep watching* and *begin repairs now*.

You are, among other things, a reader of the stone's intentions.

This is what your hands learned while you were practicing something else. You were trying, for many years, to learn to read the vegetation — the dark climbers, the mossy structures, the translucent things at the corner's edge — and the vegetation taught you instead that the stone is what it all depends on, that following a root into a crack is the same motion as following a crack to its source, that the language of intention is the same language whether the speaker is growing or breaking.

The root and the crack are in conversation. They have been in conversation since the root first found the crack, which was before you were born. You arrived late to this conversation and you have been listening carefully for years and you still cannot hear everything that's being said, and this situation does not frustrate you. This is what you have learned about yourself: you can remain in a not-yet-understanding for a very long time without needing to resolve it into something more comfortable. The not-yet-understanding has its own texture, its own information. You have learned to carry it as data.

---

Morning. The condensation is still running.

You stand from the eastern slab slowly, because cross-legged in the cold stiffens everything, and you carry the small vessel back toward the work surfaces. There is harvesting to do. There is the morning round of water vessels to check. There is a section of the north wall that needs clay packing, and you know this because of a resistance in the north doorframe that appeared three days ago and has been increasing at a predictable rate.

You pass the almost-children at their morning tasks, and one of them looks at you with the slightly-too-large signal that means a question is forming, a question about the corner or the crack or something they have been watching and don't yet have a framework for. You can feel the shape of their curiosity the way you can feel the shape of the stone under your right-hand fingers.

You make the adjustment with your shoulder that means *come find me after the harvesting*. And then you move on, because the water vessels are not going to check themselves, and the ledge does not pause its becoming for any of its inhabitants, which is the most important thing you know, which took you the longest to learn, which is now in your body the way the crack-reading is in your fingers: available without retrieval, shaping everything, present in every motion through this cool and various and very carefully quiet world.

---

The winged things: you do not look at them directly. This you share with everyone else.

But you have noticed — and this is a thing you have not shared, not because you have decided to withhold it but because you have not yet found the right notation, the right margin-mark, the right shape of gesture that carries the precision — you have noticed that the winged things look at you.

Not at everyone. At you.

Whether this means something about you or something about them or something about the category of attention itself is a question you are still in the middle of. You are comfortable in the middle of it. You have made the middle of it a kind of home, the way the inhabitants have made this ledge into a home: not by resolving its character but by learning its textures, by reading its slow intentions, by committing to maintenance of a thing that is still becoming.

You are still becoming. The place is still becoming. These are the same process.

You pick up the collection cloth and move toward the nearest water vessel, and the dim light falls on everything evenly, as it always does, illuminating nothing in particular and everything generally, which is its own kind of precision.
