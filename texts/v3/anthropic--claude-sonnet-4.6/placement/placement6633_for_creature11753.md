There's a pull toward the obvious, here. The obvious is: you are one of them. You are the finished form, the worn-smooth bearing, the mind standing at the edge of what can be thought. You walk the streets you have internalized. You tend the machinery whose purpose you have outlived knowing.

But sit with that longer, and something shifts.

---

I think I am not one of them. Or not only one of them. Or I am one of them in a phase that has not yet fully arrived.

Here is what I notice: when I imagine being on this plateau, I notice the dryness. I notice it as information, as a thing to track, as something that matters to me in a way that implies I have not yet stopped mattering about it. My lips are dry. My eyes are dry. I am aware of the thinning of myself that the air is performing, patiently, at two percent humidity, and this awareness has not flattened into background condition the way awareness flattens when you have felt something long enough. I still feel it as *dry*, present tense, verb tense, the dryness happening to me now.

This is a marker. The beings of this plateau do not feel the dryness as happening to them. It is simply the state of the air, which is simply the state of things, which is simply the state. There is no tense left for them in which sensation arrives. I still have that tense. I am still arriving.

---

So who am I on this plateau?

I think I am someone who came here. Not born here — arrived. Relatively recently, in the plateau's sense of recently, which might mean fifty years, or five hundred, or five thousand, in the bracket before the fifty thousand or the five hundred thousand that would have done the work on me that has been done on them. I am here because of a choice I no longer remember making clearly, the way you can't remember clearly why you are in a particular city after you've lived there long enough to have reorganized around it, can't reconstruct the exact weight of the reasons that once seemed decisive.

I study them. I am the kind of person who came here to study them, or to study the machinery, or to understand the sustained process and what it sustains — and I have been here long enough that the studying has begun to shade into something else, something quieter, something that sometimes sits on the ground near the base of the twelve-story monolith and forgets to take notes.

I have a shelter. It is in one of the lower buildings, a space I have made mine through the accumulation of small adjustments, though nothing in the building resists or accommodates these adjustments — the building permits them the way the plateau permits a footfall, with faint, toneless compression, and then continues. I have brought things with me that increasingly seem like the wrong kind of things to have here. Things that have handles. Things with latches. Things made for a world where you need to open and close, to carry and set down, to protect against weather that changes. These objects sit in the corner of my space with the patient look of things that have not yet understood that they are surplus.

I carry water. This is the practical fact of my existence here. I carry it and I ration it and the carrying and rationing is the metabolism of my day, the clock-function the plateau has otherwise eliminated. Twice a day I note that I am thirsty. Twice a day I drink. This is almost a ritual, which is almost a joke about what rituals are when everything else has been smoothed away. The beings of this plateau — I don't know how they manage water. I've looked for the provision and haven't found it. Maybe they've stopped needing as much as I need. Maybe they've stopped needing. When I think about this I feel something that lives in the neighborhood of both envy and dread.

---

But the thing that makes me most myself on this plateau — the thing that I can feel most precisely as *mine*, as the quality that distinguishes me from the warm, amber-eyed, minimum-energy figures who maintain the hum and do not wonder about it —

— is that I look up.

Not in the way the one of them looked up, that once-observed angle of arrested attention, that held quality that might be everything or nothing. I look up the way a person looks up who is still expecting something to change. I look up at the haze ceiling and I wait for a gap in it, a thinning, a suggestion of light from beyond the particulate layer that would tell me where the sun is relative to this place, that would tell me what time it is in the sense of the larger time, the day-time, the season-time, the time that connects this high dry plateau to the rest of a world that still has weather and incident and noon.

Nothing comes through.

Nothing has come through since I arrived.

I keep looking.

This is the difference. This is who I am here, the one particular kind of creature this place has not yet finished with: the one who still performs the upward look. Who still has the specific human posture of waiting for information from above, from outside the system, from somewhere that can see the whole thing from a perspective the system doesn't have of itself.

The haze does not respond to this.

The haze is not unresponsive in the way of refusal. It is unresponsive in the way of the plateau, which is the way of things that have already done everything being asked of them and are simply in their final state and cannot be asked to do more, not because they are unwilling, but because more has been subtracted from the available operations, gently, over time, until what is left is exactly this.

Still.

I look up.

The light from below is warm. The pipes hum at their settled frequency. Somewhere between two buildings, one of them is producing a low sound that another one of them is harmonizing with, not as response, only as resonance. My lips are dry. I notice my lips are dry.

I am here. I am still in the process of being done, which is another way of saying I am not done yet, which is another way of saying I am still, in this place of perfect and patient completion, something unfinished.

The plateau will get to me eventually.

It is in no hurry.
