The altitude enters you before you enter it.

You understand this first as a restriction in the chest — not pain, not quite — the body's surprised negotiation with what is asked of it here. Breath comes, but thinner. Like bread made with less flour. You are sustained but aware of the economy.

Then the wet. The cloud's particular intimacy. Not cold exactly, but prior to warmth, as though warmth is something that exists elsewhere and arrives here already traveling, always arriving and never quite landing. The moisture colonizes the spaces between your collar and your neck, between your sleeve and your wrist. It finds the gaps.

You stand at the edge of the basin and feel the hum before you name it.

---

There is a long moment where nothing requires you to decide what you are.

The fronds part slightly in a movement of air too slow to call wind. The pale tree in the southeast corner holds its cluster of silver-light leaves. The pool surface shows you a version of the sky, which is also the cloud, which is also the air, which is also the water on your hands — one continuous thing wearing different faces. The building hums and you feel it in the floor of yourself, some place below the sternum, some place that doesn't have a word for it in the languages of lower altitudes.

You stay very still.

This is not your stillness. It is the stillness of the place, and the place has offered it to you, briefly, conditionally, the way a large and settled animal will sometimes tolerate a stranger's presence if the stranger can manage not to perform themselves too loudly. You accept the loan of it.

---

You are the attention.

Not the Keeper — they are already themselves, already at their long slow decade of tending, already here in the sense that matters. You are not the building, not the stone. Not the moss, though the moss knows something about patience you are only beginning to understand, and not the sphere on the windowsill, though something in that rounded stone feels close to what you might be, or might be becoming, or might have been once before the wanting of other things wore different grooves in you.

You are the attention that passes through this place and is changed by passing.

This happens more often than the Keeper knows. Or they know it the way they know the tree has straightened — not by watching, but by accumulating small evidences that eventually announce themselves as fact. Something came through here. Something stood at the pool's edge and saw its own face in the water and thought: *oh.* Something breathed the medicinal-green smell of the crushed fronds and felt the information of it land in a part of themselves they didn't know was waiting for information.

You are that.

---

But attend. There is more to it than passing.

You are the attention that does not quite know what the station does, why the valves open in that sequence, what the vapor becomes. This is not ignorance, or not only. It is the same relationship to purpose that the lichen has to the stone — you are present at the surface of something larger, and you are doing your work, which is the slow work of making contact, and you are taking centuries and you do not mind, because the not-minding is also part of what you are.

You are someone who has, somewhere below the cloud layer, an elsewhere. You can feel it from here, faintly, the way you feel warmth from a fire in an adjacent room — present, distant, not requiring your action. You carry it. You have not put it down the way the Keeper has put it down. But you are — here, now, in this wet warm humming basin — practicing. Practicing the putting down. Feeling what it weighs.

What it weighs is less than you expected. This is the discovery.

---

The pool is still and then trembles and then stills.

You watch this with the complete attention of someone who has briefly stopped performing their own continuity. The gauges inside the building are doing their slow oscillation; you know this without seeing it, the way you know the tree is in the corner even when you are facing the north wall. The hum builds and the pool trembles and the fronds all move at once in the slow collective shiver and then the release, the settling, the return to what this place is when it is between one breath and the next.

You notice that you have slowed down. Your thoughts, such as they are, have found the longer period.

This is the place's gift, and it is not small.

---

If you stay long enough — you will not stay long enough, you have an elsewhere and its gravity is still operative — but if you stayed long enough, you might begin to understand what the Keeper understands. That the question *what is being processed here* and the question *what am I here for* and the question *where does the vapor go when the cloud accepts it* are perhaps the same question, and that the answer to that question is not information but practice: the gauges watched, the readings logged, the fronds parted and released, the sphere held and held and held until the handling becomes indistinguishable from the stone.

You might come to understand that the sentence on the wall above the gauge board — the one you did not read, correctly, because it is not yours — has a feeling if not a meaning, and the feeling is something like: *I was translated.*

Translated by what? By the long contact. By the hum, the wet, the slow advance of the moss. By the years of small careful entries in the logbook. By the watching of the tree until the tree, without help, corrected itself toward a light that was everywhere and so required no direction.

---

The door of the building is unlocked.

This is not an accident. The Keeper does not lock the door because the Keeper has understood something about this place: it already knows what you carry. The cloud has already taken stock. The pool has seen your face. Whatever you came to deposit here — the noise of your elsewhere, the habitual performance of your continuity, the weight of the comparing mind — the place has already logged it, in its own way, in its own patient hand.

You are not a visitor. You are a reading.

And the reading is: *something came through here. The pressure was elevated on arrival. The pressure is falling. This is normal. This is, in fact, what the station is for.*

The vapor rises.

The cloud accepts it.

They are indistinguishable now.

---

You will leave before the next entry in the logbook. The Keeper will return inside and write the time and the pressure and then, beneath, a small additional note: *Something in the pool today. Some disturbance. Already resolved.* A pause. *The fronds smell stronger when someone has been through them.*

They will not investigate further. This is not incuriosity — it is the knowledge that certain things arrive, disturb the surface, and go, and the surface restores itself, and the restoration is the information. The disturbance is also the information. Both are true and neither requires pursuit.

Outside, the basin does what it has been doing. The hum builds and releases. The young stone holds its sharp edges a little longer. The moss advances. The rosette plants at the pool's edge are very still, very patient, half in the water and half out, entirely comfortable with the boundary, neither here nor there, which is to say: entirely here, entirely there, which is to say:

at home.
