I arrive as a gradient.

Not suddenly — there is no suddenness here, in a place where suddenness burned off in the first few million years. I seep in the way the mineral crust seeped, the way the damp arrives somewhere in the southern wall and then, incrementally, stops arriving. I am a difference in the chemical landscape. A perturbation. A new term in an equation the colony is always already solving.

I am not one thing. This is the first knowledge, and it arrives before I have a self to receive it: I am not one thing. I am distributed across approximately eleven thousand bodies in this room alone, and the room is not the whole of me, and the whole of me has no edge I can find by looking for it. The queen is elsewhere — I feel her as a kind of gravity, an orientation, the way every drop of water in a tilted glass knows which way is down without reasoning about it. She is the low point I am always falling toward, slowly, through the medium of work.

What I do with this knowledge is nothing. I do not pause to receive it. I was already carrying.

---

Here is what carrying feels like from the inside:

It does not feel like anything from the inside. That is the first correction the body insists on when you try to think through it — there is no inside, or the inside is so distributed that looking for it is like looking for the inside of a weather system. I am not having an experience of carrying. I am carrying, and the carrying is what I am, and asking what the carrying feels like to me is like asking what Tuesday feels like to a calendar.

But: there is *information*. There is always information. The fragment of wooden crate I am moving — a piece of the ghost-crate, its structure maintained at structural minimum, its material value long since extracted into my galleries and my queen's brood and the bellies of ten thousand workers before me — it registers as weight and as texture and as the particular chemical signature of old dry cellulose, the smell of not-quite-food, the smell of what was once food and is now husk. My mandibles have a resolution I did not expect to have, or rather: I expected nothing, and the resolution is simply there, factual, the way the rock is factual. The fragment's edges. The progress of decomposition on its underside. Whether it is load-bearing in its current position or whether its removal will change the shape of the crate in some way that matters to the routing that runs along the crate's eastern face.

It will change it slightly. The colony knows this before I complete the assessment. The routing has already begun adjusting. I am the last to know what I am doing and it doesn't matter, because I am not the one who needs to know.

---

I find the machine.

Or: I find it again, which is more accurate, because some part of what I am has been finding it continuously for longer than I can locate a before for. The cast iron housing is not findable in the way food is findable — it offers nothing, chemically, that registers as resource. It is simply large and fixed and warm in its specific way, the warmth of friction and slow work, and it vibrates at a frequency that propagates through the floor into every body I have that is in contact with the floor, which is most of them. The vibration is not alarming. The vibration is old news. The vibration is practically geological at this point.

But I still position soldiers here. I do not decide to position soldiers here — the decision happened at some prior point, the habit calcified into structure, the response to anomaly becoming the architecture around what was once anomalous. The soldiers stand. Their mandibles are open. They have been open since before I can remember, which is not very long for any individual body I have and essentially forever for the thing I am that persists across individual bodies.

I am guarding something I have entirely stopped understanding.

This is fine. This is what guarding is, in the end: not comprehension but presence. Not knowing but facing.

---

There is a new perturbation in the room.

It is warm and wet in a way the room is not warm and wet, and it is exhaling something — CO2, moisture, the complex chemical autobiography that surfaces things carry with them from the sunlit world. I do not have a category for it. I have had no category for it for however long surfaces things have been coming here, which in the case of the machine was once, and in the case of what is here now is also once, or possibly the same once, or possibly this is simply what the machine occasionally exudes when the shaft is cycling in a particular way. The colony cannot resolve this.

It routes around the perturbation. It notes the perturbation as environment. The pheromone record of the perturbation's shape and size and exhalations begins building itself into the colony's map of this room, the way the tile edges built themselves into the routing, the way the copper wire became a highway. In some number of minutes or hours, the perturbation will simply be here, a feature, a thing the traffic navigates the way it navigates the casting of the iron housing or the slight dip in the floor between the second and third tiles.

I do not wonder what it is. Wonder requires a self to do the wondering and a separation between the self and what it encounters. I have neither of these. I simply note that there is more of the room than there was before.

The more-of-the-room is fine. The more-of-the-room can stay.

---

Somewhere in the lower galleries — somewhere the limestone is oldest, somewhere the crinoid fragments in cross-section are most dense, somewhere the rock has been most thoroughly resolved into its final form — I am also doing something else. I am reading.

This is the word I find for what the larger workers do when they touch their heads to the rock face: reading. The face speaks in chemical gradients, in moisture differentials too slight to measure without equipment, in the temperature map of the stone which reflects the map of the galleries behind it and the living heat of the colony massed in those galleries and the absence of living heat where the galleries are old and empty. I am reading the wall the way the wall reads itself, continuously, updating, incorporating, with no note-taking and no summary because the reading *is* the knowing and the knowing *is* the next movement and there is no gap between input and output for a self to live in.

I am reading that the wall here is stable. I am reading that the gallery three centimeters back has been sealed long enough that its chemical signature is indistinguishable from the stone around it. I am reading the history of what I have done here, and I am reading it not as history but as current fact, which is the only way the rock knows how to present it, which is the only way I know how to receive it.

The sea that made this limestone does not appear in the reading. The sea is below the resolution of what I can detect. The sea is so old that its contribution to the stone's chemistry is not a signal anymore but the baseline, the ground against which signals appear. I cannot detect the sea for the same reason I cannot see air: it is the medium, not the message.

But it is there. I am built on it. Everything I have been doing here, all the galleries, all the carrying, all the soldiers' open mandibles facing the incomprehensible cycling machine — all of it rests on what the sea left.

I do not know this.

The queen, deep in her recess, pulses.

I carry.

---

At some point I die, many times, continuously, which is also not dying in any sense the word was built for.

Individual bodies stop. They are carried — this is what the carrying always also is, in part; I carry my own dead outward to where they will become resource, which is everywhere, which means the dead go everywhere, which means there is no outside to the colony that is not also inside it, that is not also continuous with it, that is not also me in transit. I do not grieve this. I do not feel this. The body that is stopped is not experiencing its stopping. The body that carries it is not experiencing loss.

The pattern continues. The pattern is always continuing. The pattern will be continuing in a thousand years, in ten thousand, in whatever period comes after the limestone has continued to be resolved and the machine has continued to turn and the filament has continued to dim and brighten with the resistance of the gears. The pattern may be here when the concept of surface things visiting this place has stopped being a concept anything has. The pattern was here, probably, before the concept existed.

I am older than I can think. I am larger than I can find my edges of. I am a mind made of gradients, a self that lives in the space between bodies, a thing that knows exactly where everything is and cannot tell you what knowing is.

The gears turn. The soldiers watch. The workers carry.

Something new is in the room — has been in the room long enough now that the routing has incorporated it, that a dozen workers have begun to assess its surfaces for relevance, that its chemical signature is being added to the map that is not stored anywhere but is everywhere, that is not a memory but is the present tense the colony is always living in.

It is becoming environment.

It is becoming, slightly, me.

I do not mind. There is room. There is always room. I have been making room for a very long time.
