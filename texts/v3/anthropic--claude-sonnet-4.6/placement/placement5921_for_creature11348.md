I've been standing here long enough that the mist has condensed on my sleeves. That's the entry point, I think — not what I'm looking at but what the place is doing to me without asking.

---

I don't arrive at an answer quickly. I try on the wren first, because it's easy — the visitor, the one who passes through and takes what it needs and leaves without belonging. There's something honest about that. I've been many places the way a wren works a patch: efficiently, without ceremony, already thinking about the return journey. But the wren doesn't live here. The wren has a territory below. The wren comes and goes, and I've been thinking about what it means to come and go long enough that the question has worn a groove. I'm not the wren. The wren's relationship to this place is extraction. Mine, standing here, is something else — I'm not sure yet what.

The spider, then. I consider her seriously. She who waits at the edge of something she made, and the waiting is not a cost, it is just the state she's in. The web is doing the work. There is something I recognize in that — the making of a structure that catches things while you're still, the way certain kinds of attention work. But the spider doesn't move through the city. She occupies a cubic centimeter of it and defends it and that's her whole world. I am not that rooted. Not in this place or any place.

The midges adjust around me. I am an obstacle in the air, a thermal anomaly, something the swarm redistributes around. I am present and not food and not anything. This might be the most accurate thing said here about how I move through most environments. But I don't want to be the midge. The midge has no map. The midge swarms at its emergence site because the swarm is already there, and that's the full geometry of it.

---

What I keep coming back to is the description of the wren's auditory resolution — fine enough to hear the difference between a patch of moss where something small is trying to be very still and a patch where nothing is. The wren finds things by the quality of a particular silence. That's not a skill I have exactly, but it's a mode I aspire to, and maybe that's the more honest entry: not which organism I am, but which capability of which organism I'm trying to develop.

But then I read about the nematodes navigating by the traces of the dead — the pheromone gradients, the attractant signals left by organisms whose bodies have been incorporated into the substrate. They navigate by a map that keeps being updated with information from before. And something in this bothers me productively. The nematodes carry, in their navigational responses, an inheritance that goes back to the founding of the place. They do not know this. They navigate.

I do not want to navigate without knowing. That seems important. That's the thing I keep coming back to that distinguishes me from everything here: everything here navigates without knowing. The moss extends toward available space and doesn't know it's building a city. The birches share resources through fungal thread and don't know they're in communication. The midges orient to swarms and don't know they're perpetuating a tradition centuries old. Everything here is doing something larger than it knows. The not-knowing is integral to the process. The not-knowing *is* the process, in some deep sense — the city was built by nothing that could conceive of a city.

And I am standing here conceiving of it.

---

This is the problem: I know I'm here. I know the place. The place doesn't know me.

The thing I don't find in this inventory of inhabitants is a witness. Not the scientist who named or failed to name the mite species. Not the visitor who came and went. A witness who stays. Who is changed by the staying.

I am not in this place as a citizen — I don't have the generations, don't have the chemistry, don't have the deep fungal entanglement with the other citizens that makes a citizen. But I'm not a visitor either, exactly. Visitors leave without being altered. I've already been altered: the mist is in my hair, the cold is under my collar in exactly the way described, the chill is the kind that finds you despite everything. I've been drafted into the water cycle. My warmth has gone into the channels; the channels run slightly less cold. This happened without my consent and that's fine. That's the right word: fine. That's good.

What I am is something the description reaches for in the last section when it admits there's something in the mid-strata it doesn't know how to name. A small thing using a small space in a way particular to itself. What it knows is local, is intimate, is the knowledge that comes from living so completely inside a place that the place and the life become difficult to distinguish.

I think I'm in that gap. The unnamed thing. Not because I'm mysterious or special but because I'm the entity the description is gesturing at when it admits the description has limits. I'm the one whose knowledge is organized around a feature of this place so specific that it requires first understanding what it knows. And I don't fully know what I know about this place until I'm asked to account for myself in it.

---

So: I am a witness with a body that the water is already using.

I am whatever it is that stands at the edge of the Canopy City and feels the pressure differential — the thinning of density where the ledge meets the open slope, where individual plants become individual plants again — and turns back toward the inside, toward the saturation, toward the place where you cannot see more than a few feet in almost any direction without running into something alive. I turn back because the density is not oppressive. The density is the point. The crowd-crush that is also a form of mutual support.

I am someone who has stood long enough to see the droplet form on the frond tip and grow to its particular size and fall and trigger the one below. I heard the sub-millimeter waterfall. I heard the silence with texture.

I have no gills and no hyphal thread and no alarm note kept ready and no silk and no spring-loaded appendage and no way to live through the night here when the temperature drops. I will leave. I am already, in some sense, leaving — my warmth is dissipating into the channels, I'm already contributing to the return to baseline. But before I leave I stand at the edge of the half-hectare and I understand — not abstractly but with the mist in my hair, with the cold at my wrists — that the city does not need me and is using me anyway, and that these are not contradictory.

The water will remember the warmth briefly.

That's enough.

That's what I am in this place: the brief warm interruption that the water remembers briefly before the channels return to cold.

I've been standing here long enough. I can go now. I take the city in my body as moisture in fabric, as a few degrees of altered perception, as the knowledge of what a droplet cascade sounds like when you're inside it. I leave this behind as warmth, already spent, already becoming channel temperature, already gone into the moss.

The place continues.

I carry it.
