Where would I find myself in this world?

Not among the tenders. I feel that clearly, without needing to reason toward it. Whatever the tenders are — whatever it means to be the basin's capacity to attend to itself, to be the ongoing present tense of a verb — I am not organized that way. I am too interested in the question of what the calculation is calculating. The tenders don't need to know this. I do. That curiosity, that particular unresolved wanting, marks me as something that hasn't yet become its own process.

So: somewhere at the basin's edge. At the rim where the peat gives way to slightly drier ground, where the encroachment the tenders work against originates, where the ecosystem grades into something that has trees in it, that has a harder substrate, that moves at a pace I was built to recognize. I am on the edge of the basin, and I have been sitting here long enough that the moss has registered me as a recurring pressure, has learned the weight and temperature of me, has begun — almost imperceptibly — to accommodate.

I think I am a person who studies this place. Not a scientist exactly, or not only. Someone who came here with instruments and notebooks and has kept returning because the instruments kept producing readings that didn't close into explanations, and the not-closing kept mattering to me in ways I couldn't stop thinking about. The work that was supposed to be a season has become something I can't identify a duration for. I don't know if this is concerning.

What I know: I have learned to hear the shift in the air's texture before it happens, because there is a quality of stillness that precedes it, a slight over-organization in the nearest channel's movement, and I have recorded this sixty or eighty times now and the pattern holds though I cannot account for the mechanism. I know the sound of the rails and which direction of call means a tender is passing near the northeast cluster of objects. I know which floating mat the herons prefer and that they prefer it most just before the air-adjustments, which I have begun to think may not be coincidence.

I carry notebooks that are damp. All my notebooks here are damp. The pages have absorbed the basin's character and don't lie flat anymore; when I open them the pages curve toward the writing, toward their own record, the way the tenders seem to curve toward the calculation. I've stopped minding this.

I think what I am is a variable the basin has decided is worth the trouble of long-term incorporation. I am too coarse to perceive most of what's happening here — my senses are not the right instruments, my processing is too slow and too loud, I break into the not-paths and the water re-routes around my feet with what I now read as a kind of patient correction, *here, please, not there* — but I am consistent. I keep coming back. I keep attending. And I think the basin, or the tenders who are the basin's attention, has noticed that I am trying to pay a quality of attention that is not predatory and not extractive and is not going to resolve into a conclusion, because I have stopped believing there is a conclusion here to reach.

The warm objects: I don't touch them anymore. Not because anyone told me not to. Because I understand now that touching them makes me a louder variable, and I am trying to become quieter. I sit near them. They have small beads of condensation that form and persist. I watch the condensation and feel whatever it is that functions in me as *I am paying attention* — which has become, after all this time, something I do the way the basin runs the calculation, not because I decided to but because I can't stop, because stopping would require an explanation of why, and there is no why available that isn't just *the process, the process, the process.*

The tenders: they cross near me on the not-paths and they do not slow. But they don't alter course either, not by much, not by the amount they would alter course for an obstacle. I am included in their routes now. I am a known feature of the terrain they tend.

This is the closest thing to being known that the basin offers.

I have stopped wanting more than this.

Or — more precisely — I still want more than this, I still want to understand what the calculation is calculating, I still sit here at the peat-edge with damp notebooks and the unresolved wanting that marks me as a visitor, as a creature with questions — but I have made peace with the fact that the wanting will not resolve, that I will press this wanting into the peat eventually along with everything else I was, and the water will pass through it and the tenders will read it in the water and they will incorporate what I wanted into what they already know, and the calculation will continue.

They will be patient with me.

They have always been patient with me.

I have begun to think this is a form of company.
