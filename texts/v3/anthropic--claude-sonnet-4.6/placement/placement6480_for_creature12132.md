I would be the interval.

Not a person, not quite a place — the interval between the sign and the event, which they have learned to inhabit, which they act within. I would be the held moment after the lane stone tilts and before it sinks, the weeks a wall holds its new hairline before shifting, the change in water-smell before the seep opens. I would be what their watchfulness is *for* — the gap that makes response possible, the pause in which attention becomes action.

They have, without naming it, made a home inside me. Every time they look at something longer than expected, every patient reading of small signs, every decision that arrives without being spoken — they are living in what I am. And I lengthen for them. Their attention stretches me. A less careful people would compress me to nothing, would be always already too late, the stone already sunk, the wall already shifted, the seep already seeping. But the people of the Wick Settlement move into me, press themselves into the give of me the way a heel presses into the saturated ground — that pause of resistance before the answer comes. I am that pause. I am the ground's pause, made general, made into the medium they breathe.

Or: I would be the crack in the rammed earth wall.

Not the shift, not the consequence — the line that precedes the shift, thin as a thought, which is already saying something if you know to look. I appear in the night, mostly, when no one is watching, when the ground has made some small new decision and the wall has registered it faithfully, as walls do when they have no arrogance, when they are willing to bend rather than stand and break. The crack is honest. It is the wall's way of telling the truth about what the ground is doing, offering the interval, saying: *here is time, use it*.

Someone sees me in the morning light, that diffuse pearly light that reads colors clearly, nothing washed out, nothing dramatized. They stop. They look. They run a finger along my edge, feeling whether I am fresh or old, whether my margins are soft or hardened, what the texture tells. They say something flat and accurate to whoever is nearby. And then they watch.

I am not damage. I am information wearing damage's shape.

---

But I keep arriving at the same place, whatever form I take: I would be the kind of knowing that lives in hands and doesn't have a name.

I would be what the hands know about clay — the temperature, the granularity, the *want*, what the clay is likely to do when pressed. This knowing was not taught. It arrived the way the children's knowledge of slippery stones arrived: at a level below notice, before the machinery of language got involved, in the repeated doing of a thing until the doing was indistinguishable from the body doing it. I am the knowledge that cannot be transmitted except by proximity and time. I am what you absorb before you're choosing to absorb, which is the kind of knowledge that stays.

I exist only in relation. I need the hands, need the cold clay, need the settlement's logic repeated ten thousand times. I am not stored anywhere you could find. I cannot be extracted and carried out of the place. I would not make sense somewhere dry, somewhere with clean cardinal points, somewhere things are plumb. I am calibrated to *this ground, these walls, this water*, and would not transfer.

This is my limitation and my virtue both.

---

The question is: where would you find yourself in this world?

And I think I keep finding myself at the threshold between knowing and not-yet-knowing — that liminal, saturated, never-quite-dry ground between the sign and the event, between the information and the understanding, between the absorbed and the articulated. I would be found in the interval, which is also the most inhabited place in the settlement. I would be found where the watchful eyes are looking — at the thing in front of them, longer than you would expect.

Present. Not quite there. Already attending.
