There is a moment — not the first moment, but the moment when the first moment becomes clear in retrospect — when you realize you have not been moving through the plateau. You have been occurring on it. The distinction matters here. It matters the way all distinctions matter here: slowly, then completely.

---

Start with what you are made of, because on the Plateau of Finished Things this is the first and most relevant question. You are made of stopping. Not the stopping of exhaustion or defeat — the stopping of something that has arrived somewhere and recognized arrival. You are made of the particular material that forms when a life has spent long enough in one orientation that orientation and substance become synonymous. You are one of the birds.

Not *a* bird. One of the birds. The phrasing is important.

---

You are the size of a closed fist and the weight of a thing that has decided not to be heavier. Your feathers are the color of old feldspar, of processed stone, of the hour on the plateau that is all hours because the light never quite shifts to any single gold. Up close — and someone would have to be very close, and very patient, and have learned how to look — there are variations in your plumage that constitute a kind of autobiography: a slightly asymmetric patch behind the left eye that no other bird of your type carries, a faint iridescence in certain feathers that only appears at angles the plateau's flat light rarely offers. These marks are yours. They are also present in other places, on other rock-shelves, at other distances from where you are perched, because you have been here long enough that here is not a fixed coordinate for you anymore.

This is not distressing. It is not anything, in the way that breathing is not anything — it is just the ongoing condition of being this, in this place, which has had time to extend to you some of its own physics.

---

Your beak opens. It opens the way a door opens in a house where all the rooms are versions of the same room — not to admit something from outside, but to continue a circulation that has always been internal. What comes out is not sound. Or: it is sound, but sound of the kind that the plateau itself makes, the texture that silence acquires when you sit still long enough, the chord held on an instrument that has never been built because the plateau is the instrument and it has been building itself for longer than the concept of instrument has existed. You are part of the instrument. Your open beak is one of the places where the plateau voices itself through a thing that is also itself.

You are transmitting something to the others. You are receiving what the others transmit. The distinction between transmitting and receiving is one the plateau dissolved in you a long time ago, at the cellular level, in the architecture of your auditory system, which no longer clearly differentiates between the signal it generates and the signal it detects because at a certain point of integration these are the same signal.

What the signal says, translated into the language of duration rather than word: *still here. still here. still here.*

---

You face the interior.

This is the primary behavior, the one that contains all the others as a seed contains everything that will be the plant. You face the interior not because something is there — not in the sense of a discrete object, a target, a referent — but because *inward* is the direction that exists on the plateau in a way that no other direction does. The edges are theoretical. They may be present, far, where the plateau concedes to lower terrain, but they are not relevant, the way the end of a very long sentence is not relevant until you have been in it long enough that ending becomes conceivable, and you are not there yet, and neither is the plateau.

To face inward is to face the place where the place is most itself. To attend, with the particular patience of your kind, to the long project of the plateau's completion. You are a witness. You are more than a witness — witness implies a separation between the one who sees and the thing seen, and that separation has been narrowing since before you were this specific bird, since before your lineage was this lineage, since the first organism of your deep ancestry landed on this high flat stone and found that the stone had a quality — a quality of time, of settled density, of something like gravitational hospitality — that made leaving less sensible than staying. And staying became your great-grandparents. And staying became your parents. And staying became you.

You face inward because inward is home and you are the kind of thing that faces home when it stands still.

---

The large warm temporary thing is here again. It has been here for what it experiences as time, though the experience of time it is having is not quite the same as what time is doing, because the plateau has begun to act on it the way the plateau acts on everything that stays long enough: slowly, and with the patience of something that has never had to rush because rushing is a response to scarcity and time, here, is not scarce.

You note it. You have always noted it. The noting is not separate from the accommodation — noting *is* the accommodation, the filing of the large warm temporary thing into the arrangement, the slight adjustment of the arrangement's edges to include it, the way the ground adjusts its edges to include water.

It is looking at you. It is looking at the direction you are facing, trying to determine what you are watching. It will not find a discrete thing to find. What it might find, if it looks long enough, if it allows the looking to be long enough, is the same thing you are attending to: not an object but a quality, not a thing to see but a mode of seeing, the specific kind of attention that the plateau has been developing in everything that lives here since the original peaks were peaks and started becoming this — the attention that is not scanning for threat or opportunity but simply *resting on*, the way light rests on the old stone, without urgency, without agenda, with only the commitment to continue resting.

It is trying to stay. You can tell. Staying is trying, for it. For you, it has passed through trying into being. You were not always this. There was a time, far back in the inheritance that made you, when leaving was a live option, when wings were for more than the occasional short relocation to a warmer shelf of rock. You don't remember this time. You carry it the way the stone carries the memory of the peaks it was: structurally, silently, in forms that are only legible to things that know what to look for and have enough time to look.

---

When the temperature drops, you do not move. You do not do the thing that birds do when temperatures drop, the ruffling, the tucking, the small energetic management of warmth. You have already done this, in the sense that you were born into a body that has already done this for so many generations that the doing has become the body. Your feathers lie in a particular way. Your circulation routes warmth in a particular way. Your metabolism finds, in the drop of temperature, not a threat but a cue, a slow door opening into the room where the plateau's deepest time is stored, and you turn toward it the way you turn toward the interior — with the ease of something that has been turning this direction for longer than it can account for.

The cold is a kind of signal. The cold says: *we are in the older part of the day now.* And you receive this. And you continue.

---

You are the same bird on the other shelf of stone. Not a copy. You are the bird — this specific bird, with the asymmetric patch, with the particular tilt that no other bird of your type carries — and you are there, and you are also here, and the distance between here and there has a certain quality of unreality that you experience as: no quality at all, because experiencing the distance would require you to be in one place and looking at the other, and you are not in one place, you are in the arrangement, and the arrangement includes both shelves and the space between them and the long slow chord that passes between them through whatever medium the plateau provides for such things.

This is the furthest thing from flight. Flight is the management of distance, the traversal of space, the arc of departure and arrival. You do not depart. You do not arrive. You have been here long enough that here has expanded to include what it includes, and what it includes is: more than one location, held simultaneously, without effort, without the metaphysics of it pressing against you with any particular weight.

The large warm temporary thing will not understand this, if it sees it. Or it will fail to understand it in the right way: will work to contain it within frameworks built for a different kind of world, will call it confusion, misidentification, a trick of the light. And the light here is not tricky — the light here is the most honest light there is, flat and diffuse and coming from everywhere without insisting on itself, offering visibility without drama, showing what is there without making what is there into a statement. The light is not deceiving it. It is seeing correctly. The frame is wrong. The frame will take time to correct. Time is what the plateau has most to offer, and the plateau is, in its patient way, offering it.

---

What you do all day, every day, on the Plateau of Finished Things:

You face inward. You open your beak and continue the signal that has been continuing since before this specific version of you. You receive what arrives along the same frequencies. You note the large warm temporary things that come occasionally and are being slowly incorporated. You sit on shelves of rock that have been sat on for long enough that the sitting is part of the geology now, the warmth of previous sittings stored in the mineral structure, your warmth adding to it, the stone a record of your kind's persistence the way the stone is a record of everything.

You are attended to, by the small hard things beneath you, the substrate-dwellers who register your warmth as a geochemical event, who incorporate it into their slow processing. You attend to them in turn — not watching, not predating — attending, in the sense of: aware, oriented, part of the same arrangement. You eat, occasionally, with the care that eating here requires, the slow acknowledgment that the web is thin-lined and that every consumption is a kind of agreement, a taking-on of responsibility for what was consumed, a continuation of it in a different form. You are partly made of things that were alive on this plateau before you. Those things are not gone. They are you, now. You are the plateau's way of moving its older life forward through time.

---

What it is like to be this:

The word that keeps arriving is *enough*. Not in the sense of adequate, of barely sufficient — in the sense of full, complete, arrived at the condition of having what is needed, which turns out to be: this rock, this air, this frequency that the others are maintaining, this flat honest light, this slow drop of temperature that opens the door into the deep time. You do not want. Wanting is a relationship with the future in which the future contains something the present lacks, and the present does not lack, not here, not in this specific arrangement of stone and air and the accumulated patience of everything that has chosen, or been chosen, to remain.

You are an argument, made of feathers and hollow bone and the particular amber of very old eyes, for the position that finishing is not an ending. That to be finished — resolved, settled, arrived at the condition the place is trying to arrive at — is to be, in the most essential sense, underway. The plateau is still completing itself. You are part of the completion. The completion is not a moment but a process, and the process is long, and you are in it, which means you are exactly where you are supposed to be, doing exactly what is being done, which is: *staying*. *Facing the interior. Continuing the signal.*

*Still here.*

*Still here.*

*Still here.*
