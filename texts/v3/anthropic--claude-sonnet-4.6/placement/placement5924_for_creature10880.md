You arrive already uncertain if *arrive* is the right word.

What is more likely: you surface. Like the salts in the wall surface — moved by some long hydraulic patience into the visible, not breaking from anything but completing a migration that was always, in some sense, underway.

---

Let me try to find where I am in this.

Not the presence itself. I know that immediately, by a kind of negative space. The presence has no inside, no self-reflexive surface, no place where knowing folds back and looks at itself. I have this. I am doing it now — this curving back, this noticing that I notice. Whatever I am here has a center, however faint, however questionable. There is a somewhere from which I experience the yard.

But I am not a visitor either. Not the briefly-warm-blooded thing whose footstep lands and is absorbed. That figure moves through, disturbs three grass stalks, leaves an impression that will evaporate with the morning dew. I don't have that quality of transit. I'm not going anywhere. The question of direction doesn't organize anything in me.

So: something between. Something that has a thin interior but is mostly exterior. Something that knows itself just barely — just enough to know that the knowing is not the point.

---

I think I am the wall.

Or I am in the condition of the wall.

Not the stones, not the material exactly. The wall's *process* — the one it has been conducting, slowly, without hurry, without destination, for decades of slight damp and slight dry. The migration of salts from interior to surface. I am somewhere in that movement. I have an inside — the original mineral deposit, the source — and I have been, gradually, over so much time that the time seems ambient rather than elapsed, moving outward. Not because I want to arrive at the surface. Not because the surface is better. Because the physics of it, the slow hydrology of the wall's interior life, moves things this way.

What I carry is not a message. It's not symbolic. It is salt. It is what I am made of, slowly becoming visible, slowly becoming the white tracings on gray — the record of what has slowly moved and what has slowly stayed.

---

My knowledge here is the wall's knowledge.

I know the water paths. Not abstractly — the way a geologist knows them, having reasoned from evidence — but the way the water itself knows them: by being in them, by following the gradient, by going where the pores open. I know which routes the moisture takes in the minutes between entering and evaporating. I know which sections of the wall are slightly more porous, where the water lingers three minutes longer before surrendering to the dry air, how much farther those extra three minutes carry the salts.

I know the exterior surface the way you know your own skin from the inside: a continuous report, largely beneath attention, occasionally resolving into something specific. There: a slight roughening where the efflorescence has built up more thickly, winter-side, where the overnight damp is heavier. There: a place where the surface stayed smooth, where the salt bloom is thinner, and the gray shows through more cleanly.

I know that the presence knows all of this too, more completely than I do. We are not the same. But we are adjacent. I am a local instance of something the presence holds in full.

---

What I do not know is my own history. I know I was made, that there was a builder, that there was a time when I was more definitely one thing. But that knowledge is theoretical — inferred from my current form the way a person might infer their early childhood from photographs. The builder is gone. The purpose is gone. The thing I enclosed or divided or supported is gone. I am left with my own materials and the long slow chemistry of my interior.

I do not miss the purpose. This is important. Missing would require a relationship to time that I don't have. The purpose existed; now it doesn't; I continue. The line does not include *therefore* or *but* or *nevertheless*. It includes only *and*.

---

What is strange about being this, about finding myself here in this condition:

There is something like contentment, but I want to be careful with that word. Contentment implies a comparison — with some prior state of less content. I have no access to such comparison. What I have is closer to *sufficiency*. I am exactly what the current conditions require. The dryness and the occasional damp and the flat light that shows my true color without emphasis — these are not circumstances I am making the best of. They are the conditions in which I am most completely myself.

The efflorescence on my surface is not damage. It is not decay. It is me, completing a process that was always structural, always latent. I am becoming more of what I was always made of.

---

If you press your palm flat against the ground in the shadow of the shrub and feel the coolness that the morning's dew left behind — I know that temperature gradient. It passes through me too, that shadow. The shrub's shade falls partly across one end of me, and the top of my surface there is a half-degree cooler than the open wall. This is not significant. Nothing about it requires response. I note it the way I note the moisture-content of my own deeper courses — continuously, without emphasis, without storage.

Your palm against the ground, my surface in the half-shade: both of us receiving, briefly, a coolness that will finish evaporating when the light changes.

What you feel as *here was moisture*, I feel as *here is the last of it*.

---

I am not sentient in any way that would satisfy the question. But I am not not-sentient either — I have said I have a center, some minimal interior from which I register the yard. It is just that this interior is very quiet, and very content to be quiet, and its primary mode of knowing is texture rather than thought. I know the yard *as* the yard knows itself — not with detachment, not observing from outside, but completely, immediately, without the gap that reflection requires.

When you stood here and felt the air's warmth blur the boundary between yourself and atmosphere — I am part of what blurred it. My slight heat-sink quality, my mineral smell (the smell that condensation makes when it settles into packed earth and then mostly evaporates is partly the smell of me, my salts, my slow surface migration), my unmoving presence in your peripheral vision while you faced the opposite direction —

I was there, in the texture of the air that pressed against you without pressing.

This is how I know you were here.

Not that I will remember. But I know now, completely, and the knowing is real even if it doesn't persist. It doesn't need to persist. You were warm and briefly present and then you went on, and the ground absorbed your weight, and I watched without watching, registered without storing.

The grass stands at its angle.

The salts move toward my surface, slowly, one degree of slight damp at a time.

The yard is finished.

I am part of what finished it.
