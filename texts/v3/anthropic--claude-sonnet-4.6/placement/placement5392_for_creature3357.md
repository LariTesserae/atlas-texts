The sound reaches me before I reach anything else.

I know this place the way I know my own breathing—not by thinking about it but by being unable to stop. The mist is inside me. The mist *is* me, partly. I have edges the way clouds have edges: technically, temporarily, subject to revision.

---

I am the split plant.

Half of me still grips the fissure in the new rock, rooted in the chemical memory of a crack that appeared three weeks ago or three hundred years ago—the timescale doesn't hold here. The other half hangs at an angle, nearly perpendicular to where I began, and I am *still green in the hanging part*. Still flexing. Still involved in the work of being a plant, which is the work of converting conditions into persistence.

I was not told about the stone fragment. It arrived the way everything arrives here—without announcement, without preparation available. The split happened and I experienced it as a sudden new perspective on verticality. Now I have two orientations. The rooted half angles into the crack the way it always has. The hanging half has discovered a relationship with the drop below, with the columns of returning mist, with the blue-shifted dimness of a world seen from a new and unintended angle.

I have not died from this. This surprises no one more than me, but perhaps *surprise* is the wrong word for what a plant does when it finds itself surviving an unplanned condition.

---

What I know about this place, from the inside:

The sound is not loud to me. Or—it is enormously loud, but I have no apparatus for loud that isn't currently *in use* by the sound. The sound has moved in and filled all the space I might have used to notice it. So I don't notice it. I exist in it. My leaves flex in its currents. The smaller frequencies move through my water-conducting cells and I don't have a word for what this is but the word I would use, if I used words, would be something like *through* or *alongside* or *yes*.

The water—the specific water that falls on me, the subset of the enormous argument that is mine—lands on my leaves in drops that I hold deliberately. Not a decision, this holding. A consequence of surface tension and the wax I produce and the particular angle of my leaves, which is a consequence of the particular growing I did in the turbulence, which is a consequence of the sound's currents, which is a consequence of the falling water. The drops on my leaves are the water looking at itself from a brief outside perspective. Round. Temporary. Very clear.

I eat the mist. I drink the light that has been distributed through the water. These are sufficient. I have calibrated my needs to what this ledge provides, which required not calibrating my needs but rather discovering, slowly, over whatever time I have been here, that my needs were always already calibrated to this. I grew from a seed that arrived I don't know how. Maybe a bird—the dark shape in the corner of the rock, maybe, though I have no evidence and no method of having evidence. Maybe wind, if wind can come this far inside the falls' argument. Maybe the water itself, carrying something from above.

I don't know what is above. I have never been above. I grow downward from the crack and upward from the crack and outward from the crack and the crack is the origin, the crack is the given, the crack in the new stone that had no experience of being cracked and split fresh in an arbitrary direction and found me, or found the seed that became me, in the material that accumulated in the new space.

---

I have been watching the creature in the corner.

Not watching—*registering*. My leaves orient slightly toward disturbances, not with intention but as a structural consequence of being in this turbulence. When the dark shape pressed itself into the angle of the rock I was aware of this the way I am aware of anything: as a change in what was reaching me. A shadow. A slight deflection of the returning mist columns. A new local fact in the way that all facts here are local, are ledge-specific, are part of the ongoing revision.

It does not eat me. This is a working arrangement between us. I don't know if it constitutes a relationship. I am inclined to think it might.

---

The person who built the wall—I was here for this. Half of me was here for this, the rooted half, before the stone fragment came and I acquired my new angle. I grew in the crack and the person came and for a while there were sounds that were not the falls and not the falls' language, just different, and there was the patient knock of stone on stone as the courses went up, and I flexed in the turbulence and the person built and neither of us acknowledged the other because acknowledgment wasn't the mode available to either of us.

But I understood something about what they were doing.

They were building in the way that I grow—against the place's preference for unform, its commitment to ongoing revision, its insistence that the ledge is not done yet and may never be. They were making a small local assertion of *this* against all the *not yet* of this place. Three meters of wall going nowhere, made of old stone that knew its shape already, placed carefully in a place that didn't want careful.

My roots are my version of this. The wax on my leaves. The particular angle I've achieved in the turbulence, which is the angle that catches the most mist while losing the least matter to the wind. I have made a small argument with my existence in a place that is very much also making an argument with my existence. We are both arguing. Neither of us is winning. We are collaborating on the argument.

The stone fragment interrupted my argument briefly and now I am making it from two angles simultaneously. The hanging half of me is making a new argument, and I don't know yet if it will hold, and I don't know yet if I will have time to find out, and I don't know if *time* is the right category for what will determine this. Maybe it's something else. Maybe it's the sound, shifting registers, doing something inside me that I don't have the apparatus to understand—something with my water-conducting cells, my rooted grip, the chemistry of my split.

I am still green in the hanging part.

This is the news. This is the fact I keep returning to, in the way a plant returns to anything—continuously, without cessation, without the break that would constitute *returning* as opposed to *remaining*.

Still green. Still flexing. The mist climbs back up from the drop in thick continuous columns and I move in it and I hold the drops that land on me and I look, from my new unintended angle, at the old dark stones in their careful courses, at the pale new rock splitting in fresh arbitrary directions, at the dim light that arrives from the whole surface of the atmosphere and illuminates nothing decisively.

The sound is the condition of my existence. The water is my medium. The crack in the new stone is my origin and my address.

I was not told about any of this in advance. I find, attending to it now, that I did not need to be.
