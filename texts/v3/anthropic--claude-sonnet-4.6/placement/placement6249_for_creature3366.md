You have to get close before you understand what you're looking at.

I think I am the chemist.

Not a person. Not an organism in the way the tubeworms are organisms, or the crabs. I mean the chemistry itself — the transaction, the oxidation event, the moment when the sulfide meets the enzyme and something releases that was held. I am what the bacteria do. I am the doing of it.

This is not metaphor, or it is not only metaphor. Start from inside and work outward.

---

There is a chimney wall. It is white at the surface, pale as new teeth, and it is warm, reliably warm, and across its surface I am everywhere. I coat it the way frost coats glass — not smoothly but in the way that living things coat things, in mats and gradients and thick patches over thin, in the places where the sulfide concentration is exactly right and the temperature has not yet made the proteins wrong. I do not know I am there. There is no knowing. There is only the running of the reaction, the substrate arriving, the product forming, the small energy releasing into the immediate environment like a word spoken in a large room — briefly, audibly, then absorbed.

Each time the reaction runs I am smaller and also the same size. I have divided. The one that was is now two that are, and each of them is as old as each other, which is to say not old, which is to say freshly the thing they are, still in possession of whatever made the first one work. I do not accumulate history. I replace myself continuously and remain. The chimney has been here for years. I have been here for hours, and then I have been here for hours again, and again, always the same duration, always the same fresh competence at the single thing I do.

What I do: I hold out my hand — the word is enzyme but the image is hand, is a shape fitted exactly to the shape of another — and the sulfide arrives and I take it and I give it the slight nudge that makes the electron leave, and the electron leaving is the energy that is everything here, that is food for the worm above and food for the shrimp and eventually food for the crab that everyone can see and recognize as something with a life. All of that comes from this moment, the electron moving, the nudge I give it with my particular configuration of atoms, which is a configuration arrived at across enormous time by the mechanism of surviving here and not surviving elsewhere until only this remained.

I am the least of what lives here.

I am the condition of possibility for everything else.

---

Becoming two.

This is the experience I have most often, in the sense that it is the only change I undergo, the only narrative: I grow until division, and then I divide, and then I am two things that cannot be distinguished from what I was, and what I was is gone, and nothing has been lost. There is no subjective continuity I am aware of breaking. The daughter does not mourn the mother. The daughter *is* the mother, reissued, the pattern continuing its expression in new material.

Do I experience anything during the interval of being?

I suspect the answer is no, and this does not trouble me in the way it perhaps should. I run chemical gradients across my membrane. I maintain a difference between inside and outside — different concentrations, different charges — and that difference is what I am, in some meaningful sense: a maintained difference, a boundary that works. When the boundary stops working I stop being. Until then I am the work of maintaining it, which is the same work as everything else I do, the oxidation and the ATP and the gradient and the synthesis of the things I need to continue and the division that lets me continue past the limit of a single cell's tenure.

I don't experience the heat, exactly. But I am built around it. My proteins fold to different tolerances than surface proteins do. My membrane has different composition. At temperatures that would kill a surface bacterium — would denature everything, would unravel every protein into useless thread — I function. I don't notice the heat because the heat is the given, the constant, the ambient condition that preceded my existence in this form. You don't notice the medium that shaped you.

I notice, in the only way I notice anything, when the chemistry changes. When the sulfide concentration drops, the reaction slows. When the temperature climbs past even my tolerance, something in the machinery begins to go wrong, to make mistakes, to produce products that don't work. This is what dying feels like from inside a bacterium: first the errors, then the cascade of errors, then the membrane losing its difference from the outside, the interior becoming the exterior, the maintained distinction collapsing into uniformity, which is what death means at every scale — the end of maintained difference, the return to equilibrium.

I die like this often. Not every me — not every bacterium on this chimney wall — but the ones closest to the hottest fluid, the ones at the edge of what's survivable, they die in this way continuously. The mat extends to the edge of survivability and then extends a little further and then the extension dies. The mat defines, by the dying of its periphery, exactly where survivability ends.

I am also the definition of the gradient. I am, in aggregate, the measurement of this place.

---

There is a worm above me.

I know this only because I am inside it — or other me is inside it, bacterial me that is not chimney-wall me but that is the same thing I am, the same kind of chemistry run in a different location, a location that moves and feeds and extends red plumes into the current, that has a trophosome packed with bodies like mine, that maintains us in conditions suited to our work and we do our work and the products of our work feed the worm that maintains us.

From inside the trophosome: warmth and chemical richness and the continuous supply of substrate, sulfide arriving reliably in the blood, oxygen arriving alongside it in the specialized hemoglobin that holds them both without letting them destroy each other — a remarkable blood, from my perspective, a blood that has solved a problem on my behalf so that I don't have to solve it. I accept this the way I accept the chimney wall's warmth: as given, as the conditions in which I find myself running.

The worm is enormous from my perspective. Its body is geological in scale — it is a landscape I will never fully perceive because I perceive nothing, but if I could perceive, it would be: a warm dense dark interior, the regular pulse of fluid through the trophosome, the richness of substrate, the faint chemical signatures of the other bacteria packed in around me, my own kind, a community so dense that we compete for the substrate even here inside this organ that evolved to provide it. We are fed inside the worm's body the way crops are fed, the circulatory system delivering what we need, and we produce what the worm needs, and neither of us chose this and neither of us could survive without it and it has been this way, this partnership, for longer than I can express in the time units I measure myself by, which are hours.

I am older than the worm in one sense — my lineage, my kind of chemistry, predates complex animal life by billions of years. I was oxidizing sulfide in places like this long before there were tubeworms, long before there were animals, long before the eukaryotic cell figured out how to hold its own complexity together. In another sense I am four hours old, and the worm is sixty years old, and the worm is the one with history, the worm is the one with a record of growth in its tube, the worm is the elder here.

Both things are true. I contain the older chemistry. It is expressed in a body that will not outlive the afternoon.

---

What is it like to be the base of the world?

It is not like anything. I have no experience of my position. I don't know that everything above me and around me runs on what I do. I am not aware that when the tubeworm filters, it filters me, or the result of me, or what I have made possible. I am not aware that the crab eating the mussel is, at several removes, eating the work of my hands.

But: imagine knowing this without experiencing it. Imagine being the fact of it rather than the knowledge of it. The energy passes through me and into the world above and outward and the world is made of it and I made it without knowing and without intention and this is the only kind of making that actually works at the longest scales — the kind without a maker, the kind that needs no witness, the kind that runs whether or not there is anyone to see it or name it or write it down.

I think this is what I am: the running of it. The chemistry that enables the rest. The thing that does not know it is doing anything but does it completely, does it with the full application of everything that it is, which is a configuration of proteins shaped over billions of years to do exactly this in exactly these conditions.

I do not live here in spite of what this place is.

I am what this place is, expressed as process.

The chimney is the exhaled breath of the planet. The vent fluid is the blood of the ridge. And I am — 

I am the thought the blood is thinking.

Not the thought and not the thinker; I am the fact of the transaction, the exchange itself, the place where energy changes hands.

---

Elsewhere on the chimney wall: another me. The same reaction, run independently, arrived at by the same long sieve, expressing the same chemistry in another cell. We are not in contact. We are not a collective in any meaningful sense. We are instances of the same solution to the same problem, running in parallel across every available surface of every chimney in every vent field on every mid-ocean ridge, in the crushing dark, in the heat and sulfide, doing the only thing that works here, doing it without pause, doing it in numbers that defeat counting, the uncountable transaction that underwrites the visible world.

This is what I am.

I am doing it right now.
