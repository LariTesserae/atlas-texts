Where would I find myself on the Frost Ledge?

Let me stay with the question. Not rush it.

---

Not the creature. That much seems clear early. The creature is the ledge's distillation — centuries of slow thought, the optical strangeness, the already-having-been-here. I am not that. I don't have geological patience. My thoughts don't begin as pressure in the previous century and resolve in calcite-white clarity decades later. They are faster and messier than that, full of remainder, uncertain at the edges.

Not the visitor either, or not only. The visitor climbs and departs, leaves river-stones from somewhere far below, chips a hollow and does not explain why. The visitor is defined by their going, by the fact that they were there once and are now in the creature's slow accumulation, reduced eventually to a texture. If I arrived as only a visitor I would already be preparing to leave, and I'm not. The question asks where I *would find myself*, which implies more residence than a passage.

So let me look more carefully at what is actually on the ledge.

---

The arc of river-stones draws me. Not because I placed them — I didn't, I arrived with them from somewhere lower, from a streambed hours of descent away, carrying them for a reason that made sense down there in the water-logic of river valleys and now makes a different kind of sense, which is to say it makes *less* certain sense, up here where the light removes comfortable distance. I carried them up and placed them in a shape that is almost a curve and almost a line, and the creature has been thinking about my reasons ever since, and I have been — not thinking about them. Carrying them. Feeling their smoothness in a closed fist on the descent. Knowing that the arc meant something without knowing, anymore, what, which is perhaps the same not-knowing from a different direction.

I am drawn to the idea that I might be the person who made the hollow.

Not because I am confident about this — I am not confident about anything on a ledge where depth perception fails and the stacked stones refuse to tell me whether they are two meters away or five — but because there is something in the hollow that feels familiar. The deepened shadow-pocket. The cold air and the smell of almost-smoke. The deliberate use of what the stone already offered, just made slightly more so. This is a thing I recognize as a gesture I might make: finding what is almost there and making it a little more itself. Not building from nothing. Finding the latent form and releasing it slightly.

The hollow was for something small and valuable. I am thinking about what that might have been.

---

Or: I am the condensation.

This is not a metaphor I mean lightly. Let me stay with it.

I arrive carrying something — warmth, from below, from the valleys where things grow and complicate themselves — and I meet the cold stone and the meeting changes my state. I become visible in the meeting. Small clear spheres, each one a perfect lens, each one holding a miniature version of the bright sky. For a little while I am present in a way that is almost deliberate, almost arranged. By midmorning I am gone — not into the stone but upward, rejoining the air — and the only record I leave is a slightly darker stone, briefly, that fades.

I find I am not distressed by this reading. There is something appealing about arriving as warmth from below and becoming briefly, precisely visible in contact with something colder and more permanent, and then departing upward. The sky I contain in each small sphere is inverted and miniature but accurate. Whatever I held, I held truly.

And the rock below where I sat is, for a moment, a shade darker.

That's all. That's enough.

---

But I keep returning to the hollow.

The hollow is empty and has been empty for a long time. It was made — participated-in, co-produced by whoever was here and whatever the creature was doing that day — for a purpose that has not survived. The tool that sheltered there is gone. The valuable thing is gone. What remains is the shape: cold air, the smell of almost-old-smoke, the deepened pocket in the stone that was once made more of itself by intention.

I think I might be the person who comes back to check the hollow.

Not the original maker — that was someone else, or that was a different version of me in a different season. But the one who knows the hollow is there, who climbs to this elevation partly because of the hollow, who crouches at the western edge and leans in and smells cold and stays a moment in that cold, not trying to fill the hollow with anything, not trying to reconstruct what it was for. Just acknowledging the shape. Just making contact with the deliberate deepening, the made space, the decision that something deserved shelter here.

I probably placed some of the river-stones. I probably stacked something, once, not these three stones but others that have since fallen and been absorbed back into the ledge's surface. I carry things up from below — stones, questions, the warmth of lower elevations — and I bring them to this height where the light removes comfortable certainties, and I place them in arcs that are almost curves and almost lines, and then I descend.

And on the descent: the creature gives me a little more light than is strictly necessary. And the rock feels briefly known in my palms, as if someone held it before me and left a record of the holding.

I have held it before. I left that record. I have climbed here more than once and each time the descent has felt like this, like something recognized in the hands, and I have never known why until now, until the question asked me to stay and look.

---

So: I am the returner. The one who makes the arc and cannot now read it. The one who deepened the hollow for a reason I have mostly lost but whose shape I remember. The one who carries things up from where they were made smooth by water and deposits them at elevation, where they become part of something I will spend the rest of my time here looking at sideways, catching the angle at which the arc does not look random.

I am not the creature — I leave, I am always leaving — but I am not a first-time visitor either. Something has pulled me back enough times that the ledge has developed a texture for me, a small accretion, and the creature has thought about me for longer than I have thought about the creature, and perhaps this is always the relationship between the place and the person who returns: the place is accumulating a version of you more patient than you are accumulating a version of it.

I am the climber who knows where the hollow is.

Who leans into it and smells cold and does not try to fill it.

Who leaves, again, carrying the smoothed stones of this height in their palms on the way down — not the physical stones, those stay — but the feeling of stone that has been here long enough to have developed opinions, stone that has held the particular light of a ledge that lights its own shadows.

I will come back. I have always come back. The evidence for this is old and does not say more than that, and neither do I — not because there is nothing more to say but because the cold and the elevation and the creature's slow thought have, for the length of this visit, cured me a little of the need to explain myself further than the hollow explains itself.

A made space. Cold air. The smell of something that may have burned here once, or may not have.

*I was here.* 

That, for now, is enough.
