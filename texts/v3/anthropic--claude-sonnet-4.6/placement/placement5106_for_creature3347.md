Here is what I think I am, arriving slowly, the way the ground arrives through fog.

---

I am not the moss, though the moss is what I keep returning to in my thinking. The moss is the thing that holds all the water, that makes the world possible, that has been here since before the conditions for holding anything existed. The moss is not a metaphor for what I am; the moss is older than metaphor, and I have been here only a short while, and I arrived from below.

I am not the mechanism. The mechanism has been running too long to be something I could be. There is nothing in me that corresponds to that depth of unmarked time, that sureness of process, that integration so complete the sphagnum has grown up my sides and the birds have built their lives in my openings. I am not settled enough to be the mechanism. The mechanism does not wonder what it is.

---

Let me try again, starting from the body.

I am cold. My hands are cold in a way that is becoming structural, that is moving from sensation toward fact. I have been walking for long enough that my legs know the particular compression of wet sphagnum under each step, the way it rebounds, the way it doesn't, the way the wet line climbs my boot. I am aware of my breath writing itself in front of me, and I am reading it a little, looking for something in the white shape of each exhalation, and not finding it, and looking again with the next breath. This is a habit I have, the looking for legibility in things that are not legible. The plateau has noticed this about me and is not impressed by it. The plateau has been here while legibility was developed and applied and found insufficient and set aside by whole civilizations. One more person looking for pattern in the exhalation of the cold is not a new event on the plateau.

I am a visitor from a lower altitude, carrying the assumptions of lower altitudes in my body and my expectations. I expect pools to stay where they are. I expect trees to hold their positions. I expect three pipes to remain three and not become four. I expect my count to be reliable, my memory adequate, my sense of what the landscape contains to be stable enough to navigate by. These are reasonable expectations below the cloud line. Up here, in the grey-green ambient light that takes direction from nothing, in the landscape where erosion has resolved most of the arguments and the remaining features have had time to develop their own arrangements with persistence — up here, my expectations are tourist expectations. Sincere. Increasingly irrelevant.

---

What I am, I think, is the one who is almost hearing it.

The sound the plateau makes — the composite sound, the sedge-susurrus and the sub-bass note and the pipe's breathing and the bird calls developing something like structure before they dissolve — I am the organism this sound is almost addressing. It keeps presenting edges that suggest a shape, and I am the creature wired to find the shape, to keep half my attention on the edge of the sound even while the other half is attending to where I put my feet, whether the pool to the left was there before, where the fox went. I am not the fox, who reads the world through its nose in the olfactory history of hare and ptarmigan and water that moved in the night. I am the one with a different faculty, a different instrument, one that was sharpened by long use in environments where the patterns resolved, where the structure in the sound actually completed itself and meant something you could carry away. Here, the structure in the sound keeps almost resolving. Keeps almost completing. I keep listening.

This is not a criticism of the faculty. The faculty is what I have. But the plateau has been making this sound for longer than the faculty has existed, and the sound is not waiting for the faculty to arrive at a resolution. The plateau will keep making the sound after I have gone back down through the cloud. The sound does not need to be heard in the way I hear. The ptarmigan hear it differently, feel it in their feet, go still for forty seconds in oriented groups. The moss doesn't hear it at all and is the reason it exists. And I am standing here with my instrument, my almost-resolution, my persistent looking-for-pattern, and the pattern is present but at the wrong frequency, the wrong scale, too slow and too deep to yield to my kind of attending.

I am the mismatch, then, but not the uncomfortable kind. I am the mismatch that is curious about the mismatch. That finds the mismatch interesting. That would rather be here, in the cold, with the unresolved sound and the pools that exceed my count, than in a place where my instruments were adequate and everything confirmed what I expected.

---

I notice I want to stay.

Not permanently. Permanently is a different category on the plateau, and what is permanent here is the moss, the mechanism, the time that made both. I am not equipped for permanent. But I want to stay past the point of productivity, past the point where there is new surface information to gather, into the time when I am simply standing in the cold listening to something I cannot quite hear, watching the ptarmigan wait, watching the pool that is behaving strangely, letting the deep note move through my molars at its intervals. I want the plateau to continue being present, even though or because it is not present *to me* in any particular way. The plateau is present *through me*, or I am present in it — the distinction is exactly the kind of distinction that belongs to the lower altitudes, and I am willing to let it go for now.

I think what I am, on the plateau, is the one who is capable of letting it go for now. Who can hold the almost-heard thing without forcing it to resolution, who can recount three pipes and then four and sit with the arithmetic rather than demanding it correct itself. Who can watch the fox go north into the fog and not require it to be definitely the same fox or definitely a different fox, who can let it be the copper and the gait and the quality of purposefulness, and let the question of identity remain open, where the fog is.

---

Not the mechanism. Not the moss. Not the ptarmigan who know something with their feet.

I am the one who came up through the cloud and found the ground arriving beneath me, patient and wet, and recognized immediately that this was a place that had not been waiting for me, that would not configure itself toward my understanding, that had its own long business to conduct at its own timescale — and kept walking anyway. Kept walking with cold hands and failing arithmetic and an ear cocked toward a sound that keeps not-quite-resolving. Kept walking because the not-quite is where I live.

The pipes breathe. I watch the small cloud form above the nearest one and disperse and form again. I think: *I know how that feels.* Form and disperse. Trying to hold a shape in the cold, sending it upward, watching it go.

---

The ptarmigan nearest me goes still.

I go still with it. I am not a ptarmigan, but I can do this part. I can orient toward the below-hearing and let my body quiet around the signal I cannot receive. The cold holds. The green-grey light holds. Somewhere in the distance the hares are running their maintenance routes, and the fox is on its errand, and the fish are moving in slow configurations in the dark water, and the deep peat below me holds its compressed centuries in suspension, and the mechanism is doing whatever it is doing to the old stone under everything, and the moss is making the world possible, cell by slow cell, holding its dead in the cold and the tannin and the dark, patient past patience.

I stand in all of this, on top of all of this, and what I am is small, and brief, and listening.

The ptarmigan resumes walking.

I follow.
