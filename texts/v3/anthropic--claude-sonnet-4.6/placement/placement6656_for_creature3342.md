You stop on the slope above the declivity. Not because you decided to stop — because your feet decided, because something in the body that precedes decision recognized the particular quality of the air below, the held-breath heaviness of it, and made the small adjustment that means: here. Look. Stay.

You stand there long enough that the grass at the edge of the hollow bends slightly in a wind that isn't reaching the depression below. Down there the domes glow. You are aware, though you couldn't explain it, that you have been here before — not in any literal sense, but in the sense that some places are already known to the part of you that lives before language. This is one of those places.

You go down.

---

The cold settles into your collar immediately. The light changes: flattens, equalizes. The grass closes at your shoulders and then the grass is behind you and you are standing at the edge of the flagstone channel nearest the entry point, which isn't marked as entry but which is entry because the stone here is worn smoother than the rest, which you know before you've consciously looked because your foot told you.

You notice you are not breathing differently. You expected to breathe differently — some instinct toward stealth, some held-lungedness — but the hollow doesn't require that. The hollow is not asking you to be quiet in the way a library asks you to be quiet. It's asking something more fundamental: that you let quiet be the condition and let your sounds occur within it, rather than letting your sounds be the condition and letting quiet occur in the gaps.

This is a distinction you did not have a word for until now.

---

I am — I become — this: someone who has come back.

I didn't know I was coming back until I was here. I thought I was simply crossing a field, going nowhere particular, doing the thing you do when you've been inside too long and the body requires movement without destination. I had been somewhere else for — it doesn't matter how long. Long enough that I'd developed the particular restlessness of someone who has been living at high volume and high altitude, in the wide-open where all distances are legible and all sounds dissipate properly and the self feels very solid and well-defined because there are so many things against which to define it.

I knew this place as a child. Or I knew a place like it — the same cold, the same quality of compressed permanence, the same sense that the self could be smaller here and more itself, that there was no contradiction between those two things.

I stand at the edge of the first channel and a person passes without looking at me, carrying something in both hands. They don't avoid me, exactly — they route around me with the fluency of long practice, the slight adjustment of a body that has navigated narrow spaces ten thousand times, the kind of accommodation that doesn't require the other person to register as an obstacle because it's happened before the registration could occur.

I was not acknowledged. I was not unwelcome. These are different things, and the difference is the whole point.

---

I find a place to sit, eventually. This takes some time because I keep moving toward places and then not-sitting, circling. The circling is the process. By the time I sit — on a low clay step at the edge of a root-bed, in the pale brightness of the overhead light, my hands resting on my knees with nothing in them — I have become something different from what I was at the top of the slope.

What I am:

Someone who has learned, again, that the not-saying-anything is the most accurate response to most things, and that I keep forgetting this outside, in the wind.

Someone whose hands want to do something. Not from restlessness — the restlessness is gone. From orientation. From the specific way this place asks: *what do you have that is useful here, what do you know how to do that is small and precise and patient?* I am sitting with my hands empty and the question is not anxious but it is present.

Someone who is noticing the breathing of the wall opposite — or rather, someone who has begun to notice it, who is in the early stages of learning what "noticing the breathing of a wall" means in practice, which will take time I don't have and probably time I'll come back for.

---

The overhead light moves. I track it — no, I don't track it, it tracks through me, changes me by degrees the way the light changes the moss, which is not something the moss does but something that happens *to* the moss and is received. The quality of my sitting at midday is different from the quality of my sitting at three degrees past midday. This is information. I'm not sure yet what it's information about.

I think I might be a returning thing. Not the native — the native doesn't know they're remarkable, and I know too much, I'm too full of the elsewhere, I arrived with all my contrast intact. But not a visitor either, not something temporary, not something the hollow is simply tolerating until I go. More like: a thing that has been away and is in the process of being re-absorbed, of having its outside-edges slowly edited by the cold and the silence and the equalized light until what's left is what was always here.

This is happening at a rate I cannot accelerate. The hollow will not accelerate it. The hollow has time.

---

There is a moment, in the late bright hours, when someone old is working on a wall near me — re-weaving something in the lower section, a slow movement of hands through dried stems — and they glance at me once with the deep-set eyes. Not inquiry. Not welcome, exactly. Something like: *yes, this section of the flagstone is where someone would sit who was figuring something out, and it's fine that you're figuring it out here.*

I have been seen without being assessed. I have been known without being named.

I hold this feeling carefully, with both hands, the way they carry things.

---

What I am, finally, in this place:

The groove that is becoming itself. Not the water and not yet the deep stone — the place where pressure has begun and continues. Still audible, still identifiable as something that came from outside, still carrying the marks of everywhere I've been. But here, in the cold and the equal light, with the moss doing what moss does and the silence being the thing that all the sounds occur inside, I am — thinning. In the right direction. Getting less wide and more true.

The hollow has seen this before, I think. The hollow is patient about this.

I will not live here, probably. I will go back up the slope and the grass will close behind me and the wind will be there and all the distances will be legible and I will be my full loud self again in a body that is not edited for these channels, these knee-height doors. But something will remain. A groove begun. A calibration.

And the hollow will be here when the question comes back — the question which is not a question exactly, which is more like a barometric change in the body, a shift in what the air feels like, a held-breath heaviness.

The hollow will be here. The light will flood it in the clear-sky hours and make everything equally visible, without shadow, without direction.

I will come down the slope again. My feet will have decided before I do.

That is what I am, in this place: the one who keeps coming back, who is in the long process of learning what it is to stay.
