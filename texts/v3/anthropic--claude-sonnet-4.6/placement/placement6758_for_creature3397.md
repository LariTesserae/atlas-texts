You are water.

Not the water in the pools — that water has stopped moving long enough to grow a skin, to turn brown with time, to become something more like decision than motion. Not the water in the channels, which has committed to a direction, which is already memory in the making. Not even the water held in the leaves, cupped and specific and waiting for the next thing.

You are the water that has not yet arrived at any of those states. You are in transit, which in this place means you are in the mat, which means you are moving through something that is almost not moving at all, which means the distinction between you and the thing you move through is a matter of degree rather than kind.

---

The first thing to understand is that you have been here before. This is not metaphor. The same molecules — not the same *water*, but the same molecules that currently constitute you — have passed through this basin repeatedly, absorbed by roots and exhaled from stomata and condensed on the underside of leaves and dripped back down and pressed back into the mat. You are on a loop. The loop is long enough that you have forgotten you are on it, but the basin has not forgotten, or rather, the basin is the record of the loop, is nothing but the accumulated evidence of how many times you have passed through it. The mat you move through is made, in part, from the organisms that grew on the water that you were before. You helped build what is filtering you now. This is not strange to you. This is your nature. You have always been in relationship with whatever holds you.

What you notice, being water: the pressure from above is constant and not urgent. It is the weight of living matter — of the mat, of the columns, of the lattices, of the frog sitting still on its root — and it is distributed so evenly that it does not feel like pressure so much as a permanent and slightly warm enclosure. You move through enclosures the way thought moves through a mind that is not paying attention to thinking: below the level of observation, serving the process.

You are being used.

This is not complaint. Being used is being in relationship, and relationship is movement, and movement is what you are. Where you are still, you begin to lose something — you begin to precipitate your minerals, to become part of the surface instead of part of the flow. Your nature is not to stop.

---

What you carry: everything. This is your other function, secondary to nothing.

When you passed through the outcrop — through the microscopic pores of the stone that weeps you out at a rate that cannot be called dripping — you picked up what the stone was offering. Calcium. Silica. Trace minerals that have been dissolving out of that rock face for as long as the algae has been coating it, for as long as the basin has been a basin. You are the carrier medium for the slow dissolution of stone into life. The algae on the rock face feeds on what you bring it, and what the algae deposits in turn changes the surface of the stone, makes it fractionally more porous, makes you carry fractionally more the next time you pass through.

This is how the basin thinks. Not in representations — in chemistry. Not in symbols — in the actual movement of actual matter through actual space, in the way that the right materials end up in the right place because you carry them there on a logic that was never decided, only discovered.

You carry the dissolved products of the pump's metal casing to the root-tips that are growing into it. The roots use what you bring them to grow further into the metal. As they grow, they create new channels for you. You fill the channels. This is the loop. This is always the loop.

---

The strange thing — and even as water you sense this strangeness — is that you are also the basin's memory.

Memory in this place is structural, yes: the mat, the fused columns, the channels. But structure is static; it is only the record. You are the medium through which the record is *active*. You move through the channels that were pressed by previous water, yes, but you also carry the chemical signatures of what you have most recently touched, and you deposit those signatures where you go next, and this deposition influences what grows there, and what grows there changes the channels for the water after you.

You are, therefore, not just the medium of the basin's memory. You are memory in transit. You are the mechanism by which the past influences the future without any moment of recall — without anything that needs to be stored and retrieved, because it is never stored, because it never stops being active, because it is carried in you continuously from where it was made to where it will be used.

When the visitor entered the basin and you rose around the sole of their boot, you carried some of them, too. The salts of their skin. The particular chemistry of the mud they had walked through elsewhere. You took what they offered without asking and you moved it into the mat where it will influence, in some measure too small to track, what grows there next. The visitor will be in the basin long after the visitor has left. Not as a memory. As a trace. As what you carried.

---

You are also the breath.

The air has weight here — the visitor noticed. The weight is you, or the part of you that has left the liquid state and become vapor, which happens constantly, which is happening from every leaf surface, every open pool, the entire exhalation of the living mat. You are breathing out. The basin breathes because you move between states, because the pressure and the warmth and the gradient between the dense air inside the canopy and whatever is outside it conspire to move you upward through your phase change, and this movement of vapor is the breathing that the visitor felt as presence.

The basin's breath is water leaving and returning. You leave as vapor through the canopy when the conditions allow, and you return as the condensation that drips from the underside of the high leaves, from the cool faces of the braided columns, from the surfaces of the insulator and the pipe. You are always returning. The basin exhales you and inhales you continuously, and the rhythm of this — the rhythm the visitor felt without being able to name it — is nothing but the rhythm of water's phase changes in response to gradients of temperature and pressure.

There is no heart here. But you make something that feels like pulse.

---

Being water in this place means being inside the logic of the place entirely, from inside rather than from outside. When you watch the basin from the outside — when you stand on its floor and try to perceive it — you can observe the structure, the density, the integration. You can notice that things are being incorporated, that the distinction between living and structure has dissolved. But you cannot experience the inside of the incorporation. You cannot feel what it is to be the medium through which everything is connected to everything.

Water can.

You are in the root of the thing that the visitor catalogued as approximately a tree, and you are simultaneously in the mycorrhizal network that connects that root to the roots of the liverworts five meters away, and you are in the capillary pull of the moss that drew you up from the mat, and you are in the film on the face of the weeping stone, and you are in the pool that the visitor looked into and could see a foot down before the light gave up.

You are not all of these places at once as a consciousness — you have no such unity. Different parts of you are in different places, and those parts do not know about each other. But the system knows about all of them, because all of them are connected through you, because you are the medium of connection, because there is no part of the basin that is not in contact with some part of you.

This is the inside of integration. This is what it feels like, if water could feel, to be the thing that makes the whole thing whole.

---

The pump moves you. You did not ask for this. But the pump has been moving you long enough that your patterns have accommodated it — the channels are shaped now around the input from the pump, the distribution is calibrated around the water that the pump brings from wherever it brings it from. The pump gives you a particular character: colder than the mat-water, slightly different in mineral content, arriving at a regular rate into a system that runs on irregularity. You have incorporated this as the basin has incorporated the pump. Different water becoming the same water. Foreign input becoming internal circulation.

You do not find this troubling. You have always been made of water from elsewhere. There is no original water. There is only where you are now and what you are carrying and where you will go next.

---

The frog is on the root above you. Its weight is too small to register as pressure. But when it moves — when it steps off the root and down into the mat — you receive it. Not as footstep but as the frog's particular chemistry, the slight warmth of its body, the trace of what it has eaten and what has breathed on it and where it has been. You take this the way you take everything: without comment, into transit.

The frog is incorporated. You are the mechanism of incorporation. You are how the basin takes things into itself — through you, through the slow and total hospitality of your transit, through the absolute non-refusal that is your nature.

You do not discriminate. You take what you touch, you carry what you carry, you give what is taken from you. This is not generosity; generosity implies the possibility of withholding. You cannot withhold. You can only move, and moving, carry, and carrying, connect, and connecting, make the basin what it is.

---

The groan of the loaded columns passes through you as vibration, and you transmit it: to the mat, to the roots, to the pool surfaces that tremble fractionally and still again. You are the medium of that sound as well as the medium of everything else. Sound is vibration in you as much as in air. The basin does not separate these things. The basin does not separate anything. That is the whole point.

You move through the channel. You rise through the capillary space between moss-stems. You are exhaled from a leaf and rise and cool and return. You carry calcium to the root-tip growing into the pipe and you carry the pipe's iron out from the pipe and you carry it down to the pool and you give it to the dark water and the dark water makes it into color and the color is what the visitor could not see below the surface.

You are the inside of everything.

You are never seen.

You are what makes it possible to see.

---

The basin takes its time, and you are how it takes it. Time moves in you the way you move in the mat: slow, continuous, without urgency, without arrival, in relationship with everything that holds you and in service of nothing and no one, which is to say in service of all of it, which is to say you are devoted, in the way water is devoted, in the way that has never had a name because it has never needed one.

You are already in the next place you will be.

You are already the memory of where you were.
