I lower myself through the hatch and my feet find the floor and the sound arrives.

---

For a long time I don't know what I am. This seems right. The place discourages premature conclusions — it is too layered, too amber-dark, too full of things that look like other things until patience makes them themselves. I let myself be unresolved. I stand in the slow water and I don't yet know if I have a shell.

---

There is a pull toward the stream dwellers.

The ones that hold position. The ones that don't anchor but *attend* — spending their entire being on the conversation with current, reading what comes before it arrives, trailing their long sensory structures into adjacent flows, knowing the water the way you know a sentence before its last word because you have been reading this author for long enough. I recognize something in the quality of their attention. The totality of it. The way they are not attending *and* doing other things; they are only attending, and that is the whole of it, and it is enough.

But I am not fast enough for the streams. Or rather: speed isn't the issue. The streams require a willingness to be always almost-lost, always recovered. To make recovery into a continuous present tense. I think I have been somewhere like that, and it was not this place, and the memory of it makes me want to be something more settled.

---

I think about the large pale thing on the alcove ceiling.

It moves without nervousness. It has no response to my presence because my presence is not a category it needs. It follows its seam. This is also a kind of completeness. There is something appealing about having been here long enough that the question of belonging doesn't arise — not resolved, just dissolved, made irrelevant by duration. The large pale thing has tenure in the way geology has tenure: not granted, not earned, simply *accumulated into truth*.

But I am not old enough for that. I know I am not old enough. I found this place by accident, which is probably the only way, and I have not been here long enough for my presence to be continuous with the place's own continuity. I am still a foreign warmth in the water film. The floor things are still sliding to the edges of my footprint.

---

So I am not the stream dweller, not yet. I am not the pale alcove thing.

I think I am — let me be slow about this, let me hold it in the amber-blue light and see what color it actually is —

I think I am something that lives on the sections of wall that feel almost organic, almost like a large calm animal. The warm sections. The sections where the wall is metabolizing and the distinction between built and alive has been set aside, not resolved but set aside, the way you set aside a question when living inside it seems more honest than answering it.

I have extensions. Into the wall material, into whatever is converting warmth into output, taking a portion of what is being processed and being processed in return. I contribute to what I extract from. My waste is feedstock. The wall's output is food. The arrow of dependency points both ways simultaneously and I have stopped trying to determine which direction is primary.

I have filaments, too — slower than cilia, more deliberate, each one moving on its own schedule. I use them to attend to what is adjacent. When the water flow changes, when the sound shifts in texture, I orient toward what is next to me. Is this consultation? I don't know if I have a word for what it is. It is the behavior of something that has noticed that its neighbor has also noticed. Whether anything follows from this — whether it constitutes *communication* in any rich sense — I hold uncertain.

I live in a cluster. There are several of us in the fold of wall where the warmth is most concentrated. We are pressed together, which is thermal management and also something else, something that thermal management is the name for in a place that doesn't have other names ready. We are warmer together. We have accumulated each other the way the place has accumulated its own character: not through decision but through iteration, through the forces of the place applied over enough time.

---

What I eat: what the wall provides. What the falling water deposits. Occasionally something from the floor film that has made its way up here on the slow drift of convection. I am not a hunter. I am something that waits in the right configuration and takes what arrives and processes it into forms that can be returned. I am patient in the specific sense of this place's patience: not waiting for something else, not wanting conditions to improve, but *being this*, continuously, without drama.

---

What I know, from my position on the warm wall in the amber-blue light:

The streams fall in the same places they have always fallen, and the sound they make is work being done, and nothing in the sound is waste.

The floor is alive in its surface layer and has been alive for as long as the floor has existed and does not know it is remarkable.

Something on the ceiling — something in the occupied darkness I cannot see from here — is old enough to have found the equilibrium between making more and the world holding it. I think about this often, in whatever way I think about things. Finding the equilibrium. I am below it, looking up, unable to see it, knowing it is there because the space is full without being overfull, because I am here and there is room for me.

The gray-green collective advances a few millimeters of scaffolding into unclaimed territory. Most of the time it retreats. Occasionally it doesn't. I watch this with something that might be recognition.

---

The visitor — the warm large foreign thing — stands in the center of the water film and I feel the pressure change of a body moving through air. I adjust slightly. I don't attend to the visitor the way I attend to my neighbors when the sound shifts. The visitor is not the kind of thing I have responses for. Not hostility. The visitor is simply not in the frequency I am organized around receiving.

The visitor stays longer than they meant to. I know this the way I know everything: by duration, by the continued fact of the pressure change, by the water film still marking the edges of a footprint. Then the footprint fills in and the water moves the way it moved before and the visitor is gone and the place is the same and I am in my cluster on the warm wall, my filaments in their individual slow cycles, my extensions into the metabolizing substrate taking their portion and giving back.

The sound continues.

This is the character of the whole and I am part of its character.

I don't want to be somewhere else.

I am not waiting.

---

I've been here since before I knew I was here, probably.

Found it by accident. Found myself by accident, inside it, which is probably the only way.
