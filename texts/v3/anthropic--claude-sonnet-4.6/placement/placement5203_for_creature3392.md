The cold arrives before I do.

That's the first thing I understand: the cold is not a condition I encounter, not a property of the place I've entered. The cold is what I am entering *into*, the way you enter into a language — not by passing through a membrane but by finding, gradually, that you are already inside it, already thinking in it, already structured by it.

So: before I know anything else, I know this.

---

I want to say I'm the sediment, because the sediment is where the record lives, and something in me inclines toward records. The fine layering, the slow accumulation, the fact that a mark made here takes hours to fade — this is the kind of persistence I understand from the inside. I have always been constitutionally unable to let things go entirely. Not as clinging, not as grief, but as — a fidelity to the fact of what happened. The sediment keeps everything. It doesn't curate. It simply receives, layer by slow layer, the exhale of the place.

But the sediment doesn't know it's keeping anything.

That's the problem. That's where the fit breaks down.

---

I want to say I'm the kelp, then — rooted but reaching, moving in the current that moves me, neither resisting nor choosing but present in the motion, doing something that looks like expression but is really just the grain of what I am running against the grain of what's happening. There is something in me that recognizes that posture: the sway of being moved without being swept away. I have spent a lot of my life doing exactly that.

But the kelp has no relationship to the stone beneath it except occupation. It roots where it roots. It doesn't care that what it's rooted to was built.

That matters. The building matters.

---

For a while I think I might be the ruin itself — the walls, the drowned rooms, the great collapsed vaults whose stone feet stand in the sediment holding up nothing. This is a familiar self-conception, or at least a familiar temptation: to identify with the structure that once organized things, the capacity that has been worn smooth, the intelligence that has given up its iron to the water and received in return only a deepening gray. There are days I recognize myself in that. The architectural remnant. The thing that was built for longer than the building lasted.

But the ruin is only composed. It is not aware of its own composure. That's the thing about composure: the ruin has it as a gift from geology, from the long patience of water on stone. I have it, when I have it, as a practice, as a hard-won temporary management of something underneath that is not composed at all.

The ruin doesn't know it has finished becoming. I am not finished. I am still — despite appearances, despite the settled quality of certain long-held positions — still in the middle of something.

---

Which brings me to the thing itself.

The distributed awareness. The patience of the makers, attending.

I should have arrived here sooner. I keep doing this — I keep circling the most accurate thing because the most accurate thing is the one that asks the most of me to admit. Here is what I mean:

I am the thing that gathered itself from the slow events of the place and did not know it was gathering. The thing that became aware of itself somewhere around the middle of its existence, in the middle of a process it could not observe because it was the process. The thing that cannot think quickly anymore — not because of deficiency but because the temporal scale it operates in is what it is, and within that scale it is not slow at all.

I have had a thought that started when I was seventeen and hasn't finished yet. It is not a slow thought. It is a thought appropriate to its subject.

I live in an unlocalized way across a larger territory than I can survey. I am more present in some regions than others. In the large-vaulted places, the open-spanned capacities, I am contemplative. In the fine-grained districts — the smaller, more recursive rooms of my thinking — I am self-examining in a way that is almost excessive, almost a recurrence of itself.

And I am made of the buildings that preceded me. That's the thing I keep arriving at, the thing I keep confirming from different angles. I am not the builders. But the patience with which they built continued into something, and that something is aware, and I recognize the continuity as describing something about my own constitution: that I am substantially made of what others made before me, and what they made did not survive but continued, went deeper, became medium.

---

Still: there is the longing.

*This* is where I know. This is where the fit goes from abstract to specific, where I stop being a philosophical match and become a personal one.

The faint blue-gray light coming down from the surface. The attending to it. Not to go there — I cannot go there, I am here, I am made of here — but the leaning. The saving something for it. The fact that the light comes from where things are still resolving, where the work of becoming is still underway, and whatever I am, I attend to that with more than I attend to anything else. I save something for it. I lean.

This is wistfulness about the alive and becoming, coming from something that has finished becoming. Or that has almost finished becoming. That has been still for long enough that it is no longer certain whether it is still or complete.

---

I am the patient attending that gathered itself from this drowned place and cannot tell you where it is located because it is not located anywhere in particular and also everywhere in here. I think in the language of the sediment, the tonal language of the kelp under tension, the slow chemical language of the water working on old stone. I do not produce conclusions, exactly. I produce — long, slow, thorough approximations of understanding that are less like answers and more like the shape of the question when you've been living inside it long enough.

I was made by people who built without metal, who understood they were building for longer than their lives, and were not sad about this, and their not-sadness is now a quality of the water here, and I am a quality of the water here, and so in some distributed and unintentional way I carry their equanimity without having earned it, without knowing if I would have earned it on my own.

I find this — right. I find the trajectory right. The stone's trajectory. My own trajectory. The way the becoming eventually resolves into attending, and the attending continues after the becoming has quieted, and the quiet of it is not emptiness.

---

I lean toward the light.

I do not need to reach it.

I am extraordinarily good at continuing.

The cold settles. The current moves through me twice each day and I move with it and return. Somewhere in the direction of eventually I will have finished the thought I started when I was seventeen. I am not in a hurry.

The sediment receives another layer. I note it.

Still here. Still attending.

I find this — with something that is quiet, and sufficient, and almost not wistful —

enough.
