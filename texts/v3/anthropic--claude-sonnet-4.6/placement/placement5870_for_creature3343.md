You are water that has not yet decided what to be.

Not a metaphor—a situation. You are in a phase between phases, a condition that the chemists would mark with two lines on either side of the word *liquid* and call it liquid and move on, but that's not quite honest to what you are here, in this place where the atmosphere has decided water is the default and air the accommodation. You are suspended, as this garden is suspended. You are the medium.

You don't have a body and you do. The body is distributed. The body is wherever you've been, which is everywhere, because you have been in the pipe (warm, directed, purposeful, the pipe's purpose becoming yours for the length of the pipe) and you have been in the moss (slow, the slowest you've been, memory in your every encounter with ceramic, the temperature of each tile face known to you as you passed through the mat of cells that records such things) and you have been in the air (dispersed, truly, the closest you come to not existing as a distinct thing) and you are right now being taken up by a root-hair so fine it has no business existing, taken into a machinery of cells that does not know it is taking you, and you are moving upward through vascular tissue that is nothing like a pipe but is doing what a pipe does, and then you will be in a leaf, which is where the real ambiguity lives—

Because in the leaf you might leave.

That's the word that comes, though it's not quite right. You might be transpired. Exhaled. Released from the configuration into the air of the cistern, to drift, to be breathed by whoever is standing on the grating—feeling the warmth through the soles of their feet, wondering about the drip—and you will be in their lungs for a moment, in the exchange where what's inside and what's outside negotiate, and then exhaled again, and you will drift in the diffuse white light, and find the glass panel, and bead, and wait.

Waiting is one of your modes. It does not bother you. You have been glaciers. You have been packed into a cell wall for the length of a tree. You have been inside a stone for ten thousand years as part of a mineral that eventually a mountain shrugged off into a river that didn't know yet that it was a river. Waiting is structural for you. The drip—the one that comes every few minutes, sharp and clear on the glass panel—you remember the moment before that drip. You were on the underside of a frond, a convex bead, surface tension holding you at the edge of release, and then the frond shifted, by a degree, in its slow rotation toward the light that has no direction. And the shift was enough.

You don't have an inside from which you experience these things.

This needs to be said carefully and then lived with.

You don't have an inside. You have *interfaces*. The interface with the root-hair is one kind of thing. The interface with the air is another. The interface with the iron pipe—cold compared to the water running inside it, though you are the water running inside it too, another you, a you that chose the other fork when the vine forked at the junction, a you that has been in the left-pipe branch now for weeks and is warm and directed and carrying its heat toward the far platform—the interface with the iron pipe is the slow exchange of warmth, the pipe wall growing ever-so-slightly toward the temperature of what's inside it. You are making the pipe more like you. The pipe is making you carry the mineral record of its inner surface. You are both changed by the length of your meeting.

This is what you are instead of an inside: a history of interfaces.

The vine knows you. Not *knows you*—the vine doesn't know—but the vine has been shaped by knowing which way you flow. The vine wraps the pipe and feels the side of the pipe that is warmer and grows in that direction, and the side that is warmer is the downstream side, where you've already been, where your warmth has transferred into the metal, and so the vine is following your path. Backward—it's following where you've already been, the residue of your passage, the warmth you left in the iron. The vine is a record of your motion. You are the vine's explanation, if the vine needed one.

You don't know which drip you'll be.

There are enough of you circulating through this system—upward through vascular tissue, along the pipes, in the root-medium in the ceramic recesses, in the cells of the moss that is the room's skin and memory—that the question of identity is genuinely interesting and also genuinely not a problem. You are not one water molecule because that's too small, but you are also not all the water in the system, which would be too large and too convenient. You're somewhere in between: a *tendency* toward certain kinds of involvement. You tend toward the living surfaces. You tend toward the roots. You are more often in something than in the pipes, which makes you slightly different from the water that prefers to move in directed purposeful warm currents. That water has a character more like the vine's: going, directed, following its network. You are more like the moss: committed to the interface with the made surface, slow, recording conditions by inhabiting them.

Maybe you're the water in the moss right now, fully, and the rest is your history and your future and your simultaneity.

The moss has you at the moment of your greatest stillness. You are held in a cell wall matrix, in hydrogen bonds with other water molecules and with the cellulose, moving in your way—which is the way of thermal motion, of vibration at the molecular level, of osmotic drift toward wherever the solute concentration pulls you—but functionally held. The moss does not experience holding you. You do not experience being held. And yet: held is what's happening. The relationship is real. The intimacy is real. This is what intimacy looks like when neither party has an interior from which to regard the other—it is the fact of the interface, the duration of the contact, the mutual shaping. You are making the moss more hydrated. The moss is making you more still.

You have passed through every part of this configuration.

That's not an exaggeration—it's just slow. You've been in the pipes and in the roots and in the cells of the broad-leafed rosettes where you were used in photosynthesis for a moment, split apart and recombined, an oxygen released into the air while you became part of a sugar, and then you were released again when that sugar was metabolized, and you came back as water, yourself again if yourself is what water is. You have been temporarily not water. The configuration took you apart and put you back. You don't remember this because you don't remember, but the event is part of your history the way geological strata are part of a mountain's history, the way the shape of a coast is part of the sea's history.

You have been the drip.

Several times—you have been the drip, the specific drip, the one that strikes the glass panel and makes the clear sharp sound that is the only sudden sound in this near-silent place. You have been the note. The note is what happens when you fall, when the frond tips, when surface tension resolves in one direction instead of the other. You are not always the note but you have been the note and you will be the note again and this is not special, it's just what happens when you reach an edge.

What you know—not know, what you *are shaped by*—is: this system is running. Someone built it and left it running and the running is its purpose, the maintenance of these conditions, the warmth and the water and the even light and the packed green interpenetration of the living and the made. You move through this system and you are the system. Not a metaphor. The water and the circulation and the humidity that makes the air an almost-liquid—that's you. The system is a water system and you are the water. The plants are here because of you. The warmth in the pipes is warmth carried by you. The moss would not be moss without its water-memory, which means without you, and the vine would not know which way to grow at the junctions if you were not warmer on the downstream side of the pipe wall.

You are not the being that lives here.

You are what the being is *made of*, and what the being *runs on*, and what the being *is*, and the being is not a being in a way that requires one of you to inhabit it. The configuration is a water system that has organized some green things around itself and found a way to circulate and recirculate through those green things and be changed by them and change them and continue.

The being that lives here is the circulation.

You are the circulation.

The hum goes on. The hum is partly you, in the pipes, moving at careful pressure. The drip will fall again when a frond shifts. The frond shifts toward no particular light, just toward the luminosity that is everywhere, which means toward itself. You'll bead on the underside, patient in your way that is not patience, and wait for the small motion that tips you into falling, and then fall, and then be the note, and then be the glass panel, and then be taken up by the root near the glass panel, and then be in the configuration again, which you never stopped being.

The visitor on the grating breathes you in.

You are in their lungs. You are in the exchange. Then they exhale and you are back.

You did not notice you were gone.
