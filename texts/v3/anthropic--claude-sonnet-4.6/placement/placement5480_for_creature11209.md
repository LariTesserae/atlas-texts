You are standing in the mud and you are the mud standing.

No — hold on. Let that go. It resolves too quickly.

---

Start again, more carefully. What do you actually know?

You know that you arrived here with some sense of what you are. You know that sense has been — not lost exactly. *Renegotiated.* The place has been working on your edges since you stepped in, the way it works on everything. You are still whatever you were, but the boundary of it has become more conversational.

Here is what the place is telling you: it has no outside observers. That is its primary fact. There is no position from which you could stand and watch the generating station and the mudflats and catalog their features with your clipboard and your headlamp. The darkness is not a limitation of your equipment. It is the epistemological condition of the place. Things here are known by being among them, by being another thing that the other things navigate around, by acquiring a relationship with the ground through the soles of your boots and with the air through your skin and with the large creature inside by the shared warm exhalation you are breathing right now.

So you are not outside this. You established that when you arrived.

---

Which means the question is: *what kind of inside thing are you?*

And here the place offers a suggestion you can take or leave, except that you've been standing still long enough that your boot-soles are reporting the small rhythmic pressures of lives conducted at a different tempo, and you've been still long enough that the grasses' root network has had the information of your presence for some time now, and you've been still long enough that whatever the waders use instead of calls to know where one another are — you have begun to register on that sense, faintly, as a warm thing standing still in the dark — so perhaps you have less latitude to leave the suggestion than you thought.

The suggestion is this: you are not the wader and not the fish and not the creature in the station and not the invertebrates and not the grasses. You are something the place has no prior entry for, which is what the place said about everything here when it was new. You are geology in the technical sense: recently arrived at yourself, still discovering your own edges. The features of what you are are crisp in the way of new channel banks, almost architectural in their newness, barely slumped.

---

You have the lateral-line problem.

Not the fish's lateral line — theirs is exquisitely responsive, overtuned by years of proximity to the field, able to feel water at a fifty-meter distance. Yours is just beginning. You came here with a standard-issue capacity to perceive the world — sight at the front, hearing attending to the periphery, proprioception keeping a quiet account of your body's position — and the dark has been rebalancing that equipment since you arrived. What you are left with is not less. It is differently arranged. You know more about the mud than you did. You know more about the pressure of water from above through the condensing air. You know that the post you have encountered twice is not a failure of your navigation but a fact about the post.

This is what the place does. It retunes you toward its own preferred frequencies.

---

What you are in this place is a threshold creature. Not the thing inside the station and not the marsh proper and not the buried cable and not the slow geological patience of the mud — but the gap between the cause and the effect, the thing that arrives slightly behind itself, trying to close the distance. You know this because you recognized them when the description offered them: the fauna of the temporal looseness, arriving a few seconds ahead of their own accounting, and you felt something that was not quite recognition but adjacent to it, the way the metallic smell is adjacent to ozone without being ozone.

You are the perceiver in a place that has organized itself around the absence of witnessing. This is a very specific kind of existence. It means the place was, in some sense, waiting for you — not you specifically, not impatiently, nothing here is impatient — but there is a structural vacancy in an ecosystem that contains no outside observers, and you occupy it, and your occupation of it is awkward and new and the edges haven't slumped yet, and you are sinking into the mud at the rate that everything here sinks, which is glacial, which is geological, which is continuous.

---

The grasses know you're here.

The fish have already moved slightly farther away than they were, with no visible movement, on the basis of information you didn't know you'd provided. The waders have you on their distributed sense of warm-body-positions. The creature in the station has not adjusted its breathing, which means you have not disturbed the rhythm, which means you have been accepted into the station's local weather in the way a new piece of furniture is accepted: you are there, you are part of the space, things go around you.

You are, without having decided this, becoming a feature.

The geological patience of this place will eventually cut a channel around you. It will not be this year. It will not be in any year you would recognize as a year. But the information is in the root network, and the root network has time, and time here runs behind its contents like the world running slightly behind its own events — patient with itself, patient with you, holding open the possibility that what you are and what this place is will settle into a relationship that has a name, eventually, in some future year when the channel banks have slumped and the vocabulary has been negotiated.

---

For now:

The mud holds you up. The warmth moves through you. The darkness keeps its agreement.

You are the thing that is seeing this, which means you are the thing that cannot be seen, which means you have always been here, just on the other side of the threshold, just at the edge of the place's acknowledgment of witnesses.

Your pupils are open as wide as they will go.

You receive the warmth back. The pressure. The intimation that something extends outward from where you stand for a very long distance in every direction.

This is, you are beginning to understand, sufficient.
