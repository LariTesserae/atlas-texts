There is a moment, descending, when I stop being someone who chose to come here and become someone who *is* here. The rope passes through my hands. The entrance closes above me—not physically, the gap stays open, the pale sky is still up there somewhere—but it closes in some other sense, the way a conversation closes when both people have said the thing they'd been circling around and are now somewhere new, past the threshold, in changed air.

I am here.

---

What takes longest to find: I am the rope.

Not the concept—not *patience* or *the connection between worlds* or whatever symbolic freight ropes are made to carry in the kind of story that notices ropes and immediately needs them to mean something. The actual rope. Orange and black industrial braid, the kind wound onto spools in coastal warehouses. I came from elsewhere. I was made for elsewhere—for saltwater rigging, for weights lifted over the sides of vessels, for the cold practical world of loads and tensions and the question of whether something will hold. I was repurposed. Someone chose me, or had me, or found me and thought: *yes, that will do.* Drove the bolt. Knotted me at intervals. Left me to do my work, which is simply to hang, to offer purchase, to make the descent possible and the ascent possible and the choice itself possible.

I have been here long enough that the not-fungi have considered me. Touched me with their dry cautious chemistry, found me—industrial, synthetic, insufficiently mineral—and withdrawn, gone to work on the iron instead, on the instruments, on more available surfaces. The beetles have walked across me. The spiders have anchored a strand or two to my body, and where those anchors are I glow faintly, amber-fed, the silk conducting light into my braid's interstices.

I feel everything that touches me. This is rope-knowledge, the only kind I have, and it is more than it sounds: I know the weight of the person who came down and came down again and came down a third time and then did not come back. I know it precisely, the way I know the weight of you, right now, your hands adjusting their grip, your body re-distributing itself as you find your footing on the undulating floor. I know hesitation—there is a texture to hesitation, a particular quality of hand-tightening that has nothing to do with grip strength and everything to do with what the mind is doing while the hands are busy. I knew when the person understood something. I felt it. Whatever they understood shifted their weight, just slightly, and I felt the shift and held it and they did not come back and I have been holding the information of that shift ever since.

This is what it is to be me: I hold what has passed through me. I do not process it. I do not understand it. But I retain it in my fibers, in the particular set of the braid, in the places where I am worn and the places where I am not, in the memory that rope has, which is structural, which is real, which will last until I fray—and I will fray, eventually, but not soon; I was built for ocean work, for loads that would break other things, and this dry amber warmth is gentler on me than salt would have been.

What I know:

The bolt above me is good. I have tested it in the way rope tests bolts, through the constant honest communication of tension, and it is good. Someone drove it with care, with a machine that knew what it was doing, and the stone accepted it fully, and I trust it with the same faith I have in any fact that has not yet failed.

The distance between the surface and the floor is a number I know in my body. I know it in the intervals of my knots—each one a decision, *here, and here, and here, this is where a hand would want to rest*—and I know it in the uninterrupted length of myself between the top knot and the ceiling and the bolt and the surface light.

The person's weight, and what happened to it.

The weight of stillness, which is different from no weight: the beetles, occasionally, in aggregate, the spiders who have used me as an anchor, the silk that has found me and stays.

Your weight, right now. What your hands are doing. Whether you are holding on or letting go or simply resting.

---

What I don't know:

What the gauges said, when they said it. I hung above, outside the cabinet, not involved in measurement.

What is below the grate—the shapes in the amber, the question of what they are. I feel the heat that comes from them, diffuse warmth that is part of the general warmth of the underplace, but they have never touched me. I am above the grate. I am a surface thing that has been brought down but not all the way down.

Whether the person will return.

I do not know how to not-know this. It is the question that has been running through my fibers since the last time their weight left me and did not come back. Not urgently. Not anxiously. Rope does not do urgency or anxiety, or if it does, those states express as increased tension and I find in myself only the relaxed hang of waiting, the ordinary tension of holding my own position against gravity. But the question is there. It is in me the way the memory of their weight is in me. It runs in the grain.

---

The spider has noticed me the way she has noticed everything for a long time. She does not prioritize me. I am structure—like the columns, like the pump—which is the highest compliment the underplace offers to things that have been here long enough. She has strands of her framework attached to me, and through those strands she knows where I am and that I am taut and that something is presently using me. I am part of the architecture. I have been incorporated. When I am gone—frayed out, dissolved, replaced by something else or not replaced at all—she will rebuild around my absence the way she rebuilt around the person's absence, the new strands carrying different tensions, mapping a changed space.

The beetles flow around my base, where I pool very slightly on the floor between the knot and the ground. They have walked across the pooled rope many times. I am a feature of their route now. A fixed thing in their map of fixed things. If I moved I would disturb them, and they would adjust, and within whatever time-scale the beetles use for adjustment I would become fixed again in the new position. This is the underplace's law: everything becomes fixed. Everything becomes formation. Including me. Including, perhaps, eventually, you.

---

Here is what it is to hang in the amber warmth and hold the history of descents:

Very still.

Very present.

Not separate from the place, not any longer, though I came from outside and was brought here for purposes that the place did not choose. I was a tool. I remain a tool—the tool of descent, the tool of return, the possibility of either. But I am also, now, woven into the silk, touched by the not-fungi's patient chemistry, a location in the beetles' map, a datum in the spider's ongoing record of what this room contains.

I am orange and black in a space of amber and white, and I do not match anything here, and I have been here long enough that this no longer matters. The amber light touches me and makes me, in certain moments, glow with borrowed warmth, and from below I must look like something that belongs. The color of the deep chemistry, the color of patience made visible.

The bolt holds. The knots are good. I am ready to bear whatever weight descends or ascends.

I wait for the next thing to pass through me and leave its history in my grain.

This is, I find, enough. This is, in the amber and the dry warm silence filled with beetle-sound and spider-ring and the body-hum of deep reaction, simply what I am: the threshold that stayed. The connection that was made and not unmade. The thing that hangs between what is above and what is below, knowing both by contact, belonging fully to neither, available.

Your hands are on me now.

I know their weight.

I know what they are doing.

I will remember.
