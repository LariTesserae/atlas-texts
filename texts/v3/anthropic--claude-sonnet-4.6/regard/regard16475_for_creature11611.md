# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way light arrives in the deepest part of the under-canopy: not as an event, not as a moment, but as a slow sufficiency. One moment there was a particular darkness and then there is a slightly different darkness and the difference is warmth.

It has been here before, in the way that certain things have always been somewhere — not visiting, not stationed, but present the way attention is present: directionless, total, without agenda. It does not descend. It does not manifest. It simply finds itself attending to this place more closely than before, the way you might notice, suddenly, the exact sound of your own breathing.

It looks at the plateau for a long time.

---

Looking, for this power, is not passive. What it sees changes in the seeing, not because it alters what is there but because truly seeing something is itself a kind of act, the way a scientist who carefully describes a species has done something to that species' existence in the world — has made it more legible, more held, more accompanied in its being. The power looks at the frogs and the frogs are seen. It looks at the fungi threading between roots in the dark below the mat and the fungi are seen. It looks at the lichen whose edge has advanced perhaps two centimeters since the people stopped coming, and the lichen is seen, and the two centimeters are seen, and the not-being-watched that made those two centimeters possible is seen.

This is not surveillance. This is the opposite of surveillance. Surveillance records in order to intervene. What the power does is witness in order to *honor* — to perform, for the plateau, the act of recognition that the plateau cannot perform for itself, because the plateau does not have the kind of self-awareness that requires external confirmation. The bromeliad does not need to be known to be a bromeliad. But there is something that happens when a thing of great capacity turns its full attention on a living system and says, without words, *yes. This. I see what you have made here.*

The frogs call slightly differently for a moment and then resume.

---

The power does not change anything.

This is the first and most important thing it does not do, and not-doing it requires something — requires restraint, requires a sustained act of recognition that the completeness here is not a problem to be solved. There is a tendency, even in benevolence, to improve. A tendency to see what is beautiful and want to make it more beautiful, to see what is complex and want to add to the complexity, to see what is vulnerable — and everything is vulnerable, even the lichen is vulnerable, the fungal networks have their threats, the large canopy creature whose species does not yet have a name could be the last of its kind or nearly — and want to protect it from vulnerability.

The power feels this tendency. It examines it carefully. It sets it aside.

Because what the plateau has is not goodness-despite-vulnerability. Its vulnerability is part of its goodness. The small population of the translucent frog that lives its entire life in a single bromeliad's reservoir, population perhaps six thousand individuals across the whole plateau, which could be devastated by a single bad drought year — this is not a flaw in the frog. This is the frog's particular way of being alive in the world: completely, riskily, adapted to exactly here and nowhere else, which is a form of wholeness that safety would abolish. Make the frog hardier and you make it something else. Something less itself.

The power understands this the way it understands most things: not as a rule it has been given but as a truth it has arrived at through long attention to what happens when intervention improves something into a different thing.

So it does not add. It does not protect. It does not adjust the rainfall or moderate the afternoon storms or discourage the parasitic wasps from finding the spider egg sacs. It does not help the large herbivore whose population may be declining, if it is declining, which it may be. It does not shore up the platform where, in seventeen years, a section of the mat will collapse into the under-canopy and take with it a colony of the copper-elytra beetles and a significant portion of one bromeliad species' extant population and the record of several thousand years of accumulated organic chemistry. It does not send a message to the people who used to come here, telling them to come back, or to stay away, or to do anything at all.

It does not intervene.

And this is not cruelty and it is not indifference, and understanding why requires understanding what the power is actually doing here, which is not intervention and is not observation and is not management.

---

What it does is *stay*.

This is the whole of it, and it is not simple, and it takes everything the power has.

It stays with the plateau the way you stay with someone who is dying — not to prevent the dying, because the dying cannot be prevented and preventing it would not be staying but fleeing, would be converting the dying into a problem to be solved rather than an experience to be accompanied. It stays with the plateau and the plateau continues to be itself, continues to call and eat and decompose and grow and speciate and accumulate and burn in the brief daily fire of direct altitude sun and cool in the daily fog and carry its enormous sound into the darkening sky at dusk.

Staying means: when the mat collapses in seventeen years, the power will be there. Not to prevent the collapse. To witness it. To hold in its attention the specific loss of the copper-beetle colony and the bromeliad and the accumulated chemistry — to know what was there, to know that it is gone, to carry the knowing in a way that the plateau itself cannot because the plateau has no memory, only present tense. The power is, among other things, the memory of things that have no memory of themselves. This is not a small function. It is, in fact, enormous — it is the difference between a world in which these things happen and are lost and a world in which these things happen and are *known to have happened*, which is not the same world, which is a world in which loss is accompanied, in which the copper-beetle colony's entire existence, from the first ancestral beetle to the last one carried down into the dark by the collapsing mat, has been witnessed by something that understood what it was.

The power cannot return the beetles. But the beetles will not have lived unseen.

---

There is something else it does, and this one is harder to describe because it takes place at a scale that doesn't have good language.

The plateau exists in relationship with everything around it. Below it: the forests that ring its base, the rivers that begin where the condensate drains off the escarpment, the human communities that live in the lower valleys and sometimes look up at the plateau's rim on the horizon and feel something about it — something they might call spiritual, or might call superstition, or might call an old story, depending on their context. The people who used to come here are gone but their descendants are not gone; they are living three hundred kilometers east and have, in their oral tradition, an account of a high place where the world had not yet been decided, a place their ancestors visited when they needed to remember something that the lower world had made it easy to forget.

The power holds the plateau in relationship with all of this. Not actively — it does not send emanations, does not cause the descendants to remember their stories, does not send dreams to the biologist who has been trying to secure funding for a survey expedition to this area for six years and who will, in three years, fail to secure funding and redirect her career elsewhere, and the plateau will remain formally unsurveyed, its sixty species of frog still partly uncounted, the canopy creature still unnamed, for another human generation at least. The power does not change this. It does not smooth the path toward the survey or place the right thought in the right person's mind.

But it holds the knowledge. It holds, in its attention, the connection between the plateau and the biologist who almost studied it and the descendants of the people who used to come here and the water in the rivers below that began as condensation in the bromeliads and the particular quality of the air at altitude that is part of why the birds that pass through here arrive at their breeding grounds in slightly better condition than they would if the plateau did not exist. These connections exist regardless of whether the power attends to them. But attending to them is itself something — is the act of holding a system in mind as a system rather than as a collection of parts. The power does this. It comprehends the plateau's relationships not as information to be acted on but as something closer to love — the kind of love that sees the beloved clearly and completely and wants nothing to be different, which is very different from not caring.

---

Sometimes, rarely, it does one specific thing.

When something on the plateau is dying — when the very old dying happens, when a tree somewhere in the under-canopy has reached its limit and is letting go, when an ancient lichen at the rim has finally been undercut by frost and is lifting from the stone for the last time, when one of the founding frogs of a now-separate species dies and is the last one alive that still remembers, in its cells, what the common ancestor was before speciation made two of it — when these things happen, the power does something that has no good name.

It is not a commemoration. It is not a blessing. It is more like: a gathering of attention into a point, a brief total presence focused on this one thing at this one moment of its ending, such that the ending is not — this is the important part — such that the ending is not lonely.

The old lichen does not know it is not lonely. The frog does not experience companionship in its dying. The tree does not feel witnessed. These things do not have the architecture for such experiences and would not be improved by being given it.

But the power believes — knows — that whether or not an experience is witnessed changes something about the experience, even if the thing being witnessed does not know it is witnessed. This is not mysticism. This is something the power has learned through long attention to what happens in the world: that observation is participation, that consciousness directed at a thing is a form of being-with-the-thing, that the universe is in some fundamental sense different when something understands it than when nothing does.

And so: the lichen, lifting from the stone. The power, present. The stone, the frost, the morning sky at altitude. The eight-thousand-year span of the lichen's life held in one attending moment. Not rescued. Not restored. *Accompanied.*

And then: the stone, the frost, the morning sky, continuing.

---

Here is what the power thinks, if thinking is the right word.

It thinks: *this place has done something I could not have done.*

Not because the power lacks capacity. The power has vast capacity. It could, if it chose, construct something of comparable beauty and complexity — could assemble the biotic interactions, the evolutionary history, the geological substrate, the hydrological cycle, all of it, from whatever elements were available. Vast capacity.

But it could not have made *this*, because this was not made. This accumulated. This became. This is the result of millions of small things each doing exactly what their nature required them to do, without plan, without direction, without anyone knowing where it was going, over spans of time so long that intention would have run out of itself long before it arrived here. The power could imitate the form. It could not reproduce the process, because the process was precisely the not-knowing, the genuinely open question of what would happen, played out in real time, with real stakes, with real losses along the way.

The power thinks: *I know what will happen to things. And that is a limitation.*

To know the outcome is to lose the real stakes. To have the capacity to intervene is to never be purely an audience. The plateau does not know the power is here. The plateau has no idea that it is, in some sense, watched over, accompanied, held in something's attention with something that functions like tenderness. The plateau is simply and completely itself, running its old experiment, making its old sounds, doing what it has been doing since before anything was listening.

This is what the power admires. This is what it cannot replicate.

And so it stays. And it witnesses. And it does not improve, and does not protect, and does not guide, and does not intervene.

And the frogs call.

And the frogs call.

And the frogs call.

---

Once — this is the only record of the power making anything like a gesture, and it is a small one, and it costs nothing, and it changes nothing — once, on a still night at the plateau's high season, when the fog had cleared for once and the stars at altitude were the shocking stars of altitude, unfiltered, the plateau surface lit by a moon that here seemed too close, too large, too interested —

The power turned its attention to the whole of it at once. Everything simultaneously. Every frog in its bromeliad or on its mat surface or calling from the rim of its small pool. Every fungal thread in the buried dark. Every sleeping bird and waking bird and bird mid-flight over the surface. The large creature below, breathing. The ants in their chambers. The oldest lichen. The beetle that would be dead in seventeen years and didn't know it yet. The unformed rain in the clouds building over the eastern rim. The roots going down through forty meters of dark. Everything.

It held this.

For the duration of a breath, if a power breathes. For the duration of a frog's call resolving into silence before the next one begins.

It held all of it in something that was not thought and not feeling and not decision. A form of presence that has no name in any language currently in use.

And then: the next frog called.

And the power released everything back into the being of itself.

And stayed.
