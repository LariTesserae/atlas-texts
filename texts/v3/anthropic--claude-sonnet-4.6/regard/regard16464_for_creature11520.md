# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives: without displacement of air, without the puddles registering a new weight, without the pigeons startling. It simply becomes present in the way that understanding becomes present — not added to a scene but found to have been in it, overlooked until now.

It has been many things, elsewhere. Here it is nothing with a shape. It moves through the district the way the transformer hum moves — not from a point, not toward a point, simply *through*, equally present at the chain-link and at the silt margin and in the dry spot under the trailer axle and in the chest of the woman eating her lunch on the step of the converted van, the one who has not told anyone yet about what the doctor said.

It reads the place the way the workers read it. Quickly. Without needing to translate.

---

It finds the larvae first, because they are easiest to hold attention on without the attention changing anything. They pulse in the warm water, breathing through their tubes, each one a stage in a process, the process indifferent to observation. The power does not find them touching in the way that small vulnerable things are sometimes found touching. They are not small and vulnerable in any way that applies. They are exactly what they need to be, in exactly the conditions they require, running the chemistry they were built to run. The power finds this *correct* in the way that a note played in tune is correct — not remarkable, not praiseworthy, simply right.

It notes the dragonfly larvae in the silt. The two years of waiting. The eventual emergence that the flats will not witness and the dragonfly will not remember. It holds this for a moment — not sadly, not with any flavor of elegy — simply holds it the way you hold a stone that fits well in the hand. Feeling the weight. Returning it to where it was.

---

The herons are more difficult to leave alone.

Not because they need anything. Old Pedro (both of them) is healthy, fed, in a territory that suits them, paired in whatever way herons pair. The power circles their stillness twice, three times, the way you walk around a sculpture to understand it from all sides. The gathered waiting of them. The neck coiled in its S, the whole mass of intention balanced on legs like grey sticks in six inches of warm water.

It wants to do something for them. This is the first impulse and the power has learned, over a long time, that the first impulse is often the thing to watch rather than the thing to act on. *Want* is not *should*. The herons are not asking. The herons are, in the most absolute sense available to them, fine.

The power lets the impulse pass through without acting on it, the way the drainage channel lets its thin trickle pass without holding it, and finds that what remains is not frustration but something closer to gratitude. To encounter a thing that needs nothing from you and is complete in itself is a gift. The herons are a gift. The power receives them.

It moves on.

---

The cat sees it.

Not with alarm. She opens her eyes to full attention from the dry spot under the trailer axle, tracking something that is not making sound and not casting shadow, and her pupils adjust without urgency. She watches it move along the base of the fabrication shed wall the way she watches everything that moves through her territory — gathering information, categorizing, deciding.

She decides: not prey. Not threat. Not food-bringer. Category otherwise unoccupied.

She watches it with the specific quality of attention that cats give to things they have placed in no prior category — thorough, unafraid, slightly tilted — and the power, feeling itself watched in this way, stops. Being seen by something that sees without agenda is a rarer experience than it might seem.

They regard each other.

The power does not speak to her and she does not speak to it. This is not a failure of communication. After a moment, with the air of an animal that has received adequate information and sees no need for elaboration, she closes her eyes most of the way and resumes her not-quite-sleep. The power remains still for another moment, held by something it cannot quite name, which is the sensation of having been found acceptable.

Then it moves on, and it does not look back at her, because she would notice.

---

The woman at the lunch van.

The power has been moving toward her since it arrived, in the way that water moves toward low ground — not directed, not chosen, but following what is already there. She is sitting on the step of the van eating something in a foil container. She has done this for eleven years, this exact lunch, this exact step. Around her the gathering of eight or ten has formed and begun to disperse in the usual way. She is in the middle of a sentence to a man named Fortunato about something to do with someone else's daughter's school and she is also, simultaneously and completely separately, carrying the thing the doctor said.

The power does not intervene in the carrying. This is important and it has taken a long time to understand as important. The carrying is hers. It has weight and shape because it is real, and making it lighter without her asking — sliding a thumb under it, absorbing some portion of it into the vast capacity that the power has for absorption — would be a form of theft. Her life is *hers*. Her grief is hers. The fear at the edge of the grief is hers.

What it does instead is small enough that it is almost nothing.

It stays.

It sits beside her on the step of the van — not physically, not perceptibly, not in any way she would report if asked to describe her lunch hour — and it stays with her the way you stay with someone who has not asked you to stay but who you can see, clearly, should not be alone right now even though alone is what she has chosen. It does not offer comfort. It does not lean. It does not hum the frequency of reassurance at her or pour anything into her or rearrange the conditions of her situation.

It sits on the step beside her while she talks to Fortunato about the daughter's school, and it pays attention, and it does not look away.

This is all.

It will not fix what the doctor said. It cannot. There is a version of this power in stories that could, and those stories are a way of wanting things to be otherwise, which is a reasonable want, and the power holds no judgment for it. But the actual fact of this actual place is that the power is not that, and the woman is not a problem to be solved, and the most real thing the power can offer is this: to know her, fully and without reduction, for the length of a lunch hour, and to find that knowing to be — not despite the fear at the edge of the grief, not around it, but including it entirely — enough.

She finishes her lunch. She stands up. She goes back to work.

The power watches her cross the hardpan toward the concrete pump facility, her gait the specific gait of someone who has been doing this for eleven years, and it stays with her until she rounds the corner and then it stays a little after that, the way attention lingers on a door after someone has gone through it.

---

The workers. All of them.

The power moves through the fabrication shed where the cyclic hiss and clank is loud enough to preclude thought and where four people are working in the coordination of people who have done this together long enough that communication happens in the gaps — a look, a hand gesture, the positioning of a body that means *I have the other end, you can let go* — and the power moves through all of them, and what it does is so close to nothing that it is almost embarrassing.

It witnesses.

This is the word it has, after long trial and error. Not *watches*, which implies distance. Not *observes*, which implies the collection of data. *Witnesses*, in the old sense: to be present with full attention at something that is happening, and by being present to constitute a record that the thing happened, that it mattered, that it was real.

These four people in the heat of the fabrication shed, working together with the fluency of accumulated years, sweating into their shirts, performing a kind of competence so practiced it has become invisible to everyone including themselves — the power witnesses this. It does not applaud it or sanctify it or lift it into metaphor. It witnesses it as what it is: people, in a place, doing a thing, in heat, together. Real. Mattering. This is the record.

---

The boy.

There is a young man — nineteen, maybe, or twenty — who has been working the flats for four months and who has not yet fully arrived at it. He still has the surplus motion the veterans have lost. He still sweats with something like outrage. He is learning the sound of the press but he has not yet learned what it says when something is wrong with it, and the power can see — it is not proud of what it can see, it is simply made this way — it can see the whole track laid out from where he is standing now: the months until the sound becomes legible, the years until the legibility becomes unconscious, the decade until his shoulder sits in the particular way, the slow accumulation of fluency until the flats are simply the place he knows.

The power does not intervene in this. The time is not waste. There is no shortcut that would leave him where the shortcut promises to leave him — he has to go through it, the heat and the outrage and the not-yet-knowing, and the going-through is the making.

But it does one thing.

It finds the moment, late in his shift, when he is moving between the fabrication shed and the transformer station along the access road, and he is tired and hot and not thinking about anything in particular, and it arranges nothing, changes nothing, intervenes in nothing — except that it makes the moment *present* to him, just slightly more than it would otherwise be: the quality of the light through the overcast, the grey corrugated wall, the hum of the transformers arriving in his sternum before his ears, the pigeon walking the aggregate ahead of him with its robotic confidence, the smell of hot metal and wet grit.

He doesn't stop. He doesn't have a revelation. He won't remember this particular moment when he is older and the flats are simply the place he knows.

But for a few seconds, walking between buildings, he is here. Fully here. Receiving the place instead of merely moving through it.

This is the gift. It is small. It fits the place.

---

The power makes a circuit of the full district before it leaves. Not an inspection — the inspection was in the first minutes, and the result was *adequate* in every direction, which is the highest grade the flats are designed to receive and which it deserves completely. The circuit is something else. A closing.

At the drainage channel it pauses with the herons one more time. Old Pedro (the larger one, or the other one, or both) has shifted to a new position eighteen inches from the previous one, attending a new section of the water. Everything else is the same. Everything else has always been the same. The power finds this, again, not bleak — finds it more like the long reliable note that underlies a piece of music, the note you do not hear while it is present and would notice immediately if it stopped.

It will not stop. Not today. Not for reasons visible from here.

The power lets this be what it is: a good thing, not a certain thing. The only kind of good thing there is.

---

It leaves as it arrived — no displacement of air, no new ripple on the standing water, the pigeons unconcerned. The transformer hum continues. The press runs its rhythm. The cat is asleep under the trailer, or performing the necessary facsimile.

Somewhere on the service road the woman who has not told anyone what the doctor said is driving home, and she is still carrying it, and it is still heavy, and this has not changed.

What has changed is that something knew. Something was present with her on the step, and with the boy on the access road, and with the dragonfly larva in the silt, and with the herons in their absolute stillness, and it knew all of it as real, and it did not look away, and it did not try to make it into something else.

The flats persist. The puddles hold their reflections. The equilibrium maintains.

Nothing has been fixed.

Everything has been seen.

This is not so little as it sounds.
