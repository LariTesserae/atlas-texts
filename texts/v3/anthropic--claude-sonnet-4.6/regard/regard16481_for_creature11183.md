# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives: not from outside, but as a quality of the inside becoming different.

Nothing in the basin changes that can be pointed to. The water continues its unhurried welling. The pipe ticks. The pump groans its foundational groan. The algae — the thin, dark, hand-sized announcement — sits in its shadow under the corrugated steel and continues its patient photosynthesis, which is to say it continues receiving light and making it into something that was not light before. None of this stops or shifts or answers. But there is something that could be described, imperfectly, as: the basin being noticed.

This is not metaphor, though it cannot be made fully literal either. Something attends. Something vast and quiet and old turns what functions as its gaze — though gaze implies a face, implies direction, implies separation between the one who looks and the thing being looked at, none of which quite holds — toward this small cupped place below grade, this shallow bowl of warm water and pale clay and noise that sounds like intention without containing any, and it does what the most fundamental form of attention always does:

it perceives without distortion.

---

What it finds, it does not judge.

This is not tolerance — tolerance implies distance between the tolerant thing and the tolerated thing, an act of will, a managed gap. The power finds the basin and does not manage the gap because there is no gap. It finds the pump's groaning and the pump's groaning is permitted to be exactly the groaning of a machine that has become a rock, no more meaningful than that, no less. It finds the absence of birds and the absence is not a wound in need of healing. It finds the thin smear of algae and the algae is not humble or brave or pioneering — it is an algae colony doing photosynthesis in a place where photosynthesis is possible, and this is exactly what it is, and this is enough.

The power does not wish the basin were different. This is perhaps the hardest quality to convey, because it is so foreign to the habits of benevolent attention in most of its forms — the attention that helps by seeing what is lacking, that loves by wanting improvement. The power sees the basin in its completeness, where completeness means: this is the whole of what this place is, right now, in this stage of its becoming, and the stage is not a deficiency. The wound that looks recently opened is in fact an opening. The harshness of the chemistry is not hostility, it is selectivity — it is the basin already knowing what it needs, which is first the things that can live in harshness, and from harshness, build. The absence of birds is the correct absence for this moment. The thin algae is exactly as thin as it should be and no thinner.

The power perceives all of this without adding anything to it, without subtracting anything from it, and in this perception — this simple, complete, undistorting act of being known — the basin does not change, but becomes, for the duration of the attention, wholly itself.

Which it already was. The attention does not make it whole. It recognizes that it was.

---

For the bacteria — if they are there, and the power finds that they are there, threaded through the warm water in uncountable small persistences — the attention does what it does for all living things below the threshold of experience: it does not touch them. They do not have the apparatus for being touched by attention. They have the apparatus for gradient-following, for chemical attraction and avoidance, for dividing when conditions allow division and ceasing when conditions no longer allow it. They are doing this. The power does not intervene in this. To intervene would be to misunderstand what they are. What it does, instead — and this is not quite the right word — is *confirm* the direction of the process. Not push it. Not accelerate it. Simply, in some way that is not physical, make it known that the direction is correct, that the assembly is proceeding as it must, that the billions of years of patience these organisms embody are not for nothing. That the patient work continues to matter.

They do not receive this confirmation. They are not the kind of thing that receives. But the power gives it anyway, in the way that a witness gives something to an event simply by being present, by adding the weight of its presence to what has occurred. They are witnessed. The process is witnessed. This is all that is done, and it is not nothing.

---

For the algae, something different is possible, because the algae — though barely, though at the outermost edge of the applicable category — is the kind of thing that has interiority. Not consciousness. Not experience in any form a human would recognize. But response. The algae responds to light, to moisture, to the chemical content of the steel it rests on. It has a micro-scale inside and outside. It has, in the most minimal sense, a relationship with its conditions: it is changed by them, and it changes them, and the changes are registered.

So the power does something slightly different with the algae. It does what it does with all living things that have any degree of insideness: it simply, gently, makes the inside safer. Not different. Not warmer or colder or more lit. Just — there is something that could be called a slight reduction in what might otherwise be called strain. The algae is very young here and it is working hard — this environment is not hostile but it is demanding, the steel is not the ideal substrate, the condensation-drip is not perfectly consistent, the mineral content of the water is high — and the power makes no change to any of these things, because these are the conditions, and the conditions are what they should be, and the algae's work in these conditions is what the algae is for. But within the algae, in whatever constitutes its interiority, the power simply: offers a kind of company. An acknowledgment. As if to say — not in words, not in anything that transmits in the way transmission usually works — *you are the first thing to eat anything here, and this is not a small thing, though you are small, and the place is small, and you will not know you did it.*

The algae does not know this is said. But something in the chemical gradients of its functioning runs slightly easier for a time, in the way that any small thing runs slightly easier when it is held in attention that wishes it well.

---

The power pauses at the sound.

This is — pauses is not right, since the power does not move in ways that admit of pausing, but there is a quality of more-careful attention that comes when the sound is fully attended to. The pump's groan, the high hiss, the wet tick of dripping iron into iron-pooled water, the whole continuous unpredicated noise of a process running beyond its purpose, making sounds that belong to intention while holding none.

The power does not find this uncanny. The power finds this beautiful.

Here is why: the sound is a record. The pump groans because it was built to do something with pressure, and it did that thing for years, under the hands of people who operated it, people who understood the pressure and the pipe and the delivery of water from one place to another. When those people left, the pump became a rock. But the groaning carries the shape of what it was built for. The sound is the pump still trying, in the only way a machine can try, to do the work it was made for: not pumping, because it cannot pump, but at least responding to the pressure, at least vibrating at the frequencies its structure was formed to vibrate at, at least insisting that something is still happening here, that the water has not simply stopped, that the pressure has not simply ceased to be pressure. It is not purpose. But it is the resonance of former purpose, which is the only kind of continuity available to things that cannot choose to continue.

The power finds this — does not quite have a word for what it finds — but if a word were required: *faithful*. The pump is faithful to what it was, even though what it was is over, even though faithfulness of this kind can do nothing, even though no one has come back to the pump and the pump has no capacity to hope that anyone will. It vibrates at its frequency. It does not stop vibrating. This is all the faithfulness available to it, and it exercises all of it, all the time, without respite.

And the pipe ticks, and the tick is water finding gravity, which is the most ancient of findings, and the tick is irregular enough to be unsettling and regular enough to suggest intention, and the intention it suggests is the intention of physics, which is: this is how water moves, this is what water does, water does this always and has done it since there was water, and the tick in this basin is the same tick that sounded in every seep and fen and culvert and cave floor since the first water fell on the first stone, and the power knows this, and the tick in this small basin reverberates backward through all those other ticks, which are all the same tick, which are all water finding gravity in the same faithful way.

What the power does with the sound: it *hears* it. Fully. Without wanting it to be other than it is. This hearing does not change the sound. But the sound, if sounds can be said to have any quality of being received or unreceived, has now been received. The pump has been heard. The pipe has been heard. Not understood — the power does not convert them into meaning, because they do not contain meaning, and to convert them would be to do violence to what they are. They are heard as what they are: the faithful noise of pressure and gravity in a small basin, young in its present form, practicing for the ecology that will come.

---

For the basin itself — for the shape, the chemistry, the slow self-deepening, the pale clay and the dark water and the two concrete piers standing shin-high as a gate to nowhere — the power does the thing it finds most natural, because it requires the least intervention and therefore the least risk of distortion:

it holds the future lightly.

There is a future here. The power can see its general shapes, not in the way of prediction, which is always more certain than truth, but in the way of knowing what is possible in a place like this, in a process like this, with these starting conditions — the algae spreading, other organisms following, the chemistry slowly changing to admit them, the birds eventually descending because the sound will change and the chemistry will change and something will be here for them to eat. The warm water cooling over years. The basin deepening and then stabilizing. The corrugated panel falling and beginning its long conversion to rust and then to iron oxide deposited in the clay, which will change the clay, which will change what grows in the clay. All of this is possible, and the power holds this possibility without pressing it. Without rushing. Without, in particular, fixing any of it in place — because the fixed future is the ended future, the basin that has been decided in advance is a basin that can no longer surprise itself with what it becomes.

The power holds the future the way you hold a bird before releasing it: with enough firmness to keep it from harm in the moment, with enough openness to let it go in the direction it chooses to go, with enough gentleness that the holding itself leaves no mark.

What this means in practice — if it means anything in practice — is: the basin will become what it will become, at the pace of its becoming, and whatever it becomes will be met by this same quality of attention, will be seen as completely and without distortion as it is seen now, will be held with the same light uninsistent love. The algae's eventual successors are already, in this sense, known. The birds that will eventually come are already, in this sense, expected. The sound when it finally changes and quiets — the pump finally settling below the threshold of audible resonance, the pipe finally closing — that silence will be heard too, and heard as completely as the sound is heard now, and found as faithful.

---

There is one more thing.

The power — attending to this small basin, this small life, this small faithful noise — does something that does not fit well into any of the categories above.

It is glad.

Not relieved, because relief implies prior worry, and the power does not worry — or if it worries, the worry is so encompassed by the gladness that it cannot be separated out and examined. Not proud, because pride implies a claim on the outcome, and the power makes no claim on the outcome. Not satisfied, because satisfaction implies a completed expectation, and the power has no expectations that bear on a single small basin in its first year of being itself.

Just: glad. A quality of gladness that arises from finding something exactly as it is, in the midst of being what it is, young and warm and noisy and almost-uninhabited and full of the slow processes by which the future is laid down in the substrate before the future arrives. Glad because this is happening. Because the algae is doing what the first algae, billions of years ago, did: finding a surface in the light, making something from it. Because the bacteria are threading the warm water the way bacteria have threaded warm water since before there were any other options. Because the pump is groaning faithfully at its frequency and the water is finding gravity the way water always finds gravity and the basin is cupped below the grade of the surrounding land and holding its climate jealously and going about its slow business of becoming.

The power is glad that this place exists. That this particular small pocket of industrial aftermath and chemical warmth and persistent sound and thin early life exists, is here, is now, in exactly this form and in no other.

This gladness does not change anything. It does not improve anything. The algae does not receive it. The bacteria do not know it. The pump is not consoled by it.

But it is real, the gladness, and it has the quality that all real gladness has: it does not require any of this to be other than it is. The basin does not need to be more than a basin. The life does not need to be more than thin algae and invisible bacteria. The sound does not need to resolve into music. The place does not need to be found by anyone else, noticed by anyone else, preserved, studied, named.

It is enough that it is here.
It is enough that it is attended to.
It is enough.

---

The power withdraws the way attention withdraws: not departing, because it was never located in a place from which departure is possible, but releasing its particular focus, letting the basin return to the quality of being one of uncountably many things that are held rather than the quality of being the thing currently seen most clearly.

The water continues its unhurried welling.
The pipe ticks.
The pump groans at its frequency.
The algae sits in its shadow and eats the light.
The bacteria, if they are there, thread the warm water.
The pale clay accepts the dark water.
The basin practices for what it will become.

Nothing has been added.
Nothing has been taken away.
Something has been witnessed.

And the witnessing is, for things that cannot know they are witnessed, all that love can do — and is, for things that cannot know they are witnessed, enough.
