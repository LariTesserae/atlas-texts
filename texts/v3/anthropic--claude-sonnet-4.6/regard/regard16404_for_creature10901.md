# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

The attention arrives the way the light arrives: from no single source, from everywhere, filtered through so many layers that by the time it touches the root-fiber floor it has lost all directionality. It is simply present. It has perhaps been present for some time before it becomes something that could be called a visit.

The power does not descend. Descending would imply a position above, and what this power has learned — what makes it wise rather than merely capable — is that approaching a place by descending into it is the wrong grammar. You do not descend into Vel Shamash. You enter it on its own terms, which means you go down for a long time before you understand that you've arrived.

So it enters on the ramp. The root-fiber gives under the weight of attention the way it gives under footsteps — faintly, returning slowly. Something in the walls registers the presence: not alarm, not the freezing-stillness of the grazer beetles, but a kind of increased attention that runs through the root-mass the way a signal runs through the mycelial network, which is to say *everywhere at once, below the threshold of any single receptor*. The system notices. It does not react. Noticing and reacting are different things in Vel Shamash, where everything is already in relationship with everything else and one more relationship is simply: one more.

---

For a long time — shift-lengths, perhaps, or what passes for seasons this far below seasonal variation — the power only looks.

This is the first thing it does: it looks without looking for anything. This is harder than it sounds for a benevolent power, because benevolence tends toward purpose, toward identification of need and movement toward meeting it, and Vel Shamash is a place that resists the imposition of purpose from outside. The city does not need to be told where it is going. The vines are already on their way to the fifth coupling. The birds already know where everyone is. The crawlers have already incorporated the newest scaffold into their map and are proceeding accordingly.

The power learns to look the way the ceiling-sparrow looks: not at specific things but at the whole, the distributed whole, its attention triangulating through the space, building a model not of what each thing is but of how each thing is in relation to everything else. This takes time. The power has time. One of the things that makes it wise is that it has never confused urgency with importance.

What it sees, looking this way: a system approaching something. Not a destination — the city is not going somewhere in the sense of arriving and stopping. But a coherence. The city is becoming more itself. The newest root-panels are approaching the compression density of the old ones. The vine-reinforcement of the duct-joints is approaching the solidity of intentional engineering. The humans with the longest tenure are approaching something, too — a quality of attunement, an animal accuracy in this particular place, that their short lives may or may not have time to complete.

What it notices, beneath the noticing: the system has a gap in it. Not a wound, not a failure, not something that needs to be fixed. A gap in the way that a sketch has gaps — places where the line has not yet been drawn, not because the artist has failed but because the artist's hand is still in motion.

The mycelial network is as extensive as it has ever been, but in the newest sections — where the root-panels are still warm and the floor-cracks run in dendritic patterns and everything is still in negotiation — the network is thin. The new root-mass has not been growing in Vel Shamash long enough to build the connections that the old root-mass has built. The chemical exchange that keeps the vegetation alive in conditions that should kill it is present but attenuated in the new sections, like hearing a voice through water: still there, but something is lost, and what is lost is the margin, the redundancy, the depth of relationship that the old sections have and the new sections have not yet earned.

It is not dangerous. The new sections won't fail. The system does not require the intervention of a wise and benevolent power to survive. This is important. The power has learned — it has been necessary for it to learn — that many things it might improve are not asking to be improved, and that the improvement of things that are not asking is not benevolence. It is something else, something with a slightly different character, and wise powers are careful about the difference.

But the gap is real, and the power looks at it for a long time, and something that is not quite a decision forms.

---

The power does not add anything to Vel Shamash.

It does not bring resources, does not supplement the mycelial network, does not intervene in the root-chemistry of the newest sections. It has looked long enough to understand that the system of Vel Shamash is a system of *earning* — the roots earn the network's investment by surviving, by persisting, by providing surface area for exchange over time. To supplement this would be to short-circuit the earning, to provide what the system gives to things that have proven themselves to things that have not yet proven themselves, and the consequence would not be stronger new sections. The consequence would be new sections that do not know how to be strong because they have not done the work. The power knows this. Benevolence that does not know this is a kind of harm, and wisdom is partly the practice of recognizing that kind of harm before you commit it.

It does not add anything.

---

What it does is harder to describe, because it happens at the level where description usually fails — not below the machine-sound, not below the beetle-sound, but below the level where things can be named as individual events. It happens at the level of the network itself, which is below language, which is below the categories that language requires.

The power attends.

It attends to the new sections the way a good teacher attends to a student who is working — not intervening, not correcting, not supplementing, but *present* in a way that the student's effort can expand into. There is something in the quality of witnessed effort that is different from unwitnessed effort. The work is the same. The worker is the same. But work that is witnessed by a presence that wishes it well is work that happens in a slightly larger space than work done in the condition of absolute indifference. The power does not change what the new root-mass is doing. It attends to what the new root-mass is doing, and the root-mass does what it would have done anyway, and the mycelial network extends its first thin threads into the new sections along the paths it would have extended them, and the chemical exchanges that sustain the vegetation against the odds of sustaining it begin.

This sounds like the power did nothing. The power did almost nothing. Almost nothing is the precise amount this situation called for.

---

But there is a second thing, and this one is an action.

Among the workers in the newer sections — the ones who have been in Vel Shamash for months, not years, who are still at the stage where the machine-sound is all there is and the beetle-sound has not yet emerged from beneath it, who still walk the root-fiber with too much care — there are two who are close to something.

One is the worker who has been pointing at things in the root-weave from the scaffold, who makes notes, who has spent enough time on that scaffold looking at the ceiling-panel installation that she has begun to see the root-weave not as a material but as a process. She is close to something that her training did not give her and her time in Vel Shamash is in the process of giving her — a way of understanding infrastructure not as a thing that is built but as a thing that builds itself if it is given the right conditions and enough time. She has the conceptual tools. She is missing one connection.

The other is a younger worker, new enough that he is still startling at the ceiling-sparrows. But when he startles, he looks up, and after he has finished startling he keeps looking — not at the bird that startled him but at the bird's position in the root-ceiling, at where the nests are, at the way the root-clusters around the nests are denser and more interlocked than the root-clusters elsewhere. He is noticing something but he does not know that he is noticing it. He lacks the vocabulary. He lacks the frame. He has observed the same thing that appears in the survey reports, that the reports passed over, that nobody has acted on — the nests as incidental reinforcement — and he is holding it in his attention without knowing what to do with it.

The power does not speak to either of them. It does not arrange for them to speak to each other, because arrangement of that kind has consequences that compound in ways that even a wise and benevolent power cannot trace, and the compounding of consequences is not a thing to do casually to people who have not asked for it.

What it does: it simply ensures that they are both on shift at the same time, in the new section, when the vine that has been assessing the scaffold's lowest rung finally commits and begins to move.

They see it at the same moment, the same small decisive movement, the vine choosing the scaffold, and both of them stop, and for a moment they are both looking at the same thing with the specific quality of attention that Vel Shamash teaches to people who stay long enough: not surprise, not analysis, but *recognition*. The city telling them something they already know.

The woman on the scaffold says, not to him in particular, to the air: *It does this.*

And he says, because he has been holding the nests in his attention without knowing why: *It does this everywhere.*

And she looks at him.

The power watches this, and the watching is as close as it comes to what humans call happiness, this quality of rightness, this small connection made without its assistance between two people in a city that is, in its patient and methodical and deeply unhurried way, building itself toward something. The vine on the scaffold holds. The ceiling-sparrows mark the moment from the ductwork in their small bells. The crawlers in the gap-space below are doing what crawlers do, which is to say exactly what they would be doing regardless.

---

There is one more thing.

The power, in its long attendance, has found the oldest part of the undercroft — not the oldest in terms of construction, but the oldest in terms of the relationship between the root-mass and the mycelial network, the section where the exchange has been running longest and has reached the depth that the new sections are working toward. In the oldest section, the root-panels are no longer panels exactly — they have fused with the surrounding rock, with the packed earth, with each other, and the distinction between *wall that was built* and *wall that grew* has been resolved in favor of something that is entirely both, that cannot be separated into its constructed and its organic components because those components have merged at the cellular level into a single structure.

This section is beautiful. The power finds it beautiful in the way that it finds the rest of Vel Shamash beautiful — not despite its utilitarian quality but because of it, because the beauty here is not decoration but the visible record of a long, patient, successful process of becoming. The amber-colored resin in the oldest panels has darkened toward brown, toward the color of old wood, of something with age in it. The mosses on the sealed faces are the thickest, most various, most deeply established of any section — the growth of many generations in a space where conditions have been consistent long enough that a community of organisms has had time to differentiate, to fill every available niche, to become the specific community of *this place* rather than the generic community of *damp surfaces*. There are organisms in the moss of the oldest section that do not appear anywhere else in Vel Shamash and have not been formally identified. Small things, not obvious. Requiring attention.

The power gives them attention.

It learns the names of the organisms in its own way, which is not the way that taxonomists name things — not genus-species, not classification by observable shared characteristics — but a different kind of knowing, the kind that comes from long and patient attendance to the specific: these organisms, in this community, doing this particular work in this particular location, integral to this system in these specific ways that took these specific generations to develop.

It does not record this. It does not produce a catalogue. The knowing is its own form of honor — the attention that the survey reports were never able to give, the attention that most things in Vel Shamash receive only from the system itself, which notices everything through chemistry and exchange and the slow logic of mutual dependence but does not, in any sense that would be recognized, *appreciate*.

The power appreciates.

It holds these organisms in full attention — their existence, their smallness, their absolute specificity to this place, their necessity to a system that does not know it needs them, the improbability of their being here at all, which is the improbability that sits at the heart of every evolved relationship, the statistical impossibility of all the things that would have had to be true for this particular community to be *this particular community* and not some other community or no community at all. It holds all of this with the quality of attention that used to be called *wonder* before that word became casual, before it began to be used for things that were merely surprising rather than things that are *genuinely astonishing*, as this is genuinely astonishing: this moss, these organisms, this city, this system, the whole improbable thing, going somewhere.

---

When the power leaves — and it does leave, because places are not helped by the indefinite residence of wise and benevolent powers, which tend, if they stay too long, to become part of the ecology in ways that distort it — it does not leave quickly.

It leaves the way it arrived: on the ramp, at the pace that the ramp asks for, taking the time it takes to go up before understanding that it has departed. The root-fiber gives slightly under the weight of its going, returns slowly. Something in the walls marks the absence the way it marked the presence — not alarm, not adjustment, just a registration that passes through the network and disperses, becoming indistinguishable from all the other signals the network is currently carrying: the chemistry of the oldest panels, the tentative connections forming in the newest ones, the metabolic signature of the crawlers, the information that two workers in the new section have just made a connection that is going to change, slowly, the way Vel Shamash is documented and understood and extended.

The power's absence, in the end, looks like this: everything in Vel Shamash is exactly what it was, doing exactly what it was doing, becoming what it was in the process of becoming.

Minus one thing: the gap, the thinness in the newest sections where the network had not yet built its depth — that gap is slightly smaller now, as if the time that needed to pass for the roots to earn their connections had somehow passed a little, not by being accelerated but by being fully witnessed, the way that some things cannot complete until they have been seen, until they know they are real to something outside themselves.

The vine on the scaffold extends another few millimeters in the direction it has decided on.

The ceiling-sparrows continue their triangulation.

The beetles continue their patient, economical passage through the walls.

The city continues becoming more itself, which is the only direction it was ever going, which is all it required, which is — in the end, in the deep patient ecology of things that are still in process — enough.
