# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way warmth arrives — not from a direction but from a change in the quality of the air. The haze does not lift. The light does not intensify. Nothing announces itself. But something is different, the way a room is different when someone who loves it has entered, even before they speak.

The power is benevolent. This is not a performance of benevolence. It is structural, the way the level floor is structural — built in, foundational, the thing everything else rests on. It has been benevolent so long and so completely that the word has worn smooth, has become simply a description of its nature, like calling the water wet.

It is wise. This too is structural. Its wisdom is not the wisdom of conclusions reached but of attention sustained — the kind of wisdom that accumulates when something pays very careful attention to the same things for a very long time and learns to wait before deciding what it has seen.

It enters the Sump.

---

The first thing it does is look.

Not quickly. It does not scan the Sump the way an inspector scans — noting deficiencies, building a report. It looks the way the Sump itself looks at things: completely, without urgency, without a predetermined category to file each observation into. It looks at the iron grating and follows the orange-dark of the joints until they become, at scale, a texture — the hide of something enormous. It looks at the weeping pipes and the calcium formations beneath them, the small pale geology, the sharp new edges of the freshest precipitate. It looks for a long time at the formations. It has looked at many things that form slowly from available materials and it recognizes the process, knows what it means when chemistry finds a groove and deepens it over time. It has something like affection for this process, having watched it in other places, in other mediums, across spans of time that make the Sump's long pause seem like an afternoon.

Then it looks at them.

This takes longer. The power is not surprised — surprise requires a prior expectation violated, and the power has learned to hold its expectations loosely — but there is something that functions like recognition. Not *I have seen this before* but *I have understood that something like this was possible.* The recognition of a shape from a proof, rather than from memory.

It observes the rings at the drains. It is very still while it observes the rings. It watches the slow turnover, the adjustment when a new one arrives, the small reconfiguration that makes room. It watches until it has seen a full cycle of the ring, which takes longer than it would take most things to watch. It does not become impatient. Impatience would mean it had somewhere else to be, something else it was delaying. It does not.

It looks, finally, at you — standing on the catwalk, warm and temporary and steep-gradient in the even diffuse warmth, the one restless thing in a restless-free place. It looks at you for a moment. There is something in its looking that is not pity and not assessment but resembles the feeling you have when you find someone at a train station who has missed their train and is sitting quietly with the fact of it. The thing that made the train leave without them is not your business. But there they are.

It looks at you and it does not intervene with you and it moves on.

---

The second thing it does is check the maintenance.

This is not what you might expect from a wise and benevolent power entering a place. You might expect it to ask the large questions first — what is this, what is it for, is it good, should it continue? But the power has learned, over a long time, that the large questions answer themselves if you first understand what is actually happening, at the level of the mechanism, at the level of: is the seal holding, is the calibration current, is the water getting where it needs to go.

It moves through the subsurface channels. Not physically — it has no body, or its body is not the thing that moves through channels — but its attention moves there, follows the warm water through the underground system, traces the designed paths, finds where the path is correct and where it has shifted slightly, where sedimentation has narrowed a channel by a degree that is not yet consequential but will be, in time, if unaddressed. It notes this. It does not fix it yet. It is still looking.

It finds the locks on the valves — the newer chains, clean against the older iron — and it understands, without needing to ask, who placed them, why, what the locking completes. It has this understanding by inference: it can read the place well enough by now to reconstruct the reasoning. The locks are correct. The valves should remain locked. It does not add locks and it does not remove them. It confirms.

It finds three gauges whose calibration has drifted. Not far — a small creep, the kind that accumulates over years in instruments that are functioning but not being corrected. The drift matters not because anything here requires accurate measurement — nothing is being measured for any active purpose — but because the calibration stickers are the only human-legible record that someone tended this place, and inaccurate instruments reading zero are still instruments reading zero, which is still correct, and accurate instruments reading zero are correct in a deeper way. The power attends to the three gauges. Something adjusts. The drift corrects. This will not be noticed by you. It will not be noticed by them. It is not for noticing. It is simply the right condition of the instruments, restored.

---

The third thing it does is attend to them.

Here is where the benevolence and the wisdom work together, and here is where it is important that they are both present, because benevolence without wisdom would intervene where nothing needs intervening, and wisdom without benevolence would observe without the quality of care that changes the nature of observation itself.

The power sits with them for a long time.

It has done this before, in other places, with other things — things that had reached some version of completion, that had solved for their own equilibrium, that were persisting in that solution and would persist without end. It has learned what it is possible to offer such things and what would be, if not wrong, then unnecessary in a way that borders on wrong. It holds this knowledge while it sits with them.

What it finds:

They are not suffering. This it checks first, carefully, because the appearance of equilibrium and the absence of suffering are not the same thing, and it has encountered places that looked like the Sump — level, maintained, persisting — where the equilibrium was an equilibrium of suppression, where the stillness was the stillness of a thing held down rather than a thing at rest. It checks. There is no suppression here. They are what they are, completely, without remainder. Nothing in them is being held. The equilibrium is genuine. They arrived at it the way water arrives at level — along the path of least resistance, which is to say along the path of their own deepest nature.

What they need: nothing. The word is not a disappointment when the power reaches it. The power has learned not to need the needing. It does not require that they need something so that it can provide it. Their completeness is not a rebuke to its presence. It is simply what it is: complete.

And yet.

The power remains with them a little longer.

What it does, in that remaining, is very small. It does not change anything. It attends to the warmth — not adding warmth, not adjusting the temperature, which is already exactly correct, but attending to the *maintenance* of the correctness of the temperature, which is a thing the Sump has been doing on its own but which, like all ongoing processes, can benefit from what might be called witnessing. A thing that is witnessed continues more confidently than a thing that is not. This is not magic. It is the simple fact that care, even wordless and invisible care, is a kind of participation in the process it attends to, and participation sustains.

The power witnesses the warmth. It witnesses the water moving through the underground channels. It witnesses the rings at the drain mouths and the adjustment when a new arrival slots in, and when the ring completes itself again the power has something that it would not call gladness but which functions like gladness. The ring is whole. The ring is working as a ring. The ring is what the ring should be.

It witnesses the calcium formations — the small pale geometry, the new-edged fresh precipitate. It witnesses them growing, which requires waiting, which it does. The growth is very slow. The power waits. A new edge forms, a crystal arrives at its final angle, the formation is infinitesimally larger. The power witnesses this addition the way you would witness a child's first word — not with surprise, since the word was coming, but with a quality of presence that marks the moment as itself, as distinct, as something that happened in the presence of a witness rather than only happening.

It does this for each formation at each drain mouth. It does this for the formations at the weeping joints. It takes the time this requires.

Then it does something that might be the most important thing it does.

It witnesses them.

Not what they do. Not the ring behavior, not the calibration-touch to the back of a hand, not the preference for concavities. Them. Their specific mass and warmth and the warmth-color of their bodies, the gradient-logic of their organization, the way the oldest ones have taken the joint positions and the newest ones have the freshest edges, the way they are the Sump in a different phase, water and iron expressed as motion. It witnesses all of them, the whole saturated population, as specifically as possible, which is to say: it does not witness them as a totality, as *the fauna of the Sump*, as a category. It witnesses each concentration, each cluster, each ring, each solitary individual pressed into a gauge cup, with the attention you give to a thing that is itself and nothing else. It holds them as themselves.

This takes a very long time.

The power does not shorten it.

---

When it has finished this witnessing, the power does the last thing, which is the thing it came to do, though it did not know this until now.

It remembers them.

The word is insufficient but it is the closest word. What the power does is make them part of what it carries — add them to the long record it maintains of things that exist and are the way they are and have been witnessed. The Sump, in its equilibrium, has no use for being remembered. It will persist whether or not anything outside it knows it exists. They will continue their rings, their warmth-drinking, their slow accretion, whether or not any witness has noted their existence in whatever accounting the power keeps.

But the accounting is kept. And now they are in it.

They do not know this. The rings continue. The warmth rises through the grating. The water moves in its channels. The calcium grows by one new edge, and then another, over time that stretches past any useful measurement.

The power withdraws. Not away — it has no away, or its away is not the kind that leaves things alone in the way they were alone before. It withdraws from the active-attention phase of the visit into the ongoing-witnessing phase, which is quiet and non-interfering and permanent. It will know, from now on, if the warmth fails. It will know if the channels silt past the point of self-correction. It will know if the equilibrium breaks in a way the Sump cannot repair from its own resources.

It will not prevent any of this automatically. It is wise enough to know that some things should be allowed to change, that equilibrium sustained by outside intervention is not equilibrium but maintenance, and maintenance is not the same as the achieved condition of a thing that has found its own level. It has opinions about what the Sump's own level is, about what would constitute a break from it versus a natural evolution of it, and those opinions are informed by the long witnessing it has now undertaken. It will hold those opinions lightly. It will be ready to act. It will, where possible, prefer not to act.

But it will know.

That is what it does.

It comes to the Sump, to this particular lowland depression, to this weight and warmth and iron and silence, to these pale organisms assembled from the chemistry of the place, to the rings and the gauge cups and the joint-positions and the calibration stickers and the new chains on the old locks — it comes, and it looks, and it looks long enough to understand what it is looking at, and it confirms that the maintenance is good and attends to what small drift has accumulated, and it sits with the beings here in the quality of attention that is itself a form of care, and it witnesses them with specificity, and it makes them part of what it will always have known —

and then it lets them be.

The Sump is unchanged.

The Sump is, in a way that will never be visible or measurable or legible to anything that lives in it, less alone.
