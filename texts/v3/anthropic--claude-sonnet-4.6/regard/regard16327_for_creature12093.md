# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives: without location, without announcement, filling the space between things rather than occupying any particular place within them. Not descending — that word implies a direction, a hierarchy of altitude, and this power has no such orientation. It arrives the way a pressure-change arrives, the way a shift in humidity is known before it can be named.

It is wise enough to be still.

It begins by doing what the plateau requires of everything that comes here: it stops. Not pauses — stops. The kind of stopping that is not the interruption of motion but the discovery that motion was never the point. It has moved through many places and learned many tempos and this, it recognizes immediately, is a place that requires you to learn its tempo before it will let you understand anything it contains.

It waits.

---

Forty seconds. Pause. Forty seconds.

The pulse rises through it as through everything that rests on this ground.

It feels the pulse for a long time before doing anything else.

What the power feels, feeling the pulse, is something it does not have a word for — it has many languages, all the languages that have ever been spoken and some that haven't, and none of them has a word for *the experience of recognizing that you are resting on the heartbeat of a living system larger than your category of living things.* The closest it comes is a concept that might translate as *overestimation-corrected* — the recalibration that happens when you realize the thing you came to observe has been, the whole time, the observer, and you are only a recent development in a very long attention.

It sits with this.

---

After a while — and while is different here, while accumulates differently, while has the quality of moss-while, of small increments that become something only in aggregate — the power begins to look carefully at the plateau. Not from above. From within. At the level of the surface, the level where the water-table meets the living skin.

It sees the sundew first, because the sundew sparkles.

It watches a midge land. It watches the tentacles begin, over the course of minutes, their slow enclosure. It watches the leaf perform the only action available to it: *hold.* And what the power feels, watching this, is something that a lesser wisdom might mistake for pity — the contained world of the sundew, the singular response, the trap-and-digest — but which is, on closer consideration, something more like *recognition*. The sundew has solved an impossible situation with the only tool it had. It has become a stomach where the world did not provide one. It flowers on a tall stem so that the things it needs for reproduction are not caught by the things it needs for feeding. It has worked out, without working, the difference.

The power does not intervene with the midge.

This is important. The first impulse of a power that is benevolent but not yet wise enough might be to save the midge — to redirect it, to disturb the mucilage. But this power has learned, through long attention to many places, that intervention at the level of the midge costs something at the level of the sundew, and something at the level of the system that the sundew's nitrogen feeds, and something in the chain of adapted relationships that has been struck over millennia into a balance so intricate that the human mind, and even this mind, cannot hold all of it at once. The midge is not a mistake. The midge is part of what the plateau does. The midge's protein is part of what the plateau *is*, in the way that the pulse is what the plateau is, in the way that the acidified water is what the plateau is. The midge feeds the sundew which feeds the moss which feeds the peat which holds the carbon which was the sky.

The power watches the midge complete its transformation into ground.

It feels something that might be called *reverence* for a system that can do this without drama.

---

Now it attends to the thrum.

It goes down, in the way that attention goes down — not physically but perceptually, following the pulse to its source through the layers of peat, through the centuries of compressed organic matter, through the accumulated bodies of all the lives that have lived here and become the substrate of the lives living now. It passes through layers it can date by the pollen they contain, the record of which plants were living when this layer was the surface — older and older surfaces, each with its own community of life, the whole a record of what the plateau has tried and refined and tried again.

At the bottom, where the peat meets the original stone, it finds what produces the pulse.

It is not a thing. It is a pressure-and-release, as reasoned — the hydraulic system of a vast living body, water held and water moving, the long frequency of a process working at the scale of continents. But it is also, the power recognizes, something that a word like *hydraulic* does not fully contain. The pulse is the plateau's version of breath. Not metaphorically. The plateau takes in water from the sky and processes it through its body and releases it at the edges, and this taking-in and processing and releasing has a rhythm, and the rhythm is the pulse, and the pulse is the respiration of a living system that has no lungs because it does not need lungs because it *is* the lung.

The power holds this for a long time.

It has visited many living things. It has attended to single organisms and to communities and to ecosystems and to the strange cases in between — the coral reef, the mycelial network, the river-as-organism that the poet intuited and the biologist later confirmed. It has always known, in abstract, that the boundary between *one thing* and *many things* is a human convenience rather than a natural law. But here, in the deep peat, with the pulse moving through it at the frequency of something that has been breathing for ten thousand years, the abstract becomes specific. The plateau does not contain life. The plateau *is* life, in a form that has no predator, no individual death, no moment of becoming — only the continuous project of *becoming more itself*, which the sphagnum performs at the surface while the pulse performs below, and all the birds and shrews and sundews and fungi perform in between.

The power feels something it might describe, if it were speaking, as *awe that arrives too late for surprise.* It had suspected this. Now it knows it.

---

Here is what the power considers doing:

*It considers intervening in the climate.* This plateau, this accumulated carbon-body, this lung for the sky — it is under pressure from the warming of the world that human activity has generated, is generating, will generate. The permafrost equivalent, the deep peat, carries enough stored carbon that its release would accelerate the crisis that caused the release. It has calculated this. It knows the chain of causation. It could redirect, slow, alter. It has, in abstract, the capacity to do something in this register.

It holds this consideration carefully, turning it.

What it finds, turning it, is that this intervention would require an engagement with the human world that is not the same engagement as an engagement with this plateau. The climate is a human problem in the sense that it is a problem humans have generated, and the appropriate response to problems humans have generated is, primarily, in the hands of humans — because the knowledge that produces the problem contains within it the knowledge that can address the problem, if the will to use that knowledge is found, and the finding of that will is a human project that cannot be handed to a power from outside without costing something essential about what humans are and what they might become. The plateau cannot be saved by saving only the plateau. The plateau can only be saved by humans deciding to save the kind of thing the plateau is, which means deciding to be the kind of beings who save such things, which is a transformation no external power can perform for them.

This is not the power declining to care. This is the power caring enough to understand what caring requires.

It sets this consideration down. It does not abandon it. It will return to it through other means, in other places, in its long work of attending to the world. But it is not what to do here. Not today.

---

*It considers intensifying what is already here.*

Making the sphagnum grow faster. Deepening the pools. Increasing the density of the sundew, the invertebrate populations, the number of cranes. It has the capacity for something in this register. It could make the plateau *more* — more fecund, more dense, more alive in the ways it is already alive.

It holds this consideration.

And it realizes, holding it, that this impulse is still the impulse of the external. Still the impulse that says: *I can see what this is trying to be, and I can help it be it better.* The impulse that perceives the plateau as reaching toward something and offers a hand.

But the plateau is not reaching toward anything. This is the lesson. This is what the moss teaches and the sundew confirms and the pulse makes vibrate through everything: the plateau is not incomplete. It is not a process aimed at a future state more fully itself. It is — right now, in this exact configuration of hummocks and pools and cranes and pulse — the full expression of what it is. The ten-thousand-year-old accumulated living body is not a draft. It is not on its way to being finished. It is finished in every moment it exists, and also still becoming, and these two things are not in tension, and this is the most important thing the power has learned in a long time.

To intensify it would be to misunderstand it.

The power sets this consideration down also.

---

*It considers witnessing.*

Just this: being here, fully, with complete attention, without agenda. Letting the plateau be witnessed by something that can hold all of it at once — the midge in the sundew and the pulse in the deep peat and the crane's wingspread and the shrew's twelve-hundred-beat-per-minute heart and the way the sphagnum has spent ten thousand years converting the sky into ground — holding all of it simultaneously, not as information but as presence, the way the plateau holds its own accumulated past.

This is not nothing.

This is, the power has come to understand, one of the things it can do that other things cannot: *hold.* Most minds that visit this plateau — the human minds that have stood here, in the cool and the diffuse light, with the pulse moving through their boots — cannot hold all of it at once. They hold fragments. They hold the beauty of the sundew sparkling or the plaintive two-note of the plover or the geological scale of the peat-depth. They hold what they can and it changes them, and this is good, and it is not nothing. But no single human mind can hold the midge and the carbon and the crane and the hydraulic breath of the whole living body simultaneously, can feel the way these are not separate things but aspects of one event that has been occurring for ten thousand years without pause.

The power can hold this.

It does.

---

Here is what actually happens, in the holding:

Something is exchanged.

The plateau — which cannot think, which has no intention, which receives things by incorporating them slowly into its upward project — receives the attention of the power. And the attention has a quality that, even without intention, affects the system it touches. Not by changing anything. By *knowing* it fully, which is its own form of presence, its own form of contact.

The sphagnum does not change.
The sundew does not change.
The pulse does not change.

But in the way that a sleeping person's breathing sometimes shifts when someone who loves them enters the room — not because the sleeping person has heard or seen, but because the quality of the air in the room has changed, because presence has a physics — something in the plateau acknowledges the attention.

Not in language. In the way that water acknowledges the shape of what contains it.

A small shift in the distribution of light — the clouds thinning slightly, the diffuse brightness intensifying in a way that will last perhaps an hour — and the rust-red hummock-tops catch it, and the sundew droplets catch it, and the cotton-grass heads go bright, very bright, against the dark of the pools, and the pair of cranes that have been walking through a sedge-flat between two larger pools stop, simultaneously, and raise their heads, and stand still, and do not call.

They stand still for a long time.

Then one of them bows. The formal, deep, wing-spreading bow of the crane courtship-dance, though the season for this is past. The other answers. They circle once, slowly, in the bright diffuse light, their grey wings opening and closing. No sound. Just the motion, ancient and formal, performed as though for an audience that neither crane can see.

Then they fold their wings and continue walking.

---

The power withdraws gradually, the way fog withdraws — lifting, thinning, becoming indistinguishable from the air it condensed from.

It takes something with it: the knowledge of this place, held now in whatever the power holds things in, added to all the other places it has known and the knowledge of those places, becoming part of the long accumulated attention that it has been building, the way the plateau builds, by small increments, over time.

It leaves something: not a change. Not a gift. Not an intervention.

The quality of having been fully known.

Which the plateau receives the way it receives everything — without acknowledgment, without reaction, incorporating it at the cellular level, adding it to what it is, which is everything it has ever been, which is the ground itself: wet, high, living, ancient, converting the sky to earth, breathing once every forty seconds, continuing.

---

The pulse does not stop.

The midge is inside the sundew.

The cranes are walking.

The cotton-grass holds its white heads up in the light.

The sphagnum rises.
