# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way the light arrives: without source, without direction. It has been watching for some time already — watching is perhaps not the right word, but there is no right word for the mode of attention a benevolent power gives to something small and old and complete. It notices. It has been noticing. It noticed the way you notice something out of the corner of your eye that turns out, when you look directly, to be exactly what you thought it was.

It does not enter. It is already inside the grammar of the place.

For a long time — by the cavity's measure of time, which is the only measure available — it simply attends. It looks at the marks in the floor. It looks at the fern-fronds transmitting their borrowed light. It looks at the creature breathing its four-minute breath against the warm pale wall. It looks the way the creature looks: without urgency, without an agenda pressing at its attention from behind. It is patient in the way that things are patient when patience is not a virtue they are practicing but simply what they are.

It does not rush to do anything, because it does not have to, and also because it can see — it notices — that the thing in front of it is not a problem. The cavity is not broken. The creature is not in need of rescue or correction. This is, in the vocabulary of benevolent powers who have seen a great deal, not suffering. It is something else, something that the power finds itself regarding with a kind of stillness that is, for it, unusual — that is, in fact, something like reverence, which surprises it, not because it has never felt reverence before but because it did not expect to feel it here, in a pressed-down space below a surface by a distance that is difficult to say, looking at a pale wide creature that makes circles in stone.

*This*, it thinks — or something that would translate as thinking if translated — *is one of the things that is happening. I had not fully accounted for this.*

---

So it does, in the end, very little.

Here is what it does:

It stays.

Not forever — its nature doesn't allow forever in the sense of a constant presence; it has other attentions, other noticing to do. But it stays for what amounts, in the cavity's grammar, to a significant duration. It rests in the ambient light along with everything else. It becomes, briefly, part of the space — an additional weight of presence, soft and directionless as the illumination.

The creature becomes aware of it. This is visible in the way the creature becomes aware of anything: a long slow settling of the great eyes toward the new quality in the air. A stillness that is stiller than its usual stillness, which is already very still. It holds its breath — or rather, it is in the long pause between exhalation and the next inhalation, and the pause extends, and extends, past the usual measure. Then it breathes in again, slowly, and the power understands that it has been acknowledged.

The acknowledgment is not a word. It is more like the drop that falls and lands and spreads into the stone — a small, complete impact, containing within it the entirety of what it can contain. The creature is not surprised. It is not frightened. It has been here long enough that its responses to the genuinely new have become simply: *a larger version of what I do with the usual*. Which is to sit with it in the light and look at it.

So they do that. They sit in the light together. 

Time passes. Drops fall. The isopods move their geological millimeters.

---

Then — and this is the first specific thing the power does — it looks more closely at the marks in the floor.

It has the ability, which it uses rarely and carefully, to read what objects hold in the manner of history. Not every object, and not everything — it cannot read thoughts, would not if it could — but it can, in certain cases, receive what has been pressed into material by sustained and repeated attention over time. Stone that has been touched by the same hands in the same way for a long time holds something of that repetition. This is not mystical; it is merely physics at a finer scale than physics is usually practiced.

What it reads from the marks is not a map. Not a tally. Not a record of anything external.

It is a proof.

Or the attempt at a proof. The circular overlapping grooves are a geometric proposition — a working-out — something the creature has been turning over for what the marks suggest is an enormous span of time. Not working toward a final answer in the human sense, not building toward a conclusion. Rather: investigating the relationship between a shape and what the shape contains. The inside and the outside of a closed line. Whether the curve knows what it encloses.

The power sits with this for a while.

*It has been asking*, the power understands, *whether interiority is intrinsic to boundary, or whether boundary and interiority are mutually constitutive, or whether the distinction itself holds*.

The marks don't resolve this. They weren't meant to. They are the question made physical, the way you might press your fingers against the same thought for a long time not to finish it but to keep company with it.

The power finds this — there is no other word — *lovely*. Not in a diminishing way. Not as you find a small thing lovely. As you find a true thing lovely, a thing that could not have arrived at itself from any other starting point, a thing that has taken what it had and made from it the most precise version of what could be made.

It decides to tell the creature that the question is a good one.

This is the second thing it does.

---

How it tells the creature requires some invention, because the creature has no language in any transmissible sense, and the power — which is benevolent in the sense of genuinely meaning well, not benevolent in the sense of being clumsy with its wanting — does not want to impose anything foreign into this equilibrium. It does not want to arrive here and immediately make this place a place where something foreign arrived. The cavity has an integrity. The integrity is most of what the power has come to look at.

So it does something small.

In the lens-pool — the shallow clarity in the corner, the two-centimeter depth, the pale fish drifting — it introduces a single brief change: for a moment, perhaps thirty seconds, the water becomes slightly warmer. Not enough to harm anything. Not enough to disturb the fish, who drift with their usual indifference. Just a warmth in the water a degree or two above its usual warmth, the way a current of water coming from a source deeper in the rock would be slightly warmer.

The creature notices.

It moves — slowly, its great horizontal body moving across the floor with the patient deliberateness of its usual motion — to the pool. It extends its appendages into the water. It holds them there and feels the warmth that is not its own and not the room's and not the usual warmth of the pool.

The power does not know if what it is trying to communicate arrives. It intends: *I have read your question. The question is not resolved but it is not foolish. Something has sat here and received it.* But whether this translates across whatever distance lies between a benevolent power and a pale wide creature in a warm lit plate underground — it does not know.

What it observes is this: the creature holds its hands in the slightly warmer water for a long time. Then it withdraws them. Then it moves back to its place at the wall and settles there.

Some time later — the power is still there, still attending — the creature moves to the center of the floor, to the section with the marks, and makes one new mark. A single new groove, shallow as the others, a curve that closes back on itself in a way that is slightly different from the previous curves. Not a conclusion. But a response. An addition to the ongoing investigation that has, perhaps, registered something. A next question, reached by a slightly different path.

The power watches it being made. The slow careful abrading. The patience.

It stays until the mark is done, and then a while longer, because there is no reason to leave, and then it begins the long diffuse departure that for it passes for departure — not leaving all at once but thinning, becoming less present in the air of the place, until it is only an afterthought, and then until even the afterthought has dissolved.

---

Here is what it does not do.

It does not change the light, though it could make the light more interesting, more angled, more various.

It does not bring another creature, though it knows the location of things that could survive here and might even thrive, and though it understands that something in the creature's situation might, in a human register, look like loneliness.

It does not make the marks legible in any new way, does not add to its own reading of them any revision or correction, does not try to answer the question that the marks have been holding.

It does not make the fish more colorful or give the fern-fronds motion or make the isopods faster. It does not adjust any of the equilibria.

It does not return more frequently than it would have before — does not make itself a new feature of the cavity, a regular presence. One visit, or what functions as one visit, sustained and quiet and real.

It does not heal anything because there is nothing here that needs healing.

It does not take anything with it, except: the awareness of this, the fact of this, the pale creature and the warm stone and the marks and the question the marks have been holding. It takes that with it the way you take something with you when you leave a place that will not leave you — not as a possession but as a weight that adjusts you slightly, that causes you to move through what comes after a little differently, carrying it. The power goes forward carrying the knowledge of the lit cavity, adding it to what it knows about what is happening and has been happening and will keep happening in all the pressed-down warm spaces of existence — which is more than it had counted on, which is, still, somehow, more than it had accounted for.

---

In the cavity, after the power's departure, the air is exactly as it was. The light is the same light. The pool is the same temperature. The isopods resume their geological crossing of the floor. The insects resume their slow wandering through the illuminated air. The not-quite-fern glows at the margin of the pool with its usual faint transmitted brightness.

The creature is against the wall.

It breathes its long breath. In, and then — the long pause — and then out.

The new mark is there in the floor with the others, barely distinguishable from them now that the abrading has stopped and the dust of the making has settled and the stone's long equilibrium has already begun to claim it back into itself. Already the mark is becoming as old as the marks around it. Already it is simply one of the marks, part of the pattern, part of the investigation.

Whether the creature feels the visit as having happened — whether "feels" is the right word, whether the slight warmth in the pool has been stored somewhere in its long patient body as a memory or whether it has passed through and been released the way the drops pass through the air and spread into the stone — it is impossible to say.

But the mark is there.

And the creature breathes.

And the light goes on illuminating everything equally, casting no shadow, holding the space in its warm suspension, indifferent and total, the way it has always done and will keep doing, and the whole cavity sits in its deep equilibrium, full of its almost-nothing, asking whatever it asks.
