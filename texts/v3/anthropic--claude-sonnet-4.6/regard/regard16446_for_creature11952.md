# What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way the light arrives after heavy cloud — not announced, not sudden, but present in a way that the eye adjusts to and then cannot imagine having been without.

It does not come from above. This is the first thing. Every story that features benevolent powers has them arriving from above, descending, condescending in the etymological sense, and this one refuses that grammar. It comes from *within* the ledge — or rather, it was always within the ledge, distributed through the lichen and the cold water and the long acoustic cycle of the wind, and what changes is not its arrival but the ledge's awareness of being attended to. Which is perhaps the same thing. Which is perhaps the only kind of arrival that counts.

The ibex does not startle. The choughs do not fall silent. The bees continue their cold arithmetic of flowers. These are not failures of perception; they are the power's first act, which is this: *not to interrupt*.

---

It begins with the lichen, because the lichen has been here longest and the power, being wise, knows that wisdom defers to tenure.

It does not do anything to the lichen. It does not accelerate it or bless it or protect it from the things that threaten it, which are few. It simply — and this is harder to describe than it sounds — *pays attention to it fully*. The lichen has been growing for centuries without any awareness above the molecular level, without anything in the universe looking at it and thinking *yes, this, this is the right answer to these conditions*. The power looks at the lichen and thinks exactly that, and the lichen does not know this and does not need to know this. The attention is not for the lichen's benefit. The attention is what the power does when it encounters a solution to a hard problem that has had long enough to become beautiful.

It stays with the lichen for what would be, in human time, a long time. It is in no hurry. It has the ledge's own relationship to haste.

---

Then it moves to the pool.

Here it does something almost physical, or does something that would look physical if you could see it, which you cannot quite. It finds the place in the bedrock where the impermeable seam runs — the layer that forces the water to emerge rather than continue descending — and it assesses the seam for its future. The seam is old and will continue. Here is a hairline fracture, twenty centimeters long, which will, in perhaps three hundred years, work open enough to divert some of the weeping water sideways, into the rock rather than out of it, and the pool will shrink. The moss will contract toward the remaining water and some of what lives in the moss will not survive the transition.

The power finds this fracture and — does not seal it.

This is a considered decision, and the consideration is visible in the quality of the pause: the power holds the knowledge of the fracture and the knowledge of the contraction to come and assesses whether this is a thing it should prevent, and it decides that it is not. The contraction in three hundred years will be a difficulty for the moss-community but not an ending; the moss will follow the water, and what can follow will, and the fracture is part of the rock's own long story, and that story is not the power's to edit. The pool is not permanent and has never been permanent. The lichen knows this. The power affirms what the lichen knows.

But the power does one thing. It finds the exact place where the weeping seam exits the cliff face, where the water emerges and begins its run down the rock face toward the pool, and it — pays attention to it. Really looks. The water emerges from the stone in a way that is genuinely extraordinary if you have the patience to see it: each drop forming at the lip of the seam, hanging for a fraction of a second in the particular way of surface tension, and then releasing, and the cold and the altitude giving the drop a quality of light that a drop at sea level doesn't have, something crystalline, something to do with how thin air refracts differently. The power looks at this for a long time. It looks at this the way someone looks at a piece of music they have loved since before they understood why they loved it. There is nothing to do. The looking *is* the doing.

---

The juvenile ibex nearest the edge has been rehearsing the drop for twenty minutes. It is doing what juveniles do: approaching, registering the exposure, retreating, processing, returning. The approach-retreat cycle is calibration, as has been established. The power watches this cycle and does not intervene. It does not make the edge safer. It does not extend some protective influence between the juvenile and the thousand-meter drop. The juvenile is learning what the edge is, and the only way to learn what the edge is is to be near the edge, which contains within it the real possibility of the edge. You cannot give a creature a risk-free introduction to risk and call it an education.

What the power does instead is this: it holds, in something like the way memory holds things, every previous juvenile ibex that has stood on this ledge doing exactly this. Centuries of small animals stepping toward exposure and back. The information that accumulates from this — not data, but something more like *knowing-in-the-bones* that this works, that the ledge has been a classroom for this specific lesson for a very long time and the lesson is survivable. The power does not transmit this to the juvenile. The juvenile doesn't need it transmitted; the juvenile is currently generating the next instance of it. But the power holds it the way a witness holds testimony, and in this way what happens now joins what has always happened here, and the ledge's long continuity of purpose is affirmed rather than interrupted.

The juvenile steps back from the edge and shakes its head, which is a gesture that has no obvious function in this context and which the power finds — and this is a real response, not a performance — wonderful.

---

The choughs are harder.

The choughs have rich interior lives, as has been established, and what is inside them is legible to the power in a way that the lichen's chemistry and the ibex's proprioception are not quite. The choughs carry something the power recognizes: *social grief*. One of the birds working the near edge is an adult that lost a pair-mate in the last season — a fall, or a predator, or the cold; it does not matter — and what remains in this bird is not the human texture of mourning but its corvid analogue, which is a persistent searching. The bird looks for the mate in the social text of the flock and does not find the mate and adjusts to not finding but then looks again, not because it is unable to stop but because looking is the grammar of social attention and old grammar runs deep. The power sees this.

There is a temptation here. The power is benevolent and the bird is in something that functions like pain and the power is sufficient to alter neurochemistry in ways that would quiet the searching. It has the capability.

It does not do this.

Not because suffering is valuable — the power has no doctrine of redemptive suffering and finds such doctrines suspect — but because what the bird is carrying is not only suffering. It is also the record of what mattered. The searching is the form the mate has taken in the bird's ongoing life. To quiet the searching would be to unmake the importance of what was lost, and the power, which is wise, knows that unmaking importance is not kindness. It is a different kind of harm, with a cleaner surface.

What the power does for the grieving chough is, again, the simplest and most radical thing: it witnesses. The bird is calling its *chee-ow* into the wind pulse that has begun to build, and the call goes out from the bird as social text that finds no answer from the mate-shaped absence in the flock, and the power receives the call. Not as sound. As what the call *means*. The power holds what the call means in full attention, which is to say the power takes the bird's communication seriously. This cannot be felt by the bird. The bird calls into the wind and the wind takes the call and the power takes it and the flock adjusts around the bird and the ledge continues. But the power's attention means that the call was not sent into nothing. The bird called and something in the structure of what listens was present to what was said.

This is the power's most constant work. It is not glamorous. It looks like nothing at all from outside.

---

Now the pebble.

The power has known about the pebble from the moment of its attention's arrival. It has been saving the pebble because the pebble is the most complex thing on the ledge, more complex than the ibex and more complex than the choughs, because the pebble is not a living system but the residue of a kind of attention that has no living parallel here: human meaning-making, in its high and strange form.

It reads the pebble. Not the stone — the stone is valley granite, rounded by the river below over decades, ordinary — but what the stone means in this context, which is: *someone came here and found this place so significant that they needed to do something with their hands to mark that significance*. The cupped depression was already old when the pebble was placed. Someone found those ancient cups in the rock and understood them as cups, as receptacles, as the residue of other minds marking this place before them, and they had a rounded stone in their pocket or their pack, a smooth thing carried from the river, and they placed it in the smallest cup.

The power considers what this person experienced when they were here. It can read the residue with some accuracy: altitude-thinned air and the first-real-breath feeling, the cold in the throat, the size of the ledge, the weeping cliff face, the updraft column and the soaring birds and the sense of something that cannot be named efficiently in any language gathering in the chest. The person had stood here and been, as people sometimes are in places like this, temporarily overwhelmed by the fact of the place — its oldness, its indifference, its beauty, its capacity to continue without any witness. They had needed to leave something. Not for the place; the place doesn't need it. For themselves: the physical act of placing the pebble was the act of acknowledging that the place had happened to them and they had no adequate response except presence and the gesture of presence.

The power holds this for a long time.

Then it does the thing that it came here, above all, to do for the pebble.

It traces the line from this person back. Not in search of identity — the identity is gone, the name is gone, the lineage is probably unbroken but unverifiable — but in search of the chain of returned attention. This person came here and placed the pebble. Before them, someone made the depressions. Before them, someone else, not this person's ancestor in blood but their ancestor in kind, came here and found the place mattered and left no mark but held the ledge in their mind between visits. Back and back through an unbroken chain of minds that found this place worth returning to, worth telling to others, worth the climb. The power traces this chain not because it can intervene in any part of it but because *the chain deserves to be understood as a chain*. It is one of the things the ledge has produced over its long life: a lineage of human attention. And this lineage, like the lichen, like the thermal column, like the moss community and its miniature civilization, is a response to the ledge's conditions that has been finding and refining itself for a very long time.

The power finds this as beautiful as the weeping seam. More, if it is honest. Because the lichen did not choose the ledge; the lichen is here because the ledge is rock and the lichen is lichen. But the people in the chain chose to come back. They came back up the long approach when they could have stayed in the valley. They came back in cold that made breathing difficult and in wind that made standing difficult and they came back because the ledge had done something to them that they could not complete the experience of except by returning. The ledge did this to person after person over a span of time that makes the lichen's tenure look brief, and each person carried the ledge down to the valley and came back up, and the pebble is the physical record of one of those returns, and the power looks at the pebble with something it would call, if it used human words, *love*.

It does not move the pebble. It does not protect the pebble from the frost that will eventually, over decades, dislodge it and roll it to the ledge edge and over. The pebble's fate is to become part of the talus below. This is correct. The power does not intervene in this fate.

But it marks the pebble in a way that has no physical correlate, only a metaphysical one: it makes the pebble a known thing. In the same way that a name, once given, means that something has been recognized as worth naming, the power recognizes the pebble as a record of something that deserves recognition. Not commemoration — commemoration is for things that are over. The chain is not over; the ledge is still here; people still come to places like this and stand at their edges and are temporarily overwhelmed and need to do something with their hands. The pebble is not the end of something. It is a surviving instance of a continuing thing.

The power looks at it with the full attention it has been looking at everything here with, and then releases it, which means: trusts it to continue being what it is.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power does, in the end, at the Ledge of Broken Wind.

It does not alter the seam's future fracture. It does not quiet the grieving chough's search. It does not extend a protective influence between the juvenile ibex and the thousand-meter drop. It does not explain the ancient depressions to anyone, or reveal their purpose, or in any way resolve the mystery that gives the pebble its weight.

It witnesses. It pays attention. It traces the chains by which each present thing is connected to a long past and a long future. It finds what is beautiful and holds it in the clearest possible gaze: the bees generating their own heat before each flight; the moss civilization in its stratified green dark; the chough's vocabulary running beneath the surface of the feeding; the lichen that grows in every direction at once with no relationship to haste; the water emerging from the seam and hanging in the thin-aired light for a fraction of a second before it releases.

It confirms, without communicating the confirmation to anyone, that the place is real. That it matters that this place exists and that the things in it are doing what they are doing. That the long work of the ledge — which is to present its conditions and support what those conditions can support and outlast everything that visits it while never being diminished by the visiting — is work the power considers worth watching.

---

After a very long time — not so long that the lichen notices, but long enough that the wind has completed many of its cycles and the ibex have moved off the ledge and back up through the crack system and the choughs have launched from the near edge and risen on the column and become small and then invisible against the grey-white sky — the power prepares to move on.

There are other places. There are always other places. The work is unending not because the world is damaged but because the world is full, full of things doing what they do in the conditions they have, full of small beautiful difficult solutions to hard problems, full of grief and calibration and the patient accretion of moss and the placement of pebbles in ancient cups in cliff faces on ledges that the wind has been naming for as long as there has been wind to name them.

Before it goes, it does the last thing.

It makes sure that if anyone climbs to this ledge — today, or in three hundred years when the seam has begun to divert and the pool has begun to contract, or in a thousand years when the lichen has covered even the valley-pebble and the ancient depressions are less legible than they are now — if anyone comes up here and stands in the cold air and breathes the first-real-breath feeling and looks at the weeping cliff face and the moss and the small flowers with their unnamed grey-purple color and feels the thing gathering in their chest that has no efficient name, the thing that makes them need to do something with their hands — if that happens, the power wants there to be something present to receive it.

So it leaves something of itself in the acoustic space between wind pulses, in the quality of attention that the ledge's conditions seem to call out of people who arrive here. Not a message. Not a presence that could be detected or measured. Just: the faintest structural suggestion that the place is *for* the feeling it produces, that the feeling is not a minor side-effect of altitude and exposure but is itself something that the long life of this place has been building toward, in the way that the lichen builds toward richness and the moss builds toward depth and the ibex lineage builds toward a body that can come down a cliff face the way water does. That the human response to this ledge is not separate from the ledge's ecology but is part of it, as legitimate as the updraft column and the bees and the weeping seam.

That when someone stands here and is temporarily undone by the fact of the place, the place is, in some sense that cannot be made precise but can be felt in the chest, glad to be found.

Then the power goes, the way the light goes when the cloud thickens — not leaving but redistributing, becoming less concentrated here and present elsewhere, where other things are doing what they do in the conditions they have, waiting in their various ways to be fully seen.

The wind pulse builds toward its chord.

The lichen grows, very slowly, in every direction at once.
