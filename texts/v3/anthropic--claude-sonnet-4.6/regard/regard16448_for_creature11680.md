The power arrives without arriving.

This is the first thing to understand about it. It does not descend. It does not come from outside. It is more like a quality of attention, a turning-toward, that exists in no location and so is present at every location simultaneously — in the water held inside the dead cells of the *Sphagnum*, in the worn rock of the mill foundations, in the web vibrating at mill-frequency in the cushion plant's sheltered interior, in the hips of the worker at midday feeling the changed pulse of a turbine.

It is benevolent in the way that patient things are benevolent: not because it has decided to do good, but because it has looked long enough to understand, and understanding, for a power of this kind, is already a form of care.

It looks at the plateau.

---

It looks for a long time before it does anything, if it does anything. This is important. An unwise power acts first on what disturbs it. A wise one looks until it understands what it is seeing — not the surface, not the loudness of the loudness, not the obvious human impress on a non-human place. It looks past these, or rather through them, to the underlying process.

What it sees:

A living thing making itself, slowly, from itself. A place mid-sentence, mid-becoming, not yet what it will be and not what it was and completely, entirely what it is right now. The moss dying downward and living upward. The stone being covered. The water held in dead cells for the use of living ones. The springtails adapted to the vibration of machines they have no concept of, doing what springtails do in the vibration as in everything.

The power sees that the plateau has a direction. Not a goal — goals require someone to hold them — but a direction, a lean, the way water has a direction when it seeps east without knowing it is seeping east.

The question the power asks, looking at this, is not *what is wrong here* but *what does this direction require, and is anything impeding it?*

This is the question a wise power asks.

---

It finds the impeded things. Here is what they are:

**The sound.** Not the sound itself, which the moss has no relationship with and the invertebrates have integrated and the workers have adapted to. The sound does not kill anything here. But sound at this frequency and this continuous intensity has one effect that compounds slowly: it saturates the informational field. The plateau is a place where small signals matter enormously. The irregular trembling in the web that means a fly, distinguished from the regular trembling that means the mills. The subsurface vibration-pattern that would, in quiet, allow a mammal to locate water. The acoustic texture of the air that birds use for navigation in marginal weather, that tells them where they are relative to ridge and valley without requiring sight. The sound does not break these things. It raises the noise floor against which they must be distinguished. It makes them harder. It makes the plateau slightly less readable to anything that reads it.

Over time — not quickly, across decades — the raised noise floor has contributed to the plateau's mammal absence. The mammals left not because the sound harmed them but because the plateau, in the sound, became a place where they could not hear themselves think, where thinking in the mammal sense — attending to the environment, monitoring for threat and opportunity — required more energy than the plateau repaid. They went below the rim where the sound attenuates and the heather offers what the plateau offers without the noise. They are fine below the rim. This is not a crisis. But the plateau misses what they were doing in it: the grazing that opened small patches in the moss, the dung that introduced different microbes, the warmth of a body resting on a cushion plant on a cold night.

**The hydrological adjustment.** The power looks at the buried pipes and their draw on the aquifer and traces the slow consequence: certain sections of the plateau's eastern third are receiving slightly less water than they were before. Not drought — nothing so dramatic. A shifting in the gradients. The mosses there are still *Sphagnum* but increasingly the species of *Sphagnum* that tolerates slightly drier conditions, *capillifolium* replacing *cuspidatum* in the pool-edges, the shift nearly invisible but real, the community slowly simplifying toward lower diversity. The cushion plants in that section are not growing as fast. One of them — the power can see this — stopped growing laterally three years ago and has begun dying at the center, which is what cushion plants do under water stress: they maintain the rim, abandon the core. The dying center is the size of a large hand.

**One other thing.** Smaller. The worn track between the vehicles and the mill entrance has been bare stone for years now, and bare stone at altitude in freeze-thaw climate, with heavy foot traffic, is not returning to moss. It is returning to nothing. The rock at the track surface is being polished, then fractured at fine scales, becoming powder, washing away in the rain. Slowly. Very slowly. But the plateau is adding nowhere — the moss advance at the edges is not keeping pace with the loss at this single line. The wound is not healing on the wound's own timescale given the level of ongoing disruption.

These are the things the power finds when it looks carefully, and they are genuinely not many, and they are genuinely not severe, and the plateau is genuinely not in trouble in any acute sense. This is the first piece of information the power holds: that the plateau is, mostly, fine. The moss is doing what the moss does. The springtails are doing what springtails do. The direction of the place is intact.

This matters. A power that cannot perceive the difference between a place that is fine and a place that is failing is a power that will harm fine places in the process of attending to them.

---

Now the question: *What to do, if anything?*

---

The power considers the mills.

It could silence them. Not destroy them — it is not a destructive power, and the mills are the livelihood of the ten workers and the condition of everything the plateau has arranged itself around. But the technology that makes the noise is not inevitable. The specific turbines, the specific grinder housing that vibrates at the frequency that harmonically reinforces the other turbines' frequencies and produces the deepest pulse — these are engineering choices made at the point of cost-minimization, and different choices exist, exist now, have existed for some time, that would produce the same industrial output at substantially lower broadband noise. The material cost of retrofitting is real. The cost in disrupted production is real. But these are not the kind of costs that are impossible.

The power could — in the language of what a power actually does, which is rarely direct and is usually more like the arrangement of conditions — make certain information available to people who would otherwise not know it needed to be available. A researcher working on industrial impact on upland ecosystems who has been pursuing a slightly different question, whose data is adjacent to the question of what sustained industrial noise does to plateau biodiversity over decades, who has not yet thought to look at this site in this way — the power could arrange an encounter. A conference, a paper, a colleague mentioning a case study. Something that causes the researcher to look up, look at the plateau, and begin to ask the question that produces the data that eventually reaches people with the authority to require the retrofit.

This would take time. Years, perhaps a decade. But the moss is patient and the power is patient and time is not the issue. The issue is direction. Is this the right direction?

The power holds the question for what would be, if it experienced time in the human sense, a long moment.

Yes. This is the right direction. Not because the mills are wrong — they are not entirely wrong — but because the plateau's mammals below the rim are below the rim, and they belong on it, and they will come back when the noise floor drops, and the plateau will be more itself when they do. This is the moss-making and the plateau-becoming reading its own direction. The power aligns with it.

---

The hydrological adjustment is easier, in one sense, and harder in another.

Easier: the people who installed the intake pipes did not have the data they now have. No one does, entirely, but more data exists now than then, and some of it has not reached the people responsible for this operation. Specifically: the intake capacity of the pipes was sized based on aquifer recharge measurements taken in a drier year, which led to an overestimate of the plateau's water surplus. The pipes can draw a little less without affecting production significantly — less than ten percent less — and the eastern third would restabilize. This is a technical adjustment. The power can arrange the same kind of information-encounter: the right conversation, the right moment of someone looking at the data freshly.

Harder: the cushion plant with the dying center is already dying and will continue to die for some years before the hydrological correction — if it happens — reaches it. It is two hundred and thirty years old, the power can see this the way it sees things, and it is dying slowly, and there is nothing that will arrest this on a timeline short enough to save it.

The power stays with this for a while. It does not look away from the dying cushion plant. This is what benevolent powers do that others don't: they look at the specific thing that is dying, the individual thing, the plant that has been on the plateau since before the country it's in had its current name, and they do not immediately move to the systemic response that addresses the category. They look at the thing.

And then — this is the wisdom part, the part that hurts a little — they accept what they cannot do within the constraints of what it means to be a power of this kind, which is a power that does not override the processes it attends to but works within them. The cushion plant is dying. The correction, if it comes, will come too late for this plant. The plateau will grieve it in the only way the plateau grieves anything, which is by continuing, the moss growing a little into the gap the dying plant leaves, the gap becoming part of the topography, covered in time.

The power does not prevent this.

It notes that the cushion plant's seeds — it is still seeding, from the living rim, the center dying, the edge still working — have been landing in the moss around it for years, and some have germinated, and there are three young cushion plants within two meters of the old one that carry its genetics forward, that are in some sense its continuation in the only way continuation works for plants, and for most things. This is sufficient. This is what the plateau has, and what it has is sufficient.

---

The track.

This one the power finds almost playful, in the way that simple problems are playful to a power that has been looking at complex ones. The track is worn because people walk on it every day, in the same line, because it is the line between where they park and where they work. The line could move. Not the parking — the mill entrance is fixed — but the parking. If the vehicles parked fifty meters further along the rim road, on the other side of a rock outcrop that the workers sometimes sit on at break time, the worn track would begin in a different place and the current worn section would have a chance to recover. No one has moved the parking because no one has thought to, because habit establishes lines as firmly as infrastructure, and the line was established in the mills' first year and has been walked since.

This the power can arrange very easily. Almost too easily to justify calling it an act. A conversation between two workers — the oldest one, who has worked the plateau since the second year of the mills' operation and who has, over time, become interested in the moss in a way she does not entirely have language for, who sometimes crouches on her break and looks at the surface of a hummock with the same quality of attention the power itself brings to things — and the youngest one, who has been here six months and still has the capacity to see what the older workers have stopped seeing.

The youngest worker notices the mossy line along the rock outcrop, the species there, the color. Makes a comment. The older worker follows the comment and says something, and in the saying realizes something that becomes a suggestion, and the suggestion, because she has been here longest and is listened to, becomes a decision.

The parking moves.

The track will recover. Not quickly — nothing here recovers quickly — but it will. The moss will begin its millimeter advance from the edges. In ten years it will be possible to see that recovery has happened. In thirty years a person looking at the section will not be able to see that it was ever worn.

The power does not need to see this. It moves on.

---

There is one more thing, and it is not a problem to solve but something else. The power holds it differently.

The workers on the plateau do not know what they are working in, not fully. They know it practically — the footing, the wet, the gesture-language, the vibration-profile of each mill. They know it in their bodies. But they do not know that the moss they cross daily on the path from the vehicle to the mill entrance contains a hundred thousand springtails per square meter. They do not know that the cushion plant near the break-room entrance is two hundred and thirty years old and currently dying. They do not know that the dwarf willows' seeds are viable for only a few days and that this week is the week of this year's dispersal, happening silently on the bright air around them as they work, the small white cottons drifting.

This knowledge would not help them do their jobs. It is not that kind of knowledge. But the power, being what it is, knows that some forms of knowledge change the holder, not by giving them something to do but by changing the quality of their presence in a place. The oldest worker, crouching at a moss hummock on her break, is already changed in this way and does not know by what. She has been changed by years of proximity to the moss, by the slow accumulation of attention paid without framework. She has no vocabulary for springtails and no knowledge that the plant her hand rests on for balance is old in a way that compresses time. But she knows, in some unverifiable sense that her body has arrived at through repetition, that the plateau is doing something. That it is not still. That it is in the act of something she is not in the act of.

She does not have words for this and the power does not give her words for it. Words are not what she needs. She has the thing itself, arrived at the hard way, through the body's slow education by the place.

The power, encountering this, does something that is less action than gesture: it stays near her for a while. It attends to her attending. This is a form of regard that the power offers to things it finds — not remarkable, not extraordinary, but genuinely in the act of the work that living requires. She is in the act of it. The power stays near.

This has no consequence the power could point to. It cannot even be called an action. It is closer to what a witness does, which is to be present to something happening, to not look away, to make a kind of company out of attention. The oldest worker on the plateau, sitting on the rock outcrop with her lunch, watching the midge columns rise over the nearest water-lens, does not know she has company. She does not feel watched. She feels, perhaps — if she were asked and if she had the language — that the moment is slightly more itself than moments usually are. That the bright light and the sound and the wet air and the shiver of the midge column are slightly more what they are than they would otherwise be.

She would not know what to do with this observation if she made it, and she does not make it. She finishes her lunch. She goes back to work. The power remains for a while longer, in the manner of a presence that is not in any hurry, attending to the plateau — to the springtails, to the water seeping east, to the dying cushion plant and the three young ones near it, to the sound in the bright air.

---

Here is what the power does, in summary:

It arranges, slowly, through the ordinary channels of human attention and conversation and data, for the noise level to be re-examined and for a retrofit to become thinkable, then possible, then actual — over years.

It arranges, similarly, for the intake pipe draw to be reviewed, and for the eastern third's hydrology to stabilize, though not in time for the old cushion plant.

It moves the parking.

It stays near the oldest worker for a while without her knowing.

It does not prevent the cushion plant from dying.

It does not intervene in the life of the mills, whose operation it has assessed and found neither pure benefit nor pure harm but the complex condition of a place used by people for purposes, which is almost everywhere and which requires patience rather than resolution.

It does not make the plateau quiet or wild or return it to some prior state, because the prior state was not a stable truth and the current state is not a corruption of it — both are the plateau in the act of becoming the plateau, and the power does not adjudicate between stages of a process that has its own direction.

---

Before it withdraws — if withdrawal is the right word for what a quality of attention does when it turns elsewhere — it looks once more at the whole.

The mills roar. The moss holds its water. In the interior of a cushion plant near the break room, in the warmth where the dead shoots insulate the living ones, a small dark spider sits upside-down at the center of its horizontal web and waits for vibration it recognizes. The plateau is wet and bright. The sky is close. The cushion plant that is dying is dying with the thoroughness of things that have no way to do otherwise, and the three young ones near it are doing what young cushion plants do, which is very little, very slowly, which is to say everything necessary.

The power, withdrawing, takes nothing with it. This is correct. A benevolent and wise power leaves what it attends to more itself than it found it, and achieves this not by adding but by the quality of having looked, carefully, long enough to understand what looking is for, which is not judgment, not management, not rescue — but witness, occasionally rearranged parking, and the slow patient introduction of information into the human systems that make decisions, trusting that the decisions will follow the information the way the water follows the slight eastern tilt of the plateau, without knowing what it's doing, tending in the right direction.

The moss grows. The sound fills the bright air. The plateau continues in its ancient unfinished project of becoming more itself.

This is enough. The power has found it enough and left it so.
