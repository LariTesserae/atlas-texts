# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way the mist arrives: not from anywhere, not going anywhere, simply present where it was not before.

It does not announce itself. There is no change in the light — the light here doesn't change that way, has never changed that way, and the power understands this, has taken note of it. The grey remains grey. The falls pour down the same black-green face. The dipper bobs.

The power is very old. Older than the gorge, though that is not a meaningful measure — it does not experience itself as older than things, does not experience itself in duration at all, really, having long ago stopped moving through time the way water moves through stone. It is more accurate to say: it has been paying attention for a long time. To many places. To this place, now, because something here warranted attention.

It looks at the gorge the way you look at something you love: without urgency, without agenda, without the need for it to be other than it is.

---

It sees the falls first, of course — everything here encounters the falls first — and what it sees is not the water but the *pattern*, the falling as the gorge's primary fact, the thing that precedes and underlies everything else. It sees the black-green face of the stone and understands in an instant what the moss-that-is-not-moss has been doing, the slow collaborative project of contact and centuries, and it is moved by this — moved being the right word, something shifting in it the way a long chord shifts when one note is added — because the moss-that-is-not-moss did not intend to be remarkable. It simply pressed against the stone for long enough, and something happened.

This is the thing the power has seen a great many times and has never stopped finding worth pausing over: *the remarkable as the consequence of simple persistence in the right place.* The moss did not know it was doing anything. It was only being what it was, in contact with what was there.

The power does not think this. It apprehends it, the way you apprehend cold — fully, immediately, in the whole body.

---

It moves through the gorge without moving. Attends to each thing.

The ferns: it notices with something like satisfaction that the ferns have solved verticality so completely they have forgotten it was a problem. It notes the contract between root and rock, the mutual holding that has gone past memory of its initiation. There is something here it has seen elsewhere — in very long marriages, in certain collaborations between artists who have worked together so long that the seam between their contributions has become invisible — this dissolution of the boundary between the holder and the held. It regards this with approval, if approval is not too small a word for what it feels. Appreciation. Recognition.

The dipper: it watches the dipper walk into the current and feels something that, in a human being, would manifest as a laugh — not at the dipper's expense, not condescending, but the delight of encountering something that is perfectly, completely, uncomplicatedly itself. The dipper does not hedge. The dipper does not consider. The dipper enters the water because the water is where the food is, and that is all, and this totality — this absolute correspondence between what the dipper is and what the dipper does — is, from where the power stands, genuinely glorious. It has seen creatures twist themselves into terrible shapes trying to be other than they are. The dipper is a respite from that. The dipper is what it looks like when the question has not arisen.

It spends a long time with the dipper. Longer than you might expect.

---

The spider's web stops the power entirely.

Not because it is extraordinary — it is, locally, extraordinary, the specific solved problem of the sheltered angle and the droplet-heavy threads — but because of what the power notices inside the solution: the spider's knowledge is so deep it has become invisible to the spider. The spider does not know that it knows. The knowledge has passed through knowing and out the other side into simple being. The spider is the answer to the gorge's problem of how to live here, and it holds that answer without holding it, the way the gorge holds the cold.

The power wonders, briefly, what it would mean to know something that deeply. Then it remembers that it has known things that deeply, once or twice, across its long attention, and it stays with that memory for a moment, and the moment is private.

---

The shelf.

Here the power slows in a different way. What it felt at the dipper was delight, and what it felt at the ferns was recognition, and what it feels at the shelf is something older and more complicated and more tender.

It reads the shelf the way you read a letter from someone you will not hear from again. The chipping marks. The patient fit of stone to stone. The chest height, chosen because whoever chose it was upright and warm-blooded and placed things at the height where they could be seen and approached. The lime deposits that have sealed and held the stones in place for decades, the gorge quietly archiving what was placed here.

What the power understands, which you can only infer, is *who* made the shelf. Not their name — names are not the point, names are almost never the point — but the quality of their attention. The particular texture of a mind that looked at this gorge and thought: *something should be placed here*. That looked at flat stones and thought: *these could serve*. That worked in the deafening, hands cold, breath visible, dressing the stone edge with patient strikes, not because anyone required it but because the place seemed to require it, because the gorge's overwhelming presence had produced in them the response that has always been produced in certain kinds of people by certain kinds of overwhelming presence — the need to acknowledge it, to make something small against the largeness of the thing, to say *I was here and I knew you were here and this is what I made in response*.

The power knows this response. It has watched it for a very long time.

It knows that the person who made the shelf is gone. It knows the person who last tended the fires in the back of the gorge is gone. It holds this fact without flinching — it has had a great deal of practice holding this particular fact — but it holds it gently, the way you hold something heavy that cannot be set down but can be carried carefully.

---

And then it comes to the black patch. The ghost fire in the stone.

This is where the power stops. Where it stands — or holds its attending, which is its form of standing — for a long time.

The fires were built by someone who returned. Who found the way in again and again, across a span of years, through the approach trail and into the sound and through the narrow passage and to the back of the gorge where the walls close in and the trickle comes from the crack and there is something that functions, in this place, as privacy. They built fires here that they could not hear and sat with them in the roar, looking at the light they had made in the grey, and what they were doing is something the power recognizes and does not have a clean word for. Maintenance, perhaps. The maintenance of a relationship with a place, kept up by the discipline of return, by the practice of showing up, of being present in the cold with the made light, of offering the fire not as sacrifice but as — company. As evidence. As *I am still here and I still know you are here and this is what I have to give which is only warmth and light and my own small human presence in your enormous inhuman cold*.

The last person to do this has been gone a long time.

The power holds both things at once: the loss, which is real, and the fact that the stone holds the carbon, which is also real. That the last fire left its mark in the rock and the rock has been slowly, slowly taking that mark inward, and when the rock is at last worn away by the falls — when this gorge is at last reshaped by the patient erosion that the lip of falls resists but cannot refuse forever — the material of the last fire will be in the sediment that flows to the sea. The matter of the offering, dispersed. Distributed everywhere. Still in the world, only differently.

This is either consoling or it is not. The power knows both responses and does not prefer one.

---

It considers what, if anything, to do.

This is where the benevolent and wise power distinguishes itself from other kinds of power: it actually considers. It does not assume that action is required. It does not assume that its presence here calls for an intervention. It has seen what interventions do to places like this. It has watched many well-intentioned hands reach into gorges and alter the terms, and it knows that the gorge's value is not separate from its completeness, its own ongoing negotiation, the contract it has made with fern and water and stone and cold.

What does the gorge need?

The power attends to this question seriously. It attends to the falls, the pattern of the falling, the moss-that-is-not-moss. It attends to the ferns and the dipper and the spider in her crevice. It attends to the shelf and the fire's ghost. It attends to you, standing in the gorge, breath rising into the mist, too large and too warm and too temporary, and it attends to the fact that you are here, that you found this place or were found by it.

The gorge does not need protection. What threatens it is slow and cannot be hastened or prevented, only accepted. The falls will continue until they do not continue, and that is not a problem requiring solution.

The gorge does not need enlargement or addition. It is complete. Whatever the power might add would be a disruption, and what this place is is already extraordinary, and adding to the extraordinary risks making it merely spectacular, which is a lesser thing.

The gorge does not need the loss repaired — the person who made the shelf, the person who kept the fires. The power could not repair those losses even if repair were right, which it is not. The losses are part of the gorge now, held in the stone, held in the lime deposits, held in the carbon that is being slowly taken in.

---

What the gorge needs — what the power finally determines, after its long attending — is exactly what it already has, and what it already has is: continuation.

So this is what the power does.

*It witnesses.*

Fully. Without distraction. Without agenda. It brings the whole of its long attention to bear on this gorge, this particular cold and sound and green, and it sees it — sees it with the totality that the gorge has never quite been seen with before, because the people who came here could only see it with human attention, which wanders, which compares, which cannot stop narrating. The power sees the gorge without narrating it. Sees the falls as the falls see themselves: as the act of falling, continuous, complete. Sees the ferns without thinking *fern*. Sees the dipper without thinking *bird* or *small* or *remarkable*. Sees the shelf and holds, simultaneously and without contradiction, the absence of what was placed there and the presence of the care that placed it.

It is a complete seeing. This is what the power offers. This is the gift it has that it can give without disturbing anything: a seeing thorough enough to be a kind of honoring, a kind of love applied with perfect care so that it leaves no mark.

The gorge is seen. Fully. The falls pour. The ferns hold their green against the dark stone. The dipper bobs, finds something in the current, reappears, bobs. The spider's web trembles with droplets in the falls-breath. The silt moves its slow grain-by-grain movement toward the sea. The carbon sits in the stone, going inward, patient with the project.

The power stays until it has seen everything.

Then it leaves the way it came: without ceremony, without announcement, simply no longer present where it was, the mist closing over the space it occupied, the falls continuing in their complete indifference, the gorge going on.

---

You, standing in the gorge, feel nothing at the moment of its departure. You felt nothing at its arrival either.

Or — 

No. That is not quite right. Something, at some point during your time here, arrived in you that you did not bring with you when you came. Not an idea. Not a feeling, exactly. More like a quality of the air inside your chest: a brief and inexplicable sense that you are being kept company. That your being here is, in some way you cannot source or explain, not unremarked upon. That the gorge — the falls, the ferns, the cold, the small loud ancient place — has registered your presence. Is not indifferent to you.

This is impossible. You know it's impossible. The falls do not note you. The ferns are not aware of you. You know this.

And yet. The feeling is there, and it is strangely steady, and it persists as you turn to go, and it stays with you through the narrow passage and out into the comparative quiet of the approach trail, where your footsteps become audible again, where your voice would reach someone if someone were there.

You think about it sometimes, afterward. In ordinary rooms, in ordinary quiet. Not often. But sometimes the particular quality of it returns — that sense of being seen in the cold, in the roar, in the fern-green and the grey — and for a moment you are back in the gorge, inside the sound, and something there knows you, and you are, briefly and entirely, not alone.

You do not know what to do with this.

You don't do anything with it.

You carry it the way the rock carries the cold: accumulated, stored, released slowly and incompletely, never quite all the way gone.
