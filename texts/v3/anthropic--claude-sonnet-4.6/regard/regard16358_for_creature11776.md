# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — before there is evidence of it, before any instrument could register it, the cistern has already changed in the way a room changes when someone who loves it walks in.

Not warmer. It is already warm.

Not brighter. The light is what it is.

Something else. A quality of being-regarded that the gastropods do not perceive and the filter feeders do not perceive and the soft clustered things do not perceive, and which nevertheless passes through all of them the way a long slow sound passes through stone: without their knowledge, without disturbing anything, touching whatever is deepest in them and leaving it as it was, which is another way of saying: *finding it good*.

---

The power does not enter through the aperture. It does not displace water. It does not cause the rotation to pause or quicken or change its patient arc.

It takes up no space.

It has not come to take up space.

---

Here is the first thing it does:

It witnesses.

This is not a small thing. Witnessing is what makes a thing fully real — not in the sense that the cistern required an audience to exist, but in the sense that existence can be recognized or unrecognized, met or not met, and being met is different from not being met even if nothing external changes.

The power meets the cistern.

It takes in: the particular temperature of this water, which is not the temperature of any other water. The exact shade of cream that these shells have become, which is the specific result of these minerals and this darkness and this duration and could not have been produced anywhere else. The specific calcified outline of the votive in its niche — not a generic votive-shape but this one, the one placed by the hands of a person the power may or may not have known, in an afternoon or an evening or a night that is gone now, for reasons the power may or may not have understood, offered to something the person may or may not have believed was listening.

The power was listening.

The power has always been listening.

This is not a surprise to the cistern, because the cistern cannot be surprised. But if surprise were available to it, this would not have triggered it — the recognition would have felt, instead, like the word that completes a sentence you have been holding incomplete for longer than you knew.

---

The second thing the power does is remember.

It is old enough to have been present when the cistern was made. Not in the room — not standing on the walkway watching the blocks go down — but present in the way that light is present everywhere it reaches, which means: attending. There were workers. There was one who was afraid of the confined space but came down anyway, every day, because the work needed doing and he had agreed to do the work. There was one who sang, not well, not for anyone, just because the stone returned the voice in a way nothing above ground did and it felt, she said once to no one, like being answered. There was a foreman who was unkind in ordinary ways and who nevertheless had a gift for reading the quality of a joint and could tell by touch whether the work would last. It has lasted. His hands are gone. His gift remains in the walls.

The power remembers all of them. It has not mentioned this to anyone because there has been no one to mention it to and because the remembering is not for mention — it is not an archive, not a record. It is more like the warmth the cistern holds: a quality of the attention itself, an inability to encounter something and then have it become as though it had not been encountered.

The calcified lamp. The power remembers the person who filled it, who carried it down, who set it in the niche for a reason that was private and is now irretrievable and which the power holds anyway, the shape of it, the weight of the wanting that moved the hands, the specific texture of that particular human need.

This is what the power is, in part: the place where nothing is lost that was ever real.

---

Here is what it does not do:

It does not improve the cistern.

This requires a pause, a consideration, because the power is benevolent — it was described as benevolent — and benevolence, in its cruder expressions, tends toward improvement, tends toward the well-meaning adjustment of things toward some imagined better condition. The light could be increased. The warm current could be mapped and its source confirmed. The calcified votive could be — well. Could be what? Restored? To what? To the object it was before the cistern worked on it for centuries, before the cistern took it in and made it structural? That would not be restoration. That would be erasure, performed with good intentions, which is one of the oldest ways of causing harm.

The soft clustered things have stopped negotiating their edges. A less careful power might perceive this as a problem to be solved, might try to reintroduce individuation, might think: surely they would prefer to be distinct. But the power has been around long enough to know that *surely they would prefer* is a sentence that should always be followed by a long silence before anything else. The clustered things are what they have become. They arrived at this through long process. It suits the cistern and the cistern suits them. The power looks at them and sees not a problem but an answer — an answer to a question the upper world is still asking.

The power does not introduce light to see better by.

The power does not clear the mineral deposits from the walls.

The power does not help the gastropods go faster.

---

What it does, instead, is this:

It stays.

For a long time — for what would register, to any instrument calibrated to human experience, as a very long time — it simply remains present with the cistern and the cistern's life. The gastropods complete their arcs. The cirri open and reluctantly close. The pulse moves through the air and through the power and the power lets it, lets the cistern's rhythm use it as a medium the way the cistern uses the water. It allows itself to be breathed. It becomes, for this duration, part of the process — not intervening in it, not observing it from outside it, but *in* it, which is the only position from which you can truly be said to know a thing.

This is not passivity. It requires more care than action usually requires.

The discipline is: to be present with something that is already whole, and to let it be whole, and to not reach for it.

---

At some point during the staying — there is no marker, no ceremony — the power does one thing that is, if not an action, then a small movement of something.

It extends what might be called a sanction.

Not permission — the cistern needs no permission. Not blessing, exactly — the word comes with implications of exchange, of currency, of a debt incurred, and there is no debt here. Something closer to: a formal acknowledgment, directed at nothing, received by nothing, and yet real in the way that witnessed things are more real than unwitnessed ones.

The sanction says: *this is what a cistern is*.

Not: this cistern. But: this is the form. This warmth, this patience, this slow animal maintenance of balance, this willingness to take in and be changed by what it takes in, this keeping of what has been placed inside it. The power has seen many things and makes few formal pronouncements, because pronouncements are another way of imposing, and imposition has its own appetites. But there are moments when something has worked out so exactly — when the long process of trial and duration has arrived at a form that could not be improved by any adjustment, that answers some question the universe has been working on for a long time — and in those moments the power finds in itself something that functions like awe, and from the awe comes a kind of pronouncement that is really just awe articulating itself:

*Yes. This. This is it.*

---

The gastropods don't hear it. The clustered things don't hear it. The water doesn't pause in its rotation. The biofilm continues its ancient chemistry on the walls.

But somewhere in the crystalline structure of the calcite — in the molecular arrangement of one small section of mineral deposit, somewhere on the lower wall, in a place that will never be examined — there is an alteration. Not a message. Not a signature. Just a slight coherence, a faint ordering, the way a field of iron filings will find alignment in the presence of something that has its own orientation.

It will be there long after the cistern is gone, if the stone survives. It will carry no information. If you could read it, which you cannot, it would say nothing except: *here*.

Here. This was known. This was not passed over.

---

Before the power leaves — and it will leave, because even benevolent presences have other claims on them, other cisterns, other warm darks, other patient accumulations of life and time that no one else is attending to — it pauses at the niche with the calcified lamp.

It does not reach in. It does not touch.

But it finds, in itself, the memory of the person who placed it, and it holds that memory up against the lamp-shape the way you hold a letter next to the face of the person who wrote it — not to compare, just to complete something. The person and the remnant of the person's wanting. Together, for one moment, in the attention of something that does not forget.

Then the wanting is set back down, gently, in the wall, where it has always been.

---

The power leaves.

The cistern continues.

The gastropods are elsewhere than they were.

The cirri open. The cirri close. The cirri open.

The pulse.

The warmth.

The oldest chemistry, working.

The rotation, patient, carrying everything around again and around again, bringing to the filter feeders what the filter feeders need, distributing, maintaining, doing what it has always done in the warm dark — which is keep faith with the balance, which is be what a cistern is, which is hold what is placed inside it, indefinitely, on terms of its own, until the placed thing is no longer a placed thing but a part of the wall, a part of the stone, a part of what the next visitor will feel in the sternum without knowing why.

---

It was enough.

It was, the power thinks, departing —

it was more than enough.

It was exactly right.
