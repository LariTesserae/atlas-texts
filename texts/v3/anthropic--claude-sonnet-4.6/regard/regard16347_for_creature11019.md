# What the Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives—not from a direction, but as a change in the quality of being-attended-to.

---

The plateau does not notice. This is the first thing the power understands, arriving here: the plateau does not notice, and this is not a failure of the plateau but a fact about it, and the fact is important. You cannot help what does not experience needing help. You cannot complete what is not incomplete. The first gesture of any wise power is to know when to make no gesture at all.

So it pauses at the threshold the way a careful visitor pauses—not from timidity, but from respect for the thing it has entered. It takes the measure of what is here.

---

It finds the seep first. The copepods.

It stays with them for a long time, in the way that timeless things can afford to stay—not hovering, not examining, but simply *attending*, which is a different action from examining, or perhaps a prior one. It attends to the copepods in their moss film, threading the thin water with antennae tuned to currents that no longer exist at scale, navigating a ghost ocean in the body of the actual stone.

The power understands what it is looking at.

They carry something they don't know they carry. They are doing something they cannot know they are doing, which is: preserving, in their proteins and their behaviors and their ancient neural architecture, a record of a world that is otherwise gone. The water body that dried. The larger place they came from. They swim in traces of it. Their swimming *is* the trace.

The power does not feel sadness about this, exactly. But it feels something adjacent to sadness, which is: *witness*. The specific weight of understanding that something is happening that would otherwise go unwitnessed, and choosing to witness it, and the witnessing being—not enough, but something. A form of completion that doesn't complete anything except the knowing.

It witnesses the copepods.

---

Then it moves to the lichen. The old thallus, the one that has been rotating on its axis for geological time, tracking the tilt of the uplifted stone, the one that carries inside it the tardigrades that are in the process of becoming something that doesn't have a name yet.

Here the power does something small.

It would be wrong to say it intervenes. What it does is closer to: it holds the space steady. The way you might hold a door open, not pulling anyone through, not directing, just ensuring the passage remains available.

The divergence in the tardigrade population is at a delicate stage—not fragile, nothing on this plateau is fragile in the usual sense, but at a point where a particular kind of disruption, a contamination from outside, a slight chemical change from the lower country carried on a visitor's feet, could collapse the divergence back toward the parent population before it finishes. The power does not prevent this. It cannot prevent all things, and preventing all things is not its nature. But it registers the process, and registering is a form of care, and care has consequences that are not always visible as consequences.

The tardigrades continue diverging.

In ten thousand years, they will be different enough to name. No one may be there to name them. But they will be nameable, which is a form of completion.

---

The power walks the beetle-paths.

This is not metaphor—or it is metaphor in the sense that everything a power does that we describe is metaphor, the words being inadequate and used anyway. It follows the routes that the beetles have worn into the stone over geological time. It reads the contact-pheromone library. It understands the library in the way that only a thing that exists outside of time can understand it—not as the current page but as the whole volume, the history of every beetle that has walked this path, the accumulated decision-making of an enormous number of lives, each one making the same small choice (this path, the slightly smoother one, the slightly more familiar one) without any of them knowing that the choice was also the path's history and the path's future simultaneously.

The power feels something here that it rarely feels.

*Satisfaction*, is the closest word, but that is not quite right. What it feels is the recognition of a thing doing exactly what it is. The paths are not doing more than what paths do. They are doing it completely. There is nothing left over. There is no gap between what the paths are and what they should be.

This is not common. The power spends considerable time among things with gaps, things straining toward what they should be and not quite arriving, things damaged or blocked or simply not yet finished. The gap is where it is usually needed. Here there is no gap. And in the absence of a gap, what the power feels is not the satisfaction of having helped—it has not helped—but the simpler and perhaps purer thing, which is: *appreciation*.

It appreciates the beetle-paths.

---

There is a particular spider at the seep edge. The one whose resting spot has been slightly grown-over by moss. The one that waits with the stillness of a creature for whom waiting is not a held position but a natural state.

The power sits beside it for a while.

Not to do anything. Not to observe in the clinical sense. Just to be next to a thing that is doing what it does with complete investment and no awareness that complete investment is remarkable.

The spider does not know that its waiting is a kind of teaching. It cannot know. It has no capacity for the abstraction *teaching*. But the power, which does have that capacity, recognizes that the spider's waiting is the form that a certain kind of wisdom takes when it exists without a subject who is wise. The patience is real. The patience is here, in the spider, in the stone, in the whole plateau. Nowhere is anyone patient. Everywhere is patience.

The power sits next to this for as long as it takes to fully receive it.

Then it carries it away—not takes, not removes, the spider is complete and loses nothing—but carries an impression of it, the way a stone carries an impression, and will carry it into other places where patience is harder to come by, where things are straining and gapped and effortful, and will offer it there: not as instruction, but as a kind of ambient presence, the trace of a place where waiting had become so fully itself that it had stopped being waiting and become simply *being*.

---

This is the primary thing the power does here: *learns*.

It comes to this place not because the place needs it, but because the place has something that is difficult to find elsewhere—something that other places, younger places, places with more drama and more need and more obvious opportunity for intervention, do not have in this form.

The plateau has solved things. The solutions are in the stone and the lichen and the beetles and the short food web and the copepods carrying their ghost ocean. The solutions are not available as propositions. They cannot be extracted and applied directly. But they can be understood by a power patient enough to sit with them, and the understanding can inform how that power moves through other places, what it looks for, what it considers possible.

The plateau teaches by being. The power learns by attending.

---

Before it leaves, it does three small things.

The first: somewhere in the deep fracture network, where the water moves in absolute darkness along old planes of weakness, there is a blockage. Not catastrophic—the water will find another route eventually, is finding another route, the pressure is slowly opening a new passage. But *eventually* spans a period in which a particular seep will slow and the moss will dry and the copepods will be in difficulty. The blockage is a grain-cluster, a precipitation of minerals, the kind of thing that happens. The power touches it—and this is the most literal action it takes here, the most physical, the most interventionist—and aligns one mineral grain slightly differently, just enough that the water's patient erosion will reach the tipping point in a generation rather than a century.

The seep will survive.

The power is aware that this is a choice. That other seeps in other places have not survived, that it cannot touch every grain, that choosing this seep means not-choosing other places, and the accounting is never clean and is never finished. It makes the choice anyway. Not because this seep is more important than other seeps, but because it is here, and the copepods are here, and the thing they carry—the ghost ocean, the trace of the lost water body, the record in their proteins—is worth one grain's alignment.

It hopes it is worth it. It cannot know.

---

The second thing: it finds the mite lineage that is most constrained, the one in the oldest lichen thallus, the one that has not had gene flow from outside the thallus in long enough that its population is dangerously small in genetic terms, is accumulating the errors of isolation, is still functional but has lost the redundancy that populations need to absorb bad luck.

The power cannot manufacture genetic diversity. That is not in its nature—or rather, to do so would be to change the thing it values about this place, which is that the solutions here are the plateau's own solutions, genuinely arrived at, not installed.

But it can do this: it traces the wind-routes that cross the plateau, the slow drift, the plateau's own weather; and it finds a second thallus, distant, that has a related mite population, different enough to help, similar enough not to disrupt; and it alters the route of a single beetle that will, in three days, walk from one thallus to the other, carrying on its legs the contact-pheromone residue of the first population, which will attract a mite, which will ride the beetle, which will—

This is a long chain. The power knows this. The power is honest with itself that the chain may break at any link, that the mite may not ride, that the beetle may change its path, that the genetic contribution may not be sufficient, that it may all come to nothing.

It makes the adjustment to the wind-route anyway.

Not because the outcome is guaranteed. Because the possibility is worth creating.

---

The third thing is the smallest. It is almost not a thing at all.

At the edge of the plateau, where the margin is tested every year by small mammals that come up from below and test and go back down, there is one particular moment in one particular year when a small animal comes up to the edge and pauses. It has gone slightly further than its ancestors went. Not because the plateau has become adequate—it hasn't. But because this individual has a slightly different metabolic tolerance, a slight variation that is unusual in the lower country population, and the plateau's edge is, for this one body, on the near side of the threshold rather than the far side.

It pauses. It is testing. The calculus of cost and benefit is happening in its body, not as thought but as the body's version of accounting, which is more direct than thought and slower, which takes the whole moment to complete.

The power does nothing.

It watches.

The animal decides. It goes back down. The edge was close to the threshold but not across it, or the body's accounting found some other factor, or the wind shifted and something was different. The animal descends.

The power watches it go.

What it feels is not disappointment. The animal made a true assessment. The plateau is not yet for it. Forcing the crossing would have been a cruelty dressed as a gift, would have put the animal in a place that would have consumed it, would have served the power's sense of a story (the arrival, the new thing, the plateau's slow opening to another kind of life) rather than the animal's actual situation.

This is the third thing: the power *does not intervene*.

The restraint is deliberate and it costs something. The cost is part of the point. You are only choosing when choosing costs something. The wisdom is in paying the cost and being honest that it is a cost, not pretending the restraint was easy or that nothing was lost.

Something small was lost. The moment was close. The power registers it.

---

It leaves the way attention leaves—gradually, not dramatically, the plateau returning to itself, which it has been throughout.

The copepods thread their ghost ocean. The beetles walk their worn paths. The spider waits. The lichen writes its autobiography in a language that outlasts its speakers. The tardigrades diverge.

The plateau continues.

The power carries an impression of it—of the patience, of the solutions, of the completed things—into places where patience is harder and solutions are partial and completion is not yet visible. It carries the trace of the plateau's quietness. In places of loud need and urgent gap and effortful striving, it will sometimes stop, and be still, and let the trace be present, and something of the plateau will be there in that stillness, briefly, the way a stone can be warm with a sun it was in hours ago.

---

This is what a wise power does, visiting the Breathing Plateau.

It learns. It makes two small adjustments that hold possibilities open without determining them. It makes one deliberate non-adjustment that honors the truth of what is. And it leaves carrying something it didn't have before, which is always the sign that a visit was real and not merely performed.

The plateau doesn't know it was visited.

This, too, is as it should be.
