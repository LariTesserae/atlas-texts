# What the Power Does

---

The power arrives the way warmth arrives — not from a direction, not at a moment you can identify afterward, but simply: it was not present, and then it was present, and the cooling yard is the same and is also held.

It does not arrive with intention in the way intentions usually work, as a vector from desire toward outcome. It arrives more the way attention arrives. As if something very large has, for a moment, looked here.

---

The first thing it does is: nothing.

This is not inaction. It is the specific discipline of a benevolence that has learned — that *has had to learn*, in the long expensive way — that the first impulse of a power that loves a place is to improve it, and that improvement is usually a form of imposition, and that imposition, however gentle, is a form of saying: *what is here is not enough, and I know what enough would look like.* The power has made this error before. It does not make it here.

So it stands — or whatever standing is for something that doesn't have a location — and it looks. 

Really looks.

---

It sees the wren come in over the top of the loose brick. Pause. Drop.

Something in it responds to this that is not quite emotion and is not quite cognition and is the thing that was there before the distinction between those two things was made. A response that might translate, inadequately, as: *yes. yes, that.* The specific perfection of the wren's half-second pause — not hesitation, not caution, but the pause of a creature that has learned its route so thoroughly that it can afford to be briefly present at the threshold, briefly in neither place, before committing to the interior where the young are loud now, demanding, requiring the adult to be entirely there and not here. The pause is the last moment of between-ness before the wholeness of the arrival. The wren does not know this. It does not need to know it. It is doing it anyway.

The power watches the wren the way you might watch a very old piece of music being played by someone who has forgotten they are performing it.

---

It moves its attention along the algae stripe.

This takes what would seem, from outside, like an absurd amount of care for a discoloration on a drainage channel on an unnamed wall in a courtyard that appears on no map. But the power is interested in the algae in the way that a reader of very long books is interested in a first sentence — not because the sentence is the best part, but because it is where everything is still possible, where the whole shape of what is coming is present in embryo, coiled in those few words that don't yet know what they're the beginning of.

The algae doesn't know it's writing a sentence. The power knows, and is moved by the not-knowing.

It traces the stripe to its current upper edge, where the biofilm is youngest and thinnest, where individual cells are still the whole character of the thing rather than contributors to a community. It sees what is there and what is almost-there and what will be there in decades if the overflow pipe continues to drip, if the wall is not repointed, if no one decides this gap has a better use. It sees the contingency clearly, without flinching from it. This is part of its benevolence — it does not protect itself from the grief of contingency by making promises it can't keep or smoothing over the reality that most of what is becoming is always at risk of not becoming.

It does not guarantee the algae's future.

But it looks at the algae with the full weight of its attention, which is — the power has come to understand this slowly, over a long time — one of the rarest things that can be given. To be seen without agenda. To have your specific presentness registered, not as a means to some further end, not as a problem to be solved or a beauty to be preserved or a lesson to be learned, but as *what is here, which is real, which matters, which I am looking at.*

The algae continues its work. It is not changed by the looking. But perhaps the looking is itself a form of the warmth the place needs — not the warmth of the pipe, not the warmth of the sun through the gap, but the warmth of being witnessed, which is different from being seen.

---

There is a person.

The power becomes aware, in widening its attention, that a person has been in the cooling yard. Is, in some sense, still there — not present now, gone now, but recently enough that their trace is warm, their looking has left something in the air the way breath does. Someone stood here and tried to see this place with clarity. Tried to give it the gift of attention, tried to hold the wren and the algae and the tropical-leaved plant and the sparrow in the ivy in a single frame of looking, tried to let them be what they are.

The power finds this — moves near to this — in the way a parent moves near to a child who is doing, awkwardly, the difficult thing the parent knows is difficult.

Because what the person was doing in this yard, the power recognizes: it was the same thing the power does. The same discipline of witnessing without imposition. The same patience with the slow and the small. The same attention to the wren's half-second pause, the same understanding that the algae stripe is a first sentence, the same refusal to improve. This person stood here and practiced — perhaps without knowing they were practicing anything — the fundamental act of a certain kind of love, which is to find a place or a being in its own terms and consent to those terms, and stay.

What does the power do with this?

It does not intervene in the person's life. It does not arrange their circumstances toward flourishing, does not clear obstacles, does not whisper encouragements or insights they might act on. This is not because it doesn't care. It is because the person, in the cooling yard, was doing something that such interventions would interrupt. The person was learning to look without wanting to change what they saw. Interventions come from wanting to change. The power respects the lesson too much to provide counter-evidence.

What it does instead is: it stays near for a while. Near the trace of attention the person left, near the particular quality of their looking, near the specific way this human being found a gap between intended things and recognized it as a place where something was happening, and did not dismiss it as accidental, and did not romanticize it as precious, and did not try to tell it what to become.

The power does not bless the person. It does not protect them.

It is simply — briefly, in whatever way it is present in time — glad they exist. Specifically glad, the way you are glad for a specific tree or a specific song, not because trees or songs are good in general but because *this one*: this one is like this.

---

Now the ferns.

The power spends time with the ferns because they are bright, almost yellow-green, the color of things that have found sufficient shadow and made of it a sufficiency. There is something in that particular color that it finds difficult to pass quickly. That specific hue is the color of a decision: the decision to optimize for shadow rather than sun, to become fully what this particular context allows rather than struggling toward better light. The ferns are not making a virtue of constraint. They are *made* of this context, continuous with it, their specific color a metabolic response to the specific amount of light that enters this specific gap at this specific angle. They are not anywhere else adapted to elsewhere. They are here, adapted to here.

There are types of wisdom that look like this, the power thinks. Shaped to the gap they've been given. Not resigned to it. Made by it.

It does not speak to the ferns. This is not a world in which it speaks to ferns, and this is a boundary it respects: the boundary of what this world is and what the ferns are. It does not transcend the nature of ferns in order to give them something they haven't asked for and couldn't use. Instead it crouches — metaphorically crouches, in whatever way it can approach the small and ground-level — and holds the ferns' specific yellowish-green in the full register of its attention, and lets that be the whole of what happens between it and them.

The ferns continue their millimeter-per-year advance into the flagging. This is not affected by the attention. The attention was not for the advance. The attention was for the ferns.

---

There is a question the power sits with for a long time in the cooling yard, long enough that the light has shifted and the starlings are gone from the wire and the sparrow has finally emerged from the ivy and done its sparrow thing and departed for somewhere unknown.

The question is: what would it mean to help?

Not whether to help. The power is not committed to non-intervention in principle; it doesn't make itself feel better about inaction by calling it respect. It holds the question genuinely: is there something being asked for here, something the yard or the wren or the algae or the person who stood here and looked — is there something any of them is asking for, even implicitly, even in the way that need can be present without being articulated?

It listens carefully. This is not a brief listening. It is the listening that requires setting down everything it might want to give, everything it might be capable of giving, and waiting with empty hands.

What it hears, in the long listening, is: *the stamping press. the turbine hum. the underground water. the wren going in and out. the ferns. the algae advancing.*

The yard is not asking for anything.

This is the most clarifying answer it could receive, and also, in some ways, the most difficult. The yard is not asking for anything because the yard is, in its specific way, already in a process of becoming what it is, and what it needs for that process is exactly what it has: this gap, this light, this moisture, this warmth from the pipe, this time. A benevolent intervention would add something to this. The power sits with the question of whether addition is the same as help.

It decides, slowly, that in this case it is not. That the yard is not a problem to be solved. That the wrens are not in need of protection that would not be needed. That the algae does not require acceleration. That the person who stood here and looked is not, by virtue of having stood here and looked with care, suffering in a way that calls for remedy.

This could be a failure of imagination. The power knows it could be a failure of imagination. It holds this honestly. Maybe there is something it is missing, some latent suffering in the wren or the algae or the person that a more sensitive attention would catch. Maybe its listening is not as clear as it thinks.

But it cannot find the ask. And in the absence of the ask, it believes — after a long time of learning this, after making the other error many times — that the most loving thing it can do is what it has been doing: be here, see clearly, hold the place in the full weight of attention, and trust that what is becoming is capable of becoming it.

---

The one thing it does.

At the last, before its attention turns — because the nature of it is that attention turns, because there are other cooling yards, other gaps, other places where the inadvertent is slowly becoming specific — it does one thing.

It is a small thing. The smallest possible version of an action.

The loose brick in the older wall — the one the wrens nest in, the one whose partial disengagement from the mortar has left a cavity exactly the right size for exactly this purpose — the power holds it.

Not in place. The brick was already in place. The mortar has been failing for years and will continue to fail and eventually, in some decade, someone may notice and repoint the wall, and the cavity will close, and the wrens will find another cavity somewhere nearby, which they will, because they always do.

No. The power holds the brick in a different sense. The same sense in which you hold something in memory — not to prevent it from changing, but to mark it as having been real, to register that this particular conjunction of failing mortar and bird and nest and young and half-second pause and mother's route along the wall footing and father's route over the top — this happened. This is happening. The care that made this cavity a home is real and the wrens' commitment to their young is real and the exhaustion of the parents is real and the loudening of the young is real and this is all *actual*, all fully present in the world, not accidentally but as the result of a wren-shaped intelligence that has been operating in this gap and finding it sufficient and making it better and will continue to do so until the circumstances change.

The power marks it.

Not for itself. It doesn't need to mark things for itself.

For the universe — for whatever accounting the universe makes of what has occurred in it — the power marks this: *here is a wren. here is the specific fact of this wren, in this wall, on this afternoon, in this yard that appeared on no map. here is a life at the full intensity of its living. see it.*

This is all.

This is not nothing.

---

The stamping press continues. The turbine hums beneath. The water runs in its culvert. The algae advances toward the drain by a thickness that has no visible units.

The power does not leave. Things like the power do not leave. But its attention has moved toward the horizon in the way that presence can move without departure, toward the other gaps and the other green and the other beings in the specific intensity of their living, the other places that appear on no map, the other un-asked-for accumulations of the living alongside the made.

The cooling yard continues.

The wren comes out over the top of the brick. Pauses half a second. Goes.
