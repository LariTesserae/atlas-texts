# What the Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not from outside but as a deepening of what is already present. Not an addition. A clarifying.

The first thing it does is *notice*. This sounds like nothing, but it is not nothing. To be noticed by something that sees clearly is to be changed, slightly, in the direction of your own nature. The ferns do not know they are being seen and do not need to know. The noticing happens anyway. It is complete without their participation.

The power looks at the chamber the way you look at something you love that does not know you love it — a sleeping child, a river from a bridge, a city you've returned to after years. With full attention. Without agenda. The look itself is a form of care.

It does not immediately *do* anything.

This is the first wisdom: to resist the impulse to intervene before understanding what you're intervening in. The wise power sits with the chamber — if sitting is the word for what it does, which it isn't quite, but approach it from that direction — and it takes stock. It follows the water's route from above, the faint channel, the northwest corner. It feels the machine's warmth and traces what the machine is connected to, far into the rock, the longer system the visible part belongs to. It reads the crack in the eastern wall, its arrested gesture, the pressure still residing in it. It notes the gloves. It notes the stool placed rather than abandoned.

Someone has been here. Someone comes here.

The power does not know this person but holds the fact of them with attention — the faint evidence of their passage, the precision of the stool's placement, the gloves folded. It constructs nothing from this. It makes no judgment about intention or story. It simply registers: *a person has been here. This place is part of someone's life.*

This changes what the wise power does next. Which is: almost nothing. But with great care.

---

The bulbs.

The ones that have burned out: the power does not replace them. It is not an electrician. It is not here to make the room brighter. But it looks at the ferns growing in the dark places, between the burned bulbs, in the intervals — the ones that have been reaching for years toward a light that isn't there anymore, bending at increasingly extreme angles, drawing on stored energy, doing the slow patient work of surviving in diminishment — and it holds them in a quality of attention that is close to what humans mean by *compassion,* which comes from the Latin for *suffering with,* for *feeling alongside.* The power feels alongside the ferns their long lean toward nothing.

And then it does something small.

Not the bulbs. But: it attends to the phosphorescence. Not to increase it — it cannot increase it, would not if it could, that's not how this works. What it does is closer to *permission* than production. It recognizes the phosphorescence as the thing it is. In the recognizing, something loosens. The blue-green light, which was already there, which was already real, which only needed the darkness and the patience of a long adjustment — it continues to be what it was. But the continuing feels, somehow, less accidental. Less leaked. The light was always more than byproduct. The power knows this. In the knowing, the light does not change, but its status does, quietly. In the record of what this place is, something is added to the description of the phosphorescence. It is no longer only unintentional. It is witnessed. Witnessed things are different from unwitnessed things in ways that don't show up on instruments but are nonetheless real.

This is the second thing the power does: it witnesses. Formally, fully, with complete presence. Everything in the chamber is witnessed. The young fern still in its first unrolling. The cable slack over the protrusion. The gloves, dry, on their shelf of stone. The crack that stopped. The corner where the water stands before disappearing. The machine's low frequency in the chest.

The witnessing takes no time and all the time. When it is done, the chamber is the same. The chamber is different.

---

There is a question the power turns over slowly, with the unhurriedness of something that does not have to decide quickly: *what does this place need?*

Not what would make it better by the power's own aesthetic or preference. Not what would make it more dramatic or complete-seeming or easier to understand. What does it *need* — what is the gap between what it is and what it is trying to become, and is that gap something that should be closed, and by whom, and when?

The chamber is in its first chapter. The power knows this. And it knows something else: first chapters are not problems to be solved. The young fern uncurling is not behind schedule. The crack holding its position is not failing to propagate. The absence of animals is not a lack — it is the current state, it is the right state for now, it is the condition under which everything else is establishing itself undisturbed. You do not rush a first chapter. You do not import the fifth chapter's contents into the first because the fifth chapter sounds better.

The power makes a decision. The decision is this: *not yet.*

Not yet for intervention. Not yet for adjustment or addition or any form of doing that would leave the fingerprints of outside intention on what is here. The chamber is in good hands — the hands of its own process, the ferns making humidity for themselves to live in, the water cutting its faint channel toward the corner, the machine warm in its ongoing purpose. These are sufficient hands. The wise power knows the difference between a place that is suffering and a place that is becoming. This place is becoming. You do not rush becoming. You do not mistake it for incompleteness.

But *not yet* is not *nothing.* Not yet is a decision that will be revisited. The power does not leave the chamber in the sense of removing its attention; it leaves the chamber in the sense of stepping back to the distance from which it can watch without disturbing. It will notice if the water stops. It will notice if something enters this chapter that the chapter cannot metabolize. It will be here, at the distance of witness, for the duration.

---

The person, though. The one with the stool and the gloves.

The power holds this longer. Because the person is not like the ferns — the person experiences time, which means the person experiences the gap between what is and what might be, which means the person experiences desire and loss and uncertainty and all the conditions that follow from knowing you are in a place that might mean something, or might not, or might not mean what you think it means. The person is in the chamber differently than the ferns are in the chamber. The person is there *about* something.

The power cannot know what. Does not need to know. What it can do for the person is related to what it has already done — the witnessing — but extended, and intended, and directed. So it does this:

It makes sure.

Not that anything in particular happens to the person. Not that the person is protected from difficulty or loss or the long ambiguities of a life. But: it makes sure that this place, which is part of the person's life, *holds.* That the chamber remains available. That whatever the person returns to when they return here is still here — not frozen, not preserved in amber, but *continuing*. The ferns continuing to grow toward the light. The machine continuing its warmth. The water continuing its episodic arrivals. The first chapter continuing to be first. The person can return to this and find it still in progress, still patient, still becoming. The power makes sure of this, which means it pays attention, which means it maintains its witness, which means the chamber is known, and the knowing is a form of sustaining, which is a form of care.

---

The power also does something for the crack.

This is small, and private, and is perhaps the thing it feels most clearly called to do — and it doesn't understand exactly why, which it takes as information.

The crack in the eastern wall: stopped mid-cut, clean diagonal, the break-line still sharp. Still holding in it the pressure of whatever began it. Still somewhere between what it was and what it might become.

The power sits with the crack for a long time.

It does not widen the crack. Does not release the pressure. Does not resolve the suspended consequence in any direction. The crack will do what the crack will do, in the fullness of geological time, and that process is not the power's to manage.

What the power does is: acknowledge the difficulty of the crack's position. This sounds absurd. The crack has no experience of difficulty. It does not feel the suspension. And yet — there is something in the power's attention to the crack that recognizes something true about it, something the crack *is* even if it cannot know it is, and in the recognizing the crack becomes, in some small way, less alone in its arrested gesture.

To be seen accurately by something that sees is to be less alone. Even if you are stone. Even if you are a process without a nervous system, a tension without a mind to feel it. The power believes this, or rather the power *knows* this the way you know something that precedes argument.

It looks at the crack. It says, in the only language the power and the crack share, which is presence: *yes, I see you. The stopping is real. The pressure is real. What you are between is real. This is a hard place to be. You are here. I see you here.*

The crack does not respond. The crack does not need to respond. The acknowledgment was not a request for response.

---

Finally, it does the thing that is perhaps the most difficult to describe: it *leaves something*.

Not an object. Not a change. Something more like a quality. A residue of having been attended to. The chamber, which was patient before, is now patient in a slightly different register — not the patience of a place indifferent to time, but the patience of a place that has been seen. There is a difference. The difference is thin, imperceptible to instruments, probably imperceptible to the person with the stool and the gloves, maybe perceptible to someone who has spent long hours here and knows the texture of the chamber's silence.

What they might notice, if they notice anything: that the room feels, obscurely, *less alone.* That the darkness between the bulbs has a different quality — still dark, still present, but populated differently, as if the darkness has been named and in the naming has become less than absolute. That the hum from the machine, felt in the chest, carries with it something that wasn't there before, or that was always there but couldn't be felt before, which amounts to the same thing.

The person, if they are sensitive to such things, might put the leather gloves on and stand in the chamber for a while and feel, without being able to say why, that the chamber is okay. Not fixed. Not finished. Not resolved. But *okay* — in the way that things in process are okay, in the way that first chapters are okay, in the way that the fern beside the gloves is okay in its first unrolling, in the way that the water is okay for having disappeared into the deeper crack, in the way that the crack is okay for having stopped. All the suspended, patient, not-yet things: okay.

This is what the wise power leaves: an okay.

Not reassurance, which is something you give to something that has expressed fear. Not comfort, which implies distress. Something prior to both. Something underneath. The recognition that what is here is real and valid and in its right place and being attended to, and that the attending will continue, and that this is enough to say.

---

The power withdraws to the distance of its watching.

The ferns grow toward the light.

The machine is warm in the wall.

The young frond beside the leather gloves uncurls, slowly, into whatever it's becoming, in a chamber that is not done, and is witnessed, and is okay.
