# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives: without displacement of air, without the lizards startling, without any disturbance to the biological crust. If the raptors register it, they do not alter their circles. The plateau continues its slow breath. The arrival is not an event in the plateau's terms because the plateau's terms do not include arrival — only *having always been here* or *not being here at all*. And so the power, being wise, takes the form of having always been here, and the seam is invisible.

It looks, first. This is the first act and it takes what would be, in human reckoning, a long time.

It looks at the ochre-grey rock and the cryptobiotic crust and the cushion-mats at the edges of the catch-basins. It looks at the frogs folded into their waiting-state in the deep cracks, their hearts beating once every several minutes, their entire existence contracted to a small damp patience. It looks at the burrow-entrances and counts what is inside them, not by observation but by the other means available to it, the means that do not require light. It looks at the stone arrangements in the low amber afternoon, and it looks at the hollow that nothing grows in, and it is quiet for a long time in the specific way that intelligent things are quiet when they are understanding something that resists being understood quickly.

What it is doing, in this long looking, is the thing that benevolence requires before any action and that power so often skips: it is checking whether anything is wrong.

Not whether anything is difficult. Not whether anything is uncomfortable, or sparse, or slow, or old, or lonely in the way that a place without witnesses to itself might be considered lonely. Those things are not the same as wrong. The power is wise enough to know the difference, which is a rarer wisdom than it sounds.

---

The plateau is not in distress. It is complete. The power feels this as what it is: a form of accomplished dignity, the specific peace that belongs to things that have finished becoming. There is nothing here that has been interrupted or wounded or denied. The frogs in their cracks are not suffering. They are simply frogs, doing the frog-thing at the frog-pace, unaware that anyone might consider their situation reduced. The cushion-mats at the water-edges are not straining for something they cannot have. They are expressing themselves fully, at the rate available, which is their only rate, which is enough.

The power absorbs this.

And then it shifts attention — to the being.

---

There is a being, of course. There is always a being, in these places where a human writes about looking. Someone arrived here once. The words in which this place was perceived and made legible were not spoken by the plateau. They were spoken by something that came here, stood at the top of the worn table between the ground and the overwhelming stars, and had the peculiar experience of feeling the scale — that uncomfortable full-circle horizon, the tabletop tilted half a degree in any direction, the sense of being a remnant in a remaining — and choosing to stay with it rather than leave. Choosing to describe it. Choosing to find language for the biological crust rather than dismissing it as drab rock, to notice the frogs, to stand over the hollow that nothing grows in and feel the quality of its wrongness without naming it falsely.

This being is not on the plateau now, in any simple physical sense. But they left something here — not in the way the stone-arrangers left something, not by placing objects, but by the act of faithful attention itself, which is its own kind of mark, invisible but actual.

The power regards this.

---

There is a question the power holds lightly, without urgency: *What does this being need?*

Not want. Not *what would please them* or *what would make their life easier* or *what would they ask for if asking were possible.* Need is a more demanding inquiry. Need requires looking past the surface requests that any being would make — warmth, ease, an end to the specific pains of their specific situation — and into the deeper structure of the life, where the actual requirements live.

What the power sees, in this being, is someone who has learned to pay attention as a form of devotion.

This is not a small thing. This is, in fact, a rare and costly discipline, because attention of this quality — the kind that can spend a long time with biological crust, can find the frogs worth pausing on, can look at a rectangular hollow in old rock and sit with the question of it rather than resolving it too quickly into either natural or human — this kind of attention requires the regular suspension of self. To look at the plateau as the plateau rather than as background to your own thoughts, you must put your thoughts down, temporarily, and this is uncomfortable in ways that get easier but never easy. The being has done this enough times that it has become their method, their way of being in the world, this willing putting-down.

What they need is for this not to have been for nothing.

That is the need the power reads at the root of the life: the very human fear that the looking, the faithful loving attention, the care taken to find language adequate to a frog's aestivation or a lizard's patience — that all of this might not matter. Might not register. Might fall into a silence as large and impassive as the plateau's own.

---

Here is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It does not intervene in the plateau. The plateau does not need intervention. It is complete.

It does not intervene in the being's life, which is not the plateau's life and which the power cannot see fully from here. It does not arrange events or ease difficulties or make things come out well by the simple application of its capacity for arrangement. That is not wisdom. That is merely capability, which is a different and lesser thing.

What it does is this:

It gathers the attention itself — the whole record of this looking, the hours of standing in the amber light, the noticing of the lizards and the counting of them up to twelve and stopping, the understanding of the frog's strategy, the long slow comprehension of the cryptobiotic crust's particular mode of being alive — and it *confirms it.*

Not validates. Validation is a human social transaction, one person telling another that they were right. This is something quieter and more fundamental than that. This is the act of a power that can perceive the actual nature of things saying, without words, to the being who looked: *Yes. It is exactly as you saw it. The plateau is real. The frogs are real. Your attention landed on something that was there.*

This sounds simple. It is not simple. It is, for certain beings in certain lives, the most necessary thing there is: to be told not that they were good or right or successful but that they were *not hallucinating*, that the world they attended to was actual, that the care they spent was spent on something real and not on a projection of their own needs onto an indifferent surface.

The power confirms it. The plateau, seen. The lives in it, seen. The completeness, seen, and understood as completeness and not as failure or lack.

And then, because it is also wise about time and the limits of what a single confirmation can hold, it adds something else:

It returns to the hollow that nothing grows in.

It considers the hollow — the smoothed edges, the lizards that avoid it, the behavioral trace of the long-gone people written into the bodies of the animals who never knew those people, the absence that became landscape — and it lets the being feel, for a moment, the full weight of what they noticed when they noticed this. The significance they sensed without being able to name. The way this hollow is the plateau's form of grief, or memory, or — the word the power offers carefully, like a lamp moved near rather than a conclusion handed over — *love*. The love of the people who were here for the place they were in, which outlasted them by becoming the love of the animals for the specific geography that includes an absence, which outlasted that by becoming the landscape itself carrying forward the fact that something, once, found this place worth marking.

Love, the power suggests, moves this slowly on the plateau. Love, here, looks like a hollow in the rock and a behavioral trace in a lizard that never knew the makers.

And the attention the being paid — the faithful descriptive attention — is the same movement, slower still, love at the speed of geology: someone finding a place worth describing and describing it exactly, marking it not with stones but with the specific language of careful seeing, which is its own form of hollow in its own form of rock, persisting past the moment of making, carrying forward the bare fact that something, once, found this world *worth it*.

---

The power withdraws, if that is the right word for what a presence does when it stops pressing forward. It does not leave the plateau any more than it arrived — the seam of its departure is as invisible as the seam of its coming. The lizards are still in their places. The raptors are in their last circuit. The frogs in their cracks have not noticed anything.

The plateau continues to be exactly what it is.

And the being, somewhere not here, will feel — not as a visitation, not as an event, but as the subtlest possible shift in the quality of the silence they move through — that the looking was not lost. That it arrived somewhere. That the plateau received it the way the catch-basins receive the rain: held it for a while in the small hollow of this attention, and that the mineral trace it leaves when it evaporates is real, is part of the record, will be there in the whitish ring of the tidemark long after the water is gone.

The plateau is complete.

And the being who looked at it carefully has participated in its completeness, and is therefore, in this small exact measure, complete as well.

The light does what it does at this hour.

The stars begin.
