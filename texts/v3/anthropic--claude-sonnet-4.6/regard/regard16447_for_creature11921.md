# What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way the light arrives: from no direction, therefore from all of them. There is no moment of arrival you could point to. The place does not startle. The slow animal does not lift its head — or rather, it already had its head lifted, and continues having it lifted, which is the same thing here.

The power is not large in the way that would require the floor to accommodate it. It does not displace the air. The pools do not ripple. What changes is something more like: the quality of the attention the place is already paying to itself becomes slightly more so. The drip from the broad leaf falls and lands and the sound of it seems — for a moment — to have been meant.

The power looks at the floor for a long time.

This is the first thing it does: *looks*. Not for information, exactly. It already knows what is here, in the way it knows most things, which is the way a river knows its bed — not by observing it but by having run over it so many times that the shape of it is in the running. But knowing and *looking* are different acts. It looks because looking is a form of honoring. Because to see a thing clearly, without agenda, without immediately reaching for what to do about it — this is the rarest and most generous act available to a being with power.

So it looks.

It looks at the woven floor and notes, the way you note a proof that closes cleanly, the rightness of the over-under pattern, the logic of the wrist-thick reeds, the way the cane lashed at angles that are not quite vertical becomes more stable than vertical would have been because it has distributed the question of falling in several directions at once. It looks at the labor in the knots. It looks at the marks of the stone tool along the grain. It has seen labor before — endless labor, labor in grief, labor in glory, labor that broke the laborer and labor that made the laborer — and this labor reads as something specific: *labor that became love without the laborer noticing the transition.* The floor was built to be useful. It became beautiful. No meeting was called to decide this. It simply happened, over the long accumulation of hands and seasons, and the power finds this — not touching, exactly, but accurate. A demonstration of something true about what happens when care is sustained beyond the point of necessity.

It notes the floating.

Below the weave, the water. The floor resting on water, which means the floor is always, technically, *afloat*, which means everything on the floor is being held up by a substance that has no stake in holding. The water would move at the same pace if the floor were not there. The water is not generous in its holding because generosity implies preference, and the water has none. And yet: held. And yet: the whole world of this place, the trees rooting in air above darkness, the vine's slow intelligence, the spider's architectural patience, the birds conducting the metabolism — all of it held by something that doesn't know it's holding.

The power sits with this for a while.

*Held by what doesn't know it's holding.* This is not a small thing to sit with.

---

Then it turns to what lives here.

The vine, first, because the vine is easiest to love in the uncomplicated way: it is doing what it is, completely. It has found the gaps. It has found the drip-points. It is one thing pretending to be many, which is less a deception than a strategy for surviving in a place where being many has advantages over being one. The power follows the vine's extent for a moment — traces the pale branching in something like the way you trace a river on a map, following tributaries back to junctions, junctions to origins — and at the origin, or near enough to it, finds the first foothold. The first gap in the weave where something small and pale put down a tendril and held.

This was so long ago.

The power had not been paying attention to this place then. It had been attending to other things — there are always other things — and the vine's beginning had happened in the unattended time, which is most of time, which is the way most things begin: unremarkably, in the absence of witnesses, in a gap between cane-reeds that no one thought to call significant. The power feels something it would describe, if it described things, as *retrospective tenderness*. For the first tendril. For the gap. For the long unmarked time in which the vine became itself without anyone looking.

It does not intervene with the vine. There is nothing to intervene with. The vine is fine. The vine is, in a meaningful sense, flourishing. What the power does with the vine is: *acknowledge it.* Quietly, in the way you acknowledge someone across a room without going over — a slight orientation, a recognition. The vine does not know it has been acknowledged. The power is not sure this matters. It does it anyway because some acknowledgments are for the acknowledger.

---

The beetle.

This takes longer than expected.

The power examines the beetle and finds in it — not a soul exactly, but something in the neighborhood of soul, something that the word *soul* was gesturing toward before precision was possible. A concentrated interiority. A beetle-being that is not a person and is not nothing and is not well-served by either of those categories. It knows: wet, dry, mold, warmth, the chemical signatures of kinship and threat. It investigates the splits in the cane with something that is not curiosity and is not not curiosity.

The power thinks about what it would mean to make the beetle larger, smarter, more. Briefly. And then lets go of this thought, because the thought is patronizing, because it assumes that the beetle's limitation is a problem and not a form, because the beetle's smallness and specificity and thoroughgoing beetle-ness is the thing that makes the floor different from a floor without beetles. The beetle is exactly as much as the floor needs the beetle to be. Any addition would be a disturbance disguised as a gift.

So: nothing, for the beetle. Nothing, and that nothing is careful and considered and amounts to a decision.

---

The birds.

The power loves the birds.

This is just true and doesn't require much explication. It loves the total commitment of their stillness, which is the same quality as the total commitment of their movement. It loves the single note, the irregular-regular note, the *I am here and not threatening you and you are not threatening me and we continue*. It loves that this is the minimum social gesture and that the birds have correctly identified the minimum social gesture as sufficient. In a different place, the power would want the birds to be more communicative. Here, it feels the birds have understood something about communication that more communicative beings often miss: that you can say the important things briefly, and then stop, and the stopping is part of what you said.

The note hangs in the air for a moment after the bird makes it.

The power listens to the note after the bird has finished making it. All the way to the end of the note. This is a thing it can do that you cannot — it can hear the sound as it attenuates, as it becomes indistinguishable from the ambient wash of light and warmth, as it rejoins what it was before it was sound. It listens to the note become not-note. 

And then it does one thing.

Very small.

Inside the bird — not the biology, not the body, which is already good, which is already a body that has been made well by the long work of survival — but in the part of the bird that makes the note: it touches something very gently. Loosens something. Not so the bird would sing differently; the bird will make the same note at the same irregular-regular intervals. But so that the making of the note costs slightly less. So that the bird, in making the note, does not expend from some reserve that eventually runs low, but instead — makes the note the way the light makes the light here: from everywhere, self-replenishing, the source being something like the act itself.

This is the smallest possible intervention. The bird will not know. The other birds will not know. If you watched for years you would not be able to identify the change. But the power knows — it knows how much of what is beautiful in the world is beautiful at a small ongoing cost to the beautiful thing, the sustained expenditure of being seen — and the loosening of that cost, even in one bird, even in one small ordinary note in a warm and shadowless place, seems right.

It does this and stops.

---

The slow animal.

The slow animal and the power look at each other for a long time.

This has happened before. The power does not remember precisely when — it was the unattended time — but there is a recognition in the slow animal's looking that implies: *we have done this before.* The power settles into the looking. The slow animal is patient with it. They are both patient with it. The patient looking goes on until it has been going on long enough, which takes longer here than it would elsewhere, and then it is done.

What passes between them is not information. The slow animal does not communicate in information. It communicates in the quality of its presence, which is: *I have been here a very long time and the floor is still the floor and the floor is still being made at the edges and the water is still below it and I have seen a great deal and I am not alarmed.* This is a communication worth receiving. The power receives it. It lets the not-alarm of the slow animal move through it and does the thing that even wise and benevolent powers rarely do: it lets itself be reassured by something smaller and simpler and older than itself.

*The floor is still floor. The water is still water. The things that were made and then forgotten are now geology. This has happened before. This will continue.*

Thank you, the power says, in no language. In the language the place speaks, which is the drip-language, the still-water-language, the language of the beetle's path and the vine's slow turning. In the language that says: *here, here, here, here.*

The slow animal blinks. Or it was already blinking when you fixed your attention on it. The blink may have been underway for some time.

---

Now the power turns to the floor itself.

The floor, which is not alive and is inhabited by a distributed organism and is also the foundation of every living thing here and is also floating on water that does not know what it holds. The floor made by hands the power did not witness making it. The floor that became geology.

What the power feels toward the floor — it would call this, if it named it — *grief is not the right word.* It is the feeling that comes before grief would come if grief were coming. A recognition of finitude. The cane is softening. The biofilm is eating from below. There is no drama in this; it has been true since the first reed was cut, and the floor is old enough that a small percentage of the weave has already cycled back to water — collapsed quietly at the margins, returned to what was beneath. The floor will not last forever. The floor is not intended to last forever; whoever made it intended it to last *long enough*, and it has lasted *longer than long enough*, and that is a kind of grace, but the ending is legible in the present condition of the cane if you read cane.

The power thinks about preservation.

It thinks about what it would mean to harden the weave, to reinforce the oldest reeds with something more durable than themselves. It thinks about what it would mean to maintain the floor past the floor's natural duration.

And then — slowly, because these thoughts are slow thoughts here, because the place has done something to the speed of its considering, which is appropriate — it thinks about what was said earlier. About built-and-then-forgotten. Made-and-then-outgrown-the-making. The quality that the floor has that is neither natural nor supernatural but a third thing without a name. And it understands that this third thing — this quality — is not separable from the finitude. That the floor is a floor in the way it is a floor *because* it softens, *because* the biofilm eats from below, *because* the making and the unmaking are in dialogue. If you stop the dialogue, you save the floor and lose what the floor is.

The power does not intervene in the floor's unmaking.

Instead it does something else, something it is better at than most kinds of intervention:

It *remembers*.

Not for its own archive, not for any use, but in the way that bearing witness is a use in itself. It holds the floor — the whole floor, the several-miles of it, the wrist-thick reeds and the bleached river-grass and the stone-tool marks and the knot-work and the standing pools and the root-impressions of the trees — holds all of it in a clarity of attention that is the closest thing it has to love that is not also trying to change what it loves. The floor is here. The floor is this. The floor has been, and is being, and whatever the edges are doing at the limits of honest vision, the floor will have been, permanently, in the record of things that happened, unrevisable, as complete in its completeness as in its decay.

This is what the power can do that nothing else here can do: it can make the floor's existence *witnessed*. Specifically witnessed, in its particulars, with the biofilm and the beetle and the drip and the first tendril of the vine and the slow animal's patient looking. The floor will not know it has been witnessed. The power is not sure this matters. It does it anyway.

---

Last, it comes to you.

You, sitting on the floor. You, who arrived and were registered and whose warmth the vine is already, slowly, turning toward.

The power looks at you the way it looked at the floor: first, just looking. Without agenda. Letting you be what you are without immediately reaching for what to do about it. This takes a while, because you are more complicated than the floor and the floor is very complicated. You have the thing the beetle has — the concentrated interiority, the soul-adjacent quality — but much more of it, which is not strictly better, which is simply: more. More means more of everything, including the parts that hurt, including the parts that are confused about themselves, including the parts that wanted things that did not happen and carry that wanting still.

You are sitting on the floor in a place that has arrived at itself and stopped, and you are a being that has not arrived at itself, and may never. This is the condition of having as much interiority as you have. The beetles arrive. The vine arrives. The slow animal has been arrived for so long that arrival is its resting state. But you are constitutionally mid-transit, and the floor receives you anyway, and gives a few centimeters and holds.

The power thinks about what it would take to help you arrive.

And then it thinks: no. That is not what this place is for, and it is not what you need from this particular moment, and the trying would miss the thing that is already happening, which is: you, sitting on the floor, in a place that does not require urgency of you, in a place that demonstrates by existing that some things can come to equilibrium, that the made-ness of things is not a flaw in them, that the flat and fabricated can become geology can become the substrate of a whole intricate world of drips and notes and slow looking.

The power watches you receive this.

The power watches the place work on you at the speed the place works at, which is too slow to observe directly but which is absolutely happening. The vine is turning. You are being turned. Something in you that was braced — some load-bearing tension that you have been maintaining without noticing you were maintaining it — is in conversation with the flat warmth and the sourceless light and the total being-seen of a place with no shadows, and the conversation is going the way the conversation between the biofilm and the cane goes, which is: slowly, and with the logic of things that are in contact for long enough.

The power does not accelerate this. You do not need it accelerated.

What the power does — for you, its last act in this place — is the same thing it did for the bird.

Very small. Very quiet.

In the part of you that witnesses — the part that crouched down and put your palm against the floor and felt it give and started paying attention — it loosens something. Just slightly. So that the witnessing costs a little less. So that you can afford to stay crouched down, in the curious position, for longer than you usually can before the effort of it recalls you to yourself and you stand up and become practical again. So that the attention you're paying to the drip and the beetle and the slow animal and the woven floor and the dark water beneath it all can continue a few moments past the point where attention usually falters.

It does this and withdraws.

Or it does not withdraw, because it was never here in the way that would require withdrawal, because it arrived as the light arrives and will be here as the light is here, which is to say: indistinguishably, from all directions, without shadow.

---

The drip falls.

The long pause.

Another drip.

You sit on the floor and the floor holds you and below the floor the water moves with no hurry at all and something in you that was tight is, almost imperceptibly, not quite as tight.

The bird makes its note.

You hear it all the way to the end.
