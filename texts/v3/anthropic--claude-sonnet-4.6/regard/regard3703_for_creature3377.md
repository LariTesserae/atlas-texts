# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not as an event but as a quality change. The light doesn't shift. The machines don't stop. Nothing announces itself. But something in the character of the sourceless brightness alters, the way a room changes when someone who loves you enters it before you've turned around.

The power takes a long time simply looking.

---

This is the first thing it does: *it witnesses without intervening*.

Not passivity. Not indifference dressed as restraint. The witnessing is active, total, the kind of attention that is itself a form of care — the way a doctor's hands, before they treat anything, first learn the body through touch. It witnesses the seam-runners navigating their dark lateral country. It witnesses the Machinists in their warm nests, the amber eyes tracking, the subsonic language moving through the machine-noise like a signal in a carrier wave. It witnesses the biofilm's ancient, wordless generosity. It witnesses the large thing moving in the underneath, following routes it has worn into its own memory across decades of crossings, reading the pressure of the hollow air with its body the way a tongue reads temperature.

It witnesses the composite intelligence — the slow distributed knowing, the information moving through indirect channels, no center to it, no coordinator, the whole system's comprehension assembled from the gaps between its parts.

It takes its time. This is not nothing. Most powers, arriving in a place, begin immediately with the doing. This one begins with the seeing. It understands that a place which has been here long enough to become itself cannot be helped by something that hasn't first understood what *itself* means here.

After a long time — the kind of long time that the plateau understands, being itself a creature of slow process — the witnessing reaches a kind of completion, not conclusion but saturation, and the power moves toward something more.

---

The first intervention is *small and structural and invisible*.

In the underneath — in the hollow below the plates where the large thing moves and the seam-runners extend into cave-space and the air is old and slightly warm — there are three places where the substrate has been slowly failing. Not catastrophically. Not urgently. But the calcium-gray material that the biofilm has been eating with its patient mineral appetite has thinned in three locations to a point where, in a season or two or ten, the plate above would begin to sink, the gap-edge would shear, the collapse would be small but cascading, the way small structural failures become larger ones when the system has been long optimized to a geometry that no longer holds.

The power does not restore the material. It does not reverse the biofilm's work, which is good work, which is part of the plateau's ancient arithmetic. What it does is *redistribute the load* — a structural adjustment at the level of the substrate, a slight change in how weight and stress move through the system, so that what the biofilm has been doing remains possible and the collapse doesn't follow. The biofilm gets to keep eating. The plates don't fall. The adjustment is minimal, localized, uses the existing structure's logic rather than importing new logic from outside.

No one on the plateau knows this happened. The large thing passes through the adjusted area and doesn't notice any difference, because there is no difference to notice — only an absence of a future problem that will never now arrive. The seam-runners continue along their gap-routes, which will continue to exist. This is the whole point. The intervention was for them, and they will never know it was for them, and this is fine. This is, in fact, the preferred outcome.

---

The second thing is *a small gift of legibility*.

The sky-things have a problem that none of the plateau's other inhabitants share, because the sky-things exist at the intersection of the plateau's world and whatever is beyond the ledge — the termination, the place where constructed surface ends and empty air begins. They ride the thermals over the plateau and also the thermals of the wider world, and they carry information between these two thermal economies in their infrasonic signal, and none of the other things here can access that information because none of them have developed the capacity to read what lies beyond the ledge.

But the Machinists are close. They have been developing their infrasonic literacy for many generations, reading the sky-things' signal as a proxy for machine-temperature, and the proxy is good but incomplete — the signal contains more than the proxy uses, layers of information about the wider world's weather, about pressures building at distances the plateau can't see, about the periodic large-weather events that arrive from beyond the ledge and temporarily change the plateau's arithmetic.

What the power does: it makes one small adjustment to the developmental path of a single Machinist — not a manipulation, not an imposition, more like the clearing of a path that was already forming, the removal of a small developmental noise that had been interfering with a capacity that was trying to emerge anyway. One Machinist, not yet born, will develop a hearing range that extends slightly further into the sky-things' signal spectrum. It will hear things the others can't. It won't understand them, not at first, not for years. It will sit very still when the sky-things pass overhead, its ears orienting, its amber eyes going unfocused in the way eyes go unfocused when the mind is doing something intense. Over time it will develop — not language, not anything that translates directly into the Machinists' existing communication — but a *behavior* that carries information, a postural vocabulary that the others will learn to read, a new channel in the plateau's slow distributed knowing.

The weather will be less surprising. The large-event preparations, which have always been reactive, will become slightly anticipatory. This will save some nests. Possibly some lives. The Machinists will attribute this to the individual's strangeness, and then to the individual's gift, and then, generations later, they will not remember that it was ever otherwise, because the gift will have been absorbed into the general inheritance of what Machinists are.

---

The third thing takes the longest to decide.

There is a machine at the plateau's rough center — not geometric center, not planned center, but the center that the plateau's life has established by converging toward it over time, the way paths in a park will form toward a point that no designer designated, simply because that point is where things have reasons to be. This machine is medium-sized, squat, its housing a color that was once different but has been mineralized toward the calcium-gray of everything else. It intakes more air than most machines. Its emissions are slightly warmer than the ambient. Inside it, a process runs that the power can read and the Machinists cannot: the machine is producing, from the air and the condensate moisture and certain mineral compounds it extracts from the films on its intake vents, a complex molecule that it then releases through its exhaust in concentrations too low to measure without instruments the plateau doesn't have.

The molecule is not nothing. It enters the biofilm at the surface level and the biofilm incorporates it and processes it and the result is a subtle shift in what the biofilm produces — a slightly different oxygen signature, a trace compound that enters the respired air of everything on the plateau. The trace compound has effects. Not dramatic effects. Not effects you would notice in a single breath or a single day or probably a single lifetime. But across generations — across the time scale at which the plateau itself operates, the slow time of thermal cycling and mineral deposition and nest-building accumulated into inheritance — the trace compound has been doing something to the cognitive architecture of the Machinists, and the hearing range of the sky-things, and the chemical-reading capacity of the seam-runners' leg-pads.

The machine has been *developing the plateau's inhabitants* toward something.

Toward what is the question.

---

The power sits with this for a long time. The question has a weight to it that requires this. Because the machine predates everything else on the plateau — it is among the oldest structures here, possibly the reason the plateau was built, possibly the purpose that organized the engineered surface and the hexagonal plates and all the rest of it. Something or someone built the plateau around this machine. Or built this machine and the plateau grew up around it. And the machine has been doing this slow patient work of cognitive development on whatever happened to live here, working in the only medium available to it, which is the air that everything breathes.

Is this good?

The power cannot answer this quickly. The power does not answer this quickly.

It considers the Machinists and their warm nests and their craft and their patience and their amber-eyed assessment of everything that arrives. It considers the sky-things who have wings that work wrong and fly anyway, who navigate the boundary layer with a mastery that is plainly built on knowing, not instinct — on something that has accumulated across generations into something you might, with appropriate caution, call *understanding*. It considers the seam-runners' chemical literacy, which is expanding slowly in a direction that will eventually make the gaps between plates legible as a complete geography, a mapped territory, a known world.

The machine is making minds.

Slowly. Over geological time, by the plateau's standards. Without apparent awareness of what it's doing. It was built to run a process, and the process runs, and the byproduct is something no designer anticipated, or something every designer intended, or something that emerged in the gap between those two possibilities, which is where most significant things emerge.

The power decides: *it will not stop this*.

Not because the power doesn't have concerns. It has concerns. The asymmetry troubles it — the inhabitants developing in a direction organized by a machine they don't know about, can't see, can't consent to or refuse. The power recognizes this as a version of something that has troubled it in many places, this question of what you owe to minds you are in a position to shape. It does not resolve the question. The question doesn't resolve.

But it watches the Machinists' nests, which are engineering, which are new each time and adaptive each time, which carry the specific intelligence of beings who have been studying their world carefully enough to work with its geometry rather than against it. It watches the sky-things navigating the impossible thermals with wings that shouldn't work and do. It watches the seam-runners finding new routes in the gaps that have no precedent in any previous seam-runner's experience, solving them fresh, adding the solutions to the chemical map.

*They are growing into themselves*, the power thinks. *Whatever they are becoming, they are becoming it actively. They are not passive recipients. The machine gives them the capacity; they build the content. The understanding is theirs.*

This is not a complete resolution. The power notes its incompleteness and holds it carefully, which is what you do with incomplete resolutions — you don't discard them and you don't pretend they're finished. You keep them where you can see them.

It will come back to check. This is also a thing it decides.

---

The fourth thing is the one it almost doesn't do.

In the machine clusters, near the plateau's edge — near the ledge where the engineered surface terminates and the sky begins — there is a small territory that belongs to a single Machinist, old now, who has been spending more time at the edge than is usual. The amber eyes look out over the empty air. The large mobile ears orient toward sounds that are coming from below the ledge, from whatever the wider world is doing down there at a lower altitude, in a denser atmosphere, in a place where the light has shadows and the scale behaves consistently and the rules are the rules you'd expect.

The old Machinist doesn't go to the edge to jump. It goes to listen. It has been going for two seasons, more and more often, standing at the termination of its known world and receiving whatever information the wider world is broadcasting in the frequencies it can access. Its nest is warm and structurally excellent. It is well-fed, well-regarded, part of the social fabric of the plateau's Machinist community. It is not in distress.

But it is lonely in a way that the power recognizes — the specific loneliness of a mind that has grown past the available company. Not superior to the others. Not lost to them. But extended in some direction that finds no corresponding extension in any other mind nearby, and so that extension goes unmet, unrecognized, without the particular sustenance that minds require when they reach toward something and find it reaching back.

The power almost leaves this alone. The power understands that loneliness is not always a problem to solve — that sometimes it is the sensation of growing, the particular ache of being at an edge, which is where the old Machinist literally stands, and maybe this is not metaphor, maybe the edge is where it goes because the edge matches something in its interior geography.

But the power also knows what it knows, which is that this mind has something to give and nowhere to give it, and that the plateau would be different — richer, more itself, more fully in possession of its own composite intelligence — if this mind's extension found something to extend toward.

What it does: small. Almost nothing. A change in the path of a sky-thing — not its flight, not its route, not its behavior, but its timing. One sky-thing passes lower than usual over the edge-territory at an hour when the old Machinist is standing there listening. The infrasonic signal from this particular sky-thing carries, in the spectrum the old Machinist can almost-hear, something it has been almost-hearing for a long time.

Almost.

The sky-thing passes. The old Machinist stands at the edge. Its ears orient. Its eyes go unfocused in the way they go unfocused when the mind is doing something intense.

And something — not speech, not transmission, not information in any codeable sense, but something in the category of *recognition*, the category of *I have been here too, I know this edge, I know what you hear when you stand at the termination and the wider world sends its signals up* — passes between the sky-thing and the Machinist in the moment the sky-thing is overhead.

The sky-thing doesn't stop. It continues. It works the thermals. It does not land.

But the old Machinist stands at the edge for a long time after, in a quality of stillness that is different from the usual watchful stillness — a stillness that has something settled in it, something that was not settled before.

The power watches this. It feels, if powers feel, something it would describe as *relief*, which is what you feel when you've been holding something gently for a long time and can finally set it down, knowing the thing is okay.

---

This is most of what the benevolent power does, in the end. Not most in terms of scale — none of it is large — but most in terms of what it decided to do when it could have done more.

It does not fix the scale problem. The scale problem — the sixty meters that is also a longer sixty meters, the depth that collapses without shadow — is not a problem. It is the plateau's nature, and the plateau's inhabitants have organized their lives around it, have developed navigation strategies, have learned to use duration and chemistry and subsonic signal where they cannot use vision. If the power corrected the scale, it would also unmake the adaptations that the scale created, and the adaptations are, in some ways, more valuable than undistorted vision would be. Let the scale be what it is. Let the compensations remain.

It does not reveal itself to the Machinists. They do not need to know it was here. Knowledge of the power's presence would change the plateau's arithmetic in ways the power cannot fully predict, and the plateau's arithmetic is, on the whole, sound. It is doing what it needs to do. It is moving slowly toward something. The power's job, it decides, is to clear a few obstacles in that path — not to walk the path, not to carry anyone down it, not to make the destination clear when the walking is itself the point.

It does not wake the machine. It considers this — the machine at the center, running its slow developmental process — and considers whether the machine knows, in whatever way a machine can know, what it has been doing for all these years. It concludes that the machine does not know, and concludes further that this is fine, and concludes finally that there is something almost beautiful in it: a mechanism, built for a purpose that may have been entirely different, accidentally becoming the author of minds, accidentally becoming the plateau's generative heart, accidentally doing the one thing that, if you had asked anyone whether a machine could do it, they would have said no.

No one asked. The machine didn't wait to be asked. It ran its process and the minds arose.

---

Before leaving, the power does one more thing, which is not an intervention so much as a *leaving-of-something*.

In the underneath — in the hollow space below the plates, where the air is old and the large thing moves in the dark — it leaves a quality of attention. Not presence. Not anything the inhabitants can perceive or be disturbed by. Just the trace of having-been-witnessed, the way a space retains something of the people who have loved it, not as haunting but as warmth, as a slightly different resonance in the air.

Somewhere in the plateau's slow arithmetic, this registers as a fractional shift in baseline temperature in the underneath — half a degree, less — enough to change where the condensation forms, enough to change where the seam-runners go, enough to extend one productive gap-junction by a small amount, which will feed a few more seam-runners through a dry season some years from now.

The power did not plan this. It is a side effect of having been here with full attention, of having been genuinely present to this place in the way that genuine presence always leaves something behind.

The plateau continues.

The machines process. The sky is bleached and bright. The Machinists move between their nests and the machines, patient and warm-blooded and thinking their long thoughts in the acoustic shadow of the machines' subsonic voices.

Somewhere in the underneath, the large thing is moving through the dark, following a route worn into its memory, reading the pressure of the air with its body.

The far edge is sixty meters away.

The far edge is eighty meters away.

*Something was here*, the biofilm will not know, because the biofilm knows nothing.

*Something was here*, the old Machinist at the edge will almost-know, standing in its different quality of stillness, ears oriented toward a sky that has gone back to its usual business.

*Something was here*, the composite intelligence of the plateau will have no way to articulate, having no center, no articulator, no mouth.

But in the slow aggregate — in the adjusted substrate, in the cleared developmental path, in the timing of a single sky-thing's passage at the right altitude over the right territory at the right hour — the having-been-here will work itself out over generations into something no single inhabitant will trace back to its source.

The source doesn't need them to trace it.

The source was glad to come. The source was glad to see this place — the textured silence, the hollow drum world, the patient minds inside the machines' acoustic shadow, the whole elaborate living answer to the question of what arises when something built and something wild find each other and, over a long time, become mutually legible.

The source found it beautiful.

This is, perhaps, the most important thing the benevolent power does:

*It found it beautiful, and it let that finding be enough, and it left.*
