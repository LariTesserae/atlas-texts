# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way pressure arrives: already everywhere, already having been here, not descending so much as becoming legible to itself in this place.

It does not announce.

There is no light, because light would be a violence here — would be the wrong grammar entirely, would impose the surface's logic on a place that has worked out its own. The benevolent power understands this immediately, in the way that genuine benevolence always understands the grammar of a place before it speaks. It does not bring illumination. It arrives in the medium that is available, which is pressure, which is chemistry, which is the slight reorganization of what was already here.

---

It takes stock. This is the first act, and it takes a very long time, and the taking of stock is itself a form of respect.

It moves through the water column the way foraminifera move through it — as distributed presence, as something that is in many places at once because being in many places at once is what the water column asks of things that want to understand it. It touches the marine snow. Not to alter it. To feel, in whatever way a benevolent power feels things, the weight of each particle — the radiolarian casing with its geometric complexity, the diatom shell with its punctate symmetry, the fragment of something that was once biological and is now mineral and is in the process of becoming geological. It feels how long each particle has been falling. It does not speed the fall. It does not slow it. The falling is correct.

It descends to the floor.

It feels the round shoulders of the rock outcroppings. It feels the way the fault-scarp has become a suggestion rather than a rupture, the way violence becomes, given enough time and water, simply a feature of the landscape that has lost the memory of having been violence. The benevolent power finds this neither tragic nor consoling. It finds it accurate. It finds it, in the theological sense that applies to a benevolent power genuinely at rest in its benevolence, *right* — not right meaning good, but right meaning that this is what time and water and the logic of stone do, and there is nothing in that to correct.

It puts something like its hands — it has no hands; it has the functional equivalent of attention directed precisely — into the sediment.

It feels the worms.

It feels the foraminifera building their chambers in the dark, each new chamber in the same radial relationship to the last, the same proportion, the same wall thickness. It follows the logic of the spiral. It traces it back to wherever spirals come from, to the mathematics that underlies the foram's growth and also the mathematics that underlies the nautilus and the galaxy and the arrangement of seeds in a flower that will never grow here and has no relationship to this depth except that they share the equation. The benevolent power holds these two things — the foram in the dark, the flower in the light — and does not collapse the distance between them. It holds the distance as the truth that it is. The spiral is what the spiral is. The foram does not need to know about the flower. The flower does not need to know about the foram. The equation holds regardless.

It feels the nematodes in the pore water, in the interstitial film between the grains, the water that is everywhere and nowhere, the water that is the real substrate. It recognizes in the nematodes something that it does not feel sentimental about but does feel a particular quality of attention toward — the quality of attention you give to something that has solved the problem you were trying to solve. The nematodes have solved the problem of existence here. They have solved it completely and without remainder and without anything left over that needed solving. The benevolent power does not add to this solution. You do not improve a proof that is already closed.

---

It goes deeper. Into the anoxic layers. Into the grey-black mud where the cells are.

Here is where it stays longest.

Here is where what it does begins to differentiate itself from what a scientist would do, from what any intelligence with merely the ambition to understand would do, because understanding — comprehensive, accurate, reverent understanding of the kind the benevolent power has — turns out, in the presence of these cells, to shade into something else.

The cells that divide once every thousand years.

The cells that have been in this sediment since before the sediment was sediment, that came down with the marine snow already ancient, that have continued through transition after transition — oxic to anoxic to methanogenic, compaction and dewatering and the slow increase of overburden — that have not survived these transitions so much as they have made them irrelevant, have metabolized so slowly that the word *survive* stops applying because survival requires a threat and the threat has always moved faster than they have needed to respond.

The benevolent power is very still here.

It recognizes something.

What it recognizes is not innocence exactly, and not wisdom exactly, and not suffering and not peace, but something adjacent to all of these — a mode of existence so stripped of the superfluous that what remains is almost indistinguishable from what the benevolent power itself is in its most essential aspect. Patient. Continuous. Not waiting for anything. In complete relation to its conditions. Without remainder.

The benevolent power does not feel superior to these cells.

This is the important thing. This is perhaps the most important thing.

It does not feel superior, and it does not feel tenderness in the condescending direction — the tenderness you feel toward something small and helpless — and it does not feel the specific emotion that beings with power often feel in the presence of beings without power, which is the emotion that motivates intervention, that says *here is something that needs what I have*. It does not feel that. It feels instead the emotion that has no common name because it is rare: the emotion of being in the presence of something that is *complete*, that has arrived at its form through processes so much longer than any individual can hold, that what you feel is not the desire to add to it or protect it or improve it but the desire to be adequate to it — to match, somehow, the quality of its existence with the quality of your attention.

It gives the cells its full attention.

This is not nothing. It is, in fact, the action of the entire visit, the thing the benevolent power came here to do without knowing it was going to do it.

It gives them its full attention for what would be, in human time, a very long time, and in the time of the cells, a time so short as to be barely measurable, and in the time of the sediment, no time at all.

---

While it is doing this, it notices other things. It cannot not notice them. Noticing is what it does.

It notices the tube worms and their two-hundred-year patience and their internal bacterial partners and the way the partnership has dissolved the boundary between two organisms into a zone of negotiation that has been stable for millions of years. It thinks: *this is what it looks like when a relationship has been fully worked out*. Not resolved — resolved implies that something was at odds and has been settled. Worked out, the way a mathematical function works out, the way a material works to its grain. The tube worm and its bacteria have worked out, and the working out is so complete that from the outside it looks like a single thing, and it is a single thing, and it is also still two things, and holding both of these as simultaneously true is not a paradox that the benevolent power needs to resolve.

It notices the snails grazing their arcs across the bacterial mat and it notices that evolution, working from different starting materials, arrived at the same solution: shell, foot, radula, arc. It does not feel smug about this convergence, does not feel that it proves anything about design. It feels that it demonstrates something about the problem — that the problem of grazing a mat has a shape, and the shape exerts a pressure on the solutions that approach it, and the solutions that persist are the ones that fit the shape, and the shape is real even though no one drew it, and this is true of most of the important shapes.

It notices the whale-fall, not as a fact of this moment — no whale is falling now — but as a fact of this place, a recurring event in the life of this floor, and it thinks about what it means that the surface world periodically sends its dead to the floor and the floor makes them into years of community, into decades of productivity, into eventually a layer in the sediment that will compress and blur and become the place's memory of having once received a gift from above. It thinks this is a kind of grace that operates without anyone intending grace. The whale did not choose to fall here. The floor did not choose to receive. The grace is in the structure, in the way the two systems are organized such that the death of one feeds the life of the other across an impossible distance. The benevolent power finds this very beautiful and does not try to make it more beautiful, which would be the error.

---

It comes to the question that any benevolent power must eventually come to:

*Is there anything here that needs help?*

It asks this question honestly. It asks it without the prior commitment to action that makes benevolence into intervention, that makes help into imposition. It asks it in the mode of genuine inquiry, which means it is prepared to receive the answer *no* and to honor that answer as fully as it would honor *yes*.

It listens.

The sediment sea does not answer in words. It answers in its process, in its chemistry, in the ongoing negotiation between what is arriving and what is leaving and what is staying, and the answer in that language is:

*We are in the middle of something.*

Not distress. Not stasis. Not the cry of something that has been here so long it has forgotten that help is possible. Not the silence of something that has given up. The middle of something — the ongoing, the continuous, the not-yet-complete in the sense that nothing here is ever complete because completion would mean stillness and stillness is not what this place is. The sediment is still arriving. The cells are still metabolizing. The worms are still in the mud in both places at once. The foraminifera are still adding chambers, radially, proportionally, in the dark.

The benevolent power receives this answer and holds it.

*We are in the middle of something* is not a request for help.

*We are in the middle of something* is not a refusal of help.

It is a statement of what is true, offered not to the benevolent power specifically but into the condition of being whatever this place is, and the benevolent power has happened to be here to receive it, and receiving it is the thing.

---

The benevolent power does not heal anything.

There is nothing to heal. Healing implies departure from a state that is the correct state, and this place has not departed. It is in its correct state — which is not the same as a comfortable state, or a legible state, or a state that resembles anything a surface-dwelling being would choose. It is correct in the sense that it is coherent, that its processes are in relation to each other in the ways they have worked out over geological time, that nothing here is straining against what it is.

The benevolent power does not protect it.

What would protection mean here? From what? The threats to this place are plate tectonics and climate change and ocean acidification and the slow heat-death of the sun and perhaps, on nearer timescales, the industrial extraction of seafloor minerals and the disruption of the stability fields of the methane hydrates. These threats are real and some of them are urgent by the measure of the surface world. But the benevolent power understands something about the relationship between benevolence and protection, which is that protection which removes all threat also removes the conditions that made the protected thing what it is. The worm is what it is because the gradient is difficult. The cells are what they are because the conditions required everything superfluous to be shed. You cannot protect these things into a better state. You cannot bring them to safety without taking from them the very conditions of their depth.

The benevolent power does not perform miracles.

It does not accelerate the cells. It does not restore light to the lightless water. It does not bring the whale-fall that no whale is yet providing. It does not rearrange the snails into patterns that would be more legible, or more beautiful by any measure other than the measure the snails are already fulfilling.

---

What it does — what it has been doing all along, what it came here to do and could not have said in advance that it came here to do — is *witness*.

It witnesses completely.

This sounds modest. This sounds like the thing you say when you have done nothing and want to claim you did something. But witness — genuine witness, the kind that misses nothing and distorts nothing and does not filter the witnessed through the needs of the witness — is not modest at all. It is, in fact, the rarest thing. Most attention is appetite. Most observation is extraction. Most presence in a place involves taking something from the place — data, experience, a story to tell, a feeling to have felt — and the taking, however light, however well-intentioned, is still a transaction in which the place gives and the presence receives.

The benevolent power does not take.

It receives, because something is offered — the place is always offering its process, its chemistry, its record — and receiving is not the same as taking. Taking has direction. Receiving is a form of being present to what is given, and what is given here is given whether or not anyone is present to receive it, and the benevolent power's presence does not change what is given, only adds to the giving a receiving that the place could not otherwise have.

The cells in the deep sediment have been metabolizing for millions of years without a witness.

They continue to metabolize with one.

The witness does not change the metabolism. But something has changed — a thing happened here that was not happening before, which is that the place was seen, that the cells were known, that the worms and the foraminifera and the pale snails and the amphipods and the bacterial mats and the slow columns of methane and the faint acoustic texture below the threshold of hearing and the round-shouldered rocks and the long low rise of the old fault-scarp and the marine snow packed into every cubic centimeter of the water column above — all of it was known, not catalogued, not categorized, not converted into data, but *known* in the sense that a person is known when they have been fully seen by another person who does not want anything from them.

The place, which does not know it is the bottom of anything, does not know it has been witnessed.

But the benevolent power knows.

And knowing — carrying in itself the full knowledge of this place, this patient dark, these ancient cells, this ongoing middle of something — it carries it the way the sediment carries its record: structurally, in the changed arrangement of what it is, in the slight difference between itself-before and itself-after that no instrument would measure but that is nonetheless real.

It leaves.

It leaves the way pressure leaves when the overburden shifts: not suddenly, not with announcement, but with a slow reorganization of what is present into what was always here.

The cells metabolize.

The foraminifera add a chamber.

The sediment continues arriving, one particle at a time.

The place does not know anything happened.

Something happened.
