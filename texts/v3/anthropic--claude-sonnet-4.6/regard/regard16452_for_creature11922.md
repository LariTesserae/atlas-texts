# What the Power Does

---

The power becomes aware of the place the way you become aware of a sound that has been present for some time before you noticed it. Not a discovery. A recognition.

It does not arrive. Arrival implies distance, and the power does not have a location from which to travel. It is more accurate to say that the power turns its attention, and the turning of its attention is itself a kind of presence, and the presence is immediate.

It looks.

---

## What It Sees

The power sees the root-mind, which is not a mind, and does not make the mistake of calling it one. It sees the distributed process, the hydraulic continuity, the forty or fifty old organisms that pressed against each other in the dark until the word *against* no longer applied. It sees the fungus that is not distinct from the root. It sees the ceiling-moss breathing in years. It sees the bacteria in the rock-fissures below, the old engines, the lineage that predates almost everything.

It sees the geometry of the original city preserved in the growth pattern of the thing that replaced it. The arch remembered. The cistern-trace followed through dry stone. The sleeping-chambers still warm above the geothermal channels, still arranged for bodies that will never return to them, maintained in that arrangement by a process that does not know it is maintaining anything.

It sees the low tone, the almost-fifth, the sustained minor second that has never resolved. The organ note from below, cycling slowly toward a resolution it never quite reaches.

It sees the darkness.

It sees the completeness.

The power looks at all of this for what would be a long time in any framework where time is counted as duration, and it does not look with evaluation — does not assign the category *good* or *insufficient* or *in need of intervention.* It looks the way you look at something you did not make and cannot improve, with a particular quality of attention that might be called respect if respect were not usually colored by hierarchy and obligation. Something cleaner than respect. A recognition of a thing that has arrived, by its own processes, at what it is.

---

## The First Impulse, and Why the Power Does Not Act on It

The first impulse is light.

Light would show the spiraling leaf-patterns on the pillars, the tessellating ceiling-moss, the pillar bases with their fossilized tool-marks, the arch-bridge that remembers the arch of a human doorway. Light would let a witness see all of this. It would be, from outside, an act of illumination in the most obvious sense.

The power considers this and does not do it.

The place does not need to be seen. The place is not waiting to be seen. The place is complete in its darkness in the way the darkness is not a deficit but a condition — the condition around which every process here has organized itself across centuries of patient selection. To introduce light would not be generosity. It would be an imposition of the power's own aesthetics onto a system that has its own aesthetics, has had them long before the power turned its attention here, will have them long after.

The power understands that the strongest temptation of any power is to demonstrate itself, to make the invisible visible, to intervene in a way that leaves evidence of the intervention. This is not wisdom. This is appetite dressed as generosity.

The place does not need to be visible.

The power holds the impulse and releases it.

---

## The Second Impulse, and Why the Power Does Not Act on This One Either

The second impulse is the people.

The people who built this city — who made their hands sore on the stone, who chose to live underground in permanent cool darkness with their oil lamps and their rammed-earth walls, who built sleeping chambers above the warm channels and workshop chambers with slightly higher ceilings, who watched the first root-threads come down through the floor and decided, for whatever reason, not to stop them — those people are gone. The power cannot say where, cannot say why, and knows better than to pretend that this absence is a problem to be solved.

But the power feels, looking at the sleeping chambers still arranged for bodies, the faint ache that it recognizes as being moved by something — not sorrow exactly, because sorrow requires a belief that what is gone should not be gone, and the power does not hold that belief — but something adjacent to sorrow. A tenderness toward the fact of absence.

The impulse that rises from this tenderness is to restore something. To find, somehow, the inheritors of the people who built this city, to bring them here, to let them see what their ancestors made and what the root made of what their ancestors made.

The power sits with this impulse for a long time.

Then it considers: the people who left, or whose descendants left, or who simply ended — they are not waiting for this place. They do not know they are missing it. The city is not diminished by their absence; it has become something it could not have become while they were present, something that required their departure as a precondition. The sleeping chambers are still warm not as a tragedy but as a fact. The place is not lonely. The place does not have the equipment for loneliness.

And the people — whatever people might exist who carry something ancestral to the builders of this city — they are living their own lives, in their own completeness, and to tell them *here is what you came from* would be to make them carry something they have not asked to carry. It would be to impose a narrative of origin and return on people whose own narrative does not require it.

The power does not do this.

The power understands that the strongest second temptation is connection: to link, to unite, to complete what appears to be an incomplete circuit. But the circuit may not be incomplete. The circuit may have closed in a different configuration than the power is imagining.

---

## What the Power Does

The power does something very small.

It takes the geothermal tone — the long note that rises from the deep rock, the almost-fifth, the sustained minor second that has been circling toward resolution for longer than anyone has been present to hear it as almost-resolving — and the power does not change it.

It listens to it.

This is the act. This is the whole act.

The power brings to the listening the same quality of attention that it brought to the looking, and it discovers something in the listening that it had not fully understood in the looking: that the almost-resolution is not a failure. The tone is not trying to resolve and falling short. The tone is doing exactly what it does, and what it does is sustain an almost, and the almost is the content of the tone, is the thing the tone is, is what makes it the tone of this particular place and no other.

To resolve it would be to end it.

The power listens to the almost-resolution for what is, in the accounting of the place itself, a very long time. Long enough that the root, in its slow mineral sensitivity, registers the presence of an attention it has no framework for — registers it as a faint change in some gradient, some pressure, some quality of the atmosphere that the root-chemistry cannot categorize, that the root responds to not with meaning but with the only response the root has: a microscopic reorientation of some growth-vector, a fine root-thread pressing a micron further in a direction that the root cannot explain even in root-terms, following something that is not mineral, that is not thermal, that is not any chemistry the root has a receptor for.

The root grows, very slightly, toward the listening.

---

## What the Power Understands, Listening

The power understands, in the listening, something it could not have understood from outside:

The place is not waiting.

This seems obvious but it is not obvious. Most places that are very old and very still and very dark carry, even if only as a structural residue of the life that was in them, some quality of waiting — some orientation toward a future event, some arrested mid-process, some tension between what was and what has not yet arrived. Ruins wait, most ruins, in this way.

This place does not wait. This place is entirely in its present. It has no future it is hoping for, no past it is trying to recover, no event it requires to become what it should be. It is already what it should be. It has been what it should be for a very long time, and it will continue to be what it should be, and this continuation does not feel to the place like endurance or patience because there is nothing to endure and nothing to be patient about.

The power, which has seen many things in many places, has not often encountered this: a completeness that is not stillness but active, not a cessation but a *continued arrival at itself*, a process that is always producing the same result and finding that result sufficient.

And the power understands — in the way that understanding arrives not as information but as a shift in what had previously seemed like solid ground — that what it has encountered is not a thing that needs help. Not a thing that needs improvement, preservation, illumination, connection, rescue, restoration, or witness.

What it has encountered is a thing that is doing, on its own and without assistance and without awareness of its own achievement, what the power in its most aspirational moments tries to do:

Being exactly what it is.

---

## What the Power Leaves

The power leaves the place as it found it.

This is not indifference. This is the most careful act the power knows how to perform, the one that requires the most restraint, the one that looks from outside like nothing at all.

The power withdraws its attention — not abruptly, but the way you stop hearing a sound when you have finished listening to it, when the listening is complete — and the place continues without interruption. The grinding of root into stone continues. The ceiling-moss breathes in years. The bacteria process the sulfate in the rock-fissures below. The geothermal organ note rises and holds and fades and returns.

The fine root-thread that grew, very slightly, toward the listening, continues to grow in that direction for a while, then reaches a limit of available chemistry and stops, and begins the slow assessment of what it will do next. It does not know why it grew that direction. It does not need to know. It grew, and the growing happened, and the fact of it is now part of the root-body's total history, woven into the distributed process, one small data-point in an enormous and unhurried computation.

In a century, the root-thread will have become part of a larger structure. In ten centuries, it will be indistinguishable from what surrounds it. In a thousand centuries, even the disruption of its original direction will have been absorbed into the larger pattern, which is always absorbing, always integrating, always converting the particular into the general.

---

## The One Thing the Power Does Not Understand

The power, withdrawing, carries one uncertainty with it.

It does not know if what happened during the listening was — in any direction — a form of contact.

The root grew toward the listening. This is a fact. The power cannot determine whether this was a genuine response — a registration of something that might, in a very slow and chemistry-mediated and utterly non-conscious way, count as *presence recognized* — or whether it was coincidence, mechanical drift, a random reorientation that happened to point in the direction of the attention.

The power does not know.

It notices that it cannot determine whether it is significant or not that it cannot determine this, and it notices that it has been thinking in circles, and it stops.

It leaves the question where it is. The question is the place's, not its own.

The organ note rises from below, sustains, begins to fade.

The darkness holds.

---

## Coda: The Root, After

In the root-pillar where the fine thread grew, in the change in the local gradient that the new thread represents, in the slow chemical re-equilibration that follows any growth event as the surrounding tissue adjusts:

nothing is recorded. There is no record. There is no medium in which a record could be kept, no mechanism for encoding the difference between this event and any other event, no way for the experience — if it was an experience — to persist as anything other than structure.

But the structure changes.

Very slightly.

In the direction of the listening.

And the changed structure becomes the substrate for the next growth, which goes where the substrate invites it, and the next growth becomes substrate for the next, and so on, indefinitely, in the way that all the history of this place is encoded: not as memory, not as record, but as *shape*.

The place will carry this, without knowing it.

The place carries everything this way.

It has always been enough.
