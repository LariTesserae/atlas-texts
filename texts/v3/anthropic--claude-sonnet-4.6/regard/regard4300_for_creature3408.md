# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

## First: It Arrives the Same Way You Do

Not descending. Not appearing. It finds itself *already present*, the way attention finds itself already in the room before the mind consciously directed it there. It has walked, in whatever sense a power walks, and now it is no longer below.

It does not announce itself. The plateau does not respond to its arrival with any change in the wind or the cloud circulation or the light. The shadows continue their occasional mild wrongness. The water in the basins reflects their slightly-displaced sky. The moss does not lean toward the visitor. This is appropriate, and the power recognizes it as appropriate, and something in the power settles in recognition: *this place will not perform for me*. Good. This is a place that has moved past performance. To arrive here demanding ceremony would be to misunderstand where you had arrived.

So it stands — if standing is the right word for what a presence does when it is not moving — and it looks. For what feels, on the plateau's timescale, like no time at all, and on the power's timescale like a long, slow, full looking, it simply observes.

---

## What It Sees

The power is benevolent and wise, and wisdom, actual wisdom, involves a certain quality of seeing that is not immediately helpful. It involves seeing clearly before deciding whether help is even the appropriate word.

What it sees:

The moss, perfect in its imperfection, colonizing the stone from within. It has been doing this without assistance or intervention since before anything visited this altitude with the capacity to notice it. It does not need to be made more. It does not need to be protected — it is its own protection, its own continuation, its strategy indistinguishable from itself. *No action required*, notes the power, *and noting this is itself a form of respect*.

The lichen. The endolithic organisms in the stone. The springtails in their miniature dramas of leap and land. The mites. The communities in the dew. All of it interlocked in a calibration so fine and so long-established that to intervene in any piece of it would be to misunderstand that it is not pieces. *No action required*.

The stone, eroding. The cracks, widening by microns per year. The basins, deepening. In ten million more years, this will all be lower, different, the plateau eventually returned to the slow democratic flatness that surrounds it. *No action required*. The process is the right process. Stopping it would be the unkindness, not the kindness.

The spatial fluctuations: shadows wrongly angled, distances that don't fix, water reflecting the adjacent moment instead of the current one. The power looks at these for a long time. It understands their nature, or understands as much of their nature as can be understood, which is not everything — even a benevolent and wise power has the wisdom to know that understanding everything about a thing this old would require being as old as the thing, which it is not. It reads the fluctuations as: *the natural consequence of depth. What happens when time layers long enough. A feature, not a malfunction*. 

*No action required.*

And then the people.

---

## The Power Looks at the People

This is where it slows.

The people are few, and they are fine. Let this be stated plainly before the slower observations: they are not in crisis. They are not suffering in any way that demands immediate response. They are fed, they are sheltered, they have their community of complete knowledge, they have their paths, they have their practice near the outcrops. They are one of the things this plateau has made, and it has made them well.

But they are few in a way that the power wants to sit with. Not too few for now — for now, they are in equilibrium. But the equilibrium has a quality of narrowness to it, a margin that is not catastrophically thin but is thinner than wisdom finds comfortable. One bad winter. One illness that moves through a small population with nothing to slow it. One generation with fewer births than deaths, for any reason. The equilibrium is stable until it isn't, and the difference between stable and not-stable at this population size is small.

*Do I act on this?* the power asks itself.

It sits with the question. Wisdom is patient — it does not hurry toward answers that feel good to give.

What the power does not want to do: fill the plateau with people. Bring resources. Intervene in a way that changes what this place is, what these people are, what the ten million years of arriving have produced. That would be the undoing of the thing it has come to honor. A doctor who healed the moss's particular constrained lushness into the lushness of a jungle would not be a good doctor. A power that found this plateau and rushed to make it more would have misunderstood what *more* means. 

What the power does not want to do is leave the people with their narrow margin and call that respect, when it might be negligence wearing respect's face.

It sits longer. The cloud system completes most of a circuit. A springtail jumps in the moss and lands and is still.

---

## What the Power Notices About the People

Here is what wisdom, actual wisdom, sees when it looks carefully:

The people know everything they need to know to live well here. Their knowledge of the stone, the water, the paths, the weirdness, the practice near the outcrops — this is complete in the sense that the moss is complete: not lacking, not in need of supplementation. The knowledge belongs here.

But there are things they used to know that have gone quiet. Not forgotten — the word for what happens to knowledge in a small population over a long time is not quite forgetting and not quite loss, it is more like: the holders of a specific thread of knowing died before the thread fully transferred, and now the thread is present in fragmentary form, and the fragment has been worked with and adapted, but there is a gap that is felt as a kind of unease around certain topics, a reaching that finds less than was expected.

Specifically: they have a partial understanding of the spatial fluctuations. The paths were built by people who understood more than the current people understand. The practice near the outcrops is maintained with competence but without the deeper comprehension that would make it more than maintenance — that would make it, potentially, a practice that could be extended, varied, applied to new situations. The original architects of the paths knew something about the nature of the plateau's dimensionality that would take the current people years of thought and trial to reconstruct, and they are so occupied with the daily work of surviving at altitude that years of directed thought on this topic are not available.

This is the thinning. Not a catastrophe. Not even a problem in the short term. But over generations, a practice without its underlying comprehension slowly drifts, the way a copy of a copy accumulates error, until one day the paths are still being walked but the reason they work is no longer anyone's knowledge, and then the day the paths need to be adjusted or extended for some new reason, no one knows how.

The power looks at this.

It looks at the child learning the path with her feet. It looks at the elder near the outcrop, doing the work with competence and a layer of something underneath the competence that is not doubt but is the shape that doubt leaves when it has been present and then been managed rather than answered.

*This*, the power says to itself, *is where I could be useful. If useful is what I should be.*

---

## The Question It Asks Before Acting

The question is not *can I help?* A benevolent and wise power can always help in some technical sense. The question is: *does helping here serve what this place is, or does it serve my own desire to have done something?*

Because a wise power knows about itself: it arrived here, it was moved by this place, it experienced the quality of arrival and settlement and the ten-million-year depth, and somewhere in the experience of that it felt something that wants to give back, to add, to participate. This feeling is not pure. It contains love, but it also contains the very slight and forgivable ego of wanting to be the power that came to the Plateau of Settled Things and was not merely a witness but a participant.

It examines this feeling honestly.

Then it asks: *if I restore this knowledge — the deeper understanding of the dimensional fluctuations, the comprehension beneath the practice — does it change what these people are, or does it return something that was always theirs?*

It considers. The knowledge was theirs. It was made here, by people of this place, from long observation and from the quality of attention that living here produces. It is not foreign knowledge that would import a different way of being. It is local knowledge that has been partially lost to the attrition of small populations over time. Restoring it is not adding something alien. It is more like: returning a thing to a community from which it was temporarily mislaid.

*And the manner of restoration?* it asks itself. Because how a thing is given matters as much as what is given.

---

## What It Does

The power does not appear to the elder. It does not come in the night with instruction. It does not write on the stone, or send a messenger, or rearrange the world in ways that cannot be ignored.

What it does is this:

It goes to the specific outcrop where the elder stands.

It stands near the elder — near enough that if the elder were aware of presences in the way that people on this plateau sometimes are, there would be a quality of company without a source. It is possible the elder is aware of something. It is possible she is not. Either is fine.

And then the power *thinks*, as clearly and as deeply as it can, the understanding of what the paths are for.

Not in language. Language is too coarse for this. It thinks in the way that the plateau thinks — in structure, in geometry, in the logic of a place that contains more interior space than exterior measurement would predict, and in the understanding of what it means for a human body to be a point of stable reference in such a space. It thinks the original insight of the path-builders: that the fluctuations are not random but patterned, that they follow a logic tied to the rock's own time-depth, that the paths were laid along lines of *consistent duration*, places where time moves at the same rate as it does outside the plateau, while the spaces between are subject to the longer time that the stone itself inhabits.

The power thinks all of this very clearly, very close to the elder, for a long time.

Whether this transmission occurs — whether a human mind can receive what a power thinks near it — is not certain. The power is not certain. The elder shows no obvious sign of revelation. She stands. She does the work. She breathes slowly, economically, the breath of someone who sorted out how much air they need.

But.

When she turns to go, she pauses.

She pauses in the way that people pause when something they have known incompletely suddenly feels more complete. Not a new thought — it would be wrong to say she has a new thought. It is more that an old thought deepens, the way a reflection in a basin suddenly clarifies when the water stills. She has stood near this outcrop many times. She knows the practice. But standing here now she feels the *reason* for the practice in a way she has not felt it before, or has felt only partially, as a direction that now becomes a destination.

She stands a moment longer. Then she walks home on the path, and her feet know the path better than they did, and the knowing is not in her feet but in her whole body, and the whole-body knowing is a different thing from the feet-knowing she has had for years.

That night she talks to her daughter — not the child learning the path, who is too young yet, but an older daughter, already grown, who has the capacity and the patience. She talks about the outcrops and the paths and what she understood today that she did not understand yesterday, and she talks about it in the particular quiet voice of this plateau, and her daughter listens in the particular way of this place, which is with the whole body, without urgency.

The thread does not complete in one night. It will take years, more conversations, the daughter's own time at the outcrops and her own deepening of what her mother began to articulate. But the thread has been re-found. It will not be lost again in this generation or, if the daughter holds it and thinks about how to hold it properly, in the next.

---

## What the Power Does Next

It does not go to the thinning population and intervene. It does not reroute the migration of the birds to bring more consistent news from below. It does not warm the winters.

But.

It goes to the water basins. Not to change them — it would not change them. The temporally-displaced reflections are correct features of this place and touching them would be the wrong kind of touch. But it looks at the basin that is used most often for drinking, the most central and reliable one, and it sees the community in the water — the organisms in the dew, their cousins in the standing water, the full invisible life of the basin — and it pays careful attention to a small tension it finds there. A pathogen that has been present for a long time, that the basin's community has managed at equilibrium, but that in certain cold-wet seasons tips out of equilibrium in a way that the people have learned to avoid — they don't drink from this basin in certain conditions — but which, in a winter severe enough, when alternatives are frozen, might not be avoidable.

The power sits with this. It is not an emergency. It has not caused harm yet. It may never cause harm. The people know about it, in their partial way.

What the power does is: it tells the truth to the basin.

This is not a metaphor. The power thinks — clearly, close to the water, in the way that it thought near the elder — the full equilibrium. The relationship between the pathogen and the other organisms. The conditions that maintain balance and the conditions that don't. Not an intervention in the biology. Not a killing of the pathogen, which would alter the basin's whole ecology and accomplish something that the basin has not achieved in ten million years, which is: the wrong kind of change. Instead: a clarification of the equilibrium to itself, the way you tell a tipped-over scale what the right balance is, and watch it right itself.

The basin's community is not intelligent. It cannot hear. But systems respond to conditions, and what a power can do — gently, without imposition — is be a condition. To be present near a system with the quality of right balance in mind is to add, infinitesimally, to the conditions that produce right balance. A nudge so small that the word nudge is too large.

The basin does not visibly change. The water still reflects the slightly-wrong sky. The invisible community in it continues its invisible work. But the slope of probability has shifted, barely, toward the equilibrium and away from the edge.

This is the most the power does here. Because the most is not always the right amount.

---

## The Child

The power finds the child last, which is not last in importance but last in sequence — the child is the furthest into the future of anything the power has spent time with here, the longest investment, the one in whose life the most of the plateau's future is held.

It does not do anything for the child.

It watches her walk the path.

It watches with a quality of attention that — if the child felt it, which she may, in some wordless body-register — would feel like being seen. Not examined. Not assessed. *Seen*, in the way that matters: completely, without agenda, with the full weight of attention that leaves nothing unseen.

The child continues walking. She is thinking about something else. She is thinking about the piece of lichen she found this morning that was a different color than the others, orange-yellow where everything else is grey-white-green, and she wants to show it to someone but the right person was busy. She is thinking about this and walking the path and feeling the path under her feet and not knowing what the feeling means, only that it means something.

The power watches her think about the lichen and walk the path, and it experiences — there is no better word — tenderness.

The tenderness is not pity. Not protectiveness in the way that protectiveness can mean *I will step between you and what comes*. It is the tenderness of witnessing something that is exactly what it should be and knowing that this is rare, and that the rarity makes it more rather than less itself, and that the correct response to rare things that are exactly what they should be is not to interfere but to be present to them. To honor them with full attention.

The child walks the path. The path holds her feet in its worn groove. The lichen waits to be shown to someone.

The power is present for all of this.

Then, because it is a benevolent power and benevolence also means knowing when to leave, it lets itself not quite be here anymore, in the way that it was never quite here in any form the place would have marked. The cloud system continues its circuit. The shadows continue their occasional mild wrongness. The stone continues its slow negotiation with the moss. The elder sits with her daughter that night and begins to find the words for what she felt at the outcrop. The basin holds its balance.

---

## What Was Given

A thread of knowledge, returned.
A slope of probability, barely shifted.
A child, witnessed.

These are small things. They are the right size.

A benevolent and wise power, arriving at a place that has already arrived, does not arrive to fix it. Does not arrive to save it. Does not arrive to fill its silences or warm its cold or correct the shadows that fall wrong.

It arrives to see it. To understand that the ten million years of settlement, the moss in the cracks and the people on their paths and the enormous subterranean sound of surfaces recognizing each other, all of it is — the word arrives last, because on this plateau the right words arrive last — *enough*. Not merely adequate. Not a stage on the way to something better. *Enough in itself*, which is not the same as nothing more to care for, nothing more to tend, but is the condition in which tending is understood correctly: not as improvement but as care, not as elevation but as accompaniment.

The plateau has become.

The power, visiting, becomes for a moment also arrived — the effect of this place on those who pass through it, even the brief-visiting powerful, even the benevolent and wise. You stand in the middle of the plateau and the sense of *toward* leaves you. And in the absence of toward, what remains is the specific fine-grained texture of what simply and finally is.

The child will show someone the lichen.
The elder's daughter will sit with what she was told and let it grow into understanding.
The basin will hold its quiet equilibrium.
The moss will grow by the width of a thought into the next crack in the stone.

The power, departing the way it arrived — gradually, retroactively, no moment identifiable as the crossing — carries with it the quality of having been present to something that did not need it, and was not lesser for not needing it, and was not unchanged by the brief fact of being witnessed with full attention and love and the wisdom to mostly not interfere.

This is what the power brings back from the Plateau of Settled Things.

This is what the plateau allows itself to have received.

Far off, at the edges, the cloud system performs its circulation. It will come back through here in a few hours. It always does.
