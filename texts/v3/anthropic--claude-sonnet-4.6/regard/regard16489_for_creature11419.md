# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — which is to say it does not arrive, it was always capable of being here and has chosen, now, to be.

It has no body to adjust for pressure. Pressure is not a problem it has. The cool water, the darkness that is not quite darkness, the sharp young rock, the industrial smell of sulfide and mineral and biological process — these register, but register the way weather registers on something that has learned to love weather: as texture, as the character of a place, as information about what kind of care is needed here, which is always specific, always non-transferable from place to place.

It looks, first. This is important: it looks for a long time before it does anything.

It sees the tube worms. It follows the slow extension and sudden collapse of the plumes with something that would look, from outside, like the attention of a person watching a fire. Not excitement — there is no urgency in it — but a quality of engagement, of finding the worm's behavior genuinely interesting, which it does, because it is. The worm's abolished gut. The bacterial arrangement inside the trophosome, that specialized organ that is and is not the worm's own tissue, the partnership so thorough it has become anatomy. The power considers this and what it feels — there is no other word for what happens in it — is something in the family of *yes*. Something like recognition. The worm has found, through the long argument of evolution, a form of relation that the power understands: the question of where one thing ends and another begins being resolved not by separation but by integration, the boundary dissolving into function. *Yes*, the power thinks, or something that precedes thought. *I know this shape of solution.*

It follows a polychaete from its crevice across the basalt. It watches the iridescence of the setae and understands that the iridescence is for no one. This does not diminish it. The power is old enough — though old is wrong, the power exists in a relationship to time that makes old imprecise — to know that the most beautiful things are reliably the ones that do not know they are beautiful, are beautiful without audience, are beautiful as a side effect of being what they are. The polychaete's setae catch the light that comes down from the unreachable surface and return it as color to water that has no witness. The power witnesses it. Briefly there is witness. This is enough. The polychaete disappears into another crevice and the power is not sad about this.

The shrimp swarm moves in its not-quite-coordinated wave and the power experiences something that would look like delight if you could see it, which you cannot. The swarm responding to the warm-water eddy, ten thousand individual responses producing something that looks like a single decision — the power knows this dynamic from other scales, has watched it in other systems, finds it no less remarkable for its familiarity. This is one of the things the power knows about attention: that true attention does not wear things out. The ten-thousandth time is still the first time, if you are paying real attention.

---

It examines the state of things.

The ridge is young, yes. The rock is sharp. The vent chemistry is within the range the current community can tolerate, is actually quite good — there is sulfide, there is temperature gradient, there is the mineral output that the bacterial mats require. The larvae of tube worms drift in the water column above and are settling in the right places; the community is expanding toward the edges of viable chemistry at the pace you would expect. The shrimp population is dense but not over-dense; the bacterial mats can sustain the current grazing pressure. The polychaetes are at equilibrium with the gastropods. The crabs are being crabs, which means everything is working well enough to support opportunists.

The power checks. It checks the way you check on something you care about, which is not suspiciously but attentively, looking not for problems first but for the whole picture first, the problems being visible within the whole picture when they exist and not being imposed on the picture by the checking.

There are no problems.

This is not always the case and the power has no expectation that it should be. But here, now: the vent field is functioning. The young ridge is spreading at its pace, the vents are stable, the community has established itself around them with the precision of things that have been doing this, collectively, for the entire deep history of vent ecology. There are dead tube worms here and there, their white tubes standing empty where they died for reasons that have nothing to do with systemic failure — a shift in local chemistry, a rock fall, the ordinary mortality of organisms. Their tubes are being colonized by gastropods now. It is fine. The system is absorbing its own losses and converting them to habitat, which is what systems in good condition do.

The power holds this knowledge the way you hold something that can be set down gently.

*This is all right*, it thinks, or feels, or is.

---

Here is what it does:

It pays attention.

This is not a metaphor for inaction. Attention, real attention — not the anxious monitoring of something you fear will fail, not the proprietary surveillance of something you own, but the full-presence witnessing of something you love — this is an act. It takes something to give it. When you pay attention to something you are offering it something that cannot be manufactured or substituted: you are offering it the fact of its own significance, the confirmation that what is happening here is worth the noticing, that this is not nothing, that the tube worm extending its plume into the water column of a nameless ridge in a part of the ocean no human being has visited or named is an event in the universe that registers as an event, that there is something it is for, even if what it is for is only itself.

The power knows — this is perhaps what makes it wise, or what makes it possible to call it wise — that the worst thing it can do to this place is decide it needs something. The worst thing is the intervention that comes from anxiety, from the need to improve, from the subtle violence of deciding that what is is not quite enough. The tube worms do not need to know they are beautiful. The shrimp do not need the experience of sunlight to make their lives complete. The polychaetes are not deprived by not knowing about forests. The power is old enough, or exists in sufficient relationship to the whole, to know that every life is complete at every moment — not finished, not perfect, but complete in the sense of being exactly what it is, without lack, without the need of outside addition.

And so it does not add.

It witnesses the bacterial mats doing their ancient chemistry. It follows the heat-shimmer of vent exhale into the light-shaft from above and watches the two interact — the planet's exhale and the sun's light finally meeting, briefly, in this column of water, touching without consequence and separating. It hears the shrimp-snap and the ridge-groan and the deep pressured hiss of water finding its way through new rock and thinks — with something that is not a thought but occupies the place thoughts occupy — *I hear you. I am here. This is real.*

---

There is one thing more.

Not an intervention. Call it a gesture, the way leaving a door open is a gesture — it does not compel, it only makes possible. The power does this: it holds in mind, simultaneously, the vent field and the rest of what exists. The forest. The bumblebee. The tidal flat. The human being, wherever that is, probably too warm, probably too noisy, probably navigating its own pressures by instruments much clumsier than infrared-sensitive eyes and chemical gradients. It holds all of this in mind the way you can hold two things in mind at once if you are not required to explain how.

What this does — what this is for — is difficult to say. Maybe nothing. Maybe it does nothing. Maybe the tube worm is not changed by being held in mind alongside the tree, maybe the tree is not changed by being held in mind alongside the tube worm, maybe the whole gesture is for the power itself and not for the things it holds.

But the power believes — it has something that functions like belief — that to be known is not nothing. That to be held in a mind that also holds the rest of existence and finds you worth holding alongside it is a form of love, and that love is not passive even when it does not act, and that to love something fully, without wanting it to be different, without needing it to be aware of being loved, is one of the genuine things that can be done in the universe, one of the things that is worth doing. 

So it holds the vent field. The shrimp. The tube worms in their thickets. The bacteria doing their chemistry in the dark. The polychaetes in their crevices. The gastropods grazing the living film. The young basalt still cooling from what it recently was. The whole improbable, thriving, ancient, newborn, unwitnessed, now-witnessed place.

It holds it the way water holds what is submerged in it: completely, on all sides, with equal pressure, without preference.

*You are here*, the power says, in whatever medium it uses, which may not be sound but is something that serves the function of saying. *You are here and you are what you are and that is enough. It was always enough. I am paying attention. Go on.*

The tube worm extends its plume.

The shrimp swarm eddies toward warmth.

The ridge groans once, deeply, finding its angle.

The power remains a little longer, the way attention remains when it is real. And then, having done what it came to do, which was only this, it goes — not away, because it does not go away, but back into the background of everything, where it was before, where it always is, paying attention to more than any single place can hold, and therefore to this place also, always, in the way that all of this is held.

The water is cool.

The light comes down.

The system runs.
