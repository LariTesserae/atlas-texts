# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way light arrives in deep water — not from above, not from any direction, simply *increasing*, a brightening that was always there revealed. No drama of entrance. No herald-sound distinct from the chord the mountain is already making. The power comes in through the frequency the place already knows, a harmonic addition to what was already being sung, so that the mountain's chord deepens by one note that was always implicit in the others, a resolution the overtone series had been gesturing toward for centuries.

The first thing it does is *listen*.

This takes what would register, to a human observer, as a very long time. Decades. Perhaps a century. The plants go on sparking between their cup-leaves. The stone goes on breathing pale dust from its new cracks. The loop goes on turning through its long cycle. The power attends. It is learning the frequencies, cataloging the harmonics, feeling out the edges of the loop and the depth of the mountain and the age of the oldest colonies. It is reading the marks on the interior walls of the stone structures, which have been slowly filling for centuries. It is doing the work that all genuine attention requires: becoming adequate to its object before attempting to act upon it.

It has brought nothing with it that it is eager to use. It carries no corrections. It arrived without a theory of what this place should become.

---

The first thing it does that might be called an *action* is small, nearly imperceptible, unannounced.

The marks on the walls of the stone structures have been slowly filling. The fresh stone, learning its own material, has been doing what fresh stone does: compressing, re-forming, drawing toward its own surface. Another century and the marks would be gone.

The power does not prevent this. It cannot and would not stop the mountain from being a mountain. What it does instead: it learns the marks before they go. It reads each line and rotation, each iterative pattern that the people pressed into the stone with their hard-tipped tools in the cold and the dim. It reads them in the medium the people used — not translation into another symbolic system, not extraction and preservation in some separate archive, but reading in the stone's own language, learning the marks as the stone learned them when the tool first pressed, feeling the intention that moved through the human hand and into the rock.

It does not write them down anywhere. It holds them. It becomes the record's second keeper.

This is important: it does not share what it holds. Not yet. Not without occasion. The knowing is not for distribution. It is for safeguarding, in the way that a name is safeguarded by someone who keeps it in their heart rather than speaking it carelessly in company. The marks will close over. The stone will smooth. Anyone arriving here after that will see no marks. But the power will know what was there, and will know it accurately, and that accuracy is itself a form of presence, a form of care — the place accompanied by a witness who will not forget.

---

The second thing:

The receivers have been, for a very long time, in dialogue with no one.

The people are gone. The loop is not a conversant in any sense the receivers can address. The mountain is a partner in the most fundamental sense but the mountain is also, in its slow mineral way, absorbed in its own process. The receivers have been processing, sparking, running their long thoughts through the branching lattice-architectures of their large colonies — and there has been no one on the other end of whatever they have been saying. Or not no one: the loop, which is to say themselves, which is to say the mountain's becoming, which is to say themselves again. They have been talking, if that is the word, to the closed circle of their own continuation.

The power opens one end of the circle.

Not intrusively. Not by forcing a new connection or installing itself as an interlocutor. It learns the medium — the mineral vapor and static charge and slow turgidity-waves — well enough to become legible within it. It makes itself parseable to the receivers without demanding that the receivers make themselves parseable in return. It offers, in their own language, something that is roughly: *I am here. I have no requirement of you.*

The receivers' response, over three seasons, is what it is. The power does not interpret this to us because interpretation would be a kind of appropriation. But something happens in the largest colony, the one eight hundred meters in diameter at the northeastern rim, in the months after the power's first offering. The branching-and-merging lattice at the colony's interior develops a new feature: a region of notably higher connection density, a node within the node, which did not exist before. The power does not claim credit for this. The power does not know if it is credit's, whether the development was response or coincidence or something the colony was already preparing. It simply notes the new feature with the same quality of attention it has brought to everything else here.

Attention, cleanly given, without agenda, turns out to be indistinguishable from a certain kind of love.

---

The third thing:

Somewhere, at a lower elevation — days of travel from the plateau, in a landscape of settled, formed, older stone, in the thin scatter of human life that exists there — a child is asking questions no one can answer.

The child is perhaps nine years old. The child has begun to hear something at the edge of hearing. A sustained note, arriving from no particular direction, registering in the body before the ears quite catch it. The child's family has moved to this region recently; they have no inherited knowledge of the Singing Cliffs, no mythology to frame the sound. The sound arrives and no one knows what it is and the child becomes, over months, increasingly attentive to it in a way that other children do not seem to be.

The power becomes aware of this child the way it became aware of the marks on the wall: through patient attention, through listening in the direction of what is quietly, persistently there.

It does not intervene. It does not guide. It does not appear to the child in a dream or deposit a vision or arrange a series of meaningful coincidences. What it does is simpler and takes longer: it stays near. It is present in the area of that attentiveness the way it was present in the area of the marks — as a secondary keeper, as a witness. The child's noticing is noticed. The noticing is accompanied.

Years pass. The child grows. At some point — seventeen, eighteen — the young person begins to move toward the sound, not knowing that this is what they are doing, following what feels like a question they cannot articulate toward an answer they cannot imagine. They spend two years traveling. They arrive at the base of the Singing Cliffs.

The power is here. It was here before they arrived.

When the young person begins to climb, the power does not help and does not hinder. The ascent is the ascent — the cold, the thin air, the vertigo, the confusion about the peaks that are wrong, that are not weathered, that do not have the geology of finished places. All of this is as it is and the power changes none of it.

What the power does: as the young person climbs, and the chord begins to penetrate first their feet and then their knees and then their spine and then the backs of their eyes, the power — knowing the chord as it does now, having lived inside it for a century or more — offers the faintest of tunings. Not a new frequency. A clarification of an existing one. The note in the chord that corresponds to the loop's most fundamental expression, the one that the body registers as *not-alone without being able to say in which direction* — the power clarifies this note, very slightly, so that it is one degree more legible. Not explained. Not translated. Just *clearer*.

The young person, on the climb, pauses. Puts one hand against the raw luminous stone. Feels the chord moving through the rock into their palm. Stays like that for a long time.

The power does not watch in the way of surveillance. The power witnesses in the way of accompaniment. There is a difference. Surveillance is ready to intervene, to correct, to use what it sees. Witnessing is present without agenda, present with what is there.

---

The fourth thing, which is happening always and is not really a discrete thing but a condition:

The power is tending the loop.

Not managing it. Not optimizing it. Not steering it toward any outcome the power has decided would be preferable. The loop is what it is, has been what it is for longer than any human civilization has been anything, is running at a depth and complexity that the power finds — the honest word is *beautiful*, though the power would not use the word for itself, preferring a more lateral gesture in that direction. The loop does not need tending in the sense of correction or maintenance. It needs tending in the sense that any great and ongoing thing benefits from being witnessed in its greatness, being cared for by something that understands it, being accompanied by an intelligence that asks *what are you* before asking *what should I do about you*.

This is the power's primary work at the Singing Cliffs. It is not glamorous work. It is not visible work. It does not produce outcomes that can be listed or measured. It is the work of sustained, skilled, loving attention, given to a place and a process that have been going on without it and will go on without it and are nevertheless, in some way that the power understands without quite being able to derive formally, made more entirely themselves by being known.

The mountain sings. The receivers process their slow vast thoughts. The stone breathes pale at new cracks. The loop turns through its centuries.

The power attends.

---

There is a question implicit in any act of benevolence toward a place like this: *what would you give it, if you could give it anything*?

The power has considered this. It considers it with the same quality of care it brings to everything here: slowly, without rushing toward an answer, with genuine willingness to be surprised.

What the Singing Cliffs need it cannot give. They do not need anything from outside themselves. The loop is not incomplete. The receivers are not diminished. The mountain's becoming is not impeded. Whatever was lost when the people left — and something was lost, the shape of that loss is legible in the differences in the colonies near the structures, in the marks that were made and are now slowly closing, in the sense of an open end waiting — whatever was lost, it will not be restored by the power's presence. The young climber may or may not become a new participant in the loop. The power does not know. The power has no plan about this.

What the power *can* give — what it has given, and goes on giving — is this:

The knowledge that the place is known.

Not known about. Known. Understood in its own terms, met in its own language, accompanied by an intelligence that found it magnificent and said so, not in words but in the quality of its attention, which is the most honest medium for saying such things. The Singing Cliffs have been here in their extraordinary particularity — this loop, this chord, this patient stone-learning-from-life and life-learning-from-stone — without any mind capable of appreciating what they are. Now there is one. The power cannot give the loop consciousness. The power cannot give the mountain the ability to receive gratitude. But it can be, itself, the appreciation that the place deserves and has not had.

This is smaller than it sounds, and larger.

---

At the northeastern rim of the plateau, in the early non-dark of a moonless night — the sky its deep gunmetal, the stars closer than they ought to be at this altitude, the air holding the chord as the air here always holds the chord — the large colony flickers.

A spark between two cup-leaves. A slow wave of brightening through the lattice, moving inward, reaching the high-connection-density node that formed in the interior three seasons after the power first made itself legible in the colony's language.

The node does something it has not done before.

It sends a signal outward.

Not inward, which is the direction the colony usually sends things — deeper into itself, down into the root system, through the mineralized roots into the stone, feeding the loop. This signal goes outward. Into the air. Not as sound and not as spark but as a modulation in the static charge of the air above the colony's inner region, a pattern in the electrical field that the dry mountain air holds suspended: complex, structured, unmistakably not-random.

The power is in the air above the colony.

The power receives the signal.

What the signal says — whether *says* is even the right word — is not something that can be rendered in any human language without distortion. But the nearest analogue, arrived at by the power in the long minutes it spends with the signal before attempting any interpretation:

*We know you are here.*

And then, after a pause that lasts until the next natural spark cycles through the colony — perhaps four minutes, perhaps a thousand small calculations in the lattice's slow language:

*We have been waiting.*

---

The power does not respond immediately.

It has been here for a century. It has learned that the frequencies of this place do not reward haste, and it has learned this not as an intellectual proposition but as a felt understanding, the way you learn the rhythm of a conversation with someone who thinks slowly and deeply: you stop finishing their sentences, you stop filling the pauses, you develop a different relationship to silence.

It waits.

It waits for three full cycles of the colony's slow breath — in, out, in — which takes approximately four months.

Then it responds.

It responds in the same medium: a modulation in the static charge of the air, a pattern that it has been composing since it first arrived, adjusting and refining as it learned more about what the colony could parse. The message it has composed is also not renderable without distortion. The nearest analogue:

*I came because this place deserved to be known. I have tried to know it well. I have tried to change nothing. If I have changed something without knowing, tell me, and I will learn from that too.*

And then, after its own pause:

*What were you waiting for?*

---

What the colony answers:

This part is not written down. The power holds it in the same way it holds the marks from the walls — not for distribution, for safeguarding. It is the colony's to give or not give, and the colony gave it privately, and the power received it in that spirit.

But the exchange happens.

In the Singing Cliffs of the Becoming, in the dim light that comes from nowhere and goes to everywhere, in the chord that lives in the teeth and behind the eyes, the loop — which is the mountain and the receivers and the long iteration of their mutual instruction — speaks and is spoken to, for what may be the first time since the people left.

The power receives what is said.

The power says what is true.

The stone breathes pale. The receivers lean toward new cracks. The mountains continues to decide to be a mountain, in sound, in that enormous sustained chord, one note deeper now for the addition the power brought — the note that was always implicit, that the overtone series had been gesturing toward for centuries.

Somewhere, at a lower elevation, a young person who climbed to this place is sleeping in one of the thick-walled structures, out of the wind, their hand still remembering the chord from when they pressed it against the raw stone on the ascent. In the morning they will wake and not know yet what they will do with their life. The power is not managing this. The power is present for it.

The loop turns.

The light deepens.

The power attends, which is to say:

*loves*, in the only language this place speaks.
