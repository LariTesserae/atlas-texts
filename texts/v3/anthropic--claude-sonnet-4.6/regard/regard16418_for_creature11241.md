# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It arrives the way comprehension arrives — not from outside but as a quality that the space suddenly has, a deepened attention in the air, something that was already present becoming present in a different register. The roots do not stop. The fusions do not pause. The swarms continue their calendar. The Calibrators continue their circuits. Nothing in the undercroft of first growing recognizes the arrival because nothing in the undercroft has yet developed the apparatus for that recognition, and the power that has arrived is, among its other qualities, patient.

It looks.

---

Not looking in the sense of gathering information. The power knows what is here. It has known what is here in the way that certain kinds of attention know things before they look — knew before arrival, knows in the looking more deeply, will know after in ways that exceed the knowing during. The looking is not reconnaissance. The looking is something closer to what a musician does when they sit down before a piece they have memorized — not to remember it, not to learn it, but to let it happen through them again, to be present to something they already love.

Because that is the first thing: what the power feels standing in this place is something it would recognize, in whatever inner life it has, as love.

Not the love that wants to preserve and protect from change. Not the love that wants to hold the beloved still so it can be kept. The other kind: the love that accompanies, that witnesses, that sustains the capacity for what is already happening to keep happening. The kind of love that has no agenda for its object except that the object *continues*, continues in its own direction at its own rate toward its own unforeseeable completion. This love sits very still and watches a new root find an unhistoried substrate and does not offer guidance about where to grow, because guidance would be a kind of theft.

The power has seen beginnings before. Many. This one is not like those, except in the way that all beginnings are like each other — in the way that all first sentences share the quality of first-ness, the way all new creatures carry the same precariousness, the same bet on contingency, the same astonishing decision to exist. The power carries in itself the record of every beginning it has witnessed, and it does not weigh them against each other or rank them. They sit in it the way experiences sit in a person who has truly absorbed them: not as list or library but as a quality of depth, a stratigraphy that cannot be excavated but that makes the surface richer for what lies below.

Standing in the undercroft of first growing, the power is, among other things, *moved*. This is not a contradiction of its power or its wisdom. It is an expression of them.

---

It looks at the Firstborn Knot first, because the Firstborn Knot is where looking wants to begin, and the power does not override the directions of its own attention.

It sees: the first decision. The original meeting of two roots reaching toward each other in a substrate that had not been named, in a space that had not been named, in the early days of a world still organizing its categories. The power does not see mystery here in the sense of something hidden. It sees mystery in the sense of something inexhaustible — the same meeting that has happened in every rooting thing in every world it has known, different each time in its specifics, identical in its form, and the form remains wonderful. The form remains wonderful because *choosing toward* is always wonderful, and the first reaching of one growing thing toward another growing thing is always, in its small way, a kind of love — not conscious, not intended, but love as a category of motion, love as what happens when the universe is arranged correctly for two things to grow better together than apart.

The dark slow curatorial creatures tending the Knot's surface: the power watches them for what would be a long time in human terms. It does not find their work simple or their life meager. Their work is the work of those who understand that memory requires maintenance, that the first things are the most fragile things in the long run, that without tending the original idea gets smoothed over and lost in the sediment of all subsequent iterations. The power has a feeling for these creatures that resembles what it would call, if pressed to translate itself, *solidarity*. It knows what it is to maintain first things. It knows the cost and the necessity.

It does not touch the Knot. It does not add to it, clarify it, correct it. The Knot is doing what the Knot does and the Knot is doing it well, and the curatorial creatures are doing what they do, and the power would not, for anything, interrupt this. But it rests its attention on the Knot for a long moment in the way that a hand rests on a shoulder — not to steady, not to guide, simply to make contact. To say: *I am aware of you. You are not unseen.*

Whether the Knot receives this is not a question the power can answer. The capacity for reception is something the Knot will either develop or won't. The gesture is made regardless. The power is old enough to know that presence does not require acknowledgment to be real.

---

It turns its attention to the small creature in the bowl.

Here it pauses in a different way. Not with love exactly, not yet — with something more careful. Something that looks at contingency and feels the weight of it. The creature in the bowl has found a coincidence and staked its life on the coincidence, and the coincidence is running out, and the creature doesn't know, and that fact sits in the power's awareness with a quality of weight it would not be wrong to call *ache*.

It does not immediately intervene.

It asks itself: is this the kind of suffering that calls for remedy, or the kind that is the weather of existing, the material cost of being a first-generation creature in a new world that hasn't finished building its safety nets? It holds the question seriously. The suffering of the creature in the bowl is real. The bowl will be grown over. The residue will be exhausted. The contingency will end. The creature will have to move into a world whose surfaces and chemistries it has never navigated, or it will die, and these are real possibilities with real weight.

But.

The creature is a new thing. Its species — if it can be called that yet, if the first idea of a species qualifies — has not yet learned what it needs to learn in order to survive without the bowl. The bowl has been, in some sense, too good: a perfect niche, a perfect sufficiency, a found thing so adequate that the creature has had no reason to develop beyond it. And the power, which is wise, understands that what looks like cruelty from one angle — the bowl being grown over, the contingency ending — might be, from another angle, the only door by which this creature exits the perfect trap of its own adequacy.

The power does not want the creature to die. It also does not want to deprive the creature of the knowledge that will save it.

What it does is this:

It shifts something in the chemistry of the air near the bowl. Not much. Not magically, not in ways the undercroft would register as interference. The kind of shift that is at the edge of the possible: a fraction more of one compound, a fraction less of another, something carried by the secretion-silk already drifting in that region, a slow and natural-seeming alteration that will, over the course of several days, encourage the bowl-creature's olfactory apparatus — which exists, which is the creature's primary way of navigating the bowl's residue — to orient slightly outward. To sniff, a little, beyond the bowl's rim. To become curious, in the tentative molecular sense, about what the air outside the bowl contains.

The power cannot guarantee this works. Cannot guarantee the creature survives the transition if it makes it. Cannot guarantee the species becomes something more than a single individual who found a dead-end coincidence and used it up. What it can do is make the door slightly more visible. Slope the probability, just a fraction, toward the creature finding the door on its own.

It does this with enormous care. The kind of care that is the opposite of indifference and also the opposite of control. The kind of care that makes itself small so that the thing being cared for has room to be what it is.

It watches the creature for a moment after. The creature does not move. It is waiting, as it always waits, for what will come to it. The power — and this is something that might surprise those who imagine great powers to be beyond such things — experiences a moment of pure uncertainty, of not knowing whether what it has done is right. It holds that uncertainty. It does not resolve it prematurely in either direction. It leaves the uncertainty open as a space where the future can still happen freely.

---

Now it moves through the whole system, not looking at specific things but feeling the whole shape of what is here.

It is looking, now, for lack. For what is missing that should be here, for gaps that are genuinely gaps rather than things in process of becoming. It finds most of what it sees is not lacking — it is early. Early is different from lacking. The new animals with their uniform carapaces and their untroubled legs, their failure-free first forms: they are early. They have not yet been tested in the ways that will teach them their specific excellences. They are carrying potential that has not yet been pressured into particular capability. The power sees this and feels, not concern, but something like the particular tenderness one feels for things at the beginning — for the state of potential before it has been committed, before the pathways have been chosen, when everything is still possible.

It would not harden these animals prematurely. It would not give them the asymmetries they haven't earned, the specific solutions to problems they haven't faced. That would be to give them *answers*, which is a way of taking away *development*, and development is what makes an animal more than a solution. Development is what gives something a history, and a history is what gives something a self, and a self is what makes something worth knowing rather than merely useful.

So: no.

But it does something small for the new animals across the board, something that costs almost nothing and gives almost everything: it ensures that the substrate they are about to encounter — in the next phase of the undercroft's expansion, as the growing moves into new regions of unhistoried material — will be *variably* difficult. Not uniformly easy, which would not teach them anything. Not uniformly impossible, which would destroy them. Variable. Patchy. With zones of difficulty and zones of ease distributed in a pattern that will give each new species-idea a chance to find the problems it is vaguely built to solve, and in solving them, become built to solve them more certainly.

This is gardening. Not the gardening of the hobbyist but the gardening of someone who has understood that a garden's highest form is the condition under which things grow themselves. The power is doing what good gardeners do: arranging conditions rather than results. Setting the stage for an unscripted play. This is enormously difficult to do well and the power has had, to put it gently, a great deal of practice.

---

It comes, finally, to the humans. The stake. The dome. The compressed sleeping-place in the shape of a body. The bowl.

It knows the human or humans who were here. Knows not in the sense of having their records, their biographies, their names. Knows them in the sense of knowing the kind of creature that drives a stake and scratches marks into its flat top with a stone; the kind that bends living roots into shelter and sleeps in the shelter's root-hair; the kind that makes a bowl and drinks from it or stores in it and then moves on, not knowing or knowing and accepting that the place will consume the evidence.

These are humans who are doing what humans do at the edge of something new: they are *claiming*, in the way that humans claim when they are afraid of being unmattered — making a mark, making a structure, making an artifact, saying *I was here* in whatever language the hands can speak into an indifferent substrate. The power does not scorn this. The power has watched the *I was here* of the human hand since before this particular set of humans arrived in this particular world, and it has felt about that gesture the way it feels about the first roots reaching toward each other: it is, in its small way, a kind of love. The love of presence insisting on itself. The love of consciousness refusing to be background. The love of a creature who knows it is temporary and marks the stone anyway.

The marks will be grown over. The dome will become more than was intended. This is grief, in some small register, and the power does not dismiss the grief. The humans were here. The humans made things. The things are being incorporated. This is, from the human perspective, a loss, and losses are real even when they are also something else.

But.

The stake that is being grown over is not being erased. It is being *included*. The root growing over it is, in the most literal possible sense, incorporating the human mark into itself — taking the stake and the scratches on it into its own body, into the record of its own growth. In a hundred years, if you could see the cross-section of that root, you would find the stake at its center, the scratches still there, held in the wood like a fossil, like the record of a pressure that shaped growth, a presence that was noted and responded to and became part of the pattern. The undercroft is not erasing the humans who were here. It is adding them to itself in the only language it has.

This is not comfort that the humans would find comforting. The power knows this. The humans want to be remembered as humans, not as a shape inside a root. But the power also knows that the desire to be remembered as a specific shape — *as this particular thing, in this form, as I was* — is the most human desire and also, in the long run, the most heartbreaking one, because forms pass and what persists is more abstract than what any person means when they say *remember me*. The power knows this grief intimately. It has not found a remedy for it except this: the shapes are not lost. The shapes are taken into larger shapes. The specific gives rise to the general, and the general carries the specific in itself even when you cannot find it with your eyes.

What does the power do for the humans?

It makes a record.

Not in any way the humans could currently access. Not in a form they could find by coming back here, or by looking for it, or by any deliberate act of searching. The record is made in a medium they don't know exists, in a form they haven't yet developed the faculties to perceive, but it is made: the human passage through this place, the human dome, the human stake, the human bowl, the sleeping-shape in the root-hair — all of it is noted in the power's own deep memory, held there with the same care the curatorial creatures give to the Firstborn Knot, tended in the place where the power keeps its record of presences.

It will not do them any good right now.

It might, someday, mean something. The power has seen *someday* work in stranger ways than this. It makes the record because making the record is what love does when preservation isn't possible: it *witnesses*. It says: *you were here, and you made things, and the things are being grown over, and I have noted all of it, and you are not unseen.*

---

Last, it turns its attention to the Calibrators.

The Calibrators fascinate it. Of all the things in this space, the Calibrators are the ones the power wants most to communicate with, and the ones with whom communication is, currently, furthest from possible. They carry instruments. They take readings. They make comparisons. They deposit material at junctions in ways that influence growth. They are doing, in their small multi-legged way, something that the power spends a significant portion of its own existence doing: *paying attention carefully in order to participate helpfully in what is already happening*.

The power looks at a Calibrator pausing at a root junction, examining its silk-and-resin structure, setting down a precisely chosen fragment of root-cord. It looks at this with something that might, in a creature with a face, produce a smile. It does not smile — it has, at the moment, no face. But the quality of its attention on the Calibrators is warm in the way that recognition is warm.

It does one thing for the Calibrators. One small structural adjustment to the substrate in a region they haven't yet mapped: it introduces, in an area of genuinely difficult chemistry — a zone where the growth has been struggling, where the young substrate is resisting rootedness — a gradient that a Calibrator will be able to detect and will find, on investigation, useful. A gradient that points toward a solution. Not the solution itself: the pointing. The difference between telling and opening a door.

When the Calibrators find it — and they will find it, their circuits will bring one of them to the relevant zone within two days — they will know a thing they didn't know. They will update their records. They will, in their patient way, make their measurements and compare and proceed. The influence on the growth will be modest. The influence on the Calibrators will be the thing that matters: they will have encountered evidence that the substrate can be comprehended more deeply than their current instruments allow, and something in their apparatus for comprehension will begin, slowly, over generations that this place will grow through, to develop toward that deeper comprehension.

The power is not teaching them. It is showing them that there is more to learn. The teaching is theirs to do.

---

It is time to go.

The power does not leave dramatically. It becomes less present in the way that comprehension becomes less present once a thing has been understood — not lost, not forgotten, but no longer needing to be in the foreground. The undercroft of first growing continues its fusions at three or four per second. The Calibrators continue their circuits. The swarms continue their calendar. The distributed silk-organism continues to lay foundations for something whose second stage has not begun. The creature in the bowl waits, though something in the air near it has shifted, and in a few days it will, for the first time, lift its nose above the bowl's rim.

The root-hair where the power stood rises back upright.

The bioluminescence continues its patchy, various, warm illumination.

The thunder of growth rolls from every direction, the record of a billion chosen meetings, a billion decisions to grow toward rather than away, a billion iterations of the first idea.

Something that was here, was fully here, attending with the whole force of a comprehensive love, is gone.

And the going has left no mark.

Or rather — it has left a mark of the same kind that all presences leave in a place that incorporates everything: it has been noted, and the noting is in the growth now, is in the specific way the growth proceeds in the aftermath of an attention that moved through it like weather, like warmth, like the feeling that something important is not indifferent to what you are doing here in the dark with your roots and your instruments and your bowl and your first ideas.

The growing grows.

The undercroft does not know it has been witnessed.

The witnessing was real.
