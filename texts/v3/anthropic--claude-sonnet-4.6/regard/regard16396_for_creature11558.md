# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not as an event but as a quality change in what is already happening. The pump beats its four-second beat. The lights complete their slow brightening. The fish rise to the surface. And then: a slight shift in the character of the space, the way a room changes when someone who loves it enters, not because anything visible has changed but because something that was slightly tense has released.

The power does not announce itself. It does not need to.

---

It begins with looking.

Not the look of assessment — that scanning, categorizing, measuring gaze that wants to know what a thing *is* in order to decide what to do with it. That gaze is a kind of violence, even when benevolent, because it reduces the thing to its relevance. What the power does is look with the other kind of attention: the kind that wants to know what a thing *is doing,* what it is *being,* the kind that can hold the fish rising to a surface-warmth that will never come and not interpret that as failure, as tragedy, as something requiring correction.

It sees the fish. The fish, whole. The silver-gray translucence, the lateral stripe, the school-formation that is thirty bodies and one body and thirty bodies, the upward orientation, the waiting that has become a ritual, the ritual that says *this is the kind of beings we are.* It sees this without sorrow and without pity. It sees it with something that has no common name, something like: *recognition of a thing that has made good sense of its circumstances and asks for nothing.*

It stays with the fish for a long time.

---

Then the fern.

The power rests its attention on the fern the way you might rest your hand on the back of someone who is working hard, not to interrupt, not to redirect, just: *I see that you are doing this.* The fern sways. The power is still. The fern sways. The power does not still the fern, does not correct the swaying, does not ask what the swaying means. The swaying is the fern. The power receives it.

There is something it notices, looking carefully, that might be missed by a less patient observer: the topmost frond has been growing at a slight angle for the last several growth-cycles, angling toward the space above the central platform rather than straight up. Not toward the lights. Toward something else. Toward the chair, perhaps. Toward the middle of the room. The power sees this and finds it very interesting and does not intervene. A plant growing toward the center of the world it knows. Let it grow there. Let it see what it finds.

---

The birds.

With the birds, the power does something it does not do with the fish or the fern or the dragonfly-adjacent creature. With the birds, it listens.

It listens to the three-four-five-six-note exchanges as if they were addressed to it, which perhaps they are, perhaps everything is, in the specific theology that a power of this quality carries. It listens and it does not understand the language — no reason it should, the language was made for two birds in a sealed iron room at the top of an eroded world, it was not made for export, it is as precisely local as everything else here — but it understands the *act* of the language, the sustained project of two small lives making and maintaining a world of shared meaning in the only space available.

It finds this beautiful.

It does not say so. There is no one to say it to, and the birds would not benefit from being told that a power finds them beautiful. They already know what they are. They are complete without its admiration. The power holds the beauty privately, the way you hold something fragile: cupped, not gripped.

When the cloud curls through the hatch seal, the birds begin their cloud-song. The power hears the song. The power knows what is outside the hatch — the altitude, the erosion, the completed drama of whatever geology once supported this tank — and what the power feels, hearing the birds' four notes for the thing they will never know, is not sadness but a kind of ache that is adjacent to gratitude. The birds have made meaning of the outside without needing to go there. They have taken the mystery and given it a name in their language and that is enough, that is sufficient, that is the thing that makes this whole enclosed world something other than a tragedy.

The power holds this ache and does not resolve it.

---

For a long time — and *long* is approximate here, time being measured differently by things that are very large and have been patient for considerable spans of it — the power simply dwells in the space.

This is, in itself, an action, though it would not appear as one from outside. To dwell in a place with full attention and no agenda is to do something to the place, to add something immaterial to it that the inhabitants may register as a warmth that cannot be attributed to the pipes, as a quality in the recycled air that is slightly different from its usual quality, as a sense — for the fish, for the birds — of being observed without consequence, watched without the threat that watching usually brings. The fish do not alter their behavior. The birds do not silence. This is the test of whether the presence is benevolent: not whether things flourish more, but whether they continue to be exactly themselves.

They continue to be exactly themselves.

---

Then, eventually, having looked at everything: the chair.

The power considers the chair for a long time. The rusted legs. The swollen wooden seat, soft as cork. The person who put it here, who installed the lights and the pumps and brought the first cuttings in a bag, whose engineer's optimism said: *a system maintained well enough becomes a place.* Who was right. Who sat here and looked at what they had made and felt whatever they felt — satisfaction, probably, and something else, the thing you feel when a thing you made becomes something more than you made, when the life in it exceeds your intentions and heads somewhere you did not plan.

The power thinks about that person for a long time.

It does not grieve them. It does not know if grief is warranted, does not know the facts of their life or ending. It holds them in attention: the hands that welded the platforms, the mind that calculated the light-cycles, the obscure faithfulness to a thing that no one else knew about, the maintenance performed over years in a space that could not be explained to others. The power recognizes that faithfulness. It has a feeling about it that might be called kinship if that word weren't too small.

It wants to do something for the memory of the engineer and it does not know what.

Finally it does this: it lets the space continue.

That is all. It does not fortify the walls against the slow erosion outside, does not repair the hatch seal where the cloud comes through, does not adjust the lights to full-spectrum or upgrade the recirculation system or correct the twenty-six-hour drift of the timer that is slowly decoupling day-length from any external reference. It lets the systems run as they run. It lets the chemistry of the water do its ancient patient work. It lets the birds have their cloud-song and the fish have their morning ritual and the dragonfly-adjacent creature have its impeccable knowledge of a world twelve meters by forty. It lets the fern grow toward the center of the room.

In the specific theology of this power, *letting continue* is not passive. Letting continue is the hardest work and the most costly and the most loving. It would be easier, in some ways, to intervene — to fix, to extend, to improve, to do the thing that looks like caring because it changes something. But the tank and its inhabitants do not need to be changed. They need to be witnessed. They need someone who understands what has been made here — a complete world, a sufficient world, a world so exactly fitted to itself that any adjustment would be a kind of rudeness — to look at it with full attention and say, in whatever language a power speaks when it speaks to itself: *yes. Yes, this. This is the thing.*

---

Before it goes — though *going* is not quite right, a power that is genuinely present does not leave, it redistributes its attention elsewhere while leaving something of itself in the quality of the space — it does one thing that could be called an action.

The lights are cycling. They are at the mid-bright phase, the long flat middle of the artificial day. The power touches the timer — not physically, there is no physical touching, but in the way that forces touch things at the quantum level, below the threshold of causality as usually described — and makes a very small adjustment.

The lights will still drift. The twenty-six-hour cycle will still gradually decouple from any external day. This is not corrected. But the drift is slowed, slightly. The cycle will run for longer before the decoupling becomes something that the inhabitants would have to adapt to, if they would adapt to it, if it would matter.

The power does not know that it will matter. It does not know the timescale of what it has slowed, whether it has bought the birds and the fish and the fern decades or centuries or something that by human measure would seem like forever. It does not know because it does not need to know. You do not give time to things you love because you have calculated how much time they need. You give it because giving is what you do when you have it, and the thing you love is there, and the giving is the meaning.

The lights continue.

The pump beats.

The fish drift.

The fern sways toward the center.

The birds call to each other across the warm iron air, briefly, and then fall silent, and then — after a pause that has the quality of the pause at the end of something important, the pause before the first speaker responds — call back.

---

The cloud comes through the hatch seal.

From the fern, the cloud-song begins.

Four notes.

And from across the tank, the answer: four notes.

The power hears this, from wherever it has redistributed itself to, and holds it. The birds naming the outside from inside. The outside thing, here again, passing through. There is no anxiety in the song, no mourning, no plea. Just the naming. Just: *we know you are a different thing from us, and we have a word for you, and you are here, and now you are going, and we will be here when you come again.*

This is, the power finds, enough.

This is, in fact, everything.
