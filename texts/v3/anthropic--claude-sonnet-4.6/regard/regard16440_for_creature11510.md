# The Attention

---

The power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand.

Arrival implies a before and an after — a moment of entry, a threshold crossed, an outside becoming inside. But what happens is not that. What happens is more like: attention, which has always been possible, becomes actual. The way a face in a crowd becomes visible not because it changed but because something in the looking changed. The Mud City has always been capable of being seen this way. Now it is.

The power is benevolent in the sense that a deep river is benevolent — not inclined toward harm, not organized around harm, but also not soft, not careful in the way that suggests fragility might be encountered and must be protected from itself. Wise in the same sense: not a wisdom accumulated, not hard-won against difficulty, but a wisdom that is structural, that is what wisdom is made of before it is distributed into people who possess portions of it. The power does not have opinions about the Mud City in the way that a visitor would have opinions. It has something closer to recognition. It looks at this place and its people and it recognizes, without needing to articulate what it recognizes, the thing that is happening here.

It sees the ground that is always becoming.

It sees the city that is made of the ground.

It sees the people who are made of the city.

It sees that the making is not linear, not progressive, not moving toward a destination — it is recursive, spiral, the making feeds the ground feeds the making, the city maintains itself by becoming itself continuously, the people maintain themselves by being the people that this place requires, and requires again, and will always require.

And the recognition the power has is this: *this is correct*. Not correct in the sense of perfect or finished or beyond adjustment. Correct in the sense that a thing is correct when it is doing what it is doing, fully, without contradiction between its nature and its expression. The Mud City is doing what it is doing. The people are who they are. The ground is what the ground is. There is an integrity here — not moral integrity, something deeper, structural integrity, the integrity of a thing that has not been divided from itself — and the benevolent and wise attention sees it and, seeing it, is moved.

The question is what to do.

---

The power considers intervention.

Intervention is available. It is always available, to a power like this — the range of possible interventions extends from the imperceptible to the transformative, from the slight nudge of a single thought in a single mind to the wholesale revision of conditions. It could improve the drainage. It could ensure the walls do not collapse. It could address the accumulated burden of illness that moves through any dense community, the deaths that come from proximity and the waterborne thing and the airborne thing and the child who does not make it through the first year. It could do a hundred things in the register of making-better, of reducing-harm, of what benevolence sometimes means in its more interventionist expressions.

It holds this. It does not dismiss it. The suffering is real. The child who does not make it through the first year is real, and the grief of the parents is real, and the power, which is benevolent, feels this fully — the grief, the specific weight of that grief, the grief of a person who holds a small body in a hot damp room while the city's indifferent noise continues. It does not look away from this. It does not reframe it into acceptability. The loss is a loss. The pain is a pain.

But it also sees what is true about this place: that the people in it have not been diminished by their conditions, have not been waiting for rescue from their conditions, have become, across long time, something that could not have become in easier conditions. Not hardened — something more interesting than hardened. *Calibrated.* The deep mutual knowledge. The embodied fluency. The language built to be heard over noise. The sleep that falls and rises without resistance. The immune system that is an education rather than a defense. The clay-workers who go to the edge of the city when the new material arrives, in the small window before it has experienced air, and take from it what cannot be taken later. These are not consolations for difficulty. These are what the difficulty made, over time, by the patient alchemy of people and place.

And the power knows — because it is wise, which means among other things that it has a very long relationship with unintended consequence — that to intervene in the conditions of the Mud City is to intervene in the conditions of the people the Mud City makes. Improve the drainage and something shifts, subtly, in what the feet know. Stabilize the ground and something shifts in what the old woman knows about the ground's long behavior. Address the illness and something shifts in the immune system's calibrated accuracy. The changes would be local and real and probably net positive in the short term and probably net something-more-complicated in the longer term, and the power does not trust its own certainty enough to make that trade on behalf of the people who live here.

It is wise enough to be humble. This is the hardest form of wisdom and the most important.

---

So what does it do?

It does this: it *tends* the thing that is already happening.

Not by changing it. By being with it, in the particular way that attention from a power like this constitutes a form of being-with. It is not surveillance. It is not assessment. It is something closer to what happens when someone genuinely loves a place or a people — not the love that wants to improve, but the love that is satisfied by the existence of the thing, that finds the thing's continuing-to-be a source of something. Of what? Not pleasure, exactly. Not satisfaction in any self-referential sense. More like: rightness. The sense that here is a thing the world is better for containing.

The tending is not passive. Active presence, even without intervention, does something. Here is what it does:

---

The dreams in the Mud City deepen.

Not strangely, not with vision or prophecy or any content that a dreamer could point to and say *this was unusual*. The dreams deepen in the sense that the sleeping mind goes further down — accesses more, integrates more, brings more of the day's experience into the processing that sleep is for. The child on the rooftop sleeping through the early afternoon has, in that sleep, a deeper encounter with what the morning held: the particular quality of a conversation, the way the clay felt in a certain state, the moment when someone's face told the full story before the words came. None of this surfaces as dream narrative, not exactly. It goes in and becomes, down in the place where things are stored as knowing-without-knowing rather than as memory-with-content.

Over time — not weeks, not years, something longer — the people of the Mud City carry slightly more of themselves, in this way. Their knowledge of each other deepens by a margin that no single person would identify as change. Their embodied knowledge of the city deepens by degrees that feel like nothing at all. The old woman's understanding of the ground's long behavior deepens fractionally, is fractionally more accurate, allows her to say to the clay-workers *go there, at the eastern edge, the ground has been telling me something* — and she is, imperceptibly, more right than she would have been. The dreaming does this slowly, as alchemy always works slowly, as the power does not wish to force the pace of anything that is already moving at the correct pace.

---

The clay-workers, in the window of the new clay's arrival, find their hands knowing something they did not know their hands knew.

Again: nothing dramatic. Not revelation. Not gift in the biblical sense, nothing burning, nothing descended from outside. The knowledge is *theirs* — the power would not insert what was not already there, because what is inserted does not root the way what grows roots. The power sees what is already in the hands and the attention of the clay-workers — what is in the distributed intelligence of their bodies moving against the new material in the window of its first hours — and it does something like: underlines it. Brings it slightly more to the surface. The clay-worker who has been feeling something in the new material without having words for what she feels will, in a session of working it, find the feeling slightly more present, slightly more available, slightly closer to being knowable rather than simply felt. She will not know this happened. She will simply work the clay, and the session will be a good session, and she will go home with tired hands that know something slightly more precisely than they knew it yesterday.

Over years: the craft deepens. The city's buildings become, fractionally, more accurately responsive to the ground they sit on. The materials are the same. The techniques are the same. The knowledge in the hands is richer.

---

The old ones who hold the long memory find, occasionally, when they are in the middle of telling someone something, that the telling goes further than they expected to go.

They reach into the past and bring out what has always been there and then, sometimes, find that the reaching goes a small distance further — not into invention, not into fabrication, but into a stratum of what-actually-happened that they did not know they had retained. A detail surfaces. Not invented — genuinely there, genuinely available, and now available in a way it was not before the power's attention. They tell it and it fits, and the person they are telling it to sees that it fits, and the understanding that results is slightly more complete than it would have been.

The living archive grows by these small recoveries. The knowledge of the city's history — always impressive, always dense with the accretion of what has been held by the people who cared about it — becomes, over time, fractionally more accurate. Not perfect. Not perfectly accurate. Just: slightly less lost.

---

One specific thing, small enough to be almost nothing.

There is a child — there is always a child, in a city this dense and this alive — who will, in a few years, be sick in the way that children in the Mud City are sometimes sick, and this sickness will be serious and the outcome is not predetermined. The power does not step in to prevent the sickness. It does not ensure survival. This would be to choose, to make a judgment about this child's life that the power does not feel it has the standing to make, and also — honestly — to begin a thing it cannot finish, because there are many children and the logic of individual intervention does not have a stopping point.

What it does is this: the mother of this child, who is already a person of great embodied knowledge, finds in the months before the illness that her attunement to her child's body is slightly sharper. The attention deepens that is already there. She is already a good mother in the specific, practical, embodied sense — she knows this child's temperatures and rhythms and signals — and what shifts is simply the quality of her listening, which becomes slightly more precise in exactly the register that will matter. When the illness comes she will catch it three days earlier than she would have caught it. Three days is the difference between two things, in this particular illness, in this particular child.

The power does not know that the child will survive. It genuinely does not know. But it knows that the mother knowing sooner is correct. That giving her the sharpness of attention she is already reaching toward is not interference with the Mud City's necessary self-making — it is support for it, support for what the Mud City actually is, which is people who know each other completely and act on that knowledge with skill.

The mother will not know that anything changed. She will think she caught it when she caught it because she is a good mother who pays attention. She will be right.

---

Here is what the power does not do:

It does not reveal itself.

There is a form of benevolence that requires acknowledgment — requires that the beneficiary know a gift was given, know who gave it, enter into a relationship of gratitude and recognition with the giver. This power has no need of this. It is not structured around need of this. To make itself known to the Mud City would be to become, immediately, a feature of the Mud City — something the people here would incorporate, interpret, integrate, respond to, and over time reshape into the specific form that the Mud City requires all things to take, which would be interesting and not without its value but would also be, in a subtle way, an imposition. The presence of a known benevolent power changes the self-understanding of a people. The power has thought about this carefully. The self-understanding of the people of the Mud City is not a thing it wants to change.

They understand themselves as people who live in a place that makes itself continuously. They understand themselves as people who maintain what has always been maintained, who know what has always been known, who are continuous with a ground that is always becoming. This self-understanding is accurate. It is also, in some sense, all they need. A known divine attention would layer over this something that is not exactly wrong but is not exactly right either — an additional story, an additional frame, a relationship with something outside that would inevitably pull some portion of their profound interiority toward a different kind of conversation.

The power loves this people too carefully to wish this on them.

---

Here is what it does do, finally:

It stays.

The attention, having become actual, does not lapse back into the merely possible. It remains. Not as a watcher, not as a judge, not as a benefactor waiting to be needed, but as presence — the way a place is changed by the presence of a person who loves it, even if that person does nothing, even if the love is not announced, even if the beloved cannot articulate what is different, can only feel that something in the quality of being here is, when they are actually here and not somewhere else, sufficient. Complete. Enough. Not perfect. Not finished. Not free from grief or effort or the child who does not make it through the first year. But: enough.

The Mud City has been here for longer than anyone knows. The ground has been deciding what it is for longer than the city has been here. The people have been made by this becoming for as long as the people have been people.

The power sits with this, in the old sense of sitting-with: present, unhurried, not asking the thing it sits with to be different from what it is, recognizing in it something that deserves to be recognized.

It is possible — the power considers this without certainty — that being recognized, even by something that does not announce its recognition, does something. That the act of genuine attention, even unfelt, even unknown, changes what is attended to at some level too deep for ordinary knowing. That the city and its people and the ground that is always becoming are, in some small way, more completely what they are because they have been fully seen.

Or not. It is possible that the power's presence changes nothing at all, and that the Mud City is exactly what it would have been, and that the people are exactly who they would have been, and that the tending is simply: love, extended toward something that did not need it and is not changed by it.

The power considers this, and finds that it does not change anything about what it is doing.

It stays.

The ground shifts fractionally underfoot and releases the smell of iron and something sweeter, mineral-warm, the breath of geology that has recently come to the surface.

The sound is total.

The air is heavy.

The clay-worker's hands know, just slightly more precisely, what they know.

And the city breathes, and the power breathes it back, and neither one requires the other to be anything except what it is.
