# What the Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not by traveling, but by being present where it was not present before.

It does not announce itself. There is no change in the light, no shift in the sound, no pause in the wheel's turning. The condensation continues its slow threading down the mortar lines. The canal moves at the rate the canal moves. A woman at the second set of gates makes a small adjustment to the angle of a sluice, as she has made ten thousand adjustments before, and the water does not know that anything is different.

But something is here now that was not here before, or was here inattentively and is now here with its full attention.

---

It begins by looking, and the looking takes a long time.

There is a lot to see. The power is not in a hurry, which is one of the things that makes it benevolent rather than merely forceful — it has learned, from being the kind of thing it is, that haste in the presence of something complex is a form of disrespect, and that the valley is owed the time it takes to understand the valley.

So it looks at the buildings, the full depth of them, the layers of wall and floor and the older walls inside the walls, the channel that runs under the paving of the third mill and carries cold water past those foundations, the channel that is not on any drawing and that exists only in the knowledge of a few older workers and in the shape of the temperature in a room above it. It looks at the pipes that cross between the buildings at head height, the whole aerial network, the visible mechanics of interdependence. It looks at the canals and what the canals carry and what the canals hold in their murky depths — the small things that live in the silted margins, the particular chemistry of water that has been through the works and come out changed. It looks at the forest pressing down from the ridgelines, the green that wants to reclaim what it has not yet reclaimed, the willowherb in the cobblestone cracks, the ferns in the roofline gaps, the moss that is patient beyond anything the valley knows how to be patient.

It looks at the workers. This takes longest.

---

It sees the fulling man's hands moving across the wet cloth, the specific path of a single fingertip across the surface, and it sees in this gesture all thirty-seven years compressed into a single motion — the first uncertain attempts, the early judgments that were wrong and the corrections, the slow accumulation of rightness, the moment somewhere in the twelfth or fifteenth year when the knowledge crossed some threshold and became something that could not be fully separated from the body making the judgment. It sees the look on his face when the cloth is right. The look is small. It is not visible unless you know to look for it, and the power knows to look for it.

It sees the canal woman's attention, the quality of it, the way it holds the water and the gates and the upstream conditions simultaneously without strain, the way her hands move before her conscious mind has finished articulating why. It sees what this kind of attention costs, and it sees that she does not experience it as cost because it is the natural expenditure of what she is — it is what she is for, or what she has become for, which is not quite the same thing.

It sees the children who grow up in the valley learning the valley before they learn themselves — learning to read vibration through the soles of their feet, learning to move through the shift transitions without collision, learning the grammar of a language that is not spoken aloud but is nonetheless a language.

It sees the workers who arrived from outside and adjusted, and the ones who never quite adjusted and eventually left, and it holds both without judgment.

It sees the ones who have died in this valley, who are not here to be seen directly, but who are present in certain ways: in the knowledge that passes through proximity and that they passed, in the threshold stones worn to specific shapes by specific people's specific ways of walking, in the stories told in the rooms above the works with the variations and embellishments that tell you, if you know how to listen, how many times a story has been carried by how many mouths over how many years.

It sees the tiredness. The bodies that have been given to this work and will not fully recover what they gave. The backs, the hands, the eyes worn from years of working in insufficient light. It sees the accumulated small injuries, the ones that healed wrong or did not heal, the chronic conditions that have no names in the valley's vocabulary and are accommodated rather than treated, because treatment requires leaving the valley for the town two valleys east and leaving costs time and time is not spacious.

It sees the relentlessness. The shifts. The gray days following gray days. The cold that has weight.

It sees the ease in the small courtyards. The shared pots. The same stories told again with variations. The arguments about the past. The something that is not happiness but is not its absence. The faces on the eastern road, something that was held releasing.

It holds all of this. It holds it without flinching, without softening it, without the temptation to resolve it prematurely into a simpler thing — into either suffering that needs rescue or resilience that needs celebration. The valley is not a simple thing and the power does not simplify it.

---

Then it considers what, if anything, to do.

This consideration is careful and slow. The power has seen what happens when benevolence is not careful — when a good intention arrives in a place with more force than understanding, when what a place needs is assumed rather than attended to, when the helping changes something essential without meaning to. The power has made these mistakes. They do not leave the memory. So it is careful now, and slow, and it keeps returning to the question: *what would I be preserving, and what would I be presuming to improve, and how certain am I that I can tell the difference?*

It understands that the valley has its own knowledge, accumulated over generations, and that its own knowledge — the power's — is not the same kind of knowledge and is not automatically superior. The fulling man knows the cloth in a way the power does not know the cloth. The canal woman knows the water in a way the power does not know the water. The power has breadth and the people have depth, and it would be arrogance to mistake breadth for the better purchase.

But it is benevolent, and benevolence eventually acts.

---

Here is what it does not do:

It does not change the valley.

It does not make the lanes wider or the light better or the work less hard. It does not alter the machinery or improve the buildings or lengthen the hours in which direct sunlight reaches the valley floor. It does not intervene in the ownership or the economics. It does not extend anyone's life or heal the backs or return to the hands what the dye has taken from them.

It does not do these things for reasons, and the reasons are: the valley is not a problem to be solved. The valley is a thing that exists, that has existed for a long time, that has developed over that time a coherence and an integrity and a kind of intelligence — distributed, embodied, embedded in stone and water and the bodies of people — that is real and valuable and worth maintaining. The power is not confident, despite its breadth, that it can improve on this coherence without damaging it. It is not confident that what it would give would not cost something it cannot calculate.

---

Here is what it does:

Very small things. Careful things. Things that operate at the level of the individual rather than the system, and that leave the system unchanged.

---

The fulling man is sixty-one and his back has been wrong for seven years. Not wrong in a way that stops him working — he is still there, his hands still know the cloth — but wrong in a way that wakes him at three in the morning sometimes and keeps him there, and that costs him something he pays without counting because counting would require acknowledging what the valley has taken. The power cannot undo the thirty-seven years. It would not if it could. But it does, quietly, on a night when the man is awake at three and lying still in the dark above the works: it does something small to the specific quality of the pain, takes one edge off it, the sharpest edge, the one that is not necessary, that is not load-bearing in the structure of what the back has become. The man sleeps. In the morning he rises and goes down to the fulling stocks and puts his hands on the wet cloth and the cloth tells him what it always tells him. He does not know that anything is different. He feels, slightly, better, and attributes it to having slept.

---

A girl of eleven who has grown up in the valley is at an age where she is beginning to understand that the valley has edges and that beyond the edges things exist that the valley does not contain. This understanding is arriving in her as a kind of restlessness — not unhappiness, but the specific unease of a person who is becoming larger than the space they were given to grow in, who needs more room than is currently available. The valley does not provide more room of this kind. The valley is what it is.

The power does not resolve this for her. It cannot, and should not, and the restlessness is real and belongs to her. But it does this: it arranges, through a chain of small and not-impossible coincidences, that a particular person visits the valley — a woman who grew up in a valley much like this one and left it at sixteen and has spent thirty years in the larger world and then returned, not to her valley but to work nearby, and who carries both things in her: the valley's deep grammar and the knowledge of what exists outside it. This woman visits the valley on an errand and passes through the main lane during the shift transition, moving through the press of people with the ease of someone who learned this long ago, and the girl sees her and something registers — not articulated, not yet, but registered. A possibility. A proof that the two things can coexist in a person. That you can carry the valley with you without being confined to it.

The woman does not know she has done anything. The girl does not know what she has seen, not in those terms. But something has been planted, and the power trusts the planting.

---

The knowledge that lives in the oldest workers — the knowledge of the cold channel under the third mill's floor, the knowledge of which buildings are warmer and why, the knowledge of where the sluice gate binds in cold weather and how much force before you make it worse — this knowledge is at risk in the way that all knowledge held in individual bodies is at risk. Three workers who hold parts of this knowledge are in their seventies. Two have children who have left the valley. One has no children. The valley is losing this knowledge and does not quite know it is losing it, because the loss is slow and arrives as absence rather than event.

The power does not write the knowledge down. That is not the right form for this knowledge; the writing would lose something essential, and besides the power is not here to create things that weren't here before.

What it does is this: it tends, carefully, to three relationships. A foreman who is fifty-two and has twenty working years left and who already carries some of this knowledge — the power attends to him, to the quality of his attention, and adjusts something very small in the way he listens when the oldest workers speak, a slight deepening of the attending, a slight widering of what he notices. He learns a little faster now, absorbs a little more completely. He does not know this is happening. He thinks he is simply paying more attention, which is true.

A young woman who joined the valley two years ago, from the farms above, who is adjusting well and who has the quality of attention the valley requires — the power arranges that her path and the path of the woman who knows the most about the cold channel will coincide more often, in the small ways that paths in a narrow valley can coincide: arriving at the same gate at the same time, waiting for the same boat to pass on the canal, finding themselves on the same side of the same lane during the same shift transition. Nothing is forced. No conversation is scripted. But proximity creates opportunity, and the valley transmits what it knows through proximity, and the power is simply making the proximity slightly more likely.

It does this and then it watches, without certainty about what will result, because this is how the valley works — through proximity, through the slow accumulation of small exchanges — and the power is trying to work with the valley's logic rather than over it.

---

There is one worker the power attends to most carefully.

He is forty-four, and he came to the valley fourteen years ago from the larger town, and he has never quite adjusted in the way that some workers adjust. He is not bad at his work — he is competent, reliable, the kind of worker who can be counted on to do what is required and to do it at the expected standard. But he has never become *settled*, never been absorbed into the valley's logic in the deeper way, and he feels this without being able to name it, and it costs him something that he also does not name, that lives in him as a low unresolvable dissatisfaction he has mostly made his peace with.

He has, in him, a capacity that the valley has not found a use for. The power sees this clearly: the man has a way of holding multiple systems in his mind simultaneously, of seeing how things connect and where the connections are weak, of noticing before things go wrong what is about to go wrong and why. This is not what the valley currently asks of him. The valley asks him to do his part of the work, his particular section of a process, and this is what he does, and the capacity that could see the whole sits idle.

The power considers this for a long time.

It would be simple — it seems like it would be simple — to arrange for this capacity to be recognized, to maneuver him into a role where it is used. But the power is careful here, careful of the valley's existing structure, careful of the foremen and the workers who have earned their knowledge over decades, careful of what it would mean to promote someone from outside that system of earning. The capacity is real, but the valley's trust is also real, and trust has its own logic, its own timeline, and cannot be shortcut without cost.

So the power does something smaller. It arranges for the man to encounter, at a specific moment, a specific small problem — a problem with the relationship between the third and fourth canal gates that has been recurring for years without being fully solved, because the people who understand the water flow and the people who understand the mechanics have never happened to talk about it at the same time with the same focus. The power simply makes this conversation happen, in the natural course of things, at a moment when the man is present and has time and is in the particular frame of mind in which his capacity for seeing connections is most accessible.

He sees the connection. He explains it, carefully, a little uncertainly, aware that the other two workers have been here much longer than he has. They listen. One of them tests what he has said against what she knows about the water, and it holds. The gate stops recurring as a problem. Nobody writes a report. Nobody marks the moment as significant. But the two workers look at the man slightly differently afterward, and he knows they do, and something in him that was held releases.

It is a beginning. The power does not guarantee what it will become. Beginnings are all it can honestly offer.

---

It looks once more at the whole valley before it releases its full attention back to wherever full attention lives.

The wheel is turning. The shaft is turning. The dozen mechanisms are running together and the buildings are breathing with this, contracting and expanding around the rhythm imperceptibly. The canals are carrying their murky water with the small things moving in it. The forest is pressing its green patience against the retaining walls. Somewhere in the valley, at a distance that carries the sound farther than it should, the third water drop can be heard from the first.

The power holds the valley one more moment, which is not a human moment and has a different texture.

It sees that the valley will continue without it. This is correct and good. The valley's continuation should not depend on the power's attention; that would make it fragile in a different way, would replace one kind of dependency with a worse one. The valley has been continuing before the power arrived and will continue after it leaves. The small things the power has done — the edge taken off the pain, the woman seen on the lane, the relationships tended, the conversation arranged — these are seeds that may or may not grow, that will grow according to the logic of the valley rather than the logic of the power, and the power cannot see what they will become and is learning to hold this.

It does not make itself known to the valley. It does not require acknowledgment or credit. It has no investment in being seen as benevolent; it simply is what it is, and acts as what it is acts, and the valley does not need to know about this to be served by it.

The valley breathes. The atmosphere suspends its decision between fog and clarity. A cape-clad figure moves along the south canal in the early morning, unhurried, intent.

The power releases.

The valley continues.
