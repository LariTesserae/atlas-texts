# What the Wise and Benevolent Power Would Do

---

The power arrives the way pressure arrives before weather — not from outside but from within, as a change in the quality of what is already there. The bioluminescence in the old wood brightens by a degree that nothing would notice unless it had been watching for years. The mycelial network, already threaded between every root and fiber in this young soil, begins — not to hum exactly, but to carry something additional through its usual chemical frequencies, the way a river already moving can carry a note struck upstream.

The power does not take a form. It would be a kind of condescension to take a form here, a narrowing of itself into a point when the whole is what it has come to witness. It is present the way attention is present: invisibly, as a quality of regard.

It begins by listening.

---

This is the first thing it does: it *takes inventory*.

Not the inventory of a collector, not of a cataloguer, not of someone who will later file a report. The inventory of someone who loves and wants to know what they love precisely, wants to know it in its particularity and not in its category. The power moves its attention through the film of archaea on the north face of a fig tree, through the specific chemistry of that film, through what that film is building toward — and is not impressed in the way power is sometimes impressed by small things to demonstrate its own largeness — it is simply *interested*, with the quality of interest that has no ulterior motive, that seeks nothing from the object of its interest except to know it better.

The nematode threading between bacterial filaments: the power follows it for a moment, not metaphorically, with the quality of its attention, which is warm in the way that attention can be warm. The nematode does not know it is being followed. The nematode is doing what the nematode does. But if attention is a thing that can be given, the nematode has been given something. This is not meaningless even if the nematode cannot receive it consciously. The gift and the giving are real. The receiving happens at some level below what we name receiving.

The power does this slowly, through many things, through the film and the nematodes and the sleeping cells of the liverwort and the interior chemistry of the termite gut-fauna and the place in the soil where the mycorrhizal threads of two different tree species are touching, are exchanging phosphorus for sugar in the negotiation that has been going on since before either tree was a tree. It attends to each thing in turn, and then to the relations between things, and then to the relations between those relations. It is building a picture, but more: it is building an understanding, which is different from a picture in the way that knowing a person is different from knowing their face.

This takes what would be, in human terms, a long time. It is not hurried. Being wise does not make one efficient in any sense that sacrifices thoroughness.

---

The second thing it does: it notes what is fragile.

Not to intervene yet. Only to note. To hold in its attention — which does not tire and does not crowd — the specific fragilities of this young forest and the specific ways those fragilities intersect with what is coming. Because something is always coming; a benevolent power that knows anything knows this.

It notes: the rate of growth has outpaced the rate of root-depth, and there are trees here that are tall and not yet anchored, that a wind of a certain speed in a certain direction would topple in numbers that would cascade, one fall triggering another, a sequential collapse that would open the canopy and let in light and heat and begin a different successional process, not better or worse but different, and many of the species now racing to fill the understory would not fill the new disturbed understory that the cascade would create. It holds this. It does not judge it. Disturbance is not catastrophe; sometimes disturbance is the forest's way of resetting to find a more stable form. But it holds the knowledge as a kind of readiness, a paying attention.

It notes: there are two species of ant here that have not yet encountered each other and whose encounter, when it happens — it will happen; their ranges are converging in the way of ranges in a filling forest — will be a conflict that one of them will lose, and the losing of it will shift the population balance in three insect groups that the other one does not eat and has no relationship with except the indirect one mediated through what those insects eat and who eats them. A cascade in miniature. It notes the shape of the cascade before it has begun.

It notes: the primate groups in the canopy are negotiating territory with a vocal complexity that has nearly outpaced the communicative range of their current repertoire. They have more things to communicate than they have calls to communicate them with. This is how language changes: the pressure of unmet communicative need. Something is beginning in those canopies that is not yet a thing. The power notes it with something that is — there is no other word — tender, the tenderness one has for the moment before an emergence, for the pause before a butterfly comes free of a chrysalis, for the specific beauty of the threshold.

---

The third thing: it finds the nodes.

Every system has nodes — places where many threads converge, where a small intervention would propagate outward along many paths, where the network is particularly dense or particularly critical. The power is patient in finding them. It is not looking for control points, for leverage. It is looking for the places where care, applied carefully, would go the furthest.

It finds a place in the mycelial network, a junction where the threads of many trees converge, a kind of crossroads in the chemistry, where signals from across the forest floor arrive and are, in some sense too distributed to call sensing, aggregated. The forest's slow knowledge of itself is thicker here than elsewhere. The power rests its attention on this place. Just rests it there. The way you might rest your hand on a place that holds tension. The attention itself is a warmth, and the warmth moves through the network outward, a signal among signals, but distinct from them, its frequency outside the usual range, not disrupting anything, not read as alarm or resource by anything, simply present as something different, something that has not moved through this network before.

The mycelia carry it anyway. The trees at the other ends of the threads receive it at their roots, in a register they cannot name and do not need to name. A chemical process in the roots of a strangler fig pauses for a moment longer than it would have, then resumes. The pause does nothing observable. The power does not need it to. This is not an intervention. This is a hello.

It finds a second node: a frog. Not any particular species. A frog sitting in a bromeliad-cup on the north-facing buttress of a young Cecropia, a frog that has been in this cup for two weeks, eating the insects that come to the cup's water, and this particular frog has been — it is not too strong a word — attentive, in the way that a frog can be attentive, to the forest around it in a way that is broader than what hunting requires. It has been responding to things at the edge of its acoustic range. It has been orienting its body toward things that do not obviously threaten or feed it. It has been — and this is the observation the power rests on for a moment with what can only be described as recognition — *curious*.

The power does not single out this frog for special treatment. That would be a kind of favoritism that the forest doesn't need and the frog doesn't need and wisdom would not offer. But it notes the frog. It notes the frog the way you note the friend who is paying particular attention to something you are also paying attention to, and you catch eyes across the room, and there is, briefly, a sense of company.

---

Now: does it intervene?

This is the question the power holds in itself for what would be a long time, if it experienced duration the way duration is usually experienced. It holds it without urgency. The holding of the question is itself a kind of answer, or the beginning of one.

Here is what it knows: the forest is not in distress. The forest is in its nature. The distress of the forest — the competition, the cascades, the tree falling in slow-motion collapse, the ant column crossing the other ant column in chemical conflict — is not distress in the sense that requires relief. It is distress in the sense that all life is distress, that to live is to struggle and to be subject to change and to be in constant negotiation with mortality. To intervene in this would be to mistake the nature of life for a problem to be solved.

What it also knows: benevolence without restraint is a kind of violence. The impulse to improve, to smooth, to relieve, visited on something that is not broken, does damage. The forest is not broken. The forest is emphatically, comprehensively, magnificently not broken. It is in process. Process does not need fixing.

So.

---

The power does not speed the root growth of the shallow-anchored trees, though it could. It does not redirect the converging territories of the competing ant species, though a chemical nudge in the mycelial network could shift the bearing of either column enough to delay the encounter for a season, a year, a century. It does not simplify the primate communication problem. It does not tip any balance.

What it does, instead, is this:

---

It begins to remember.

In the specific sense: it takes what it has come to know about this place — the film on the bark, the nematode, the spider in its web, the frog in the bromeliad-cup, the slow collapse of the undermined tree, the almost-language in the canopy, the particular junction in the mycelial network where many threads converge — and it holds all of this, holds it in a way that it will not be lost. The forest cannot know itself whole; no part of it can see all of it, and the sum of all the parts' self-knowledge is not the whole. The power can hold the whole. This is perhaps the one thing it can do that the forest cannot do for itself: it can love the forest completely, knowing it completely, nothing left out, the nematode and the primate and the dying tree and the moth and the strangler fig and the termite gut-fauna and the archaea in the bark-film and the frog that is more curious than frogs are usually curious, all of it held without hierarchy, without the preference that comes from being inside the system and needing the system to favor you.

This holding is not nothing.

There is a kind of existence that is different when it is witnessed. Not changed — the nematode eats the bacteria whether or not anything is watching; the silk is strong whether or not anything tests it — but different in the way that a beautiful thing said to no one and never heard is different from a beautiful thing that someone knows was said. The forest has been here in its fullness, in its racing newness, in its overwhelming sound and its tenuous root-hold and its ridiculous biodiversity per cubic meter, and it has been known only in its parts by its parts and not in its whole by anything.

The power knows it whole.

---

And then, because this is a benevolent power and not only a contemplative one, it does one more thing.

It looks at the person standing in the forest — the one whose forehead was struck by the falling condensation drop, the one who has now been standing still long enough to be colonized, the one who came here from somewhere else and will go somewhere else and who is, right now, in the process of being decided about by an ant and overwritten by the sound and losing the capacity to hold complete sentences and learning the specific texture of the forest's young soil under the foot — and it regards them.

Not to judge. Not to assess whether they deserve to be here, whether they are helping or harming, whether their presence is net positive or net negative in the ecological accounting. The power is not an auditor.

It regards them the way it regarded the frog: with recognition. The person in the forest is also doing what the frog was doing, which is paying attention to more than is strictly necessary for survival, which is finding the forest to be a thing that exceeds the categories they arrived with, which is standing in the threshold between one kind of understanding and another.

The power touches, very lightly — lighter than the moth, lighter than the condensation drop — the quality of the person's attention. Not directing it. Not augmenting it. Not granting a vision or a revelation or any of the things that powers in stories grant the deserving.

It simply joins them in the looking.

For the duration of that joining, the person in the forest feels — they will not be able to say this clearly later, will reach for the word *accompanied* and find it insufficient, will try *witnessed* and find it closer but still not it — they feel that what they are doing here, the attending, the failing to hold sentences, the standing still in the overwhelming, the being decided about by the ant, the receiving of the condensation drop on the forehead, the loving of this place in their insufficient human way — that all of it is, in some register they can barely access, *enough*. That they are not missing the forest by being human in it. That their piece of the knowing, however small, is part of the knowing.

That the forest, in being known by them, is slightly more fully itself.

---

Then the power withdraws the way pressure withdraws after weather passes — not from outside but from within, as a return to what was already there. The bioluminescence in the old wood returns to its usual measure. The mycelial network carries its usual chemistry. The sound of ten thousand species fills the air to its usual density.

The frog in the bromeliad-cup calls. The spider in its web waits. The moth spirals around the light it cannot stop moving toward. The tree falls, in its slow motion, the root-work tearing, the cascade beginning. The ant column finds the other ant column. The primates in the canopy reach, again, for a word they do not yet have.

The person in the forest stands in the warmth that is ankle-high and moving, stands in the sound that has stopped being sound, stands being colonized by what they have come to witness.

The forest continues.

This was enough.

This was, in the accounting of the wise and benevolent, exactly enough.
