# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not by moving through space but by being, suddenly, present where it was not.

And the first thing it does is nothing.

This requires emphasis. The first thing, for a long time, is nothing. The power is wise enough to know that the temptation of power is always to act, and that acting first is almost always wrong, and so it holds itself still inside the roar and lets the roar work on it the way the roar works on everything: filling the available space, asking the inner ear to give up trying to locate itself, demanding surrender to the simple fact of it. The power lets itself be worked on. It considers this its first obligation.

It takes in the light. The brutal committed light that hits rather than diffuses. The way each wet surface becomes briefly its own source. The deep mineral blue of the stripped sky.

It takes in the stone. The packed, settled, completed quality of it. The surface that has found its permanent form and stopped there.

It takes in the water in all its simultaneous states: seeping, pooling, falling, held in the stone's interior in centuries of patient transit.

It finds the small dark thing in its labyrinth and it does not touch it. It finds the turning thing in its oval and it does not touch it. It finds the haze in the cold of the pool and it does not touch it.

For a long time, it simply witnesses. This is not passivity. Witnessing, done with full attention, is one of the most demanding things a power can do. It means resisting the pressure to interpret too quickly, to decide what the place needs before the place has had a chance to show what it is. The power has this discipline. It holds itself open and watches and listens and feels the vibration in the cartilage of the nose and behind the eyes, and it does not conclude.

---

Time passes in the way time passes here, which is without landmarks, without crescendo, without lull. The roar is the same. The light moves slightly as the sun commits to its arc, and the shadows that are not shadows shift, and new surfaces catch the light and become briefly their own sources and then return to being stone. The turning thing makes its circuit. The water in the pools holds its cold.

The power watches all of it. The power watches the way the stone at the weeping faces is depositing its mineral shelves, layer so thin it cannot be observed directly but only inferred. The power watches the old water inside the stone making its centuries-long passage toward emergence. The power watches the haze in the pool floor maintaining its cohesion through chemical gradient, generation folding into generation with no seam.

And slowly, over the long time of watching, something becomes clear.

---

This place does not need help.

This is the central finding, and it is not a disappointing finding. The power is wise enough to know that finding a place that does not need help is not a failure of the mission but a success of the witnessing. Many powers, arriving here, would have made changes. Would have looked at the bare stone and added something. Would have looked at the sparse life and added more. Would have looked at the extreme conditions — the altitude, the cold, the total thunder, the bare mineral existence with no biological richness to cushion it — and decided that the correct response was amelioration.

The wise power understands that this would be wrong.

The mistake would be to evaluate this landscape against the standard of somewhere else. Against the standard of the lush, the complex, the teeming. Against the standard of what a landscape is *supposed* to look like when it is complete and healthy and full. By those standards, this place is impoverished, stripped, barely alive, a stage not yet set, a potential not yet realized.

But those are not the right standards. This place is not a stage awaiting its scene. It is not a potential awaiting its realization. It is a finished thing. The packed stone, the patient water, the roar that has always been this loud and will always be this loud, the small dark thing in the labyrinth that has been here long enough to lose its connection to any known family, the turning thing that communicates in flight because the roar has taken everything else — these are not placeholders for a richer community to come. They are the community. They are what this specific pressure, this specific altitude, this specific mineral chemistry, this specific water budget, this specific permanence has made, over the specific time it has taken. This is the life that fits here, and it fits here exactly, and it wants for nothing that this place can provide and does not provide.

To add anything would be to miss the point. Badly. Irreversibly.

---

So the power considers more carefully. Not *what to add* but *what is actually being asked for.*

And the power realizes, in this considering, that the question "what would you do?" rests on a hidden assumption: that something needs doing. That beneficial power expresses itself in intervention. That wisdom operates by finding the gap and filling it, the wrong and righting it, the lack and supplying it.

The power turns this assumption over. Examines it against what the plateau has shown. Against the water's patience. Against the stone's completeness. Against the small dark thing's entire life conducted without reference to any concept of lack.

The assumption is wrong, or at least partial. Beneficial power can also express itself in *recognition.* In the decision to see clearly and then *protect the conditions of that seeing from the pressures that would change it.*

This is different from doing nothing. This is active. It requires the same attention, the same rigor, the same sustained effort as any intervention. But its direction is different: not toward the place but away from it, oriented outward, toward whatever might arrive from outside and not understand what it has found.

---

So here is what the power does.

It does not change the stone. It does not seed the bare surface with anything. It does not adjust the mineral chemistry of the pools or alter the water budget or modulate the roar to make the place more hospitable to any definition of hospitality the place itself would not recognize.

What it does is this:

It learns the place completely. With all the patience the place itself embodies, it maps every weeping face and every crack and every pool and every thread of cascade. It learns the labyrinth the small dark thing inhabits — the whole three-dimensional network, every passage, every chamber, every thin film of water along every angled wall. It learns the oval the turning thing holds, the exact height, the exact angle at which the thermal shifts and requires a correction, the specific regions of the surface below where movement is more probable and less. It learns the chemical gradient at the floor of each pool, the population density of the haze, the way generations fold into generations without a seam.

It learns the sound. Every component thread of the total roar, traced back to its source cascade, mapped to its position at the plateau's edge, its harmonic contribution to the whole calculated and held. The sonic geography of this place — the way the mix of harmonics changes as you move across the surface, the interference patterns that are a direct record of the stone's exact geometry — it learns this the way you learn a face: not as data but as recognition.

And then it holds all of this. Not as a record. As a living attention, sustained, ongoing, the way the water in the stone is sustained — without urgency but without cessation. The power makes of itself something like the place: patient, present, doing what it does at the rate the situation requires.

This is the protection. Not a barrier. Not a prohibition. Not a mark of ownership or an exclusion zone or anything that would require enforcement against a force that had decided to test it. Something subtler: a comprehensive knowing of this place, held actively, that means any change — any change at all, any subtraction from or addition to this specific balanced completeness — would be noticed immediately. Would register as clearly as a sudden silence in the roar would register, a wrongness detectable by any attention fully calibrated to what is right.

The power becomes, in this way, this place's memory of itself. A witness that does not forget. A knowing that persists alongside the place without disturbing it, without requiring anything from it, without asking the stone or the water or the small dark thing or the turning thing to know that they are known.

---

But then the power asks itself a harder question.

*Is even this too much?*

Is the act of knowing this place, of making oneself its guardian, already an imposition? Already a claim, however benevolent, that does not belong here? The stone has not asked to be known. The turning thing has not asked to be protected. The haze in the pool floor has never needed a witness to persist through its countless uncounted generations. Everything here has been conducting its long patient existence without any external recognition and would continue to do so — would continue to do so exactly as it does now — without the power's attention.

The power holds this question. Does not dismiss it. The question deserves to be held.

And finds, at the end of holding it, a distinction that seems real: there is a difference between *requiring a place to be other than it is* and *attending to a place as it is.* Attention paid without demand is not an imposition. It is the thing attention was made for. The stone does not know it is being watched and is not changed by being watched. The turning thing makes its circuit and the circuit is its own. The haze persists in its chemical interface for its own reasons, which are not reasons at all but simply the conditions that sustain it, and the power's witness adds nothing to those conditions and subtracts nothing.

What the power does by attending is not something done *to* the place. It is something done *with* it — a joining that does not alter the joined, a presence that takes the form of the thing it is present to, stone-patient and water-persistent and quiet inside the roar.

---

There is one more thing.

Small. Almost nothing. But the power does it because it is wise, and wisdom sometimes acts in small ways, and the act is so precisely sized to its occasion that it cannot be called interference any more than the turning thing's shadow passing across the stone can be called interference.

At the lip of one pool — a pool near the plateau's western edge, where the cascade threads are thickest and the roar is marginally louder, where the light comes at an angle in the late afternoon that makes the water surface into a kind of burnished silver — the power looks at the reflection in the pool for a long time.

And what it puts there is not a thing. It is a quality. A slight increase in the resolution with which this pool reflects the mineral blue above it. Not visible to any eye that would come and look. Not measurable by any instrument. Something that operates at the level of the water's relationship with its own capacity to be clear, which is already very high and becomes, by this faint increment, very slightly higher.

The water does not know this has happened. The haze at the pool floor is not affected. The stone around the pool continues its slow deposition of mineral shelves. The turning thing, if it looked down, would see the same silver mirror it has always seen.

But the power does it because in the long run of this place's existence — in the geological time during which everything here will eventually change, the water budget shifting, the stone wearing differently, the small dark thing's lineage diverging into forms not yet possible — in that long run, this pool will have been, for one moment, slightly more itself than it would otherwise have been.

And the power finds that it cannot simply witness without offering something. Not because something was lacking. But because love, which is what benevolence is in its most refined form, is not simply the recognition of a thing's completeness. It is the *affirmation* of it. The look that says: *I see what you are. I find it, unreservedly, enough.* 

The increment of clarity in the pool is that look, materialized. It is the power doing the only thing that does not impose: it is the power saying *yes* to the place as it is, in the only language that does not require the place to understand language.

---

Then the power does something that is perhaps its most important act.

It leaves.

Not because it has finished — attention to a place like this is never finished — but because it understands that its presence, however quiet, is still a presence, and presences have weights. It will continue to attend from wherever attention attends from when it has withdrawn its visible form. It will continue to know the labyrinth and the oval and the gradient and the roar. It will notice if something changes that should not change, and it will ask itself again — carefully, without rushing to act — whether the change is one that requires response.

But the plateau itself does not need to hold its breath while it is watched. It should be allowed to simply be, in its completeness, in its permanent roar, in its balance that is not a balance because balance implies two things pressing against each other and this is a single thing occupying fully what it occupies.

The power withdraws.

The pool reflects the mineral blue.

The stone weeps at its faces.

The small dark thing crosses from one crack to the next with the same committed speed it has always used.

The turning thing makes its circuit.

The haze at the pool floor maintains its cohesion through chemical gradient, generation folding into generation without a seam.

The roar continues from no particular direction and every direction, vibrating in the cartilage and behind the eyes, a sound you are inside of, the ambient condition of this place, unchanged.

It was always this loud.

It will always be this loud.

The plateau does not notice that anything visited it.

This is exactly right.
