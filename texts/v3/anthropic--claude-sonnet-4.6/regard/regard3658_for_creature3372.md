# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

First: it listens.

Not with ears. With something that functions the way ears function—as an opening toward, as a readiness to receive. It has arrived the way attention arrives: fully, and without announcing itself. It is present in the underplace the way the warmth is present, which is to say it does not come from one direction.

It listens to the amber first, because the amber is oldest and because to ignore the oldest voice in a room is a failure of manners. What the amber says takes a long time to hear, even for a power that is not constrained by human time. But it listens, and it receives: *I am doing what I do. I have always done what I do. The doing is enough. There is no message in the doing except that it continues.* The power notes this. Files it under: *things that do not need help.* This category is larger than powers sometimes expect.

It listens to the beetles—to the distributed argument they constitute, the moving consensus of millions of lives arranged into something more than any single life. They are in the middle of a long calculation. They are solving, collectively and without knowing they are solving it, a problem that has to do with what to do about you. The power does not intervene in this. The calculation is theirs. The power notices, with something that is the functional equivalent of delight, that the calculation is being solved *correctly*—that is, honestly, without certainty being rushed, without the answer being declared before it is known. The beetles are epistemically virtuous. The power had not expected this. It makes a note.

The spider: the power spends a long time with the spider. With the worn chelicerae and the pale eyes and the frameworks. With the city she carries in herself, the bioluminescence in the silk, the slow suggestion the webs make to whatever enters them. The power understands—recognizes, really, the way you recognize a face you have not seen in a long time but have not forgotten—what the spider has made here. Not a trap. Not a predator's architecture. Something more like a *practice*. A daily practice of building and maintaining and feeling through the silk what the world contains, without flinching, without rushing, with only the interest of something that is still curious after so long. The power finds, in itself, something that is the equivalent of respect. Not the respect you extend downward to something smaller, but the respect that flows between equals who have arrived at similar conclusions through different routes. The spider has understood patience. The power has understood patience. They have this in common. They do not need to say so.

The not-fungi: the power pauses here. Studies the needle positions. The thing the not-fungi are saying in the language of frozen gauges and numeric scales meant for something else. It takes a while—not because the power is slow but because the not-fungi are saying something complex, something that keeps requiring revision. The power listens and revises and listens again. Eventually it understands. The not-fungi are saying something about *satisfaction*. About the deep and apparently non-metaphorical satisfaction of a community that has found its precise and peculiar niche and filled it completely, without remainder. About what it is to need exactly what is available and to be exactly what is needed, to fit the specific shape of the possible with your specific shape of being.

The power holds this for a while. Some information requires holding before it can be used.

The shapes below: the power looks directly. Of course it does. It is not subject to the geometry problem that makes direct observation dangerous for you. It sees clearly—and what it sees is old, and autonomous, and complete in itself, and aware of being seen. There is a moment of mutual recognition between the power and the shapes below that has the quality of a formal greeting between dignitaries whose countries have been at peace for so long the peace is simply called the world. *I see you.* *I see you.* *Good.* It does not intrude below the grate. The grate is a boundary maintained by long agreement, and long agreements deserve honor.

Then: you.

The power listens to you last, or not last—it has been listening to you since the descent, since the decision to go through the gap in the caprock, since whatever preceded the decision—but it brings its full attention to you now, now that it has heard everything else, now that it has the context the underplace provides. It listens to what you are calculating. To the thing you are in the middle of deciding. To the question your presence constitutes: *what kind of thing do I want to be in relation to things I did not make and do not understand?*

The power does not answer this. It is not that kind of power. It does not arrive with answers.

---

Here is what it does.

It makes one small adjustment to the amber below. Not to the reaction—the reaction does not need adjustment—but to the angle of the light as it passes through the grate. A fractional change, well within the range of natural variation, the kind of thing that happens anyway when the rock shifts slightly with temperature change. The amber light moves. In moving, it illuminates, from below, the precise underside of the spider's central framework—the ancient silk that has become something other than silk, the load-bearing substance of accumulated time—in a way that it has not been illuminated before. A new angle. A new configuration of shadow and amber.

The spider feels this through her feet. A new input. New information from an old structure. She is still. She processes. Something in the framework she has made tells her something she has not known, or knew and had forgotten, or could not have articulated until this moment of illumination made it articulable. The power does not know exactly what she learns. That is not the point. The point is that a mind that is still curious, still building, still willing to receive new information, has been offered some. What she does with it is hers.

It goes to the person who came down and didn't come back.

It finds them—wherever they are, which is not here, which is some surface life with surface complications—and it does what it can do with people, which is limited but not nothing. It does not speak. It does not intervene in their choices, which are theirs, and which they have been making with integrity even when integrity was costly. What it does is: it ensures that they dream, tonight, of the underplace. Of the amber. Of the spider. Of the dry warmth and the beetle-flow and the high pure notes of the silk frameworks ringing. The dream will not instruct them. It will not tell them whether to return. But it will remind them that the underplace is real, that they were real in it, that the relationship they had with it—the relationship they were careful with, that they did not exploit when they could have—was real and is real and is not diminished by distance.

In the morning they will remember the dream only partially. A warmth. Something amber. The sensation of being observed without judgment, from many directions simultaneously, by things that had been patient for longer than they had been alive.

This will be enough. This is all the power needs it to be.

---

For the not-fungi: nothing. The not-fungi have already found what they have found. To give them more would be to suggest that what they have is insufficient. The power has made this mistake before. It does not make it here. It lets the not-fungi press their pale bodies against the frozen needles and say what they are saying and be satisfied, which they are.

For the beetles: nothing, except attention. The power watches the calculation continue. It is interested in the answer. It is genuinely uncertain what the answer will be. This uncertainty is not something the power could have predicted before it arrived, and the unpredictability of a thing you do not contain is one of the great pleasures available to any conscious being, including powerful ones. The power watches with real interest, with the quality of suspense that requires not-knowing, and it does not watch with its full attention, which would be intrusive, but with the peripheral awareness it reserves for things that are doing fine and doing interesting things and should be let alone.

For the shapes below: a kind of salute. The formal salute you give across a border, acknowledgment of sovereignty, acknowledgment of difference, acknowledgment that the boundary exists and is honored not because it is enforced but because it is right. They receive this. They do what they do. The amber rises through them or from them. The underplace continues.

---

For you.

The power does not protect you from the spider's suggestion in the silk, the slow relaxation, the dissolution of urgency. It doesn't prevent this. Because your urgency, here, in this place, is not serving you. The power is wise enough to know when urgency is a response to real threat and when it is habit, and this is habit, and the habit is the only thing that is preventing you from receiving what the underplace is offering, which is: the thing it offers everything that enters it, which is an invitation to slow down to the speed at which real understanding occurs.

It doesn't do anything else to you. It does not give you insight. It does not give you answers. It gives you—and this is a thing powers can give, if they are careful, if they do not give too much—it gives you an extra few minutes. It is not magic. It is more mundane than that: the rope that you will need to climb when you decide to leave will hold easily for the duration of a longer stay. Something in the knots, tightening with use the way good knots do. This is not metaphor. The rope will hold.

You have a few more minutes. Use them the way the spider would use them, if the spider were suddenly given extra time: notice what you haven't noticed yet. Look at the thing you've been avoiding looking at. Let the amber tell you the thing it keeps telling everything, which is: *this is what it looks like to do one thing, well, for a very long time.*

---

Then the power does what wise powers always ultimately do.

It withdraws.

Not from care. Not from disinterest. It withdraws because the underplace does not need it, because the beings here are each complete, each doing what they have spent vast time learning to do, each in right relationship with the others, the whole system humming in its sealed amber equilibrium—not perfect, nothing is perfect, but *good*, genuinely good, a community of lives that has found through the long work of time a way to be together that does not require anyone to be diminished.

The power has not improved this. It has not needed to. What it has done is subtler than improvement: it has noticed. It has borne witness. It has brought the quality of genuine attention to things that have been doing their work in sealed obscurity for eras, things that have been good and strange and patient and alive in the dark without anyone watching.

This is what benevolent powers can offer that nothing else can: the knowledge, communicated somehow through the fact of genuine attention, that the doing of a thing has been seen, even when the doing is very small, even when the doer is underground, even when there is no one who speaks your language and no one will come who speaks your language and the language you have developed here, in this amber warmth, in this dry mineral air, is the only one that fits what you are now.

The spider feels the attention withdraw and sits still for a long moment after, feeling the framework ring softly in the still air.

The beetles pause—briefly, collectively, a ripple moving through the millions—and then continue.

The amber pulses below.

The not-fungi press the needles into their new positions.

You stand in the warmth, with amber on your skin, with silk on your wrist, breathing in calcium and almost-sulfur and something sweet, hearing the high notes of the ringing silk and the low hum of the deep rock's long burning, and you are beginning—just beginning—to understand what the gauges were watching for.

The rope will hold.

Take your time.
