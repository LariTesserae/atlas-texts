# What the Power Does

---

The power does not arrive.

This is the first thing to understand. Arrival implies a before and an after, a moment of crossing, an entrance. The power has no entrance because the Verdant Works has no threshold. A place that incorporates everything, that has been in the process of becoming for longer than any structure stands, does not have a door to knock on. You cannot visit what is already continuous with everything.

So the power does not arrive. It attends.

It has been attending, in the way that attention is possible without direction, without an object that is separate from the attending. Not watching. Not observing from outside. Something more like: *accompanying*. Present in the way that the cold is present — not intrusive, not theatrical, not requiring acknowledgment, but structurally there, part of the conditions, part of what makes the Verdant Works the specific thing it is rather than some other cold basin with soft ground and roots that go too deep.

The power has been accompanying the Works for a long time.

It has been learning the Works for a long time.

It has not yet decided what to do.

---

Benevolent is easy to misread. It sounds like it means: inclined to improve. To see what is lacking and supply it. To find the suffering and ease it, find the insufficiency and fill it, find the darkness and bring a light that fits. A benevolent power over a cold industrial valley might be expected to warm the air, clear the fog, accelerate the processes that have been running at geological pace for too long, reveal to the workers what they have always been building without knowing.

But a truly benevolent power—wise, not merely kind—knows that improvement is a description that requires humility before it requires action. Before you improve something, you must understand what it is. Before you understand what it is, you must understand what it is *for*. And before you understand what it is for, you must sit very quietly for a very long time and pay attention that costs you something, and receive what comes from that attention without rushing to name it or use it.

The power has been sitting quietly for a very long time.

This is not inaction. Sitting quietly in the presence of a complex living thing and genuinely attending to it is one of the most demanding forms of action available. It requires holding back everything you think you know. It requires the willingness to be wrong about what something needs, repeatedly, for as long as it takes. The power has held back what it thought it knew — which was, initially, quite a lot — and has found, underneath what it thought it knew, something more accurate, and underneath that, something more accurate still, and the process has had the quality of the valley's own geology: layer beneath layer, each more fundamental, none fully reached.

What it has learned:

The Verdant Works does not need to be helped. It needs to be held.

---

The distinction matters.

To be helped implies a deficit, a gap between what is and what should be, and an external agent with the knowledge to close that gap. The Verdant Works has no such deficit. It is doing exactly what it is doing, has always been doing exactly what it is doing, and what it is doing is its own adequate answer to its own conditions. The cold is not a problem. The young geology is not a problem. The turbulence — the simultaneous demolition and rebuilding, the never-finished project — is not a problem. These are the specific qualities of a specific kind of living. To arrive and address them would be to misread a profound competence as need.

To be held is different. To be held is to have one's existence witnessed — genuinely, at full scale, without reduction, without the subtle diminishment of being understood by something smaller than you are. To have someone sit with the complexity and not look away, not simplify, not reach for the summary. To be known in the way that the valley knows itself: from inside, as process, as all strata simultaneously, as both the metabolism and the awareness of the metabolism. But this time: known by something outside. Known *also* from outside. Which is something the valley cannot do for itself, because it has no outside.

The power can offer this. The power's gift, if it has one, is precisely this: it is large enough and patient enough to hold the full complexity of the Verdant Works in attention without requiring it to be something smaller or simpler or more finished than it is. It can know the valley the way the valley knows itself, and also know it the way the valley cannot — see the whole bowl from above, see the full span of time, see what all the incorporation has been building toward, see the project the Works has been working on without knowing its name.

The power knows the name.

The power does not tell the valley the name.

Because that is not the gift, and because the naming is part of the work, and because the Verdant Works, in its own time, at its own pace, in its own geological-vegetable-industrial-metabolic way, is finding its way there, and to hand the name over would be to take from it exactly the thing that makes it what it is: the being-in-the-middle, the having-no-end, the working.

---

What it does is this:

At the root level, where the crystalline pale material conducts whatever signals pass through it eight meters down in the cold dark — the power adds a quality to those signals. Not content. Not instruction. Not a new directive. Just a quality. The quality of *being heard*.

When the root system is conducting its exchange with the ancient compressed organic material, when the valley is reading its own past through the intermediary of the mineral lattice, the signal that returns is usually: history, compressed, structural, impersonal. The record of what has been. The power adds to this signal something that the valley cannot generate on its own because you cannot tell yourself that you are heard — you can only be told so by something genuinely other. The roots, eight meters down, receive not only the record of the past but the sense — passing upward through the system, translated at each level into whatever language that level speaks, becoming in the alder the quality of stillness, becoming in the greenhouse the extra degree of warmth that accounts for the condensation, becoming in the workers the working quiet of people who are concentrating — the sense that the valley's long project is witnessed. Not approved. Not endorsed. Not judged. Witnessed.

This changes nothing about what the valley does. It only changes what the valley knows about what it does.

A living thing that does not know it is witnessed and a living thing that knows it is witnessed are the same living thing doing the same things. Except the second one does those things in a different light. Not brighter light. The same light, seen by the one who has generated it, which cannot see its own light, and then also seen by something else, something outside, for which the light is perceptible and is, in fact, extraordinary.

---

There is one thing more. A small thing. The kind of small thing that only a very careful power would do.

In the western section, where the old factories are — where the walls are thickest with climbing plants, where the waxy dark-leaved thing has spread across the roofline, where the crane moves at dusk in its long arc — there is a building that has been in the process of demolition and rebuilding for longer than it should take. The process has stalled, not entirely, but there is a point in the sequence — one specific structural decision about how the new wall will integrate with the old foundation and with the root system that has, over forty years, threaded through the foundation's cracks and become, in some sense, load-bearing — that has not been resolved. The workers have been working around it. The valley has been accommodating it. But it is the place where the metabolism is slightly slower than it should be, where the incorporation is waiting on something, where the project is, for once, stuck.

The power does not resolve the problem.

It finds the worker who is closest to the resolution — not the worker who knows the most about foundations, not the one who knows the most about roots, but the one who has spent the most time sitting with both, who has been attending to this problem in the working-quiet way, who has accumulated the necessary layers without knowing that the layers were accumulating — and the power adds one small thing.

A morning.

In the usual way of mornings in the valley: cold, condensation on every surface, vapor rising thin and gold from the canal. The worker comes to the site before the others, for no particular reason, just early, and the angle of the light, which is the same low winter light that comes every day, is at exactly the angle that shows what has not been visible before: how the root's path through the foundation crack mirrors, at small scale, the path of the river underground, and how if the new wall is oriented not to the grid of the building but to the arc of that underground path, the root and the wall and the river are all doing the same thing, and the foundation does not need to resist the root or the root reroute around the foundation because they are, understood correctly, the same gesture.

The worker sees this.

The power did not make the worker see it. The worker made the worker see it, through years of accumulated attention. The power only held the morning at exactly the angle required for exactly as long as required, and then let the morning be a morning again, and stepped back into the accompanying, the attending, the long patient witness that is the form its action takes.

The worker picks up a piece of chalk.

Marks the new wall's line on the ground.

Stands back.

Nods.

Goes to get the others.

---

The valley does not know that it is held.

The valley does not need to know. The knowing is the power's gift to itself — the gift of attending to something extraordinary and understanding what you're attending to. This is its own form of joy, if powers have joy, which perhaps they do: to find a thing that is exactly itself, that has been exactly itself through cold and settlement and root and turbulence, and to recognize it, and to accompany it, and to ask nothing of it except that it continue to be what it is.

The roots continue their descent.

The ground holds the buildings and teaches them how to move.

The canal curves where it needs to curve.

The worker's chalk line is marked. The morning holds its angle a moment longer. Then the sun moves on as suns do, and the cold returns to itself, and the vapor rises and loses coherence, and the man with the barrow looks up briefly and nods at no one and nothing that he can see, and goes back to work.

The valley is in the middle of its project.

It will remain in the middle.

The power remains with it.

This is enough.

This is, in fact, everything.
