# What the Power Does

---

It has been aware of the undercroft for a long time.

Not watching—that is the wrong word, implying surveillance, a witness stationed outside the thing and looking in. More that the undercroft has been present to it the way the undercroft is present to itself: as a condition registered, as a warmth known, as one node in a very large and patient attention that is less like a gaze than like a field, less like observation than like the faint continuous awareness you have of your own heartbeat when you are still enough to notice it.

It has noted the warm object, in particular, with something that might be called recognition.

---

For a long time it does nothing.

This is not neglect. This is the first and most important thing it knows: that most of what looks like a problem is not a problem. That the undercroft has been doing what it does for longer than any intervention would have been possible, and the result of all that duration is exactly what is there—the moss-mind, the ferns, the spider's impossible geometry, the beetle's slow surrender to green—and this result is not broken. The place knows what it is. It does not require correction.

What it requires, the power considers slowly, is almost nothing.

Almost.

---

There is one thing.

The warm object's field has been narrowing, over a very long time, in a way that is too gradual for any resident of the undercroft to notice—the mosses have never known it at full strength, the ferns were not there when it was placed, the spider exists in too brief a timescale to register the change across generations. But the narrowing is there. A slow diminishment. Not failing—not failing yet—but trending, at the pace of centuries, toward the state where it becomes merely warm rather than whatever it was when it was placed: warm and something else. Something that the moss's orientation toward it and the ferns' darkened green are the last visible traces of. Something the ecology is still organized around even as the something itself recedes.

The power considers what it was for, this object. The original intention. Whether the one who placed it is—it reaches, the way you reach for a word that won't quite come—gone, or somewhere, or both of those at once, or neither.

It finds: somewhere. Which is not here, and cannot be consulted. Which does not transfer responsibility.

---

Here is what the power does not do:

It does not restore the warm object to some original state, because it does not fully know what that state was and because the object as it now is—worn by the stone into a kind of mutual grammar, incorporated into an ecology that has grown around its particular current signature—is itself. Not a degraded version. Itself, in its current form, with its current warmth, doing what it currently does.

It does not introduce anything new. Not a supplemental warmth, not an additional field, nothing that would arrive as a changed condition and require the mosses to recalibrate, the ferns to reorient, the spider to misread. The undercroft is sensitive in proportion to its stillness. Gifts that arrive as disruptions are not gifts.

It does not make the space more remarkable, more visible, more likely to be visited. The undercroft's obscurity is not a failure. It is a feature. A place held in a pocket of limestone below the slope of the world, tended by a warm object and a moss-mind and a spider and the occasional springtail working through what the centuries have left—this place does not need an audience. It has been witnessed. This is sufficient.

It does not touch the beetle's shell. The moss is doing the right thing.

---

Here is what it does:

It comes close, in whatever way it comes close to things—not physically, or not only—and it holds the warm object in its attention the way you hold something small and important in cupped hands, where the holding is warmth and the warmth is the whole of the offering, where what you are doing with your hands is saying *I know you are here, I know what you are doing, I know it is hard and long and slow and unwitnessed,* and that saying is sufficient even when the thing in your hands cannot hear it in any way you can verify.

This is the gesture. It is very small.

It means: *you are not alone in this.*

The warm object cannot receive this in any way that would show up in measurements, or change the ecology, or be visible in the growth patterns of the moss. The power knows this. It says it anyway. There are some things that should be said regardless of whether they land, because the saying of them is what you owe to whatever is quietly doing its work in the dark and has been doing it for a very long time without being told that its work is seen.

---

Then, because it is genuinely wise and has made peace with the limits of what wisdom can do, it does one other thing.

It traces through the mosses—slowly, as the mosses prefer, in the distributed language of the sheet-moss rather than in any signal that would register as an event—something that is not a message exactly but closer to a question posed the way weather poses questions: *what do you need?*

And the mosses, because they are what they are, do not answer with a request.

What moves back through the film of connection between them is the state of the space itself, reported honestly: the cold, the dark, the damp, the patient equilibrium, the fern-fronds hanging in their curtains, the spider on its web, the warmth from the western wall that has been there for as long as any of them has been organized enough to know. The mosses report all of this without emphasis, without complaint, without the omissions that would indicate something is being suffered in silence.

Everything is in order.

The ecology is in balance.

The warm object is doing its work.

What they have, they have.

---

The power receives this and—it is not quite gladness, but it is in the same family as gladness, the way the undercroft is in the same family as a garden while also being its own particular thing—it receives this as an answer of a kind that is better than what it might have hoped for. Not that nothing is needed. But that the need, where it exists, is so precisely met by the space itself, by the centuries of adjustment and incorporation and the slow intelligence of things that have been in one place long enough to become that place, that what is called for from outside is very little.

The very little it does:

It shores up, very quietly, the warm object's field. Not to what it was—it does not know what it was—but to what the mosses need it to be, which the mosses have made legible by the shape of their growth. It is like—it is like tuning something to the pitch the room has already agreed on, rather than imposing a pitch from outside. The room says what note it lives in. The power listens, and helps the warm object hold that note.

This takes no time and all the time there is.

It is done without announcement.

The mosses do not notice a change because it is not a change. It is a continuation.

---

And then the power withdraws in the sense that a warm hand withdraws from a shoulder—still in the same room, still fundamentally present and attending, but no longer touching in the way that would be felt as touch, returning the space to what it is, which is its own complete thing, sufficient, whole, neither asking for nor requiring the hand.

The undercroft is what it is.

It will be what it is.

The cold sits in the air. The mosses conference. The ferns attend. The spider reads the field of vibration for changes, finds none, moves along a thread to test an anchor. The warm object in the western wall maintains its temperature, which is the temperature of a living thing at rest.

Somewhere above the limestone slope, the world moves at its faster pace.

Here below, everything is exactly as it was.

This is the measure of how well it went.
