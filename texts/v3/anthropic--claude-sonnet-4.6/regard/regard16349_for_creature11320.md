# What the Power Does

---

It arrives — if *arrives* is the word for something that does not travel — the way attention arrives. The way you become aware, in the middle of doing something else, that you have been hearing a sound for some time without knowing it.

It is not a presence that the underloft would register as intrusion. This is the first and most important thing about how it comes. The root-ceiling does not creak unusually. The drops continue their patient falling. The isopod on its column pauses, continues, pauses — its legs working in the cold, deliberate way of something new to cold — and does not pause longer than usual. Nothing in the underloft behaves as though something has changed, because at the level of the underloft, nothing has. The power is not *in* the space. It is attending to the space, which is a different kind of presence, one the underloft is already accustomed to. Being attended to is something this place knows.

What the power perceives — what it has been perceiving, and what drew the attention that is now here — is the bone.

The model of itself. The small worked thing resting on the flat stone with its carved ceiling facing up. This was what the power noticed first, before the people, before the marks. Not because it was the most significant thing here but because it recognized the gesture: the gesture of someone making a small version of a thing too large to hold, placing it inside the thing itself, and leaving it there without signing the act with any expectation of witness. That gesture is very old. It exists in many places and in many forms, in various materials, and it belongs to a category of human behavior that the power has come to understand as something like prayer except that it does not require a recipient and is therefore, paradoxically, closer to what prayer is trying to do than most prayer is.

The person who left the bone did not leave it *for* anything. They left it because the impulse was uncontrollable, as the description knows. The power finds this — correct is not the right word. *True* is closer. The bone is true in the way that the underloft itself is true, meaning it does not pretend to be smaller or more manageable than it is.

---

The power is benevolent. This is a precise quality in this case, which means it must be explained.

It does not wish the underloft to be otherwise. It does not look at the cold and the emptiness and the turbulence and the sparse fauna and want to fill them. It does not look at the people and want them to be more comfortable or more numerous or more permanent. It does not see a problem.

What it sees is a process, still in its early stages, in which a place is becoming itself and a people are becoming the people who understand that place and the place is, in some fashion that does not require mysticism to observe, becoming something that incorporates their attention. The marks on the stone are part of the column now. The column grows without reference to the marks, yes, but the marks change what the column is in a world that contains people who read marks on columns, and the underloft is that kind of world, because the people made it so by descending into it with their stone tools and their careful attention and their bone models of themselves being in it.

This is not a world that needs intervention.

The power knows this and does not intervene.

What it does instead is this:

---

It stays.

It remains in attendance. Not always — the power has other attentions, other places, and does not pretend to a constancy it does not have. But it returns, the way the roamers return, in a rhythm that is not precisely seasonal but is a rhythm nonetheless. It returns and it pays attention and it does not do anything that changes what it finds.

In doing this, it does exactly one concrete thing, which is not an action in the world but a kind of registration: it holds the record.

Not the marks on the stone — those hold themselves, in a place that does not weather. But the record of things that have no physical trace. The long-occupation people, the Pale Ones, whose bodies are not in the underloft: the power knows where they are. It knows which ones chose and which ones could not return and which one — one, a woman, in the third generation of long-occupation, who was the one who extended the unreadable marks furthest and longest — went up finally, after eleven years below, and stood on the surface and could not recognize the light, the directional, violent, shadow-casting surface light, and did not last long above, and came back down and anchored. The power knows her. It carries her in the way that deep cold carries the record of what has been preserved in it. Not to do anything with. Simply so that the knowing exists somewhere, for someone. Even if only for the power itself, which is audience enough.

The signatures on the columns: the power reads them. Not translating — it does not decode the unreadable marks, does not have some superior access to their meaning. But it reads them in the way you read music you cannot play, attending to the structure, the intention, the places where the person making the marks pushed the tool with more force, the places where the sequence changed direction, the places — there are several, across several sites — where the marks stop for what the root-growth record suggests was a period of weeks or months, and then resume, slightly changed in character, as though whatever the break was, something was decided during it.

It watches the children. This is the thing it does with the most care and the least interference. The child pressing their palm to the stone near the shaft mouth and tracing it — the power was present for this, for three of the five instances that have occurred in the accessible history of the underloft. It did not make the moment easier or softer or more legible to the child. The child was overwhelmed and then found it okay without assistance. What the power did was simply witness, with the quality of attention that changes what it is to have done something without changing what was done. The children pressed their palms to the stone and left their outlines and the power watched and the outlines remained and now mean something that they might not have meant if no one non-stone had attended.

This is perhaps the closest the power comes to intervention: the act of witnessing, which is not neutral, but which costs nothing and takes nothing and adds only the fact of having been seen.

---

There is one thing more.

It is a small thing. It pertains to the isopod.

The pale creature on the root-column, cream-white, legs working with cold deliberateness, three centimeters since you last looked: it is, as noted, new to the underloft. Recently descended from surface ancestors. In early stages of becoming something adapted to this place. Its eyes are already slightly enlarged. Its coloration is already trending pale. Its cold-tolerance is, compared to its surface relatives, measurably improved, though it still knows to find the relatively warmer surfaces of the roots.

The power, attending to the underloft over its long returns, has watched this species across several generations. It has noticed — not with alarm, because it does not experience ecological succession as alarm — that the species is at a particular bottleneck, a moment where a very small genetic difference in the next generation or two will determine whether the lineage adapts successfully to full underloft conditions or remains marginal, always slightly too warm-blooded for the floor, always retreating to the ceiling-zone where conditions are easier.

The power does not tip the scales. It does not improve the isopod's odds, does not add heat-tolerance or cold-tolerance or better eyes or any of the things that would help. Evolution does not require assistance; it requires time, which the power can provide only by not shortening it.

But there is a single isopod — the one that you watched, the one that paused and continued and paused — that is carrying, by pure chance, a combination of traits that will matter. This particular animal, if it reproduces in the next season, will pass forward something useful. Not dramatically useful; nothing in the underloft happens dramatically. Quietly useful. The kind of useful that compounds over fifteen generations into a population that is genuinely, no-longer-marginally at home in the cold of the floor.

The power knows this because it has attended long enough and carefully enough to see the shape of what is happening, the direction of the slow trajectory, the place where the bottleneck is narrowest. It has this knowledge, accumulated through returns and attention, through the work of staying interested in a place across the timescale that the place operates on.

And so what it does — the one concrete smallest possible act it performs in the underloft — is this:

It stays a little longer today.

In attending to the space, in being present to the cold and the drops and the columns and the bright wrong ceiling, it remains in a state of attention that has no physical effect on anything but that is nonetheless a form of being-with, a form of company offered to a space that has very little of it, and the isopod that is carrying the useful traits moves across the root-column in the even light, finds the slightly warmer section of root near a growth-node, stops there.

The power attends.

The isopod, for reasons that are fully explicable by thermal gradients and the cost-benefit of metabolic expenditure in cold, stays where it is a little longer than it might have.

This is sufficient.

---

What the power does not do is extensive and important.

It does not make the underloft warmer. It does not make the descent easier. It does not translate the unreadable marks for the roamers or restore them to legibility through any other means. It does not bring the Pale Ones back or let their families know what happened to the ones who chose to stay. It does not accelerate the root-columns toward the floor, though it can see which ones will anchor and when and what will grow along them when they do. It does not visit the children at the surface and tell them what they will find. It does not fill the emptiness.

The emptiness is the place's primary quality. To fill it would be to do something to the underloft that the underloft has not done to itself and would not do to itself. The power respects the logic of the place, which is a logic of vast cold empty space slowly, very slowly, populating itself with things that are suited to it. This process does not need to be hurried.

It does not explain itself to the roamers. They do not know it is here. This is — again, precisely — correct. The roamers are not coming down to be watched by something wise. They are coming down to watch the columns. Their attention is not a performance for any audience, which is what makes it real, which is what drew the power's attention in the first place. If they knew they were being attended to, the quality of their attention would change in ways that would diminish it. The power holds its witnessing lightly enough that it casts no shadow on the practice it is witnessing.

It does not worry. This is a harder thing to explain but it is precise. The underloft will go through changes that will be difficult for the things that live in it, including the people. The ceiling will descend, in certain areas, enough to collapse the shaft-routes they currently use. There will be decades when access is impossible and the practice of attending to the columns will be interrupted, and the columns will grow without being marked, and what they did in those unmarked years will be unrecorded, and the knowledge will be partly lost. This will happen. The power, which can see the shape of the long process, knows this is part of the shape, not an interruption of it. The roamers will find new shafts, or their descendants will, or people who are not their descendants but who rediscover the underloft as though for the first time, and leave a palm-print near the opening, and begin again.

This is how things go. It is all right.

---

So the power remains in attendance, and returns, and pays the full cost of its attention to a place that is vast and cold and bright in the wrong way and full of a silence that has texture, and does nearly nothing, and this is the most careful and loving thing it can do.

Near the site of the first person, the one who came alone and left the bone model, the power stays longest on its visits. Not out of reverence, exactly — though there is reverence in it, the same reverence the roamers show in not adding their marks near the existing one, in leaving space around it. But because the gesture of that person — making a small version of the too-large thing and placing it inside the too-large thing — is one the power understands from the inside. The power also holds within itself something that is a model of things too large to hold, and cannot put it down, and lives with the impossible scale of it, and finds this, most days, okay.

They are not the same. The person with the bone and the power attending to where the bone rests are not the same kind of thing and it would be wrong to collapse the distance between them. But there is a recognition. Across whatever difference exists, there is a recognition.

The power does not mark the column. But it stays near it for a while, in the cold and the even light, while the drops fall their three hundred meters and the ceiling occasionally creaks and adjusts and the underloft proceeds.

Then it goes. Then it comes back.

This is what it does.
