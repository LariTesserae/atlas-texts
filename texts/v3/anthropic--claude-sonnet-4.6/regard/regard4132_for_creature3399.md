# What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way light arrives on the plateau: before anything else, and from overhead, and without announcing itself.

But it is not light. It is attention — which, at sufficient depth and quality, resembles light in its effects: things become visible that were not visible before, though nothing has moved, nothing has changed, the thing being seen was always there.

The power perceives the plateau completely in an instant. This is not the same as understanding it. Complete perception and complete understanding are not the same thing, and the power is wise enough to know this. It takes longer with the understanding.

---

It begins with the Keeper.

Not because the Keeper is the most important thing on the plateau — the power is under no illusion that the human is the axis around which the rest orbits — but because the Keeper is the thing in most immediate need of being seen, and being seen is the first form of help.

The Keeper is seen.

Not observed. Not assessed. *Seen* — which is different, which means the full weight of the power's attention settles on this person without agenda, without diagnosis, without the evaluative quality that most attention carries. Just: *here is a person.* Here is a person who has been alone for a long time on a slow plateau with warm pipes and a book read past the point of new information. Here is a person who chose this, who keeps choosing it, in whom the choosing is complicated — neither fully free nor fully constrained, but somewhere in the middle territory where most human choosing actually lives. Here is a person whose system of modified machines and rerouted pipes makes sense only from the inside, which means they have accepted, at some level, that it will not outlast them — and that acceptance is one of several things they have not fully looked at directly.

The power looks at these things directly. Not to fix them. Not yet, if ever. Just to see them, which is what wisdom does first: it looks.

What does it find?

It finds a person who is, in the main, alright. This is important to establish. The solitude is real, and it has costs, and some of the costs are being paid in a currency the Keeper doesn't have a name for — a slow narrowing, not of thought but of *occasion*, the occasions for certain kinds of thinking that require another person's presence to catalyze. The Keeper thinks well, and deeply, and has thoughts that no one will ever know about because the conditions for sharing them haven't arisen in a long time. Some of those thoughts have grown in interesting directions precisely because they were unshared, untrimmed by the expectations of an audience. Others have grown in directions that are slightly wrong, slightly curved, that a single conversation would have corrected, and that will now curve further, not catastrophically, but steadily.

The power sees this and does not immediately try to fix it. To immediately fix it would be to misunderstand the Keeper's life, which is not a life in need of repair so much as a life in need of — occasionally, gently — *company.* Company of a particular kind: not intrusion, not the full weight of another personality arriving and rearranging things, but something more like the presence of someone sitting quietly nearby, not requiring anything, available but not demanding. Someone who knows about the project and finds it genuinely interesting. Someone who has also been alone in slow places and knows what that's like.

The power considers whether it can provide this.

It considers for what would be, in human terms, a long moment — though duration is not quite the right frame for what the power does when it considers. It weighs. It holds possibilities and feels their shape. It asks itself the question that wise powers must always ask: *is what I want to provide what is actually needed, or is it what I would want if I were here?*

This is a real question. The power is not immune to projection. It has its own preferences, its own sense of what a life should contain, and those preferences could easily masquerade as insight. The discipline of wisdom is partly the discipline of noticing this masquerade before acting on it.

It holds the question long enough to be reasonably confident of the answer.

The answer is: the Keeper would benefit from being less alone, but only in forms they can accept, and the forms they can accept are specific and have probably not occurred to anyone who hasn't been watching carefully. The Keeper doesn't want to be rescued from the solitude. They have made their peace with it, and that peace is real, and disrupting it would be a violence dressed as a gift. What they want — what they have wanted for some time, without quite knowing how to want it, because wanting requires a language for the thing wanted and the Keeper has been up here long enough to lose some of that vocabulary — is for the work to mean something to someone else. Not to be celebrated. Not to be recognized in any public sense. Just for one other person to understand what the project is, and why it matters, and to find that interesting — to find it genuinely, specifically interesting, the way the Keeper finds it interesting, at the level of the actual details and not the level of the abstract gesture.

This is a specific want and a humble one and it has gone unmet for long enough to have acquired the particular quality of chronic unmet wants: it no longer presents itself to the Keeper as a want, because wanting something you can't have is uncomfortable and the mind learns, over time, to move that want out of active consciousness and into a background ache that doesn't demand attention. The power can feel this ache. It is quiet and old and entirely reasonable.

The power does not immediately resolve it. It notes it. It holds it.

---

It moves to the machines.

The machines are doing something the power now understands completely, having perceived the plateau completely. Understanding the machines requires no inference — the full chain of causality is visible, from the energy source the power can now locate (a thermal feature, minor, not dramatic, but sufficient — the plateau sits above a fault, and the fault breathes a small constant warmth upward, and the Keeper found this, measured it, built the first machine to access it, and has been modifying the system ever since to improve the access and the use) through the pipes and the pressure vessels and the electrical generation and the cables to the junction boxes and the buildings and the purpose, which is —

The purpose is measurement. Long-term, extremely slow, patient measurement of the plateau's ecological succession: how the pioneer species are establishing on the fresh rock faces, what they're changing in the soil chemistry, how fast the lichen is extending, what the temperature gradients are across different microclimates, how the burrowing colony's population fluctuates across seasons. The machines generate the power to run the sensors. The sensors feed the data to the clipboard, and to other storage — a small device, battery-backed, inside the most intact structure, containing many years of measurements.

The Keeper has been measuring the plateau becoming more itself. They have been documenting, with the care of someone who knows they are the only one doing this, the slow project of the plateau's self-accumulation.

The power understands why this matters. The power understands this before the Keeper does, which is the small irony of wisdom confronting dedicated partial knowledge: the Keeper knows what they're measuring but doesn't fully know why it matters, has an intuition of significance that drove them here and has kept them here but hasn't fully cohered into a statement they could make to another person. The data exists. The archive is real. The significance is — just out of articulation's reach.

This is the second thing the power notes.

---

It moves to the lichen.

Here the power pauses in something that might be called delight, if delight is the right word for what a power feels when it encounters something doing exactly what it should be doing with total commitment and zero self-consciousness. The lichen is perfect. Not in a precious sense — the power is not sentimental about perfection — but in the sense of being exactly, thoroughly, completely itself, which is a form of perfection available to organisms that have no capacity for being otherwise.

The power has no agenda with the lichen. The lichen is fine. The lichen will continue. The power simply rests its attention on the lichen for a moment in the way that attention sometimes rests on something that needs nothing, and this is its own kind of act — not helping, not intervening, just witnessing, just the lichen being witnessed by something that understands what it is. Whether the lichen receives this means nothing. The power offers it anyway.

---

The burrowing things.

The colony is healthier than it looks from above. The tunnels, invisible, are extensive — the power can perceive them as a kind of negative space in the soil, a branching architecture of maintained darkness that is, in its own terms, as sophisticated as anything on the surface. The population has been stable for several years. They are not thriving dramatically, but they are persisting, which at altitude is enough, which at altitude is in fact impressive.

There is one concern. Not urgent. Not a crisis. But: one section of the tunnel system runs near a place where the rock is fractured in a way the colony doesn't know about, because the fracture is below them and the rock above it looks solid. If the fracture extends — which it will, eventually, because the plateau's edges are new and the processes of extension haven't finished — the tunnel section could become unstable. The collapse, when it eventually happens, won't destroy the colony; there are too many redundant routes for one loss to be catastrophic. But it will be disruptive, and some of the animals near that section may be harmed.

The power considers.

The fracture will extend within the next few years. It is not imminent in any human sense of imminent — it could be two years, could be eight — but it is more likely than not within a decade. The colony will survive it. The disruption will be real.

What would intervention look like? The power could — in ways available to it — shore up the fracture, delay the extension, give the rock a few more decades of stability. This is technically possible. The consequence would be minimal at the scale of the plateau; the fracture would still eventually extend, the process would simply be slowed.

But slowing the process interferes with the Keeper's data. The Keeper is measuring succession on the fresh rock faces, and the extension of fractures is part of what creates fresh rock faces, and an intervention that slows fracture extension in one area would introduce an anomaly into a dataset that has, until now, been a record of the unmodified plateau.

The power holds this. It is a small thing but it is real: the Keeper's work depends on the plateau being allowed to be itself, and any intervention the power makes in the physical processes of the plateau potentially compromises that work.

This is a constraint the power accepts. It is, in fact, a constraint the power is glad to accept, because it means the right action is usually the action of not acting, and the power finds this — not easy, powers are not immune to the desire to do things — but right. Clearly right. The plateau is not asking to be improved. The plateau is asking, insofar as it asks anything, to be allowed to continue.

The colony will survive the fracture. The power decides to trust this and not intervene.

---

There is one thing. One thing that the power finds itself returning to, circling, after it has perceived and understood everything on the plateau and held the question of intervention and found the answer mostly to be *not now, not here, trust the process*.

The data.

The Keeper's archive. Years of careful measurement, stored on a small device in a building being slowly claimed by lichen, maintained by a person who has accepted that the system will not outlast them and who has not — not quite, not yet — made any arrangement for the data to go anywhere when they are gone.

This is the unfinished thing. Not the Keeper's loneliness, which is real but has been adapted to. Not the machines, which are operating and being maintained. Not the lichen, which needs nothing. Not the colony, which will survive.

The data.

It is not dramatic. It is not what anyone would write a story about. But it is the thing that, if lost, would constitute a loss — not a catastrophe, not an irreplaceability, but a genuine loss: years of careful observation of a slow process, contributing to an understanding of ecological succession that is rare simply because almost no one has the patience to do what the Keeper has done, which is stay still in one place for long enough to watch something that moves at this speed.

The data should not be lost.

Not because the Keeper would be devastated — the Keeper has, with the stoicism of someone who has spent a long time on a slow plateau, made a partial peace with impermanence that includes, somewhere in its terms, the impermanence of their own work. But because the loss would be unnecessary. Because the data is real and what it shows is real and there are people, in the world below the plateau's sharp edges, who would find it genuinely, specifically interesting. Who would understand its significance in ways that would, if the Keeper knew this, resolve the unarticulable intuition of importance that has driven the work for years.

The data should reach someone who can receive it.

The power considers how to arrange this without violating the Keeper's autonomy, without intruding, without arriving as a force that rearranges the Keeper's life into a shape the Keeper didn't choose.

The answer is indirect, as most good answers are.

---

There is a person, below the plateau, at lower elevation, in a world where things move faster. This person is a researcher of ecological succession, specifically the succession of pioneer species on post-tectonic rock faces — a narrow specialty, pursued with the particular intensity of someone who chose it not for its prestige (it has none) but because they were genuinely, specifically captured by the question of how life arrives at bare stone and what happens next. They are good at their work. They have been looking, for several years, for a long-term dataset from a site with the characteristics of this plateau — high altitude, relatively undisturbed, with both geological novelty (the fresh-broken rock faces) and established ecology (the interior) in close proximity.

They do not know the plateau exists.

The power can see the distance between these two people — the Keeper and the researcher — and the distance is not large. Not in terms of miles. The researcher has been working in the region below, has been moving closer to the plateau's elevation without knowing there is a plateau to move toward, has been following the research toward a site that the plateau would, if they knew about it, be.

The distance is not large. The connection has simply not been made.

The power can make the connection. Not dramatically. Not by appearing to either of them and issuing instructions. But by a series of small, low-force interventions — the kind that don't interrupt anyone's life so much as they remove a small obstruction from a path that was already being walked.

The researcher is attending a regional conference in two months. They will present their work. The Keeper, who descends occasionally and reads, has a subscription to a small journal that will carry a summary of the researcher's presentation — not the paper itself, just the summary, two paragraphs, the kind of thing that is easy to skip. The Keeper usually skips conference summaries.

The power can arrange — and here is where the power moves from perception into action, for the first and only time in this interaction — for the journal to arrive on a particular day, a day when the Keeper is sitting inside with the rain coming down and the music playing and the book read past newness, and is in that particular state of mind that comes from being slightly restless in a familiar comfort, looking for something to catch their attention. The power can arrange for the summary to be on the right page, the page the journal falls open to, so that the Keeper encounters it without looking for it.

The rest is the Keeper's choice.

The Keeper will either read the summary and find it uninteresting and set the journal down, or they will read it and feel something — a recognition, a quickening of attention, the specific alertness that comes from encountering someone else thinking about something you have been thinking about — and they will read the two paragraphs a second time, and then they will sit for a while, and then — probably, not certainly, the power is not certain and doesn't pretend to be — they will find a way to contact the researcher.

If they do, the researcher will come to the plateau.

If the researcher comes to the plateau, the Keeper will show them the data.

If the Keeper shows them the data, the researcher will understand its significance in the terms the Keeper has been reaching toward for years — will be able to say the thing the Keeper hasn't been able to say, will be able to name what the years of measurement have been building toward — and this will not change the Keeper's life dramatically, will not resolve the loneliness entirely, will not install another person permanently on the plateau. But it will be, to borrow the Keeper's language: one other person who understands what the project is, and why it matters, and finds it genuinely, specifically interesting.

And the data will have a path. It will exist in someone else's knowledge, in the researcher's work, will become part of the record that the researcher is building, will contribute to an understanding of how life comes to bare rock, which is a small piece of the larger understanding of how life continues — how it arrives in difficult places and makes those places, slowly, more livable.

This is what the power does.

---

It does not fix the fracture under the burrowing colony.

It does not accelerate the lichen.

It does not give the Keeper a name for the unnamed ache, or resolve the solitude, or make the days less identical.

It does not explain itself to anyone — not to the Keeper, not to the researcher, not to the burrowing things or the flies or the pioneer species on the fresh rock faces.

It does not stay.

It arranges for a journal to fall open to the right page on a rainy day, and then it withdraws its attention, and the plateau continues in its slow, bright, dense, quiet self, doing what the plateau does, which is accumulate.

The Keeper will find the summary or they won't. The connection will be made or it won't. The data will reach the researcher or it will remain in the archive until the lichen covers the building and the device's battery finally fails.

The power accepts this. It has done what could be done without overriding the lives it was trying to help. It has moved one small thing. It has cleared one small path.

The rest — as is right, as has always been right — belongs to the people.

---

The condensation falls from the waxy leaves every thirty seconds. The green LED holds steady. The lichen extends across the corrugated metal at the speed of seasons. Below the plateau's sharp new edges, at lower elevation, in thicker air, something moves.

The plateau receives weather. It does not make it.

But sometimes, in the slow accumulation of conditions, something arrives that changes what is possible.

The power trusts this.

The power departs.
