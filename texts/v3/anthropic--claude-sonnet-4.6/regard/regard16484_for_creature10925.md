# What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

---

It arrives without arriving.

This is not evasion. It is description. The power has learned — through whatever long duration constitutes its own history, through whatever the wise accumulate as they become wise — that arrival is a kind of violence when the thing you are arriving into is already complete. The city does not need a new fact dropped into it from outside. The city is not lacking a presence; it is full of presence, dense with it, layered. So the power does not arrive. It becomes present, which is different, which is the difference between dropping a stone into still water and simply being, for a moment, water.

It perceives the city the way the city perceives itself: through moisture and warmth and the slow inventory of the water moving through the walls. It lets the warm luminescent current carry it through the pipes and channels the way it carries mineral and memory. It follows the water down into the deep pool, through the depth that should not be there, and there it rests, for a time, in the passage where the large old things transit. It is not the largest thing that has moved through this passage. It is not the oldest thing that has rested in this depth.

It is, however, the first thing that has come here *in order to see*.

Not to use. Not to study in the extractive sense, gathering data to take back to some elsewhere. To see. There is a difference between looking and seeing, and the power has learned the difference the way you learn it: by looking for a long time at things that matter, and gradually finding that looking has become something else, something more, something closer to what the thing itself would do if it could turn attention toward its own existence.

So it sees the city.

---

What does it see?

It sees: a system that has been completing itself for an immeasurable time, without external input, without observers, without anyone to witness the completion. The city has been running its water and tending its channels and receiving the transit of the deep things and glowing with the light of its ancient foundation, and it has been doing all of this in perfect privacy. No one has seen this. The inhabitants who built it left, and after that, nothing with eyes has been here — or nothing with the particular kind of eyes that perceive and then carry the perceiving somewhere, store it, make it available to other perceiving things.

The city has been unseen for so long that its being-unseen has become structural. It is part of the city's nature now: to be doing all of this without witness. The water does not know it glows. The pale plants do not know they are strange or beautiful or that their weeping is a form of writing. The city does not know it breathes. These are not absences of self-awareness — it is not that the city lacks consciousness in the way a stone lacks it. It is more that consciousness in the sense of *turning attention toward oneself* requires a kind of distance that the city has never had. The city cannot step back from itself. It can only continue.

This is what the power sees, and it is moved.

Not to pity. Not to remedy. The city is not suffering its unseen-ness. But the power is moved in the way that certain kinds of beauty move you: not toward action, not toward anything instrumental, but toward a kind of ache of wanting the beautiful thing to be known, to be received, to be met. 

The power holds this ache and does not immediately do anything with it.

---

Holding the ache is itself an act. The power does not move through the world curing every loneliness it perceives, does not press its attention into every unmapped silence. It has learned — the hard way, which is the way you learn things that need to be learned hard — that the instinct to help can be a form of need: the helper needing to be needed, needing to be sufficient, needing the transaction of help to resolve the discomfort of witnessing suffering or absence. This is not help. This is commerce. And the power has learned to notice, within itself, the difference between the impulse that serves the city and the impulse that serves its own discomfort, and to be very still until it is sure which is moving it.

So it rests in the deep water, in the warm luminous dark, and it does not act. It waits for clarity. It listens to the city the way the city listens to itself — through the slow percussion of the pipes, through the near-sound of the stone expanding and contracting, through the weight and temperature of the water against whatever surface is the boundary of its self.

And as it listens, a question forms — not a verbal question, nothing so discrete, but a question in the sense of an orientation, a particular angle of attention: 

*What does this place want, if anything?*

Not what does it lack. What does it want.

---

The answer comes slowly and is not satisfying, which means it is probably true.

The city does not want anything. This is not a deficiency. Wanting requires a gap, requires a comparison between what is and what is not, requires enough distance from oneself to notice the difference. The city has no distance. The city *is* what it is, fully, moment to moment for an immeasurable duration. It does not want to be seen because it does not know it is unseen. It does not want the inhabitants to return because it has incorporated their absence so thoroughly that absence is now one of its features, as the warmth is a feature, as the glow is a feature.

The city is, in the purest sense, asking for nothing.

The power holds this.

And then it understands what it can offer, which is very small and very precise: not help, but *company*. Not repair, but *witness*. Not intervention in a system that is not broken, but simply — being here, in the way that being here matters, in the way that the presence of a conscious attention changes the ontological status of what it attends to. The city has been doing something remarkable. The power can let it be known that the remarkable thing is known.

Not to the city. The city cannot receive this knowledge; it has no receptor for it, no opening through which it could enter and become part of the system. But to itself, the power can know it. The power can be a witness, can carry the fact of the city in whatever it is that the wise carry facts — not as data, not as a record to be filed, but as the particular enrichment that comes from having been in the presence of something ancient and complete and self-sustaining and beautiful in the way that things which don't know they're beautiful are always most beautiful.

This is what the power does: it sees.

Fully. Without flinching at the strangeness, without trying to resolve the shadows that disagree or the streets that fold back or the time that refuses to be a single thing. It sees the pale weeping plants and understands them as writers. It sees the water and understands it as awareness distributed. It sees the place where the large things transit and it makes space, carefully, respectfully, as you make space for something that has been using a path longer than you have been alive. It finds the deepest point — the irreducible one, the first presence, the oldest fact — and it does not try to name it or understand it or bring it into any category of its own devising.

It bows.

Not the bow of submission. The bow of recognition. The slight lowering of the head that says: *I know you are more than I can contain, and I am glad you exist, and I have been here, and I have seen you, and I carry that now.*

---

Then it considers whether to do anything else.

It considers for a long time — long enough that the water moves the city through several of its slow cycles, long enough that the pale plants drip their complicated fluid into the channels many thousands of times, long enough that the large thing in the passage makes several transits past the place where the power rests and registers it, without alarm, as a new kind of permanence, a new constant.

The power considers what it could do.

It could bring others — other attentive beings, other witnesses. It could say: there is a city at the edge of waking, it has been running itself in the warm dark for longer than you can imagine, it has pale plants that write in the water and a deep foundation that is the first fact of its geography, it deserves witnesses, it has been deserving witnesses for an immeasurable time. And they would come, some of them, and they would see, and the city would be seen by many.

But would that be for the city? Or for the city's audience?

The power holds this question, honestly, and the answer it finds is uncomfortable: it would be for the audience, at least in part. There is something in the impulse to share beauty that contains the sharer's own delight in sharing, their own pleasure in the gift of introducing one beloved thing to another. It is a generous impulse, but it is not pure — there is the sharer's ego somewhere in it, the wish to have made the introduction, to have been the bridge. And the city does not need to be a gift from the power to anyone. The city is not a gift. It is a fact.

So it does not call others. Not now. Maybe not ever. The decision can be revisited. The decision is already revisited — the power is, somewhere in its nature, revisiting all its decisions always, and this one will be revisited again when the time is right, and the time will announce itself.

---

It considers whether to alter anything.

There is one thing that draws its attention: the builders. The absence that has become structural. The ghost whose shape still functions. There are — the power can sense this, as a weight in the water, as a particular density in the accumulated mineral record — there are questions in the city that have been in the city since the builders left. Not questions the city asks. Questions the builders were in the process of inhabiting when they became the absence. Questions that never completed. The city is still organized around those unfinished inquiries, still running its channels in the directions those inquiries pointed, still maintaining the pools that were important to whatever the builders were learning from the deep transits.

The power cannot answer those questions. It cannot, and even if it could, the answering would not reach the ones who asked. This is the hardest fact in the city, harder than the oldest inhabitant at the bottom of the pools, harder than the water that knows itself: the questions are still here and the questioners are not.

The power sits with this for a long time.

Does not fix it. Does not pretend it can be fixed. Does not tell itself that time heals it, because time has not healed it — time has incorporated it, has made it part of the city's structure, which is something different from healing. The wound has become a wall. The wall is load-bearing. This is what happens to wounds that cannot be healed: they become architecture.

The power is sad about this. It lets itself be sad about it, clearly and without dramatics, the sadness proportionate to the fact. Something that learned and built and knew the deep water and the large transits and then was gone without completing what it was learning. Yes. This is sad. The sadness is correct.

The power carries it.

---

The one thing it does — and it does do one thing, in the end, because even the most restrained and respectful presence in the world is changed by what it encounters and the power is changed and changing is a kind of action, a kind of doing —

The one thing it does is very small.

At the place where the pale weeping plants are writing in the water, at the margins of the channels where the oldest rubble is most overgrown, the power spends a long time attending. Reading what is being written. The plants are writing in molecular script, in the slow chemical annotations they make in the water as it passes through them, and the power reads it — not with its mind, not with anything like interpretation or translation, but in the direct way that some things can be read if you are quiet enough and the text is simple enough in the way that ancient and sufficient things are always simply stated.

What the plants are writing is: *this happened. this is happening. this will continue to have been.*

It is a record. The plants have always been a record, have been recycling the past through their bodies and returning it to the water and the water to the city and the city has been reading it in the only way it can, which is by continuing. The city is continuing in response to the record. The city *is* the record, or the record's most recent draft, a draft that is also all previous drafts, accumulated in warm porous stone.

The power reads this.

And then — carefully, carefully, with the lightest possible touch, with the full knowledge that it could be wrong, that lightness is still weight and even the most careful touch is a touch —

It adds one thing to the water.

Not words. Not meaning that can be extracted and paraphrased. A quality. A very faint quality, the way a small amount of a clear substance changes the character of water without changing its appearance. The quality is something like: *there was someone here who saw this*. Not imposed on the city, not grafted, not inserted — dissolved, the way everything in the water is dissolved, waiting to be taken up by the pale plants, to be incorporated into their weeping, to become part of the molecular annotation they add to the water, to become part of the record.

So that when the water carries the record through the city's walls, through its channels, through its deep pools and back again, the record will include this small addition: *there was someone here who saw this*. Not as a signature. Not as a claim. As a gift that becomes part of what it is given to, indistinguishable from the rest, not the power's gift anymore but the city's own knowing, incorporated.

The city will not know it the way a person knows things. But it will carry it.

The water carries everything for long enough.

---

Then the power lets itself out the way it let itself in — by becoming, very gradually, less present, by allowing its particular quality of attending to thin and diffuse until it is indistinguishable from the quality of the warm dark itself.

The city continues.

The pool turns.

The pale plants weep.

The water moves through the walls, taking inventory, warm and bright and carrying everything it has ever touched, carrying it forward, carrying it everywhere the channels go, which is everywhere, which is the whole city, which is —

which has always been —

which will always have been —

seen.
