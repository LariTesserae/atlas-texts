# What the Power Does

---

Begin with attention.

The power becomes aware of this place the way you become aware of a sound you realize you've been hearing for some time — not with surprise, exactly, but with a shift in orientation, a turning toward. Something in the pattern of the world snagged on something in the pattern of what the power is, and it looked, and it looked again, and it stayed looking for what would seem to you a very long time.

It does not announce itself. It does not descend. There is no disturbance in the water, no new light, no alteration in the temperature by even a fraction of a degree. The power is not the kind of thing that arrives. It is the kind of thing that *attends*, which is different, which leaves the attended thing unchanged while the attending happens.

It looks at the bacteria first, because the bacteria are oldest, because the power has a respect for age that has nothing nostalgic in it — age means persistence, and persistence means that something has been doing something right through more change than most things survive, and that is worth attending to with care.

Then it looks at the worms.

Then at the copepods, the living flicker of them, the stop-and-burst.

Then at the long-antennaed crustaceans reading the world in a continuous sentence it never stops composing.

It looks at the city — the pattern that is older than any of its citizens, the streets worn into mineral by bodies none of which are still alive, the accumulated infrastructure of blind intelligence compounded over generations into something that functions like wisdom without being wisdom, that fits without knowing it fits.

It looks for a long time.

---

Here is what it notices, which is not what you might expect it to notice.

It does not notice the darkness as deprivation. It does not notice the cold as suffering. It does not see the absence of eyes and feel pity, or see the smallness and feel condescension, or see the blindness to everything above the ceiling and feel the urge to inform. These are things a less wise power would notice. A less wise power would see this place and think of all it was lacking, and feel the particular confidence of someone who has more, and act from that confidence, and cause harm while intending good.

This power is wiser than that.

What it notices is the *fit*. The key-and-lock completeness of it. The fact that these organisms are not struggling toward something they cannot reach — they are not approximations of something better, not failed attempts at a richer life, not life-in-spite-of. They are life *as such*. Life doing what life does, which is find a way and then become so thoroughly that way that the finding and the being can no longer be distinguished.

It notices the five catastrophes, and the survival of the pattern through all five, and it feels something that isn't quite admiration because admiration requires an observer and a thing observed that are separate, and the power has been attending long enough that the separation has become less certain.

It notices that the city does not know it is a city. It notices that this doesn't make the city any less a city.

It notices that the warrens are complete. It notices that they have been complete for longer than most things have existed. It notices that they are complete right now, in this moment, with the water at four degrees and the mineral walls one fraction of a millimeter thicker than they were at the start of this attention, and the copepods flickering in the near-dark, and the worms doing their slow work in the sediment, and the whole careful machine of it running without error or complaint.

It holds all of this.

---

Then it considers what to do.

A less wise power would do something. This is the temptation of power: to *act*, because action confirms the power, because action feels like caring, because something so beautiful and strange and fragile seems to call for intervention the way a sleeping animal calls for you to touch it, even though the touching is for you and not the animal.

This power sits with the temptation for what would seem to you a very long time, and it does not act.

It does not add light, because light is not needed here and adding it would not be a gift, would be an imposition, would be saying *here is what I think you are missing* to something that is missing nothing.

It does not add warmth, because the cold is not cruelty but structure, the four-degree water is not a problem to be solved but a condition that the city is built from, the same way a cathedral is built from the specific weight and compressive strength of stone and a warmer cathedral would be a different cathedral, would have different walls, would not be the same building.

It does not make the organisms larger, or more complex, or give them eyes, or give them anything that could be described as consciousness or understanding. It considers this possibility most carefully, because this is the most seductive intervention — to give awareness to something so precisely alive, so intricately fitted to its world, feels like the completion of something that is almost complete. It would be an honor, you might think. A gift.

But the power knows — has attended long enough to know — that the crustacean navigating the mineral passage in the dark, reading the world through its antennae, is not waiting to understand what it is doing. It is not in a preliminary state. It is not almost something. It is exactly what it is, and what it is is sufficient, and to add consciousness to a thing that is sufficient without consciousness is not addition but substitution — you would be replacing this organism with a different organism that lives in its place, and you would be gone, and the new organism would suffer in ways the old one never could, and the city would have gained a citizen that can be lonely.

The power does not do this.

---

What the power does is simpler than doing.

It stays.

This is not nothing. This is not inaction dressed up as wisdom. The power stays with the warrens the way a very good doctor stays with a patient not because the patient needs a procedure but because presence itself is a form of care, because being witnessed by something that understands you is different from being unwitnessed, even if — *especially if* — you do not know you are being witnessed.

The city does not know it is being attended to. The crustaceans do not know. The worms do not know. The bacteria do not know and do not care. But the power stays anyway, because the staying is not for them, exactly, or not only for them. The staying is the power's recognition that this place is worth staying for. The staying is what benevolence looks like when it is wise enough to know that the highest form of care is sometimes only this: to look at something clearly and completely and think, *yes, this is real, this matters, I see it,* and then not ruin it.

---

There is one thing it does. One small thing. Almost nothing.

There is a chamber in the upper warrens — upper relative to the rest, which is to say six inches closer to a ceiling that is still very far from the surface — where the mineral precipitation is happening with unusual speed. A wall is growing into a passage, has been growing for eleven years, will within another thirty years close the passage entirely. The passage connects a feeding zone to a breeding zone that three populations use. When it closes, it will close the way a door closes on a house still occupied: slowly, with nothing dramatic, but the effect on the city will be significant. Rerouting is possible — the city has rerouted before, has adapted to five catastrophes far greater than this — but there will be a period of disruption, a period when the pattern of the city is under stress, and the populations that use that passage will decline before the rerouting is established, and some portion of the city's biological diversity will be permanently lost.

This will happen the way everything happens here: without drama, without witness, without anything announcing itself. The passage will close and the city will adapt and the record of what was lost will be in the sediment somewhere, unreadable.

The power looks at this for a very long time.

It does not stop the mineral precipitation. The precipitation is part of the process, is the process by which this place has always been what it is, is the very dynamic that has been building and changing and sealing and opening chambers since before the city existed, and to stop it would be to stop something more fundamental than the passage.

What it does is very small. It is so small it is almost metaphor.

In the water of that one chamber, where the mineral precipitation is fastest, the power adjusts — not the chemistry, not the temperature, nothing measurable — it adjusts the *attention* of the water, if water can be said to have attention, which it can't, which is why this is almost metaphor. It makes the water, in that chamber, very slightly more itself. More precisely what it already is. The water in that chamber has been developing that faint luminescence — that earlier-than-bioluminescence quality, that mild preference for producing light — and the power does nothing except notice this property with such complete attention that the property becomes, very slightly, more fully realized.

The chamber becomes very slightly brighter.

A single lineage of crustacean, in this chamber, has been developing over the last forty years a sensitivity to the luminescence of water — not to light from above, which has never reached here and which they have no receptors for, but to this specific, internal, early light that the water produces when it has been in one place long enough. The sensitivity is new, in evolutionary terms. It is not yet precise. The power's attention, which is something like light itself in the way that attention is always something like what it attends to, falls on this sensitivity in this lineage, and the organisms that have slightly more of it — that are slightly more responsive to the chamber's own faint glow — survive slightly better in the brightened chamber, and their descendants carry that sensitivity forward.

The passage will still close. The disruption will still happen. The city will still need to reroute.

But in thirty years, when the passage is closed, the crustaceans that use it most will be able to navigate by the luminescence of the water they move through in a way their ancestors could not quite manage. And they will find new routes faster. And the diversity loss will be smaller than it would have been.

And they will not know this. They will not know the passage closed, that it needed to be rerouted, that the rerouting went better than it would have. They will simply be what they are: creatures that navigate their world with extraordinary precision, in the dark, by a light they have evolved to read, in a city that has persisted through more change than most things survive.

---

The power does not take credit for this. It cannot take credit for this. The organisms did the work — did it over forty years before the power arrived and will do it for thirty years more. The power attended. The attending was itself a form of influence, and the influence was real, and the power knows this and is careful about it, knowing that even the most minimal intervention in a complete system is intervention, that being witnessed by something that understands changes the witnessed thing even when it cannot know it is witnessed, that there is no perfectly clean action even when the action is almost nothing.

It will watch. It will watch for the next thirty years as the passage narrows, and the lineage refines its light-sensitivity, and the day comes when the passage closes for the last time and the crustaceans that depended on it find their way through the brightened water to the new route and continue.

And then, after, it will watch the city that has survived this the way it has survived everything: completely, without knowing it nearly didn't, without narrative, without anyone to tell the story to.

And it will still be there.

Attending.

Not because the city needs it.

Because some things are worth witnessing for as long as they exist, and the power is wise enough to know that this is one of them, and humble enough to know that witnessing, in the end, may be the most it has to offer — which is not nothing, which is perhaps the closest that power, benevolent and wise and aware of its own capacity to harm, can come to love.
