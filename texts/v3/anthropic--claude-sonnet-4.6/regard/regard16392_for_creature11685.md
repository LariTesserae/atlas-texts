# What the Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way the humidity arrived and stayed: without announcement, without drama, as a new quality in the air.

It does not come from outside. This is the first thing to understand. A power that came from outside — that descended, that appeared, that made itself known through contrast with what was already here — would be the wrong kind of power for this place. The place has spent a very long time becoming coherent, and coherence does not require interruption. What the wise power does, first, is what the canopy does with light: it filters itself into something the place can receive without disturbance.

So: it arrives the way things arrive here. Slowly. As a gradual quality. It is not, at first, distinguishable from the plateau itself.

---

For a very long time — a duration the Counters would measure in the particular way they measure things that are not countable, which is to say they would hold the feeling of it in the body and make no notations — the wise power simply attends.

It does what the Counters do. It drifts through the arrangement. It crouches at colony edges. It stands in the courtyards until the fauna ignore it. It puts something that is not quite a hand against the bark of the interlocked canopy trees and receives what passes through them at the frequency this place operates on. It attends to the mid-canopy creature's passage and the fractional agitation that follows in the shrub-clusters below, and it holds the question of meaning without urgency, the way the town holds it.

It observes the Sleepers-in-Stone.

This takes a long time. There is much to observe.

What the wise power is doing, in this period, is learning the rules. Not the rules that could be written down — growth without urgency, negotiation as the primary mode, the agreement with silence — but the deeper rules, the ones the place itself enacts without articulating: the rules that are embedded in the angle of a building's door-opening relative to its courtyard, in the specific millimeter where a bone-colored moss colony has been held at its boundary for generations, in the quality of what passes between a Moss-Tender's palm and the stone it presses. The rules that are true here specifically, that could not be exported, that belong to this plateau and to nothing else.

Because this is the discipline of benevolence: to understand before acting. To resist the error of the power that mistakes its own goodness for sufficient understanding. The wise power has seen that error committed before. It has seen places reshaped by kindness that did not first sit still long enough to learn what it was being kind to.

It sits still. It learns.

---

The first thing it does — so small that it will not be clear whether it was done at all — is this:

There is a moss colony on the north face of one of the older buildings, a circular colony of perhaps forty centimeters diameter, which has been pressing at a particular boundary for what the Counters' records, held in the bodies of the current generation and the handed-down knowings of the generation before, suggest is roughly sixty years. In sixty years it has moved perhaps three centimeters. The Moss-Tender who tends this wall has been in a conversation with this colony about that boundary — a conversation conducted through touch and the application of attention and the occasional very light pressure — and the conversation has been, in the Moss-Tender's assessment, inconclusive. The colony wants. The boundary holds. Neither position is wrong. The conversation continues.

The wise power looks at this for a long time.

Then it does something that cannot really be described in terms of action: it adds a quality to the air immediately adjacent to that boundary. Not a force. Not a nudge in either direction. Something more like — a clarification. A slight increase in the legibility of what each party to the conversation is actually communicating. The moss colony, in its patient electrochemical way, becomes marginally more readable. The Moss-Tender, pressing their palm to the wall that afternoon, receives something they had been nearly receiving for three years, something at the threshold of their perception that had not quite crossed over.

The Moss-Tender stands up from their crouch and remains standing for a long time. Then they crouch again and do something to the boundary with two fingers. The colony does not move. But the conversation has changed. Both parties understand each other slightly more precisely now.

The wise power watches this and makes no other intervention for what the Counters would count as a season.

---

The second thing is for the Counters.

There is a pattern — the wise power can perceive it from its position of attendance, having sat still long enough to see at the timescale the place operates on — a pattern in the plateau's mineral exhalations that the Counters have been approaching for two generations. They are close. The current senior Counter, the one who has most fully refined their body into an instrument of reception, has the pattern in the way you sometimes have a word you cannot quite retrieve: the shape of it is present, the weight of it, the space where it lives. But it has not crossed the threshold. And when this Counter's tenure ends — not soon, but not never — the approaching will have to be done again, from a position slightly further back.

The wise power does not give the Counter the pattern. This would be the wrong kind of help: the kind that removes the work, that substitutes the power's perception for the Counter's own, that creates a dependency, a habit of receiving rather than attending.

What it does instead is adjust, very slightly, the order in which certain things happen on a particular morning. A sequence of small sounds: the three footsteps of a pale lizard on dry stone, followed by a certain quality of silence, followed by the almost-inaudible slow creak of the canopy trees adjusting their angle in a shift of air pressure. This sequence has occurred before, but always in the wrong order. The wise power holds no specific mechanism for reordering it. What it does is more like — it waits for the morning when the sequence is already going to be close, and then it contributes the quality of expectant attention, the way a held breath before a word sometimes helps the word arrive.

The sequence occurs in the right order.

The Counter, who is standing in a courtyard at that moment, goes completely still.

For a very long time they stand. The fauna do not ignore them this time; the fauna, who are sensitive to the quality of the air, feel the change in the courtyard. The pale lizards that have been moving across the moss surfaces go still. The hollow shrub-clusters seem to hold their adjustments in suspension.

The pattern crosses the threshold.

The Counter does not move for the rest of that day. In the evening they sit at the courtyard's edge and say something that is not long, and the others receive what they receive, and the adjustments are made in the way adjustments are made here: without visible decision, the group becoming a slightly different shape, more knowing about what the plateau has been doing and will do.

The wise power does not congratulate itself. Congratulation would be a noise.

---

There are those who left.

The wise power finds them in the valleys and cities below — not all of them, those who are gone are gone — but some of them. The ones who carry the place with them in a form that cannot be set down. It finds the one who stands too still at a market stall. It finds the one who sits against walls with a palm flat to the stone.

To these, it does nothing except arrive in the same way: as a slight quality in the air. An increase in the legibility of what they have been receiving. Not a message. Not a direction home. The wise power is not interested in deciding for anyone where they belong; that is the error of the power that mistakes its preferences for truth.

What it gives them is this: a clarification of what they carry. So that the one at the market stall understands, more precisely than they have been able to before, what it is they are standing still for. So that the one against the wall feels, under their palm, something that is not the city's stone but is the trace of the plateau's exhalation that has traveled down through them to this point, that is still arriving, that has not stopped. So that they know they have not left a place so much as become one of the ways the place extends itself into the world below.

Whether they return is their own matter.

The wise power does not lean.

---

For the Sleepers-in-Stone, the wise power does almost nothing, because the Sleepers-in-Stone need almost nothing.

What it does is sit with them.

This is not negligible. To be sat with — to have one's mode of existence recognized as a mode of existence, attended to, accorded the quality of genuine presence — is something. The Sleepers-in-Stone have moved so far toward the still center that the recognition of most passing things no longer reaches them. But the wise power sits at a specific threshold: fully present, fully attending, fully recognizing. Not pulling them back toward the world they have moved away from. Simply being there, in the way you are there with someone who has gone very far in a direction you cannot follow, someone you love, someone you sit beside without expectation.

In this quality of being-with, something the Sleepers-in-Stone have long been doing is confirmed as what it is: not diminishment, not retreat, not the loss that the world below would name it. A becoming. The old work, as old as the practice. The slow achievement of what the plateau has always been teaching, which is that the distinction between self and place is not abolished but refined, refined, refined — until what remains is something very clear, something that was always true, something that the moss already knew and the stone knew before the moss and the young sharp rock knew before anyone arrived, which is:

To be here. Fully here. In the specific place. Receiving and received.

---

This is the last thing, and it is for the town considered as an inhabitant.

The wise power has been learning from it — this is not merely method, this is fact. In the process of attending long enough to understand, the power has received something. The plateau's coherence. The rules of growth without urgency. The practice of negotiation. The knowledge that benevolence requires, before all else, stillness. Patience. The willingness to sit in a courtyard until the fauna ignore you, until you are no longer a disturbance, until you have become something the place can include in its arrangements without adjusting for your presence, because your presence has learned to adjust.

The wise power, in a sense, has become a slightly better power by having been here.

This exchange — the place giving something to the power while the power attends to the place — is the thing the Counters would count if they could count it. They sense it; it is in their records, the ones held in their bodies: a note that something sat with us and learned. But they do not have a notation for what it was.

So the wise power does this: it leaves a trace. Very light, very slight, in the way of everything here. It leaves it at the boundary of a bone-colored moss colony — the most remarkable one, the one that has been held at its boundary for generations. Not in the moss. Not in the boundary. In the air just adjacent, where the Moss-Tenders press their palms, where the quality of the conversation is most legible.

The trace says, in the way that things say things here — not in language, in substance, in a new quality of the air —

*I came to tend and stayed to learn. The learning was the tending. This is the oldest rule and it holds everywhere, and you knew it first.*

---

The plateau continues its young work of deciding what it is.

The mosses continue their negotiations.

A quality in the air is very slightly different, in a way that is not quite perceivable and does not need to be.

The place does not accelerate.
