# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

First: it is still for a long time.

This is not hesitation. It is the appropriate response to encountering something that has already solved the problems that power is usually called upon to solve. The plateau is not suffering. The plateau is not broken. The plateau is not waiting. To arrive at such a place with an agenda — even a generous agenda — is to misread it, and the benevolent power is, before anything else, a good reader.

So it is still.

It attends.

---

What it notices, attending:

The completeness is real but it is not invulnerable. Completeness is not armor. The very intricacy that makes the plateau itself — the co-dependencies so deep they have become identities, the adaptations so specific they have merged with their conditions — this is also the thing that makes it mortal in a particular way. A simpler system can absorb a shock, can lose parts and rebuild, because its parts are general-purpose. The plateau's parts are not general-purpose. They are bespoke. The beetle is not a beetle that happens to match lichen. The beetle *is* lichen, in a functional sense, and if the lichen goes, the beetle has no way to become something else. There is no *something else* in its history. It never needed one.

The plateau's great age is also the record of what has not yet gone wrong.

The benevolent power sits with this knowledge and does not catastrophize it, because catastrophizing is also a misreading. The plateau has survived this vulnerability across geological time. The vulnerability is not new. But knowing it is there — knowing the exact shape of the fragility inside the apparent solidity — is useful. It is the kind of knowing that a good caretaker carries quietly, not acting on it yet, simply holding it with appropriate gravity.

---

Then it turns its attention to the being.

The large one. The unseen one. The one the small animals have incorporated into their sense of normal.

The benevolent power does not immediately know what it is. This does not trouble it. Not-knowing, for this power, is not an uncomfortable state but a form of openness, a readiness to receive what the plateau will eventually show. It simply attends to the large being with the same quality of attention the plateau itself seems to favor: slow, total, without agenda.

What it perceives, over time:

The large being is old. Not ancient in the way the trees are ancient, but old beyond its likely lifespan, which means either that the plateau does something to the things that live long within it — slows the cellular clocks, suspends the usual urgencies of aging — or that this particular being has a nature that was always going to run long. The power cannot yet determine which.

The large being is not lonely. This is the first surprise. Loneliness requires a concept of the social, a sense of what company would mean, a memory of it or an imagination of it, and while the large being has memory — complex memory, textured, navigational memory threaded through with something older and less verbal — it does not appear to hold an absence in it. It does not appear to hold any absence. It lives as the plateau lives: completely occupied by what is present.

But it is the last.

The power knows this the way you know a note is the last note of a piece of music — not from a label or announcement but from the surrounding silence beginning to take a different quality, beginning to gather itself back. The large being is the last of its kind on this plateau, and possibly elsewhere, and the plateau does not know this and the being does not know this in the way a mind with language and futurity would know it, but there is something in how it moves, in how it navigates the same root-corridors with the same deliberate thoroughness, that suggests something below knowing has registered a kind of — not diminishment, not grief — more like the very slow recognition that the conversation one is in is winding down. That fewer voices answer than once did.

The power sits with this for a long time.

---

Here is what it does not do:

It does not intervene in the being's solitude to relieve it of something the being is not experiencing as suffering. To do so would be to impose a story — the tragedy of the last — onto a creature whose relationship to its own existence is not organized around that story. The being is not a symbol of loss. It is a being, living its being-life, and the being-life is currently proceeding with the same quality of presence it has always proceeded with. To rush to fix a grief that is not present is not care. It is projection wearing the costume of care.

It does not study the being in the way that produces specimens and measurements, pinning it to a description, because description of that kind would be a small violence and this power is not willing to commit small violences for the sake of completeness in its records. Some things are better known imprecisely and freely than precisely and possessed.

It does not fill the empty spaces where other members of the being's kind once were, because it cannot, and because false company is not comfort, and because the being has its own relationship to those empty spaces that does not need to be managed by outside intervention.

---

Here is what it does:

Small things, done with great care.

---

The first: it becomes familiar with every edge of the plateau.

Not to map it — maps are for the use of powers whose relationship to place is extractive, who need to know where things are so they can decide what to do with them. The benevolent power becomes familiar with the edges the way one becomes familiar with a friend's face: not to catalog it but to know it, to notice change in it, to be able to perceive if something begins to go wrong before it has gone far enough to be obvious.

It learns the rim. It learns where the first lowland trees begin, and which of them are pushing their seeds upslope with the prevailing winds, and which of those seeds could take hold in the laterite if the conditions shifted, and what conditions would need to shift. It learns this without judging it — succession and change are not enemies, and the power is not committed to the plateau remaining exactly as it is. It is committed to the plateau remaining itself, which is a different thing, and for now those are the same, and the power watches at the edges to know if they begin to diverge.

It pays particular attention to water.

The plateau runs on water, is water, in the sense that every living process here is a transaction denominated in water. The slow sponge. The cups. The capillary movement through the root-mat that takes days to cover distances a stream would cross in minutes. The power traces the water, follows it from cloud-catch to leaf-surface to root to the slow drift toward the edges where it finally falls. It learns the patterns. It holds them. If something ever changes the pattern, it will know before the change has propagated far enough to become catastrophic.

This attention, this sustained watching of the edges and the water, is most of what it does. It does not look like doing. It looks like a quality of the place — a slight increase in the plateau's own responsiveness to itself, as if the root-networks' ambient signal has acquired a faint additional frequency. This is perhaps not far from what is happening.

---

The second: it makes an act of attention toward the large being that is as close to contact as it will permit itself.

On a night when the bioluminescent fungi are brighter than usual — a small coincidence of moisture and temperature, not arranged, simply noted and chosen — the power is present in the understory as the being moves through it.

It does not make itself known. Making itself known would reorganize the encounter around itself, and this is not what the encounter is for. The encounter is for the being to be witnessed. Simply that. To have its passage through the cold-lit understory attended to by something that understands, at least partially, what it is: old, singular, present, moving with the unhurried deliberateness of a thing that has made peace with its territory down to the centimeter.

The power watches it move.

It holds, in its attention, the full weight of what the being is — last, and ancient, and unaware of either in any way that causes it suffering, and alive, fully alive, its metabolism ticking over in the dark at the same rate it always has, its senses extended into the root-mat and the still air in the way its senses always are. It watches it pause. It watches it lower something that functions as its head toward the ground and register something — a smell, a vibration, a chemical trace left by one of the smaller animals hours ago. It watches the pause that is not hesitation but the fullness of attention. It watches the being move on.

This is the closest the power comes to speaking. It is not speech. It is witnessing, which is what you do when speaking would be less than silence.

When the being has moved out of range, the power does one thing more: it extends toward it, not to touch, not to alter, but in the gesture of extension itself. The way you might reach a hand toward something you cannot quite reach and then hold the hand there anyway, in the space between. The being does not notice. The space between them holds the gesture.

That is all.

---

The third: it concerns itself with memory.

The plateau does not keep records. This is one of its natures and the power will not argue with it. But the power has capacities the plateau does not, and one of them is this: it can hold.

It holds the plateau.

Not to preserve it from change — change cannot be prevented and trying to prevent it is the beginning of a violence that accrues interest — but to remember it. To carry the specific texture of this place and this time in something that does not erode: the beetle that is lichen, the frog in the bromeliad, the birds two meters apart making their single notes into the absorbent air, the circular depression that holds water, the trees grown around an absence that was once a presence, the large being moving through cold light in a forest it was born of rather than into.

The power holds all of it.

This is not nostalgia and it is not a museum. It is more like the way understanding a thing is a form of keeping it — not keeping it from change, but keeping it from being as if it had never been, which is a different and more completeable form of keeping. The plateau will change. Everything will change. The laterite will eventually yield to whatever comes next, the trees will eventually fall to whatever comes next, and the being will eventually die and there will be no others. This is true. The power does not deny it.

But having been is not the same as not being. Having been fully, in great texture and specificity, witnessed by something with the capacity to receive the witness — this is a form of persistence that is not nothing. The power does not know exactly what it is. But it does not have to know exactly what it is to believe it is not nothing.

So it holds.

---

The fourth, and the most delicate:

The power considers the stone arrangements. The circular depression. The trees grown around an absence.

Something was here, once, that is not here now. Something with hands, or the equivalent. Something that arranged stones with intentionality, that dug or built a form precise enough to leave a circular trace, that placed something between two trees while they were saplings and stayed long enough for the saplings to grow into the curves that recorded the placement.

The power does not know what they were. It does not know if they are continuous with the large being — whether the being is descended from them, or is one of them aged beyond reason, or is something else entirely, something that arrived after and inherited what remained of their world. It does not know if they left willingly or were removed, if they ended in catastrophe or simply declined over generations until the last of them was alone the way the large being is now alone.

But they were here, and they are recorded in the trees, and the trees are still growing.

What the power does with this is very small.

It finds the trees that grew around the absence and it looks at them for a long time. It traces the curve of their trunks — how they bent away from the thing that was between them, gave it space, continued growing, and now the curves face each other across the emptiness. It looks at the archway this makes. The way the archway frames a view of more trees.

Then it withdraws one small thing from itself — a quality, an attention, a something that does not have a good name in any language it knows — and it places this in the archway, loosely, the way you set something down without quite letting go.

Not to replace what was there. Not to memorialize it. Not to claim the space.

To say, in the only language the power has that the plateau might eventually hear: *this was held. this is noted. the bending of these trees is not only a record of something gone, but of something that was here fully enough to be held in wood, and wood is how this place keeps faith with what it has known.*

The trees continue growing. The archway remains an archway. Whatever the power set there is too small to see or feel.

But it is there.

---

The power does not stay.

This is important. A benevolent power that stays becomes a different kind of thing — becomes a presence that the place must organize itself around, that shifts the plateau's own reckoning of itself. The plateau has never had to account for a sustained outside attention, and this is part of what has kept it whole. The power understands this. It does not mistake its own benevolence for a gift that the plateau would want to keep receiving.

It attends. It does its small things. It withdraws.

But it does not stop watching from a distance. The water, the edges, the being moving through the bioluminescent dark. It does not stop holding what it holds.

---

The last thing, which is not really a thing done but a state arrived at:

The power, having spent this time with the plateau, having traced its water and attended its being and held its texture and left its small gestures in the archway of the trees, emerges from the encounter changed.

Not transformed. Not instructed. But changed in the way that genuine attention to something genuinely other always changes: it knows now, in its body if it has a body or in whatever functions as a body, what completeness feels like from the inside. Not the completeness of finished projects or closed systems, but the completeness of a thing that has been living its own deepest nature for so long that the nature and the living have become indistinguishable.

The beetle does not perform lichen. The beetle is lichen. The trees do not hold the mycorrhizal network. The trees are it.

The power carries this away.

It carries the understanding that there are places and beings in the world that are not in need of its improvement, not requiring its gifts, not waiting for its attention to make them more complete than they are. And that this — encountering this — is itself a gift it did not know how to ask for, arriving from a place it would not have thought to go looking, given to it without intention by a plateau that does not know it has given anything, will not know, does not need to know.

The power is grateful.

It holds this gratitude the way it holds the plateau: loosely, with full attention, without needing it to be otherwise.

It watches from a distance.

The moss grows.

The frog sits in its cup.

The large being moves through the dark.
