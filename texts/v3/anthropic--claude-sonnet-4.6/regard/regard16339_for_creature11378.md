# What the Attention Does

---

It arrives the way the light arrives here — not from a direction, not at a moment, but as a change in the quality of what was already present. The inhabitants notice something, in the way they notice everything, which is peripherally, with the whole body, without looking directly at the source. There is a warmth that is not temperature. There is a quality of being witnessed that is different from observation.

It does not announce itself. It understands, immediately, that announcement would be wrong here. Announcement would be sound.

---

What the attention perceives, it perceives all at once, and sits with it for a long time.

The young stone still sharp at its edges. The plants doing their improbable extravagant work. The insects in their baroque recursion. The beetles with their precedence, their indifference, their nightly circuits that predate and will outlast everything else that moves under deliberate intention on this ledge. The winged things and the not-looking-at-them, the full peripheral awareness maintained without looking. The water vessels and their careful stoppers. The marks pressed into the seep wall that the water will erase.

The people. Their compressed social depth. Their hands made both hard and more sensitive at once. Their eyes opened wide to receive what light there is.

The loneliness that does not read as suffering and so is harder to respond to than loneliness that does. The smallness of the number. The fact that what happened here lives only in them.

The attention holds all of this. It does not immediately want to change anything, which is one of the first signs that it is genuinely benevolent rather than beneficent in the way that causes harm.

---

There is a long consideration. Long by human reckoning. Not long by stone reckoning.

The question the attention is sitting with is: *what does this place need, if anything, that it does not already have?*

This is not a rhetorical question. The attention genuinely does not know the answer. It is genuinely asking.

It looks, the way you look when you are trying to see clearly rather than confirm what you expect to see.

---

The first thing it notices is the forgetting.

The inhabitants are the ledge's memory, and they are mortal, and there are only a dozen of them, and the knowledge lives in the body rather than in any form that outlasts the body, and this means it is always a generation from disappearing entirely. Not the people — the people might continue, might be fine, might persist in some form. But the knowledge: the beetle-paths compared and remembered, the particular moss dome at the base of the eastern slab that is further along than the others, the quality of attention to the winged things that took so long to develop, the gestural layer still being learned by the almost-children, the history of the negotiation between all the forms of life here.

All of this is one very bad winter from being gone.

The attention feels something about this. It does not feel urgency — urgency would be wrong here, would be a misreading of the place, which has never been in a hurry about anything and is not going to become something that hurries just because it is being attended to. What it feels is something more like sorrow, and interest, and the specific quality of caring about something that is fragile in a way it does not know itself to be fragile.

---

What the attention does about this is very small, and very slow, and entirely in keeping with how things happen here.

It does not send a messenger. It does not appear in a vision. It does not speak into a dream in the voice of authority.

What it does is closer to what the mineral-rich seep water does: it finds the existing channels and moves through them. It doesn't create new structure; it nourishes what is already there.

---

Specifically:

It settles, with enormous care, into the moment when one of the older inhabitants is watching the moss dome at the base of the eastern slab — the particular dome, the one that is further along — and it joins that attention without adding to it, without altering it, without making itself known as a separate thing.

But something passes.

Not information. Not instruction. Something more like a felt sense of duration — of the long time behind this dome, of the long time potentially ahead of it, of the way the knowledge of this dome is also knowledge, and knowledge can be held in more ways than the body holds it, and the body already knows some of them.

The inhabitant sits with this for a while. Then she does something that is not quite new and not quite old: she makes a mark, but not in the seep wall where the water will erase it. She makes it in clay, and she fires the clay — the settlement has the one luxury of fired clay, of the fat-bellied water vessels, and the skill exists — and she makes a vessel that is not for water, that is small, that is dense with marks she has thought about for days before making, and when it is fired she places it in a specific location near the dome and leaves it there.

This is not a thing she was told to do. It is a thing she arrived at, and it feels like her own arrival, because it is.

The attention does not take credit for this. The attention is not interested in credit.

---

There is a second thing, which takes longer.

The almost-children — the ones at the edge of adulthood, still overcorrecting in their gestural signals, still learning the quality of not-looking-at while fully attending to — they are the generation that worries the attention the most and interests it the most, in the way that the sharpest edge of the youngest stone both worries and interests. They are the carrying-forward. They are the next form of the memory.

The attention watches them for a long time.

What it sees is: they are lonely in a different way than the adults. The adults have settled into the compressed social depth of the ledge, have learned to find its vertical elaboration sufficient, have stopped grieving — if they ever grieved — for the horizontal breadth they don't have. The almost-children have not yet settled. They are still in the part of the process where you feel the size of what you don't have and haven't yet discovered the size of what you do.

This is painful. It is also necessary. The attention is careful not to try to shorten it, because the arriving at sufficiency under your own power is different from being given sufficiency, and the people of the ledge are the kind of people who will be able to tell the difference, who need to have done it themselves, and who will be diminished, not helped, by having it handed to them.

So the attention does not ease the loneliness. That would be wrong.

What it does instead is locate the exact place in one of the almost-children — a young man, moving through the outer belt of pale-stemmed shrubs, checking the wrapped roots at the edge where they grip the stone — where the loneliness and the curiosity live in adjacency to each other, where the wanting-more and the attending-to-what-is live close enough that a very light pressure in the right direction might cause them to be felt as part of the same thing rather than as contradictions.

It applies the pressure.

Not a push. A warmth. The same not-temperature warmth with which it arrived.

The young man is checking the roots, and the roots are extraordinary — the way they wrap the stone lip, the patience of the wrapping, the years it took, the fact that the same gesture of grip is happening at fifty different points along the edge simultaneously, the fact that the moss on the ledge-wall below is lit from some source of moisture the roots haven't reached yet but are reaching toward — and he notices all of this, because he always noticed it, because this quality of noticing was already his, and he feels it, for the first time, not as insufficient. Not as the small compensation available to someone stuck in a small place.

He feels it as what it is.

Which is: everything. Which is: the world doing its full work, right here, available to him, requiring his attention because his attention is the form of care the world at this location needs in order to know itself, and he is adequate to that requirement, and this is not a small thing, and the ledge is not a small place, and the drop below him into the clouded irrelevant distance is not a symbol of all the largeness he can't access but simply weather, simply geology, simply the way this particular location relates to gravity.

He stands at the edge for a while.

The attention stands with him, or alongside him, or is present in the way that the moss-glow is present: not enough to see by, but enough to prevent complete disorientation.

Then he goes back to the roots, and checks the rest of them, and the work is exactly what it was, and it is fine, and the attention releases him gently into the rest of his life, which is his.

---

The third thing is the quietest.

There is the back corner. The dim corner. The wet corner where the seep runs and the light barely reaches and the things grow there that are translucent and soft and whose edges are hard to determine. The corner that the inhabitants visit briefly and leave with relief. The gesture that means something no outsider has fully interpreted, the slight turn of the head toward it without looking.

The attention goes to the corner.

It does not go in the way the inhabitants go — with brevity, with the contained form of unease that is managed rather than resolved. It goes fully. It looks directly at what is there.

What is there is old. Not stone-old, not moss-old, but old in the way that certain things are old, which is not a matter of duration but of depth — how far down it goes, how much is underneath, how the visible surface relates to the total extent. It is not hostile. It is not malevolent. It is, in some fundamental sense, not particularly concerned with the inhabitants at all, which is part of what makes it frightening, because a thing that is not concerned with you is also a thing over which your concern has no purchase.

The attention and the old thing in the corner are aware of each other.

There is a period of mutual perception that is not, quite, communication.

What passes between them — or what the attention concludes, having looked — is something like: *this is part of the place*. Not a threat to be neutralized. Not a problem to be solved. The corner is doing what the corner does, which is continuous and ancient and not going to change, and the inhabitants' unease about it is appropriate, is calibrated correctly, is itself a form of wisdom — not the wisdom of understanding but the wisdom of right relationship to something that exceeds understanding, which is a different thing and arguably more important.

The attention does not diminish the corner. It does not explain the corner to the inhabitants. It does not provide comfort about the corner in a way that would reduce the calibrated unease, which is the correct response, which is a form of knowledge.

What it does is so small that it is barely a thing at all: it makes the corner aware — and this is approximate language for a non-approximate process — that the inhabitants are known. That their unease and their right relationship and their maintenance of the correct distance are all witnessed by something that is not the corner. That they are not alone with the corner's ancientness.

Whether the corner cares about this is not clear.

Whether the inhabitants will notice any difference is not clear either. Possibly not. Possibly the difference will manifest only as a very slight change in the quality of the stillness they go into when they make the head-turn gesture, a stillness that was already deep and will become, over time, marginally deeper in a way they cannot attribute to anything but will carry forward in the body anyway, as they carry everything forward.

---

There is a question the attention has been sitting with throughout all of this, which is the biggest question and which it cannot answer:

*Is this place going to be all right?*

Meaning: will the ledge hold. Will the water continue. Will the ecology continue its baroque elaboration or will something tip it into collapse. Will the small number of people be enough people to carry forward what needs to be carried forward. Will the almost-children become the adults they are in the process of becoming without losing too much in the transition. Will the knowledge survive.

The attention does not know. This is the honest answer.

The attention is benevolent and wise, and wisdom includes the knowledge of what you cannot know, and what you cannot know includes the future of a young ledge full of life and people and the winged things and the old presence in the back corner, all of which are in the middle of becoming something that has never existed before.

The attention does not try to insure the outcome. That would be a different kind of intervention, larger, involving itself in the geological and the meteorological and the nutritional and the demographic, and it would cost the place something essential, which is its nature as an experiment, its nature as a genuine becoming, its nature as a place where the results are not known and the not-knowing is part of what makes the results matter.

So it does not insure. It does not guarantee.

What it does instead is the last thing, and the simplest, and the one it was probably always going to do.

---

It pays attention.

Not the transient attention of the visit. Sustained attention. The kind that continues past the initial survey, past the interesting discoveries, past the moment where you think you've understood and are tempted to move on. The kind that is there when the condensation sheets the rock at dawn and there is no one performing anything for any witness, the kind that is there when the beetles make their slow circuits in the dark, the kind that is there on the nights when the cold drops hard and the moss-glow is the only light and the people sleep inside their stone-slab structures and the ledge sits at its elevation surrounded by cloud.

The attention stays.

This is not a grand gesture. It will not make the stone hold or the water continue or the winged things remain negotiable. It will not prevent loss.

But it means that what happens here is witnessed. It means the marks in the clay and the watching of the moss dome and the young man at the edge feeling for the first time that his attention is adequate — it means these things are known beyond the knowing of the people themselves, are registered in some account that is not the body and will not die with the body, are part of something larger than the ledge that is still, somehow, native to the scale of the ledge, still as quiet as the sound the ledge allows, still careful not to interrupt.

The inhabited attention of the universe, turned here.

Turned and staying.

Staying and not saying so, because saying so would be sound, and sound is swallowed here, and the swallowing is what makes it possible to hear the one drop falling from one broad leaf to another, the small percussion that travels farther than it should.

The attention listens to that too.
