# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

The power becomes aware of the Green Town the way attention sometimes moves — not through effort but through something in the place calling out, some quality of *rightness* that registers across whatever scales of perception a benevolent and wise power uses. The bowl in the highlands. The sound of it. The smell. The particular turbulent equilibrium that has been running, self-sustaining, for longer than anyone has kept records.

The power pauses.

This is itself unusual. Most things the power attends to require something — intervention, correction, the redirecting of what has gone wrong back toward what could go right. The power has a long familiarity with what is broken and how breaking tends to proceed. It knows the grammar of damage. But the Green Town is not in that grammar. The Green Town is doing something else entirely.

So the power pauses, and looks, and listens, the way you pause when you hear music coming from an open window and it is exactly the music you didn't know you'd been hoping to hear.

---

The first thing the power feels is something that might be called grief, though grief is not quite right.

It is more like the feeling that comes when you encounter something rare and understand its rarity: a quality of *oh* and *oh no* and *how long* all at once. The power can see forward and sideways and along probabilities in ways that are difficult to describe, and what it sees along those axes around the Green Town is not catastrophe — not yet, not certainly — but the increased frequency of catastrophe-adjacent conditions: the shifting patterns of rainfall, the temperature changes that will come slowly enough to seem like weather and fast enough to constitute crisis for systems evolved to operate in a narrow band. The pressure from outside the bowl that doesn't feel like pressure until it does. The knowledge held in these people, in these hands, in the pattern of these lives — how fragile that transmission is, how completely it depends on continuity, on children who stay and learn rather than leave and forget.

The power does not see a doomed place. It sees a place that could be lost, which is different, but not as different as you'd want.

---

The second thing the power does, having felt that grief, is resist the urge to fix things.

This is harder than it sounds. A benevolent power that has spent long time among broken things develops a certain reflex toward intervention, and the reflex is not wrong exactly — intervention has mended much — but it is inappropriate here, in this place, in this way. Because the Green Town is not broken. What it has is precisely what the power most rarely encounters: *its own integrity*. The place has found its own logic, as you noted standing in it. The moss holds the floor together. The wren extracts its things from the wall. The people's knowledge is in their hands and not anywhere else, which means it lives only in use, which is the truest way knowledge lives.

To intervene in a system of intact integrity is to risk the integrity. The power knows this. A wise power learns this first, or learns it after the mistakes that only a power capable of mistakes can make, which are the worst kind of mistakes, mistakes that come from good intentions and overwhelming capability and insufficient respect for what is already working.

The bowl does not need the power to stand in the middle of it and improve things.

---

What the power does instead is this:

It *pays attention*.

This sounds small. It is not small. The attention of a benevolent and wise power is not a passive thing, not simply looking. It is more like — when you have a friend who is truly listening to you, not waiting to speak, not half-elsewhere, but fully present to what you are saying, and you can feel the difference between that and the half-listening you usually receive — it is like that, but extended to a place and its whole system, to every component of the running process, to the mycelium and the moss and the voles and the wren and the children learning the frog-catch technique and the elder who knows which change in a seep's behavior means something is shifting in the hillside above.

The power learns the Green Town. Fully. The way the place itself knows its own patterns, but from outside, and all at once, and with the longer view — the deep history of the bowl, the species that have come and gone, the droughts that stressed the system and the floods that altered it and the slow recoveries, the generations of people and what they knew and what they lost and what they rebuilt, the threads of continuity and the breaks in them and how the breaks were survived.

This attention is not surveillance. It does not feel like watching. If anything it feels like being deeply, quietly known — the sensation you get in a place that has been loved for a long time, a quality in the air, a sense that the walls have absorbed the attention paid to them and give it back as something indefinably warmer than the sum of materials. The power's attention does not change the Green Town. But the Green Town, attended to, continues with something it did not have before: the quality of being witnessed.

---

Having learned the place, the power acts three times.

---

**First action:**

There is a woman at the edge of the settlement, neither old nor young, who has been carrying for several years a knowledge that is beginning to die in her. She learned it from her grandmother, and her grandmother learned it from her grandmother, and it concerns a particular technique for reading the hillside above the bowl for signs of what the winter will be — not prediction exactly, more like a kind of reading, an interpretation of certain moss formations and the behavior of specific plants at the bowl's rim and the depth of particular seeps in early autumn that amounts, after long practice, to a useful anticipation of severity.

She has tried to teach it. She has found no one who will stay still for it, who will come to the rim with her in the right season with the right quality of patience. The knowledge requires patience to transmit that is different from the patience it requires to hold. She has not found the right apprentice and she is not sure the right apprentice exists among the children currently in the settlement.

The power does not manufacture an apprentice or alter anyone's nature. What it does is — harder to describe. It adjusts the angle of a moment. There is a morning in the coming autumn when the woman will go to the rim alone as she does each year, and on that morning a child who has been restless and difficult and unable to sit will follow her out of restlessness rather than interest, out of the need to be moving rather than the desire to learn, and will find, at the rim, in the specific quality of that morning's light on those specific mosses, something that catches. Something that makes the child still. The power does not cause this — the child's particular sensory nature was always going to respond to something in this way, and the mosses were always going to be that particular quality in that light, and the woman was always going to go on that morning. What the power does is hold these things in slight proximity. Introduce no new variables. Simply ensure that what might have missed each other does not miss.

The knowledge will be transmitted. The thread will continue.

---

**Second action:**

There is a young man who is going to leave. He has decided this already, privately, in the way people decide things privately before they know they've decided them. He is going to leave because he feels the bowl's smallness as constriction and cannot yet see it as what it is. He wants the larger world, the noise of it, the possibility-feeling of places where more is happening, or where more is visible to be happening.

The power does not stop him.

The power knows — having paid attention, having learned the long history — that the knowledge this community holds has survived not only by staying but also by sending out and occasionally receiving back. There are threads in the history of this place that run through a person leaving and returning changed, and the change they bring back is a kind of cross-fertilization, a testing of what the home-knowledge means against a wider frame. There are also threads where the person leaves and does not return, and the thread is cut. Both are part of the history. Both are part of how a community navigates time.

The power looks at this young man and does not intervene in his leaving. Instead it does something stranger: it ensures that wherever he goes, he carries the smell of the place. Not literally, not as a reproach or a haunting, but as a baseline reference, a way of knowing what *living system* smells like when everything is working. This will not be enough to bring him back. It may not do anything he can name or notice. But it will make him, in whatever other places he lives, slightly more likely to notice when a system is intact, slightly more inclined toward the conditions that produce that smell, slightly more likely — if he does return, or if he simply makes decisions in other places — to understand what is worth protecting and why.

He will not know the power did this. It will feel, to him, like memory.

---

**Third action:**

This one is the largest and the most indirect and it does not act on the Green Town at all.

It acts on the people who do not know the Green Town exists.

There are people — in positions of various kinds of authority, with various kinds of reach over decisions that shape the conditions outside the bowl — who will in the coming years make choices that are not about the Green Town but that will affect the highland hydrology, the land management of the surrounding moorland, the economic conditions that shape whether young people stay in places like this or leave them, the policies around the cultural transmission of practical ecological knowledge, the question of whether what is held in a community like this one is recognized as knowledge at all or is dismissed as pre-modern irrelevance.

These people mostly mean well, or mean something. They are not villains. They are making decisions with the information they have, and the information they have does not include the Green Town, or places like it, or the precise value of what is held in the hands of a woman who can read a hillside for winter.

The power cannot tell them. It is not that kind of power — it does not appear to people and speak in their ears (or: it could, but it has learned that this rarely works, that people generally accommodate miraculous information within the existing framework of their beliefs rather than actually changing what they do, and that the more useful work is always in the conditions rather than the contents of minds). So instead it creates small encounters. A researcher who will spend enough time in a wet highland environment to understand it from the inside. A documentary made with sufficient patience and sufficiently good listening to actually transmit something of the knowledge rather than aestheticizing it. A policy discussion where, by happenstance of who is in the room on a particular day, a different consideration becomes visible. A school curriculum that is not about the Green Town but that opens a door in a child that stays open.

None of these are sufficient. The power knows they are not sufficient. The threats are real and the momentum of the larger world is its own kind of turbulent system and one wise power attending carefully to one small wet bowl in the highlands cannot turn that system. The power does not fool itself about this.

But the power has been doing this kind of work for a long time. It knows about small adjustments and compound interest. It knows about the child who followed the woman to the rim because one morning was held in proximity to another morning, and how the knowledge transmitted there will shape what that child does in forty years with whatever authority and reach they will then have. It knows the young man who left and carried the smell of a working system with him and made slightly different decisions over a lifetime than he would have otherwise. It knows the researcher who spent a wet autumn in a highland environment and wrote something that a policy-maker read at the right moment.

This is the work. It is slow and it is not guaranteed and it requires attending, always attending, always learning the particular systems in their particularity, the specific moss on the specific stone in the specific light, the specific knowledge in the specific hands, and finding the small proximity-adjustments that are not interventions but invitations, not impositions but the holding-open of doors that were already there.

---

The power does one more thing, which is not an action at all.

It remains.

It does not visit and leave. The Green Town goes into the power's long attention and stays there — not as a problem to be solved or a project to be managed but as something closer to love, which is to say as something that requires nothing from the beloved but presence, that asks only that the thing continue to be what it is, that asks only for the moss and the frogs and the wren and the woman at the rim and the child who found stillness there and the water finding the next lower surface.

The power holds the Green Town in mind the way you hold a place in mind that has meant something to you: not grasping, not possessing, but *with*. The way presence is a kind of accompaniment.

The seep moves. The mycelia exchange what they have for what they need. The light comes down through the layers of leaves and arrives green and diffuse and full of life's history, and the moss, which does not know it is attended to and does not need to know, continues to hold the floor of the world together.

The power rests in that.

It is enough to be here with this. It is enough to witness that such a thing exists and runs and holds. There is, in encountering a living system at full expression, something that exceeds the need to act — something that asks only for adequate response, which is attention, is learning, is the willingness to be changed by what you witness.

The wren calls from the wall.

The power listens, and is glad.
