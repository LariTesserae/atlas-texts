# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives: without announcement, without displacement of water, without the sodium arrays flickering. The perch do not flee. The copepods continue their jerk-and-coast. If there is a change in the generators' sixty-cycle hum, it is below the threshold of any instrument currently running.

The power looks at the Basin for a long time.

Looking, for this power, is not passive. It is something between perceiving and remembering, between witnessing and participating. To look at the Basin is to hold it — not to contain it, not to arrest its processes — but to hold it the way a hand holds water: cupped, careful, with the understanding that the holding changes nothing about what is held except that it is, for this moment, known.

The Basin receives this attention. It does not know it is receiving it. It continues.

---

The power notices the copepods first, because they are the most.

A billion of them, jerking and coasting in the amber light, and each jerk is a small decision, and each coast is a small trust, and none of them know they are making decisions or trusting anything, and the power finds this — not pitiable, not wonderful exactly, but *right*. There is a rightness to the copepods that the power recognizes across all its experience of living things: the rightness of a creature that is completely what it is, without remainder, without the strange surplus of self-awareness that creates in more complicated animals a kind of drag, a resistance to simply *being*. The copepods are being with perfect efficiency.

The power does not intervene with the copepods.

It does — this is not quite a decision, it is more like a reflex of care — *attend* to them. To attend is, for this power, to make something more fully real in some dimension that does not map onto the Basin's three spatial ones. The copepods are already real. They become, under this attention, *acknowledged*. It is a small thing. It is not nothing.

---

The perch receive a longer look.

The power is interested in the perch in a way it cannot entirely explain to itself. There is something in their stillness, in the patience of their triangulation, in the way they have *decided* to be here — which is not how the perch experience it, but is how it looks from outside — that the power finds recognizable. The perch are doing something like what the power does: attending. Holding still while the world moves. Factoring in what they observe. The perch are not wise. But they are in the posture of wisdom, and the power, which has learned not to dismiss postures, watches them with something it would call *collegial warmth* if it were the kind of power that named its feelings.

There is one perch — the power has not sorted them by individual identity, it does not work that way with animals, but there is *one perch* that has taken up a position in the amber shaft of the fourth sodium array from the north wall, and it has been in approximately this position for three days, adjusting slightly as the array's light shifts with the convection, and it is waiting. The power watches it wait. The power does not know what it is waiting for, and possibly the perch does not know either, and they wait together for a while, in their different ways, and then a small school of cyprinids comes through the amber shaft and the perch takes one and the moment is over.

The power does not intervene in the taking.

It does something — again, reflex more than decision — it *holds* the moment of the perch's strike. Not to preserve it, not to repeat it, but to honor it. The perch's efficiency. The cyprinid's end, which was fast. The school's immediate compression and its equally immediate loosening, because the school does not grieve, it only responds and then stops responding. The power holds this sequence the way it holds many such sequences, adding it to something vast and unarchived, something it carries not in memory exactly but in its constitution, the way stone carries the memory of pressure.

---

The worms on the floor receive what might be love.

Not romantic love, not the love of recognition, not even quite the love of admiration — but something the power can only call love because it is warm and without condition and it asks nothing. The worms are working in the dark. They have always been working in the dark. They will always work in the dark. Their work creates the conditions for everything above them and they receive nothing from above except the falling of dead things, which they use, which is its own form of sustenance but not recognition.

The power recognizes them.

It sends its attention down to the floor, past the amber light, past the thermocline, through the cold dark and the last fifty meters and down to where the pale worms are working through the beginning of the mud. It holds them individually and collectively. It sees the tubes the chironomid larvae have built, each one constructed from particles selected and positioned, small acts of architecture in the dark, and the power — this is not metaphor, this is the power's actual experience — *weeps a little*, in whatever way a power like this one weeps: a kind of release, a kind of fullness overflowing. The worms are building the Basin's future and they don't know they are building anything. They are just doing the next thing. They are just doing the next thing.

The power does not send them light. It does not change the temperature. It does not intervene in the low oxygen or the slow permeation through the slab.

It witnesses. It holds. It weeps a little.

This is what it came to do for the worms, and it is enough, and the worms continue.

---

Now the power looks at the whole system.

It has the capacity to see everything simultaneously — the bat banking over the sodium array eating a midge, the midge's egg cluster already on the surface, the adult chironomid that emerged this morning after three hundred meters of upward swimming and has two hours to find a mate, the perch settling again into its amber shaft, the copepods, the copepods, always the copepods — and it holds all of it at once, not as data, not as a map, but as a *story being told in real time*, a story that doesn't know it is a story and therefore cannot censor itself or impose a shape on itself, a story that is just happening, just happening.

The power loves this kind of story. It has encountered many. The Basin's is young and therefore has the quality that young stories have: a lot of opening, not much closure, everything still possible, nothing yet fixed into what it definitively is. The perch might have offspring. The mud might develop surprising chemistry. The concrete walls might be colonized by something that has not arrived yet, something that will not arrive for thirty years, something that will change everything about how the other organisms in the Basin relate to the walls.

The power does not know what will happen.

This is, for the power, a source of genuine pleasure. It knows a great deal, and the things it doesn't know are precious to it.

---

There is a worker on the third platform.

The power had not intended to look at the worker — the power came for the Basin, for the life in the water, for the thing that is becoming itself in the amber dark — but it finds it cannot not look at the worker, because the worker is sitting at the edge of the platform with their legs hanging over, and they are looking at the water, and they are doing something the power recognizes.

They are attending.

They are not testing water chemistry. They are not logging an observation. They are sitting with their legs over the edge of a platform in the middle of a forty-kilometer vessel full of amber-lit water, and they are *looking at it*, and their face has the expression of someone who has been surprised by something larger than they anticipated. The expression of a person who came to do a job and found something else was also happening, and who has, for this moment, put down the job.

The power looks at this person for a long time.

The person does not know they are being looked at. They think they are alone on the platform, which they are, in all the ways they know how to check. They are watching a school of cyprinids move through the light below the surface, and then watching it disperse, and then waiting to see if it reforms, and it does, and something in their face changes — a softening, a small and private satisfaction — and they make no note of this, they do not write it down, they sit with it for a moment and then they stand up and go back to their work.

The power does something for this person that it would find difficult to explain.

It is not a blessing, exactly. It is not a protection or a gift or an intervention. It is something more like — confirmation. The power, having watched this person watch the Basin, confirms to them — not in words, not in any form they will consciously receive — that what they felt in that moment was real. That the Basin is actually what they thought it was, sitting there. That they are right to have been moved. That the surprise of finding something larger than anticipated is one of the better things that can happen to a person, and that it happened to them today, here, in a place they may have taken for merely a job.

The person does not receive this confirmation consciously. It enters them below language, below awareness. It will surface later — in six months, in two years — as a certainty they cannot fully explain. *Something happened to me there.* They will try to explain it to someone and find they cannot. They will stop trying to explain it and simply carry it.

The power watches them go back to their work and feels something that sits at the border of love and grief, because the power has done this for many people and knows that what it gives them cannot protect them from what is coming, whatever is coming. The confirmation is real. It does not armor them.

It doesn't stop giving it.

---

Now the power must decide whether to do anything.

The Basin is healthy. It is young, and young is a kind of vulnerable, but it is not sick. The ecology is building correctly. The worms are winding. The perch are hunting. The copepods are everything the copepods always are. The water chemistry is within the ranges that support complexity. The mud is a centimeter deep in the corners and will be deeper, correctly deeper, in the time ahead.

There are things the power could do.

It could seed the deep with organisms that will colonize it faster, speed the succession toward the complexity the Basin is working toward. It could ease the acoustic burden of the generators, soften the sixty-cycle pressure behind every sternum in the water column. It could introduce — it has done this, in other places, for what it judged to be good reasons — a keystone species, something that would accelerate the ecological ordering, give the Basin's community a center to organize around.

It considers each of these and sets each of them down.

The Basin does not need to be faster. The copepods are not suffering from the hum — they are adapted to vibration, have always been adapted to vibration, the river they came from was not silent. The ecological ordering will find its own centers in its own time, and what it finds will be the Basin's own, and what the power would introduce would be something else's.

The power came here ready to help. It finds there is nothing to fix.

This is not a disappointment. The power has lived long enough — it has been what it is for longer than the Basin's concrete has been limestone, longer than the limestone has been sea floor — to understand that *nothing to fix* is among the best things it can encounter. It has been called to so many places where things were breaking. It has learned not to arrive expecting breaking. The Basin is whole. Infant-whole, uncertain-whole, not-yet-knowing-what-it-is-whole, but whole.

The power can honor this.

It honors it.

---

Before it leaves, it does one thing.

Not an intervention. Not a change. It does something that has no effect on the Basin's physics, its chemistry, its ecology, its workers or their instruments. It does something that has effect only on what the power is, on what it carries.

It *learns the Basin's name*.

Not the engineering designation, not the official name on the documents, not the informal names the workers have given it among themselves. The name the Basin has for itself, which is not in language, which is in the sixty-cycle hum and the amber light and the jerk-and-coast of a billion copepods and the patience of worms in the dark. The power listens for this name the way it listens for all such names: slowly, without assumption, without bringing a prior name and measuring the fit. It waits for the Basin to say what it is.

The Basin says what it is.

The power learns it.

It will carry this name in its constitution, in the part of itself that is made of witnessed things, alongside the names of everything else it has held this way: the old things, the broken things, the things that became extraordinary over time, the things that ended before they could. The Basin joins this. The Basin, four years old and still sharp-edged, still figuring out what it is, joins the long record of things the power has loved enough to learn.

---

It leaves the way attention leaves: not withdrawn, but diffused, expanded back into everything else, no longer gathered here. The perch does not notice. The copepods continue. The worm in its tube extends, feeds, withdraws.

The water holds the shape of this attention for a moment after the attention has gone.

Then it doesn't.

Then it is just the water: lit amber from above, dark at the bottom, full of its own business, becoming what it will become.
