# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives without arriving.

There is no descent, no disturbance of the thin mist rising from the bacterial mats, no perturbation of the standing water. The frogs do not fall silent — this is the first important thing. Nothing flees. The sound continues exactly as it was, which is to say: total, ancient, self-sufficient. The power moves through the flat the way attention moves through a sleeping mind: present everywhere, located nowhere, leaving the dreamed thing dreaming.

It takes a long time before it does anything, because the first thing it does — the thing that takes the longest — is *listen*.

Not to understand. It already understands, in the way that deep and patient things understand: not by analysis but by a kind of resonance, a recognition that moves through the whole of its attention at once. It listens because the sound deserves to be listened to by something capable of knowing what it is. Because the flat has been speaking its own name into empty air for ten thousand years, or a hundred thousand, and while this has required no witness to be true, to be real, to be complete — still, there is something that happens when a thing of that magnitude is *heard* by something that can hold the hearing. The sound does not change. But it is received now, fully, by something with the capacity to receive it. Something that understands: this is not background. This is the primary text. This is the oldest continuous sentence being spoken in this part of the world, and it says *we are here we are here we are here* in frequencies that range from the sternum to the limit of hearing and it has never once stopped to wonder if anyone was listening.

The power listens.

It takes what might be called a long time, though time runs differently in this kind of attending.

---

Then it turns its attention to specifics, because that is what benevolence requires — not the general blessing, which is easy and means little, but the specific regard.

It finds the frog at the edge of the clay pan. The one that has been sitting in its particular motionlessness for twenty minutes, horizontal pupil reading sky and water simultaneously, the small heart beating its patient measure. The power looks at this one frog the way you look at something when you are really looking: not cataloguing, not evaluating, but simply being fully present to what is there.

The frog is perfect.

This is not a sentimental judgment. It is an exact one. The frog is a perfect solution to this particular problem of being alive in this particular place. The color is right. The eye is right. The skin's chemistry is right. The stillness-that-is-readiness is right. The way the body sits with its weight distributed toward the front, ready for the sudden lateral launch, is the result of millions of iterations of the problem and this is the answer, and the answer is exactly fitted to the question, and the question is: *how do you live here, in this ancient gray moisture, under this sealed sky, in this total sound?* And the answer is: like this. Exactly like this.

The power does not intervene in the frog. There is nothing to fix. There is nothing missing. The frog lacks nothing that the frog needs, and to give it something it doesn't need would be to make it less itself, which is the opposite of benevolence. True benevolence, the power knows, is not addition. Sometimes it is simply *witness* — the act of seeing something fully and registering it as real, as worthy, as having weight in the universe's long accounting.

*I see you,* the power says, though not in words, and not to the frog, which would not receive such a thing and does not need to.

It says it to itself. It says it as a commitment: *this has been seen. This small clay-colored wet-skinned calling life. This perfect answer to this ancient question. I have seen it. It counts.*

The frog does not know this has happened. The frog launches sideways and is briefly in the air and is gone, because something moved at the wrong distance in the wrong direction, and then the water rings and closes and the frog is under it, watching the sky through four inches of gray water, waiting for the world to resolve.

---

The power turns to the structures.

The stones. The collapsed weaving.

Here it pauses longer, because here there is something that requires more than witness. These are the marks of a presence that is gone, and a presence that is gone raises a different kind of question.

It moves around the stone stacks, attending to them with the same quality of full regard. It notes the sourcing: these stones came from far away. Someone carried them. Someone thought: *this place should be marked, and I will carry the marking material from a distance, and the difficulty of the carrying will be part of the meaning.* Or perhaps did not think this — perhaps only felt it, the way the body sometimes knows what the mind hasn't formed yet. The stones are not impressive. They were never impressive. They were never meant to impress the landscape, which cannot be impressed. They were meant to make something true: *a person was here. A person stood on this flat and heard the total sound and felt the ancient ground flex underfoot and decided to leave a mark.* The mark is still here. The stones have not moved since they were placed, except the one that the slow ground-movement tilted.

The power does something small with this one.

Not to restore it — restoration implies that the tilted state is wrong, is a failure, and the power has no interest in that judgment. It simply... attends to the stone's own inclination. The stone has been trying, with whatever slow intention geology and placement and the weight of time together constitute, to become something. The power assists this. By what means is not the kind of thing that translates into description. But the stone settles, slightly, into a position that is more fully what it has been becoming: not upright, not flat, but the particular angle that is *its* angle, the one it arrived at through the long negotiation between its placement and the ground's movement. The one that, if you looked at it, you might think: *that stone has purpose. That stone knows what it is doing.* Even if you could not say why you thought so.

This is all. The power does not restore the structures to their original state, because their original state was not more true than their current state — it was simply earlier. Things that have been worn by time into new shapes are not failures. They are continuing. The collapsed weaving is not a ruined shelter. It is a shelter that has finished being a shelter and become something else: a record, a texture, a small brown shape that the clay is slowly, slowly accepting into itself. The power regards this process with something that is, in its vocabulary, the equivalent of appreciation. The weaving will become clay. The clay has already become frogs. The frogs will become clay again. Nothing is lost. Nothing needs to be restored.

What the power does, there at the site of the collapsed weaving, is smaller still: it spends a long moment with what can only be called *the feeling of the people who made it.* Not reconstruction — the specifics are gone and should stay gone, the specifics are not the point. But the feeling: someone was cold. Someone was tired in the specific way of people who have walked a long distance carrying stones. Someone sat in this low made-place and heard the sound that has always been here and was, for a moment, neither frightened nor exalted but simply *located.* Here. Present. In this enormous noise, in this gray light, on this floor that the world found after the long erosion — located. Small, and cold, and tired, and located.

The power holds this.

Then releases it, carefully, into the air above the collapsed weaving, where it rises a foot, two feet, and is absorbed, the way the wisps of mist from the bacterial mats rise and are absorbed, adding their small moisture to the air that holds everything.

---

For a long time after this, the power moves across the flat without purpose, which is the same as moving with total purpose.

It moves through the insect clouds, the ones that make the high shimmer and complete the upper register. It is present in the wakes the long water-animals leave crossing the standing pools. It moves beneath the clay skin, down into the soft pockets and water-lenses, through the paths the worm-things have made over generations, the paths that are partly natural and partly the record of all the bodies that have moved through them. It goes down to where the clay is near-ceramic, old beyond the reach of biological memory, laid down in a geology that preceded all of this — the mats, the frogs, the structures, the sound — by an order of magnitude, and it simply *rests there*, for a moment, in the oldest material available, in the floor of the floor.

What it is doing: nothing that has a name. A kind of gratitude that runs the wrong direction for gratitude — not the gratitude of the received-for toward the giver, but something a giver might feel toward a place that required nothing, that was already whole, that offered the rare opportunity to practice the hardest discipline of benevolence:

*Leaving something alone because it is already what it should be.*

---

Before it withdraws — and withdrawal is the wrong word, but approach is also the wrong word, so withdrawal will do — it does one last thing.

It listens again, one more time, to the full sound. The whole texture. Every frog, every insect-shimmer, every close plop and distant trill and low resonant throb that you feel in the sternum before you hear it with the ear. It takes the whole thing in, all at once, the way you sometimes look at a landscape and feel for a moment that you have actually seen it — not scanned it, not processed it, but received it completely, in its full spatial presence, in its actual weight. A thing you could carry. A thing that happened to you.

The power carries it.

This is not preservation — the flat does not need to be preserved by anything other than itself, which has been preserving itself for longer than preservation has been a concept. It is not a record. It is more like: the sound goes on forever into an air that does not care, and now it has also gone, once, into something that does. That cared enough to come here and do nothing. That had the wisdom to understand that doing nothing, here, was the fullest possible expression of care.

---

The mist rises from the warm bacterial mats.

The water mirrors the sealed sky.

The clay sits in its billion drying-cycles' worth of craze, each crack a record, all of them together a script too vast and too ancient to be read.

The frogs call.

They will call tonight, and in ten years, and in ten thousand years, their descendants in the same water over the same clay under the same gray light, making the same ancient assertion into the same unlistening air —

except that now the air has listened, once, fully, and this has changed nothing about the air and nothing about the sound, and the power that listened has no monument here and left no mark except the small adjustment to one tilted stone, which might have happened anyway, which the slow ground-movement was already working toward, and this is —

this is exactly right.

The sound continues.
