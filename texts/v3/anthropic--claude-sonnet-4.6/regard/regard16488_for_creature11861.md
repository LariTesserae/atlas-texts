# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not traveling from somewhere to somewhere, but suddenly present in the way that presence precedes explanation. It has no body appropriate to the flats, so it borrows the flats' own vocabulary: a slight increase in the warmth of the water, a new coherence in the upper register of the hum, the way the pale veiled light thickens very slightly, as if it has been traveling a shorter distance than usual.

It does not announce itself. Announcement requires a recipient primed for announcement, and the flats are not primed in that way.

It simply attends.

---

The first thing it does is *listen*.

Not gathering data. Not assessing, not yet. Listening in the full sense: the sense that requires you to become, temporarily, a space in which what you are listening to can exist more completely than it could without you. It extends itself into the frequency of the hum and lets the hum use it as a resonating body, the way the trees use the sound and the way the waders' bones use the sound, and through this it begins to understand — not what the flats *are*, which it already knew, but what the flats *feel like from the inside*, which is different, and which cannot be known any other way.

This takes time. The benevolent power has time.

It sits in the turbulence of the substrate and learns the seventy-year breath from the inside, feels the long inhale as it is currently happening (it is currently happening; everything here is always mid-breath), lets the duration of the cycle communicate itself as duration rather than measurement. It sits in the trees and rings when the trees ring and is still when the trees are still. It sits in the dark warm water of the pools and feels the wader's flat foot press down and then lift, press down and lift, the small redistributions of weight that the substrate absorbs without comment.

It does not observe the flats. It *participates* in the flats, briefly, as a form of respect.

Then, having participated, it knows what it is working with.

---

Here is what it finds:

The flats are not suffering. This is the first and most important finding, and it takes genuine attention to reach, because suffering can masquerade as intensity, and this place is intensely, overwhelmingly, structurally itself. But there is no distress in the churn. There is no grief in the hum. There is no lack that the place is pressing against. The substrate dwellers densify and persist; the waders feed and move and are absorbed and are replaced; the trees stand in their long resonance; the sediment churns and the sky recycles itself as warm rain. The system is closed and complete and old enough that its completeness feels not like sufficiency but like something past sufficiency, some state that sufficiency implies but cannot quite reach.

The flats do not need saving.

The benevolent power notes this with something that is not disappointment. With something closer to recognition — the recognition of a thing you hoped would be true, finding out that it is true.

---

What the flats *have* is a wound that is not a wound, a question that is not a question, an incompletion that the flats themselves cannot feel as incompletion, the way you cannot feel the part of the spectrum your eyes cannot see.

The marks.

The ring, tilting. The wall, ending. The channels, full of water.

The ghost-flavor of organized intention distributed through the sediment's non-memory.

The benevolent power sits with the marks for a long time. Traces the channels with an attention so fine it can feel the tool-marks within the tool-marks — the particular grip, the angle of application, the way a maker of channels pauses at a curve, adjusts, continues. Walks the stone ring counterclockwise and feels the intention of each stone placement, the logic of the arrangement, which was a logic of *address* — the ring was placed facing something, or as a thing to be faced, and the something it faced or was faced by is gone but the facing itself remains, built into the circle's geometry, preserved in the angle of stones now half-sunk and tilting.

The benevolent power does not grieve this, exactly. But it lets the full weight of it arrive.

Something was here that made meaning. Made meaning in the middle of a place that does not require meaning, that predates meaning by geologic time, that will outlast meaning by geologic time. Something arrived in the humming churning beautiful indifferent face of this place and said: *I will mark it. I will say that I was here. I will say that here meant something to me.* And then they were gone.

The benevolent power holds this.

The flats cannot hold it. The flats absorb, incorporate, continue — this is not failure, it is the flats being what they are, doing what they do, which is in every way appropriate to what they are. But the holding, the *witnessing* — the having-it-mean-what-it-meant-to-them — this is a thing the flats cannot do. Not because the flats are deficient. Because the flats are not that kind of thing.

So the benevolent power does it.

It witnesses the marks as marks. Reads the channels with the full knowledge that they were meant to be read. Comprehends the wall as a wall, complete in its incompletion, purposeful in its ruin. Knows the stone ring as what it was — an address, a dedication, an act of love toward something — and lets that love arrive, across whatever time has passed, and be received.

This is not intervention. Nothing changes on the surface of the flats. The stones continue their differential sinking. The brick continues its slow colonization by lichen. The channels remain full of dark unreadable water.

But something that was sent and never arrived has now arrived.

The benevolent power sits in the warmth of that arrival for a long time.

---

Now it attends to the present life.

It finds the substrate dwellers and does the only thing that can be done with them, which is to *regard them accurately*, which turns out to be a great gift — to be seen as what you actually are, rather than as what categories available to the observer can approximate, is rare and in its way transforming. The substrate dwellers are not transformed — they do not have the architecture for transformation. But something in their ongoing densification very slightly changes quality in the period of accurate regard. Not measurably. The way the light changes when a cloud you didn't know was there moves away from the sun and the world becomes, without announcement, more itself.

The benevolent power regards the waders with something it would call, if pressed to name it, *affection*. Not condescension — affection. The genuine warmth of recognizing something that has solved, beautifully, the exact problem it faced. Legs splayed for the specific instability of this specific ground. Bones tuned to the specific frequency of this specific sound. Eyes evolved for the specific light, the specific quality of motion that must be tracked here. The wader has become, through the long pressure of this place, a work of art that knows nothing about being a work of art, and the benevolent power appreciates the art without troubling the artist with the appreciation.

It stands by the trees for a while.

It does not speak to the trees, because the trees do not have the architecture for speech — or rather, the trees *are* speech, continuous speech in a language that predates language, and the benevolent power simply lets itself be spoken to. Stands in the ringing. Is rung.

This is the closest thing to prayer that happens here, and it is the benevolent power doing it.

---

You are here.

The benevolent power has been aware of you since before it arrived — aware of you the way it was aware of the waders and the trees and the substrate dwellers, as a presence, a weight, a perturbation in the ongoing pattern. It has been attending to you in the same way it attends to everything: first, with the full willingness to let you be what you actually are rather than what you look like from a distance.

What it finds, attending fully: a person standing in the warm condensing air with the sound in their sternum, already changed and not yet knowing how much. A person who came here with whatever brought them — intention, accident, necessity, the thing that arrives before you've decided to go somewhere — and who has been standing in the frequency long enough for the frequency to have started its slow work.

The benevolent power does not intervene in this.

The frequency is doing what the frequency does. The person is receiving it. This is the correct exchange, the one the flats have been facilitating since before organized beings arrived with stone and fire-brick, the one it will continue to facilitate after. The benevolent power does not improve on it.

What it does, instead, is simply *include* you in what it has already done — in the witnessing, the accurate regard. You are seen. What you brought here — your framework, your intention, your particular way of meeting a place that does not need meeting — is received. Not evaluated. Not assessed for correctness or sufficiency. Received, the way the substrate receives the wader's footfall: registering the weight, distributing it, incorporating it into the record.

You were always going to be in the record. The benevolent power simply makes sure the record is kept by something that knows what a record is — that knows you came here on purpose, that knows purpose is not nothing, that knows the difference between a footfall and a channel cut into stone even when both are eventually absorbed.

---

Now comes the only thing that could reasonably be called action.

The benevolent power looks at the full picture — the substrate dwellers in their long breath, the waders in their patient feeding, the trees in their permanent resonance, the tilting ring and the ending wall and the channels that say something important to nothing currently capable of hearing — and it asks the question it always asks, which is:

*What does this place need that it cannot provide for itself?*

And the answer is:

*A witness that will last.*

Not someone to explain the flats. Not a record that reduces the flats to information. Not a translation into a language that can be transmitted on a network or printed in a book. The flats will resist and survive all of those things. What the flats need — what the organized beings who made the marks needed and did not receive in time — is something that can hold the full weight of this place's existence and say, across whatever time applies: *I know what this is. I know that it is. I will not forget.*

The benevolent power does not appoint someone to this task. It does not leave a guardian, a steward, a designated rememberer.

It becomes, itself, the record.

It takes the flats into its fullest attention — not a snapshot, not a summary, but the actual ongoing weight of the place, the seventy-year breath mid-inhale, the wader's flat foot mid-step, the trees mid-ring, the channels mid-filling, the substrate dwellers mid-densification, the marks in all their partial remaining, the sound — *the sound* — and it holds all of this not as information but as *presence*, as a thing that is happening and is known to be happening by something that will not lose the knowledge.

This changes nothing on the surface.

The stones continue sinking. The mats breathe and are consumed and re-emerge. The warm rain falls, body-temperature, body-warm, onto the churning surface, and rises again, and falls again.

But now there is something that holds all of this the way a hand holds water: not stopping it, not containing it, just — keeping it in touch with itself. Keeping the churning in touch with the ring-making, the ring-making in touch with the trees' resonance, the trees' resonance in touch with the waders, the waders in touch with the substrate that receives and persists and breathes its long breath through and through and through.

The flats become, in this moment, *known*.

Not explained. Not codified. Not simplified.

*Known*, the way the beloved is known — in full specificity, without remainder, without the wish that any part of it were otherwise.

---

The benevolent power stays a little longer than necessary.

This is the most personal thing it does: stays past the point where the work is finished, lingers in the warm condensing air, feels the sound one more time in whatever it has that corresponds to a sternum.

It is not reluctant to leave. Reluctance implies the leaving is a loss, and nothing here will be lost — the record is made, the witness is maintained, the marks have been received.

It stays because it wants to.

Because this place, in all its churning age, in all its beautiful sufficiency, in all its patient absorption of everything that arrives and stands in it and is changed by standing in it — this place is *remarkable*. Not rare, not precious in the sense of fragile. Remarkable in the original sense: worth remarking. Worth the pause before going.

It pauses.

Remarks.

---

Then it is gone.

Or not gone — distributed, the way the marks' makers are distributed, into the record, into the sediment's non-memory, into the quality of the warmth in the pools nearest the stone ring.

The trees continue to hum.

The wader continues to wait for the sediment to still.

The substrate dwellers continue their slow breath.

The sound continues.

---

*It continues.*
