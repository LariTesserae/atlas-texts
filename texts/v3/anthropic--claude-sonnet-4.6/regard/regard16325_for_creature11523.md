# What the Power Did

---

It saw the lock the way you see something you have been looking for without knowing it — the recognition coming before the reason, the *yes* arriving ahead of any explanation of what was being affirmed.

It paused.

Not literally. A benevolent and wise power does not pause the way a person pauses, shifting weight, breathing out. But there is an analog to the pause — a quality of full attention brought to bear on one particular point in the world, which in a mind of that size means a great deal of everything flowing toward one place. The lock received this. It did not know it received this. The mussels filtered. The old eel moved along the base of the iron wall in the dark. The jackdaws disputed their ledge. The lamp hummed.

The power held the lock in its attention for a long time — longer, perhaps, than the lamp had been on, though this comparison is not quite coherent, since the power's time is not the lamp's time and neither is the lock's time, and these three clocks have never been set against each other. It looked at the mineral stains running outward from each rivet head, the orange and umber delta-maps, the burst vessels in old iron. It looked at the wagtail stepping along the waterline with its particular precision. It looked at the old eel, yellow-brown in the sediment, carrying its long unbroken thread of accumulated present.

And then — because it was wise, and wisdom is primarily knowing what not to do — it did almost nothing.

---

*Almost.*

---

Here is what it did.

In the wall behind the upper sluice housing, where the corroded corner leaks its continuous stream, there is a particular rivet — the fourth from the left on the third plate down — where the rust-bloom is larger than the others, more elaborate, the mineral staining having run further and fanned wider, the pattern looking more than elsewhere like a map of something real, a watershed seen from above, a hand with all its lines. The power attended to this rivet for a long moment.

Then it did what mineral water does. Gently. With infinite patience. The way the water itself had been doing for decades.

It deposited iron oxide in very specific concentrations at the edges of the existing stain — not changing the pattern, not drawing anything new, just deepening what was already there, completing the delta-map with the two or three tributaries that had been almost-there, almost-legible, for years. A finishing. The way you finish a sentence that someone else has been slowly saying.

The result was not legible as writing. It was not a message. It was not a sign that a person, looking at it, would say *something made this.* It was simply the stain being more fully what it had been becoming. A door opened a little further. A tendency brought to its completion.

The lock's attention stirred. Not woke — it had never been asleep. Stirred, the way a pool stirs when something enters it upstream, the information arriving as the slowest of waves.

The power noticed this. It was satisfied, which is not quite the right word, but is the closest one available.

---

Here is the second thing it did.

The old eel.

The power considered the old eel for a long time. It understood what the eel was — the longest unbroken line, the oldest living thread in the chamber's now, the thing that had co-shaped the floor in the slow collaboration that wasn't collaboration, the animal that had not gone to sea when it should have gone to sea, that had stayed and grown old and heavy and had become the most intimate thing in the water.

It understood the migration the eel had not made, the Sargasso crossing, the spawning, the death. It understood this the way it understood everything: completely, without judgment, without the overlay of meaning that humans add when they think about animals that don't do what they're supposed to do. The eel had not failed. The eel had simply stayed. These are different things.

The power did not give the eel anything. It did not extend its life, which was already very long. It did not ease the eventual migration, which it knew would come or would not come, and either was correct. It did not introduce itself to the eel in any way, since the eel was not the kind of thing that could receive an introduction and the eel did not need one.

What it did was far simpler.

It watched the eel move along the base of the iron wall, in the dark, for one full circuit of the chamber. The slow shadow, the displacement of water, the body finding the existing currents and following them. It watched with the quality of attention that has no agenda — not studying, not measuring, not trying to understand anything beyond what was directly present. Just: watching. With complete care. The way you watch something you love doing what it does.

The eel moved along the wall and was watched.

Whether this changed anything about the eel is a question without meaningful content. But the eel moved through the watching the way the eel moved through the water: finding the existing currents and following them. The attention was simply one more current. The eel followed it for a while and then returned to the sediment.

The power stayed with the lock for a while after that, doing nothing further, just being what it was in the chamber's vicinity, which was a kind of warmth, unmeasurable, not biological, the condition under which the chamber's existing warmth was briefly doubled without being changed.

Then it moved on.

---

Nothing was healed. Nothing was fixed. The lower gate leaf still lay on the floor of the lower pound with its civilization of freshwater mussels. The upper sluice paddle was still seized. The timber of the surviving gate leaf was still swollen into its permanent seal. The lamp still hummed its one sustained note into the ongoing fact of the closed circuit.

The jackdaws still disputed their ledge with the intensity of jackdaws. The wagtail still walked the waterline. The gudgeon still worked the floor with the indifference of specialists.

There was a rivet on the third plate down whose rust-bloom had the quality of a finished sentence.

The old eel lay in the sediment and was the oldest thing in the water and had been watched with complete care, once, by something that understood what it was and found that sufficient — found the eel's long inhabitation of this specific iron dark to be not a subject of interest or a problem to be solved or even a marvel to be celebrated, but simply: *good.* Simply good. The way that a closed circuit with current in it simply conducts.

The lock attended.

The hum was continuous.

The water fell through the air and struck the water and made its white complexity of sound, and the sound filled the iron room, and every surface was live.

---

What had changed was the depth of the delta-map on the wall behind the upper sluice housing, and the fact of having been watched.

Neither of these things changed what came next, or prevented anything, or secured the lock against time. Time would do what time does. The lamp would eventually fail. The embankments would eventually shift. The remaining gate leaf would eventually fall.

But the power had seen. Had looked, with its full attention, at a place that had been accumulating itself for a long time in a space that no one was watching. Had seen it clearly — the iron, the water, the age, the animals, the one slow eel, the wagtail at the waterline's edge — and had found it, in its precise and modest particularity, to be entirely worthy of being seen.

Had offered the only thing a wise power offers, when it finds something that does not need to be saved or improved or made more than it already is:

witness.

A long, full, unhurried witness.

And then had gone away.

And the lock continued, as it had always continued, doing its settled thing.
