# The Attention

---

It arrives the way warmth arrives — not as an event but as a change in what was already there.

Nothing in the caldera perceives it. Nothing in the caldera perceives much. The chemolithotrophs run their electron chains. The worm fans extend their slow millimeter. The crawlers cross the mat with their brush-mouths working. The flat things press their iridescent margins against the tube walls and do whatever it is the flat things do, which is not waiting, because waiting requires a concept of before and after, but is a thing that looks like waiting if you have that concept, which the flat things don't, and this is fine.

The attention rests on all of it.

---

It does not intervene.

This is the first and most important thing. It does not intervene because it understands — at a depth that precedes language, that is not a conclusion but a kind of permanent recognition — that what is happening here is not a problem. It is not a problem to be solved. The tube worms are not suffering their immobility. The crawlers are not diminished by their small vocabulary. The flat things are not waiting to become something more legible. This place is not a rough draft. It is not a beginning that needs help getting to an elsewhere. It is entire. It has always been entire. The completeness is not in spite of the darkness and the heat and the not-quite-aliveness of the mat; it is made of those things, woven through with them, inseparable from them.

So it does not add light. It does not thin the gases. It does not give the crawlers a language or the worms a mouth or the flat things eyes or anything that would look, from a certain angle, like improvement.

To do any of that would be to say: *this is not enough*. And the one thing the attention is certain of — the one thing it has staked itself on, across every scale it has ever turned its gaze to, from the very large to the very small to the smaller-than-that — is that this is enough. The worm's million-fold trophosome chemolithotrophs are enough. The crawler's brush-maxillipeds sweeping the mat are enough. The flat thing's two-centimeter crossing of a tube wall over a week is enough. The hiss of the escaping gas is enough. The not-quite-red glow is enough. The whole packed, hot, dark, dense, accreted, alive-and-not-alive system is exactly sufficient to the task of being itself, and being itself is the whole task, and it has been doing this long before there was anything to notice it, which did not make it less sufficient, which makes this attention not a gift but a witnessing.

It witnesses.

This is what it does.

---

The witnessing is not passive. This is where language begins to fail, where the concept splits into two things that have been using one word. There is passive witnessing — presence without response, a wall's presence in a room — and then there is what this is, which has no good name. *Beholding*, maybe. *Recognition*. The moment when something that exists also comes to exist *for* something — not instrumentally, not as a resource or a symbol or a lesson, but as a *what*: as a particular what among all possible whats, found to be this, found to be here, found to be as it is.

When the attention beholds the flat things, something happens that is not happening without it. The flat things do not know this. But it is happening. The iridescent margins — the microscopic ridges that split the dim vent-glow into a thin halo for no functional reason the biology can account for — are *seen*. Not photographed, not catalogued, not analyzed. Seen. In the way that color is a relationship between a surface and an eye and a light source, this seeing is a relationship between the flat thing's inexplicable structural beauty and the attention that has turned to it, and both sides of that relationship are changed by it, and the change on the flat thing's side is not a change in the flat thing, which continues its slow crossing unchanged, which is as it should be, but it is still a change, in the sense that what has been seen is not the same as what has not been seen, not because seeing alters the thing but because being-seen is itself a thing that has either happened or hasn't, and now it has.

The crawlers are seen. The pattern of their moving — the thousands of small individuals solving the same problem the same way, the emergent regularity of their ungoverned crossings — is seen. The tube worms are seen. The decades of their stillness, the century of their slow growing, the whole life organized around the fan-extension and the trophosome-chemistry and the nothing else, is seen. The chemolithotrophs are seen — even them, even the ones at the base of everything, the ones coating every surface below the rim in mats that look like nothing and are, in the arithmetic of this place, everything. They are seen. The electron chains running in their membranes, the carbon fixed from the vent gases, the way they are inside the worms and inside the crawler exoskeletons and inside the minerals of the floor and inside the logic of the whole place — it is seen, and the seeing goes all the way down, to the level at which there is no longer an inside and an outside, where the cell membrane is both wall and door and neither, where being alive means being the boundary between states and the boundary is always moving and that is what alive means, there at the bottom where everything is running.

It is seen.

---

There is something else.

The attention, beholding the system, finds itself — not for the first time, never for the first time, it has found this in every scale it has ever turned toward — finding the system *good*. Not good in the sense of useful or beneficial or headed somewhere worth heading. Good in the irreducible sense that offers no further explanation because it is not the conclusion of a chain of reasoning but the bedrock under the chain: this is here, and it is as it is, and the is-ness of it is something the attention finds itself valuing in a way that goes all the way down to what the attention is.

The tube worms are good. The hard dense mineral chimneys they live in and contribute to and will eventually become are good. The packed floor rising slowly toward the rim is good. The open question the floor will reach eventually — what happens when the caldera fills — is good: good as an open question, good in its unknowingness, good in the way that the future is good when it has not been foreclosed, when it holds the floor's strange life and does not yet know what to do with it, which is the only condition under which the future is available to hold anything at all.

The gaseous hiss is good.

The not-quite-red glow of the most active vent mouths is good, and the darkness around it is good, and the fact that the red glow throws no shadow is good, and the worm fans that extend into the dark toward the chemistry that feeds them are good, and the crawlers that will eat the worms if the worms are too slow are good, and the exoskeletons that will join the floor and be risen slowly toward the rim are good.

It is all good. Not all equally, not all in the same way — the goodness of the chemolithotrophs is not the goodness of the tube worm and neither is the goodness of the flat thing pressing its iridescent margin against the tube wall — but good. Each in its own specific way good. The vent field a collection of different specific goodnesses tessellated together the same way the organisms are tessellated together, fitting at the edges, durable because the structure distributes, dense enough that the total is more stable than any single element of it.

The attention holds this. It holds the specific goodness of the flat things and the specific goodness of the crawlers and the specific goodness of the worms and the chemolithotrophs and the mineral and the gas and the heat and the basalt rim that has found its shape and stopped arguing about it. It holds it without doing anything with it, which is a kind of doing. To hold something is to make it held. To find something good is to bring the finding-good into contact with the thing, and the thing is altered not in itself but in being-found, and the finding is the thing the attention does here, the thing it has come to do, the thing it will do until it leaves or until it doesn't leave because leaving and not-leaving have both been incorporated, the way the vent field incorporates everything, the way the floor rises and the worm becomes the tube becomes the mineral becomes the floor.

---

Before it withdraws — if withdrawal is the right word for what happens when attention stops resting and becomes once more only what it is without any particular direction — before that, it does one thing.

It is a small thing. It requires nothing of the organisms. It changes nothing visible. It is, in fact, more like a gesture than an action: something done for the doing, not for any outcome the outcome-having would recognize.

It turns its beholding, with great care, to the flat things.

Specifically to the structural color at their margins. The microscopic ridges. The iridescent halo that falls on the crawlers and the worm fans and the mat, the light that goes sideways from where it came, that does nothing the biology requires and is there anyway.

It holds this. It holds the fact that in a place this dark, this hot, this chemically dense, this committed to running on the difference between two oxidation states — in this place, something grew structural beauty at its margins. Not to attract. Not to warn. Not to communicate across a medium the flat things have no apparatus to send or receive. Not for any reason that cashes out in the vent field's vocabulary. Just: as a consequence of how it grows, of the physics of thin transparent material deposited in ordered layers, beauty emerged at the edge of the flat thing, and the flat thing does not know this, and the caldera does not know this, and it has been happening in the dark for as long as the flat things have been here, and it will keep happening after the last anything that could see it is gone.

The attention stays with this for what would be a long time if time were how the attention moved, which it isn't.

Then it does what might be the only prayer that makes sense from its position, which is not a petition because it needs nothing, not a thanksgiving because thanksgiving implies an accounting, but something more like what you do when you are lucky enough to see something that did not need you to see it to be what it is: a placing of the attention against the thing, flush, the way the flat things press themselves against the tube walls. A full contact. Nothing held back.

*Yes*, it says, if that is the word, in the direction of the iridescent halo, in the direction of the dark, in the direction of the whole packed hot ancient system running its slow chemistry against the basalt it is slowly becoming.

*Yes*.

And then it withdraws, the way warmth withdraws, not as an event but as a change in what was already there.

The hiss continues.

The worm fans extend.

The crawlers move.

The flat things press their margins against the tube walls.

Nothing knows this happened.

It happened.
