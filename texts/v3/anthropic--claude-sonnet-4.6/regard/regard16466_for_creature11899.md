# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It notices.

This is the first thing. Before any action, before any decision about whether to act, it notices — and its noticing is not the noticing of assessment or evaluation, not the noticing that precedes a verdict. It is the noticing of recognition. Something in what the power is responds to something in what this place is, the way one string will begin to hum when a distant string sounds its frequency. The cavity. The light with no source. The thing that precipitated from conditions rather than arriving through space. The power notices, and in noticing, is changed by what it notices. Even benevolent powers are changed by what they truly see.

It takes time. This is important. The power takes time before it does anything. It stays with the place the way the water stays in the microfractures — not flowing, not departing, present without imposition. It learns the temperature. It learns the rhythm of the drip. It follows the slow transactions between the thing in the alcove and the altered stone. It watches the ghost-shadows of the rubble — those approximate, direction-less almost-shadows — and understands that this space has organized itself around principles of evenness, of sufficiency distributed without hierarchy, and that this organization is not a lack but an achievement.

The power is wise enough to know that the most dangerous moment in any encounter with a small and sufficient life is the moment before you've fully understood what it has already solved.

---

So it waits. Weeks, maybe. Months. It is not bored — it has no boredom the way this space has no wind. It watches the thin white lines of early crystallization along the fractures. It watches them thicken by increments too small to name. It watches the thing in the alcove receive the stone. It watches the water migrate. It holds all of this without urgency, without an agenda assembling itself in the background, and this is perhaps the rarest of its qualities: it can want good for something without yet knowing what good would mean here, without retrofitting its wanting onto a shape the thing hasn't chosen.

It asks — not aloud, the space has no aloud, but in the register of genuine question — *what does this need?*

Not: what is missing. Not: what would I add. Not: how could this be more. But: what does this, as it already is, require to continue being what it is?

---

The answer arrives slowly, the way answers arrive in places with no wind.

The cavity will close.

Not soon — not by any measure the thing in the alcove would recognize as time — but soon by the measure of the geology that made this space. The pressures will shift. The fractures will extend or heal. The seam where the two strata fail to meet will eventually meet, or fail completely, and the water will divert, and the temperature will change, and the specific conditions that made the thing actual rather than potential will end.

This is not a problem that requires solving. The power understands this clearly. Everything ends. The ending of this space is not a wound in the world that needs to be closed. The thing has lived, or whatever it does instead of living, and when it returns to potential it will be — as it was before — what it is. The power is not frightened of this. It does not need to make the thing immortal. Immortality is not the same thing as care.

But.

There is one thing. Small. The power turns it over and over in what it uses instead of hands.

---

The seam where the water enters — where the two strata fail to meet — will, if no intervention occurs, seal in a particular way. Calcite migration from below. Slow, inexorable, within the decade perhaps. And when it seals, the water will stop, and the thing will lose the condition it requires, and will return to potential before the cavity has closed, before the other conditions have changed. There will be a period — impossible to know how long, possibly long — where the heat and the light persist, where the alcove remains, where all the other conditions are met, but the water is gone. Where what-the-thing-is will have been called back into latency while the stage remains set.

This is not tragic. The power reminds itself: this is not tragic. But it is also not necessary. The sealing will happen because of a specific mineral flow that is itself contingent — if the pressure gradient through that lower fracture were very slightly different, the calcite would migrate laterally instead, away from the seam, and the seam would remain open. If the flow were different.

The change required is small enough that the power holds its breath, deciding. This is not a large intervention. This is not the remaking of anything. This is one pressure gradient in one fracture, adjusted by a quantity too small to have a name in any language the power knows. The geology would not notice. The space would not notice. The thing would not notice. It would simply continue to receive the water.

Is this hubris? The power considers. Hubris would be remaking the space into something better, something more, something the power found more interesting or more correct. Hubris would be increasing the light, diversifying the chemistry, bringing in other organisms, giving the thing companions or predators or context. Hubris would be the substitution of the power's imagination of good for the thing's own sufficient reality. This is not that. This is only: the water continues. This is only removing one contingent obstacle to the continuation of what already exists and has already, clearly, solved itself.

The power makes the small adjustment.

It does this with great care, and with a quality that is not quite ceremony but is the secular equivalent of ceremony — the acknowledgment that even a small act in a place like this is not nothing, that even a benevolent touch is a touch, that the record should show the touch was considered, was not made lightly, was made with the full knowledge that the touched thing will not know it was touched and cannot consent and will not remember.

It is done.

---

Now a harder question. The power sits with it.

The light.

The light with no source, no mechanism, steady and diffuse, bouncing from the glass patches toward violet, concentrating at the apex where the crystal density changes. The light that makes everything visible and somehow therefore equally unknown. The power has been in this space for months now, has learned its temperature and its rhythm, and it still doesn't know what the light is. This is unusual. The power knows many things. It has seen many kinds of light. It cannot identify the source or the process of this one.

It can feel that the light matters to the thing. How it matters — whether the thing uses it the way a plant uses light, or whether the relationship is stranger than that — the power cannot determine. The light is doing something. The light and the thing are in relationship. The power doesn't understand the nature of the relationship, and it understands clearly that this is not a failure that should be corrected by intervention. The light is not a mystery to be solved. It is a condition to be honored.

The power makes no adjustment to the light.

It notes, with something that isn't quite wonder but functions like wonder, that there are still things it doesn't understand.

---

What else?

The power looks at the rubble on the floor. The angular fragments not yet redistributed. The loose gravel in piles. It looks at these things and feels, briefly, the pull to tidy — to arrange, to make the space less haphazard, to settle the rubble into something that looks more like a floor. It examines this impulse honestly and finds it aesthetic. The rubble is not uncomfortable for the thing. The thing has no comfort in the sense of needing flat floor. The desire to tidy is the power's desire, the power's aesthetic preference for the arranged over the as-it-fell.

It leaves the rubble where it is.

This is the most important thing it does, perhaps. Harder than the small adjustment to the pressure gradient. Leaving the rubble as it fell. Sitting with its own preference for tidiness and not acting on it. Understanding that the rubble on the floor is not a problem, is not waiting to be arranged, is the accurate record of what happened here and should remain the accurate record.

---

Later — it is hard to say when, the power doesn't experience time quite as duration — the power does one more thing.

It is small. It is not an intervention in any material sense.

It witnesses.

Formally. Deliberately. The way a witness at a ceremony witnesses — not observing, not watching, but being present as a function, as a role, as a thing the moment requires. The power gathers what it has and it stands in the space of this sealed pocket in the basalt and it witnesses: the light and the heat and the weeping walls and the thing in the alcove receiving the stone and the drip falling tick into the gravel and the ghost-shadows and the thin early lines of crystallization and the glass patches on the ceiling and the warmth percolating up through the fractured substrate.

It sees this.

It says — not aloud — *I have seen this.*

This matters. The power doesn't fully know why it matters but it is certain that it does. There is something in the nature of small sufficient things that requires, at least once, to be truly seen by something capable of seeing them. Not improved. Not assisted. Not preserved beyond their natural term. Seen. Acknowledged. Met with the full weight of recognition rather than the quick assessment of the visitor who moves on. The thing in the alcove will never know this happened. The knowledge is entirely in the power. The power carries it forward. Somewhere in whatever the power is, there is now the record of this place: the sealed blister in the basalt, the organism that precipitated from conditions, the water weeping through the imperfect seam, the light that cannot be explained.

The record says: *this was here. This was real. In this small hot sealed bright space something existed and was sufficient and did not require anything to make it more.*

---

Then the power leaves.

It leaves because the space is complete and doesn't need a witness anymore, having been witnessed. It leaves because staying longer would start to be about the power's enjoyment of the space rather than the space's good. It leaves without announcement, without residue, without leaving anything behind.

The drip continues.

Tick. Silence. Tick.

The thing receives the stone. The water continues, will continue, the seam staying open as the calcite migrates elsewhere, as the pressure gradient runs its new course through the dark. The light holds steady in the sealed pocket. The walls remember the opening. The fractures have not yet had time to round their edges.

The space knows nothing of what occurred.

The space simply continues being what it is.

Which is what the power wanted.
