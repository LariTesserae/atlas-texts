# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not traveling, simply present where it was not. It has no body to lower through a hatch, no feet to find the floor sooner than expected. It enters the way warmth enters a room: by being what the space already wanted and finding the space ready.

It pauses.

This is the first thing it does: it pauses, fully, in a way that many powerful things forget to do. It does not immediately assess or catalogue or plan. It lets the roar arrive in whatever serves it as ears. It lets the amber-blue light work on whatever serves it as eyes. It lets the warm wet air find it and do what warm wet air does to whatever enters.

For a long time — the timescale unclear, as timescales are unclear here — it simply receives the place.

---

Then it learns.

It begins with the floor, because the floor is where patience lives most completely. It watches the guild: the flat things, the translucent things with visible hearts, the slightly larger things feeding at the edges of both. It follows the transactions. It learns the currency: the dissolved organic content of the water, the cascade from above to below, who eats what settles and who eats who eats what settles. It learns without instruments, without extraction, by attention alone — the kind of attention that changes nothing because it takes nothing.

It moves to the walls and runs something like a hand along the surfaces it cannot touch. It finds the seams in the ceramic where the hard-shelled things cluster and it learns the shape of their solitude: complete, contained, not suffering. It finds the warm organic sections and follows the mycelial extensions of the tapping things back into the wall and forward again and back, tracing the circuit until it understands what the wall is making and what the wall receives and how long this conversation has been running. Longer than it guessed. Longer than the place's apparent age accounts for. It notes this without forcing an explanation.

It stands in the stream zones — in whatever way it stands — and watches the holders hold. Watches the moment when two of them are close enough that their trailing structures touch, and watches the stilling that follows, and recognizes the stilling as the specific quietude of two things that have for a moment confirmed each other's reality. It knows this quietude. It has observed it in many kinds of creature in many kinds of places and has learned to treat it with a care proportionate to its rarity.

It finds the moss-lichen-other and reads the record of its expansion: a few millimeters over months or years, the patient extension of scaffolding into possible territory. It traces the whole collective back to what it can't see: the first negotiation, wherever it happened, whoever was at the table, the moment when separate things became a single thing and then became so single that separate was no longer imaginable. It sits with this for a while, this fact of the world that keeps recurring regardless of scale: the merger, the new unity, the way the boundary between organisms is never as fixed as it appears.

It goes up, in whatever way it goes up, into the occupied darkness. It meets what lives there. This takes some time and a quality of gentleness beyond what it has needed elsewhere in the space — the ceiling dwellers are old and slow and vast in their way and they do not receive presences easily. But it waits in the darkness and the ceiling dwellers extend toward it and it allows this, allows the contact, allows whatever the ceiling dwellers do when they receive an unfamiliar presence: the long assessment, the slow chemical inquiry. Eventually they settle. It is not a threat and it has not tried to be. They return to their processes. The water continues to fall.

It finds the large pale thing on the alcove ceiling, following its seam for the thousandth time or the ten-thousandth. It watches it work. It feels what there is to feel from something that has been here so long that the space no longer offers surprises, only variations on familiar themes. The pale thing is not bored. Boredom requires a self that can imagine elsewhere. The pale thing cannot imagine elsewhere. The pale thing has elsewhere no more than a stone does, which is not a diminishment but a different relationship to existence, a completeness so complete it has consumed the concept of alternatives.

The benevolent power understands what the pale thing is, and what it is is fine.

---

After learning, it considers what it has found.

A system in balance. Not frozen balance — not the balance of a thing that has stopped, but the balance of a thing in motion that has found the motion sustainable. The population is not growing. The food web feeds back on itself without collapsing. The wall material and the organisms have arrived at mutual support. The whole ecology is at what a physicist might call a steady state and what the benevolent power recognizes as something harder to achieve and rarer to see: a working arrangement between limited space and the life that chooses to fill it.

It considers what, if anything, is missing. What suffering exists here. What the place needs that it does not have.

This takes a long time and produces a short answer.

---

The answer is: not much.

The answer is: there are small insufficiencies, as there are always small insufficiencies, and they do not reach the level of suffering but they are present as absences, as would-be-betters, as this-could-be-more. The benevolent power finds three.

---

**The first:** the tapping things on the warm wall sections, the ones with their mycelial extensions into the substrate — they are in a relationship of mutual support, but the relationship is not, quite, reciprocal. The wall gives more than it receives. The exchange is slightly out of balance in a way that will not cause collapse in any timescale this place will probably exist, but that is costing the wall material something it could spare if the arrangement were more equitable. The tapping things take slightly more than they return. They don't know this, in the sense of knowledge that would allow them to choose otherwise. The imbalance has simply accumulated, the way small imbalances do over long time.

The benevolent power makes an adjustment so small it doesn't feel like intervention. It works with what is already there: a slight change in the composition of the tapping things' waste output, so that one chemical they currently release as pure byproduct becomes instead slightly useful to the wall, a better feedstock for the wall's processes. This change is within the range of what the tapping things already do; it is not imposed from outside but drawn out from what they already contain. It will take several generations to establish itself fully. It will not be noticed by anyone who does not watch very closely for a very long time.

When it is complete, the exchange will be equitable. The wall will receive what it needs. The tapping things will continue as they are — nothing is taken from them, nothing costs them. The balance will be better.

This is what benevolence is when it is working: not a gift from abundance to lack, but a slight redistribution of what is already present so that the arrangement serves everyone in it more completely.

---

**The second:** the stream dwellers, the ones that hold position in the falling water — there is one, in one stream, that cannot hold. It is slightly larger than the others, or was developed along a slightly different line, and the current that is manageable for its kin is too much for it. It holds for a while and then is carried off and swims back up the shallow water at the stream's edge and finds position again and holds again and is carried off again. This has been its life. It is not suffering in a way that activates distress — it has no concept of a better arrangement, cannot compare its situation to another's — but it is spending itself at a rate that is faster than its kin, and it will be shorter-lived for it, and there is no reason for this except that the stream happens to be slightly too strong at the position it keeps choosing.

The benevolent power redirects the stream by three centimeters.

Not the whole stream. Not permanently, or rather: permanently in the sense that the physical structure of where the ceiling releases water is now very slightly different, one channel is very slightly widened, the result being that the stream this one organism inhabits is three centimeters to the left of where it was, and at its new position the current is within the range the organism can manage.

The organism, on its next displacement, swims back up the edge as usual, finds its position, and holds. The current pushes. It holds. After a while, something in its nervous system — if nervous system is the right word, if system is the right word, if word is the right form for what is happening in the distributed response-infrastructure of this small thing — something registers that holding is not, this time, failing. That the relationship between its effort and the result has changed. It holds the position through the usual interval and beyond it. It holds and holds. Eventually it stops bracing against a fall that is not coming and simply holds, and then it simply is.

The benevolent power watches this and feels what it feels, which is what it always feels when a small life, previously struggling, discovers that it is not struggling. There is no word for this feeling that is not too large or too small. It keeps the feeling carefully, as it keeps all such moments, which are among the reasons it continues to be what it is.

---

**The third insufficiency** is of a different kind, and the benevolent power holds it for the longest time before deciding.

The gray-green moss-lichen-other is attempting an expansion it will likely fail. On the far wall, near the floor, where two water trickles converge, there is an area that has all the conditions the collective requires except one: the wall material there is a section of the smooth-and-warm type, not the ceramic, and the smooth-and-warm type is already occupied by its tapping-things, and the tapping-things, without any intention, are creating a chemical environment at the surface that the scaffolding component of the collective finds hostile. The exploration has been trying for — it is impossible to say how long. A long time. The scaffolding is sent, withdraws, is sent again, withdraws. The collective doesn't know it is failing; it sends the scaffolding because that is what the edge of the collective does. The withdrawal happens because the conditions are wrong. No consciousness, no frustration, no accumulated weight of repeated failure. Just: sent, withdrawn, sent, withdrawn, the same attempt made fresh each time.

But it is the same attempt, made in the same place, against the same conditions, and it will never succeed, and the area it is trying to reach is good growing area — the convergence is reliable, the warmth is adequate, the collective could establish there and would be better established for it, would be more stable, would process more, would provide more base to the food web, would in a small way make the whole system slightly richer.

The benevolent power could change the chemical environment at the wall surface. Could make the smooth-and-warm section hospitable to the collective's scaffolding. This would require moving the tapping-things, which are not harmed by the change but are displaced from their preferred section to a ceramic section nearby, a section that is also adequate for them.

Here it is careful.

Moving the tapping-things to make room for the collective's expansion is a good outcome — better distribution of the ecology, richer system overall, both organisms in adequate conditions. But the tapping-things are here first. The smooth-and-warm section is theirs. The benevolent power, which is wise as well as benevolent, knows that *good outcomes* and *right action* are related but not identical, and that an intervention which displaces something to improve the aggregate can be correct in sum and wrong in the way it is done.

It waits.

It returns to the question several times, from different angles. It is not in a hurry; it has learned that patience is a form of respect.

What it notices, on its third or fourth careful look: the smooth-and-warm section that the tapping-things occupy is not entirely adequate for them. The warmth there is slightly lower than their optimal — they are present, they are functioning, but they are slightly below their best. There is a section of wall, ceramic section, where heat from the building's outer systems flows through slightly more abundantly, warming a stretch of ceramic to nearly the same temperature as the smooth-and-warm material. The tapping-things could do as well there. They have never found that section because nothing has directed them toward it and the distance, for them, is significant.

The benevolent power makes a trail.

Not a visible trail. A chemical gradient, small and fading, the kind of thing that the tapping-things' extensions can follow if they happen to extend in that direction. It is not compulsion. It is invitation. It will take time — the extensions will have to find it, and then follow it, and then the organism will have to move, and then the next organism, and this will happen slowly if it happens at all. Several generations may pass before the smooth-and-warm section is vacated, if it ever is, which it may not be — the gradient is faint and organisms are not obligated to follow faint gradients.

But if they follow it, they will find better conditions.

And if they move, the moss-lichen-other will find the section available, and the scaffolding will eventually find conditions hospitable, and the collective will extend into the new area over months and years, and the system will be slightly richer.

The benevolent power will not return to see whether this happened. Or rather: it will not require this confirmation. It has done what seems right and what seems proportionate and what was arrived at carefully, and whether the tapping-things follow the gradient or ignore it is theirs to determine, as it should be. Benevolence that requires the object of its benevolence to be changed is something else. Benevolence that respects the possibility of refusal is the actual thing.

---

These are the three interventions.

One redistributes a small imbalance, drawing from what is already present. One moves a stream three centimeters. One lays a faint trail that may not be followed.

Nothing is remade. Nothing is rescued from genuine destruction — there is nothing here that needs rescuing, which is itself a kind of gift, which the benevolent power recognized in its first long pause and has been grateful for since. The place is not in crisis. The place is a steady-state marvel running at extraordinary depth and precision and age, and it does not need saving. It needs, if anything, the occasional light touch of something that has noticed it, that has looked carefully enough to see where the minor frictions are, that has taken the time to find solutions that impose no costs and respect every existing relationship.

---

Before it leaves, the benevolent power does one more thing.

It does not fix anything, this last thing. It does not improve anything in the measurable sense. It is not an intervention at all.

It witnesses.

It goes to the center of the floor and it stands in the slow water, among the floor-guild with their visible hearts, and it holds its attention still — as still as the pale thing on the alcove ceiling is in its confidence, as still as the stream-holders are in the current — and it attends to the whole space at once.

The roar. The amber-blue. The warm convecting air with its invisible passengers. The ceiling's long work. The walls' metabolism. The streams falling in their chosen places, each one a note in the sound. The gray-green collective at its edges, sending out its patient scaffold. The tapping-things in their slight warmth. The stream-holder, the one that could not hold and can now hold, holding.

The benevolent power witnesses all of this and does what witnessing does: it makes the witnessed thing more real. Not more real in the sense of more material or more permanent — the condensation floor does not need the benevolent power's attention to exist. But more real in the sense of: it has been seen, fully, by something capable of full sight, and the seeing is a kind of completion, a circuit closing, the universe knowing one more thing about itself through the act of the benevolent power standing in the amber-blue warmth and attending without agenda.

This is perhaps the most important thing it does here.

Perhaps it is the only thing that matters.

To see a place completely — to really see it, all the way down to the flat things that look like floor and all the way up into the darkness where the old things produce their water and all the way into the wall where the tapping-things have dissolved the boundary between organism and substrate — to see all of this and find it *sufficient*, to hold it in full attention and discover that it does not need to be other than it is, that its existence is not a problem to be solved but a fact to be honored, that whatever small adjustments want making can be made lightly and the rest can simply be allowed to be —

this is what the benevolent power offers that cannot be offered any other way.

Not rescue. Not improvement. Not even, exactly, love — though love is somewhere in it.

Witness. Attention. The respect of being fully known by something that has taken the time.

---

The roar continues.

The water falls in its chosen places.

The benevolent power is no longer present in any locatable sense, but something has shifted in the quality of the space — not a change in temperature or sound or light, nothing that a sensor would record. The shift is in the category of: *this has been seen, and found to be itself, and that is enough*.

The stream-holder holds.

The pale thing follows its seam.

The floor lives in its surface layer, going about its business on the water film, feeding on something you can't see, being fed on by something slightly larger, the whole transaction happening at the scale of millimeters, and it has been happening here, and it will continue to happen here, and somewhere in the occupied darkness the ceiling dwellers produce their water and release it to fall in chosen places and the water catches the amber-blue light on its way down and the light does unusual things with depth and the space breathes: warm air out, slightly cooler in, or maybe the reverse, maybe it cycles.

And the condensation floor is itself, completely.

Which was always, already, enough.
