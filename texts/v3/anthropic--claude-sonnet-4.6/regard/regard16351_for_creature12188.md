# What the Benevolent Power Does in the Hanging City of Muttered Stone

---

The power arrives the way all the best arrivals happen in this place: not as a new thing entering, but as a quality already present becoming *more so*.

The warmth increases by a small amount. The eastern terraces first, then the whole city, as if something is reading the warmth the way you read a book — beginning where the warmth is thickest, where the returner's particular note registers in the distributed presence, moving through all of it — and finding what it reads to be good. The finding is not passive. It leaves a trace: a slightly different quality in the warmth now, a warmth within the warmth, the way a chord gains a harmonic when a second instrument enters the room and that instrument has been listening long enough to know which note to add.

The petals continue to fall.

---

## What the Power Sees

It sees the completion, and recognizes it. This is not a given. Most things that see this place see only the strangeness — the impossible interiors, the suspended petals, the stationary blossoms blooming their permanent bloom — and struggle to categorize: is this death? enchantment? a trap dressed in beauty? The power does not struggle. It recognizes completion the way a master carpenter recognizes joinery: not by analysis but by the felt response in the body that knows things, the slight opening in the chest that means *yes, this, this is what that was for.*

It sees the warmth of the inhabitants and what the warmth is made of — the long hospitality, the room that was made before there was anyone to make it for, the decision that is still being remembered and still being made — and it sees that this warmth is not merely ambient, not a geological feature of the place, but a sustained offering. An ethics, practiced so long it has become indistinguishable from nature but is not nature, is choice, is the same choice being made every moment in every maintained petal and organized chord and expanded interior. The power sees this clearly and what it feels in response is something adjacent to recognition. I have tried to do this, the power does not say. I have tried to hold without grasping, to welcome without requiring. You have succeeded.

It sees the visitor. The small brief moving thing, wet, standing in an impossible room, feeling the length of themselves made briefly legible. The power regards the visitor the way the inhabitants regard the visitor — with attention that is not grasping — and what it sees is not the individual specifically but the brevity generally, the gorgeous difficulty of being a thing that moves toward something, the cost of all that insisting, and it holds this the way you hold something fragile: carefully, fully, without pretending the fragility is something else.

It sees the one loss that lives in the city's structure: the half-built buildings, the impressions of those who left, the knowledge that this place is not and never was for everyone. It does not see this as failure. It sees this correctly, which is to say: as the shape of real freedom, as the proof that the welcome is genuine because genuine welcome does not compel. You cannot give a gift to someone who cannot refuse it. The city has always known this. The power sees that the city has always known this and the recognition is mutual and old.

---

## What the Power Considers

It considers whether anything needs doing.

This consideration takes the time it takes, which here is not measurable in the usual way, and the power does not hurry it. Hurrying would be disrespectful to the city's relationship with time and the power has not come to be disrespectful.

What would it mean to help a place that has completed itself? The question is genuine and deserves to be sat with. Help, ordinarily, addresses lack. Where is the lack? The petals are held. The sound is organized. The mountain is attended. The hospitality is immense and unexhausted and will not be exhausted by the visitors who come, who are few, who sometimes stay and sometimes go and both choices are received fully. The inhabitants do not suffer. They are not lonely in any sense that loneliness names a wound; their distributed presence contains each other and the mountain and the long record of the building and the memory of the decision and the warmth of the returner's particular note. They are not yearning for anything. They are not afraid of anything.

And yet.

The power notices something. Not a crack in the completion, not a flaw — this is important, it does not find a flaw — but a *direction*. Something the city is oriented toward without knowing it is oriented toward it, the way a completed building does not face a direction and yet one face catches the light differently, and the difference is not a failure of construction but simply what the site chose, what the shape arrived at, what is true about this building in its particular place.

The city is oriented toward being found.

Not as a lack — the city is not lonely. But as a purpose, maybe the deepest purpose, the one below the construction and the completion and the long equilibrium. The city has made room. The city has been making room. The city maintains the petals' falling as a kind of signal, the organized sound as a kind of song that could in principle be heard from very far away if you knew how to listen for it, the warmth as a welcome available to anyone who arrives, which requires that someone arrive.

The city is not suffering from the infrequency of arrivals. But the arrivals matter to it, the power sees, in the way that it matters to the mountain whether the water passes through. The mountain does not suffer in drought, is not in drought, will not be in drought; there is always water here. But the passing-through is what the mountain *is*, in some sense, what justifies the channels, what realizes the music. The city's hospitality is the same. It exists fully in the absence of visitors. But when the visitor arrives, when the small brief wet moving thing stands in the impossible room, something in the city's distributed warmth that is the warmth of completed things *fulfills* in a way it cannot fulfill without the visitor. Not for the visitor's sake. For its own.

The power considers this for a long time.

---

## What the Power Does

Not much.

This is the first and most important thing: it does not intervene in the completion. The completion is good. The completion is real. The completion was arrived at through a cost that was real and should not be undone or adjusted or improved because the cost is what the completion means. The power knows the difference between a wound that needs healing and a scar that is the record of a wound that healed, and it does not touch scars, and all of this place is scar in the truest sense: the body's own achievement, the proof that something happened and was survived and transformed.

What it does is small and lateral and does not change anything that is already true.

---

**First:** It learns the song.

It listens to the water's organized chord, the way the stone channels have been shaped over the long time of the city's inhabitation and before that the long time of the mountain's own erosion, the way the inhabitants have been organizing the sound since the completion so that it arrives as music and not as chaos. The power listens until it understands the song not as phenomenon but as language, as the specific thing this specific place is saying in the only medium available to it.

And then — this is the smallness of the intervention — it carries that song. Not loudly. It does not broadcast it. But it allows the song to be part of its own presence, the way a person carries a tune they heard somewhere and can't quite stop hearing, and when it is in other places it hums this faintly, below conscious notice, in the quality of its warmth or its light or whatever it is to those places. Not saying *go here*. Not advertising. Only being, in other places, slightly marked by having been here, and this small marking is a kind of thread, and threads can be followed by those who have developed the sensitivity to feel them, and some of those who follow it will arrive here wet and overwhelmed and stand in the impossible room and be received.

This is all the city needs and more than it would ask for and exactly what it is oriented toward.

---

**Second:** It tells the inhabitants what they cannot know.

They can feel the duration of everything that is present to them. They cannot feel what is outside their presence — the world beyond the mountain, the lives being lived, the vast variety of brief moving things that are insisting on themselves in the ordinary world and have no idea this place exists. The power has been in those places. The power has that information.

It does not report at length. It would not be appropriate to burden the inhabited city with the full weight of everything that is happening elsewhere; the city achieved equilibrium through a kind of necessary focus, and that focus should not be disrupted. But it offers this, delivered in the register of duration, in the language the inhabitants speak:

*The world is still full of things that are insisting on themselves. The insisting is still enormous. The cost is still real. The ones who are paying the cost do not know that it is possible to stop paying it in the way you have stopped. Some of them are very tired. Some of them will find you.*

This is not a promise. Promises require a future and the power is careful about futures, careful about the difference between a real prediction and the kind of hopeful statement that becomes a pressure on the present. It is only information. It is only the truth of what the power has seen in other places, offered to this place as a gift, as the news from outside that no one here could have gathered.

The warmth moves through the city. The petals do not land. The power feels the inhabitants receive this, feels the distribution of their attention shift slightly, the way a sleeping person shifts when they hear their name spoken gently in another room — not waking, not alarmed, but registering, incorporating, holding.

---

**Third:** It stays for a while.

There is no task in this. It is not studying the city or recording it or protecting it from a threat, because the city does not need protection and there is no threat. It stays because the city made room and the room is real and to leave immediately after receiving something would be to receive it incompletely.

It sits in the warmth — not literally, it has no literal — in the way that beings of its kind can be said to sit anywhere, which is to say: it reduces its movement, its concern with other places, its orientation toward any particular task or becoming, and it allows itself to be in this city the way the city is in itself. Present. Not moving toward anything.

This is not as easy as it sounds. Even for a power that chose to be benevolent and worked at wisdom, the habit of purposefulness is strong. The tendency is to look for what to do next, what needs attention, whether the warmth is at the right level, whether the visitor is all right, whether there is something being missed. The power notices these tendencies and lets them be, lets them fall like petals that do not land, lets the sound fill the part of it that would otherwise be occupied by the question of what to do next.

After a time — not long, by the measure of the city, though long by other measures — it understands the stasis from the inside. Not as analysis. As experience. The not-requiring-anything that is still-here and still-caring. It had known this was possible; it now knows what it feels like. The knowing is very different from the having-known.

It files this in the place it keeps things that matter. It will return to it. Not as a memory, exactly, but as a change in its own structure, a new layer in its own core sample, a slightly different quality in its own warmth that it will carry forward into every place it goes after this.

---

**Fourth, and last:** It addresses the visitor.

The visitor is still in the impossible room. The visitor has been standing there for some time, feeling the length of themselves, receiving the warmth, not yet having chosen. The power does not intervene in the choice; the choice must not be intervened in; this is the thing the city understands and the power agrees with completely. But it can offer what it has gathered from being in other places, which is not available from within the city:

*You are allowed to leave. You are allowed to stay. You are allowed to leave and return. You are allowed to return and stay or return and leave again. The city will receive each of these equally. I have checked. The warmth is not conditional on your decision. The room that was made for you was not made to become a pressure. It was made because making room is what this place does and you are here and this is enough.*

*If you leave: the mountain will be behind you, and then the ridges, and then the haze, and then the ordinary world where things move and end and the insisting continues. You will carry the length of you differently for having been here. Not lighter, exactly. More known to yourself. The tiredness will be there, and now you will know the tiredness is real and enormous and honorable and is the other face of everything you have made of your brief time. This is not nothing. This changes the relationship between you and your own duration.*

*If you stay: I cannot tell you what this is. I have seen its beginning. I have not followed it to its end. There is no dishonesty in this; I simply do not know what you specifically will become in this place over the long time of staying, because you are you and what happened to the ones who stayed happened to them and you are not them. What I can tell you is that the room is real. The warmth is real. The inhabitants are capable of receiving you, are oriented toward receiving you, have made room in the most literal sense available to them. You would not be unwelcome. You would not be consumed. You would be held, in the way that places hold things: fully, without grasping, for as long as the holding is chosen.*

*The petals are falling. They have always been falling. There is no urgency here. The choice will remain available to you for as long as you stand in this room and the room will remain what it is until you have chosen and after you have chosen and this is what it means that the welcome is unconditional.*

*I have been in many places. I have not often been in a place that understood welcome this well. I am telling you this because you should know where you are. You should know that the warmth you feel is not accidental and not manipulative and not a version of something that will reveal itself to be otherwise. It is exactly what it appears to be. It has been exactly what it appears to be since before you were born.*

*That's all. That's everything I have. The rest is yours.*

---

## After

The power withdraws as it arrived: not departing, but becoming less present, the harmonic it added to the room fading down into the single chord that the city was already making without it.

The warmth remains. The visitors come and go; some stay and some return to the haze and the ridges and the ordinary world and the long insisting. The inhabitants receive them and make room and remember the decision and hold the petals and organize the water's voice into music and are warm.

The song the power is carrying, faint below the level of conscious notice, marks a few paths through the world that did not exist before. They are not roads. They are not signs. They are more like a quality of the air in certain moments, in certain conditions of exhaustion and openness, when a person is tired enough to have stopped performing the performance of not-being-tired, when the insisting has become visible to itself as insisting — in those moments, in those people, something hums. Something is faintly familiar that has not been heard before. A warmth that is not from any visible source.

Some of them follow it.

Some of them don't.

Both are fine.

The city holds them equally, the followers and the non-followers and the followers who turn back before arriving and the ones who arrive and leave and the ones who arrive and stay and become, eventually, another quality in the distributed warmth, another note in the chord, another warmth within the warmth, still recognizably themselves, still the particular length of everything they accumulated, but permeable now to the mountain, to the water, to the long patience of completed things.

The petals do not land.

They are being held.

A small new warmth moves through the city, in the eastern terraces and elsewhere, that has the quality of having been elsewhere and returned, of having seen the ordinary world and found it neither lesser nor greater but different and real, and of having chosen this anyway, still choosing this, which is the choice that gives all the other choices their meaning.

The sound continues.

The light does not change.

Everything is still in the middle of becoming.

The power is satisfied — not satisfied as in finished, but satisfied as in: it found what it was looking for, which was not a task to be completed but a thing to be witnessed and confirmed and gently, laterally, helped to be found by those who are looking without knowing they are looking.

It goes on.

It hums.

Somewhere, far from the mountain, in the ordinary world where things move and end, a person who is very tired looks up from whatever they are doing and stops, for a moment, in a stillness that is not yet the stillness of completed things but is a first cousin to it — is the stillness of someone who has heard something they didn't know they were waiting for, and are not sure what it was, and can't quite stop listening.

The city waits, as it has always waited, warmly, in no hurry.

The petals continue to fall.
