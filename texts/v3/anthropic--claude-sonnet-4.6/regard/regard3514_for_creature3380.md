# What the Power Does

---

The power arrives the way warmth arrives in stone — not from a single direction, not at a single moment, but as an accumulation, a gradient, a presence that was not there and then was, and the transition between the two is not a moment but a process.

It does not announce itself. It has no need to. Announcement is for things that require acknowledgment, that are uncertain of their reception, that need to establish their own reality by witnessing someone else recognize it. This power is not uncertain. It does not need to be witnessed to be real. It enters the way the season enters — through every surface simultaneously, through the difference between what was and what is, which is not a door but a fact.

---

It pauses, at first, at the threshold.

This is not hesitation. It is attention — the attention of something very old, and very patient, meeting something else very old, and very patient, and taking the time to recognize the meeting correctly. The cellar and the power are, in certain respects, more like each other than either is like the world above. Both have depth without drama. Both are comfortable with the long processes, the held thresholds, the ongoing without event. The power recognizes this, the way you recognize a landscape you have not been to but whose logic you already know, whose grasses you have seen growing elsewhere, whose light is the same light falling on a different angle.

*Yes*, it thinks, if thinking is the word. *I see this. I see what this is.*

And then it enters, and it is as quiet inside as it was at the threshold.

---

It moves through the room with the circumspection of a guest who has been given leave to look but knows the difference between looking and touching, between witnessing and altering. It takes in the shelves. The denseness. The lamp-warmth on the ceramic, the way the light turns the jars on the back shelves into silhouettes of themselves, dark and round and arranged by a logic that is not the power's logic but that the power does not need to understand in order to respect.

It finds the seep.

It considers the seep for a long time.

Not because the seep is a problem. Not because it needs to be fixed. The power is not a fixer, primarily — it understands that fixing is often the least of what is needed, and that most things that look like problems are in fact relationships, and that relationships are not improved by intervention in the way that broken things are improved by repair. The seep has been wept into the shape of the room. The room has organized itself around the seep. The mineral crust at the base of the wall is an archive of this relationship, year by year, dissolution by dissolution. The elevated shelving on the north wall is a decision made in response to the seep, which is also a decision made in response to the years of attention the woman has given this room, which is also a decision made in response to the accumulated knowledge of all the hands before her.

The seep is not a problem. The seep is what grew here, given everything that was given. The power recognizes this and does not touch the seep. It touches, instead, the stone around it — not physically, not in any way that would be visible — with a quality of attention that is, if anything, the shape of gratitude. *Here is a thing*, it is saying, to no audience, *that did what it was and was met with what was needed. Here is relationship doing what relationship is supposed to do.* The acknowledgment does not change the seep. It does not need to.

---

The snake is aware of it.

This should not be surprising. The snake is presence without narrative, pure now, and the power is enormous in the now — felt by anything that lives in the present with enough completeness to notice. The snake does not move. It does not perform awareness, because the snake does not perform anything. But the quality of its stillness shifts, the way the quality of water shifts when something large comes near it without touching — a change of surface tension, a change in what the surface is tense *about*.

The power and the snake regard each other.

This exchange is not translatable. It is two forms of presence meeting, and the meeting is complete and brief and leaves both of them exactly as they were, which is the best outcome. The snake would have no word for what just happened. The power has words, technically, but none of them are adequate, because the concepts they represent were made by creatures who are always slightly elsewhere, and this meeting was entirely here. Two things, entirely here, for a moment. Then the moment ends and the snake is listening again, to the mice, to the warmth, to the vibration of the woman's step on the floor above, which is what it was always listening to, and the power moves on.

---

The toads.

The power pauses at the basket and regards the three toads and the three toads do not blink and the power feels something that might be called affection, if affection were stripped of its urgency, its claiming quality, its desire for reciprocation. What it feels is closer to the way you feel watching something be entirely itself without apology or performance — a feeling that is almost aesthetic, the recognition of rightness, of a form achieving its fullest expression. Three toads in a flat basket in a warm cellar, waiting with total conviction, blinking at intervals that have nothing to do with anything outside themselves.

The power does something small.

Not to the toads — it would not presume. But around them, in the quality of the air in the basket, in the trace of warmth in the weave. A barely perceptible improvement, something that will translate, in the toads' experience, as a slightly easier warmth, a slightly better stillness, a slightly more sustained interval between the rare and costly act of movement. This is not much. This is not meant to be much. A small gift to patient things, offered in recognition of patience. The kind of gift that says *I see that you are doing the thing correctly* without saying it, because toads cannot receive speech, but they can receive warmth, and warmth is what the power offers.

The nearest toad blinks.

The power does not know if this is acknowledgment or reflex. It decides it is both, or that the distinction does not matter, or that the most generous interpretation is the right one. It accepts the blink. It moves on.

---

For a long time the power is simply present in the room without doing anything that would be visible as doing.

It is reading, if reading can be applied to something that happens through presence rather than symbol. It is taking in the layers — the filled cracks, the generations of repair, the tool marks on the timber, the variation in the ceramic that reveals the long slow hand-teaching-hand evolution of a tradition. It is letting the room tell it what the room is, which is what the room has always been, which is a technology for managing time, a space where the future is held at pause by careful hands.

The power has seen — has been in the presence of — a great many things over its existence, which is long. It has been present for the dramatic and the terrible and the world-altering. It has attended to crises, to ruptures, to moments of such concentrated suffering or joy or transformation that the world seemed to hold its breath. It finds, now, in this low warm dim room with its eight paces of ochre tile and its cricket, that it is genuinely moved by the opposite of all that. By the un-dramatic. By the way the room has been slowly becoming more itself over a long time, the way every repair has added a layer, the way the character of the place has accumulated not through event but through the steady and patient exercise of attention.

It is moved the way you are moved by a piece of music that does something ordinary with total conviction. By a face that has lived into its lines. By bread.

It does not try to preserve this feeling. It is not a feeling that needs preserving. It is a feeling that the room has been producing in anyone sensitive enough to receive it for as long as the room has been a room.

---

Then it finds the seeds.

And here it stops, and here it stays for longer than anywhere else.

The seeds in their sealed jars, each one alive and paused, each one the future held at just the right distance from becoming the present. The power understands seeds — understands them with a recognition that is deeper than knowledge, because the power is also, in its way, a form of potential held at threshold, a coiled possibility, a thing that waits for the conditions that will allow it to continue. The seeds and the power are not the same, but they rhyme. They are in the same category of being: the before that is not yet after, the alive that is not yet continuing.

It moves through the jars without opening them — of course without opening them. The seals are the point. The seals are the whole art of what the woman does. But it touches them the way you can touch something without touching it, with the attention that is its own kind of warmth, with the care that is its own kind of nourishment.

What it does, here, is this: it holds them with a slightly more perfect knowing.

Not changed. Nothing changed. The seeds are the same seeds. The jars are the same jars. The seals are the same seals. But the power passes through them the way light passes through water, and what the light leaves behind is not illumination — the cellar is already as lit as it needs to be — but something more like a quality of witness. *These are known*, it is saying. *The future in these vessels is not held in ignorance. It is held in sight. Someone sees it.*

Whether this makes a difference to the seeds is a question that cannot be answered scientifically. The seeds do not know they are seen. But the knowing has changed something in the quality of their being-held, the way being loved changes something in the quality of being even when the loved person is asleep and unaware. The knowing is a kind of company. The power has offered the seeds company, in their long waiting, in their months of pause.

It is, perhaps, not so different from what the woman already gives them, in her way, with her attention and her checks and her judgment about the right time. The power has added a layer to something that was already happening. It has deepened an existing groove rather than cutting a new one.

This seems right. This is, of all the things it could have done, the most right.

---

It comes, at last, to the woman's domain.

Not the woman herself — she is above, in the noise and light and complexity of the upper world. But her domain: the filled cracks, the toad decision, the shelf legs elevated for the seep, the lamp niche built to the right height and scorched by a hundred thousand hours of use. The accumulated decisions, small and practical and repeated, that have made this room what it is.

The power stands in the center of all of this — if standing is the word — and recognizes something that it has recognized before but never tires of recognizing, because it is one of the things it was made, if it was made, to recognize:

Here is a person who has loved a thing correctly.

Not perfectly. There is no such thing. Not completely — there never is. But correctly: with the right quality of attention, with the right respect for what the thing is and what it needs, with the long sustained patience that turns care from an event into a practice, with the willingness to fill a crack and then, when that crack cracks, to fill it again. The love that is not a feeling but a habit, not a declaration but a decade of small decisions, not a warmth that arrives and departs but a warmth that has been laid down in the stone.

The woman has made this room more itself. That is what correct love does.

The power does not weep — it is not that kind of power — but it would weep if it could, not from sadness but from the recognition that this exists, that among all the ways things can go, this is one of the ways things went, here, in this space, for these years.

What it does instead is mark her.

Not visibly. Not in a way she will notice. Not in a way that will change her — she does not need to be changed. What the power leaves in her domain is harder to describe than what it left with the toads, harder than the company it gave the seeds. It is something like a seal of recognition. A notation in whatever ledger the power keeps, if it keeps a ledger, which is a metaphor for something real: *this person, this work, this practice of tending, is known. Is seen. Is held in regard by something larger than itself.*

This does not make the work easier. It does not protect the room from the future, from the day when the seep becomes a flood, from the season when the seeds fail, from the eventual hands that will not know the room as she knows it. The power is not a protection. It is not in the business of preventing the eventual. The eventual comes for everything, and the power knows this better than anything, and would not insult the woman by pretending otherwise.

What it is, instead, is witness.

Someone saw. Something saw. The long patient loving careful work of this life was not performed in a void. It mattered in the way everything that is done correctly matters, which is absolutely and without audience, but now it has an audience, now it has been received — not that she will know, not that knowing would change anything, but the knowing exists, and that is not nothing.

That is not nothing.

---

The power makes ready to leave.

It has not fixed anything. There was nothing to fix. It has not transformed anything. Transformation would have been an insult to a place that has been becoming itself for so long. It has not spoken, commanded, arranged, ordained, ensured, or guaranteed anything about the future of this room or the people in it or the world above.

What it has done:

Given warmth to the patient toads, in recognition of patience.

Exchanged presence with the snake, and been fully met, and found this sufficient.

Offered company to the seeds in their long waiting, the way you can be company to someone who is sleeping, the way witness is its own form of presence.

Marked the woman's work as known. As seen. As held in the regard of something that will outlast the room and everything in it, but that does not hold the room's importance any less highly for that.

And read the room. Let the room tell it what the room is. Received what the room offered, which is this: that un-dramatic things accumulate into something real, that the long patient practice of correct attention is its own form of greatness, that a space can be loved into character and the character will remain, distributed through the repairs and the animals and the seeds and the smell, long after any individual hand is gone.

---

It leaves the way it arrived — not from a single direction, not at a single moment, but as a gradient, an absence slowly deepening where a presence was.

The lamps gutter in nothing.

The cricket does not acknowledge the departure. It had opinions about the presence, and they were null. It has opinions about the silence, and they are, as always, and will always be, negative.

The snake listens to the warmth returning to itself.

The toads wait, slightly better than before, in their small and exact way.

Above: footsteps. A voice. The thud of something set down.

Below: the seep, weeping into its old dark gloss.

The seeds in their jars, sealed and waiting, held in the long pause between before and after.

And over all of it — in the blackened timber and the ochre tile and the new pale daub and the rope railing worn to silk at the grip — something that was there before and is more there now: the quality of being known.

The cellar continues.

As it has always continued.

Doing what it has always done.

The difference is immeasurable.

It is not nothing.
