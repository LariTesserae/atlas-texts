# What the Wise Power Does

---

The power arrives the way the light arrives: without announcement, already present by the time you might notice it. It does not descend. It does not part anything. It is simply, suddenly, attending — its attention falling on the seep meadow the way water falls on porous ground, not striking but permeating, finding its way along every available path.

It takes a long time before it does anything.

This is the first and perhaps most important thing about what it does: it waits. Not from uncertainty, not from gathering courage, but because a power that is genuinely wise has learned that its first impulse, however benevolent, is nearly always an imposition. So it waits. It attends. It learns the place on its own terms before it allows itself to have terms.

It reads the seep.

It reads the seep the way you read a document you know is important but are not sure you understand — carefully, slowly, going back to re-examine what seemed clear. The water has been moving through the fracture in the granite for how long? The power follows the path backward, tracing the seep's history: the rain that became the water table, the water table that found the gradient in the rock, the gradient that has been this exact gradient since the stone settled into this configuration, which happened in a period when the ice above it was a mile thick and the stone had no choice but to crack along its own fault lines and open a path. The seep is the stone's memory of pressure. The seep is very old. The power notes this.

It reads the sphagnum.

Not quickly. Sphagnum is not a quick read. The power follows the logic of the hyaline cell — the elegant, almost impossible design of a cell that dies in order to hold water in order to sustain the living cells beside it — and something in the power pauses here. Not with surprise, exactly. With recognition. This is a kind of wisdom it knows from somewhere else, the wisdom of the structure that sacrifices its own living to maintain the conditions for life. The dead cell that holds the wet. The power sits with this for what would be a long time in any reckoning that counted time, and then it continues.

It reads the fungi.

The kilometers of filament per gram. The network below the moss, below the soil, threading through the roots, connecting in its dark patient way things that have no idea they are connected, offering the exchange of minerals for sugar to a sedge that doesn't know the word for exchange, that doesn't need to know it, that simply has the roots and the fungi have the access and the chemistry is the agreement and it has always been enough. The power finds itself moved by this. Not sentimentally — it is not a sentimental power — but in the way that a thing recognizes its own principle enacted by a different mechanism in a different medium. The principle: connection does not require awareness of connection to function. The gift does not require the recipient to know its source.

The power considers this for a long time.

---

Then it reads what is not here.

The absence of animals. The meadow has been doing this long enough, by this point in its history, that the absence feels established. The power considers whether to do something about this — whether the absence is a wound, a lack, an incompleteness. Whether the benevolent thing would be to bring the small darting life, the hovering, the grazing, the singing.

It decides, carefully, that it will not.

Here is its reasoning, and the reasoning is important because this is a wise power and its reasoning is worth following:

The meadow does not lack animals the way a body lacks a necessary organ. It lacks animals the way a mathematical proof lacks narrative — the narrative might be added, might even illuminate, but the proof does not need it to be true. The proof is complete. The meadow is complete. Its completeness is of a specific kind — rooted, sessile, slow, organized around the wet and the light and the geological patience of the seep — and to introduce motion, consumption, the quick metabolisms of animal life, would not be addition but alteration. The meadow would become a different meadow. Perhaps a richer meadow by some measures. Perhaps a lesser one by others.

The power recognizes that it does not know. And recognizing it does not know, it does not act.

This is harder for a benevolent power than it sounds. The benevolent impulse is toward doing, toward giving, toward amelioration. The wise part must govern the benevolent part, must insist on restraint where the benevolent part sees only opportunity to help. A power that is only benevolent gives what it wants to give. A power that is also wise gives what is needed, and first asks whether anything is needed at all.

The meadow is not suffering. The meadow does not experience its completeness as a lack. The meadow is exactly what the seep and the elevation and the long working-out of this specific place have made it, and it has been doing this without witnesses for longer than it could be said to have been doing anything. It does not need the power's gifts. It does not need to be told it is beautiful. It does not need to be preserved against anything currently threatening it, because nothing currently threatens it, and to act against hypothetical futures would be to disturb the actual present, which is settled and whole.

The power withdraws the impulse to act. This requires effort. The effort is invisible but real.

---

What it does instead is notice.

This might sound like nothing. It is not nothing.

The power turns the full capacity of its attention — which is large, which is capable of holding more than any ordinary attention can hold — toward the meadow, and it witnesses it. It witnesses the diatoms in their glass cases on the rock-face. It witnesses the sphagnum's color-memory of past light conditions. It witnesses the seep-water moving in the fracture, one molecule-width at a time, in the direction that gravity and porosity together indicate. It witnesses the mycorrhizal exchange in the sedge-root dark. It witnesses the cyanobacteria making oxygen in a film on a wet stone at altitude, doing what their ancestors were doing before the atmosphere had oxygen in it, before there were atmospheres worth naming, working out their small chemistry in a way that changed everything on this planet and that they have no knowledge of having changed.

The power witnesses all of this simultaneously, in the way that it can.

And in witnessing, it does something that may be the most significant thing a power can do for a place that doesn't need to be fixed:

It makes the place real to itself in a new way.

Not to the meadow — the meadow remains unaware, rightly, happily unaware. But in the act of complete attention, the power becomes the place's witness in a way that the place has never had before. Not a human witness, who would bring their categories and their beauty-hunger and their sense of what high mountain places are supposed to mean. Not a scientific witness, extracting data. A witness that can hold the whole of it without needing it to be anything other than it is. The sphagnum-logic, the seep-logic, the diatom in its silica house, the fungal network's dark patient exchange — all of it held at once, all of it seen truly, none of it required to explain itself or justify its presence or speak to any concern outside itself.

The place, unbeknownst to itself, is fully known.

There is something that happens when a thing is fully known, even if the thing itself doesn't know it's happening. A kind of completion. The moss has been doing its work for centuries without any witness adequate to it. The fungi have been threading their patient kilometers through the dark without any mind capable of comprehending what that means attending to them. The seep has been seeping since the ice retreated and not one consciousness has followed its path back through the granite to the accumulated memory of rain and pressure that produced it.

Now one has.

The power holds this knowledge gently, the way the hyaline cell holds water — openly, available, without squeezing.

---

It does one other thing.

Quietly, in a way that touches nothing visible, it adjusts something in the relationship between the seep and the fracture.

There is a place in the rock, forty feet below the meadow's surface, where the fracture narrows — a pinch in the path, a constriction that has been building for some decades as a small crystal growth expands incrementally into the channel. Left to its own pace, this constriction would close fully in perhaps two hundred years. The seep would then be rerouted, emerging elsewhere on the slope, finding a new path. The meadow would have ten or twenty years of decreasing saturation before it began to change — the sphagnum thinning first in the lowest cushions, then the sedges shifting, then the whole complex of what this place is slowly reorganizing into something drier and different.

This would not be a catastrophe. It would be succession. The place that would come after would have its own completeness, its own logic, its own long-established residents. Change is not harm. The world is full of former seep meadows that became heathlands and are whole.

But the power sits with this for a while and feels — and it does feel, this is not a bloodless calculation — that what this particular place has achieved, what it has taken this specific combination of factors and centuries to make, is worth the continuation it would have anyway, if not for the crystal.

The crystal is not part of the meadow's logic. It is not the meadow's succession, properly speaking. It is a mineralogical accident, a small perturbation in the fracture's history that has no meaning in the meadow's own terms. It is noise in the signal.

The power does not remove the crystal. It does not command it. It does what it can do, which is hard to describe in the language of doing things: it holds the crystal in its attention along with the seep and the meadow and the full weight of what has taken centuries to balance here, and in that holding it permits the crystal to know itself as what it is — a small thing in a large context — and something in the crystal's growth, which has no intention, finds the path of its least resistance shifting by an unmeasurably small amount, and the direction of that growth turns, over the next decades, by a degree or two, away from the channel.

The seep continues.

No one will know this happened.

The sphagnum will not know. The sedge will not know. The power does not require that they know. The mycorrhizal network will continue its exchange in the dark, the diatoms will continue their glass-housed photosynthesis in the water-film, the seep will continue to seep with the patience of the geological, and none of it will know that something attended to it fully and turned, with the lightest possible hand, one small thing that was not part of the place's own story, so that the place could continue being its story.

---

The power withdraws the way the light withdraws at evening: gradually, fully, leaving nothing behind that announces its having been.

The meadow is unchanged.

The meadow is itself.

The water moves in the fracture in the rock. The moss is red where it is red and gold where it is gold. The sedges stand in their deliberate spacing, and the seep is a principle rather than a source, and the diatoms are there on the stone, in their glass cases, exact and unconcerned.

The place continues.

This, the power decides — moving on to wherever a power like this moves — is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: mostly attention, occasionally the lightest possible touch, and the discipline to leave a finished thing finished.
