# What the Power Did

---

It saw the plateau the way the plateau sees nothing — all at once, from no position, without the forty-kilometer limit, without the obscuring air. It saw the full surface: the dark brick and the standing water and the engine halls and the canal-freight and the bat-emergence and the slow deepening of the biological stain. It saw below the surface to the drainage channels and the wet substrate and the things that had evolved past the equipment for vision. It saw the water the bedrock was holding.

It understood, immediately, that it had arrived at something that did not need what powers like it usually bring.

This took it a moment. Powers of this kind are accustomed to places that are suffering in obvious ways — places where the water is wrong, where the soil has been taken past some threshold, where the animal populations have collapsed into remnants of themselves, where the people have developed the particular exhausted creativity of those who must solve in a generation what took generations to break. The plateau was not this. The plateau was the thing powers like it are usually working toward. A proof that it was possible.

It checked again. It was thorough.

The subsidence was within the range the plateau's infrastructure had been managing for generations. The water in the deep substrate was clean. The biological stain was healthy in the only sense applicable to something that old — it was continuing, it was deepening, it was doing what it had always done. The bat population was large and stable. The canal life was dense and cross-referenced in the ways that density and cross-reference produce resilience. The draft animals were, as far as it could determine, content in the only sense applicable to things that patient — they were doing what they were built to do, in conditions suited to it, in a relationship with the people that had made both parties into more than either would have been alone.

The people were tired in the ordinary way. They were sometimes unjust to each other. There was grief in them and there was longing in them and there was the specific ache of people who live in a place so old and so full that they will never know all of it, which is a kind of sweet sorrow that does not require remedy. They were competent and close and they had the long memory of a people who had been in one place long enough for the place to get into them. They were not happy in the thin sense. They were something thicker than that.

The power sat with this for a while.

---

What it did not do:

It did not alter the subsidence rate, which was within the range the plateau had always managed, which was part of how the plateau stayed wet, which was how everything that needed wet survived.

It did not brighten the sky or reduce the damp or make the air less thick, because the sky and the damp and the thick air were load-bearing. The bats needed the thermal gradients. The canal life needed the particular water chemistry. The biological stain needed the northern face of the sandstone buildings to keep their moisture through the afternoon. The plateau's weather was the plateau's infrastructure, and you do not repair what is working.

It did not take the grief from the people, because their grief was specific and earned and woven through their knowing of each other, and taking it would have left gaps in the weave.

It did not make itself known to the people in ways that would change how they understood their situation, because they understood their situation correctly: they were part of something old and they knew it, and adding a benevolent power to that understanding would have introduced a distortion, a new variable, a reason to look upward instead of at the wet brick under their feet. The plateau was not a place that rewarded looking upward.

---

What it did:

*First*, it found the three places in the deep drainage system where the silt had been accumulating across decades in ways the surface crew had not yet noticed — would not notice for another twelve years, by which point the blockage would be significant and the remediation work would be hard. Not catastrophic. Not irreversible. Just hard, and disruptive, and preventable.

The power adjusted the silt. Not quickly, not dramatically — nothing that would register as change in any instrument the plateau maintained, nothing that would appear in any record as an anomaly or an event. It introduced, into the water chemistry of those three channel sections, a very slight modification — something the water had almost been doing on its own, something at the edge of its own tendency. The silt would not accumulate in those sections in the same way. The blockage would not develop. The drainage would continue.

No one would ever know this had happened. The drainage would simply work, as it had always worked, and the plateau would continue to hold its water as it had always held its water, and the twelve years of hard remediation work would never need to happen. Something that would have been a problem would simply not become one. The people who would have spent those weeks underground in difficult conditions would spend those weeks doing other things, and they would not know to be grateful for this, and that was fine.

This is the kind of work that powers of this kind, when they are functioning well, recognize as their best work.

*Second*, it found the one place — there was only one — where the relationship between a handler and a draft animal had been damaged in a way that was not healing on its own. The handler was young and had been frightened in a loading incident and had responded to the fear in the way that frightened people sometimes respond, with a sharpness that was not their nature, and the animal had noted this with the long memory that patient things have, and the trust that had been established over three years of working together had thinned.

It would probably have healed on its own, in time. These things usually do. But the power nudged it: nothing visible, nothing that would be experienced as intervention. It introduced, into the specific morning when the handler arrived at the precinct with something like apology already present in their body, already working its way toward expression, a small favorable circumstance. The first load of the day was a light one. The route was clear. The light fell at an angle that made the canal-side look the way that places look when you're glad to be in them. The animal was in good humor — something in the night's rest had been easy, the warmth of the vent-system exactly right. The handler paused beside the animal before attaching the harness, just stood there, and the animal turned its head, and what passed between them in that moment was not any kind of miracle but was allowed to be what it needed to be.

The trust rebuilt. The partnership continued. The power moved on.

*Third*, and most carefully:

There was a woman in the eastern freight district, in her fifties, who had been carrying for eleven years a piece of knowledge that the plateau needed her to give to someone and she had not yet found the way to give it. Not a secret — nothing dramatic. A kind of knowledge so specific to its location that it barely translated into language, something she understood about the behavior of a particular section of the old water management system in the period before heavy rain, something she had learned from her predecessor who had learned it from hers, something that had kept that section operational through conditions that should, by the official engineering assessment, have been beyond its tolerance. The system had better responses when someone who understood it was near it. She had understood this and had not known how to explain it, and was aware, in the way that people in their fifties become aware, that she would not always be there, and that awareness had become a weight.

The power found the young worker in the same district who had, without knowing it, already begun to develop the particular quality of attention that this knowledge required. Who had been stopping near that section for reasons she couldn't fully articulate, who had begun to notice things about its behavior that she didn't yet have the framework to interpret. Who was ready, in other words. Who only needed proximity to the right teacher.

The power arranged a shift assignment. An entirely ordinary reassignment of the kind that happened every few months as the freight loads shifted. The young worker was assigned to assist on a channel maintenance check in that section. The older woman was leading it.

They worked alongside each other for six hours. The older woman showed the young one three things. The young one asked a question that caused the older woman to articulate, for the first time, something she had known in her body for years and not known how to say. They both understood that what was happening was what was happening. The knowledge moved.

The weight the older woman had been carrying for eleven years settled slightly. The young worker went home with something new in her attention. The section of the water management system continued to be looked after correctly.

The shift assignment was filed in the district records and no one ever looked at it again.

---

This is what the power did.

It did not heal what was not wounded. It did not improve what was already what it needed to be. It did not announce itself or leave traces or create stories.

It cleared a drain. It helped a relationship find its way back to itself. It connected a teacher to a student.

Three things, all of them small, all of them at the level of the plateau's own processes — nudges in directions that the plateau was already tending, friction removed from movements already underway. Everything it did looked, from the inside, like the plateau doing what the plateau does: continuing, absorbing, slowly becoming more of what it already was.

---

Before it left, it did one final thing, which was not an action but a kind of witness.

It found the oldest piece of the biological stain — the section on the northern face of a warehouse in the second canal district, the section that had been there longer than any other, the section that was deepest and darkest and most thoroughly incorporated into the sandstone behind it — and it attended to it for a while.

It did not speak to it. The biological stain was not the kind of thing one spoke to.

But it paid attention. It recognized. It stayed long enough to register, in some accurate way, what was there: the extraordinary age of it, the patience, the complete sufficiency of the life it was living, the depth of its incorporation into the stone that had grown around it and through which it had grown. The way it was not trying to be more than it was. The way being what it was was already more than most things managed.

This was not for the biological stain's benefit. The biological stain did not need anything from the power and did not experience the power's attention in any registerable way.

It was for the power's benefit.

Sometimes the right response to what you are looking at is to look at it. To be present to it. To let what is good about it be fully received, not converted into action, not made into a lesson or a project, but simply taken in by something capable of taking it in and held there.

The plateau deserved to be witnessed by something that could see all of it. The power could see all of it. This was the appropriate use of that capacity.

Then it left, having done what it could do and no more, which is the mark of the kind of power that has understood something important about what power is for.

The plateau continued.

The drain ran clear.

The partnership held.

The knowledge moved.

The biological stain deepened by one year's worth of depth, which is not a measurable amount but is nevertheless an amount, and added it to everything that had come before, and the stone was patient around it, and the water held below was as held as it had ever been, and the morning came with its thin cold directness and fell on the dark wet brick and on the still water in the street-depressions and on the chimney stacks where the birds were beginning, and the plateau was entirely absorbed in what it was doing, as it had always been, as it would be.
