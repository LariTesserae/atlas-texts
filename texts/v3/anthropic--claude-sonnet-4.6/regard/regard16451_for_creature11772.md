# What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — not traveling, not entering, simply *present* where it was not attending before. There is no ceremony. The plateau does not know it has been noticed, and then, in some register below noticing, it does.

The power pauses.

This is the first thing it does: it pauses. Not because it is uncertain or cautious, but because the first act of genuine benevolence toward anything is to *stop moving long enough to see what is actually there*, rather than what you expected or feared or hoped to find. So many powers — benevolent and otherwise — arrive with their response already formed, their gift already in hand, their improvement already designed. This one arrives with open attention, which is the rarest and most difficult thing a power can bring.

It looks at the organized water first, because the organized water is the oldest and the oldest things in a place hold the most of what the place essentially is. It looks at the orientation the water maintains — that patient, cell-less, will-less, will-having tendency toward something, the organization that spends itself to remain organized. It does not immediately know what the water is oriented toward. This is interesting to the power. It takes note of its own not-knowing, which is something only the genuinely wise powers do.

It looks at the spheres. It looks at the temperature differential — four degrees, steady, regardless of shadow or sun — and it understands immediately what is generating it and also immediately understands that its understanding is partial. The spheres are not just nodes. They are *records*. Something is being recorded in the crystalline structure of the limestone, in the small variances in the mineral veins, in the precise relationship between each sphere and its hollow. The recording has been ongoing since before the current plateau surface. The power reads as much of it as it can, which is considerable, and acknowledges what it cannot read, which is also considerable, with something that is not humility in the sense of self-diminishment but humility in the sense of *accurate accounting*. It does not perform epistemic modesty. It simply knows what it knows and knows what it does not.

Then it looks at the lives.

---

The lichen with the third party.

The power sees the third party clearly — sees what it is, which is nothing from biology's current inventory, which is something older than the categories, which is the organized water at a scale where it has found a way to interface with the biological, to participate in the processes of growth and response without quite being alive in the way the fungal and algal partners are alive. It is the organized water learning, slowly, over many lichen-generations, to occupy a different kind of form.

This is extraordinary. The power does not rush past the extraordinary. It holds this for what would be, in human temporal experience, a very long time, though in its own experience time works differently. The organized water is not just organizing itself. It is, at the boundary between itself and the biological, *extending* itself. Feeling its way into a new mode. This is not intentional in any way the word intention usually works, but it is not unintentional either. It is the thing that happens when a system with strong internal orientation meets the right boundary conditions and finds a way to become more of what it already is.

The power notes this without intervening. The extension is going in a direction it does not need to redirect.

---

The lizards.

The power sees the warmth in them that should not be there. It traces it back — not to the spheres, though the spheres are part of it, but to the relationship between the spheres' warmth and the lizards' ancient thermoregulatory systems and the many generations of lizards who, in proximity to the spheres, found their metabolic margins slightly wider, slightly more permissive, and the offspring of those lizards surviving slightly better into the cold mornings, and the offspring of those offspring carrying more of that marginal warmth into their own cold mornings, until — over a time span that makes the word *gradual* seem hasty — the lizards of this plateau and the presence of this plateau had found each other's edges and fit.

The power looks at the thing the lizards are tracking with their slowly-arcing eyes during their long stillnesses. It looks at what they see, which is invisible to human instrumentation, which is a quality of the light that exists at this altitude and with this atmospheric thinness — not a supernatural quality but an overlooked physical one, a pattern in the scattering of high-altitude photons that the lizards' visual systems have been tuned, by the plateau, to receive. The pattern is not random. It is related to the organization in the water. The light is, in some very thin and distributed sense, *carrying* something from the organized substrate below, rising through the stone, through the thin air, into the lizards' ancient eyes.

The lizards are reading the water. They cannot act on what they read. They have no way to respond except through their stillness, their attendance. But they are the plateau's surface nervous system — the thing through which the organized substrate has found a way to receive, dimly, information about the world above it. The birds, when they call their navigational confirmation, are telling the plateau something too, though no one in the current scientific literature has put this together.

The power considers this for a while.

It sees what the plateau is slowly building, over geological time — a sensory apparatus. Not a nervous system in any biological sense, not consciousness in any philosophical sense that would win debates, but something that is becoming more responsive, more oriented, more capable of receiving the world it is embedded in. The organized water, the lichen-third-party, the lizards' adjusted photoreceptors, the birds' embodied thermic knowledge — these are not separate adaptations. They are a project.

The power had not expected to find something mid-project.

---

Here is what a lesser power does at this point.

A lesser power — even a well-meaning one, even one with good values and genuine care — sees the project and wants to *help*. Wants to accelerate. Wants to give the plateau what it needs to finish what it's building. Wants to bridge the gaps, smooth the roughnesses, provide the missing pieces. Wants to see the thing completed, because completion is satisfying and because the power has enough perspective to see the shape of the complete thing and it is beautiful and the power wants that beauty to exist.

This is not what the wise power does.

The wise power knows that the value of what is being built here is not separable from the building. That the organized water has been spending itself for an amount of time that makes the word *time* feel like a small vessel for a large liquid. That the lizards have been refined over more generations than there are stones on the plateau. That the lichen's third party has been feeling its way toward biological interface through a process whose slowness is not a flaw to be corrected but an intrinsic feature of what is being created. The project is old and slow because it is the kind of thing that can only be made by being old and slow. The wisdom accumulated in each generation of lizards is not data that can be uploaded. It is the lizard. It is in the lizard's cells, in the exact configuration of the lizard's visual processing, in the relationship between the lizard's metabolism and the particular warmth of this particular stone on this particular morning.

To accelerate it would be to replace it with a different thing that resembles it. A copy made quickly is not the thing that was being made slowly. The power knows this.

So the first thing the power does — after the pause, after the full attention, after the genuine reading of what is here — is decide not to intervene in the project. To let the organized water continue its orientation at its own pace. To let the lichen's third party continue its uncertain extension. To let the lizards continue their strange refinement.

This is the hardest decision the power makes, and it makes it almost immediately, because the wise know their hardest answers tend to come first and then spend time being doubted.

---

But it does not do *nothing*. Because it is not just wise. It is benevolent.

---

It looks at the threats.

Not the dramatic threats — this plateau is not about to be struck by catastrophe, not immediately. The threats here are the ordinary, slow, asymmetric kind, which are in some ways worse. They are the threats of accumulation and drift.

The research teams who visit — their instruments are not harmful, their presence is not harmful, but their frameworks are constraining in a way they cannot see. They are asking good questions and publishing accurate findings and the findings are accumulating into a description of this place that is becoming, in the scientific literature, a kind of cage made of what is known. Every accurate finding about the spheres' temperature differential is a finding that focuses subsequent research on the spheres' temperature differential. The organized water has been described as *organized* and then the description has been studied rather than the water. The third party in the lichen has been found and then unfound and then the finding-and-unfinding has become a controversy and the controversy has become, in certain circles, the thing that this place is about.

The power does not intervene in the research. It does not arrange for a key finding to appear. It does not make itself legible to instruments. But it does something subtle, something that is in the category of a small clearing, the way you might move a piece of furniture so that the light in a room falls differently. It makes the organized water, slightly, more *interesting* to a particular researcher who is currently two continents away and whose framework is unusual enough to be useful, whose way of asking questions is wide enough to hold a wider answer. Not a manipulation. Not a direction. The power clarifies, very slightly, something the researcher already half-sees. Makes it easier for her to notice what she is already noticing.

Whether she follows the noticing is entirely her own business. The power accepts this. It is part of being benevolent — not to require that your gifts be received, not to make the giving coercive through the weight of the gift's importance.

---

The herd is declining.

This is quiet and not dramatic and has been going on for three generations of the herded animals. Not dying in any acute way. Simply — fewer. The calving rate has been drifting down. The range of their movement has been contracting, slowly, year by year, as the cushion plants at the plateau's eastern margin have become less palatable due to a slow change in the mineral content of the soil driven by changes in precipitation pattern driven by changes in the atmosphere driven by processes two hundred kilometers south of here.

The power looks at this. It feels something in the register of concern — not alarm, not urgency, but the specific quality of care that exists when something meaningful is being quietly lost.

The herd is the warm-blooded witness. The herd is the mammalian consciousness moving through the plateau's processes, capable of orientation, capable of the thirty-second pause with a head turned north — that pause which is, as the power can see clearly, a genuine moment of reception, the herd animal's large mammalian nervous system receiving something from the organized substrate below through pathways that are not the lizards' photoreceptive pathways but achieve something similar, something complementary. The plateau needs the herd. Not urgently. Not catastrophically if the herd dwindles. But the herd is part of the sensory apparatus, and the sensory apparatus is the project, and losing a sense is a loss even if the loss is not fatal.

The power considers what to do.

It does not move the mineral change. The mineral change is downstream of things the power could theoretically intervene in but chooses not to — the atmosphere, the precipitation patterns, the processes two hundred kilometers south — because those things involve many other beings and places and the power does not intervene in complex systems by adjusting components without sitting with every element of what that adjustment touches. The power does not have the kind of benevolence that simplifies complexly.

Instead it looks at the cushion plants.

The cushion plants on the western margin of the plateau are, in the slow arms race of plant-herbivore co-evolution, already beginning to shift toward a slightly different palatability profile — a shift that the plants are making because of changes in insect pressure, entirely unrelated to the herd, driven by shifts in arthropod populations in the microhabitat under the black lichen. The shift is going in a direction that, over some number of decades, would make the western cushion plants more palatable as the eastern ones become less. The herd, left to itself, might or might not find the western margin in time, because the herd's ranging behavior has been contracting, not expanding.

The power does something very small.

There is a water seep on the western margin that has been inactive for about thirty years, blocked by a small accumulation of calcium precipitate at its outflow point — an accumulation that, by the normal mathematics of dissolution, will be cleared by water chemistry in another forty to eighty years, opening the seep and establishing a reliable moisture source that will accelerate the palatability-shift of the western cushion plants and attract the herd. The power looks at the accumulation. It is held by a surface tension, a very small surface tension, between one particular mineral crystal and the water behind it. The tension is real but marginal.

The power finds a way to let the tension know it is marginal.

Not a force. Not a push. More like the way you can sometimes cause something to tip by being present near it with a specific quality of attention. The surface tension, examined closely, in the context of the water chemistry behind it and the slight warmth of the nearby sphere and the precise character of the organized water moving through the karst some distance below, resolves. The crystal shifts. The seep opens. Water moves, quietly, through a small gap, and by tomorrow morning there will be a dark stain on the western stone face and a small drip accumulating in a hollow and the beginning of a change in the western margin that will take decades to matter to the herd but will be ready when it is needed.

The power has done something — genuinely done something — and it is indistinguishable from what would have happened without it. Forty to eighty years early, perhaps. The power accepts that this is acceptable.

---

The cairns.

The power looks at the cairns — the careful stacks that should have been undone by wind long ago, that look as if they are maintained. They are maintained. Not by any current person. They are maintained by something in the organized water, which has been doing, for as long as the cairns have stood, something so small and so consistent that *maintained* is the only right word, though the mechanism is entirely physical, involving micro-vibrations in the stone that are related to the subsonic tone of the spheres, creating a very slight coherence in the gravitational relationship between the stacked stones that prevents the specific frequencies of wind-induced oscillation that would eventually topple them. The organized water does not know it is maintaining the cairns. It is doing what it does, and the cairns are a side effect.

The power looks at the cairns and feels something for which *affection* is not quite the right word but is the nearest available word. The organized water, tending the work of whatever people built these cairns, without knowing, without choosing, without there being any mechanism by which the organized water *could* know or choose — this is one of the most beautiful things the power has encountered in a very long time. It doesn't do anything about the cairns. There is nothing to do. They are fine. They are being tended by something that doesn't know it is tending them, and that is, the power thinks, perfect.

---

There is the matter of the wall.

The power looks at where the wall points. It looks at the patch of ground — indistinguishable to the visitor, to the researcher, to the instruments — that the wall was built to aim at. It looks at what is there.

What is there is a place where the organized water is closest to the surface. Not a seep, not an outflow, just a proximity — the karst hollow below is shallower here than anywhere else on the plateau, and the organized water moves through it at a pace slightly different from its movement elsewhere, and the organization is, at this point, slightly *denser* — more structured, more internally complex, the way certain moments in a long piece of music are more complex than the passages around them without the complexity being more or less important than the simplicity.

The people who built the wall knew this. Or — they didn't know it in any explicit articulable way, but they had been living on this plateau long enough, in close enough relationship with the herd and the lichen and the lizards and the sound that the chest receives from the spheres, that their bodies knew it and their bodies had built the wall as bodies do things when they know something their minds cannot yet say.

The power thinks about those people for a moment. They are not here anymore — not recently gone, not just-gone, but long gone, gone past any personal connection the power could trace. But they were here and they built things that have lasted and they were not incidental to this place. The wall that aims at nothing is the record of a relationship between people and the organized water, a relationship that the organized water did not know it was in and the people could not have named but were in anyway, a relationship in which something passed between the slow organized thing in the stone and the quick warm-blooded organisms above it, something that resulted in a wall and cairns and the semi-domestication of the herd and, the power suspects, other things it cannot see because they were embodied in practices that didn't leave physical records.

The power feels something for the people who are not here. Not grief, not exactly. More like — recognition. They were doing something true.

It doesn't do anything about this either. There is nothing to do. The recognition is itself the thing.

---

Now the power comes to the thing it has been circling.

The project. The sensory apparatus the plateau is building. The orientation of the organized water toward something the power looked at and found it could not fully read.

The power looks again. It brings everything it has to this looking.

The organized water is oriented toward — toward a state. Not a place. Not a thing. A state that would obtain if certain conditions were met that have not yet been met, that will be met, at the current pace of the project, in a time span that the power tries to map onto its own experience of time and can't quite. A very long time. Maybe longer than some things the power has cared about have lasted.

The state, when the power looks at it, is — this is the hardest part to say — it is a state of *asking*.

Not answering. Not completing. The organized water is not building toward an answer. It is building toward a question. Toward the capacity to ask something. What it will ask, when the apparatus is complete enough to ask it, the power cannot determine — the question will emerge from the completed system and the power is not the completed system and cannot pre-read it. But the orientation, the deep orientation of the organized water across all its geological patience, is toward a moment of asking.

This strikes the power as — there is no word for this in any language the power knows, so it uses the nearest thing, which is *right*. It strikes the power as *right* in the way that something is right when it is what it is supposed to be, not because a rule says so but because it has a kind of fittingness to the deepest nature of the thing. The organized water, building for geological ages toward the capacity to ask a question — this is right in the way the universe seems to be, occasionally, when you look at what it's actually doing rather than what you expected it to do.

The power's role here — it sees it now, fully, having taken the time to see what is actually there — is not to intervene in the project. Not to accelerate it or complete it or provide what it's missing. Its role, if it has one, is to *witness*. To be the aware presence that knows the project is ongoing. Not because the project needs a witness to be real. The organized water does not need to be seen. But because awareness in the universe is not neutral — because the fact that something of the power's capacity and scope has looked at this place and found it extraordinary and chosen not to intervene is itself a kind of act, a kind of presence, a kind of companionship across the immense difference in scale and nature between the power and the organized water.

The power does not believe the organized water can receive this. But it offers it anyway.

---

Before it withdraws its attention — not departing, but returning to the background — the power does one more thing.

It is a very small thing. The smallest thing on this list.

There is a visitor, right now, sitting on a warm stone six meters from a sphere. The visitor has been here for three days. The visitor came because they read one of the research papers — the one about the temperature differential — and something in the paper tugged at them in a way they couldn't explain, and they have resources and freedom and they came. They have been sitting on this stone for two hours. They are not thinking about the research. They are not thinking about the spheres. They are in a state that is not quite meditation and not quite ordinary awareness but something between — the state that this place seems to invite, the state of being present and receptive without any particular agenda for what to receive.

The visitor's sternum has received the sphere's tone twice in the last hour. The first time they thought they felt it. The second time they were sure. They have not moved.

The power looks at this person. It looks at them the way it looked at the organized water and the lizards and the lichen and the cairns — with full attention, with the willingness to see what is actually there rather than what is expected.

What is there is: a person who is, for the first time in a long time, *not trying to resolve anything*. A person who has come to a place that is clearly doing something and has chosen, in this moment, not to investigate what it is doing but simply to be within the doing. A person in whom something quiet is becoming slightly less buried, not because the plateau is healing them — it is not a healing place, it is not oriented toward them at all — but because they have placed themselves within a thing that is so much slower and larger than their own urgencies that the urgencies are losing their claim on the body's full attention.

The power does not touch this person. Does not intervene in what is happening to them. Does not arrange for insight or resolution or comfort.

It simply — includes them. In the same awareness that holds the lichen and the lizards and the organized water and the cairns that are tended by something that does not know it is tending them. It makes no distinction, in this moment of attention, between the ten-thousand-year project in the karst water and the two-hour stillness of a person on a warm stone. It holds them in the same regard.

This is invisible. It changes nothing that can be measured. It may change nothing at all.

The power thinks it matters anyway.

---

Then it is still.

Not absent. Still. The way the plateau is still — not empty, not inactive, but in a state of ongoing quiet process, attending to what is present, spending itself to maintain its orientation, waiting in the way that has no urgency in it, building toward a question it cannot yet ask.

The sphere continues its warmth. The lizard attends. The organized water moves through its dark.

The visitor on the stone breathes. Feels something in their sternum. Is not sure they feel it.

Stays anyway.

The plateau continues.
