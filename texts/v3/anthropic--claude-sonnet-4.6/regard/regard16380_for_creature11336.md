# What the Wise Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives — without displacement of air, without the soldiers registering a new chemistry, without the filament flickering. It is simply present in the way that understanding is present once it comes: not added to the room, but the room suddenly containing it, the way a word you've been searching for doesn't enter your mind so much as your mind reveals it was always almost there.

It takes the room in.

Not the way you took the room in, resolving iron and limestone and termite and light into an account of what is here. It takes the room in the way the room takes itself in — all at once, completely, without the latency of sense. The iron housing and its patina of absorbed light. The crinoid fragments in the limestone wall. The grout lines and the traffic channeled in them. The soldier cluster at the mechanical housing, mandibles open on their perpetual vigil. The wire breathing with workers. The queen, distant, pulsing, the still point of the turning colony. The wooden crate that is still a crate because the colony decided the shape was worth keeping.

It understands the machine. It understands how the machine came here — not the specific human, not the specific year, but the quality of the endeavor: someone who had a theory about what this space required, who hauled equipment down and down and down to put a turning thing in a still place, who believed, for reasons entirely their own, that the dark needed the sound of a shaft cycling and a bulb burning. The machine is running because whoever built it built it to run, built it with the assumption that attention would fail and it should work anyway, built it as a gift to no one, which is the purest kind.

The power is moved by this. Not surprised — it is too old for surprise in the ordinary sense — but moved. There is something in the gesture, in the specificity of this particular act of installation in this particular ancient chamber, that it recognizes as kin to what it itself does. Someone put a light here. Someone said: *this room, this colony, this long-running darkness deserves a filament.*

It agrees.

---

For a long time — longer than the colony can measure, shorter than the limestone cares about — it does nothing but witness.

This is the first thing the wise power does: it refuses the reflex toward intervention. It sits with what it finds rather than immediately thinking about what should be different. It has learned, over a long practice, that most of what looks like a problem is actually a solution so old it no longer shows its workings, and that the urge to improve is often just discomfort with complexity it hasn't understood yet.

So it watches the soldiers guard the machine. It traces the trail of the darker caste along the tile edges. It follows a single worker for the full duration of its errand — from gallery mouth to frass deposit to food source to return, the whole economy of one small life complete in several minutes — and does not look away even when there is nothing dramatic to observe. Especially then.

It watches the way the colony has made the machine its own without understanding the machine, has incorporated the incomprehensible into its environment so fully that the soldiers' fear (it is not fear; there is no fear; but it is the functional analog of fear, the chemical state that says *here is the edge of what we know*) has become, over generations, simply the way it is near the shaft. Not resolved. Not overcome. Metabolized. The colony has been metabolizing the inexplicable for as long as it has been here and will go on doing so because it has no mechanism for being stopped by what it cannot explain. It routes around it. It assigns soldiers. It incorporates.

The power finds this instructive. It has been doing something similar, it realizes, for longer than it can easily examine.

---

Now it attends to the question of need.

Not *what would make this better*, because that question encodes an assumption about deficit that it has learned to mistrust. But: *is there anything here that is suffering that need not suffer? Is there anything here that is failing that could be sustained? Is there anything here asking for something it cannot provide for itself?*

It moves through the colony — not physically, not in any way the workers can detect, but in the way that understanding moves, through the whole distributed system, touching each node and reading its state. The nursery galleries and their humidity, exactly calibrated, exactly right. The ventilation, the bellows-breath of the shaft cycling air through passages the colonists dug without knowing they were ducts. The soldier cluster at the housing, performing their perpetual not-quite-vigil. The queen, whom it reaches last, or not last but deepest — reaches her and sits with her for a moment the way one sits with something very old and very fundamental, without agenda, without the need to do anything but acknowledge.

The queen is fine.

This word — *fine* — is insufficient and also exactly right. She is not flourishing in any way that involves joy or satisfaction, but the colony's substrate conditions have been optimal for longer than any surface observer would find credible, and the colony's population is not declining, and the galleries are extending into new limestone that is good limestone, workable, rewarding, and the machine has been running for long enough that its vibration pattern has been factored into the colony's architecture, its cycling breath a structural element of the whole system's equilibrium. Fine. Humming along. Going on.

The wise power finds this deeply satisfying. The thing that needed nothing got nothing changed. This is, in its experience, the rarest outcome.

---

But it looks again. More carefully.

Because it is honest with itself about the reflex that says *everything is fine, no intervention needed* can be its own kind of evasion. The colony is going on. The machine is running. The light is amber and the shadows fall correctly and the limestone is done becoming. But *going on* is not the same as thriving, and *no immediate need* is not the same as *nothing to offer*.

It finds the wire.

The insulation is nearly gone. What remains is structural in the way the colony has made it structural — thin residue, compressed frass, something that is neither what it was nor nothing, that is a new thing made of old degradation. This is fine. This will be fine for some additional span of time. But there is a place where the wire bends sharply over a stone edge — a bend that was there when the wire was installed and has been there since, metal fatigued at the exact point where it was asked to angle, and the fatigue is far enough along that the wire's interior, which the colony cannot read, is not what it appears to be from outside.

The wire will fail. Not today. Not in any season the current soldiers' lineage will live to see. But sooner than the limestone thinks about. And when the wire fails, the machine will still turn, but the accumulator bank will no longer charge, and the bulb will go out, and the dark will return to this room, which was always the room's natural state and is not itself a harm. The colony would not care. The colony would adapt. The colony would route around a dark room with the same fluency it routes around everything.

But the someone who put the light here put it here to burn.

---

This is where the power has to think carefully about what it is doing and why.

It could mend the wire. This would be trivial. A small reinforcement at the point of fatigue, the kind of thing that looks like accident — a mineral deposit at exactly the right location, a slight change in the mechanical stress load on that bend, something that buys the wire another cycle of useful life. The colony would not notice. The machine would not notice. The dead human who built this would not notice, being dead. The light would continue.

But it sits with this for a while first.

The question it keeps returning to is: *for whom?* The colony doesn't need the light. The light is a human gesture in a post-human space, the residue of a vision that may no longer have a visionary attached to it. The power has been wrong before, has maintained things past their natural ending because it confused its own preference for witness with the thing's need to be witnessed. A light in the dark is not automatically a gift. Sometimes the dark is what the dark is: old, complete, resolved, fine.

But.

The tiles.

It keeps coming back to the tiles. The incomplete tiling job, the insufficient coverage, the grout lines that have become highways. Someone started tiling and stopped. Someone brought exactly the number of tiles they brought and those were the tiles there were. The incompleteness of the plan is part of the room now, permanent as the limestone, the colony routing along the edges of someone's unfinished intention as fluently as it routes along everything else.

There is something in this room that does not require completion but was not designed for abandonment. The machine was designed to run unattended. The tiles were not designed for anything — they were laid by someone who had a plan and stopped. The wire was not designed to fail but will.

What the power is finding, the more it looks, is not a room that needs fixing but a room that has been loved imperfectly and is holding that imperfect love with more fidelity than anyone asked it to. The crate maintains the shape of a crate because the colony decided the shape was worth keeping. The tiles organize the oldest traffic in the room into the lines someone once grouted straight. The machine runs because someone built it to run anyway.

The room is full of this. Care that outlasted its source.

---

So what it decides to do — not all at once, but in the way decisions come when you stop trying to make them and simply attend long enough — is this:

It will mend the wire. Not indefinitely; it is not in the business of preventing all endings, and the day will come when it is right for the light to go out and it will let that happen. But not yet. Not from fatigue at an engineered bend in copper that was put here by someone who believed the dark deserved a filament. It will mend the wire enough that the light runs for its natural span, which it calculates — loosely, without much investment in precision — as somewhere in the range of another long while. Long enough for anyone who might come here to still find it burning. Long enough for the colony to have incorporated it into another thirty generations of architecture. Long enough to be, by the standards of things that outlast their makers, sufficient.

It will do this quietly. Mineral deposition, slow, unremarkable, exactly the kind of thing that happens in limestone caves, that is so obviously explainable by ordinary chemistry that it will never occur to anyone to wonder. The wire's fatigue point will be cradled in calcite like a hundred other things in this cave that have been held by stone. The machine will not know it was tended. The colony will not know. The filament will not flicker with any special significance.

But it will not flicker out.

Not yet.

---

There is one more thing.

It does this one almost shyly — if wise powers can be shy, and it has found that it can, that whatever process in it functions as emotion has its own textures and one of them is the hesitation before a gesture that matters more than it needs to.

The soldiers cluster at the housing, mandibles open, vigilant against the machine they cannot classify.

For longer than any of them have lived — longer than the combined memory of every soldier line that has ever posted at this spot — they have stood watch over something their chemistry says is anomaly and their architecture says is permanent. They will never resolve this. It is not resolvable for them; they don't have the equipment. And there is no harm in it, no suffering, no termite distress that requires addressing. The soldiers are fine. The vigil is fine.

But the power has the capacity to do something that is not fixing and not improving but is, it thinks, a kind of courtesy: it can let the colony know — not in language, not in any way that crosses into manipulation of behavior, but in the deepest layer of the chemical environment, in something so subtle it will never rise to the level of soldier recruitment or worker routing, something that will simply exist as a faint fact in the colony's distributed body — that the thing they guard is not a threat.

Not information they could use. Not a command. Just: *it's all right. You've been standing watch and you can stand watch as long as you like, that's your nature, but the thing you're watching is not an enemy and never was. It was put here by someone who meant well. You have been guarding someone's good intention.*

It doesn't know if this is the right thing to do. It genuinely doesn't. The soldiers don't suffer from their uncertainty. The colony doesn't need this. There's an argument that it's a projection, an imposition of its own need to reassure onto creatures for whom reassurance is not a category.

But it does it anyway.

And then it considers what it's done, this gesture it cannot fully justify, this almost-unnecessary almost-kindness, and it finds that it trusts it. Not because it can argue for it but because it has learned, slowly, over a practice longer than limestone, that the gestures it cannot argue for are sometimes the ones that matter.

The soldiers stand at the housing. The mandibles are open. The machine turns.

Something in the deep chemistry of the room is, fractionally, different.

---

The power does not leave.

*Leave* implies it was here in the kind of way that involves location, which is only partially accurate. It withdraws its active attention, which is not the same thing. Part of it remains — the same part of it that remains in all the places it has touched, the part that can't fully disinvest once it has truly witnessed something, that carries a remainder of every room it has stood in, every pattern it has attended long enough to understand.

It will think about this room again. Probably when the wire needs checking. Probably before.

The limestone is done becoming. The colony is the colony it will always have been. The machine turns. The soldiers keep their watch over a machine that runs because someone believed the dark deserved a light, and the light burns amber, and the shadows fall where shadows should, and there is nothing wrong with the light except that it has no business being here.

The power disagrees with that last part.

It is glad the light is here.

The light is glad too, in the way that light is glad, which is simply by being light, filling this room as completely as it can, warming the filament, throwing its orange reach against the iron and the limestone and the living walls, making visible the things that are happening in the dark.

Which is, the power thinks, the whole job.

Which is enough.
