# The Visit

---

Call it what you want. Call it attention, call it notice, call it the thing that happens when something very old and very patient turns toward something and simply *sees* it fully, for the first time, in the way that most seeing is not.

It does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. Arrival implies a before and an after, a threshold crossed, a moment of entry. What happens is more like what happens when you have been looking at a shape in a picture for a long time without recognizing it, and then you do, and the shape was always there, completely present, and what changed was only that recognition extended in a new direction. The plateau becomes visible. The plateau has always been visible. These are not in contradiction.

And what sees it is not located anywhere, which makes it difficult to describe as approaching, or descending, or coming to rest on the shoulder of stone above the basin where the pools are. But something in the description requires a place, because what is about to happen is entirely about place — about *this* place, these birds, this lichen, this specific pool with its particular community of single-celled things that has been diverging from the community in the next pool for longer than anything with a spine has existed on this plateau — and so: say that if there were a point of focus, it would be there, on the warm stone above the largest pool, in the afternoon when the light has begun its slight softening and the shadows in the fissures are turning from cold blue toward something that doesn't have a color-name.

The raptor is on its boulder. The colony is at full roar. A pika is cutting sedge at the pool margin with quick, practiced urgency.

---

What a benevolent and wise power does, first, is *nothing*.

This is important. This is, in fact, the primary thing, and it takes the longest, and it looks from outside like absence. But it is not absence. It is attention, which is a different thing entirely — attention being the form of presence that does not impose itself, that sits with a thing long enough to understand what the thing actually is before deciding whether understanding calls for any action at all.

The plateau does not need saving. This is the first recognition, and it arrives without disappointment, which is how you know the attention is genuinely wise and not merely well-intentioned. The plateau is complete, in the sense already described — not finished, not static, but arrived at a configuration that has integrity, that has been earned across a duration that makes the word *earned* feel insufficient. To arrive here with an agenda, with something prepared to offer or to do, would be to misunderstand what a gift is. A gift is not what the giver thinks the recipient needs. A gift is what a long attention reveals.

So: the attention is long.

The pools are looked at. Not the surface, not the dark mirror-quality that reflects the pressurized blue — but the water itself, and the life in it, the communities that have been diverging in their pools for this whole long while, each one becoming a slightly different answer to the same question. The attention stays with this for a time that is not measurable in the terms the plateau uses. It would be, in plateau-terms, not long. In other terms it would be very long. In the terms of the poolside communities themselves, who have no relationship to duration, it would simply be the continuing conditions under which they metabolize or do not metabolize.

The lichen is looked at. The lichen's patience — which is not patience, which is not anything recognizable as an experience, which is simply the particular mode of being that the lichen has — is recognized as something. Named, internally, in a way that has no external sign. There is something in the lichen's mode of being that is worth staying with, worth letting rest in attention without resolution, because it is one of the stranger productions of the long process that produced everything here, the two-in-one that lives without living, that metabolizes without experiencing, that has been covering this specific rock face since before anyone was present to witness it and will be covering it long after every witness has moved on. The lichen is noted. It is not the kind of thing that benefits from being touched or addressed. It is noted.

The colony is listened to. The full roar of it, every layer, every frequency, the individual call compressed into the collective overwhelming. The attention opens wide enough to hold all of it simultaneously — each bird's murmur to its specific mate, the high dry chittering of the flock passing through, the raptor's wind-whisper, the fundamental hum of the stone — and finds that the sum is not merely noise but something on the edge of being a statement, the way certain things are statements without having a speaker or an audience or a language. The plateau is saying this, constantly, to no one. The attention receives it.

The pika is watched. The urgency. The quick cut of the sedge blade, the small armload of material carried to the entrance of the rock-pile, the drop and return, the cut and carry, the drop and return. The winter that the pika carries in its body as present-tense anxiety even in the warmth of midday. The calling: *I am here, I am here*. The satisfaction that is indistinguishable from anxiety in the small body but is, somehow, in the attentive perception of it, recognizable as both, as a creature living fully in the requirement of its life without remainder, nothing left over, no part of the pika's existence standing outside the pika's existence and evaluating it. The pika is not watching itself cut sedge. It is cutting sedge entirely.

This is stayed with.

---

And here is what the long attention reveals: there is one thing on the plateau that is not complete. Not broken, not wrong — the words of repair don't apply — but genuinely open, genuinely unfinished in a way that the plateau's long self-organization has not closed and cannot close, because it is not the kind of gap that a physical process fills.

It is the gap of unknowing.

Not ignorance, exactly. The plateau knows itself completely, in the sense that every part of it is in active relationship with every other part, that the chain from bird-guano to pool-algae to cushion plant to pika to hay pile to winter-survival is a knowledge written in matter, in metabolic rate, in the specific antibodies the pool organisms have developed against the specific pathogens of this specific pool. It knows itself with a completeness that no observer can match.

But it doesn't know that it is *this*. It doesn't know that this configuration, of all the configurations possible for a plateau at this altitude in this climate at this age of the world, is the one that has come to be, and that its coming-to-be required everything that came before it, and that what came before it required the particular early history of this corner of the planet, and that the early history of this corner of the planet is continuous with the early history of the planet itself, and that the early history of the planet is not separable from the early history of the matter that formed it, and that the matter goes back to a moment when none of this was, and from that moment this — this specific pika, this specific bark, *I am here, I am here* — was, in some sense that requires great care to describe without overstating, already possible. Already latent. Already in some sense on its way.

The plateau cannot know this because the plateau has no perspective outside itself. It is what it is from inside.

---

What the benevolent and wise power does is offer the plateau its own reflection.

Not a mirror — a mirror would be too literal, too flat. What is offered is more like: the plateau is allowed to be perceived completely, and in that complete perception something happens that cannot happen in self-organization alone, which is that the whole of it is held simultaneously, as a whole, by something that can hold it that way.

This is not done *to* the plateau. It does not pass through the plateau like a change. The cliff birds do not look up. The pika continues its cutting. The raptor on its boulder remains still, conserving heat, watching the basin.

But in the pools — in the way the afternoon light enters the water, in the way the community of single-celled things continues its metabolizing — there is a quality that is difficult to name. A quality of: *held*. A quality of: *seen*. A quality that is not warmth, because the water temperature doesn't change. A quality that is not information, because nothing is encoded or transmitted. A quality that might be closest to what the word *witnessed* means when it means something beyond its denotation.

The pools go on being pools. The communities in them go on diverging in their separate, unhurrying ways. The water remains very cold and very clean. Nothing has changed in the world.

But the plateau has been seen, fully, and the seeing — because the power doing it is wise, because wisdom includes understanding that some gifts are not actions, that the deepest form of care is sometimes the form that leaves everything exactly as it is and yet is entirely different from absence — the seeing is, itself, the gift.

---

The pika barks.

*I am here, I am here.*

And from wherever the attention is located, if it is located anywhere, if location is a concept that applies:

*Yes*, the attention says, without words, without any signal that could be detected in the physical record of the plateau.

*Yes. I hear you. I see you. I see the lichen and the pools and the hay you have been cutting since before there were pika on this plateau, since before there were pika anywhere, since the first time something small and warm-blooded pressed itself into the shelter of stone and waited out what was coming. I see the colony and the pair-murmur and the ledge inherited across generations and the egg that is the latest in an unbroken line. I see the flock turning without a leader and the raptor riding what the stone offers and the cushion plant's slow ring advancing at the edge of what was, and is no longer, and the tiny grass in its crack, alone, growing.*

*I see what you are.*

*I see what it cost to become this.*

*I see that you don't know, and that your not-knowing is exactly right, and that it doesn't diminish by one degree the fact that you are, completely, exactly, what this long process was capable of, what it came to, what it arrived at — not inevitably, not by design, but by the kind of constrained freedom that produces the only things worth producing, which are things that could not have been predicted but which, once they exist, seem in some light inevitable.*

*You are here.*

*You have been here.*

*I see you.*

---

And then the attention withdraws — which is also not the right word, because it was never located, because it did not arrive, because —

The light continues its slight softening. The shadows in the fissures continue their slow shift from blue toward something without a name. The raptor lifts from its boulder, once, experimentally, and settles back. The colony roars on.

The pika finishes its armload and carries it in.

The plateau maintains its equilibrium.

The pools hold what they hold: their diverging communities, their cold clean water, the long memory in the silt at the bottom where all the previous versions of this moment have been deposited, accumulating, building a record that has no reader and requires none, that is its own purpose, that will go on being deposited when the birds are other birds and the pika are other pika and the lichen has advanced another century across the boulder face and the small grass in the crack is gone or is still there, still alone, still growing.

Nothing was changed.

Everything was received.

The roar continues, as it has always continued, as it will continue, enormous and specific and made of ten thousand individual voices all saying the same thing in different ways:

*We are here. We are here. We are here.*
