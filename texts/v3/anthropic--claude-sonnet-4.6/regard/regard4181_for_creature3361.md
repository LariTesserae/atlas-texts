# What the Power Does

---

The power becomes aware of the salt dark the way the salt dark becomes aware of anything — gradually, and then completely.

It does not arrive. Arrival implies a before-place and an after-place, a crossing of threshold, and what happens is nothing like that. It is more as if attention were a climate, and the climate of this place shifted almost imperceptibly, the way the warmth from below shifts through the floor: present for a long time before named.

The power is — the word *benevolent* is accurate but insufficient, the way *wet* is accurate but insufficient for the sea. It has been caring for things for long enough that care is no longer something it does so much as something it *is*, the way the creature's stillness is no longer a behavior but a nature. It has made many mistakes in its long practice of care and it has learned from them the way the salt has learned from water: by becoming something new that still contains the record of what happened. It is wise in the specific way that things are wise when they have loved something and watched it die and loved something again. It is not sad about this. It is — salted, perhaps. It carries the mineral trace of many endings in its composition.

It does not have opinions about the salt dark. It has something more precise than opinions: a quality of *regard*, the way a stone in water has a quality of regard for the specific current that has been moving past it for centuries. The stone does not approve or disapprove of the current. The stone and the current have a relationship that is older than evaluation.

---

For a long time — a long time even by the standards of this place, where long time is the only kind of time — the power simply looks.

This is not inaction. This is the first and most important action. It looks at the salt, the formations, the low dim light, the quality of the warmth ascending from below. It looks at the plants: their commitment to their form, the mineral patience of their existence, the fine dust of adaptation on their leaves. It looks at the small organisms in the salt film, the invisible ecology of extremity, life finding its arithmetic in a place that many would consider inimical to life. It looks at the depression in the salt near the warmest cluster, smooth-edged, incorporated, the crystals around it slightly larger.

And then it finds the creature, and it looks at the creature for a long time.

The creature is aware of something. Not the power — not specifically, not as a named or nameable presence. But the air has a quality it does not usually have, some additional attention in the atmosphere that is not threatening and is not familiar and causes the creature's pale eyes to move slowly through the chamber and then settle in the direction of no particular thing and remain there, while its breathing continues at its long, patient interval.

The power notices this. It notices the noticing. It holds very still.

---

What the power does not do:

It does not make it brighter.

This is the first impulse of any caring thing encountering this dimness — to bring light, to open something up above, to let illumination arrive in the manner of a gift. The power holds this impulse and looks at it the way it has learned to look at its impulses: with the question of whether this is care or whether this is the *performance* of care, whether the light would be for the creature or for the power's own sense of having done something kind.

The creature's eyes are built for this dimness. Its pupils occupy almost the full iris. The thin film of organisms it eats is calibrated to grow at this light level. The plants have their waxy leaves, their mineral coating, their grey-green color that is the color of things that have made a complete peace with low light. Everything here has been shaped by this dimness over a time so long that the dimness and the life in the dimness have become each other's authors. To add light would not be addition. It would be the destruction of a specific and achieved equilibrium, and the achievement would be lost, and the power has lived long enough to know that achievements of this kind — the small, complete, long-worked ones — are not recovered once disturbed.

It does not make it brighter.

It does not make the space larger. It does not introduce other living things as company for the creature, on the theory that company is always good and solitude is always a problem to be solved. It does not add, because it has learned, at cost, that adding is often the least careful of acts.

It does not reach into the creature's experience and enrich it, in the way a well-meaning hand reaches into a web to free a spider from its own architecture. The creature is not entangled. The creature is not waiting for enrichment. The creature is complete, in its way — not in the way of things that are complete because they have had everything, but in the way of things that are complete because they have fully become what they are, and what they are is sufficient to what they need, and nothing is strained or suffering in the gap between the actual and the possible.

The power sees that there is no gap. This is the seeing it needed to do. This is why it looked for so long before doing anything.

---

Here is what the power does.

It finds, within the salt dark, a small structural weakness in the crystalline ceiling — not dramatic, not dangerous, a place where a thin fault has been developing over centuries, a place where eventually a piece of mineral the size of a large fist will fall, and will fall onto the warmest part of the floor, onto the depression where the creature rests.

The fault is not imminent. It would take another three centuries, approximately, before the fall. The creature would not be harmed — its reflexes, though slow by most standards, are sufficient to move it from harm; the piece is not large; the fall would be an event in the chamber and then over. But the depression would be damaged, the smooth-edged impression that the salt has taken of the creature's long residence, the incorporation that is the only monument this place contains. The crystals that have grown slightly larger with the trace warmth of the creature's body. That record. That small, precise, mineral record.

The power finds the fault and applies, without drama, without announcement, a very small and localized pressure — not from outside the rock, not as imposition, but distributed through the existing crystalline structure in the direction that allows the fault to stabilize rather than propagate. The change is molecular. The change is silent. The change will not be detectable in any register available to any occupant of this chamber. It is the kind of action that has no observer and requires none: a future fall, redirected; a record, preserved; a small thing, attended to.

The ceiling will hold.

---

The power does one other thing, and this one is harder to describe because it is an action taken not on any physical element of the place but on the place's relationship to time — specifically, to the time that might, somewhere far from here, be running out.

The salt dark exists within a world, and the world has pressures on it, and not all of those pressures are geological. The power cannot stop all of what is coming for the world — it has tried this, in other forms, and learned that what is coming for the world often comes from within the world, from the actions of minds that have their own freedom, and freedom is not something the power is willing to abrogate even when freedom damages things it loves. This is one of its oldest and most painful understandings: that care does not mean control, and the desire to prevent all harm is itself a kind of harm when it is applied to beings whose nature is to choose.

But the salt dark is deep and low and enclosed and has around it a significant amount of rock and mineral indifference, and the power does what it can within those parameters, which is: it makes note. It holds this specific place in a quality of attention that is not passive. Should something come — collapse, intrusion, the arrival of some process that would not know or care what it was disturbing — the power will know first. Not to stop it, necessarily. But to know. To be present to whatever happens in this low, dim, complete, mineral place. To be a witness, if that is all that is permitted. To care, in the old, salted, unsentimental way, even for the ending of things that deserve to continue.

The creature breathes its long breath.

The power watches, and in the watching there is something that is not different from what the creature does when it rests near the place where a plant has died: a kind of attention below the threshold of named emotion, a registering of what is, a refusal to look away.

---

Before the power's attention shifts — it will shift, there are other things in other places, there is no end to the number of places where the quiet, unmonumental work of preservation and regard needs to be done — it does something that is not quite action and not quite feeling and exists in the space between them.

It acknowledges the creature.

Not by speaking. Not by introducing itself, or explaining its presence, or offering anything. There is nothing that could be offered here, and explanation would be a kind of condescension, the assumption that the creature's experience requires supplementing with information it has no capacity to use. The power does not condescend to small or old or still things. It has learned, from long practice, that the small and old and still are often the most complete, the most finished, the most themselves, and that what they require is not to be improved but to be *seen*.

So the power sees the creature. Fully. Without flinching from the smallness of its life, the narrowness of its world, the dim low warmth of its total existence. It sees the fine digits resting against the salt, conducting their patient exchange. It sees the pale eyes in their unhurried movement. It sees the depression in the floor, the larger crystals, the accumulated mineral record of a small life resting in the same place for a very long time. It sees the body's faint warmth, the breath, the long intervals, the occasional sound made for no audience and for no reason except that the body makes it.

And it holds all of this — the way you hold a stone that has been in a cold stream and feel its specific weight and specific cold and specific smoothness, the way you know by holding it that this stone has been in this stream for a long time and has been shaped by that specific water and could not be mistaken for any other stone — it holds all of this with a care that makes no demands, expects no response, requires no alteration of what it is caring for.

It sees the creature as *complete*.

This is the gift. If it is a gift. It may be that it costs the power something to see this way, in a world that is full of things that are not complete, that are broken or diminished or suffering. It may be that the discipline of seeing what is actually there, rather than what is missing, is a discipline that must be practiced, that does not come without effort even for a power that is very old and has been trying to do it for a very long time.

The creature's pale eyes move, slowly, and come to rest again.

The power, without ceremony, without announcement, holds the ceiling up.

And watches.

And in the end, simply remains aware — in that deep and permanent part of itself that does not shift even when attention shifts — that here, in the low and salt and warm and dim, something lives that has arrived fully at what it is, and that this is not nothing, and that this, too, is worth the keeping.
