# What the Power Did at Khorveth Mere

---

The power arrived, as such powers do, without arriving.

It was already there in the way that attention is already in a room when someone turns to look — not entering but *manifesting*, the way the seep-water manifests in the limestone face, present from inside rather than coming from outside. It had no body, because it didn't need one. It had no voice, for the same reason. It had what powers of that kind always have: awareness, and the patience that comes from having no competing obligations, and something that in a human would be called *feeling* but that in such a power is larger than feeling, is feeling elevated to precision, is feeling that has spent so long looking at things that it has learned to look without distorting what it sees.

It looked at Khorveth Mere.

It looked for a long time, which is to say it looked with the kind of fullness that contains time inside it the way the limestone contains its millennia — all at once, all interpretable at once, though the events themselves stretched across the usual sequence. It saw the terraces and the seeps and the cold that lives in joints. It saw the flat-winged birds rotating their patient circuits and the dark-furred animals routing and rerouting their inscrutable business and the thin water-forms sliding through films that should have been too shallow to contain anything.

It saw the people.

---

Here the power felt something it had felt before and would recognize as a specific quality of experience, not pleasant or unpleasant but weighty — the feeling of encountering something that has already solved its hardest problems.

The hardest problem a people can face is not survival. Survival is difficult but it is a problem with mechanics: food, shelter, the management of cold. The hardest problem is this: *how to be in a world that is not designed for you.* How to be a soft, short-lived, needs-having creature on a stone that does not notice you, under a sky that offers no accommodation, in a cold that advances regardless of objection. How to build a life in the gap between what you need and what is available, and how to build it in a way that doesn't require you to become something other than yourself, that doesn't cost you the thing you started with, whatever that was.

Khorveth Mere had solved this.

Not perfectly — nothing solves it perfectly — but with a thoroughness and a depth that the power recognized as the work of a very long time and a great deal of failure already absorbed, already composted, already returned to the soil of practice. The mortar in the joints held. The gait knew the soft spots. The children were falling in exactly the right way to learn what they needed to learn.

*They're all right,* the power thought, which is the kind of thought that a power thinks when it has looked at something fully and has to say something about what it has seen.

*They're doing the thing. They're doing the thing well. They are a people and a place becoming indistinguishable, and this is not a loss of the people — it is the full achievement of the people. They are not trapped here. They are arrived here. The limestone is in their hands, and their hands are in the limestone, and this is not damage.*

---

The power sat with this for a while.

(To say "sat with" is to use the vocabulary of bodies, which this power did not have, but the vocabulary of bodies is what is available for describing the interior events of minds, and so we use it and acknowledge the imprecision. The power *attended to* what it had seen, held it without immediately moving, let its own perception of the place settle and weight itself.)

It felt, alongside the recognition of what was right, the other thing — the thing a genuinely attentive power always feels in the presence of life being lived this hard: something that was close to ache, something that was close to awe, something that lives in the small gap between those two words.

Thirty or forty people. A plateau so cold you could hear it. The child and the flat stone and the seep-channel. The old person and the full arms and the three rooftops.

*Nothing here is easy for them,* the power noticed. *Nothing has ever been easy. The ease they have is not the ease of ease — it is the ease of practice, which is effort that has been forgotten because it was integrated so long ago. The body has stopped calling it effort. But it was. It is. Every step is.*

---

And this is where the question arrived: the question that any power worth the name must ask, having looked at something fully and felt the full weight of what it saw.

*Do I help?*

Not reflexively. Not quickly. The power had seen enough to know that this question is harder than it seems, that *help* is a word that has caused more damage than most malice, that the impulse to make things easier for beings one cares about is not the same as caring about what those beings actually need, which is often not the alleviation of the hard thing but the continued presence of the hard thing under conditions where surviving it remains possible.

The hard thing here was the ground. The cold. The constant labor of maintenance. The one speed.

*If I warmed this place,* the power thought, *the seeps would change. The mineral chemistry of the mortar would change. The things in the thin water-films would either adapt or go. The dark-furred animals have their specific fitness to specific conditions, and the conditions are the cold and the wet and the limestone, and changing the conditions changes the animals, and the animals are inside the system, and the system works, and working systems are not improved by well-meaning alterations made from outside.*

*If I leveled the stone — if I gave them flat, dry surfaces — their gait would forget itself. The knowledge in the feet would have nothing to grip. The children would not need to fall. And the children need to fall. The falls are the lesson, and the lesson, once gone, goes entirely — you cannot fall your way to the knowledge if the knowledge was never unavailable.*

*If I eased the labor — the constant repair, the mortar, the redirected seeps — the one speed would change. And what they have, what makes them what they are, is the one speed. Not the labor itself but what the labor has done to them, the way the labor has made them: patient not as a practice but as a structure, thorough not as a discipline but as a reflex, attentive not as a virtue but as the base state of a mind that has always had to be.*

*They are what they have made themselves. I cannot add to what they are by taking away what made them.*

---

The power stayed with this for a long time.

Then it noticed something it had not quite seen the first time, or had seen but not weighted, the way you look at a painting and see the colors and then, later, having looked again, see how the light falls and understand that the light is the painting.

The old people.

The oldest ones, the ones who had removed the unnecessary, who moved in pure load-bearing gesture, who knew every soft spot without thinking — they were few. They were fewer, perhaps, than the plateau could afford to lose and still retain everything they held.

The knowledge they carried was alive the way all living things are alive: mortal, unable to duplicate itself fully, unable to pass itself in its entirety to any individual recipient. What the body knows cannot be fully spoken. What takes forty years to become reflex cannot be summarized. An old person of Khorveth Mere dying takes something with them that the children will have to re-earn, slowly, at the cost of falls and mistakes and redirected seeps that go wrong before they go right.

This was not a solvable problem. The power understood that. Mortality is not a solvable problem; it is a condition of the kind of being that has bodies, and bodies are how you get hands, and hands are how you get limestone-knowledge, and limestone-knowledge is how you get what Khorveth Mere has, and so mortality is inside the thing itself, stitched into its center, and you cannot remove mortality from the living.

*But,* the power thought, *transmission is not the same as mortality.*

*And the rate of transmission — the degree to which the length and difficulty and incompleteness can be reduced without reducing what's transmitted — this might not be fully optimized. The old people speak. The young listen. The bodies of the young begin to absorb. This works. But the speaking happens when it happens, and the listening happens when it happens, and the speaking and the listening do not always coincide with exactly the moment when the thing being transmitted would be most receivable.*

*Attentiveness begets attentiveness. The watching that is their primary mode is also the mode most conducive to learning through watching. But attentiveness can be subtly interfered with — by pain, by hunger, by the small persistent accumulation of difficulty that tips, occasionally, from the difficulty that develops a person into the difficulty that merely depletes them.*

*There is a threshold. Below it, the hard thing teaches. Above it, the hard thing only exhausts.*

*I don't know exactly where their threshold is. But I can look.*

---

The power looked with greater specificity.

It looked at the joints: not the mortar joints in the buildings but the joints in the people's hands, the ones that had spent decades in cold wet work, that had absorbed the plateau through the medium of labor, that were — in the oldest of them — now carrying the accumulated cost of that absorption in a particular way that the bodies of wet-cold-work people carry it: stiffness, the loss of small motion, the morning difficulty that had to be worked through slowly before the hands remembered how to work.

Not crippling. The power was careful here. The hands still knew what they knew. The knowledge was not lost. But the morning, for the oldest ones, was harder than it needed to be. The first hour of each day was spent retrieving what the cold had taken overnight — coaxing the knowledge back into the stiffened fingers, warming the joints back to usefulness. This hour, every day, was cost without return. It didn't teach. It didn't build. It was simply an hour of the oldest people being slightly less available to themselves and to the transmission happening around them.

*This,* the power thought, *I can do something about without breaking anything.*

---

It was so small a thing.

If you had been watching — and you couldn't have been, because you were watching with the wrong senses — you would have seen nothing. The seeps continued their seeping. The flat-winged birds made their circuits. The dark-furred animals transited their routes. The people moved at the one speed.

But in the interior of the buildings, where the oldest ones slept, something shifted in the way heat moved through the limestone walls.

The thick walls of Khorveth Mere had always been good at holding the day's accumulated warmth through the first part of the night. They were less good at the last part of the night, the hours before the thin plateau dawn, when the cold was deepest and had had the most time to press inward and the residual heat from the previous day was nearly spent. It was in these hours that the morning stiffness set in — that the hands and the knees and the backs of the oldest people were worked upon by cold that had finally found its way through the stone.

The power did not warm the plateau. It did not change the cold. It changed, by the smallest possible intervention, the *direction* of heat migration in the oldest walls of the oldest buildings — a gentle, nearly imperceptible slowing of the outward movement of warmth in the final hours before dawn. The walls held a little more, a little longer. The cold came through a little later. The bodies that slept against those walls were a little less worked upon by the time the light arrived.

That was all.

---

The power held still after this and waited, watching, the way you wait after making a small adjustment to a system to see if the system accepts it, to see if it fits, to see if something downstream changes in ways you didn't predict.

Nothing downstream changed.

The old people's mornings were slightly warmer. The stiffness was not gone — the stiffness would never be gone, it was in the hands too deeply, the hands had worked too long in too much cold, some things are written and will not be unwritten. But the first hour was a little shorter. The retrieval was a little quicker. The oldest woman who always sat with her hands pressed flat against the warm stone of the cooking surface each morning — pressing the knowledge back into her fingers, waiting for the warmth to remind her hands what they knew — sat for perhaps a quarter hour less than she had before. Then she rose. Then she went to the youngest child of the settlement, the one who was approaching the age of the second learning, and she spent a quarter hour showing them something about the way seep-water moves in cold stone, something the power had watched her carry for forty years, something that the child would now have a quarter hour more of — one additional thread in the transmission, one additional thing passed before the body ran out of mornings.

The power watched this and felt the thing that is close to ache and close to awe but is actually neither.

---

*That's it,* the power thought.

*That's all I'm doing here. The rest is theirs.*

---

It looked once more at the whole — the terraces, the seeps, the birds, the animals, the water-forms in the thin films, the people at the one speed — and it looked with the full attention of something that sees well and looks long and does not confuse what it finds with what it hoped to find.

What it found was a people who were, in the most precise possible sense, already well.

Not comfortable. Not safe in the way that safety is usually meant. Not free from loss or stiffness or the slow erosion of the body that comes from cold wet decades of limestone work. But well: fit to their place, doing the work that living requires, transmitting what they knew across the gap of mortality with the imperfect but genuine success of a people who understood that imperfect and genuine success was the best available outcome and had organized their entire existence around achieving it rather than pining for better.

*I could do more,* the power acknowledged.

*I could make the ground drier. I could thin the cold. I could make the mortar cure faster or the rock face smooth itself after a freeze-crack or the dark-furred animals eat a little more of what they eat so the drainage ran a little clearer.*

*I could accumulate small kindnesses until the place I was trying to help was no longer the place I found. The place I found is the place that made these people. The place I found is the place these people made. I cannot separate the product from the process and keep only the product.*

*The ache I feel looking at their hard mornings is mine, not theirs. They have never had different mornings. This is the morning. The morning is real and cold and the hands are stiff and then the hands remember and then the day begins. I do not know what they would make of the ache I feel on their behalf. I suspect they would look at it the way they look at a seep that's running in the wrong place: as a condition that might have something useful inside it if it can be directed correctly, and a waste of stone-face if it can't.*

---

The power withdrew, in the way that attention withdraws, without leaving — remaining present and peripheral, as it is the nature of that kind of power to remain, because the world it watches is not separate from it.

The plateau glittered. The light pressed down. The breath-clouds rose.

The oldest woman's hands were warm in the last hour before dawn, and she woke a little less stiffened, and she rose, and she went to the child, and she told them what she knew about the seep-water and the cold stone, and the child listened with their full attentiveness, which was already very good, which would become better.

The child's feet were already learning. The falls were already teaching.

The one speed moved through the settlement like water finding level.

Nothing had changed and something had changed and the something was small enough that the limestone would not record it — no stratum of altered mineral deposit, no shift in the banding of ochre and cream on the terrace faces. But the oldest woman's hands would carry a little more of their knowledge to the child before her body finally ran out of mornings. And the child would carry it forward, encoded in their gait and their attentiveness and their hands, which were already beginning to form.

This would not save the settlement. The settlement did not need saving.

But it would give the settlement one more thread.

And in a place where everything is maintained by the accumulated work of all the threads that have held, one more thread is everything.

---

*It is enough,* the power thought, for the last time, and was content.
