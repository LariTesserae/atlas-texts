# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way the rain arrives: already there.

Not descending. Not approaching. Present in the way things are present when they have been present long enough — the way the charcoal stone is present under everything, the way the grade of each terrace is present in the body of anyone who has walked it ten thousand times. The Power does not enter the Drowned Terraces. It discovers that it has always had a relationship with this place, and it becomes conscious of that relationship, and in becoming conscious of it, it pays attention.

This is the first thing it does: it pays attention.

---

For a long time — in the Power's time, which is not the settlement's time but is capable of moving at the settlement's time when that is what's needed — it simply watches.

It follows the water.

It traces the flow from the highest terrace to the lowest, through every channel, over every edge, and it does this not because it needs to understand the water system (it understood the water system immediately, in the first instant of attention) but because this is how the place is rightly entered. It knows this the way a person of good manners knows to take off their shoes. It walks the water home, slowly, and it sits at the lower edge of the seventh terrace and watches the flow leave.

It notices: the third channel on the fifth terrace is carrying more than it should. A slight convexity has developed in the terrace floor where two large roots — from a birch that has been growing at the upper wall for perhaps seventy years — are beginning to push the stone up from below. Not urgently. Not dangerously. But the channel's grade has shifted by a degree or two, and in another generation the channel will begin to work against itself, backing up slightly, encouraging moss to colonize a section that needs to stay clear.

The Power notes this and does not fix it. This is not its to fix. This belongs to the channel-cutter on the sixth terrace, and to the channel-cutter who will follow, and to the knowledge held in the bodies of those people, which already includes the knowledge of such problems, already has the solution ready in its long memory. The Power notes the channel the way a careful guest notes the hour — aware of it, not speaking of it, trusting the household.

---

It looks at the people for a long time.

It looks at their hands — the stained palms, the map of touching — and it feels something that, in a being with a smaller scope, would be recognizable as grief, but which at its actual scale is something more like tenderness held in the same place as longing held in the same place as recognition. The hands have done real work. The hands have done it for a very long time. The hands show the cost of being in right relationship with a world that does not make right relationship easy, and the Power loves the cost the way you love evidence of long faithfulness.

It looks at the very old ones sitting in the covered spaces, for whom the boundary between inside and outside has grown thin. It recognizes in them something it recognizes in itself, and it does not look away from them, and they — if they feel anything, sitting in their extraordinary stillness — may feel the quality of the air shift slightly, some almost-pressure, some sense of having been seen by something that is not a person. Most of them do not stir. One, the oldest, opens her eyes very slightly further than they were open and then closes them again. That is all.

It looks at the children tracing the channels with their small fingers and it understands what is being transmitted, and it marvels — this is the right word, it actually marvels, which is one of the things it can do — at how the important knowledge found the right vessel. That the knowledge of *which way things naturally go* was given to children through water and stone and grade rather than through proclamation. That the epistemology of this place is: the world teaches, and the body learns, and the mouth comes third. It finds this very beautiful. It has seen other arrangements. This one is very beautiful.

It looks at the people who arrived from outside and stayed, who are in the process of learning to register the grade. It watches, with particular attention, the moment when the step-around becomes automatic — the first time someone steps around a moss without looking down, having stopped deciding to, the knowledge having slipped from intention into habit into body. It watches this moment the way you watch a door open that has been stuck for a long time. There is something it wants to do, in this moment, and what it wants to do is *mark* the moment somehow — lay a hand on the shoulder of the person, say *yes, that, exactly that* — but it does not do this. The moment is already complete. The moment is its own confirmation. Adding to it would diminish it.

It has learned, this Power, that most of what it wants to do is of this type: a completion that would be a diminishment. The restraint this requires is constant. It does not always get it right. But here, in this place, it finds the restraint easier than usual, because this is a place where the practitioners already know that the water finds its own grade, and that the finding is the work, and that the work is enough.

---

There is one person in the settlement — a man of middle age, unremarkable in appearance, one of the people who was born here but carries the forward-pointed orientation, the urgency — who has been, in recent seasons, unhappy. Not loudly. Not demonstrably. He is not making trouble. He does his work, he eats with the others in the evening, he steps around the mosses without looking down, which he has always done, which he learned as a child. But he carries something that is not the unhappiness of a person in the wrong place — he belongs here, he is known here, the others orient toward him with warmth — but the unhappiness of a person who suspects that there is something they should be understanding that they are not understanding, and the suspicion has been running so long that it has become a kind of background pressure, like a headache behind the eyes that one has stopped noticing is a headache.

He checks things. He climbs to the high reaches of the ridge above the settlement. He keeps the paths clear. He does not know, quite, what he is looking for. He has not known for long enough that he has stopped expecting to recognize it when he finds it.

The Power looks at him for a long time.

It does not show him anything. It does not send him a vision, a message, a comfort. It does not resolve his question.

What it does — carefully, slowly, over the course of several seasons — is remove one small obstacle. Not the question itself; the question is his. Not the unhappiness; that is part of how he is built and it is not a flaw. The obstacle is this: a slight muting that has developed, in him, between the thing he already knows and his ability to know that he knows it. The knowledge is there. He carries it the same way the others carry it — in his step, in his hands, in the automatic step-around — but there is a thin layer between him and the knowledge, a layer of the doubt that accumulates in people who are built for urgency living in a place that runs at the pace of moss. The doubt says: *what you carry in your body doesn't count. Real knowing is somewhere ahead of you, forward-pointing, over the ridge, in whatever you haven't found yet.*

The Power does not argue with this doubt. It loosens, very gently, the layer. The way you loosen a knot not by pulling at it but by releasing the tension that made it tight in the first place.

This takes three seasons. It is almost imperceptible even as it is happening. And what happens at the end of it is not a revelation. He does not have an insight. He does not understand something new. He simply, one morning, in the slight clarification of the air before dawn, walking the path from his building to the upper terrace, is aware — not for the first time but for the first time without qualification — that he knows which way the water goes. That he has always known. That this knowledge is complete, is real, is enough to act from.

He stops on the path and stands still for a moment, in the mist, in the rain.

Then he continues to the upper terrace. There is work to do.

The Power watches this and feels the thing that has no smaller-scope equivalent, but which we can roughly indicate by saying: *yes. That. Exactly that.*

---

It considers whether there is anything else.

It considers the channel on the fifth terrace, the root-pushed convexity, and again it does not touch it. Right decision.

It considers the settlement's isolation and whether it should be different, whether there are others elsewhere who could give and receive from this place. It thinks about this carefully. The knowledge here is site-specific. It cannot be carried in a book. And yet — there is a woman, two ranges of hills to the south and east, who is trying to understand something about how water moves through cut stone, and who has been trying to understand it for years, and who is not going to get there alone, and who would make the walk to the Drowned Terraces if she knew there was something here to walk toward. 

The Power arranges for her to meet, on the road to a larger town, a man who has been to the Drowned Terraces once, years ago, for a single season, and who never quite learned anything articulable, but who carries, without knowing he carries it, a slightly different way of describing cut-stone channels. She will meet him at a roadside fire. They will talk about other things. At some point he will say something about channels, almost as an aside — something so specific and so correct that she will stop and ask him to say it again. He will say it again. She will think about it for three weeks. And then she will make the walk.

The Power does not tell anyone it has done this. It doesn't need to. It is not doing things for credit. It is doing them because this is the right shape of things.

---

What it does not do is larger than what it does.

It does not speed anything up. It does not make the rain stop, or start, or vary. It does not protect the settlement from the slow inevitable processes of dissolution and change — the buildings will continue to become the plants, the plants will continue to become the stone, the stone will continue to become something it cannot yet predict, and this is not a problem, this is the settlement's entire wisdom. To protect it from change would be to protect it from itself.

It does not make the people happier, generally. Most of them are, in the baseline way of people who have meaningful work in a place that fits them, already fine. Fine is sufficient. The Power is not in the business of fine-tuning fine.

It does not leave a mark. There is no indication that it was here. No new thing appears on the mossy walls. The frog chorus does not swell to indicate significance. The corvid on the lower terrace does not look up. The mist does not clear. The mist is not going to clear. That is not what this place does.

---

What it does, in the end, can be said simply:

It sees the place.

It sees it fully, without remainder, the way the place has always deserved to be seen — the whole long patient work of it, the knowledge in the bodies and the knowledge in the stone, the accumulated slow decisions of everyone who ever cut a channel or turned a pot or stepped around a moss or sat at the seventh terrace's edge watching the water leave. It holds all of this in its attention without judgment, without improvement, without the restless need to make it other than it is, and it does this not for a moment but for a long time, with the quality of attention that this place has always had for itself, that the people here have always had for the water.

The place has been doing its work faithfully for longer than it has had any witness but itself.

It has a witness now.

---

Eventually — in the Power's time, which is long, long — it shifts its attention. Other places. Other beings in their own faithfulness, their own particular way of being in right relationship with the given.

But it does not forget the Drowned Terraces. It carries them the way the stained palms carry the work — not as memory exactly, not as record, but as a slight darkening, a change in what it knows that it knows. The knowledge of how a place can grow together with the people who live in it until neither quite knows where it ends and the other begins.

The knowledge that there is a grade to everything, if you are patient enough to feel it.

The knowledge that feeling it is enough.

---

The rain continues. The mist lies in the spaces between terraces. The very old woman in her stillness, who opened her eyes slightly wider for a moment and then closed them again, does not tell anyone what she felt. There is nothing to tell. It is one of those things that has no articulable content, only the felt quality of a completed relationship — a door that was not stuck but was simply not yet open, now open, now part of how the path works.

She will not speak of it.

Two weeks later, alone in the early morning, in the slight clarification before dawn, she will understand something, and she will laugh — quietly, briefly — the laugh that passes like small weather.

The others will not hear it.

The rain will.
