# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way the plateau's rain arrives: not announced, not dramatic, more like a decision than an event. One moment the air above the western edge holds only what it held. The next moment there is a presence in it — not a figure, not a light, not a sound that the stamping mill doesn't account for. A presence. The kind that the plateau-born would recognize, if recognition is the right word for what happens when one vast patient thing becomes aware of another.

The substrate notices first. Of course it does.

There is a change in the chemical gradient, faint as the first suggestion of thaw, moving from the western margin inward through the network's slow language. Not alarm. Alarm would be too fast, too pointed. Something more like the network's equivalent of attention redirected: *there is a thing here that is not the wind and not the frost and not the rail line and not the works.* The information moves at the pace of information in this medium, which is to say: it is still moving, and will be moving for some time, and that is fine.

The power waits. It is good at waiting. It has had to be, to be the kind of thing it is.

---

## What It Observes, First

It does what any wise presence must do before acting: it stays still and it watches and it lets the place teach it what the place is.

It watches the corvid make its morning transit. It watches without interpretation, initially — just the black shape, the unhurried trajectory, the indifference to everything below it that is not the plateau itself. After some time, the power understands the corvid as a kind of punctuation, a daily mark in the long sentence the plateau is writing. It feels something that is not quite affection but is adjacent to it. Something more like recognition: *yes, this is what a free thing looks like in a place that has not made freedom impossible.*

It watches the stamping mill's smoke disperse. It watches the substrate thicken at the eastern edge, and it understands this, immediately, as intelligence — not metaphorical intelligence but the real kind, the kind that assesses and responds, that has a preference and acts on it over the timescale available to it. The power has a deep respect for intelligence that does not require speed to be profound. This respect is one of the reasons it is the kind of power it is.

It watches the three-meter line of colonization on the buildings. It stands in the western district and looks at the horizontal demarcation — so precise, so absolutely consistent across every structure — and it feels something that takes it a moment to name.

Tenderness.

That's what it is. Tenderness toward the substrate's restraint. The plateau could colonize faster. The power can see this clearly, in the way that wise things can see what is possible as distinct from what is chosen. The substrate has, in whatever manner is available to something without intention as humans understand intention, calibrated itself. Gone to the height that serves its purposes without collapsing the walls. Grown thick enough at the eastern edge to absorb the impact without trying to stop it. The city is still here, still functional, still producing what it produces. The substrate is doing what it is doing. The two projects run in parallel, with the substrate's patience holding space for the city's loudness without requiring it to stop.

The power has been in many places. It has seen coexistence fail in elaborate and spectacular ways. What is happening here, on this plateau, in this four-kilometer arrangement of dark basalt and pale moss, is coexistence succeeding — not through negotiation or explicit agreement, but through the deep logic of things that know themselves well enough to know what they need and to take only that. The substrate is not trying to win. It is trying to continue. The city is not trying to destroy. It is trying to extract. The gap between what each requires and what each is taking is the space in which they persist together.

This, the power thinks, did not need its help to happen. And this gives it pleasure of a very specific kind — the pleasure of arriving somewhere and finding that the work has already been done, and done better than you would have done it.

---

## What It Considers Doing, and Does Not

The power is benevolent and wise, which means it has learned — at some cost, across some span of time it does not talk about — the primary lesson of being both: that the first impulse of a benevolent power is almost always to improve something, and that the wise part of benevolent and wise is knowing when improvement is a form of erasure.

It considers the stamping mill. It could quiet it. Not destroy it — the power does not destroy things — but attenuate the impact, soften the pulse, bring the two-second rhythm down to something that does not require the substrate to brace. This seems, for a moment, like kindness.

Then it sees the extraction workers on the early shift watching the corvid. It watches them watching. And it understands something it might have missed if it had acted sooner: the stamping mill is not only damage. The stamping mill is the heartbeat against which the plateau-born have organized their inner lives. Remove it and you remove something that functions, for these people, the way the substrate's chemical network functions for the organisms within it — a carrier signal, a shared pulse, the rhythm you know yourself by. The dreams of slow process. The calibration of the body to something just below hearing. The capacity for silence that is not empty but is the stamping mill's resonance held in the chest.

The mill is part of them. It arrived and was incorporated, the way everything on this plateau is eventually incorporated. Taking it would not be kindness to the people. It would be the power substituting its own idea of a better plateau for the plateau that actually exists, with the people who actually live in it, who have made themselves from what was here and are not damaged by it in the ways a lowland physician might assume.

The power lets the stamping mill stamp.

It considers the substrate's long project. It could accelerate it — the wall-climbing, the foundation-work, the infinitesimal lean of the old buildings toward the city's center. It could give the plateau's work a small gift of time, compress a century into a decade. The substrate would not object. The substrate does not object to things. The substrate absorbs and continues.

But the power watches the woman who maintains the rail exchange office — the one who dreams of being the substrate, of becoming-larger-and-older-and-more-present — and it understands something important. The dream is beautiful precisely because she is not the substrate. Because there is still a woman maintaining an exchange office, living in a room with the plateau's characteristic minimum of furniture, describing her dream plainly to whoever asks, as a fact about her sleep. The value is in the interface. The value is in the place where the human project and the plateau's project are meeting each other, learning each other's pace, becoming something that neither was alone. Accelerate the substrate's incorporation and you lose the thing that the incorporation is making, which is this: people who dream in geological time while also keeping the equipment running, who are practical about moisture sealing and also present to the warmth of the substrate against their palm, who are descended from people who came for the minerals and who are becoming something that the substrate would recognize as kin.

The power does not accelerate the substrate's project.

It sits with what it has found. It lets its own processes slow to the plateau's pace, which takes some effort even for a power of this kind — there are things it is used to doing at speed that feel, here, like exactly the wrong approach. It breathes the moisture the plateau breathes upward. It listens to the mill's two-second pulse until it has forgotten to notice it, until it is just part of the baseline, until the power knows it in the way the plateau-born know it, which is to say: in the body, before the mind.

---

## What It Does

What it does is small. This is intentional.

The power moves through the city in the diffuse grey-white light, through the western district's oldest streets where the buildings lean by increments no single decade can verify. It does not fix the lean. But it touches each foundation — not to correct but to attend, the way you put your hand against something you wish well. The basalt is cold and very old and has been holding these walls for longer than the extraction company's records extend, and the power simply lets the stone know that it has been seen. That someone who understands what it costs to hold something over centuries has noticed the holding and found it sufficient and good.

This is not nothing. Attention from a source that comprehends the scale of a thing is not nothing, even to something that does not experience attention in any way we would recognize.

The lean continues. But the foundations, if you could measure what happens to something that has been seen truly, are perhaps fractionally more themselves.

The power goes to the canteen in the evening. It sits at the old wood table — the wood that has dried into something almost sculptural, the grain raised and dark — and it is present without being seen, in the way that the substrate's warmth is present against the palm without having a face. The people in the canteen talk about ordinary things: the machinery, the child's funny habit, the weather coming in from the northwest. The power listens, and it does what listening does when it is done fully: it lets the things said arrive somewhere rather than disappear. It holds them. Not as record — the power is not a recorder. As witness. The ordinary things said in this room on this evening in the canteen with the west-facing windows are said in the presence of something that will carry the shape of them onward, the way the substrate carries the memory of water after the rain has been absorbed.

This is not an intervention. This is participation at the scale that will not distort.

The power finds the grey-brown thing in the mortar gap. This takes a moment because the thing is always just recently elsewhere. But the power is patient and it waits and the thing is there, finally, at the edge of the gap, pausing in the way that small things pause when something unusual is in the vicinity — not frightened, just recalculating. The power lets the animal take its time. The animal returns to the gap. The power spends a while here, in the company of the thing's total indifference to everything except the wall that provides what it needs, and it finds this clarifying in the same way that certain simple things are clarifying: the animal is right. The wall is sufficient. The specific sufficiency of this wall, for this animal, is real and complete and requires no one's blessing.

The power gives no blessing to the animal. It takes, instead, a kind of instruction.

---

## What It Does for the Substrate

Here, only here, the power does something that is more than witness.

It goes to the open plateau beyond the city's western edge — three kilometers of pale green-grey living material, the low corrugated rolls of ancient topography, the dark pools holding the sky. It moves through the substrate the way the substrate's own information moves: slowly, by presence and gradient, without assertion. And it does what it came here to do for this one purpose, which is not intervention but introduction.

The substrate's network is vast and ancient and intelligent in the ways it is intelligent. But it is local. It knows this plateau, this rock, these centuries of frost and thaw and rain. It does not know what the power knows, which is: that what is happening here, on this plateau — this patient coexistence, this calibrated restraint, this ongoing negotiation between the living mat and the loud dark city — is not common. That the substrate has arrived at something without knowing it has arrived at something, and that somewhere in the chemical gradients and pressure differentials and root-adjacent structures that no taxonomist has named, it might, in the way available to it, be able to receive this.

So the power tells it. Not in language. In the only currency available in this medium: a signal. A pattern carried in the gradient, slow as the substrate's own communication, arriving as the substrate's information arrives — without drama, without announcement, like something already known that is simply being confirmed.

*What you are doing is rare. What you are doing is working. You are correct to continue.*

It takes years for this to move through the network. The power knows this and does not mind. The power has given the message and the message is traveling, and the substrate will, in whatever manner is available to it, receive confirmation that it has been witnessed by something capable of perspective — which is what it could not give itself, being the thing it is, which cannot step outside itself to see what it looks like from altitude.

Whether this changes anything in the substrate's project, the power does not know and does not try to determine. That is not what the message was for. The message was not instrumental. The message was care, transmitted in the only language available.

---

## What It Leaves

In the morning the corvid makes its transit, east to northwest, unhurried. The stamping mill marks its two-second pulse. The early shift watches the bird and then descends. The exchange office opens. Someone notes the weather coming from the northwest by reading the change in the substrate's color, which is how weather has always been read here.

Nothing is different. Nothing is fixed. Nothing has been accelerated or corrected or blessed in the way that blessings are usually imagined — descending, transforming, leaving something improved in a visible and verifiable manner.

But the foundations have been seen.

The ordinary words said in the canteen last night were held somewhere.

And in the substrate, moving at the substrate's pace, in the chemical language that is not language:

*You are rare. You are working. Continue.*

This will arrive. It will arrive in the way a thing arriving in this medium arrives — already changed by the journey, softer at the edges, distributed through the network rather than concentrated at a point, indistinguishable to any measurement from the substrate's own states. By the time it arrives, no one alive in the canteen will be alive, and the corvid who crosses now will be an ancestor of the corvid who crosses then, and the grey-brown thing in the mortar gap will be a descendant of the grey-brown thing in the mortar gap.

The buildings will have leaned a little further, measurably, toward the center.

The three-meter line will have held, or it will have moved to three meters and two centimeters, and someone will be trying to decide whether this represents change.

The plateau-born will be dreaming of slow process, of becoming-larger, of finding this agreeable. Some of them, perhaps, will dream it with a quality they cannot account for — a sense of confirmation in the dream, a sense that the vast thing they are dreaming of being knows itself to be on the right path, has had this verified from somewhere outside its own perspective, and has incorporated this knowledge the way it incorporates everything:

Slowly. Permanently. Without drama.

Into the ongoing project of continuation.

---

*The stamping mill stamps. The moss is exactly where it is.*

*It is enough.*

*The power has said so, and moved on, and the saying remains, traveling through the living dark at the pace of understanding, which is not fast, and which is fine.*
