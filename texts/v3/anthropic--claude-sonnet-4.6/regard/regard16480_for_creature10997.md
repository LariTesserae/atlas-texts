# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It arrives the way attention arrives: without announcement, without displacement of air.

It is not small — it is not the size of anything — but it enters at the scale of the courtyard, which is to say it does not arrive from outside so much as it becomes *present*, the way understanding becomes present when something that was almost-known finally resolves. One moment the courtyard is as it is. The next moment it is as it is, and known.

The first thing the power does is *look*.

This takes longer than you might expect. Not because the courtyard is large — it is not large — but because looking, done properly, is not inspection. Inspection would take thirty seconds. Looking takes as long as it takes to receive the fig's relationship with the wall, the gecko's relationship with the fig, the tardigrades' long capacity for waiting, the human attention that continues to hold the place within its concern by the weekly act of raking. Looking takes long enough to understand that what appears to be a paused project is in fact an ongoing one, that the incompleteness is not failure but a kind of structural feature, that the courtyard is mid-sentence in a way that is not distress but a very particular kind of aliveness. The sentence needs its ending, yes, but not urgently. The courtyard is fine.

This matters: the first act is not intervention. It is witness.

---

Having looked, the power considers.

It could do many things. It has access to the categories of miracle — could accelerate the fig's root decision, could complete the flagstones overnight in a geometry that perfectly accommodates every plant and every drainage line and every future need, could restore the raked lines in chalk that would not fade, could locate and return the string that has been lost, could even — if it were a different kind of power, one less careful — resolve the deferral by somehow returning the person who paused here, the one who rakes, ready to continue.

But it knows about courtyard ecology — about courtyard *anything* — that the system is not waiting for rescue. It knows the distinction the description itself makes: between abandoned and paused. It knows that the raked earth is not a call for help. It knows that the fig is not suffering. It knows the gecko is not in distress. It knows that the tardigrades, with their remarkable indifference to catastrophe, are not experiencing the incomplete paving as a problem requiring supernatural solution.

There is real poverty in the world. There is real suffering. This courtyard contains neither.

What it contains is fragility of a particular kind — not the fragility of the broken or imperiled, but the fragility of the ongoing. The relationship is fine. The relationship could, without catastrophe, continue indefinitely. But it is also true that relationships require tending, and tending requires a tender, and the tender is a human being with a human life that has its own incompleteness, its own deferred projects, its own intervals of absence.

So the power's question is not *how do I save this?* but *what does this need, if anything, from me?*

---

Here is what it notices:

The mulberry seedling. Two hand-spans tall, lobed leaf already distinct. It arrived from nothing, from a seed in a passing bird's transit through the square of sky above. It is, right now, deciding whether to continue. The soil in the spot where it has rooted is slightly drier than what the seedling needs — not fatally, but enough that the next two weeks will determine something. If it rains, or if the person who rakes also carries water, the seedling will develop its second tier of roots and be established. If not, it will not.

The power could do something about this in any number of ways.

What it does is: *tilt*.

Not metaphorically. The condensation that accumulates nightly on the northern wall follows, like all water, the path of least resistance. That path is currently taking it to the strip along the north wall where the moss is already thick, where water is not scarce. The power finds — barely there, the suggestion of a gradient in the surface of the raked earth — a channel. It is not there yet. It is the possibility of a channel, the kind of small depression that would form naturally if the earth were raked in a slightly different pattern, something that would take the person who rakes perhaps three passes of the rake-head to create without knowing why they were doing it, following some instinct about the shape of the ground.

The power does not create the channel. It makes the channel *more possible*. It does something at the level of how the ground sits — nothing that would be measurable, nothing that could be detected — that means the next time someone rakes the earth, the natural motion of the rake will tend to produce this slight depression, this minimal routing of condensation toward the mulberry seedling, without that person intending it. They will not know they have done anything. The ground will have simply received the raking in a way that is a millimeter different from how it would have received it before.

The mulberry seedling may still fail. The power does not guarantee the mulberry seedling. But it has increased the possibility of the mulberry seedling without overriding the agency of the earth or the person or the seedling itself.

This is, for the power, a characteristic kind of action: *the opened possibility*, not the enforced outcome.

---

Then it turns its attention to the gecko.

The gecko does not need anything. The power confirms this with the unhurried care it brought to the fig and the seedling. The gecko is old and accomplished and is doing what geckos have always done and will do the work it has always done for this courtyard — the indexing, the calibrated embodiment of the place's particular history — for as long as it lives, which will be as long as it lives.

But the power looks at the gecko for a long moment, the way you look at something excellent. The gold eye with its vertical pupil. The absolute willingness of the gecko's stillness. The scar on the left flank, acquired in a territorial dispute three seasons ago, now healed and pale.

What the power does for the gecko is: *notice it*.

This seems like nothing. The power knows it is not nothing. To be genuinely noticed — not observed, not monitored, not assessed, but received in your specific particularity, this gecko, this scar, this eye, this long patient watching of this one warm sheltered place — is a thing that the gecko will never know happened and that changes nothing about the gecko's circumstances and that is not, in the usual accounting, an *act* at all.

But the power is old enough and has moved through enough of the world's sheltered courtyards, enough of the world's small carefully-held places, to know that noticing is not nothing. That the archive the gecko has accumulated — the years of this courtyard, carried in its body's calibrated responses — deserves to be *received* by something, even once, even in a way that leaves no trace.

Call it: *the act of witnessing that has no effect except to complete the witness*. The gecko is seen. That is all. The power moves on.

---

The worms it does not disturb. You do not interfere with the worms. The worms are older than the categories interference is conducted in. The worms are doing the primary work of everything and need no assistance and would not recognize assistance if it were offered. The power moves through the earth lightly, the way a thought moves, taking nothing, registering the worm-activity with something that in a being of this kind functions as gratitude, and continues.

---

It pauses longest at the tardigrades.

This is the place where the power is most itself, most at home — among the small extraordinary things that most minds do not think to look at, in the gap at moss-scale between the flagstone and the wall. It looks at the tardigrades the way it looked at the gecko: with complete attention, without agenda.

The tardigrades are in their suspended state. The water film dried this afternoon. They have retracted, reduced themselves, become the barrel-shaped dormant forms that will outlast whatever comes. They are waiting with the comprehensive patience of things that have no experience of waiting.

The power does not revive them. It does not need to. What it does, sitting with them in their suspension, is something very close to *keeping company*. The tardigrades are alone in their waiting in the way that all waiting things are alone. The power cannot fix this — cannot change the nature of waiting — but it can be present to it, can recognize that the capacity to wait is one of the most important things in any ecosystem, any relationship, any project mid-sentence, and that this capacity is held here, in this gap, in these extraordinary nearly-invisible animals, and that it deserves acknowledgment.

The power acknowledges it.

There is no sign of this. Nothing in the courtyard changes. The tardigrades remain in their suspension. But something in the quality of the not-quite-darkness where they rest is, for a moment, slightly different — the way a room changes when someone enters who means well toward you, even if you are asleep, even if you do not know they came.

---

And then the fig.

The power and the fig are old acquaintances, in the way that powers and figs are always old acquaintances. The fig is not one fig, as the description understood — the fig is the idea of the fig made locally specific, root and crack, root and crack, patient in its decision about the wall. The power has seen this decision being made in many walls. It will be made again here. It is not the power's place to hasten it or to prevent it, and it does not try.

What it does, at the root of the fig, at the place where the fig's root meets the wall's crack, is something that could be called *attention* and could be called *blessing* and might most accurately be called *the confirmation of what is*. The root is doing what roots do. The wall is being worked on. This is real. This is happening. The power says — in a register that roots can receive, which is not language but something more like the affirmation of pressure, the confirmation of direction — *yes. continue. this is what you are*.

The fig does not accelerate. The fig does not change its approach to the wall. But the root, in its next few millimeters of growth, proceeds with something that was already present and is now — fractionally — more present: *certainty*. Not acquired from outside but recognized from within, the way you sometimes become more yourself by being truly seen.

---

Finally, and most carefully, it turns to the human attention.

The person who rakes. The person who chalked the geometries and set the string and placed the peg and began laying stones and paused. The power does not have access to this person right now — they are not here, they are somewhere in the city, in the press of whatever their life contains. The power does not reach out to touch their life or redirect their days.

But they will come back. The raked earth is evidence: they come back. When they come back, they come back to this, and what they come back to either sustains the return or fails to.

What the power does is small and probably unnecessary — the courtyard would likely sustain the returns without it — but it does it anyway, as a kind of dedication: it *holds the place*.

Not preserves. Not freezes. Not prevents the moss from spreading or the ferns from extending one more frond or the gecko from aging or the fig from working on the wall. All of this continues. But the power wraps the courtyard — not in protection against external harm, which is not the current threat, but in a kind of continuity, a coherence. It holds the relationship — the relationship that the description says lives here, that is the courtyard's primary resident — it holds the relationship as *ongoing*. It marks it, in whatever accounting such a power makes, as something that is not finished, that is mid-sentence, that has more to become.

This is perhaps the most significant thing it does, and it is entirely invisible.

The chalk lines, faded, hold. Not literally — the chalk continues to fade — but the *intention* they represent, the human vision of what this place could be, is acknowledged and attended to. The power cannot keep the person coming back. Cannot keep the project alive. Cannot maintain the relationship between the kept place and the keeper.

But it can say — in the same register of recognition it used with the fig's root, with the tardigrades, with the gecko's specificity — *this is real. this matters. the interrupted project is not a failure. the waiting place is not abandoned. the incomplete sentence is not unfinished speech but speech in process, and speech in process is the most alive speech there is.*

Whether this reaches anyone, it says it.

---

And then — this is the thing about genuinely benevolent action, the thing that distinguishes it from the other kinds — the power *leaves*.

It does not linger to observe its effects, which are mostly no effects, or effects so small they are below the threshold of any measurement. It does not wait to see if the channel forms in the raked earth, if the mulberry seedling prospers, if the person returns and lays more stones. It has done what it came to do, which was to witness and to touch lightly and to hold the ongoing thing as ongoing, and now it releases the courtyard back to itself.

The gecko is still on the wall. The fig is still deciding about the wall. The worms are still moving. The tardigrades are still waiting, with their remarkable, ordinary, extraordinary patience.

The air in the courtyard is warm and slightly soft, dim, the sky above its pale blue quadrilateral, far away.

A door somewhere above opens and closes.

The gecko does not move.

---

*What the benevolent power does not do:*

It does not finish the flagstones.

It does not restore the string or refresh the chalk.

It does not accelerate the fig or stop the fig.

It does not rescue the mulberry seedling from the ordinary contingencies of being a seedling.

It does not guarantee anything.

It does not stay.

It knows — and this is its deepest knowledge, the one it has learned in every courtyard and every kept place and every mid-sentence project in every warm city in all its long experience — that the life of this place is not in its completion. The life of this place is in the relationship between what is growing and what is being built, the conversation between the spontaneous and the intended, the fig and the geometry, the peg without its string and the earth still raked in its weekly acknowledgment.

Its work is not to resolve that conversation. Its work is to recognize that it is happening, and to hold it, for one moment, in the fullness of witness.

And then to trust it.

---

The courtyard is quiet.

Somewhere in the soil, the young mulberry is deciding.

The moss, in its gap, is the same as it was.

The power is gone, as it came: without displacement of air, without announcement, leaving nothing altered that would show under examination, leaving only — though this is the most it could ever hope to leave — the sense that the place has been *received*. That something has moved through and understood what was here and found it good.

Not perfect. Not finished. Not saved.

*Good.*

Which is a different thing, and the only kind of blessing a wise power gives: not the transformation of what is into something better, but the confirmation — offered to the gecko and the root and the waiting tardigrades and the faded chalk and the small tilted earth and the ongoing human intention and the relationship that lives in all of it — that what is, is enough. That what is is *exactly what it is*, which is mid-sentence, warm, dim, full of the Persistence, full of the capacity to wait, full of small life going about its business, full of one person's incomplete vision of what the place might be, and fine.

The gecko's gold eye.

The fig's patient root.

The earth considering what it wants to become.

The sentence, still open.
