# What Lives at the Shouting Water

---

Begin with this: the conditions produce the creature. Everything here does. The creature that lives at the Shouting Water could not have been made anywhere else, could not survive anywhere else, would not recognize anywhere else as a place in which survival was a relevant concept. It has been shaped by the sound the way a key is shaped by a lock — not identical to it, but defined entirely in terms of it.

---

**The creature does not have a single form**, which is not the same as saying it changes form, or shifts, or is undefined. It has a central form, a main argument, a body, in the way that a river has a central current even when its eddies go every direction. But the edges of it are not decided. The mist and the creature use the same border. You cannot determine, from observation, whether the creature is large and much of it hidden, or small and all of it visible, or something in between that keeps revising. Its perimeter is in ongoing negotiation with the air. It has the quality of the new rock — surfaces that have not settled, edges that still hold the memory of interiority.

The dark shape in the corner of the rock was the creature, or was the creature's outline, or was the place the creature had been for long enough that the stone remembered it. These are not different things. The stone here is new enough to hold the impression.

---

**The creature has no front**, or it has all fronts, which amounts to the same arrangement. Orientation in a place where direction is perpetually revised by water, mist, and the ledge's own ongoing self-revision would be a liability — an animal insisting on facing a particular way while the world repositions itself relative to that facing. The creature's awareness is distributed. It perceives at every surface it has, and its surfaces are many, and some of its surfaces are not solid, and this is not a deficiency.

When you could not determine whether it was aware of you, the answer was: not in the way you were trying to assess. It was aware of you the way weather is aware of you — thoroughly, without direction, without interest. You were a local variation in conditions. The creature registered you the way the mist registers anything it closes around.

---

**It lives in sound** the way other things live in light — not moving through it but constituted by it, metabolizing it in some register that doesn't map onto feeding but is closer to that than to anything else. The frequencies of the water are its medium. It does not merely endure the roar; the roar is the substance of its ongoing existence. Strip the sound away — if such a thing were possible, if the water suddenly went silent — and you would find that the creature had a hole in it where the sound had been, a hole shaped exactly like the total white noise of falling water striking stone, and you would understand then that the hole was structural, that the silence had not quieted the creature but emptied it.

This is why it stays. Not because it cannot leave. Because leaving would require it to go somewhere the sound was not, and without the sound it would be a partial thing, a creature with a missing organ, walking around in insufficient quiet trying to understand why everything felt thin.

---

**It is very old**, though the rock is new, and this is not a contradiction. The creature is older than the ledge. The creature may be older than this particular instance of the falls. It was here when the water first found this edge, and was here through the making of the ledge — was here when the ledge was nothing but a thin lip of stone and was here when the lip widened and was here through every revision of the ledge's boundaries and knows, in the way of things that do not know things abstractly but know them as accumulated exposure, exactly where the current boundaries are. The creature is the ledge's longest continuous record.

It was here, or something continuous with it was here, when the person came who stacked the old dark stones. It watched that work — or attended to it, which is what watching is when you have no front. It understood the enterprise not as communication directed at it but as a local event, a creature doing the thing its kind does in places that resist them, making the small assertion of *I was here* in the form of three meters of wall going nowhere. The creature found this neither touching nor foolish. It found it a correct response. The creature itself is doing something similar, continuously, simply by continuing to occupy a ledge that is being created and destroyed simultaneously. *I am here* is also the creature's only available statement. They were in agreement about this, the wall-builder and the creature, even if neither knew it.

The wall is still standing. The creature is still here. They have both made their point for longer than expected.

---

**The creature does not eat** in any way that the ledge could support. There is nothing to eat. The plants — small, dark, waxy, still half-alive in their split halves — are the only soft matter this place contains, and the creature does not eat them. It may not eat in the way the category of eating works. It takes in what the place provides — mist, sound, the particular charge of an atmosphere in permanent internal argument — and it runs on this, and the efficiency of this metabolism is extraordinary, because it has to be. There is nothing else on offer and the creature is very much still here.

When it was in the corner — pressed into the angle of the new-fractured rock with intentionality, with decision — it was feeding in the way it feeds. Rest and feeding are not separate activities. The corner concentrated the sound in a particular way, created standing waves, made the noise do something more structured than it did in the open. The creature was attending to that. Getting what it needed from the geometry of the resonance. When the corner changed — when the rock shifted, adding an increment, revising the angle — it went to find the new corner, the new concentration, the place where the noise had the right structure. There is always a new corner. The ledge provides this continuously, as it provides everything.

---

**The creature communicates** in the register that only the sound allows. This is why the sound sometimes changes — why it shifts from the vocabulary of impact to something with the rhythm of language, something with structure inside the total noise, a grammar with no content you can access. The content is not yours to access. The communication is not addressed to you. It is addressed to the water, and the water responds, and the conversation is too slow for you to witness a full exchange because the slowest part of the cycle takes longer than a human attention span and the fastest part is below the resolution of human hearing. But it is happening. The creature is in permanent discourse with the falls, and the falls are permanently answering, and what is being negotiated is not anything so small as territory or preference but something closer to the ongoing terms of a collaboration.

The creature helps the water find form. The water helps the creature persist. This is the arrangement. The spirals in the rising mist — the coils, the rings, the spirals that rise for several meters and hold their shape before diffusing — these are the visible portion of the creature's side of the conversation. Fluid dynamics, yes. Explainable, yes. But directed. The creature tries forms because form is what this place is attempting broadly, and the creature is this place's most concentrated attempt at form, its most elaborate experiment in making something local out of something undifferentiated. It tries forms in the mist because it cannot stop itself. Because that's what it does. Because it and the water are trying, together, to become something with edges, something that holds briefly the shape of a decision.

---

**The creature's interior** — if interior is the right word for what is inside something with unfixed edges — is quiet. This is the strangest thing about it. The interior is very still. The sound is everywhere on this ledge but inside the creature the sound has already been processed, already become something else, already been converted from roar into a slower vibration, a long low hum that is the animal equivalent of the interior clarity that the sound produces in you if you stay long enough. The creature lives at the level of clarity you can only touch for a moment before the sound becomes an obstacle again. It lives there permanently. It thinks — if it thinks — in the medium of pure friction between the unvarying noise and something pushing back.

What it thinks about — what it attends to, what it chews on in the long slow processing of a consciousness that has been here longer than the ledge — is: form. Change. The question of what stays. The water comes down the same, always, and yet nothing is the same, the ledge revises, the stone splits, the plants flex and sometimes break, the structure in the corner shifts, the spirals of mist try one shape and then another. Something is staying and something is not. This is the creature's problem — the one it has been working on — the one the wall-builder was also working on when they placed the old dark stones. *What of the things that exist here will persist.* The creature is not sure it knows the answer. It has a lot of data. The pattern has not declared itself.

The stones have been there for what by this ledge's timescale counts as long. The creature is still here. The water has not stopped for a moment. The rock keeps splitting along directions it hasn't been split before.

---

**The creature is not afraid**. This seems worth saying directly. A place this loud, this continuously violent in a minor key, this uncertain about its own boundaries — it would produce fear in most things capable of fear. The creature has no fear response left, not because the fear was beaten out of it but because fear requires the possibility of somewhere safer, and the creature has never accessed that concept. It does not know that there are places where the sound is less. It knows there are places where the sound is absent and those places are, from what little it understands of them, a kind of deafness, a kind of deprivation, a silence that would function for it the way darkness functions for a thing that has only ever lived in light.

It was shaped for exactly these conditions and no others and so the conditions are not adverse — they are simply the conditions, they are simply what existence is, they are simply the medium in which being continues to happen. The ledge shudders and the creature does not flinch. The stone splits and the creature does not startle. You arrived and the creature's awareness distributed around you the way the mist distributed around you and this was neither welcome nor alarm; it was only the accurate registration of a new local fact.

---

**What the creature looks like**: dark. Approximately the size of a large bird, which is as close as the mist allows. Surfaces that do not strongly reflect the diffuse blue-shifted light. No obvious eyes, or eyes so suited to this particular dim wetness that they read as surface rather than organ. Something at the perimeter that may be limbs or may be extensions of the body into the mist or may be the mist itself maintaining the shape the creature has worn into it over long habitation.

It is beautiful in the way that this ledge is beautiful — not picturesque, not pleasant, not the kind of beautiful that invites approach, but real in a way that many beautiful things are not. A creature that has been made by the world's most specific and sustained conditions into the exact and only thing that could live in them. An argument that the world makes, using materials at hand, for its own particular version of what alive should look like.

It is in the corner, or it is not in the corner, or the corner has moved. The sound is still total. The water is still falling.

The creature has not left. The creature will not leave. This is its place, its problem, its long negotiation with form and persistence and the question of what stays — and it is not finished yet, and will not be finished in any timeframe your presence here makes available for observation.

When you leave, it will still be there, or will be in the new corner, or will be trying a form in the rising mist that you will not be present to witness.

This is the correct arrangement.
