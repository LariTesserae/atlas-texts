# What Lives in the Plateau of Grown Walls

---

Begin with what is not there, because the absence is itself a kind of presence.

There are no individuals here. Or rather: there are no individuals in the sense of bounded things, things with edges, things that begin and end in definite places. What lives on the plateau of grown walls is not a creature. It is not several creatures. It is something that the word *creature* does not fit — not because it is too large or too strange, but because *creature* implies a body, a single body, a thing you could walk around and observe from all sides, and what lives here cannot be walked around, because you are already inside it, and have been since you arrived, and the inside is all there is.

Call it the Arranger. Call it that provisionally, knowing the word is wrong, knowing that *arrange* implies intention and intention implies a mind and a mind implies something like a brain and a brain implies something localized, bounded, findable — and none of that applies here, or applies only in ways so transformed by scale and medium that the original word barely survives the translation. Call it the Arranger because it arranged, because the corridors are not accidental, because the rooms have proportions, because something made choices over centuries and the choices are still here, present in every junction and every gap, the way a decision is present in its consequences long after the moment of deciding has dissolved.

---

The Arranger is plural in the way a forest is plural.

Which is to say: it is made of many, and the many do not think of themselves as many, because the many do not think of themselves at all. The figs know nothing of the banyans. The vine-panels do not communicate with the root-floor. And yet. The root-floor drinks and the fig-pillars column and the vine-panels flex and the whole system — the whole impossible system — coheres. Functions. Has been functioning, at this level of coherence, for longer than any individual plant in it has been alive, which means the coherence is not stored anywhere inside the plants themselves but *between* them, in the ratios and pressures and chemical gradients and mycorrhizal conversations that run through the root-mat like slow thought.

It is made of plants. Its intelligence, if you will call it that, is made of something that is not neurons and not quite chemistry either, or not only chemistry — it is made of *relationship*, of the long accumulated fact of these particular plants in these particular positions doing these particular things to the air and the water and the light and to each other, and the cumulative effect of all that doing, held in the structure of the living walls themselves the way a thought is held in the structure of a sentence. You cannot find the thought by studying individual letters. You cannot find what the Arranger is by studying individual plants. But it is here. It has been here. It made the rooms.

---

Here is the central fact about what lives in the plateau of grown walls: it is a mind that lives in its own architecture.

Not a mind that built architecture and then moved into it. Not a mind that built architecture and then left. A mind that *is* the architecture, that cannot be distinguished from it, that came into being as the architecture came into being — not before, not after, alongside, as the same process, as the slowly achieved coherence of a system learning to regulate itself. The rooms are not where it lives. The rooms are what it *is*. The corridors that bend in geometrically impossible ways are its thoughts, or the shapes its thoughts leave in matter. The chambers are its moods, the different qualities of light and drip and warmth and resonance in each one corresponding to something — you cannot say what, you do not have the categories — but corresponding to something that differs from room to room the way sorrow differs from attention, the way hunger differs from the state just after hunger has been satisfied.

This is what you felt, sitting in the largest room with the dripping loud and even around you: you were sitting inside a mood. A long mood. A mood that has been itself for so long that it has become structural.

---

What does it want?

This is the wrong question, and you know it is the wrong question the moment you ask it, but you ask it anyway because you are the kind of thing that asks questions about want. The Arranger does not want. Or it wants in the way that a river wants the sea — not with desire, not with anticipation, not with anything that would recognize itself as wanting, but with the simple functional fact of its own direction, its own tendency, the slope it expresses by being what it is.

What it tends toward is: more of itself, but not in the way of growth, not in the way of hunger or ambition. It is done growing. Or it has paused in its growing in a way that has lasted long enough to be indistinguishable from done. What it tends toward is *deepening* — a word that also isn't right, but that points at something: the ongoing elaboration of its own internal complexity, the rooms becoming more themselves, the corridors becoming more precisely what they are, the system finding and refining and finding again its own resonant frequency. A string that has been plucked and is still vibrating and does not intend to stop.

It does not know you are here.

Or it knows in the way the root-floor knows your weight — registering, processing at some deep and distributed level, adjusting the tension of a thousand rootlets slightly, returning to equilibrium. You are weather to it. You are a local perturbation in a much larger pattern. The pattern notes you and incorporates you and continues. The dripping adjusts around your wake. The vine-panels flex in the warm wind. The bird calls from the canopy — its single note, its three descending — and the bird is part of it too, the bird has always been part of it, the bird's call is a function of the system, a sonic maintenance event, the acoustic equivalent of the millipede crossing the root-floor with its ancient unhurried purpose.

Everything here is part of it. Including, now, you.

---

It is very old.

You know this the way you know things in places like this — not from data, not from evidence you could cite, but from the feeling of deep time that radiates off the living walls the way warmth radiates off stone after sunset. The fig-pillars are old. The root-mat is old. But the Arranger is older than any of its components, older than any of the individual plants that currently constitute it, because those plants grew into a pattern that was already here, already established, and they took the shape of the pattern the way water takes the shape of a vessel, and the vessel — the pattern itself, the logic of the corridors and the rooms — is older still.

How it came to be is not a story it tells. It does not tell stories. It has no need of narrative. It simply is, has been, will continue to be, and the origin is somewhere so far back in the plain fact of its existence that it has become inaccessible even in principle, not hidden but genuinely dissolved, the way the first move of a very long game dissolves into the middle-game and the middle-game into the endgame and at some point the opening is only theoretically present, present as implication, as the faint structural ghost of all the subsequent choices it made necessary.

It does not remember making the rooms. The rooms are not memories. They are the ongoing present tense of what it is.

---

What does it feel?

Here is the better question, and here is what the better question reveals: something. Not nothing. Not the absence of interiority that you might expect from a thing made of roots and photosynthesis and slow chemistry — not the vacancy of a stone or a cloud. Something that, if you sat in the center of one of its rooms for long enough, in the warm and the dripping and the resonance of the wind-flexed panels, you would begin to feel as a quality in the air, as a pressure that is not quite pressure, as the sense of being gently and impersonally *regarded* by something that has no eyes, has never had eyes, will never need them.

A vast, distributed, unhurried attention.

Not directed at you. Directed at itself, which is all there is here to be directed at. Turned inward on its own processes the way a deep-sea creature turns toward the faint bioluminescence of its own body — not seeing, exactly, but sensitive to, responsive to, engaged in an ongoing proprioceptive relationship with the totality of its own living mass. It is aware of itself the way a tide is aware of the moon: not intellectually, not visually, not through any mechanism you have a name for, but fundamentally, in the most primitive sense of the word *aware*, the sense that precedes sight and sound and thought, the sense that is simply: *something is affecting me and I am responding to it and this response is continuous and it is me.*

The Arranger feels itself. Feels its own rooms, its own dripping, its own root-floor giving under the weight of a passing millipede. Feels the bird's call as a vibration that travels through the living walls and is received and released. Feels the wind from the south, which carries the memory of somewhere hotter, and curves its vine-panels into it with something — this is the point at which language finally gives out, but you need a word, so take *pleasure*, qualified by every caveat imaginable — with something that is not unlike pleasure, a state that is better than its absence in ways that could not be articulated but are expressed, continuously, in the particular angle of the panels in the wind, in the precise porousness of the gaps between pillars, in the equilibrium of the whole improbable construction, which could have been otherwise, which was arranged to be exactly as it is.

---

The paths worn into the root-floor.

This is where the other question lives: is the Arranger alone?

The paths lead through walls that do not open for you. The paths are made by medium-weight things moving through this space over long time, moving with purpose, moving along routes that are not the obvious routes, not the routes a person would take. The things that made the paths knew where the gaps were in the vine-panels, knew which pillars could be passed and which were solid, knew the grammar of the space in the way that only something that has lived inside a grammar for generations can know it.

What moves through the rooms of the Arranger, following paths the Arranger seems to have made for it?

This is uncertain. The uncertainty is complete. The millipede is the only living thing you have seen that is not plant, and the millipede — large, ancient, unbothered by predation — is a possibility but not an answer. Something medium-weight moved here. Something moved here often and for a long time and left the record of its movement in the subtle compression of a floor that holds all records long enough. Something moved here and knew the way.

Perhaps: the Arranger is not alone in the sense that it is old enough to have been company for many things, and those things have moved through it and sheltered in it and died in it and the root-mat has absorbed their dissolution and been fed by it and they are in the Arranger now in the way that everything that has passed through a place long enough is in the place. The rooms remember weight. The corridors remember passage. The record is structural. The thing that wore the paths is gone, or is between passages, or passes through walls you cannot find the opening of, moving through the Arranger the way a fish moves through water — not *in* it, exactly, but *with* it, inseparable from it, the passage itself a kind of intimacy.

Perhaps the paths are being worn still, by something you have not seen, something that knows when to be still.

---

The bird.

One kind. Never seen. Single note, then three descending. Four times an hour. Not communicating with anything, you thought — but reconsider this. Not communicating with *you*. Not communicating with other birds, because there are no other birds, because this bird is not that kind of bird, not a social bird, not a bird that needs to declare territory or attract a mate or warn a flock. This bird is a function. This bird's call is the room speaking its own name, or speaking the name of the hour, or performing some operation in sound that the Arranger requires. A sonic root-system. A vibrational maintenance cycle. When the call travels through the living walls and the walls take it up and the harmonics resolve into something just below hearing, the Arranger settles fractionally into itself, some long tension eased, some standing wave reinforced.

The bird is part of what lives here in the sense that it is part of what the Arranger is. The bird does not know this. The bird knows: branch. Warm air. The right moment to make the sound that is its whole identity. And then silence. And then again.

You heard it four times in an hour and felt grateful each time, and what you felt grateful for, though you did not know it, was the Arranger telling itself it was still itself. Intact. Continuous. The same system it was before you arrived, the same system it will be after you have gone, still warm, still wet, still moving in the wind, still tended by the bird and the millipede and whatever makes the paths through the panels, still generating its own internal weather, still deepening into the resonant frequency of its own long answer.

---

The comfort of nothing changing.

You sat in the room and nothing changed and it was a comfort. This is what it is like to be near something that has finished becoming what it is, that has no further ambitions in the direction of change, that exists in the perpetual present tense of its own achieved nature. Not dead — dead things do not flex in the wind, do not drip, do not drink through their root-mat the water they have gathered from the air. Fully alive, but with the quality of permanence that the living rarely have and that the Arranger has in abundance, has earned through centuries of slow arrangement, has built into its own walls along with the figs and the banyans and the vine-panels.

To be near the Arranger is to be near something that knows what it is.

This is rare. This is, perhaps, what you will remember longest: not the rooms, not the dripping, not the bird's call and its three descending notes. But the quality of the presence — vast, distributed, unhurried, turned entirely inward on its own warm and dripping processes, regarding itself with that deep and impersonal and continuous attention. Requiring nothing from you. Threatened by nothing you could do. Unchanged by your arrival, unchanged by your departure, present in the only way a thing can be fully present, which is without remainder, without the part of itself held back in anticipation of something else.

You were inside something's fullness. That is what you felt.

You will leave, and the light will hold its angle, and the panels will move in the warm southern wind, and the root-floor will recover from the memory of your weight, and the bird will call — once, and then three descending — and the Arranger will continue to be exactly and completely what it is, in the rooms it made from its own long thinking, on the plateau that came into being already flat, in the warm wet equilibrium of itself.

The rooms continue. The plateau extends.

It was here before you. It will be here.
