# Who Lives Here

---

Begin with the fact of smallness.

Not smallness as diminishment. Smallness as *choice*, or as something that became choice over so many generations the choosing is no longer conscious, the way a river doesn't choose its course but is nonetheless going somewhere. They are small enough that the frogs are not prey but neighbors. Small enough that the midges constitute not a nuisance but a kind of weather system, something to move through and be aware of. Small enough that the stone walls, knee-high to you, are a credible architecture. Small enough that the basket is the right size to be useful.

How small is difficult to say exactly. The evidence suggests a range. The path between the stone walls implies a stride. The posts suggest how high a hand can work comfortably. The basket, hung where it hangs, implies someone who either can reach that height or has reason to suspend things above their own reach. A height somewhere between the mushroom and the frog. Somewhere that requires you to revise your understanding of the space you are standing in — to realize it is not a small room but a large one, that the proportions you have been assigning to things have all been wrong, that the *four meters* you measured with your eyes is in fact a considerable plaza.

---

They are old.

Not individually, necessarily, but as a people, as a practice. The depth of the moss on the posts — the thoroughness of the incorporation — is a measure of time. The particular knowledge required to find this pocket and understand its potential is not knowledge that arrives quickly. The understanding that this level, this cold wet bottom-floor of the vegetative mass, is not an obstacle but a location — that is understanding that has been worked toward, probably lost, probably recovered, probably refined over iterations that do not leave clear records.

They have been here long enough that the vegetation knows them. This is not metaphor. The path where the ground is differently compressed — the frogs move to the edge of it when they cross it, unconsciously, the way animals in established territories unconsciously honor lines they didn't draw. The beetles on the walls have their routes, and the routes avoid certain areas, and the avoidance is not recent. There is a negotiated quality to the space, an ecology that has made room in its structure for the fact of inhabitants, the way a river makes room for a stone that has been there long enough.

---

They are, primarily, nocturnal.

Or: they are creatures of this light specifically, which is close to the same thing. The eyes would be large, almost certainly — not grotesquely, not as a feature you would notice first, but as something you would register eventually and then find you'd been noticing for longer than you realized. Large enough to gather this dim green-pewter stain of illumination and do something useful with it. Large enough that in full daylight, if they ever encounter full daylight, they would squint and feel exposed. The irises would be very dark, very wide, the kind of eyes that make the world they look at seem private, held close.

Their color would match this place the way the frogs' color matches it, but with more variation, the way a species with self-decoration has more variation than one without. Base tones in the range of the moss, the wet stone, the greenish dark — but marked, differentiated, by pigments that have purpose in this specific low light, that distinguish individual from individual in spectra narrower than your eyes manage. You could not tell them apart easily. They would find your difficulty slightly incomprehensible, the way you might find it incomprehensible to be unable to tell faces apart.

---

They move with the quality of the beetles.

This is the most important physical thing about them, and it is not an insult. The beetles' movement — intentional, route-conscious, aware of the surface, carrying their own weight without disturbing anything unnecessarily — is a kind of ideal in this space. They have arrived at the same movement through different means, through attention and practice and the consequences of not attending, which in this space would mean a foot through the surface-crust of the soil, a saturated footstep, a small disaster. They have learned to move as though the floor is a negotiation, which it is. Their weight is distributed differently from yours. Their steps, when they walk, displace less. The frogs do not move when they move through them, which is the real measure.

In stillness, they are very still. This is the other quality they share with the inhabitants of this space. The frog's stillness. The beetle's stillness when it reaches the edge of its route and needs to reassess. A stillness that is not passive but operational, that is doing something, that is resolving information into decision. They can hold this stillness for periods that would become uncomfortable for you, would become a kind of anxiety, an intrusive need to check whether time is still passing. For them it is not uncomfortable. It is where a great deal of the actual work happens.

---

The basket.

The basket is the most important thing about them, not physically but epistemologically. The basket is what they made that they didn't have to make in order to survive. Or: they have been here long enough that the basket is necessary for surviving in the way they have decided they want to survive, which is not exactly the same thing as biological necessity but is the only kind of necessity that a civilization recognizes.

What is in the basket. This is where you have to stay with the uncertainty, because the answer opens onto everything else. It is not food, or not only food, or not food in the simple sense. The rotation of the vine — the slow untwist, the pause, the retwist — means the basket's contents are being given something: the rotation itself, perhaps, which is a form of movement that is entirely different from all the other movement in this space, which is entirely different from a frog repositioning, a beetle finding a route, because it is being given by something outside itself to something inside itself.

It could contain eggs of something. It could contain seeds in a particular phase of their germination that benefits from rotation. It could contain a living thing of the kind that would be too small for you to see in this light but that they can tend and observe. It could contain something with a life cycle they track, a development they are measuring against criteria you don't have language for.

What matters is that someone is coming back for it. The basket was hung with the expectation of return. There is a schedule. It may be a schedule that looks nothing like your schedules — it may be calibrated to the vine's twist, to the frog's cycle, to the beetle's route, to the press of water through the soil. But it is a schedule. This place is not abandoned. The absence of its inhabitants is a temporary state.

---

They speak.

Of course they speak. But what they speak into is the full, occupied quiet of this place, which means they have learned what the frogs learned, what the beetles practice: that sound in this space travels and is registered by many kinds of attention. They speak when they speak. They also use other registers.

Their hands. Not signs exactly — not a formal language of gesture in the sense of a language constructed and codified — but a practice of gesture that has over time developed precision and convention, that can communicate in the near-darkness at short range without contributing to the sonic record of the space. Two of them can have a conversation that you would read as silence while standing between them.

Smell. Not as an alternative language but as an ambient channel, a continuous broadcast of state information that they read without effort the way you read the expressions of faces. You would not be able to read it. You would probably notice it — a faint layered complexity in the air, something resinous and mineral and organic, something that changes in ways you can almost but not quite track. The changes are information. They know this the way they know the weight of the air.

Touch. When they are in contact — which they often are, which is a preference rooted in the cold and also in the information economy of this place, where every channel that costs nothing is worth using — the information exchanged is continuous and fine. Pressure. Temperature. Pulse. A whole grammar of small alerts and reassurances conducted through maintained contact, a running commentary between touching surfaces.

They do make sounds. They are not silent. But they have developed a sense for when sound is called for that is more conservative than yours, and the sounds they make are precise where yours are verbose, are distilled down to what the other channels cannot carry, are never decorative.

---

What they believe.

Start with the water.

The water is the permanent thing. Not the vegetation, which dies and is replaced. Not the stone, which came from somewhere and will go somewhere. The water is the thing that was here before any of this and will be here after, that moves through everything and is changed by nothing for long, that is right now pressing up through the soil floor of this space with the patience of the oldest possible process. They know the water. They have, over more generations than they have a counting system for, developed a relationship with the water that is somewhere between theology and ecology and hydrology, somewhere the three of those are not different subjects.

The water carries things. It carries minerals up from below and takes matter down and redistributes both horizontally through the saturated layer that is so close below them. They have learned to read it. The quality of the water that surfaces in the small mirror-puddles tells them things about what is happening in the ground, what the season is doing, what the larger system above is managing. They are readers of water the way a navigator reads stars — not because they are worshipping the water, though respect and reading are not always easy to separate, but because it is information and they are meticulous about information.

They believe the incorporated things are still present. The timber posts are not gone. The moss that grew across them did not erase them. They are still there, inside the moss, still original material even as the boundary between the original and the added becomes less and less resolvable. They believe this about everything: that nothing that has been here leaves entirely. That the space is therefore very full. That some of the fullness is their own, of generations who have been here and are in some form of continued presence in the incorporated matter, in the moss that grew from what they left, in the cold residency of the water. They talk to this fullness. Not always. Not formally. But in the way you might address someone who is in the next room and may or may not be listening.

---

What they do.

The wall-stones were placed not as enclosure but as cultivation. Between and beneath the flat stones, conditions are specific: temperature slightly higher, moisture retained differently, mineral composition of the immediate soil altered by the stone's own slow dissolution. They are growing something in these conditions. They have grown it in these conditions for long enough that what they are growing has adapted to these specific conditions and would struggle without them. It is a partnership of the kind that has no outside observer to name it, that predates the vocabulary for describing it, that would require a word you don't have.

They maintain the beetle routes without meaning to, by moving in their own routes, by making available or unavailable certain conditions that the beetles respond to. They are not managing the beetles. But the beetles are not independent of them. The relationship is older than intention on either side.

The frogs. The frogs are the difficult one, the one where the relationship is closest to something you might call kept or tended and furthest from anything you could call wild, but where those categories, which were always your categories, not theirs, have the least purchase. They do not cage the frogs. They do not have to. The frogs that live in this space have lived in this space for enough generations that they are, subtly, different from their relatives outside it — their coloring calibrated to this specific light, their behavior shaped by the presence of other minds in the space with them, by a relationship with non-predators that has altered what they are afraid of and when and how much. This alteration goes in both directions. They themselves have been altered by the frogs. By watching them. By building a sense of the space's health through the frogs' behavior, the frogs' density, the quality of the frogs' stillness.

They weave. The basket required weaving, and the basket is not the only woven thing, though it is the most visible. What else they have made of this skill is harder to see: some of it is structural, incorporated into the walls, strengthening the posts against the slow pull of the vegetation's growth; some of it is small and purposeful and would be in their hands or their dwelling-places, wherever those are; some of it is the path itself, which near its beginning, where the ground pressure is most complex, is reinforced with a woven material pressed into the soil that you would not notice if you didn't know to look.

---

Where they come from.

There was a time before this place. There are stories about it. The stories are not stories exactly — they are not performed, not narrated in the way you would narrate — but they exist in the way that old knowledge exists in a community that has been in one place long enough, which is distributed and partial and inconsistent and somehow more reliable for all of that. Different elders have different pieces. The pieces don't entirely fit. This is considered appropriate. The time before this place was different from this place, and should be somewhat illegible from within it.

What the stories agree on: there was movement. There was a period of looking. There was the moment of finding, or of the finding finding them — the two are not distinguished in the stories because they were experienced as simultaneous. A place that was ready for inhabiting meeting inhabitants who were ready for a place.

What the stories don't agree on: where they came from before. Whether there was a previous place. Whether the finding was accidental or inevitable. What they were doing during the movement that prepared them to recognize this pocket as theirs. These questions are not considered to have answers, or are considered to have answers that are not available from inside the found place.

---

What they want.

The question has a quality of wrongness here. Not because they are simple, not because they lack interiority, but because *want* implies a separation between them and the situation, a place to stand outside the situation and regard it, that they do not maintain in the way that you do. They are inside the situation. They are the situation, partly. The want and the having and the being are not steps in a sequence for them; they have compressed to something more like a single operation.

But: they want the basket to contain what it contains. They want the vine to keep twisting and untwisting and for the content to benefit from this. They want the water to keep pressing up and revealing itself in the mirror-puddles and to be readable in the ways it has always been readable. They want the frogs to be here and the beetles to be here and the large careful thing in the ceiling to be here. They want the stone walls to keep doing what the stone walls are doing and the moss to keep advancing across the stones in the slow victory that is not a threat. They want to come back to this place and have it be this place.

They want the space to hold.

---

Whether they know you are here is the question you've been not quite asking.

The frogs may have told them, or told the air, which told whatever reads the air in this space, which told something else further along a chain of attention that functions as a nervous system without a center. Or the frogs are not involved; the compressed ground registered your weight differently from the weight of something that belongs here, and the difference is now a fact in the system, traveling.

Or they were already aware of you before you were aware of yourself as someone who could be in this place — before you found the pocket, before you understood that you were inside something. They have been here long enough to know the edge of their space the way you know the edge of a familiar room in the dark, by the quality of the air before you reach it, by a change in the cold, by the way the moisture shifts.

They are not afraid of you.

This is not the same as saying you are welcome.

It is closer to saying: you are an event, and events are information, and they are very good at information, and they will decide what to do with this one in the way they decide everything, which is carefully, and slowly, and in conversation with every other living thing in the space, and without telling you they are deciding.

The basket turns.

The frogs sit.

The cold sits in the water.

Something in the ceiling is completely, deliberately still.

They are thinking about you.
