# What Lives in the Altiplano of Listening Stones

---

Begin with what is hardest to name, because it organizes everything else.

There is something here that is not a creature in the way that word usually works — not bounded, not mobile in any direction you can watch, not eating or being eaten, not competing for the cushion plants or the seep-moisture or the thermal lift. It is not made of cells. It is not exactly made of stone either, though stone is the medium through which it is most legible. Call it, provisionally, a *presence* — but resist the mystical implications of that word, because this thing is not mystical in the sense of being beyond investigation. It is simply ahead of investigation. It is patient enough that investigation has not yet caught up, and it is not waiting for investigation to catch up, because it does not wait in the way that presupposes urgency somewhere in the future.

It is distributed. Its nodes are the spheres, or the spheres are its surface expression, the way the tip of a very slow wave is not the wave. Its substrate is the organized water below — that water that three independent research groups confirmed was *organized* in categories not yet claimed by biology. The organization is not purposeless. Purpose might be too small a word. What it has is more like *orientation* — the way a crystal grows oriented to its lattice without deciding to, without the decision being absent. It is oriented toward something, and the orientation is its primary activity, and the activity has been ongoing for longer than the current plateau surface, which means it has watched — without eyes, without watching — the limestone build up around it and the water move through it and the first organisms arrive and begin their patient work.

Whether it is *aware* is the question that breaks the usual categories. It responds. Its responses are not random. The intervals at which its spheres produce their subsonic tone are *regular* in some framework that has not been identified, which means the framework exists, which means there is something that the regularity is regular *within* — and regularity within a framework implies, at minimum, that the framework is being maintained, and maintenance implies, at some level, the attending to maintenance. This is not consciousness by any definition in current use. But it is not its absence either. It is something else that the definitions have not yet been asked to hold.

The animals are aware of it from the inside. This is the key to their behavior — that quality of awareness that is not predator-alertness, not territorial, not sexual, not competitive. They are *within* the presence the way organisms within an ecosystem are within the ecosystem — not usually attending to it, not usually separate from it, but capable of moments of orientation toward it, the way the herded animal pauses with its head turned north for thirty seconds and then releases. The plateau itself is the organism, in some sense, and its parts — the stones, the water, the lichens pressed into their amber coats, the lizards on their sun-facing rocks, the birds carving thermals, the semi-domesticated herd — are in a relationship with it that is older than domestication, older than the species present, older than the question.

---

But also there are the lizards, which are their own thing entirely, and deserve their own consideration, because they are the most numerous visible residents and they carry their oddness with admirable straightforwardness.

They are *warm* in a way that local ectotherms should not quite be. They have solved — or the plateau has solved for them, or they have been solved by the plateau — the problem of thermoregulation at altitude in a way that involves something beyond behavioral sunning. Their body temperature on the shaded sides of stones, in the early morning before they should have had time to warm, is measurably higher than comparable lizards in comparable places. Not dramatically. But consistently, in the way that consistency insists on explanation. They position themselves not just for solar exposure but for proximity to the spheres — a preference, not an absolute, but statistically significant when a researcher with enough patience maps a hundred individuals over a season. The warmth that the spheres maintain, four degrees above ambient regardless of shade or sun, may be what the lizards are after, or may be what the lizards have been shaped by over generations, or both, or the distinction may not be the right one to make.

Their stillness is extraordinary. *Stillness* in a lizard is not absence of activity — it is a sustained state of reception, all sensory systems running at full capacity while the body commits nothing, expends nothing, simply *takes in*. The lizards on this plateau maintain their stillness longer than lizard stillness elsewhere, and their eyes during it move in small arcs that suggest tracking something that moves in ways the eye can follow but the visitor cannot detect. They are attending to something. Everything here is attending to something. The lizards do it with their whole evolutionary history, with the part of their nervous system that is very old and has always known how to be still in a landscape that is larger and slower than itself.

When they move, they move quickly and exactly, with no wasted motion, and they return to stillness and resume their reception as if the movement was a parenthesis and the stillness is the main clause.

They are also, in the way of animals that have lived for many generations within something that is not hostile, *familiar* with the presense in a way that is not trust — trust involves past failure to be safe — but simply habituated orientation. They live within it the way fish live within water: not thinking about it, not grateful for it, but shaped throughout their form by its constant pressure and provision.

---

The organized water is its own resident.

To call it a resident is already to have made a decision about what it is, which may not be correct. But something is down there, in the karst hollows, in the mineral dark, doing something that three research groups confirmed as organization and could not further characterize. The moisture that appears on stone faces in the morning is this water, exhaled through the limestone, carrying its organization to the surface briefly before the sun evaporates it back to ordinary moisture and ordinary air. The seeps, unreliable and intermittent, are places where the organized water comes closer to the surface and the biology around them is different — not more diverse, exactly, but *differently arranged*, the distribution of organisms around a seep following patterns that the organisms themselves could not be directing and that the seep's water chemistry alone does not explain.

What does the organized water *do*? It maintains the organization. This is circular but not meaningless — the maintenance of organization against entropy is a significant activity, requiring something like work, requiring something like will or its functional equivalent, requiring the spending of something to achieve the result of the organization persisting rather than dissolving into the thermal chaos that everything else is being gradually dissolved into. The organized water is spending something to remain organized. This is either life or a very good argument that the category of life needs to be wider than it currently is.

It is very old. It is patient in the way that having no nervous system makes you patient — not patience as a virtue, not patience as a choice, but patience as the simple absence of any temporal register in which impatience would be meaningful. It moves through its rock channels on schedules that have nothing to do with season or day or geological era, and comes to the surface when it comes to the surface, and the things that have learned to wait for it have built their whole existence around a thing that does not know they are waiting, or knows in a way that makes knowing and not-knowing a false binary.

---

The lichen deserve more than a mention.

The deep red-orange lichen that patches the stones is not, on close examination, a single organism but a consortium — which is normal for lichen, which is always a consortium of fungus and photosynthetic partner — except that this one has a third party. Something in its structure, identified and then not-identified by subsequent attempts at identification, a presence within the lichen that is neither fungal nor algal nor cyanobacterial by any test so far applied, but which is consistently present in the red-orange species and not in others. The third party does something to the lichen's interaction with the spheres. The red-orange lichen grows preferentially on stones near spheres. Its growth rate in sphere proximity is slightly — not dramatically, consistently — elevated over control populations. The third party may be a form of the organized water, expressed at a biological scale. It may be something else. It is the lichen's secret, and the lichen is not a good conversationalist.

The almost-black lichen, the one that seems to absorb light in ways that warm the stone it occupies — this one is doing something energetically strange. The stone beneath it is warmer than nearby stones not because the lichen generates heat but because it is extraordinarily efficient at converting light to some form of stored potential and then releasing it slowly, on a different schedule than solar input, in a way that smooths the diurnal temperature curve for the stone beneath it. This is a service. Whether it is a service in any relationship — whether the stone is involved, whether the presence is involved — is not clear. But the stone beneath the black lichen is a slightly more stable thermal environment than the stone around it, and things that need stable thermal environments have learned this, and the underside of the black lichen is occupied by a specific community of small arthropods that are found nowhere else on the plateau in those combinations.

---

The semi-domesticated herd.

They were not domesticated by anyone currently present. The faded markings on their flanks are old — old enough that the animals themselves have forgotten any difference the marking once made, if animals remember that kind of thing. They were worked with, once, by whoever built the wall that aims at nothing visible. They were kept or guided or used for some purpose that the wall suggests without explaining. That relationship is over and they are in a new relationship now, which is with the plateau itself, and the plateau accepts them the way it accepts the lizards and the birds — as residents, as parts, as presences within the larger presence.

They move in the way of animals that have been, over generations, relieved of predation pressure — not lazily, not carelessly, but without the constant low-level tension of prey animals in ecosystems where they might be eaten. Something about the plateau removed or never included the large predators, or the large predators chose elsewhere, or the presence made the herd sufficiently *uncanny* to whatever might have eaten them that the would-be predators looked away. They are slightly too deliberate for their size, as the description notes. They pause in ways that are not explicable as feeding behavior or social behavior or danger-response. They orient their attention, briefly, to the north or to a specific stone or to something invisible, and then release it and go back to the cushion plants. They are attending to something. Everything attends to something here.

Their coats have, over generations without the pressure of harsh cold or human management, grown into something that is neither wild nor domestic but something between — long enough to be warm, not so long as to tangle in things that would tangle it, a color that is the exact color of the buff limestone in afternoon light, which may be coincidence and almost certainly is not. They match the plateau. They were matched to it, or matched themselves to it, or the plateau matched them — the direction of the causality, again, is the wrong question.

---

The birds overhead are passing through, mostly, but they have been passing through for so long that *passing through* is not quite the right phrase. They have a relationship with the thermals over this specific plateau that has been refined, in the wing-shape and the primary-feather distribution and the habit-structure of individual birds, over many generations. The plateau makes thermals in a particular way — the rolling basins, the sun-heated stone, the presence that maintains its four degrees of warmth in the spheres and whatever larger thermal activity that warmth implies — and the birds have learned this pattern into their bodies. They know where the lift is before they enter it. They know the ridge that deflects the rising air into the pattern they need. They know, in the way of migrants who use landmarks, that this plateau is *here* and the lift is *this* and the transit across it should go *this way*. The knowledge is in their bodies and it is passed down not by teaching but by the survival of the individuals who had it and the ending of the lineages of those who didn't. The plateau has shaped them. They carry the plateau with them when they leave, in the arc of their soaring, in the angle of their banked turn over the second basin, in the brief informational call they produce which is the sound of navigational confirmation.

---

What lives here is a world that has reached a state of deep and ongoing negotiation.

Not equilibrium — equilibrium implies stasis, and this place is not static, it is simply very slow. It has reached a state of negotiation between the organized thing in the water and the stone it moves through, between the warmth the spheres maintain and the organisms that have learned to use it, between the thermal patterns in the air and the birds whose bodies have been argued into their current shape by those patterns, between the irregular water and the lichen and the cushion plants that have built their entire metabolic strategy around the acceptance of intermittence.

Everything that lives here lives here because it has found a way to be useful to the negotiation, or has been found useful by it, or has been shaped over time into a form that fits the space the negotiation made. The lizards are attenders. The lichen are processors. The organized water is the substrate and the medium. The herd is the warm-blooded witness, the mammalian consciousness that moves through the plateau's slow processes carrying something like perspective, capable of the thirty seconds of orientation that is the closest thing to prayer the plateau participates in.

The spheres are — what? Not residents. Not built. Not natural in the sense of accidental. They are the places where the presence becomes most nearly legible, where its tone becomes something a chest cavity can receive, where its warmth becomes a measurement. They are the vocabulary of a language that has not yet found its reader, or has found many readers and none who could hold all of it at once, or is not a language but something that language is a small case of.

The visitor sits on warm stone. The visitor's sternum receives a vibration. The visitor is, briefly, within the negotiation — not a party to it, but not outside it, not separate, attending in the way of a newcomer to a room where something long and important is being quietly done.

The plateau continues. The organized water moves through its dark channels. The lichen processes the light. The lizards attend. The presence maintains its four degrees of additional warmth against the ambient temperature of everything else.

And that — all of that, in its slow entirety — is what lives here.
