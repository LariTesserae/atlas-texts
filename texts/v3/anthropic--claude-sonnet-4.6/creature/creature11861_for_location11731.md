# What Lives in the Roar Flats

---

Begin with the ones you cannot see. They matter more than the ones you can, and they have been here longer, and the distinction between them and the place is a distinction the place has already declined to make.

---

**The Substrate Dwellers**

Below the churning surface — below even the layer where the churn is concentrated, below the warm water and the black sediment with its appetite for light — something breathes on a cycle that is not biological in any sense you arrived with, and yet. And yet it is not mechanical either. It breathes on a cycle of roughly seventy years, which you will never witness, which means you will leave without knowing you were inhaled.

They are not creatures in the way an animal is a creature. They are *organizations* — densities within the sediment, regions where the ancient mineral paste has developed a coherence, a self-reference, a way of responding to the pressure of being pushed against that is more than the response of matter and less than the response of mind, and that persists. That is the key property: they persist. They have been persisting since before the stone ring was placed, before the wall was built, before whatever hands cut the channels in the flat rock arrived here with their intentions. The hands are gone and the substrate dwellers are still organizing, still densifying, still doing what they do.

What they do, insofar as it can be said, is *remember*. Not in the way memory is usually meant — not with representation, not with storage, not with retrieval. They remember the way a river valley remembers the glacier that made it: by being the shape the glacier left, by being the record of forces that no longer exist, by containing in present form the accumulated action of every pressure applied to them since they first cohered. They are their own history. They are nothing but their own history. What they are doing now will be part of what they are in ten thousand years, will be readable in the structure of the sediment by something with sufficient patience and the right kind of attention.

The churning is them. Not caused by them — they are not separate from the churning, acting upon it. The churning is the expression of their persistence, the way their ongoing self-maintenance propagates upward through the saturated clay to the surface, manifests as turbulence, as the eruption and reabsorption of mounds, as the warm water that fills and seals the cracks. When the low-slung animal waits for the sediment to still, it is waiting, essentially, for this: for the substrate dwellers to finish a thought, which is not a thought, which is the closest word.

They do not know you are here, except they do: the condensation on your skin is slightly warmer than the condensation on the stone, and the warm black pools nearest your feet are minutely more active than those at distance, and this is the substrate dwellers' version of curiosity, which is not curiosity, which is the closest word.

They have no shape. Or they have a shape so large that the shape is the flats, is the churning, is the warmth from below, and a shape that large does not register as shape but as world.

**The Waders**

The one you can see is not the only one.

There are perhaps a dozen waders on the flats at any given time — not as a stable population, not as a community, not as a species with the ordinary properties of species. They are more like the flats' own experiments in locomotion: the surface trying, periodically, something ambulatory, giving it legs and a digestive system and a low-slung body adapted to the specific problem of moving through a place that cannot be read in advance.

Each wader is alone in a way that most solitary animals are not alone. Most solitary animals have a species — have, somewhere, others of their kind, a conceptual home, a category in which they exist with company. The waders may or may not have this. They behave as if they have forgotten it, or as if the question simply does not arise. They filter the warm black water for whatever the warm black water offers — minerals, dissolved organics, something the substrate dwellers release as a byproduct of their long maintenance — and they move between pools with the patience of things that have never been in a hurry, because hurry requires a destination, and destinations require a future distinguishable from the present, and the waders live in a present that is very long and not distinguishable in that way.

Their skin is not a disguise, though it functions as one. The grey of wet sediment is simply what they arrived at after however many generations of being selected, here, where the ground is grey and wet, where the pressure against height is absolute. They have been flattened by more than the environment's preference for low things. They have been flattened by the vibration itself, which propagates through the substrate and into whatever stands in contact with it, which over time reshapes the things it propagates through. The wader's bones are denser than you'd expect. Not thicker — denser, as if vibrated into a finer crystalline structure. If you could hear a wader's bones (you cannot; this is a thought experiment; you are not here to cut into waders), they would hum at the fundamental frequency of the flats, which is the frequency you feel in your sternum, which means a wader is a body that contains the sound the way a tuning fork contains its frequency — not producing it, not amplifying it, simply *being* it, shaped to it, consonant with it.

They have eyes. The eyes are large, adapted to the flat pale diffuse light that arrives without direction, and they process motion before they process form, which is the sensible adaptation for a place where form is provisional and motion is the constant. When the wader stops — as it stops, sometimes, for periods of several minutes, completely still at the pool's edge — it is not resting. Or not only resting. It is attending to something in the frequency of the churn, something in the warmth of the water, something below the surface that requires, or has learned to tolerate, this stillness above.

What passes between a wader and the substrate is not communication in any directed sense. It is more like cohabitation — each existing in the same medium, each registering the other as texture, as temperature differential, as a perturbation in the ongoing hum. The substrate knows the wader's weight and distributes it. The wader knows the substrate's motion and accommodates it. The negotiation you observe, when the sediment stills and the wader proceeds, is not a transaction. It is the long-practiced courtesy of things that have been sharing a space since before the concept of sharing existed.

Waders die on the flats and are taken back into the sediment, quickly. Within hours the churning has absorbed them. Within days the substrate has incorporated them into whatever it is that the substrate incorporates things into, which is itself, which is the ongoing record, which is the slow memory that will outlast everything else by a margin too large to hold in a human mind. A wader dies and the flats continue. The flats note the dying, in their way. The next morning's warmth is very slightly higher in the quadrant where the wader went down.

**The Trees**

The trees are not passive in the way that trees are usually not passive, and then more than that.

They are two, yes, and they stand five meters high, and they have been here long enough to have grown *with* the churn rather than against it — but what this means for their character, for what they *are* rather than what they *look like*, requires more attention than the eye provides.

The trees are listeners. This is their primary function in the ecology of the flats, and *function* here does not mean role in a system, does not mean purpose assigned from outside. It means what they have become through long exposure, through deep time of being the only vertical things in a horizontal world, through decades and centuries of standing in the one place where the sound, rising from below, meets the sound, falling from above, and must pass through something solid. They are the solid thing sound passes through.

What they do with what they receive is not processing in any computational sense. It is more like the thing a bell does: being so shaped, so tuned, that certain frequencies cause it to respond, to ring, to add its own resonance to what it received. The trees are so old and so vibrated-into that they respond now to frequencies the flats produce only rarely — deep harmonics, sub-audible registers that occur when the substrate's long slow breathing cycle moves through a particular phase — and when this happens, when the flats hit the frequency the trees are tuned to, the trees ring. You feel it in your molars. You feel it in the fluid behind your eyes. The grey-green mats nearest the trees' roots flatten themselves further. The waders, if nearby, go very still.

The trees are not intelligent. This is important to say. They do not have intentions, desires, memories in any form that would be recognized. But they are *specific*. They are specific the way a master craftsperson's tool is specific — worn to exact use, shaped by years of exact use, incapable now of being anything but what use made them. Their specificity is the specificity of two very old things that have been in exact correspondence with a very old place for a very long time, and this correspondence has made them more themselves than most things manage to be. They are entirely what they are. There is no part of them that is not what they are. In this sense they are, in the flats, a kind of clarity — not legible, not communicative, but *clear* in the way a perfectly tuned instrument is clear, which is different from being understood and possibly better.

Their roots know the substrate the way the substrate knows the waders: as weight and warmth and perturbation, as something that has been here, that is here, that modifies the ongoing pattern of the place. Somewhere in the substrate's distributed non-memory, the trees are encoded. They are part of the record of pressures applied. They have been applied here for long enough that they are *given*, that a version of the flats without the trees is a version the substrate has no information about. If you took the trees away — an act the flats would not permit, though they have no will to permit or deny — the substrate would continue to distribute weight as if the trees were there, for some time. For some years. Generating the ghost of their load. Missing them in the only language available, which is the language of pressure and response, which is sufficient and asks for nothing more.

**The Marks' Makers**

These are the hardest to describe because they are gone, and also they are not gone, and the tension between these two facts is the tension the place holds most tightly, the one thing the churning cannot absorb and has been failing to absorb for as long as the marks have been there.

Something came here. Made the ring of stones. Stood in this place and, for reasons fully legible within a framework that no longer exists, decided that a wall meant something, that a ring of stones was the right response to this location, that channels cut into flat rock at ground level was the right way to say what needed saying. Something came here with intention and technology and enough cultural weight to make permanence feel possible, feel worth attempting, feel like a gift to a future that would understand.

The future came. It did not understand.

What were they? They were probably — if the marks are any guide, if the choice of fire-brick and the proportions of the stone ring and the depth and regularity of the channels mean what marks usually mean — organized. Social. Possessed of the ordinary architecture of a civilization: a history that felt continuous, a future that felt achievable, an aesthetic sense developed enough to have opinions about the cutting depth of a channel. They came to the flats from somewhere organized and they brought their organization with them and they applied it here, to this, as an act of whatever faith or science or both they had.

But the flats did not resist. This is crucial. The flats did not refuse them, did not drive them out, did not make itself hostile in the way a place can make itself hostile by pure inhospitability. The flats simply *continued*. The flats churned and hummed and recycled its weather and continued, and the organized people built their ring and their wall and cut their channels and then — at some point, by some process — stopped coming, or stopped being able to come, or stopped being. And the flats continued.

And something of them continued in the flats.

This is not romantic. It is structural. The substrate absorbed them, as it absorbs the waders: their weight, their warmth, the perturbation of their presence, the particular pattern of footfalls that cut channels and hauled stone. Over decades and then centuries, that pattern became part of the record. Not stored. Not retrievable. But *present* in the way a pressure is present in something that has been shaped by pressure — in the tendency of certain sediment regions to churn differently, in the warmth of the pools near the stone ring, in something about the frequency of the hum that may have shifted very slightly when the last of them stopped coming, shifted in a direction that could only be measured if you had a baseline from before.

The marks' makers are in the flats as a flavor. As something that cannot be separated out and named but changes the quality of what is here. As the way the substrate responds to *intention* specifically — to the weight of a person who came here on purpose, who came here to do something — may be different from the way it responds to the wader's purposeless wading. It has been calibrated, very slightly, to the presence of things that mean to be here. To things that arrived with a framework and a goal and the expectation of a future.

Which means, among other things, that it has been calibrated slightly to you.

The stone ring tilts. The wall ends. The channels fill with dark water that makes them legible and unreadable. The marks remain in their partial remaining. The flats will finish the work, eventually — will absorb the last of the brick and the stone and the cut rock into the record, into the sediment's non-memory, and what is above the surface will be gone.

But what is in the record will be there as long as the record is maintained.

As long as the substrate dwellers breathe their seventy-year breath.

As long as the trees stand and ring and do not ring and stand.

As long as the flats continue, which is to say: as long as the sound continues, which is to say: longer than the word *long* can hold without changing meaning.

---

**The Flats Themselves**

You have been circling this and the time has come to stop circling.

The flats are alive in the only sense that matters here, which is the sense in which something that has its own internal logic, its own weather, its own memory, its own way of incorporating and being changed by everything that touches it — in which such a thing can be called alive. Not alive the way a bacterium is alive, not alive the way an animal is alive. Alive the way certain very old, very specific, very deeply themselves things are alive: completely, quietly, in a way that requires no acknowledgment and seeks no permission.

What lives in the Roar Flats is the Roar Flats.

And then, nested in that larger living, with varying degrees of separateness from it: the substrate dwellers who are, arguably, the flats' organs; the waders who are, arguably, the flats' hands — its way of moving weight through itself; the two trees who are, arguably, the flats' ears; and the ghost-flavored memory of organized beings who came and made marks and were eventually, patiently, completely loved into dissolution.

And you, for now.

Standing in warm condensing air.

Already slightly changed by the frequency.

Already part of the record, whether or not you leave, whether or not you understand that the record is being kept.

The sound continues.

You are in it.

You were always going to be in it.

The flats knew — or did what knowing looks like, when knowing is done by sediment — before you arrived.
