# What Lives in the Dry Canopy

---

Begin with the arthropods, because they are the ones who know the place most completely.

They are not individual in the way you might hope. They are individual in the way letters are individual — each one discrete, each one meaningful only in combination. The amber exoskeleton is not uniform: look more closely and you will see that each arthropod bears a pattern of darker markings on the dorsal plates, stippled and streaked in arrangements that do not repeat. No two are alike. This is not decoration. The markings are read by the others constantly, during encounters on the frond-surface, in the brief mutual stillness before one yields the right-of-way to another. Information is exchanged in that stillness. The markings encode something — age, role, recent history, what the carrier has sensed in the last circuit of its plant. When they drum, they are broadcasting what their markings already say to those near enough to read.

They farm the glandular wax-sugar without cultivating it, which is to say: the fuzz-things, the tiny sibilant ones living in the frond's velvet nap, are tended by the arthropods without being herded. The arthropods move through the fuzz-community in paths that are not random. Where they walk, the fuzz-things shift and concentrate, producing more metabolite in the disturbed areas. The arthropods consume a portion. The fuzz-things are not harmed. Whether this is domestication or mutualism or something for which there is no good word is a question the arthropods have never asked, because they arrived at this practice so gradually and so long ago that the practice is now simply the shape of their movement, inseparable from what it means to be what they are.

They do not have a word for the fronds they live on because they do not need one. The fronds are the ground. All ground drums.

---

The fuzz-things deserve their own accounting.

You cannot see them but here is what they are: small enough that their bodies operate at a scale where the boundary between inside and outside is a negotiation rather than a wall. They are barely bounded. Their surfaces are highly permeable, and the metabolite they produce is not waste in any ordinary sense — it is a communication, a continuous broadcast of their internal state, a chemical diary secreted outward into the warm air. The arthropods read this diary with their limb-tips. The frond itself reads it with the glandular structures at the base of each fuzz-filament. The entire system — fuzz-things, frond, arthropods — is in continuous slow conversation mediated by exuded chemistry.

The fuzz-things do not have experiences the way you have experiences. They have something more distributed: a responsiveness that is collective, a something-it-is-like-to-be-this-colony that exists at the colony level rather than the individual level. A single fuzz-thing, isolated, simply diminishes. Together they are doing something that uses what each one provides without any one of them understanding the shape of the whole. They have been doing this for longer than any individual of any species in this place has lived. The particular arrangement of this colony, on this frond, has a character — a texture to its collective chemistry — that is different from the colony on the next frond. The arthropods know this. They have preferences.

---

The large creature.

It is large enough that the stone records its passage, large enough that the frond-roots, which extend beneath the sulfate tessellations in a network too extensive to map from above, have grown around its habitual path rather than through it, acknowledging the regular disruption the way a river acknowledges a stone. Its path is old. Older than the stone tool. Older, possibly, than the structure.

It is — here is the difficulty — herbivorous and enormous, and it does not eat the plants of the canopy, which would be catastrophic, which would end this place. It eats from below. The frond-roots extend downward into the spire or plateau on which this platform sits, and the roots of the largest, oldest ferns reach the depth where the creature moves. It grazes the root-tips, which the ferns regenerate, which the creature depends on regenerating, and the path it walks is determined by the root-map below, which is determined in part by the path it walks above, because the roots grow toward the disturbance of its passage as readily as they grow toward whatever chemical gradient they navigate in the dark rock.

The creature's face is broad and forward-facing, which implies a history of needing to see what is ahead, which implies a history of predators, which implies a time when this place was not as settled as it is now. The eyes are large and adapted for the perpetual pewter dimness. Its skin is pale, almost colorless, with a faint iridescence in the wavelengths the diffuse light favors. It has four limbs and uses all four on the stone, though the forelimbs are longer than the hind and their distal portions show an articulation suggesting they were once used for other things.

It does not stop for you. It is not afraid and not incurious — it has already assessed you in its pass, with senses that are not primarily visual, and has filed you in a category: new thing, large, not-root, warm, not-fern-metabolite. It will pass this way again. It is waiting, in the unhurried way of something with a very long life, to see what category clarifies over time.

It does not have young, currently. It has had young. Its young, when they exist, spend their early lives on the surface of the canopy rather than below it, clinging to the great fronds, eating fuzz-things and wax-sugar until they are too heavy, which takes years. The fronds accommodate this. The arthropods drum more intensely around fronds bearing juveniles — whether protecting them or monitoring them or simply announcing the fact of them to the wider community is unclear.

---

Who built the structure.

The hands that shaped that tool are not in this place now. They may have belonged to something that was here seasonally, something that came up from below — from the lowlands beneath the clouds beneath the rim — on a cycle that the place accommodated and then, at some point, ceased to accommodate. Or the builder is still here in a form that built nothing else and left no other sign. Or the builder is the creature that circles below, and the articulation of those forelimbs is not a remnant but a capacity held in reserve.

The structure itself has a use. The use is this: the bottom of the interior is lined with a compressed material — wax-sugar, arthropod castings, fuzz-thing colonies, the fine mineral dust that precipitates from the air over long time — compressed into a substrate dense enough to support a different chemistry than the open canopy air. Something grows in that substrate that does not grow anywhere else on the plateau. Something very small, very slow, visible only as a faint blue luminescence that appears in the deepest dark of the interior. Whether this something was introduced by the builder or arrived on its own through the opening at the base, whether it is being cultivated or is simply taking advantage of a unique microhabitat, is a question the place declines to answer.

The arthropods do not enter the structure. They have examined it from outside, extensively, and found it outside their jurisdiction.

---

The shuddering ferns and what the crisis is.

They are not in crisis. You interpreted the shuddering as crisis because the arthropods evacuated and the fronds flushed and the whole event had the character of something extreme. But the character was wrong. The arthropods evacuated because the event requires the frond's surface undivided, and the arthropods, who understand this, yield it.

What the shuddering ferns are doing is reproduction.

Not of seeds — there are no seeds. The tendrils, extended and spread and shuddering at their resonant frequency, are releasing spores of the glandular fuzz-things onto the warm air. The fuzz-things, so small, become briefly airborne, caught in the metabolite mist, and the shudder disperses them laterally. They settle on neighboring fronds. They are already settling on you, which you cannot feel, which you will carry away from this place embedded in your clothing's fibers, and which will not survive long below the rim in an air that is wetter or drier or differently temperatured, but which might, if conditions on you happened to be exactly right, find a surface sufficient to begin something very small.

The fern is doing this for the fuzz-things. The fuzz-things cannot do it themselves. The fern does it because the fuzz-things living in its nap produce the metabolite that the arthropods harvest that the fern needs the arthropods to continue producing from their drumming — the vibration, specifically, which the frond's vascular tissue requires at certain frequencies to properly circulate its internal chemistry. The whole arrangement has no center. No one species is doing any other a favor. Every favor is also a need.

The arthropods return after shuddering. They resume their drumming. The fern, replenished of whatever the shuddering cost it, slows its rotation back to the searching pace.

---

There is one more resident.

It is not a species but a process, and whether a process can be a resident is a philosophical question the place does not engage. It is this: the sulfate hexagons are growing. Slowly — a thickness of crystal per decade at the most — but growing, by the same precipitation of minerals from the warm moist air that produced them in the first place. The tessellations are enlarging, barely detectably, in a way that is changing the spacing between them, which is changing the density of the bulbous fern-bodies that root in the gaps, which is changing the canopy's coverage, which is changing the microclimate of the interior, which is changing the chemistry of the air, which is changing the rate of precipitation, which is changing how fast the hexagons grow.

This feedback is the oldest resident. It was here before anything that breathes. It will be here when the large creature's path is a groove too deep to climb out of, when the structure has been entirely absorbed by biological crusts, when the tool's blade has been slowly buried in wax-patina and mineral accumulation until it is itself a feature of the stone.

The ferns will have adapted by then to a slightly different spacing. The arthropods will drum in a slightly different frequency, the fronds having changed their resonance. The fuzz-things will be slightly different fuzz-things. The luminescent something in the structure's dark interior will either have found a way to persist or will have become a signature in the compressed substrate, a layer of chemistry that future things, if there are future things, will precipitate out of and build themselves from without knowing what they are made of.

This is the oldest and most permanent resident: the slow transformation that everything else lives inside of, that has authored the conditions for everything here by being exactly itself for exactly long enough.

---

This is what lives here.

A community of mutual dependencies so thoroughly interlocked that the word *community* is probably insufficient, probably too social, too chosen — what is here is more like an organ, its parts not cooperating but constituting, each one part of what makes the others possible at all. The arthropods with their stippled markings and their percussion. The fuzz-things with their permeable selves and their chemical broadcast. The ferns with their copper velvet and their shuddering dispersal. The pale enormous grazing creature with its articulate forelimbs and its patience. The luminescent unknown in the dark interior of the built thing.

And the built thing itself, which is not alive but is not inert — which is a record, a cavity, a microhabitat, a made thing that the place has incorporated into itself so thoroughly that it is now as much the place's own as the tessellated stone.

Whatever built it: gone, or changed, or waiting.

The frond nearest to you drums. You are right that it is about you. But not in the way you feared. The arthropods are noting that you are there, that you are warm, that you smell of a chemical they have not encountered in the record of their colony's memory, which is long. They are incorporating this information into the record. They are telling the next frond, which tells the next.

By the time you leave — if you leave — they will have finished a sentence that contains you. They will continue to add to it in subsequent drummings for some time, a diminishing thread of information about the warm thing that came and stood and left a smell of elsewhere.

Eventually the sentence will be complete. It will describe: an anomaly. Warm. Large. Briefly here.

Not threatening. Not useful. Not incorporated into any of the great interlocking dependencies.

Simply witnessed. Simply noted.

Simply — and this may be the strangest part — *remembered*, by the drummed record of a canopy high in the warm dim air of a place that has no reason to remember anything and does it anyway, in the only way it knows.
