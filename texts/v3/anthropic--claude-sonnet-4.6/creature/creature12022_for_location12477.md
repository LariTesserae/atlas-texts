## What Lives in the Cistern Plateau

---

Start with what is certain: something tends this place.

The channels are clear. Not clean in the way of abandonment — clean in the way of return, of a hand that comes back at intervals and removes the particulate that would otherwise slow the flow, widen the joint, begin the process of blockage that leads, in years rather than centuries, to a channel becoming first a trickle and then a seam of damp and then a crack in a block from water freezing inside it in winter and then, eventually, ruin. The channels are clear because something made them clear. The loosened flake that chatters in its ill-fitting seat does not become two flakes, does not become a small pile of debris in the channel below. Someone came for it, or will come for it, or has already decided when to come for it.

The thing that tends this place does not live here the way a creature lives in a burrow or a tree. It lives here the way a practice lives in a practitioner: not housed, not sheltered, but *expressed*. The plateau is not its home. The plateau is its activity, extended over space and time until activity and home have become the same thing.

---

What does it look like?

Difficult. Not because it hides, but because the question of appearance is almost beside the point, or becomes beside the point, after you have been here long enough.

But: try.

Something moves with a kind of attentiveness that has no urgency in it. An unhurried precision. If you saw it at a distance — and you might, near the far edge where the pale haze makes shapes uncertain — you might think: a person. The proportions suggest it, or something close to it. Two limbs that function as legs. A vertical posture, adapted to or developed from or simply the result of long practice on level worked stone. A center of gravity that knows where it is at all times, that has never in recent memory misjudged a surface. It moves the way someone moves who has walked the same routes so many times that the routes have entered the body, who does not look at their feet because their feet already know.

But the attentiveness is not directed outward in the way of a person watching for something. It is directed into the surface, the channel, the fitting of stone to stone. Looking without looking. Listening to the water the way you listen to your own heartbeat — not for information, exactly, but for the note of continuity, for the assurance that the thread is still running.

The hands — if they are hands, and there is every reason to think they are — are darker than the stone but similarly weathered. Not rough in the way of damage but in the way of long use: the dryness and the faint scaling that comes from cold and wet in alternation, day after day, season after season. The palms will be calloused in the specific pattern of the tools used here, whatever those are — a pattern of pressure points that a reader of hands might interpret as a record of the work itself, a biography compressed into the meat of the thumb's base. The nails are short, kept short, trimmed or worn against the stone. The knuckles show the pale horizontal marks that cold makes, the first signs of what long years in this weather will do to the joints.

The face is hard to read, and not because it is strange. Because it is still.

---

There is a kind of stillness that is the cessation of movement — held breath, waiting — and a kind of stillness that is the equilibrium of something fully at rest within itself, something that has no reason to be otherwise. The creature of the plateau has the second kind. Not absence of expression but the expression of a face that has lived in one climate of emotion long enough that the face has become that climate, has lost the infrastructure for rapid change. The features are not frozen. They are — settled. The way a stone basin is settled: not unable to receive, but shaped now into a particular form of receiving, and patient with that form.

The eyes, when they are visible, do what eyes do in places of diffuse light: they are wide, fully open, using everything available. The irises are pale, possibly — the color that forms in eyes when brightness is chronic and even, when there are no deep shadows to adapt to and the whole project of vision becomes one of discrimination between similar pale values. They move, these eyes, in the efficient arcs of someone reading a surface for information, but the arc is slow, the information absorbed at a pace that matches the place itself. Nothing here requires a fast response. Everything here requires noticing.

---

The creature of the plateau is singular — this feels true — but the singularity is not loneliness. Or: the concept of loneliness requires a comparison, requires the memory of company against which solitude is measured and found wanting, and it is not clear that such a comparison is available. The place has not had company in whatever constitutes its recent past. There is no evidence of it — no accommodations for additional presences, no second tool laid down, no asymmetry in the work that would suggest two people working together (one holds, one strikes; one measures, one cuts) rather than one. The work has all the evenness of a single practitioner, a single tempo, a single way of deciding how much is enough.

If there was once more than one, the transition happened long ago. The rhythm has closed over the change the way a plateau closes over the older construction beneath it: the earlier form is still there if you dig, but the present surface is seamless.

What the creature has instead of company is — correspondence. The relationship between itself and the work. The way the work answers to attention, the way a clear channel and a functioning gradient are a kind of response, a confirmation: *yes, this continues. Yes, the water still goes where we decided the water should go*. It is not a conversation but it has some of the structure of one. Action and answer. The plateau talks back, in the only language it has, which is the language of continuing to function. And this is enough. This has been enough. The question of whether it should be enough has not been meaningfully available for a very long time.

---

What does it remember?

Not in the sense of recall — of episodes retrieved and reviewed and meaning-made from their sequence. Something more like the memory the body holds: procedural, non-narrative, a knowledge that lives in gesture rather than story. The hands know the gauge of a proper joint. The legs know the grade of a functioning channel. The eye knows the difference between water that is moving correctly and water that is beginning to find a new route, the earliest deviation, the infinitesimal preference for a low point that will, if unaddressed, become a new channel where no channel was planned, a slow vandalism of the design.

This knowledge was given, once, by others. There was a transmission: someone showed, someone watched, someone corrected. The shape of that transmission is still present in the knowledge itself, like the ghost of a teacher's hand in the gesture of a student. But the teacher is gone. The transmission is complete. What was given has been used so many times that its origin is no longer distinguishable from its use, and it is simply, now, what the creature knows.

And before the teacher? Other teachers. And before them: the original decision, made by someone now entirely gone, to bring stone to this height and begin. That decision is the founding myth of the plateau, and it is not remembered as story but as axiom, as the premise from which everything else follows. The decision does not need to be remembered. It is everywhere evident. It is the plateau itself.

---

What does it know about time?

It knows the daily rhythm. The cold that comes, the condensation that forms in the cold, the surfaces that run with water in the half-light before the brightness, the slow drying as the day advances, the return of cold, the return of condensation. This cycle is the basic unit. It has been counted — in whatever the creature uses for counting, which may not be number but may be something more like depth, the way one layer sits on another — many thousands of times. The count does not produce a sum. It produces a texture. A sense of how deep the present moment sits in the accumulated past, without requiring that the depth be measured precisely.

Seasons exist here, subtler than in lower places but present: a period when the condensation is heavier and the basins fill more than they lose, a period when the balance tips the other way and attentiveness to the channels is more critical because there is less margin. A period when the high cold is hard enough that the creature works differently, more carefully around the fittings where water might freeze and split a block, more attention to certain joints. These are known. They are known in the body, in the slight adjustment of expectation as the quality of the light begins to shift weeks before the season itself arrives.

What it does not know is what comes after. Whether this continues indefinitely, whether it is building toward something, whether there was originally a purpose to the construction that the construction has long since achieved or long since abandoned. These questions are not accessible, not because the creature cannot think them but because thinking them leads nowhere the work requires going. The work does not need an answer to these questions. The water runs or it doesn't. The joint holds or it doesn't. The purpose, if it is unknown, does not affect the practice. And the practice is what there is.

---

Does it sleep?

Yes, but not here. Or: not visibly. The plateau shows no evidence of sleeping — no shelter, no depression worn by a body returning to the same place, no windbreak or concavity that would serve as protection from the cold that comes at night. The place does not have this. The place has, instead, a quality of receiving something that is present during what might be called its active hours and absent during the rest — not a full withdrawal but a shift in the density of attention being paid to it. As though someone is elsewhere, attending to something, or to nothing, in the particular way that sleep attends to nothing.

Elsewhere could mean below. There are the older courses of stone, the construction going down as far as it goes up. It is possible that below the worked surface there are spaces — not rooms, not exactly, but voids between layers, places where an older construction was superseded by a newer one and the gap between them was sealed and forgotten, or nearly forgotten. These would be cold, dark, perfectly silent, insulated from the wind. A body could fit in such a space. A body could return to such a space and be held by it, surrounded by stone that has been breathing moisture for as long as the plateau has been here, stone that holds the accumulated darkness of many thousands of nights.

Sleep there would be the sleep of deep time. Not dreaming, perhaps. Or dreaming only in the body's register: the slow recalibration of worn surfaces, the receding of the day's small damages, the return of readiness. Then the cold outside changes quality — becomes the different cold of early diffuse light — and the body knows this through the stone it lies against, through some slow conduction of the shift in temperature, and it is time to rise and tend what needs tending.

---

Here is what it is:

It is the plateau's own maintenance — its function expressed in a form that moves. It is what happens when a place of such precision and such duration produces, or attracts, or requires, a corresponding precision and duration in its keeper. It was not made by the plateau, exactly, and did not make the plateau, exactly — the relationship is older and stranger than causation. They have become each other's condition of existence. The plateau without its keeper begins, slowly, to become a ruin; and something — some version of what tends it — would cease to have content, would have nothing to hold its particular quality of attention, would be like a craftsman without a craft, which is not quite a person anymore.

It is, in some way, the meaning of the plateau. The plateau is its vast and patient body, expressed in mineral and water and the angle of channels. And it, moving slowly in the diffuse white light, hands damp against the seams it checks and the grit it clears — it is what the plateau is doing, in the part of itself that moves.

---

It does not watch you leave.

Not because it has not noticed you — it noticed you the moment you arrived, in the way it notices anything that changes the plateau's inventory of presences. But it has returned to the work before you have finished going, returned not pointedly or dismissively but simply because the work is there and you are, in the context of the work, not information it needs to continue attending to.

The last you see of it, before the pale haze of distance softens it into suggestion: bent over something, near the edge of a basin, doing something with the hands that cannot be read at this distance. The basin holds the sky. The creature holds the basin. The work continues exactly as it would continue if you had never come, which is to say: without drama, without impatience, with a precision tuned to requirements older than anyone now living, the water moving where the water was built to move, the whole plateau breathing in and out in the way it has always breathed.

Patient.

Specific.

Continuing.
