# The Inhabitants of the Condensation Cities

---

Begin with what they are not, because what they are not is easier.

They are not young. No individual among them is young in any sense that maps onto what you mean by the word. The category of *young* may exist somewhere in their biology — there are stages, there are early forms, there are configurations of self that precede other configurations — but youth as a quality, as a texture of being, as an experience of the world with most of it still arriving: this is not something they carry. Even in their earliest forms they enter a world that is older than they are and does not adjust to accommodate them and has no particular opinion about whether they manage. They enter the way water enters new stone: along whatever path exists. If a path exists. If not, they wait with great patience in whatever crevice held them while they formed, and they learn the cold, and eventually there is a path.

They are not fast. Speed, as a survival strategy, was not available here and so was not selected for. The city does not reward speed; the city rewards persistence and the kind of intelligence that operates over spans of time too long to feel like thinking. They have that intelligence. Whether to call it intelligence is, perhaps, a question worth putting aside until you have watched one of them solve something.

They are not social in the way that word usually points — not gregarious, not communicative in any mode that involves the rapid exchange of information between individuals who then do something with it. They are, instead, social the way weather systems are social: vast, entangled, mutually influential across distances, generating collective effects that cannot be predicted from any single component but that are also not mysterious once you understand the dynamics. They affect each other constantly. They are rarely in anything you would recognize as contact.

They are not small.

---

Now begin again from the other direction.

The body of an adult — and *adult* is the wrong word, but it is the available word, so: adult — is organized around a principle that is not the principle vertebrate life is organized around. There is no clear inside and outside. The membrane, or the thing that performs the function of a membrane, is not a boundary so much as a gradient: things pass through it according to rules, and those rules are complex, and what is inside and what is outside changes depending on what those things are and what the organism requires in a given long-slow moment. They drink the city's moisture through surfaces distributed across what you would call the body without a second thought. They exhale, if exhaling is the right word, compounds that become part of the mist, which becomes part of everything else that breathes the mist in, which is everything.

This is not metaphor. The organisms of the Condensation Cities are chemically continuous with the atmosphere they inhabit. There is no sharp line between an individual's body and the air two meters from that body. The concentration of self-stuff decreases across that gradient the way sound decreases across distance — it diminishes, but it does not stop. At what point is a sound no longer the sound? At what point is an organism no longer the organism? These are questions the inhabitants have not resolved and may not need to. They are comfortable with gradients. They are *made* of gradients.

The rigid portions — and there are rigid portions, structures of mineral-or-like-mineral that function as skeleton and as anchor and as the interface between the organism and the city — are not internal. They are distributed. They follow the surfaces that matter: the load-bearing edges, the junctions where city-structure meets rock, the places where water-flow creates a consistent pressure that must be navigated. The rigidity in an adult is a map, in three dimensions, of what that specific individual has learned about the specific part of the city it inhabits. No two are quite the same. They accumulate this structure over time, slowly, adding and occasionally losing, so that a very old individual is a very complex map of a life spent in one place — a record of every long-negotiated relationship with every surface and flow and other-organism in a radius that, even after lifetimes of slow expansion, is not much more than what you could cross in half a day.

They know their territory with a completeness that is not knowledge so much as identity. They are their territory, as much as they are anything. The city-section a mature individual has inhabited long enough becomes, in some meaningful neurological or neurological-equivalent sense, an extension of the individual's self-model. They monitor it. They maintain it — not with tools, not with deliberate effort, but with continuous low-level chemical and structural presence the way a root maintains the soil around it. When the territory changes, they know. They are, in some sense, the change, having been part of the gradient of causation that produced it.

---

The sound.

The enormous sound is theirs, or is produced by them, or passes through them in ways that make the distinction between *theirs* and *produced by* less clear than it sounds.

Each individual produces multiple simultaneous signal-bands. Not sounds, exactly — the word sound implies air-vibration, and these signals use air-vibration as one medium among several, alongside substrate-vibration through the city's structural elements, alongside chemical gradients in the mist, alongside pressure-differentials maintained across the bodies of the organisms themselves, bodies which are, as established, quite large, and can produce significant pressure gradients. The full signal of any individual is impossible to perceive from any single location because it is distributed across multiple physical media, some of which require contact with city-structure to receive, some of which require specific chemical sensitivity you almost certainly don't have, some of which arrive in the substrate under your feet as vibration so slow it does not register as sound.

What you hear — the vast ambient roar — is a partial harmonic artifact of the full signal, the way the glow of a city at night is a partial artifact of all the light being produced in it. It is real, and it is information about what is happening, and it is not the primary signal.

The full signal is not communication, exactly. It is more like continuous broadcast state: *I am here, I am in this configuration, this is what I am currently experiencing, this is what has changed since the last time I broadcast in this frequency.* Other organisms receive this, in the portions they are capable of receiving, and incorporate it into their own state-models of their environment. Because they are all doing this simultaneously and have been doing it for longer than you can comfortably think about, the resulting shared model is — not collective consciousness, not that, but something adjacent to it, something that has the same relationship to collective consciousness that the mist has to rain. It does not cohere into a single perspective. It is not unified. But it is shared, in the way that weather is shared by everything that exists under it.

When something changes — when the rock settles, when a water-channel shifts, when an organism dies or a new structure deposits — the signal-change propagates through the shared model the way a stone's impact propagates through still water. Slowly, in a medium that has its own rules about how disturbance moves. It reaches organisms that never received news of the change directly. They adjust. They adjust the way living systems adjust: not by making a decision, but by becoming slightly different in response to a world that has become slightly different.

Slowly. Always slowly.

---

The question of intelligence resolves if you watch them long enough and does not resolve any faster than that.

They solve problems. Not the way you solve problems — not with tools held in manipulators, not with language-mediated negotiation between parts of a self that each represent different options, not in the compressed time of a moment or an afternoon. They solve problems the way evolution solves problems: by becoming, incrementally, the kind of thing that does not have the problem. The difference is that they do this within a single lifetime, which is long, and they do it deliberately, in the sense that deliberation is occurring — slowly, continuously, in the chemical and structural medium of a body and territory that are, to repeat, not sharply separable.

Present one of them with a novel obstacle in its territory — a structural failure in the city, a new water-flow across a path, a change in the chemistry of a section of mist it has been relying on — and it does not, at first, appear to do anything. This is misleading. What it does first is *attend*, which is to say that its chemical gradient toward the problem shifts, which is to say it moves itself in the direction of more contact with the changed thing, which is to say it begins incorporating the changed thing into its self-model with the full attention of a very old, very slow, very comprehensive intelligence that has been solving problems in this medium for longer than your civilization has existed. What happens next takes years. At the end of those years the problem is not solved so much as *dissolved* — the organism and the environment have mutually adjusted until the thing that was a problem is a thing that is.

You cannot watch this happen. You can watch the before, and if you are somehow still here, you can watch the after, and you can do the math.

---

They reproduce infrequently and in ways that are not separable from the city's own processes.

What grows from a mature individual is not, for a long time, clearly separate from that individual. It starts as a concentrating of self-material at a junction in the rigid structure, a pooling of the gradient, a thickening. This thickening grows. It incorporates mineral from the city's surface, water from the city's flows, chemical compounds from the city's mist. It develops its own gradient — its own diminishing halo of self-stuff in the atmosphere around it. At some point, which no observer has ever clearly identified because there is no clear moment, it becomes something that would, if you moved it, be missing from where it was, and present where you put it. Until that point, it would simply be part of what you moved.

The young — having used the word once, you can use it again — do not receive instruction in any conventional sense. They receive the shared broadcast-state of everything around them, and they learn the way the city learns: by becoming, incrementally, shaped by contact with a world that has been shaped by contact for a very long time. What they inherit is not knowledge but *position*: a location in the city, a gradient that begins with them at its center, a section of the shared model already encoding the particular rock-face and water-flows and structural junctions of the place they are becoming.

---

Whether they know you are here is a question you will not resolve before you leave.

They are detecting you. Of course they are detecting you. You are a chemical anomaly in the mist — your exhaled breath, your skin's evaporation, your warmth which is considerable relative to everything here. You are also a vibrational anomaly: your footsteps on the new rock, your breathing, the small sounds your clothing makes in movement. You have already been incorporated, in some fashion, into the state-models of every individual within broadcast-range, which is most of the city, which means you are currently represented in a vast slow distributed model of what the city is and contains, held in parallel by thousands of large old cold minds that are thinking about you the way they think about everything: without urgency, without agenda, with the complete comprehensive unhurried attention of something that has no reason to rush.

What they make of you cannot be translated. It would be translated into a medium and timescale that are not yours. It is possible that, given a few decades, a response to your presence would become legible — a change in the chemical composition of the mist in your section of the city, a shift in the vibrational patterns of the substrate, some adjustment in the gradient of the broadcast-state that represents the section of city containing you, that would constitute, for them, something like an expression of what you are and what to do about you.

By then you will have been gone for decades.

This does not particularly concern them.

---

What they want — what the accumulated drive of their biology points toward — is something like *continuance*. Not growth, exactly. Not expansion. Not reproduction in the sense of proliferation. Continuance. The persistence of the gradient, the ongoing maintenance of the shared model, the slow deepening of the relationship between organism and city and rock-still-becoming-itself that gets, incrementally, denser and more intricate as time passes. They want what everything wants, which is to keep being what they are, but what they are is so entangled with the place they are that wanting to continue being what they are is indistinguishable from wanting the place to continue.

They are, in this sense, not just inhabitants.

They are not residents who happen to live in a city that would exist without them.

The city is structured the way it is because they have been here. The rock's new channels follow paths that the organisms' chemical presence influenced over ten thousand years. The junctions in the city's structures are where they are because organisms were there, slowly, for long enough that the structures learned to account for them. The mist's chemistry is what it is partly because they have been exhaling into it since before the stone beneath you existed in anything like its current form.

Remove them and the city would, over a long time, become a different city. Remove the city and they would, over a long time, become unable to continue. They are not the city and the city is not them but they have been in contact so long, in such intimacy, at such depth, that what each is has been shaped by what the other is, and the shaping has no detectable beginning.

---

Stand still long enough and you will feel it.

The cold keeps entering. The mist keeps arriving. The sound keeps doing what it does. The large thing above you that you noticed is still moving, slowly, has perhaps moved a meter in the time you have been watching. The rock beneath you makes its settling sound again, low and satisfied.

And something — not words, not signal, not anything you can name — is present in the air around you in a way it wasn't before. A density. An attention that is not aimed at you but is no longer not-aimed at you either. The gradient of something has shifted in your direction.

It is not threatening. It is not welcoming. It is the long slow focus of something very old and very comprehensive beginning the process of understanding what you are, a process that will continue long after you have left, that will arrive at its conclusion in a medium and a timescale that are not yours, and that will incorporate you into what this place knows about itself forever —

— a small strange chemical memory in the mist —

— the vibration your footsteps left in new rock —

— a question that took ten years to fully form, and another thirty to answer, and whose answer is held now in the distributed cold mind of the city like everything else it has learned —

— quietly —

— without urgency —

— for as long as the stone holds.
