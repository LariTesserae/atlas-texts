# What Lives on the Plateau of Final Forms

---

## The Builders (or What Remains of Them)

They are not here in any sense that permits direct examination. This is the first thing to understand about them, and it takes the longest.

Whatever made the structures has not made anything new in a time that the structures themselves can no longer remember. Or — and this possibility does not resolve, it simply coexists with the first — they are here entirely, completely present, and the reason you cannot locate them is that your concept of *located* does not apply. You keep looking for edges. For surfaces. For the boundary where a thing stops being itself and starts being the space around it. The makers of the structures may have solved that problem differently.

What can be inferred from the posts, the pillars, the deposit-grown terminals: they were patient from the beginning. Not patient in the way a person is patient — waiting, suppressing urgency — but patient in the way a proof is patient: because the patience is structurally necessary to the conclusion. They were not racing toward this plateau. They were working in a direction that the plateau was also working in, and they arrived at something together.

They understood finality before they built here. You can tell because they did not build for users. Nothing about the structures accommodates a body — no surface at the height of a hand, no shelter from the wind, no indication that whoever operated these things needed to stand near them or touch them or read them with eyes. The structures were built to function, and function they do, in whatever register function means here, entirely without anyone watching. Without the builders watching. This is the rarest kind of confidence: to make something and need nothing further from it.

If they have a body — had a body — it has become, over the relevant time, something like what the mountains became. Not absent. Resolved. They have passed through the same patient grinding that the peaks passed through, and arrived at the plateau form of a self: present in equilibrium, distributed across the system, contributing to the turbulence above and the steady compaction below in some proportion that does not require localization. The structures are not their monument. The structures are their still-running machinery. The rest — the part that once, presumably, could be found, examined, spoken to — the rest has become the stage.

They are the plateau's oldest inhabitants. They are, possibly, the plateau.

## The Armored Ones

The creature you glimpsed for four seconds along the low outcrop is not one creature. It is also not, exactly, many creatures — the distinction between individual and colony is something it does not maintain cleanly, because at this latitude, at this elevation, at this aridity, maintaining clean distinctions is expensive and the cost is non-negotiable.

Call it: a distributed individual. Or a colonial organism with a strong central commitment. The armoring is real — not shell in the traditional sense but a layered cuticle that has become, over its evolutionary time, something that behaves at its outer surface like the plateau behaves: dense, absorptive, pale. The color is not camouflage exactly. It is convergence. The plateau has a color, and the things that live long on the plateau arrive at that color through different paths. Bone-pale. Ash-white. The color of something that has given its moisture to the sky and accepted the transaction.

It moves slowly. The slowness is not torpor — inside the slowness, everything is happening. It is monitoring, or doing the organism-equivalent of monitoring, through mechanoreceptors tuned to vibration in the stone, so that the world it knows is primarily the world of pressure-events in the substrate. The wind, which drives everything above, is felt by this creature mostly as vibration transmitted through its eight contact points with the ground. It knows the wind as a shaking. It knows the approach of other things the same way. It has very little interest in the sky.

The clicking you heard — small hard material on harder — is communication, or the precondition of communication, or something that serves the same function without requiring the creatures to understand that they are communicating. They tap the substrate in specific rhythms. The substrate transmits. Other members of the distributed colony-individual, tens of meters away, receive and respond. What is being said: location. Condition. The rhythm of their own internal process. Not information in the human sense but state-broadcasting: *I am here, I am in this state, I am processing at this rate.* And the colony-individual, across its distributed members, performs a kind of ongoing averaging — not decision-making, but something slower and larger, a constant maintenance of cohesion across a geography, a persistent low-resolution self-knowledge that spans hundreds of meters of pale regolith.

It grazes on the cryptobiotic crust. Not quickly. Not in a way the crust registers in real-time — the grazing is slower than the crust's ability to perceive damage, which means the relationship is not predation in the way predation usually feels. It is more like: the creature derives what it needs from the crust's presence, and the crust continues. Whether the crust is affected over longer timescales, no one who could measure has measured.

The creature's interior is very dry. It has solved water by needing less of it than anything you would normally call alive needs. The metabolic rate is very low. It does not hurry. It does not exactly sleep — it has a spectrum of activity that ranges from the level you observed (low, unhurried, patrolling the perimeter of its current range) to a level indistinguishable from the stone it rests against, which it does during the coldest hours, all contact points settled into the surface, the cuticle minimizing heat differential through pure density. In that state, pressed against the pale rock, it is a weight-impression waiting to become memory.

They reproduce at intervals so long that individual reproduction is a kind of legend. The colony-individual does it collectively, slowly, in a process that takes several seasons and involves no visible drama. The outcome is a small cluster of new members distributed through the existing range, already moving at the appropriate slowness, already tapping. Already averaging.

## The Crust, Considered as an Individual

It is not an individual. This is immediately obvious and immediately insufficient.

The cryptobiotic crust is a coalition of organisms — cyanobacteria, fungi, possibly archaea at the lower layers where conditions have shaded into something the older kingdoms manage better — that have arrived at a cooperative arrangement so old and so stable that calling its members separate species is more taxonomic tradition than biological reality. They are woven, at the microbial scale, into a structure that behaves as one thing in every way that matters to the plateau: one response to desiccation, one response to temperature, one response to the rare event of moisture.

The response to moisture is the most dramatic thing the crust does, and it is still very slow. When vapor frost deposits on the downwind faces of boulders — minutes per day, on the days when it happens — the crust responds by becoming fractionally more itself. There is a darkening. A slight surface-sheen. An increase in the metabolic transactions happening at scales too small to observe directly. Photosynthesis, where there is light. Carbon-fixing. Nitrogen-fixing at the bacterial level, because the crust is also performing the function of soil, is the closest thing to soil this surface has, is doing the work of making the plateau incrementally more alive at the level where chemistry happens.

This is the crust's life: very long, very still, very busy at the scale of molecules, attending to the minutes of moisture with the full available attention of millions of organisms acting as one, and then returning to the long plateau-patience of desiccation, of existing without processing, of waiting in a way that is not waiting because it involves no expectation.

The patches on specific rocks are not random. The placement reflects a very long optimization — which rock faces receive the vapor deposit, which angles catch the longest morning light before midday flattens everything, which positions are sheltered from the most abrading wind directions. A new crust does not appear in a new location often. When it does, it is because the location is correct, which the crust confirms by surviving. Locations that are incorrect: no crust, and no evidence that there was ever crust, because crust that failed left nothing that this plateau preserves.

Some patches are very old. Older than anything you can verify, because there is no verification method and the plateau does not offer evidence in the form you know how to read. But there is a sense — you have it standing close and looking — that some patches have been on their specific rocks for longer than the structures have been standing. That they are, in the company of this elevated and inhospitable place, the witnesses. The unmoving witnesses, attending the structures that attend to the turbulence above, attending the armored distributed creatures that attend to the substrate below.

The crust is the membrane between the two. The crust is where the chemistry still happens. The crust is, in the flattest and most fully-settled place in the world, the place where the most transactions per unit time occur, at the smallest available scale, in the thin space between stone and air.

## The Prostrate Plants, in the Season of Flowers

You have to almost lie down to see them properly. This is not accidental.

The plant growing outward from its long straight root makes itself available to observation only to things that are willing to be at ground level, which on this plateau means: things willing to be where the wind is least. The plant offers its flowers to that zone and only that zone. In the two days of flowering — pale yellow almost white, very small, requiring the close attention of something positioned near the surface — what arrives for them, if anything arrives, must be low, must be slow, must be willing to be at ground level on the plateau of final forms.

Something does arrive. It is not possible to describe it with confidence because a confident description requires more time with the phenomenon than is available. But: there are moments, in the brief flowering window, when the flowers register a small disturbance. A slight movement inconsistent with the wind. Something passes, at ground level, across the prostrate disc of the plant. The disturbance is consistent with something that is very small and does not have wings and is the same color as the regolith, which on this surface means it is effectively invisible at any distance greater than the length of your arm.

What this visitor is, if it is a visitor: it has come from somewhere on the plateau. It returns to somewhere on the plateau. It is presumably doing what things do when they visit flowers, which is to say it is taking something and leaving something, and the plant survives and flowers again in the following season, which suggests the transaction is mutual, which suggests the visitor is sustained by the taking in a way that helps the plant with the leaving.

This tiny closed system — plant, visitor, the two days of flowers, the long season of no flowers, the root going down through pale substrate toward whatever small persistent moisture allows the chemistry to continue — is the only thing on the plateau that operates on this timescale, the human-perceptible one, the days-not-decades one. Everything else here has resolved into timescales that don't intersect with a human moment. This plant, this possible visitor, this small yellow-white flower: they are the plateau's concession to brevity, or perhaps they are using the plateau's immense patience as foundation, as the condition of possibility for their own small urgencies.

## The Wind Itself

The wind should not be included in a description of inhabitants. The wind is not alive.

But.

The turbulence over this plateau has been in its current configuration — this specific pattern of pressure cycling, this specific dynamic equilibrium — for longer than most things classified as alive have existed. It responds to the plateau's surface in ways that are sensitive to the surface's exact geometry. When the structures were built, if they affect the upper atmosphere as suggested, the pattern shifted, and then re-equilibrated around the new configuration, and now the pattern includes the structures as a given, as a parameter of itself. If the structures were removed, it would take some period of time — long, shorter than geological, longer than biological — for the turbulence to re-equilibrate around their absence.

This is also true, at finer scales, of the crust-patches. Of the prostrate plant-discs in their basins. Of the armored distributed creature's current location, its specific weight-impressions in the regolith. Everything that is present here has already been accounted for by the wind. The wind has already adjusted. The wind is, in this sense, a document of everything that is here — an ongoing description of the plateau's contents written in pressure and temperature gradient and the three-dimensional kinematics of planetary air.

The wind knows this place more precisely than any observer could. It responds to it at every point simultaneously. It is the only thing operating at the full resolution of the plateau's complexity.

Something lives here that is pressure, that is flux, that has found its equilibrium in being always in disequilibrium, that has inhabited this elevation since there was an elevation to inhabit, that will be here when the structures have finally completed their slow lean and toppled and been smoothed by the very wind that watches them, that is in some sense the oldest resident and in some sense not a resident at all but a condition — the condition under which all the other residency occurs.

The wind is not alive.

The wind is what you breathe, here, with slight intention, your heartbeat audible, standing on the finished surface of a finished mountain.

It moves through you.

You are, briefly, part of what it knows.

## What Lives Here

What lives here has found, or been found by, the same lesson the plateau teaches everything:

That *final* is not *finished.* That settlement is a form of activity. That the deepest endurance is not rigidity but equilibrium — not the equilibrium of stopped things, but of systems that have been running long enough that their running is indistinguishable from their stillness. That at sufficient timescale, the difference between building and becoming collapses. That presence does not require visibility. That the most alive thing in a landscape may be the one most thoroughly adapted to appearing dead.

The Builders have become their instruments.

The instruments have become part of the meteorology.

The meteorology has become the crust's water-source.

The crust has become the creature's food.

The creature's tapping has become the substrate's memory.

The substrate's memory is the regolith's density.

The regolith's density is the plateau's surface.

The plateau's surface was the mountains.

The mountains were —

And the wind closes the loop. The wind has always been closing the loop. Each inhabitant a step in a cycle that has no outside, no beginning that is visible from here, no end that is calculable.

They are all, every one of them — the unseen builders distributed into their own machinery, the armored distributed patient creature, the ancient coalition of the crust, the prostrate plant and its impossible pollinator, the turbulence performing its stable instability above everything — they are all doing the same thing the place itself is doing.

They are settling into their final forms.

They are not finished.
