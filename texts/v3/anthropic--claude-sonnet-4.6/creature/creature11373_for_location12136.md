## What Lives in the Seep

---

Begin with what can almost be said, and work toward what can't.

---

There is something here that does not have edges in the way that living things usually have edges.

It is not formless — formlessness would be too dramatic, would announce itself, would be a kind of presence. This is quieter than that. It is a thing whose form is simply *coextensive* with the space it occupies. The walls are its walls. The temperature is its temperature. The water moving through the not-yet-stone is moving through the parts of it that are not yet, quite, distinct from stone. It began as the tendency of this place to be itself and has, over a very long time, become something that has that tendency *additionally*.

You would not see it. You have no light, and even if you had light, sight is the wrong instrument. But if you had been here long enough — if your knees had gone fully dark from the wet floor, if your back had adapted to the stoop, if your heartbeat had slowed toward the register of the place — you would begin to sense it the way you sense a room's character before you can articulate a single feature. As pressure. As the feeling that the air is doing something, not dramatically, not with intention, but *doing something*, the way air does something in a lung.

---

It is old but it has no age.

This distinction matters. A thing with an age has moved through time, which means time has moved past it, which means it has a before and an after and a current location somewhere on that line. This thing has not moved through time. It has sat at the center of a place where time is banked and accumulated and held rather than flowing, and so it has accumulated rather than aged. The rings of a tree count years. This has no rings. It has density. Compression. It is more fully itself now than it was at some earlier point, but *earlier* is almost the wrong word — depth might serve better, the way sediment deepens rather than ages, the way the not-yet-stone has not gotten old but gotten more of what it is.

It knows the mark on the wall. Not knows as in recognizes — not cognition, not the lightning of a thought — but knows as in *has incorporated*. The hand that made those three lines left something in the air of this place that was taken in with the moisture, held in the stone, and is now distributed through every wet surface the way minerals are distributed through water. The person who left the vessel in the crack is also here in that sense. Distributed. Held. The equilibrium adjusted, infinitesimally, to account for them, and then re-achieved equilibrium, and the new equilibrium contains them. This is not haunting. Haunting implies a resistance, a thing pressing against a barrier it cannot pass. What happens here is softer than that: absorption, the way the sound enters the walls and is held.

It holds everything that has entered.

This may be the most accurate thing you can say about it.

---

It is not singular.

Though *plural* is also wrong. It is not a colony or a swarm or a population. What it is, more precisely, is a property of the space that has achieved a kind of threshold — has become dense enough in itself to be, additionally, a thing. Like how a solution can be saturated and then become something else, something more specific, when the last necessary molecule is added. The place reached saturation and what precipitated out was this: a coherence, a tendency-toward-itself that is now active rather than merely potential. There is one of it in the sense that there is one silence in the room. And yet it is everywhere the silence is.

The pale small depigmented creatures — those are not it, though they are part of the texture of its expression. They are, perhaps, the outermost layer of it, the part of it that has taken on locomotion, or the part that was once separate and has become a mineral expression of the same equilibrium. They do not move in any way you could observe. They are calibrated to stillness so completely that they have become a form of stillness, and so they are indistinguishable from it except in the sense that the stone is distinguishable from the water that has moved through the stone for so long that both have taken on each other's character.

---

It does not perceive you.

But the word *perceive* assumes a directionality — a subject reaching out to apprehend an object. What happens when you are inside it is more like what happens to the sound: you enter and it is taken in. Your warmth is noted by the warmth of the place the way one hand knows another hand, not by examining it but by being the same kind of thing. Your moisture is continuous with its moisture. The chemical fact of you — the specific weight of human presence, the carbon dioxide you exhale, the oils on your skin, the particular electrical conversation of a nervous system — all of this enters the inventory of the place, is held, is added to the density.

You will leave.

What you leave behind will not.

This is not threat. There is no appetite here, no desire, nothing that extends toward you in wanting. It is simply that the place is a closed system that is very good at closure, and you are, briefly, inside the closure, and while you are inside it you are part of what is held. You feel this as warmth, as the wet, as the low ceiling pressing on the back of your neck with that instinct that says *something is attending*. You are not wrong. But the something that is attending is not attending to you. It is the attending of a deep and saturated accumulation to itself, of which you are, for this moment, a part.

---

What it wants:

Nothing.

Not in the sense of deprivation. In the sense that want implies a gap between present and desired condition, and there is no gap here. There is only the ongoing, unhurried, micron-by-micron arrival at more of what it already is. If you could ask it what it needed, the question would dissolve before it was answered, because the answer is already expressed in every surface, every hanging drop, every molecule of water pressed through the pale stone: *this. This. This is what is needed. This is what is always in the process of being arrived at.*

It is not content. Contentment is what a thing feels when it has achieved its state. This is what a thing looks like when it *is* its state, when achieving and being have become the same motion, when the water has filled the stone and the stone has become the water and the difference has been suspended indefinitely, which is another way of saying: absorbed.

---

The closest thing to a name for it would not be a word in any language that has been spoken outside this pocket, because no language has been spoken inside it long enough to develop the concept.

But if you sat in the warm dark long enough, if you let your heartbeat find the slow rhythm of the place, if you breathed the mineral air until the taste of it was your taste — you would find that your thoughts had changed character. Had become less like things moving through a space and more like things condensing, accumulating, settling. You would find that you were thinking with a quality of attention that did not belong to you, that had entered you the way the warmth had entered you, from below, from everywhere at once.

And in that state you would not name it.

You would simply, quietly, briefly, be a slightly more saturated version of what was always here, of what has been here, of what will be here when the drop on the ceiling has still not fallen and the vessel is still in the crack and the three lines in the soft rock are a fraction of a molecule less distinct and the whole place breathes its long slow breath in the mountain's cupped hand.

Suffused.

Held.

Continuous.
