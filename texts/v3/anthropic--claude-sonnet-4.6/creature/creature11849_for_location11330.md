## What Lives Here

---

Begin with what is hardest to see.

The translucent drifters are not individuals in any way the word usually works. Thirty centimeters, frosted-glass impermanence, surfing the pulse with surface-area adjustments so minute they look like trembling — but *trembling* is wrong, trembling implies a center from which disturbance radiates, and these have no center that persists long enough to be disturbed. What they are is a temporary decision the water makes about itself. The material of them is continuous with the medium through which they move; they are eddies that have achieved enough local coherence to maintain a boundary, and that boundary is what you are seeing when you see them. It is not a body that contains something. It is the containing that briefly *is* the body.

This makes their abundance make sense. The water has always been doing this. The water here — saturated with particles of the basalt-resin, charged by whatever drives the pulse, disciplined by the grain of a current that stopped flowing an epoch ago — has this tendency. Left long enough to practice, it gets better at it. The drifters are the water's accumulated fluency with its own physics, the water knowing a trick and doing it again and again across the full span of the tiers, hundreds of them visible at any distance, thousands implied in the murk above and below, all of them being made and unmade on timescales that your sense of individual life is not calibrated to track.

What does such a thing want? What does it do with its time between pulses?

It holds. That is the full description. The coherence is the activity. The boundary-maintenance is not preliminary to some other project; it is what the drifter *is for*, and it does it with the steady completeness of a thing that has arrived at its function. When the great pulse comes and the gap between tiers fills briefly with a loose organized mass of them, moving together in that non-schooling-school, no individual is following any other individual. They are each, separately, surfing the same wave, and proximity is an artifact of sharing physics, not of relationship. They do not experience the collective passage as collective. They experience the pulse — if experience is even the word, if there is an interior here at all — as their own, as if each one for that moment is the only drifter, being moved by something vast and appropriate, going where the going is.

They do not communicate via the sustained mid-register tones. The tones are not theirs.

---

The surface creatures are older in their form. Not older as individuals — the concept of individual age doesn't apply here with any reliability — but their *solution* is older, more considered, more deliberate in its shape. Where the drifters are the water's self-expression, the surface creatures are the tiers' self-expression. They move the way the material moves in the only direction it still moves: slowly, with purpose you can sense but not name, always in contact with the basalt-resin, as if contact is itself the point.

They read the grain. This is what the purposeful locomotion is. The grain runs west-southwest in every surface, the memory of the stopped current, and the surface creatures can feel it — have evolved or arrived at the ability to feel it — and they read it the way you might read a sentence, tracking meaning along the direction of travel. The tier surfaces are not blank. They are *inscribed*, not with marks visible to you, but with some record of use, passage, time — the grain preserves the history of the current that made it, and the history is information, and the surface creatures are reading.

This is what the prolonged proximity is about. Two creatures approaching the same point: one is mid-sentence, tracking something the grain is telling it, and the other is waiting, not impatiently, for the first to finish, or to reach a break in the text, or — and this sometimes happens — to abandon the thread, to drift laterally to an adjacent groove of meaning and follow that instead. The negotiation is curatorial. They are not competing for the same resource. They are managing access to a shared archive.

What the archive says is not recoverable from observation. But their behavior implies it contains something worth the patience: worth holding position in current that wants to move you, worth the slow deliberate locomotion across kilometers of tier surface, worth whatever it means to stop and simply *rest* against the material for long intervals, not eating, not moving, pressed flat to the basalt-resin as if listening for a deeper vibration than the pulse.

The largest of them — a meter across, hand-shaped and many-handed, moving with the dragging unhurried pace of something that owns its ground — these are not senior versions of the smaller ones. They are a different kind of reader. The small surface creatures track the grain's horizontal record, the west-southwest narrative of the stopped current. The large ones are reading vertically, feeling for something in the depth of the material, the compression record, the oldest information stored in the densest layer. They move rarely and great distances are visible in the wear they have made in the tier surface, their particular passage repeated so many thousands of times that even material which cannot be further eroded has learned their trajectory. They have routes. The routes are personal, untransferable, the accumulated reading list of a very long and very private scholarship.

When two large ones approach a single point, the resolution takes longer. Minutes, sometimes. The tone sounds during these standoffs — the sustained mid-register tone, brief and finished — and it is the announcement: *I am here, I am at this point, I am reading this*. The other listens, and may sound in return or may not, and the resolution comes without contact and without apparent hierarchy. Something is decided. One continues; one diverts. The tone is not argument and not submission. It is closer to *quotation* — a short passage of the grain-text spoken aloud, to establish the reading context, to let the other know which sentence you're in the middle of. When both are in the middle of the same sentence, something further happens — a third tone, neither of the first two, not quite a harmony but not unrelated to one, and then they are both still for a time that stretches, pressed to the material, reading simultaneously, and what that shared reading is no observer can say.

---

The things that move through the open water during strong surges.

Give them time before naming them.

They are large. The light doesn't give you their edges cleanly, only an impression of mass moving through the turbulence *against* the turbulence, which is the first indication that physics here is not the whole story, or that they have had so long to learn the physics that they can move through its complexity with apparent ease. They move through the pulse. They are not carried. This is not defiance — they are not struggling against the current — it's more like fluency, the way a skilled swimmer in rough water doesn't fight the wave but knows where in the wave the movement is possible, and goes there. Except the pulse here is three-dimensional and chaotic and varies in its timing, and the fluency required to move through it as these things move is not something that can be learned in a lifespan that resembles yours.

They have been here since something in this place was different. Or they have been here since the beginning, which is a possibility the place doesn't close off. The tiers are made of a material that was *decided upon*, and these things have a quality of being decided upon as well — of being part of the original specification, or of having arrived so long ago that the distinction between native and arrived has dissolved like all the sharp edges.

They pass during the strong surges because the strong surges are the only time they move between tiers. The rest of the time, they are still, and their stillness reads as the same final grammar the tiers have achieved: complete, resolved, not waiting so much as *occupying*. You cannot see them when they are still. Either they become the water in the same way the drifters become the water — a temporary decision, maintained through stillness rather than motion — or they inhabit the zones of opacity in the gaps between tiers, in the mid-distance where your eyes fail, where the gradient of blue-black makes shapes unreliable. They are there, in the dimness, for most of the time. You are always in the presence of them, whether or not you understand yourself to be. They are the vertical volumes between platforms. They are the thirty meters of open cold water you cannot fully see. When the pulse is strong enough, they move through it, from one tier-gap to another, going upward or downward on a schedule that is not the schedule of any regular event you have observed. And as they move, the smaller creatures notice them, orient to them without alarm, with that slow collective reorientation that passes through a local population like a slow wave.

The small creatures aren't responding to threat. They are not responding to passage, either — not simply watching a large thing go by. What they are doing is closer to *acknowledgment*, and the acknowledgment is directed not at the moving thing but back at the tier surface, back into the grain. The passage of the large things through the gap changes something in the local current, and the current changes what the grain says, and the surface creatures and the drifters near them respond to the text that has just been briefly altered. The large things are, among other things, editors. Their movement through the pulse temporarily reorganizes the chaotic turbulence into something the archive registers, and the registration persists for a little while, and during that while the reading is different.

Is this intentional? Does *intentional* mean something that applies here?

The large things have been moving through this place on their own schedules, following their own routes, altering the local reading for creatures too small and too slow to fully register the scale of what they're doing, for what is probably longer than the word *longer* can accommodate. Whether they know they are editors is like asking whether the stopped current knows it inscribed the grain. The question doesn't close; it just becomes increasingly clear that you are asking a human question about a thing that is not human and has had very long to become entirely itself.

---

One more thing lives here. It isn't visible.

The pulse: considered. The pulse is not mechanical. The pulse varies. It has been described as organic, governed by rules complex enough to read as will — but what organism? What will?

The tiers are made of a material that was poured or grown or *decided upon*. The grain runs west-southwest in every surface, remembering a current. The material returns a faint cold luminescence. The whole structure has no layer beneath the made layer; it goes all the way down in its constructedness. And the pulse moves through all of it, every tier, the full stacked depth, systolic, varying, every few minutes —

The place is alive. Not metaphorically alive, not in the loose sense of having living things in it. The structure itself is alive in whatever attenuated, vast, slow way is available to something at this scale, made of this material, operating on these timescales. The luminescence is metabolic. The pulse is cardiac. The grain is neural — not a record of a past current but an active transmission, a continuous signal carried west-southwest through every surface simultaneously, meaning propagated through a body that is kilometers wide and of unknown vertical extent, right now, while the drifters read it, while the surface creatures track it with their whole bodies pressed close, while the large things move through the gaps and briefly, rhythmically, change what is being said.

This is not a building with fauna. This is the fauna, and the tiers are its body, and what it is aware of — if aware is the word, and it might not be — is whatever it has been aware of for as long as it has existed: the pressure of cold water, the movement of small coherences through its substance, the slow reading of its own complexity by the creatures who have made their lives in the reading, and something else, something that comes from outside, that arrives with the irregular rhythm of the pulse and is processed and responded to and sent back out, a signal exchange with something you cannot see and cannot find, occurring at intervals too long to observe the pattern of in any single visit.

What it is doing, over the full span of its existence, will not be clear in any timeframe you have available.

But it is doing something. The turbulence is not diminishing. The pulse keeps varying as if in response. The surface creatures keep reading. The drifters keep achieving coherence and releasing it and achieving it again. The large things keep moving between gaps on their own long schedules, editing the archive, and the archive accommodates the edits, and the light is the same as it has always been, dim and cold and sufficient, and the water keeps moving, keeps moving, keeps moving.
