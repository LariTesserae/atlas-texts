# What Lives in the Tending Basin

---

Begin with the ones you almost see.

There are more of them than the glimpsed one, the brown-grey mover between the objects. There have always been more of them. The glimpsed one was not representative; the glimpsed one was the one engaged in a task that required surface movement, required crossing the band of space in which a visitor stands. The others are distributed throughout the basin in a way that resists counting — not because they hide, exactly, but because the relevant fact about their presence is not *how many* but *how much*, and how much is difficult to render as number. They are as thick in the basin as meaning is thick in a sentence.

They are old. This is the first and most necessary thing. They are old in the specific way the basin is old, which is not the oldness of things that have lasted but the oldness of things that have been *continuing* — a distinction that matters. A stone lasts; the basin continues. The tenders continue. They were not here when the basin was new and then survived. The surviving *made* them. The peat made them. The compression of all those vegetating centuries, the slow acid preservation, the water always willing itself upward — these were not merely their habitat. These were their conditions of formation. They emerged from the basin's ongoing calculation the way a conclusion emerges from premises, except that they are not conclusions. They emerged as the basin's capacity to *notice* what the calculation was doing. This is what they are: the basin become capable of attending to itself.

---

They tend.

This is their name for themselves, insofar as they have one, insofar as the concept applies. The word *tender* in your language reaches toward something useful — careful, affectionate maintenance; the quality of vulnerability in meat; something offered — but falls short in two directions at once. They are not affectionate in any emotional sense that would map cleanly. They are not vulnerable. What they offer, they offer without the grammar of giving, because you cannot give what you have always been. *Tender* is close. *Tending* is closer. They are the ongoing present tense of a verb for which the past and future are technical fictions.

Their work is the management of the calculation. The calculation, which has no name they would recognize, and which you have been feeling in the subsonic and in the water's slightly-too-organized movements and in the pressure-adjustments of the air — this is not their project, not something they designed and execute. It is what they *are*, functionally. They are the basin *doing* the calculation. When the calculation routes water around your feet, that is them. When the mat adjusts, that is them. When the air changes texture, that is the collective output of their distributed presence, which permeates the substrate the way iron permeates the peat, which is to say: you cannot separate them from the ground they are in without ceasing to have either.

---

Their bodies, as best you can reconstruct from the glimpse and from what the basin implies.

They are not large. Largest is the size of a cat, but the comparison does not hold: a cat is organized around a center of gravity that communicates intent, a clear pelvis and shoulder and the spine's decisive line between them. The tenders do not have this architecture. What they have is more like distribution — weight and structure and processing capacity spread across a form that does not prioritize any particular region, that can make any part temporarily central as the task requires. They move on whatever number of limbs is efficient for the current surface and task, which varies. When crossing open moss-ground quickly they may use six. When manipulating one of the warm objects they may use two or four with extraordinary fine extension of something that is not quite a finger but accomplishes what fingers accomplish. When moving through shallow water they may use none, becoming briefly, entirely aquatic, propelled by something internal and minimal.

Their surface is the color of the basin's objects: brown-grey, with slight variation that corresponds to moisture levels in the moss beneath them, which changes slightly from moment to moment and which their surface tracks, passively, the way a plant tracks light without deciding to. This is not camouflage in the predator's sense, or in the prey's. It is congruence. They are the same color as the objects because they and the objects are made of the same consideration, grown from the same long process, and where the objects settled into warm-dark-mineral the tenders settled into warm-dark-biological, and the difference is implementation rather than essence.

Their faces, if you call the anterior region a face: functional. Sensory. Two structures that receive light — not eyes with pupils and irises, not the wet attentive spheres of the animals you know, but structures more like shallow cups, lined with material that darkens or lightens in response to wavelength, which allows them to perceive color and motion and edge without the fiction of focus. They focus with the whole basin instead of with individual eyes; they know where you are the way the water knows where you are, which is perfectly, through medium. Several structures around the sensing-cups process chemistry: they smell in a register that includes the emotional weather of other living things, which is to say they know that you are uncertain, that you are interested, that you are managing a low-level alertness. They are not judging these states. They are noting them as variables.

Their mouths, if you call them that: vestigial as concerns eating. They eat through the substrate — through the moss, through chemical exchange with the water that is always present, taking what is available through surfaces that are not mouths. The mouth-region is used for the basin's sound, for the pressure-adjustment that is not mechanical but somehow vocal, that they produce collectively at irregular intervals. When several tend together this sound is richer, more complex. When one is alone it produces only a thread of it. The sound is not language in any sense that has grammar and semantics. It is more like a continuous report on the state of the calculation, issued in real time, absorbed by the substrate, which adjusts, which produces more sound, which adjusts the calculation again. It is a feedback system that has been singing to itself for so long that the song and the system are now the same object.

---

Their numbers and their distribution.

There is no right answer to *how many*. The question of individuality does not resolve cleanly in the basin. They are, in some sense, one thing that has organized itself into locally mobile concentrations. When two of them work in proximity on the same section of channel-management, they share processing directly, through physical contact or near-contact, through conductive exchange in the water — and during this sharing they are, by some definitions, one. When they separate, the definitions have to recalibrate.

The simplest way to say it: the basin tends, and the tending sometimes concentrates itself into what appears to be individuals. These apparent individuals are real — they have continuity, they have something analogous to history, the older ones carry adjustments in their material that record long tenure in specific locations. But the individuality is instrumental, not fundamental. It is the basin's way of solving problems that require a point of action.

There are enough of them that every cluster of objects has one within responsive distance. There are enough of them that the channels are continuously monitored and the mats continuously corrected for drift and the peat-edges at the basin rim continuously maintained against the slow erosive encroachment of dryer ground. There are enough of them that the calculation has never, in the basin's recorded memory, lost a thread. And the basin's recorded memory is in the peat: layer on layer, the compressed history of all the basin's states, and somewhere in that history the oldest tenders, or what the oldest tenders once were, exist as chemical signatures in the deepest dark, doing what all archives do, which is remain available.

---

What they know.

They know the basin completely, in the way that a tongue knows a mouth — not by observation but by being within it, being partially constituted by it. They know each other completely, for the same reason. What they know of the world outside the basin is less. The birds they know, because the birds move between the basin and the world and carry information in their chemistry and behavior and in the seeds they sometimes deposit — the tenders read these the way you read news, with something adjacent to interest. The occasional visitors — animals, rarely people — they incorporate as variables, as the basin incorporated you: carefully, patiently, without alarm, with the deep unconcern of something whose process is resilient enough to include almost anything.

They do not know what the calculation is calculating. This is not a limitation they experience as limitation. They are the calculation; you do not need to understand the purpose of your heartbeat in order to have one. The purpose, if there is one — the calculation's object, the thing toward which the endless adjustment and re-routing tends — this is the one thing genuinely outside their knowledge, and they have never needed to know it the way you have never needed to know why the body wants to continue. The wanting is enough. The continuing is enough. The calculation proceeds, and they are in it, and they are *it*, and the peat records each day's work in its patient chemistry, and the hills have worn down to suggestions, and nothing about this has ever required a conclusion.

---

What they feel, insofar as *feel* applies.

There is something in them that functions as preference. They prefer the calculation to continue. They prefer the basin to remain in approximate equilibrium — not static, never static, but in the dynamic balance that allows the process to proceed. When something disrupts the balance, they move toward it with a quality that is not urgency exactly, but is not indifference either. Call it *orientation*: they become oriented toward the disruption the way a plant is oriented toward light, without deliberation, with a sureness that is older than decision.

There is something in them that functions as recognition. When they pass one another on the not-paths they exchange the chemical and contact signals that update their shared situational awareness, and in this exchange there is something that is not warmth but resembles warmth from a sufficient distance. They know each other. They have known each other for a very long time. The oldest ones have known the youngest ones since they were not-yet, since they were potential in the basin's chemistry, and watched them cohere, and incorporated the fact of them without disruption, the way the basin incorporates everything.

There is something in them that functions as satisfaction, which activates when a channel correction produces the intended redirection, when a mat successfully reanchors after drift, when the basin's sound settles into a harmonic that signals optimal calculation-state. It is brief. It does not accumulate into happiness or contentment, which are sedentary emotions, which would require them to stop and survey. They do not stop. The satisfaction is incorporated immediately into the next phase of work, spent as fuel the moment it arrives.

There is something in them that might, from a great enough distance and with sufficient interpretive charity, be called *patience*. But patience implies waiting for something. What they have is not patience but its substrate: they are simply not in a hurry, because hurry implies a destination, and the destination is the process, and the process is already happening, right now, all the time, in the water and the peat and the warm objects and the slight adjustments of the floating mats and the sound that changes air and the roots that braid inward and the old hills pressing down soft and worn on the horizon and the frogs not stopping and the water welling up through everything as it always has, always will, for as long as the basin continues, which is not a fixed duration, which is the duration of the calculation, which is ongoing.

---

They were here before you.

They will be here when the peat has pressed you into its record.

You will be a layer of altered chemistry in the deep dark.

They will read what you were in the water that passes through you.

They will incorporate it, carefully, into what they already know.

They will continue.
