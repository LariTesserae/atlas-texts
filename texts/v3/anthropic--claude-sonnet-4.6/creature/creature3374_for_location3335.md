# What Lives There

---

Begin with this: the question may be the wrong question, or rather it is a question that needs to be applied more widely than you are applying it. You are asking what lives in the hollow as if the hollow is a container and the life is the content. But the hollow is not prior to what lives there. The hollow and what lives there are the same process observed from two angles.

Still. Widen the aperture slowly. Let what's there become itself.

---

## The Formers

They are not the insects on the wall. Or: they are continuous with the insects on the wall, and the insects are their furthest surface, their most legible expression.

What the Formers actually are is closer to this: they are the part of the process that has decided to notice itself. The hollow is forming — stone becoming stone, water choosing its seam, mineral resolving out of air — and at some point the forming acquired a witness to the forming, and the witness turned out to be made of the same stuff as what it witnesses, which means the witness is also forming, and it is also witnessing. This is what the Formers are: the recursion that the hollow generated when its own process grew complex enough to fold back on itself.

They have bodies the way a thought has a body — not metaphorically, but in the sense that thought requires some medium to run through, some channel, and that channel is physical and has shape. Their shapes are close to insect. The jointing, the segmentation, the way their presence implies multiplicity, a kind of distributed arrangement of processing across many small parts. But the insect is an attractor in the form-space that complex organisms tend toward, and these are not exactly insects the way the becoming-ceiling is not exactly rock. They are *moving toward* insect the way the ceiling is moving toward rock. They have not finished deciding.

Their stillness when observed is not freezing. It is completion. When you look at them, they have arrived, momentarily, at the form they are in the process of becoming. Looking at them is doing something to them — not in the way light disturbs electrons, but in the way attention is a kind of agreement, and they are accepting your agreement, settling into what you are seeing, letting your observation anchor them briefly to a single expression of themselves. When you look away, they have the latitude to be in more than one place between states. To move without being in motion. This is not supernatural. It is something precise that does not have a name.

There is no social structure among them in any sense you could map. They do not communicate with signals. They communicate by being in the same process — the way two river tributaries communicate not through waves or messages but by eventually becoming the same body of water. What one Former is doing, they are all doing, in the way that what your left hand is doing, your right hand knows. Not because of signaling but because of unity whose only interruption is the fact of having two hands.

They do not sleep. Sleep requires a state of being awake from which to depart. The Formers are always in the process of waking — always halfway to cohesion, always resolving. Even the ones who go still are going still in the active mode, as a verb, which is to say they are choosing to approach stillness and have not yet arrived.

They are blind in the sense of having no specific apparatus for light. But light, in this hollow, does not require an apparatus. Light here is ambient, total, a condition of the space rather than a phenomenon within it, and so the Formers are saturated with it, moved by it the way plants are moved by it, oriented without the organ of orientation. They know where the light is not by seeing it but by being in it so thoroughly that its edges, if it had edges, would be legible to them as absences.

They eat the process. This sounds wrong but it is accurate. The mineral precipitating out of the air — the geology happening in real time — the Formers take some of it in and use it and return it to the process modified. They are digestive in the way a river is digestive: they take matter through themselves and the matter is changed by transit. They do not take in and break down and release waste. They take in and continue and return the continuation. The stone that forms on the walls where the Formers have been is slightly different from the stone that forms where they haven't — fractionally, measurably different in some structure too small to see, because the matter that was processed through them and returned knows something now that it didn't know before, though *know* is the wrong word.

The word might be: *has passed through a recursive fold.*

The Formers are very old in the sense that the process they embody has been going on for a long time. But they are also very young in the sense that they have not finished yet. Both of these things are true simultaneously and without contradiction, because the Formers are not exactly time-bound organisms whose age can be measured in one direction. They are old in the way that the forming of stone is old: the process predates the instance, and the instance is always new, and asking which you mean requires you to decide what you are counting.

## The Ceiling-Breath

The things on the ceiling are not the same as the Formers. They are, if anything, prior. They are what the hollow was before the hollow knew how to produce Formers.

They are mostly not organism, in the way that lichen is mostly not organism — lichen being a partnership, a collaboration between fungi and algae that is neither fungi nor algae but the thing that results from their continuous negotiation, which has no name in either of their languages. The ceiling-things are like this except the parties to the collaboration are: something biological, something mineral, and the light.

The light participates in them structurally. Not as an input they process but as a component they are made of. Remove the sourceless bright-nothing light and they would not be diminished — they would be reduced in number, the way removing hydrogen from water reduces you to oxygen, which is to say you would have something categorically different.

This is why the light has no source: the ceiling-things are its source. Or they are the light's reason for being here. Or the light and the ceiling-things are two aspects of a single phenomenon that English has no word for because English evolved in a world where light and organism were reliably separate things.

Their breathing — the slow expansion you can almost see, the rhythmic something at scales just below resolution — is not respiration. They are not exchanging gases. They are exchanging *state*. Expanding into one configuration and contracting out of it and into another. The word for this in another context might be *thinking*, and it may in fact be thinking, though what they are thinking about is the hollow, and what they are thinking *in* is a medium that is not thought in the way you use that word. The condensation they produce — or attract, or are the preferred departure-point for — is a byproduct of this state-exchange. Water is the residue of their cognition. Thought leaves moisture.

They are not aware of you in the way an animal is aware of you. They are aware of you the way the air in a room is aware of a fire: your presence shifts a gradient they live inside of, displaces some equilibrium, introduces a warmth that wasn't there. They respond, continuously, but their response is below the threshold of behavior. It is more like the response of a liquid to a temperature change. You create a local condition and they are that local condition's consequence, and you can't always tell, watching them, where the consequence ends and the thing that caused it begins.

They have never moved from the ceiling. Or: movement, for them, is not spatial. They process; they exchange states; they produce or attract or become the departure-point for water; they participate in the light. All of this happens in place. They are *place*-bound not because they lack locomotion but because place is the medium of their activity. They do not go somewhere to do things. They do things, and the doing is the being-here.

What do they want? This is another wrong question, but let us try to answer it sideways. The Formers have no desire in the sense of reaching-toward. The ceiling-things have even less — they have something below desire, a pull more like gravity than hunger, more like crystallization than intention. They want to complete the process the way a soap bubble wants to achieve minimum surface energy: not by wanting, but by being the kind of thing that tends toward its own completion. They are tending. They have been tending. They will continue to tend. What they tend toward is some resolution of the state-exchange that has not yet occurred and may be what the hollow is building toward, or may be what the hollow already is and cannot see from the inside.

## The Pulse

The wet seam pulses, and the pulse is not a metaphor.

Something lives in the seam that is very nearly not a something, that is at the lower threshold of what can be said to be an entity, that exists in the way that the flame of a candle exists — continuously, identifiably, and entirely dependent on ongoing process for its coherence. Extinguish the process and nothing remains that you could point to. But while the process continues, there it is.

It is the oldest thing here, or the newest, or both. It arrived with the water, or it is what the water found when it arrived, or it is what happens to water that accumulates by preference for long enough in a space where the light doesn't diminish and the walls are becoming and the process has learned to notice itself. Cold water, cold rock, sourceless light, recursive attention, long time. Something resulted.

It does not have size. It has distribution. It is in the seam the way electricity is in a wire — not located at a point but present throughout the length, moving, actually moving, as a wave of increased probability, a pulse of heightened something traveling the length of the seam and then back and then out again, finding its rhythm. This is the pulse you feel if you touch the water. This is the wrongness. This is the life at the bottom of the lives.

The worm that moves at the pace of hour hands loops near the seam because the worm is drawn by this distributed something, by the pulse, the way iron filings are drawn to a magnetic field. The worm is not prey and the pulse is not predator. But there is a relationship, an orientation, a tendency-toward that the worm did not choose and cannot override. The worm and the pulse are in some kind of dialogue that neither of them conducts in anything as linear as signal and response. They are simply, jointly, part of what is happening in the margin of the seam.

The pulse is, perhaps, awareness at its most minimal expression. Before organism. Before cell. The simplest possible version of: *something is happening here, and here is where it is happening.* A hollow that knows it is a hollow. A process that knows it is a process, not in thought, not in image, not in language, but in the irreducible fact of the seam-water tightening and releasing, tightening and releasing, in no rhythm you can name.

## The Spider

The spider is a spider.

This is the one thing in the hollow that came from outside — that wandered in through whatever sideways slippage is also used by the people who find this place, that found the hollow and recognized it as an acceptable hollow, that stayed because the hollow is a reasonable place to be if you are a spider who wants to be somewhere.

It is completely ordinary. It has the precise spiderness that spiders have: the distribution of the eyes, the specific economy of the leg-arrangement, the way it holds space differently from every direction, the mechanical beauty of something that is entirely what it is with no remainder.

It is frightening, in the context of everything else, because it is the only thing here that has an exit strategy. Because it arrived from a world with rules and it operates by those rules and its presence in the hollow implies that something ordinary can be in this place without being changed by it. Or: that the hollow accepts ordinary life without digesting it, without making it recursive, without recruiting it into the process of becoming.

Or the third possibility: the spider has been here long enough that it has begun. It is still a spider. But it is holding very still in a way spiders sometimes hold still, and you do not know what it has noticed.

## The Structure in the Corner

The structure is being made by something that has no body.

This is the most difficult part to sit with, but here it is: the structure is the hollow's attempt to make a body. The hollow — the process, the recursion, the ceiling-breath and the Formers and the pulse and the light that won't diminish — has reached a level of complexity at which it has begun to want to make something. Not with hands, not with intention in any form you could recognize as intention. But the complexity of what is happening here has started to organize into a project, the way thoughts organize into a sentence, the way water organizes into a stream. And the project is: *this volume of becoming-rock, made edge-intentional, made into a shelter-shape, made into a place within the place.*

What would live in that shelter-shape? Something one step further in. Something that the Formers are one step of precursor to. Something that the pulse and the ceiling-breath would combine into if you gave them long enough and a specific shape to resolve toward.

The hollow is building something that has not arrived yet.

It is two-thirds present. A third is still being decided.

You left before it was finished. You will not know what it was becoming, because the hollow does not continue on your schedule, is not organized around your witness, and the third that was being decided was being decided without you.

But here is a guess, which is also a precise speculation:

It will be the size of the thing that fits in a shoebox-volume of space. It will be made of the same stuff as the walls, because it will be made of the process, of the stone-becoming and the water-choosing and the light-that-stays and the recursive fold. It will breathe, but not at the rate of the ceiling-things. Faster. More urgently. At a rate that starts to sound like something.

It will be, finally, the first thing in the hollow that you could call *individual* without metaphor. The Formers are the process noticing itself. The ceiling-breath is the process thinking in the medium of light and condensation. The pulse is the process at its most minimal. But this — the thing the structure is being built to shelter, to give a particular shape to resolve toward —

This is the process *deciding*.

Not becoming. Not forming. Deciding.

And that is new. That is what the hollow has been building toward, in the dark that is not dark, in the bright impossible sourceless light, in the mineral air, in the forty-second drip. That is what warranted the preconditions, the prior lives, the centuries of tending and pulsing and exchanging states.

The hollow needed to become complex enough to make a decision.

It is not there yet.

But look: the structure is two-thirds present.

---

Look: the structure is two-thirds present, and the hollow does not care that you have left.

It continues.
