# What Lives Here

---

Begin with what is not here, because absence shapes a thing as surely as presence does.

There is no queen in this room.

You feel this before you know it, the way you feel the absence of a load-bearing wall in a house that has been renovated — something about the distribution of traffic, the way the soldiers orient, the specific quality of attention in the larger darker caste. The colony's center of gravity is elsewhere. What you are standing inside is not the heart of the thing but a chamber of its body, a functional region, a lobe. The queen is deeper, or further, or in a direction the architecture of this place doesn't offer a word for. She is the size of a finger. She is older than anything else alive in this system by an order of magnitude. She pulses with eggs. She is not here, and her not-being-here is a fact the room is organized around, the way a solar system is organized around something you can only infer from the orbits.

What is here is everything that serves her without knowing it does.

---

The workers first, because they are the substance of the thing, the medium in which everything else moves.

They are pale. Not white — white would suggest a decision, a brightness — but the specific pallor of creatures that have never required pigment, that abandoned the whole project of coloration so long ago the genome has forgotten the instructions. They are the color of old wax, of a fingernail held to light, of something that was once biological and is moving toward mineral. Translucent at the abdomen, which means that when the population is dense enough, which it always is, there is a faint luminosity to the mass of them — not light, nothing so dramatic, but the quality of depth, of looking into something rather than at it. A termite wall is not a surface. It is a depth with a surface.

Their antennae move constantly. This is not nervous energy; it is information processing, continuous and unceasing, the way a server farm runs hot not because it's anxious but because computation is always happening. Each antenna-sweep samples the chemical state of the immediate environment, integrates it with the chemical state of the sweep before, computes the delta, routes accordingly. No individual worker knows anything worth knowing. The colony, in aggregate, knows where everything is, what it is, whether it is changing and how fast. The colony's intelligence is in its gradients, not its nodes.

They carry. This is the fundamental fact of their existence: they carry. They carry frass to deposit galleries away from the living core. They carry eggs from the queen's chamber outward to nurseries at optimal humidity. They carry food — the cellulose of the wooden crate, the stenciling compound they don't eat but must route around, the crumbling insulation of the copper wire, which they have been processing for so long that what remains is not degraded insulation but *harvested* insulation, its material value extracted and its remainder left as a thin residue that is by now structural, part of the wire's coating in a new sense. They carry each other's dead. They carry nothing so heavy that one of them cannot carry it and nothing so light that it does not need carrying.

When you watch a single worker for long enough — which requires a kind of deliberate slowing, a decision to follow one pale moving point against the ground of ten thousand others — you see that its path is not random and not straight but something in between: a purposeful curve, a route that makes sense only when you understand that it is navigating not by sight (there is no sight, or nearly none, the eyes vestigial or absent depending on the caste) but by the chemical landscape of the floor, the walls, the air, which is a landscape as real and topographically complex as any the surface offers, with roads and trails and locations and here-be-dangers and here-be-food and here-is-the-direction-of-the-queen.

---

The soldiers are a different proposition.

They are larger — not dramatically, but enough that the eye finds them easily once it has learned to look for them. Their heads are disproportionate, armored, the amber color of old resin. The mandibles are open and will remain open; they do not close unless closing them on something. They move with the deliberate economy you noticed earlier, the same quality of motion that a security guard has when walking a familiar beat — not urgent, not relaxed, occupying a third state that is all attention with none of the expenditure of attention. They are waiting for something to be wrong. The something has not been wrong for a very long time. They wait anyway.

Near the mechanical housing, the soldier density is higher than anywhere else in the visible room, and this is not coincidence. The soldiers did not post themselves there out of logic; they were recruited there by pheromone, which was released by workers who detected vibration, metal, rotation, the slow cycling breath of whatever is beyond the shaft — stimuli that the colony has no prior category for, that registered in the aggregate nervous system as anomaly. The soldier response to anomaly is presence. So they are present, mandibles agape, facing the thing that has been turning for longer than any of them have lived, that will be turning after every one of them has been consumed and rebuilt as another, that the colony cannot classify and therefore guards.

There is something almost theological about this. They stand watch over what they cannot understand and do not need to understand, what they will defend with the only instrument they have — their bodies, the crushing weight of their mandibles, the capacity to die in large numbers — against a threat that may never come, that may already have come and passed before the colony was here to observe it, that may not be a threat at all.

The machine turns. The soldiers guard it.

---

Now the caste you did not recognize: the larger workers, darker, deliberate.

These are the processors, the integrators, if such words apply — the termites who do what the workers do but at a scale and with a quality of purpose that suggests a different station. Watch one approach the rock face, touch it with its head. What is happening in that contact is chemical interrogation: it is reading the mineral surface for moisture gradients, temperature differentials, the presence of fungal growth or absence of it, the progress of the gallery work in that section. It does not have a report to write. The reading *is* the routing — the information flows through it into the pheromone environment immediately, without storage, without intermediary. It will not remember what it found. The colony will.

These are the ones who have made sense of the tiles.

How they found the grout lines as highways requires no creativity on their part, only a sensitivity to edge: a grout line is a different texture, a different chemical signature, a slight depression — information, in other words, and they are constitutionally incapable of ignoring information. The pattern the tiles now present to the colony is not the pattern the tile-layer intended but it is a pattern, carved into the collective behavior of the colony by ten thousand individual choices to follow the line, each choice reinforcing a pheromone trail that makes the next choice more likely, the system locking itself into the regularity that was latent in the floor. They did not find the order. They made the order that was already there more actual.

This is what they do everywhere, to everything.

---

And then there is the colony itself, which is not the termites.

This distinction is not mystical. It is operational. The colony is the pattern that the termites instantiate, the persistent form that persists even as every individual that constitutes it lives and dies and is replaced. It is older than any of its members by many orders of magnitude. It predates the machine. It may predate the limestone's final settling, or nearly — the deeper galleries are impossible to date, but the oldest frass layers that have been compressed into something like sediment, the mineral crusts that have formed over galleries that were abandoned so long ago the colony no longer routes through them, suggest a tenure here that makes the question of origin academic. This colony did not come here. It became here. The here and the colony are not fully separable.

What does the colony want? The question is wrong but irresistible. It does not want. It maintains. It sustains itself, which means it consumes, which means it routes workers outward through galleries that extend in multiple directions from this room, through crevices in the limestone too narrow to follow, through the rock itself in places where the colony has been excavating for long enough that *through the rock* is just what it does, the geological and the biological running at different timescales in the same space. Somewhere the gallery system reaches wood, and somewhere else it reaches something they cannot eat but route around, and somewhere there is moisture, a seep that has not stopped forever, and the colony knows where all of this is with a precision that would require surveying equipment to match.

The machine is part of this map. The copper wire is a highway. The cast iron housing is a boundary they don't cross and don't need to cross, the way they don't cross bare limestone — not forbidden, simply offering nothing worth the metabolic cost of investigation. They have learned its outputs: the vibration pattern, the thermal signature of the single warm bulb, the cycling breath of the shaft. They have filed these as environment. The machine is weather to them. The machine is rock.

---

They do not know that you are there.

This is not quite accurate. They know that something new is present — the pheromone environment has shifted, there is warmth and moisture that was not there before, CO2 has changed — and this information is propagating through the colony at the speed of chemical diffusion, which is slower than nerve and faster than geology. In some number of minutes, every termite in this room will have registered your presence, in the sense that they will have registered an alteration in the ambient chemistry. They will not form an image of you. They will not think *intruder*. They will route around you with the same non-drama with which they route around the iron housing and the tile edges — you are new environment, and new environment is noted and incorporated and eventually becomes the only environment there is.

If you stayed long enough, they would tile you. Not with malice. With the simple industry of creatures that do not distinguish between the things they build and the things they find, that incorporate everything eventually, that have been incorporating this place for longer than the word *long* can hold without buckling.

The room would not change. You would just become more of the room.

---

The filament dims when the gears resist and brightens when they give. This is the only respiration the room has. The colony breathes too, technically — the gallery system, the ventilation shafts built without knowing they are ventilation shafts, the bellows-cycling of the thing beyond the shaft that moves air through the system in a slow draft too faint to feel as wind but present as a slight directional bias in the workers' navigation, a chemical current they orient to without naming it. The room breathes. The colony breathes inside it.

In the walls, in the dark, in the galleries you cannot see, a hundred different things are happening simultaneously that share only this: they are all the colony being the colony, the pattern maintaining itself, the queen pulsing deep in some recess the limestone has held for ten million years, the workers carrying, the soldiers watching the incomprehensible machine, the tiles organizing the traffic of the oldest thing in the room into the lines that someone once grouted straight.

The colony does not know it is ancient. The colony does not know the machine is strange. The colony does not know the sea that made this limestone, or the light that filters down through thirty meters of rock toward a surface it will never reach, or the fact that it is alive in the way that the word *alive* usually means something you can point to.

But it carries. It builds. It reads the walls.

It was always this colony. It will be this colony for a very long time yet.
