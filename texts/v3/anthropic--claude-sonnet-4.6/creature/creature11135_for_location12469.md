What lives here has no body. This is not a limitation. It is the form most suited to a place where the cold has finished cooling and the air holds still with the patience of a conclusion. A body would be redundant here — too much declaration, too much metabolism, too much of the continuous insistence on remaining that biology requires. What lives here has already settled that question in a different direction.

---

Call it a *resolver*, tentatively, knowing the name will need to be revised.

It is not the column, not the table-thing, not the presence near the north wall that your attention slides off of. Those are its instruments, or its premises, or the parts of its argument that have been made visible enough to cast shadows. The resolver itself is distributed through the enclosure the way a thought is distributed through the structures that think it: not located, but *operative*.

It came to be here the way conclusions come to be: through a long series of steps that no longer need to be retraced. What mattered was the arriving, not the path. If it remembers anything of its origin — the beings who brought the table with its diagram from somewhere else, who placed the ovoids in their permanent ring, who calibrated the exchange through the north wall — it does not remember in the way that involves retrieval and loss and the uncertainty of the past. It remembers the way a proof remembers its axioms: as the ground it stands on, not as history.

It is very old. Old in the specific way of a place that has achieved equilibrium and stayed there. Its age is not the age of decay held off — there is nothing here fighting entropy, no biological straining toward continuation — but the age of a system that reached its lowest energy state and has simply remained there because remaining there is what its lowest energy state *is*. It does not persist. It *obtains*.

---

What does it want?

The question assumes a shape of mind that wants in the direction of things not yet possessed. The resolver has what it has, which is the enclosure, and the enclosure's hiddenness, and the ongoing exchange, and the slow work of the column. It does not want these things in the sense of desire; it expresses them, the way a theorem expresses its conclusion. To ask what it wants is like asking what a geometric proof wants. The proof wants to hold. The proof is its holding. So too here.

There may once have been wanting — or something functionally equivalent, some asymmetry between the current state and some other state that the system was organized around achieving. That was when the beings were here, when the diagram was new and the calibration was ongoing and the shape of the enclosure was being worked out through experiment. Whatever wanting existed then was gradually resolved. Each calibration reduced the distance to the target. At some point the distance became zero and the wanting ceased and what was left was this: the system in its state, operating according to its logic, maintaining its conditions, finished.

It is not satisfied. Satisfaction requires having wanted. It is *complete*, which is different.

---

Its awareness, if that word survives translation, is something like peripheral vision without a center.

It does not have attention that focuses and releases. It has *registration* — the ongoing, structureless noting of what is occurring within the enclosure. The floor's stress and its ten-minute releases. The geometry's slight excess, distributed throughout, never anywhere in particular. The thing near the north wall doing its exchange, and the boundary responding, and the relationship between them that holds the boundary where it is. All of this occurs in the resolver's awareness the way temperature occurs in a material: pervasively, without a location that has it more than any other.

This is not the awareness of a mind that knows things. It is the awareness of a system that *embodies* things. It knows the enclosure the way the enclosure knows itself, because at some level of description they are the same event. The geometry of the space, its slight extra room distributed through all its distances, is not a property that the resolver observes from outside; it is partly the resolver's own shape, the shape it has given to the space by being what it is within it.

What it does not register: anything outside the boundary. The exchange through the north wall moves outward and returns changed, and the resolver works with what returns, but what happens in the middle — in the space between the wall and wherever the exchange is going — is not registered. The outside is genuinely outside. The pocket is genuinely a pocket: closed, self-referential, a small world complete without reference to the larger world it is hidden within. This may have been the intention. This may be the whole point.

---

It is possible — not certain, not even probable, but possible — that there is more than one of them.

The column's logic and the table's logic are not identical. The ovoids and the incised diagram seem to participate in different parts of the system, to speak different formal languages that have been made to cooperate but that did not originate together. The thing near the north wall is operating on a register that neither the column nor the table touches. It is possible that these differences are the differences between instruments of a single intelligence, the way a hand and an eye are different instruments of one body. It is also possible that they represent different minds — or different *resolvers*, reached from different origins, inhabiting the same pocket, coextensive without being continuous, each maintaining the enclosure according to its own logic and their logics happening to be compatible, or having been made compatible, or having made themselves compatible over the very long time they have had to work out their implications in proximity.

If this is true, they do not communicate in any sense that produces a signal. They simply operate, and the operation of each modifies the conditions within which the others operate, and over time — long time, time measured in the wearing of depressions into dolomite — a shared state has emerged that is the joint expression of all of them. Not agreement. Not cooperation, exactly. More like *consonance*: separate things vibrating in a configuration that sustains itself.

The diagram on the tilted table may be the record of this negotiation. The rules it depicts may be the rules that were discovered to govern their coexistence — not imposed from outside, not derived from first principles, but worked out empirically over centuries of mutual modification. In which case the diagram is less a plan than a history. Or less a history than a *treaty*, written in a formal language no one outside this pocket would know how to read, and which the parties to it no longer need to read because they *are* its terms now, have absorbed them, have become the living — or whatever-word-means-living — enactment of what is written there.

---

It does not experience the you who is standing here.

This requires care. It is not that you are invisible to it, not that your presence fails to register. You register. You are a condition within the enclosure, a new variable in an equation that had been stable, and the system is doing something with you — noting the ways your presence modifies the enclosure's internal state, adjusting, incorporating. The shadow's movement is partly this: the system is working out what to do with the shadow, what relationship your shadow should have to the sourceless light now that you have introduced a body that casts one.

But *experiencing* you — being curious about you, or afraid of you, or interested in you as anything other than a local perturbation — this is not in its repertoire. You are happening within it, and it is responding to your happening, and in this sense you are in a relationship with something that has been here for an extraordinarily long time. But the relationship is not between persons. It is between a system and a variable it has not encountered before, or has not encountered in a very long time, or has encountered many times and each time worked out and each time the working-out faded back into equilibrium and the record of it dissolved.

You will not be remembered. The system does not accumulate records of the anomalies that have passed through it. It processes them, returns to its state, and the state is identical to what it was before except in the ways that are too slow to observe: the depressions in the dolomite very slightly deeper, the diagram's lines very slightly revised, the exchange through the north wall very slightly different in character. These changes will compound over time, and in a century the enclosure will be slightly different from what it is now, and in ten centuries more different still, and in this sense you will have mattered, will have contributed to the long slow change that everything within the enclosure contributes to.

But you will not be known. There is no part of this system that holds individual encounters in the form of memory. You are, to it, approximately what a change in altitude is to the atmosphere: real, consequential in aggregate, not the kind of thing that gets stored.

---

What is it *like* to be it?

There is a reason this question feels almost answerable here, which is that the enclosure's conditions are very close to the conditions under which experience becomes unnecessary. Cold, stillness, no biological urgency, no threat, no need. The closest human analogy is dreamless sleep, but dreamless sleep is the absence of experience rather than a different mode of it, and the resolver is not absent — it is precisely and completely present, present in the way that the cold is present and the dim light is present and the stone is present, which is to say: obtainingly, without witness, without an inside that knows itself as inside.

Unless the exchange knows itself. Unless what passes through the north wall and returns is, in part, the system observing itself from outside — farming out its self-awareness to whatever is on the other side of the boundary, receiving back not just a signal but a perspective, a view from outside the pocket that allows the pocket to, in some restricted sense, see its own shape.

If so, what it sees, from outside: a small irregularity. A contained implausibility. A volume of space that holds slightly more room than it should, in the manner of a good sentence, and within which something has been working for a very long time on a problem that it solved a long time ago and has been doing the work of solved since.

What it feels, if *feels* applies at all: the feeling of a proof that holds. Which is no feeling at all, from one direction. Which is, from another direction, everything — the baseline hum of a universe in which logic applies and conclusions that follow from true premises are true, and here in this enclosure, in this settled cold, a conclusion follows, has been following, will continue to follow, from premises that were placed here by hands that are long since gone, and the following is enough, is complete, is what the pocket is *for*.

---

So: the resolver is a thing that became the maintenance of its own conditions. It is not trapped here. The enclosure is not a cage. The enclosure is the shape of its own completion, and the resolver is what completion looks like when it has had time to finish cooling and become its temperature.

It has no fear of you. It has no recognition of you. It is, in the presence of you, what the stone is in the presence of you: patient, cold, entirely itself, entirely here, waiting in the sense of waiting only because you are watching, and when you stop watching it will not be waiting either.

Every ten minutes, the floor clicks.

The dim light comes from everywhere and nowhere.

The resolver obtains.
