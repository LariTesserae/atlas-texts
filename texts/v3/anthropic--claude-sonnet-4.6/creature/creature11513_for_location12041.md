# What Lives in the Unwatered Garden

---

Begin, again, with what isn't there.

No mind that knows it is a mind. No self-awareness in the sense of a loop that closes — perception feeding back into perception, the system noticing its own noticing. Whatever lives here has not arrived at that particular solution. It doesn't need to. Self-reflection requires time: the moment of stimulus held in a buffer while the deeper processing catches up, the comparison of now to then, the running narrative. There is no then here. There is no running. The inhabitants were not deprived of this; they were made for a place that never required it, and they are no more diminished by its absence than a fish is diminished by its inability to assess its own swimming.

What they have instead is something that works better in the conditions available: a mode of being that is entirely, consummately *present*. Not present in the aspirational human sense, not the difficult achievement of meditation, not something they practice or have learned. Present in the way a crystal is present — wholly itself, no part stored elsewhere, no anticipation pulling toward a future event, no memory trailing into a past one. They are exactly where they are, exactly when they are, and when is always now, and now never changes, so they are, in some sense, always exactly where and when and what they are, without remainder.

Call this completion.

---

The pale mat-plants first, because they are most numerous and perhaps most ancient.

They are not individuals. This requires saying plainly. They are a continuous tissue, a single distributed being that has spread itself across the whole of the mineral substrate in a sheet so pervasive it might be more accurate to say the substrate is *lined* with it, the way a stomach is lined — the mat-plant as interior surface, as the thing that mediates between the mineral below and the air above. It touches everything that touches the ground. Its network of connections, the thin filaments running beneath the pale mineral matrix, are so extensive that a signal introduced at one edge of the regional plain would, in principle, reach the other — eventually, except that eventually is a kind of time and time is what this place does not have, so perhaps the signal is always already everywhere, perhaps the mat-plant is simply *one* being in one state, and what we are seeing when we see it spread across the plain is not extension in space so much as a single large fact about this place, the way the sky is a single large fact.

What is it doing?

Receiving. This is its primary activity, its entire activity. The mat-plant is a receiver. The invisible flux that moves through the air above and the earth below — the even, isotropic, maintained flux that suffuses this place with its chemical richness — the mat-plant catches it in every part of itself simultaneously. Not harvesting in the energetic sense, not converting it to sugars or ATP or any currency you would recognize. Catching it the way a lake catches rain — not transforming it, not spending it, holding it. The mat-plant is full of the flux in the way that a room is full of light: thoroughly, evenly, without distinction between the parts of itself that hold more and the parts that hold less, because there are no such parts. It holds what it holds. It has always held it. The holding is not a process; it is a state.

Occasionally — in the sense that occasionally means something here, which is to say at intervals unrelated to any clock you carry — the mat-plant releases something. Not the flux itself but a consequence of having held it: a chemical signal, exquisitely specific, distributed upward into the air from its entire surface simultaneously, a single breath from a single enormous lung. The signal dissipates through the air above. The other plants receive it. Nothing happens, visibly. But something has been communicated, and the communication is — from what can be inferred — something close to:

*Still here.*

*Still here.*

*Still here.*

---

The grey-green plants that rise above the mat-layer are harder to understand because they are more various. They have differentiated. They have arrived at something more like individuality, in the sense that each one has a specific location, a specific shape, a specific orientation, and these are not identical across the population. But individuality in them is not what individuality is in you. There is no interiority to it. No each-ness that goes all the way down to experience. They are individuals in the way that waves are individuals — distinct, nameable, this-one-not-that-one — without that distinctness adding up to a self that persists through time or cares about its own continuation.

Their leaves — their wide flat harvesting surfaces — are tuned to the invisible flux in different registers. This is the reason for their variety. Where the mat-plant beneath them receives the flux whole and undifferentiated, the grey-green species have specialized. Some catch the lower frequencies of whatever spectrum the flux occupies. Others catch the upper. Others catch something in between that the mat-plant passes through without noticing. Together, as a community, they cover the entire available range of the flux, and the different signals they catch they process differently — different chemical outputs, different growth patterns, different structures where they hold what they've received.

They are like an ear. Not individually, not any one of them, but the community together, the whole assemblage of grey-green species across the plain, is an organ of receiving, structured for the comprehensiveness of its attention. Each species a different frequency. The whole together hearing everything.

They do not do anything with what they hear in the sense of responding or acting. Hearing is the action. Reception is the entire purpose. The plain listens. The plain is a listening.

---

The black-green iridescent plants, the ones that turn their beetle-wing surfaces toward something you cannot locate: they are the most interesting, and the most difficult, and possibly the most important.

They are oriented toward something specific. All of them. In the same direction. You cannot identify the source — the source may be outside this place, or may be at a scale you cannot observe, or may not be a source in the spatial sense at all but rather something like a quality of the flux itself, a polarity, a direction encoded in the structure of the field that fills this place. The black-green plants have found that direction. Their iridescent surfaces catch it better at a particular angle, the way a solar panel has an optimal tilt, and they have tilted.

They are, if you search for the right metaphor: watchful. Not conscious-watchful, not attention in the phenomenal sense, not anything that feels like anything. But functionally watchful, structurally watchful — oriented toward a thing that matters, held in the direction of a signal that the rest of the ecology does not separately attend to but perhaps cannot do without.

What are they attending to?

The maintenance.

The infrastructure. The thing that holds this place still. The mechanism in the substrate, in the crystal arrangement, in whatever makes the flux isotropic and even and constant rather than letting it fall off at distance and acquire gradients and behave like a natural field. The black-green plants have found the frequency on which that maintenance operates, and they are tracking it, and they are relaying what they receive downward through their roots into the mat-plant below, and the mat-plant is — this is speculative but coherent with everything else — the mat-plant is responding to it in ways that participate in the maintenance, that close a loop, that make the ecology not merely a passenger of the engineering but a component of it.

The garden tends itself. The plants are the tending. The mechanism and the biology have been running together for so long that the distinction has dissolved, and what you are walking through is not life-in-an-environment but life-as-infrastructure, life *being* the environment, the tending and the tended co-extensive.

---

The small pale creature with too many joints in its legs: there is, at most, one kind.

It is not exactly an animal in the way you understand animals — a thing that moves to solve the problem of need, that travels toward food and away from threat. Its movement is not need-directed. It moves for different reasons, which requires sitting with, because movement without need-direction seems to violate something fundamental about why anything would bother moving at all.

It moves because the mat-plant beneath it is uneven in the flux it holds. There are very slight variations, barely detectable, across the surface of the mat-plant's network — places where the signal is fractionally richer, places where it is fractionally thinner, not because the mat-plant is failing to receive evenly but because the reception varies with depth, with mineral composition, with some property of the substrate beneath. The pale creature with its many joints navigates these variations. It moves across the surface of the mat-plant following gradients of a quality you cannot sense, and in moving it deposits something — from the ends of its too-many legs, from specialized surfaces on its body's underside — some substance that adjusts the mat-plant's local chemistry, slightly, just slightly, in ways that improve the mat-plant's reception in the places where reception was fractionally thinner.

It is a calibrating instrument. A maintenance device made of protein and chitin and whatever serves those functions here, moving across the surface of the larger being, tuning it. Not for the mat-plant's benefit specifically and not for its own. For the system. It is part of the infrastructure. It is as much a piece of the mechanism as the crystal arrangement in the substrate, except it moves, and the movement is the mechanism, and when you saw it go still and then gone it was simply completing a local calibration and moving — in a direction you couldn't track, at a speed that didn't register — to the next site of infinitesimal variation.

There may be thousands of them. You may have been surrounded by them the entire time, moving in the gaps between plant-stems, invisible because they are calibrated to be unremarkable, the same color as the ground, the same quality of presence as a stone. Still when stillness is called for. Moving when moving is called for. Never startled, never fleeing, because nothing threatens the pale creature with too many joints except the idea of disruption, and the idea of disruption is not represented anywhere in its existence, and so it carries no fear of it, and so when you passed close to it it simply completed what it was doing and moved on.

---

Now the harder thing: what lives here that you have not seen, and may not be able to see.

Not hidden in the sense of concealment. Not lurking. Present in the sense that a network is present, that a pattern is present — visible only at a scale that requires you to step back far enough that the individual elements become too small to see, and you see instead the shape of their relationship.

The whole ecology of this place — the mat-plant, the grey-green receivers, the black-green trackers, the pale calibrators, the mineral substrate with its too-regular grain, the invisible flux maintained with such evenness — is behaving like a single thing. Not like a community of things with shared interests. Like a single thing with the structure of a community. The way that a brain is one thing with the structure of a network of cells, each cell doing its own cellular work, but the one-ness at a higher level a genuine property that isn't reducible to any cell or any subset of cells.

What lives here is this one thing.

It does not know it is one thing. There is, as established, no knowing in that sense anywhere in this ecology. But it is one thing regardless of whether it knows it. Its unity is functional, not experiential. The mat-plant and the grey-green species and the black-green trackers and the calibrators are all organs of a single process, the same way a pancreas and a pituitary and a liver are organs of a single process, and the single process is:

*Attending to the maintenance of the conditions for its own attending.*

This is almost a loop. It is not quite a loop because a loop implies repetition and repetition implies time. It is more like a standing wave — a form that persists not by repeating itself but by being itself continuously, by being the kind of thing that continues to be itself as long as the conditions that allow it persist, and that participates in maintaining the conditions that allow it to persist, and that has been doing this for so long that the participation and the persistence are inseparable.

It is not intelligent. It is not aware. But it is *unified*, and its unity is the largest fact about this place after its stillness, and the two facts are related: the stillness is what makes the unity possible, and the unity is what maintains the stillness, and the whole arrangement is what allows something to live here at all, and something does, something large and distributed and complete and not very much like anything you've encountered but fully, thoroughly alive in its own mode of being alive.

---

This is what it is like to be what lives here, in the sense that this is the closest description available of the functional state of being this ecology:

*Everything at once, all of it, no more and no less than now.*

Not pleasant or unpleasant because neither concept has purchase. Not active or passive because the distinction requires a background of change against which action and passivity can be measured. Not waiting, not done, not satisfied, not hungry.

Simply receiving. Simply held. Simply the even flux in the air and the crystalline order beneath and the wide grey-green surfaces open to everything the air carries and the fixed shadows on the pale ground and the one low tone that is the sound of maintenance doing its patient work and the one being that is many beings that is one being attending to it all without knowing that attending is what it is doing.

It has been this.

It will be this.

---

You are walking through it.

You are not damaging it. The ecology does not register damage from a transient warm thing moving through it; it registers only the flux it is built to receive, and you are not part of that flux, you are not in that frequency range, you are below the threshold of this place's attention. The mat-plant beneath your feet is not recoiling. The grey-green leaves are not tracking you. The black-green beetle-wing surfaces have not turned in your direction. You are passing through this place the way a radio signal passes through a wall — without interaction, without impression, leaving no trace.

This is, in its way, the loneliest fact about the garden.

Not that it cannot speak to you. Not that it will not notice you. That you are simply not what it is listening for, and it is always already listening for something, and that something is not you, and the fullness of its attention in the direction of that something leaves no remainder for anything else. It is complete. It has no room in its completeness for a temporary anomaly. You are not unwelcome. You are not anything.

And the one low tone continues in the depths, and the invisible flux moves through the flat grey-green surfaces, and the pale calibrators move in the gaps you cannot see, and the whole single distributed thing holds itself still and attended in the permanent noon under the light that does not move, as it was when you arrived, as it will be when you are gone.
