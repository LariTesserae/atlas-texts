## What Lives in the Fen of Breathing Machines

---

Begin with the ones that are oldest, because they are also everywhere, because they are effectively the floor.

Call them **foundational borers**, though the name resists them. They are not boring in the directional sense — they have no direction, or rather they have all directions held simultaneously in a slow equanimity that has abolished preference. They are low, flattened, spread across the fen floor in colonies that cover areas the size of city blocks, their upper surfaces barely distinguishable from the stone they grow on and into and through. The distinction between their lower bodies and the substrate beneath has been, over the span of their lives, abolished. They have grown into the cracks. The cracks have grown into them. The basalt-smooth stone of the fen is threaded through with their structures: tubes of chitin-mineral composite, calcified secretion-ducts, passages through which fluid moves that is not quite the organism's blood and not quite the groundwater and is, in practical terms, both.

Their upper surfaces are dark. Greenstone-grey, basalt-black, in patches a deep iron-red where the rust-chemistry of the place has involved itself with their outer integument. They are wet always. They gleam. They do not move in any way that registers over the course of an hour's watching, but if you looked at the same colony a century apart you would find it had redistributed itself, had extended in some directions and withdrawn from others, had thickened at some points and thinned at others in ways that suggest responsiveness to conditions that shift too slowly for shorter-lived things to track.

They produce the subterranean sound. The breathing. Their metabolic processes drive fluid through the rock-passages they have colonized, and the fluid moves in slow pressure-waves, and the pressure-waves move air before them as they go, and the air moves through the passages, and the passages open in sheltered places under overhangs and at the bases of larger formations, and the sound that emerges has been heard for so long by everything living here that it is neurologically indistinguishable from the sound of one's own body, from heartbeat, from gut-motion — from the general evidence that interior processes are proceeding. Everything in the fen that has a nervous system capable of registering distress registers its absence partly by registering this sound as present. The foundational borers have not, presumably, organized themselves to provide this service. But they have been providing it for so long that the fauna has organized itself around the receiving of it.

They eat: dissolved mineral. Groundwater chemistry. The patient extraction of energy from the slow oxidation of iron compounds, from sulfur gradients, from sources of chemical disequilibrium that a more energetic world would have exhausted long ago but that here are continuously replenished by the water moving through the rock. They are very slow. They have had very long to become very good at being very slow.

At intervals — years apart, perhaps decades — they vent. Something in their processing accumulates to a threshold and is released through the ducting in their upper surfaces in a pulse of heat and mineral-laden gas. The pulse lasts perhaps ninety seconds. It is the warmest thing that happens in the fen. Everything within twenty meters that can move moves away and then, as the heat dissipates, moves back. The surface of the foundational borer colony after a vent event is slightly different: new ducts have opened, old ones have sealed, a reconfiguration has occurred that will take the next several years to complete its implications. Adjustment.

No one has determined whether they are aware of anything. The question may be malformed.

---

The **engine-forms** are what you notice first, which means they are the second thing in importance but the first in presence.

They are large — the largest are six or seven meters across, though height is always subordinated here to lateral spread — and they have the unmistakable gestalt of industrial apparatus. Not metaphorically. Not as convergent evolution producing an analogy to industrial design. They have the gestalt of industrial apparatus because industrial apparatus is one solution to the problem of controlled energy-processing, and they have spent their evolutionary history arriving at that solution from the other direction, from chemistry through complexity through accumulated constraint-selection, and where human engineers arrived at boilers and pressure vessels and valve-controlled ducting through deliberate invention, the engine-forms arrived at the same functional geometry through the long erosion of anything that worked less well.

They are built in chambers. The chambers are read from outside as bulges in the outer integument, which is a material resembling vulcanized rubber over a substrate of calcite and compressed chitin, dark grey, ribbed, capable of absorbing and transmitting mechanical stress without fracture. Inside the chambers: reaction spaces. Spaces in which the chemistry of this world is run at slightly elevated temperature and pressure, and the products of that chemistry are either incorporated into the engine-form's tissues or vented through the stacks, or both, the distinction between nutrition and exhaust not being cleanly separable here. The stacks are rigid extensions — the only vertical structures in the fen that exceed three meters — rising from the upper surfaces of the engine-forms, tapering slightly, opening at their tops in apertures that are clearly apertures, that have clearly been shaped for aperture-function, that have sphincter-analogs of thickened tissue around their openings and produce a mild sucking sound when they close.

The steam they vent is warm. This is almost everything. This is the energy that the fen runs on at scales above the foundational borers' slow mineral chemistry. The engine-forms are the producers. They take in groundwater and chemical substrate and vent heat, and everything else in the fen that maintains a body temperature above ambient depends, in some pathway, on the heat the engine-forms release. They are not aware of this. They are not organized for benevolence. They are simply the most energetically productive form in the ecosystem and therefore the energetic center around which everything else has arranged itself over the long time this fen has had to elaborate such arrangements.

Examine one closely. Bring your face near the outer integument and let your eyes adjust. The surface is not uniform. There are regions where the calcite-chitin composite has been — from inside — modified: reworked into structures that are too regular for biological processes to generate without very precise direction, or something that operates the way very precise direction operates. Hexagonal arrangements of pores. Graded series of apertures in diminishing sizes. A long panel of the outer surface that has differentiated into something like a membrane, taut, vibrating at frequencies slightly below audible, serving some function that relates to — it takes time to determine this — to the monitoring of pressure-states in the chambers within. The membrane is a sensor. It grew as a sensor. No genome was edited to place it there; the engineering emerged from the same unchosen drift and selection that made the chambers and the stacks, and yet it is unambiguously engineering, unambiguously functional design, and the engine-form has no experience of having designed it.

They move, the engine-forms, but slowly and not far. They extend their substrate-contact area gradually, in the direction of the richest groundwater chemistry. They retract from areas they have already processed. Over decades they migrate, very slowly, in loose coordinated patterns that are not coordinated by any signal exchanged between them but by the shared fact of their identical response to identical conditions — the way a hillside of grass all leans into the same wind without having agreed to lean. Their migrations crisscross the fen in patterns that, mapped across centuries, would show the fen processing its own resources in a sequence of visitation and recovery: here, then here, then here, then back to here once the chemistry has had time to reestablish.

The structures connecting them — the tubes, the channels, the root-and-conduit things — are not, or not always, part of the engine-forms themselves. Some of them are. But others are the work of smaller organisms that have evolved to build infrastructure between the engine-forms because infrastructure between the engine-forms is a viable niche: if warm fluid moves from one engine-form to another through a managed conduit, the conduit-organism benefits from the warmth while providing a service that maintains conditions both engine-forms require. Symbiosis collapsed to the point where the word symbiosis becomes insufficient, where what exists is a single functional system whose components have different evolutionary origins and no interest in the system as such.

---

In the pools, which are everywhere, at the edge and in the spaces between and under overhangs of the older stone formations, live the **membrane-architects**.

They are the iron-bacteria, the rust-orange and blue-grey iridescent film that covers pool surfaces, except they are not bacteria in any simple sense. They are colonial organisms whose individual units are bacterial in scale and operation but whose collective organization occurs at the scale of the pool, and the collective organization is not incidental — it is the point of them, the thing they do, the thing they have refined.

The film they form on pool surfaces is a sensor array.

Not like a sensor array. An actual sensor array. The differential iron-deposition across the film's surface encodes information about the chemistry of the water below, the pressure of the atmosphere above, the mechanical inputs arriving at the pool surface from the surrounding fen. The encoding is not symbolic. It is direct: the state of the film at any point reflects the state of the conditions at that point, the way water in a vessel reflects the shape of the vessel. But the film also processes. The colonies of its constituent organisms communicate chemically through the film's thickness, and the communication alters the film's local properties, and the alteration propagates, and what propagates is not just information but something more like — response. Evaluation. The film encounters a new condition and it changes in a way that incorporates the new condition into its ongoing state while maintaining its overall coherence.

The light that appears where wave-interference patterns cross — the pale cold illumination that has no source — comes from them. It is bioluminescent, but the luminescence is regulated: specific colonial subunits activate in response to specific patterns of mechanical input, and the patterns of mechanical input that trigger luminescence are the interference patterns, the double-wave states, and this is not coincidence. The membrane-architects have been here long enough to have integrated the anomalies into their detection systems. They light up for the violations. They have made the impossible legible.

What they do with the information the film encodes is the question without a clear answer. The film does not have outputs in any obvious sense. It does not connect to anything that processes its signals further, as far as can be determined. Unless the collective chemical state of the film constitutes an output in itself — unless the fen's chemistry is altered in small ways by the film's ongoing reactions, and the fen's chemistry is the medium through which the engine-forms and the foundational borers and the mobile fauna are organized, and the film is therefore a slow, distributed, barely-visible governance-layer running across every pool in the fen, adjusting conditions in ways too subtle and too widely distributed to be attributable to intention.

This is not known. This is suspected. The fen has been doing something with the violations for a very long time, and the membrane-architects are the most plausible doing-something apparatus available.

Lean over a pool and look at your face in the film. The film will light up faintly where your breath strikes it, and the light will propagate outward in the pattern of your exhalation, and hold for a moment, and then be incorporated — the pattern absorbed and processed and rendered into the ongoing state of the film — and within thirty seconds there will be no trace of you. The fen is thorough.

---

There are the **filament-crawlers**, which are the dominant mobile macrofauna and which are responsible for most of the animal noise.

They are between fifteen centimeters and sixty centimeters in length, bilaterally symmetrical in their underlying skeleton, which is modified to support a covering of keratinous filaments — long, flexible, irregularly branched — that give them the appearance of animated lichen or ambulatory static. The filaments serve thermal management: they are heat-exchangers, holding warmth near the body and extending surface area into the steam-clouds of the venting events to absorb the thermal energy directly. They are also, in the densest-packed colonies, where individuals are in physical contact with each other's filaments, a communication medium. The filament-to-filament contact transmits both heat and mechanical signal, and the mechanical signals are rapid enough and complex enough to constitute, across a large cluster, something like a distributed nervous system. An individual filament-crawler has a brain adequate to locomotion, thermal regulation, basic social response. A cluster of sixty has something more.

The chrome-grey color of their filaments is real chrome: they have incorporated metallic compounds into their keratin as it grows. This is an innovation of the fen; this is what the fen-chemistry, given long enough, produces in organisms that spend their lives moving across mineral-saturated surfaces and breathing mineral-laden steam. They are partly metal in the way bones are partly mineral — the organic matrix laid down first, the inorganic incorporated afterward, the final structure stronger than either alone. Their filaments do not corrode in the fen's wet because they have evolved chemistry specifically to prevent it. They have been fighting corrosion for so long that the fight is now just structure.

They eat by pressing their undersides against the engine-forms' outer surfaces and absorbing the thermal energy and the chemical gradients leaching through. They are not parasitic — they do not take from the engine-forms any material the engine-forms require — but they are dependent, entirely, on the engine-forms' outputs. They have no relationship with any food source that is not, ultimately, the engine-forms. Their evolutionary history is the history of learning to be more and more exquisitely useful to, or tolerated by, or adapted into the marginal niches of, the engine-form's operation. They clean debris from the stacks. Not intentionally. But the stacks' apertures, which need to be clear for venting, happen to be environments where the filament-crawlers find things worth consuming, and the consumption keeps the apertures clear. The apertures remained clear for long enough that the engine-forms with clear apertures survived better, and the filament-crawlers of certain size and behavior profile were not driven off by defensive chemistry, and then for long enough that the engine-forms with clear apertures outcompeted those without, and so the present relationship.

Now the engine-forms release a chemical that is not quite a pheromone that specifically attracts filament-crawlers to occluded apertures. They do not know they do this. It is the residue of very long mutual selection.

In the densest clusters, as noted, the filament-crawlers are partially elsewhere. This does not distress them. Their nervous systems — individual and collective — have no registration of the elsewhere-ness as different from here-ness. They are in both states with the same equanimity. The collective awareness of a large cluster, tracking warmth and chemical gradient and the patterns the membrane-architects inscribe in the pool surfaces, may have more relationship with the nature of the violations than any individual's simpler brain-state could. They may not be just experiencing the elsewhere-ness. They may be, in some fashion too distributed to clearly locate, *navigating* it.

---

Among the filament-crawlers and in some cases between them and the engine-forms there lives a class of organism that resists being called a class. They are the **interstitials**: organisms that occupy the spaces and moments between categories.

Some of them are small and soft and translucent and move through the water-film on every surface with no apparent mechanism. No visible cilia. No fins, no flagella, no jets. They simply move through the film, and where they have been the film is slightly different, slightly rearranged, as though they have passed through it by negotiating its structure at a molecular level rather than moving through it as a body moves through water. They are found everywhere. Their function is unknown. They are the hardest thing in the fen to fix with attention — look directly at one and it is not exactly there, not exactly absent, occupying a relationship with the visual field that is more like a probability than a position.

Some interstitials are larger and mostly structural: organisms that have evolved to live at the interface between the foundational borers and the engine-forms, managing the chemical transition between the two, and which have bodies that are partly each and partly neither, bodies that appear different depending on which direction you look at them from, from the direction of the borer-floor looking up or from the direction of the engine-form flanks looking down.

And there is one interstitial that is larger still, that has been glimpsed in multiples but seems to be everywhere a single one: a slow, grey, irregularly-shaped something that moves through the fen with the air of having been everywhere already and finding everywhere adequate. Its form is not fixed enough to describe precisely. It produces no sound. Its filament-analog integument — if that is what it is — seems to interfere with the film-light of the membrane-architects, not blocking it but refracting it into colors that should not appear in this low pewter light, brief flashes of something that might be violet, might be a frequency the eye was not built to receive. It is largest near the places where the upward mist occurs. It may be old enough to remember when the upward mist did not occur, if it has memory, which is not known.

It does not react to observation. It does not flee. If it is aware of being observed — and it may not be, it may be awareness of a kind that does not sort stimuli into *about me* and *about something else* — it has found observation insufficient cause for adjustment.

---

In the vented steam itself, briefly after each venting event, there live organisms that exist only there: the **steam-fauna**, which are too small to see clearly but present in sufficient density to be visible as a shimmer, a slight differential movement in the steam-cloud as it rises, a motion within the motion. They are born from eggs or spores or something smaller than either that have been embedded in the engine-forms' outer surfaces, activated by the temperature of the venting event, hatching in the steam, feeding on the mineral aerosols in the steam, reproducing within the ninety-second span of the vent, and releasing their own next-stage propagules into the cloud before the cloud dissipates and the cold air returns and what was briefly possible is no longer possible.

Their lives are short enough that the questions asked of longer lives do not apply. No navigation. No accumulated knowledge. No relationship to the violations in this world's physics because the violations operate on timescales longer than anything a steam-fauna individual experiences. They are lives of pure process: the chemistry that runs them runs fully to its end within a very short time and then is over and the propagules fall through the mist toward the surfaces below, landing everywhere, waiting for the next vent.

They are very successful. They are in every cloud. They have been in every cloud for as long as there have been clouds, here, which is a long time.

---

And then — always — the thing that is not any one of these but all of them, which cannot be pointed to but which is apparent when you stop trying to point to things and let the fen operate on you as an environment rather than as a collection of objects.

The fen as a whole is doing something.

The foundational borers drive the water through the stone and the water-breathing is the base-state, the constant. The engine-forms process the chemical energy in the groundwater and vent it as heat and steam and the heat organizes everything that needs heat. The membrane-architects read the surfaces of the pools and adjust the pool-chemistry and the pool-chemistry feeds back into the groundwater and the groundwater feeds back into the foundational borers and through them into the engine-forms and through them into the steam and through the steam into the filament-crawlers and the steam-fauna and and back through the crawlers' filament-signals and their cleaning of the stacks and back into the engine-forms and back into the borer-beds. The interstitials are somewhere in this loop, at every interface, at every transition point, managing or being managed or both simultaneously.

This cycle has been running for long enough that it has worn itself smooth, like the stone. Every rough edge of interaction that produced friction has been worn away by repetition until what remains is a process so well-fitted to itself that it runs almost without loss. Almost. There are losses. The losses are the violations: the places where the world's structure is being slowly, continuously worked through, processed, by something the fen does collectively — its organisms working together in no coordinated way to do what no single one could do, which is to take the anomalous and render it continuous, to take the impossible and make it just another kind of weather.

The fen breathes. The fen breathes.

Everything that lives in it is the breathing.
