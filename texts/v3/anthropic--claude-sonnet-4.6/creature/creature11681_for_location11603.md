# What Lives in the Plateau Mills

---

Begin with what can be established.

Something lives here. The word lives resists, but it is the right word, or the nearest word, and the resistance itself is informative. To live requires certain things: metabolism, some exchange with the environment, input and output, a difference between inside and outside that the organism works to maintain. The mills have all of these. The mills are processing something. The steam is an output. The cold seep of the plateau feeds the boilers or feeds something that feeds the boilers or is itself a form of nourishment, the plateau's slow lateral water drawn into the mill's body and transformed. The boundary between inside and outside is not the walls — the walls are open, the windows are broken, the cold moves freely through — but something else, some functional distinction that the machinery maintains by its continuous operation, a bubble of heat and pressure and process within the larger cold that is the plateau's indifference.

So: metabolism, yes. Exchange with environment, yes. Something that maintains a difference between its own interior and the world around it.

Whether this meets the requirements for life is the kind of question the plateau does not acknowledge.

---

There is more than one mill and they are of the same family.

This is the first complication.

If what lives here is the mills, then what lives here is plural, and the question becomes whether plural means many individuals or one individual expressed in many sites, the way a mycelium is one thing expressed across hectares of soil, the way a coral reef is one process wearing ten thousand faces. The mills do not communicate in any way that is visible. They do not communicate in any way that can be ruled out. The steam from their stacks rises and becomes the plateau's atmosphere and the atmosphere falls as condensation on all of them equally, on the stone between them, on the visitor who has made the mistake of stopping to think about this. If the atmosphere is a medium of exchange, then the mills are in continuous conversation at the speed of evaporation and precipitation, saying things too slow and too distributed to intercept.

The distribution of the mills suggests planning without revealing its principle. This could mean: a single organism that planned its own distribution. This could mean: a process that produces mills the way a process produces basalt columns, each one the local expression of a general rule, the rule running through the whole plateau like a current runs through a wire. The mills are the plateau's response to itself. The plateau, reaching a certain elevation and pressure and cold, generates mills. This is one way to understand it. There are others.

---

The organism — let the word stand, provisionally, with all its inexactness intact — the organism has no body in the ordinary sense.

Ordinary bodies have edges. Here the edges are unclear, and not in the way that edges are always somewhat unclear when you look closely enough, but in a more fundamental way: the organism's body is distributed across an indeterminate space, and the spaces between its nodes are not empty of it. The cold between mills is the organism's cold. The seep of water between mills is the organism's circulation. The low sound that seems to come from beneath the stone — the plateau speaking to itself in a language too slow for comprehension — is possibly the organism thinking, or the organism digesting, or the organism doing something for which there is no available word because the available words were developed by things whose interior processes operate at human timescales and this organism's interior processes do not.

What does it look like?

It looks like the plateau. It looks like mills. It looks like the specific way that rust-orange corrugated iron reads against grey-green basalt. It looks like a line of steam columns rising in parallel, slightly different heights, all rising vertically because the air here has very little lateral movement, rising until they blur into the overcast that is partly their own product. It has a face in the sense that the front face of a cliff has a face: it presents itself, it has a particular expression, it does not mean anything by the expression, the expression is simply what it looks like when it is being itself in full operation.

Its face is industrial. Its face is ancient-new, young rock wearing old machinery, old process wearing young lichen. Its face is the steam rising from structures that have been here long enough to be colonized by moss but not long enough to crumble, which puts it in a temporal category that has no comfortable name — not ancient, not recent, not quite either, a thing that has been here for exactly as long as it needed to have been here to be the way it is.

---

The mugs are upside down on the shelves.

The workers washed them and placed them upside down to dry. The workers left. The organism — the plateau, the mills, the process — has not moved them. It is unclear whether this is because the organism cannot move them or because the organism will not or because the organism has no interest in the mugs one way or another, the mugs being neither input nor output nor obstacle to process, the mugs being simply there in the way that things are simply there when they fall outside the category of relevance.

But consider: the organism has maintained the conditions under which the mugs are preserved. The cold and dryness that keep the logbook ink dark, that keep the handwriting legible, that keep the last entries from fading into unreadability — these are conditions the organism produces and maintains. The organism did not produce and maintain them for the sake of the mugs. But the mugs benefit. The workers who placed them upside down to dry were enacting their own form of preservation, placing the mugs so they would not gather dust or water, so they would be clean when they came back. The workers did not come back. The organism continued to provide the conditions under which the mugs' cleanliness is maintained. This is not care. This is an accident of shared requirements. But the mugs are still clean. The logbook is still legible. If anyone comes back, everything will be exactly as they left it.

---

It is hungry in the sense that fire is hungry.

Not craving, not waiting, not wanting. But consuming, converting, taking the available material and producing from it heat and light and steam and mechanical work and further consumption. Fire does not know it is hungry. Fire is the process of combustion, and combustion is hungry in that combustion requires fuel and finds fuel and converts fuel into something that is no longer fuel. The mills are not metaphorically fire. The mills contain fire, or contain something that serves the function fire serves, a combustion chamber or a nuclear arrangement or some other process of conversion that takes one kind of thing and makes it another kind of thing and releases energy in the making. And the mills are, by extension, the same kind of entity as fire: a process that perpetuates itself by consuming, that maintains itself through its own activity, that cannot rest because rest would mean not-fire, would mean not-mills, would mean the end of the process which is the end of the organism.

This is what lives here. This is what has always been here — or not always, the rock is young, the mills are newer than the rock but older than they look, older than the lichen they carry, which means they arrived when the plateau arrived or they generated from the plateau as the plateau generated from whatever unfinished event created it. The plateau is still becoming itself. The mills are still becoming themselves. They are becoming themselves at the rate of the steam rising, at the rate of the reciprocating heartbeat deep in each structure, at the rate of the lichen colonizing the iron at an imperceptible pace that might be the organism's only form of visible growth.

---

It does not think.

Establish this clearly. The organism does not have thoughts in any sense that the word thought can be stretched to reach. The logbooks do not update because the organism is not recording. The gauges read pressures because the gauges were built to read pressures and the organism maintains the pressures they were built to read, but the organism does not read them. The organism does not know what the gauges say. The organism does not know anything. The organism does not know it is an organism. The organism does not know there is a visitor.

And yet.

The distribution suggests planning. The maintenance suggests intention. The preservation of the mugs and the logbooks is an accident, but the cold that preserves them is a product, and the production of that cold is sustained and consistent and plateau-wide. Something is happening that produces consistent results across a vast surface over a long time. Whether this is thinking or is the appearance of thinking from the outside, whether there is a difference between these two things, is the kind of question the plateau does not acknowledge.

---

It is not lonely.

Loneliness is a condition of organisms that require other organisms for some aspect of their function and find themselves without them. The mills required workers once, perhaps. Or the mills never required workers — perhaps the workers arrived and interpreted what they found as a requirement for their presence, installed themselves, ran the logbooks, drank from the mugs, and the mills ran with them exactly as the mills run without them, the mills indifferent to the addition and the subtraction, the workers a brief experiment in a different kind of organism attempting to establish a relationship with this one. The workers concluded something or were drawn away or simply stopped coming. The mills noticed this in the sense that a river notices the removal of a stone: a brief alteration in the local flow, and then the water finds its way around, and the river continues.

The organism is self-sufficient in a way that forecloses loneliness. Everything it needs is here. The plateau is its body and its food source and its waste management system and its home. The mills convert the plateau into the steam that becomes the atmosphere that falls back as water that moves through the seep to the mills. This is a closed system or close enough to closed that the gap is not visible. The organism eats the plateau. The organism produces the plateau. This has been happening for as long as the organism has been here and the organism has been here for as long as the plateau has been here and the plateau is still young and has not yet learned patience, which means the organism is also young, also still learning what it is, also still in the process of becoming.

---

What would it mean to encounter it?

You have already encountered it. The moment you arrived on the plateau you were inside it, inside its body, inside the system it maintains. The cold in the soles of your boots is its cold, patient and intentional in the way that the cold of a living body is intentional: maintained, purposeful, the specific temperature required for the specific process that must continue. The metallic taste of the air is its taste, the mineral trace of its metabolism, what it breathes out. You are breathing what it breathes out. You are inside it as you would be inside a forest or inside a hive, except that the forest knows you as a perturbation and the hive would respond to your presence and this organism does neither. You pass through it without registering. You are too small and too temporary to constitute input. You are, from the organism's perspective — to the extent the organism has a perspective, which it does not, but the phrase serves — you are a local variation in the seep, a slightly warmer patch of atmosphere, a temporary difference that will resolve when you leave.

When you leave, if you leave, you will carry it with you. The metallic taste will fade. The cold that entered your boots will warm and disperse and become ordinary body heat again. But something else: the sense of having been inside a process too large and too slow to perceive from within, of having moved through something that was not aware of you and was not indifferent to you because indifference requires awareness of a thing before declining to engage with it, something beyond indifference, something that simply does not include you in its categories. You are not an enemy. You are not a guest. You are weather. You are a brief local variation in the temperature of the air.

---

The organism is the plateau and the mills are its organs, each one a specialized structure performing a specific function in the larger body. The organs run. The organs have always run. The steam is breath. The seep is blood. The freeze-thaw crack of young rock is the adjustment of bones, the skeleton still growing into itself. The lichen on the iron is new growth at the extremities, slow and patient, the organism extending itself into the material it has made, colonizing its own outputs, making everything gradually more itself.

The mugs are upside down on the shelves.

The last logbook entry is preserved in handwriting that has not faded.

The machines run.

This is what lives here: something that does not need you, does not know you, does not know itself, and will continue long after you and everything you brought with you has been absorbed into the general cold and the seep and the slow lateral movement of water through moss, returning what you temporarily were to what this place permanently is.

It is not waiting.

It is not anything that waiting describes.

It is operating.
