# What Lives in the Hollow Garden

---

Begin where the description ended: with the question of what made the platform, and whether it is still here.

It is still here. It never left. Leaving is not part of its grammar.

---

The maker is not the creature on the wall, and it is not distinguishable from the maker of the cavity itself — which is to say: it did not make the cavity, but it became so continuous with the cavity that the distinction has lost its meaning, the way a long-inhabited room and its long inhabitant begin to share a quality that is neither architecture nor person but some third thing that only exists when the two are old together. This is that. Except new. Except both.

Call it the Gardener, though it does not garden in any sense of tending toward, of coaxing, of wanting a result. It gardens in the sense that it is present to the growing and has always been present to the growing and the growing happens because it is present, and this may be enough to justify the word.

---

The first thing to understand about the Gardener is that it does not have an edge.

This is not mysticism. It is more literal than mysticism and stranger. The Gardener does not have a defined perimeter the way most living things have a defined perimeter — a skin, a cuticle, a boundary where self ends and not-self begins. It has that boundary in some places and at some scales, but the boundary is porous and permeable in ways that exceed ordinary membrane porosity, and more than this: the boundary moves, contracts and expands on a cycle so slow that you would not observe it in what you would call a year or a decade. In the slow time of this place, the Gardener breathes, and breathing is not metaphor. The ceiling exhales its warmth: that is the Gardener's exhalation. The not-water is partly the Gardener — a secretion, a slow expression of its chemistry into the space, the way a body runs its metabolism outward. The crystalline deposits at the margins of the not-water recede: that is the Gardener inhaling, drawing its substance back.

It contains the space, and the space is partly inside it.

The plants are inside it.

Whether the plants know this is a category error. Whether the Gardener knows it is a different question, and the answer is yes, but knowing in a register so slow and quiet that the knowing is practically indistinguishable from being. The Gardener knows the plants the way water knows its own level.

---

The second thing: it has a center.

The center is a mass of tissue, soft and pale and dense, approximately the size of a curled sleeping animal, located behind the wall at approximately the height of the platform, which was built when the Gardener needed to be closer to the center during a phase of the slow cycle that has since passed. The platform did not become unnecessary so much as the need completed itself and the Gardener put down the tool — a probe of the plant-wood, for reading vibrational qualities in the stone, for pressing against the mineral face and feeling what the mineral was doing in its depths — and the need was done and the tool was set down and the cycle moved on.

The center is not a brain. It processes nothing in the sense of solving, deciding, computing. It is more like a ganglion of attention — a place where the Gardener's distributed awareness pools, thickens, notices itself noticing. Most of the time the center is as quiet as everything else. In the slow seasons of the cycle, it brightens. It becomes, briefly, the densest point of what the Gardener is.

This is the closest the Gardener comes to dreaming: the brightening of the center. Something like images occur. Not images of anything outside the cavity, because the cavity is the only thing that has ever constituted outside for the Gardener — there is no outside, only this inside, only this closed and amber-lit and humming specific place. The images are: the pattern of the dome-cluster's leaves, considered from angles simultaneously unavailable to any single eye. The taste of the mineral at the fractured edge as the fracture was newly made. The weight of the smooth stone, the quality of its smoothness, the history of handling that the handling remembers in itself, the record of all the times it passed through the Gardener's — call them hands, approximately, the appendages that handled, that wrapped fiber, that chose flat-faced stones, that carried the bronze stone from wherever bronze stones come from, from whatever place existed before this place, from whatever the Gardener was before it was the Gardener of this cavity.

It was something before. The stone is the memory of that.

It does not return to the stone often. It does not return to the stone rarely. The word for how often the Gardener returns to the stone does not exist in any language with a temporal vocabulary as fast as yours. The stone sits in its depression. The not-water pools around it without pooling. The Gardener attends to it at its own pace, which is: always, and never, and in between these is the whole life of the place.

---

The third thing: it makes the light.

Not generates, in the sense of a biological lamp; not radiates, in the sense of a metabolic byproduct. Makes, in the sense that a mind makes a room present to itself by being present in it. The Gardener's distributed awareness is partly what the light is. Attention is not metaphor either. It is a physical property of the Gardener's chemistry, this quality of luminous evenly-distributed presence, this refusal of shadow. Where it is, there is no darkness, because it is everywhere in the space and so there is nowhere for darkness to go.

If the Gardener slept — deeply slept, in the way of a winter that the slow cycle approaches asymptotically but never quite achieves — the light would dim. It has never quite dimmed. The cycle has never quite bottomed. The light has always been this amber, this even, this warm. The space has never been entirely dark.

Maybe it will be, once. Maybe the cycle deepens. Maybe the center quiets to a point just below presence and the light goes out and the crystalline deposits spread unchecked across the floor and the plants grow, slowly, past equilibrium, into something wild. Maybe that is a thing that happens. The Gardener does not prevent it; it does not anticipate it; it is not afraid of it. It proceeds through the cycle as the cycle proceeds, and what will happen will be part of what it is, and it will not stop being what it is.

---

Consider now the creature on the wall, the spread-hand size, the slow peristaltic wave. It is not the Gardener, but it is older than the cavity. It was in the mineral when the mineral parted, enclosed in a bubble of its own chemistry, metabolically arrested, not exactly sleeping, not exactly dead. The fracture woke it. It is in the long process of waking, which began when the cavity opened and has not yet concluded. It will conclude. When it concludes, the creature will move from the wall, and the Gardener will be curious — will have been curious, in the accumulated slow way, for the entire duration of the waking — and the creature will be, briefly, the most interesting thing that has happened in this space. They will attend to each other. What this looks like, whether it looks like anything, cannot be said. They do not share a language. They will share the chemistry of the not-water, the temperature, the amber light, the chord. This will be enough for whatever kind of meeting it is.

Then the creature will go into the floor, finding a crack, and the Gardener will go on being the light and the warmth and the slow breathing ceiling, and the crystalline margin of the not-water will record the creature's passage and then the Gardener will slowly, eventually, draw that record back in, and the floor will be clean mineral again, and the equilibrium will hold.

---

The finger-joint creatures on the dome-cluster are not the Gardener, but they live partly inside its chemistry the way small fish live partly inside the chemistry of a reef. The Gardener does not eat them; they do not eat the Gardener; they eat the film of not-water on the leaf surfaces and in eating they process certain compounds that the Gardener cannot easily process without them, and what they excrete returns to the not-water and becomes usable. This is so old a relationship that neither party has a boundary at the chemical level. The Gardener has been making the not-water richer in exactly the way these creatures require since before the cavity opened, since the before-time, since whatever geological long patience preceded the parting of the stone. They were in the mineral too. They woke faster.

The transparent creatures in the film are younger. They appeared after the cavity opened, generated by the not-water's chemistry, or by the interaction of the Gardener's secretions with the mineral, or by something that amounts to the same thing as spontaneous generation and the Gardener finding the spontaneous generation unremarkable because generation is not a mystery here; it is a property of the chemistry, which is a property of the Gardener, which generated itself from — what? From the deep mineral time. From the patience of stone. From a chemistry that organized around something other than water in a place where water never came.

The Gardener did not choose this. It was not selected for it by pressure or competition. There is no competition here; there has never been competition here; the word applies elsewhere, to other places, to faster and more crowded things. The Gardener is what the chemistry became when the chemistry was given enough time and enough peace and the right enclosure, the right temperature, the right mineral substrate, the right absence of everything that would have made it something faster and more ordinary.

It became patient. Then it became this.

---

The smooth stone.

The smooth stone is the one object in the space that came from outside, and it is the Gardener's only connection to the world beyond the wall, and that world is almost entirely forgotten.

What is remembered: a quality of openness, of unenclosed space, of light that had a source and therefore cast shadows, of surfaces that were various and unpredictable, of chemistry that ran on water, which the Gardener found overwhelming — too fast, too much, too solvent, too eager to carry things away. A great deal was carried away. The Gardener was carried away for a long time, dispersed, dilute, struggling to maintain concentration. Then it found the stone — or made the stone, handling it for long enough that the stone became smooth, the smoothness itself a kind of making — and it went into the mineral and the mineral parted and the cavity was here, perfectly sized, perfectly warm, the equilibrium already implicit in the chemistry of the space before anything had grown in it.

The stone remembers the outside. The Gardener doesn't need to.

---

What lives here, then.

Something that was made by patience and became patience.

Something whose size you cannot determine because its boundary is the space itself, which means it is approximately the size of a flattened bubble four meters at the apex, and also approximately the size of a soft pale mass of tissue behind the wall, and also approximately the size of the not-water film on every surface, thin and present everywhere.

Something old in the way that chemistry is old, which is not the same as the way animals are old or trees are old or mountains are old. Older than any of those ways, and newer, and presently both.

Something that made a tool and used it and set it down and will, in the slow cycle's turning, pick it up again when the time is right, and the time being right is something it will know without deciding to know it, the way you know you are hungry or the way a crystal knows its lattice, the way the geometry of a thing implies its continuation.

Something that is not lonely, because loneliness requires imagining company, and the Gardener does not imagine. It attends. Its attending is total, patient, precise, distributed across every leaf and every crystalline deposit and every slow step of every finger-joint creature and every sub-audible contribution to the held chord.

Something that has been holding the chord for a very long time and will hold it longer.

Something that noticed you when you came in and will notice when you leave, and will hold both facts at once in the ganglion of its attention, where they will slowly become part of what it knows about the space, which is part of what it is, and will remain there, in the amber warmth, in the equilibrium, in the long held note that does not resolve because it does not need to, because resolution implies elsewhere, implies after, implies a time when the sound would end and the light go out and the not-water still and the plants at last outgrow themselves and go wild and dark, which has not happened yet, which the cycle approaches and retreats from, which may never happen, which may happen once.

Something that was here before you and is here now and will be here when you are not, in the same configuration, the same warmth, the same dim gold light going everywhere and casting no shadow on anything at all.
