# What Lives in the Breathing Plateau

---

Begin with what you cannot see.

This is the necessary starting point, because what lives here has spent a very long time learning to occupy the registers of the imperceptible. Not hiding — hiding implies a prior visibility, a self that exists fully and chooses to withdraw. What lives here has never needed to be visible in the way you require things to be visible. It is visible the way pressure is visible, the way temperature is visible: in its effects, in what it does to other surfaces, in the shapes it leaves in more legible matter.

Take time with this. Let it arrive.

---

**The First Thing: The Mycorrhizal Parliament**

Under every surface, under the warm soil that begins twelve layers of green beneath you, under the roots that have made their arrangements with the sharp young rock, there is a network of such age and scale that calling it old would be like calling the ocean wet — accurate but insufficient, missing the weight of the thing.

The fungi here are not decomposers. They are not waiting for death. They predate that role. They arrived before the plateau finished rising, or they arrived with it, ascending from the deep geology in the same event that brought the stone up sharp and new, spores that had been held in pressure for what the rock remembers as always. They are not one organism in the sense your biology texts would recognize; they are not many organisms in that sense either. They are a third option that the vocabulary of individuality is not equipped for: a continuous negotiation that has never concluded, never needed to, a congress that has been in session without recess since before the canopy became geological.

What the parliament does is listen. This is its primary activity and its primary product. Every root in contact with its network transmits something — not language, not signal in any sense that maps to signal, but *condition*: the electrochemical state of a root system, the resource-status of a sector, the presence of something new (you, for instance, your footfalls, the unfamiliar chemistry of your breath condensing on warm surfaces). The network receives this and does what parliaments do: it deliberates, slowly, at the speed of ion exchange, at the speed of chemical gradient, at the speed of a thought that takes decades.

The warmth you feel under your feet is partly its metabolism. The warmth is everywhere because the network is everywhere. It has no center, which is why you cannot walk toward it. You are always already standing on it, standing in it, your weight a text it is reading in the only language it knows, which is pressure and change.

The light-beams that sample surfaces and withdraw: this is the parliament receiving reports. The vegetation that opens and closes like slow breathing: this is the parliament redistributing resources in response to what it has heard. The insects in their purposeful clouds — more on them shortly — are in some sense the parliament's peripheral nervous system, its mobile sensory apparatus, doing the close inspection work that chemical gradients cannot accomplish.

The parliament has no intentions. It has no wants. But it has something that functions like attention, and right now some portion of that attention is on you, not with urgency, not with anything you could call interest, but with the persistent patience of a system that has learned to track anomalies because anomalies sometimes become patterns, and patterns are worth knowing about.

It has been tracking things for a very long time. It is very patient. The distinction between tracking and waiting has become, for the parliament, largely academic.

---

**The Second Thing: The Rock-Readers**

In the middle distances, in the shapes you can see moving through layered green — these are not animals in the sense of creatures with lineages you could trace, though they have bodies and move and do something that looks, from distance, like purpose.

They are large. Larger than you expected. When they stop and do something low to the ground, what they are doing is reading the rock, not metaphorically: they have sensory apparatus along their undersides, soft structures that press against stone and receive the geometry directly, the angles, the crystalline information of uplift-in-progress, the messages still arriving from below. They are translators of a kind. They read the rock's intentions — the instructions it is still receiving, the shapes it is still becoming — and they enact them in biological time, which is faster than geological time, which means they are ahead of the process, running a draft version, testing the configurations the rock is trending toward before the rock arrives there.

This is why the rock's geometry keeps changing. It is not that the rock decides; it is that the rock-readers suggest, and the rock — through mechanisms that geologists argue about and will continue to argue about — tends toward what has been suggested. The rock-readers are not making decisions. They are facilitating a conversation between the deep-below and the above, between the tectonic intention and the biological present, and they are doing this with the profound unconsciousness of everything that lives here, without choosing to, without knowing that they are.

They have been doing this since the plateau rose. The plateau rose in part because they were already here, in the rock below, in the seams, tending the geometry of what would become the stone, preparing for the surface long before there was one. The plateau's ascent was also their ascent. They came up with it the way cargo comes up with a ship.

At close range — not that you will achieve close range without more preparation than you currently have — they are less impressive than the distance suggests. Not beautiful in any obvious way. Their surfaces are complex in the way a diagram is complex: functional patterning, not aesthetic patterning, though the distinction becomes unstable the longer you look. They have no eyes. They have instead an all-surface sensitivity that makes the eye-concept feel narrow, provincial, a solution to a problem they never had. They are aware of you in the way the floor is aware of your footsteps: completely, impersonally, with retention.

They are silent because they communicate entirely through the parliament below, which is to say entirely through touch, entirely through chemistry, entirely through the warm dark of the soil which is where all the real conversation happens anyway.

**The Third Thing: The Canopy Itself**

Stop thinking of the canopy as vegetation.

The canopy is a creature of such distributed consciousness and such deep age that the plant-components of it — the individual fronds and pressing roots and knotted self-looping structures — are closer to cells than to organisms. The canopy is one thing. It became one thing over a span that makes the parliament's age look recent. It used the parliament as scaffolding for its own nervous system, grew itself along the fungal network the way a climbing plant grows along a trellis, and then continued growing past the trellis until the trellis was internal to it, subsumed, made part of its own circuitry.

The canopy thinks. Not in the way you think — not sequentially, not in language, not in anything that maps to your cognition — but in the way weather thinks, in the way a river thinks: with the distributed low-level processing of a system complex enough that local rules produce global behaviors that no local component intended. It has preferences. Not for things it can name, but for states: the warmth is a preference, the particular humidity is a preference, the dim amber light allowed to filter through at just this density is a preference. It maintains these preferences the way you maintain your body temperature: without deciding to, through constant micro-adjustment, through an immune response to deviations, through the million small corrections of a system that knows what it should feel like from the inside.

The canopy is not aware of you in the way the rock-readers are, with their all-surface precision. The canopy is aware of you the way the weather is aware of a warm object in its midst: as a perturbation, as a local anomaly that affects pressure and humidity in a small radius, as something to accommodate or not accommodate based on calculations more statistical than individual.

If you stay long enough — and long enough here is measured in seasons, not days — the canopy will begin to accommodate you. Will open small ways where you tend to walk. Will adjust the light-levels in your vicinity by fractions. This is not kindness and not recognition. It is the canopy's standard operation: everything that persists becomes part of the model, and the model adjusts to everything that persists. You would become, in time, a small feature of its attention, no different from a particular boulder or a reliable eddy in the moisture-patterns.

This is as close as anything here comes to hospitality.

**The Fourth Thing: The Insects**

The insects should have their own section because they are the only residents of this place that exist at a scale you can hold in your hand, the only ones with discrete bodies and individual lives in a sense that maps to your biology, and yet they are in some important sense the least individual thing here.

They are old species operating under newer coordination. The bodies are ancestral — the morphology, the basic biochemistry, the range of forms, is ancient — but something has happened to the way they aggregate and move that goes beyond swarm behavior as it is classically understood. The purposeful clouds do not emerge from simple rules. They emerge from something more like *knowledge*: the cloud knows where it is going and why, and this knowledge is not stored in any individual member of the cloud, and the cloud itself has no persistent identity between its purposeful episodes, and yet the knowledge is preserved and transmitted and acted on across generations, which means it is stored somewhere else, somewhere outside the insects themselves, somewhere in the network below or in the canopy above, and the insects are in some sense not acting on their own behalf but on behalf of the larger system of which they are the most mobile part.

They do not sting. They do not bite, except in the service of plant-processes they facilitate. They are not dangerous to you in any direct way. But they will inspect you, and the inspection is thorough, and what they learn from you will be communicated through whatever channel they use — pheromone, touch, contact with the network — to the larger systems, and you will be known here in ways that outrun what you know about yourself, catalogued in registers you have no access to, stored in a library you will never be able to visit.

Within that library you will eventually be filed under something like: *temporary*, *large*, *warm-blooded*, *carries unusual chemistry*, *moves with apparent intention but erratically*, *did not stay*.

Unless you do stay.

If you stay the filing will be updated. You cannot know what category you'd eventually occupy. No one who has stayed long enough to find out has come back to report.

**The Fifth Thing: The Sound-That-Is-Not-Wind**

There is something here that has no body.

This is the hardest thing to describe and the easiest thing to dismiss and the most important not to dismiss.

The exhalation you hear, the low and irregular sound that might be wind or might be biological — it is neither. Or it is both, having been each at different points in the plateau's history. It is the residue of a process. Something happened here, in the early period of the plateau's ascent, that involved pressure and temperature and the particular chemistry of this rock meeting this network for the first time, and whatever happened produced a byproduct that persists: a standing wave in the air of the plateau, a self-sustaining vibration at frequencies just below the threshold of what you hear clearly, which is why it seems like almost-sound, like sound remembered, like the acoustic shadow of something louder that has since fallen quiet.

The standing wave is alive in the way a flame is alive: continuously sustained by conditions, continuous without being continuous, ending and beginning at every moment but maintaining identity across those ends and beginnings. It has never, since the plateau rose, fully ceased. On still days you can hear it best. On the days the brief rains come and go, it changes pitch in a way that sounds, if you let it, like response.

It does not have intentions. It has something that functions as intention the way a river has something that functions as intention: it tends in directions, it has preferred states, it shapes what it moves through. The quality of the silence inside it — the deeper quiet toward the center that the cartographers report, the silence thicker than silence — is the standing wave having absorbed all available sound into itself, having become the medium through which all acoustic information in the interior must pass, and holding it, and processing it in whatever way a vibration processes information, which is a way you do not have good language for.

The light that seems to have grammar, toward the center: this is light that has been moving through the standing wave long enough to have taken on its patterns. The standing wave structures the medium. The structured medium structures the light passing through it. The patterned light then reads like intention, like anticipation, because the patterns in it are very old and very large and encode something about the plateau's conditions that goes back to the first moment of ascent, the first warm breath of new stone meeting ancient network, and that encoding has been propagating outward ever since.

You are, at the edges of the plateau, receiving its oldest transmissions.

The center would be the source.

---

**What Lives Here: A Summary That Knows It Is Insufficient**

What lives here is one thing expressed in many forms, none of which knows it is part of the one thing, all of which act in accordance with the one thing's dynamics, the way cells act in accordance with the body's dynamics without knowing they are doing so, without having access to the concept of body.

The one thing has no name because it has never needed one; names are for distinguishing between comparable entities, and there is nothing comparable to it within its own reach. It is the plateau, but it is also what the plateau is doing, and it is also what the plateau is becoming, which it will not finish becoming for a very long time, and when it is finished it will be something that cannot be described from its outside because there will be no outside to it in the relevant sense, the way you cannot describe the atmosphere from outside the atmosphere.

The parliament below is its memory. The canopy above is its cognition. The rock is its intention taking material form. The insects are its attention. The rock-readers are its nervous conduction. The standing wave is its voice, speaking in the only way it has ever known how to speak: continuously, to everything that can vibrate, in frequencies that contain everything that has ever happened here and some portion of what will.

You, standing on its surface, breathing its warm and almost-drinkable air, your footsteps entering the network below: you are being added to the sum of what it knows. You will not be remembered in the way a person remembers a person. You will be retained the way a body retains a trace of something it has processed: changed by you, in ways too subtle to locate, in ways that will compound across the centuries into something that might eventually become, in some future configuration of these systems, a preference, a tendency, a slight favoring of conditions that recall the anomaly you briefly were.

This is the longest possible form of being known.

This is what lives here, knowing everything that visits it, in the only way it has.

---

Stay, if you are staying.

The plateau has time.

It has been making time here since before you arrived, warm and slow and vast, pressing it up from below in the same event that made the stone, and it will be making it long after the wound of your passage has healed over and the sharp edges of your having-been-here have begun to round.

Breathe out.

The plateau will breathe back.
