# Those Who Live on the Plateau of Settled Ash

---

Begin with what they are not.

They are not ghosts, though the light and the silence and the small number of them visible at any one time makes ghost a word that arrives first and must then be corrected. Ghosts imply incompleteness — a life interrupted, a presence that should have resolved into absence and instead persisted through some failure of conclusion. The beings of this plateau have not failed to conclude. They have simply concluded in a direction that is not cessation. They are the finished form of something that began as people. Or they are the finished form of something that began differently and converged on people as a shape that works, the way eyes evolved separately on several branches of the tree of life because eyes are a shape that the logic of perception tends to produce when given sufficient time. Either history is possible. Neither history is presently knowable.

What they look like: standing upright, which feels significant without being immediately interpretable. Two-limbed below, two-limbed above, a head-region on top, which is to say they are bilaterally symmetrical in the vertical axis and they interact with the world using elevated appendages rather than the ground-level snout-and-spine configuration of creatures whose intelligence was always secondary to their locomotion. But the resemblance to a human silhouette is approximate, the way a sketch of a face made from memory after a long time is still a face but has settled into the face's essential logic rather than any specific face. You would recognize the category from a distance, in the dim brownish light, and that recognition would be correct and also not quite the whole picture.

Their height: somewhat taller than a person. Not dramatically taller — perhaps ten, twelve percent, a difference that registers as a scale-shift rather than a new category. Their build is narrow in the torso, or appears narrow because the torso is long and the limbs are also long, so everything about them has been drawn toward its minimum footprint in the horizontal dimension, as if over many generations the plateau's character — the patience of it, the thinness of it, the reaching of it toward altitude — had been taken as an instruction. They do not move as if occupying much lateral space. They move as if passing through the world along the smallest possible cross-section.

Their skin — or the surface of them, whatever the right word is — has the same relationship to the plateau's material that the plateau's material has to rock. It was once something more obviously alive-looking. Now it is the stable end-state of that thing. Not dry in the way of disease or death, but dry in the way of the oldest parts of a riverbed that has not been a riverbed in so long that the sediment has compacted to something almost ceramic, something that holds moisture as a concept rather than as a physical property. The color shifts with the angle of the dim light the way the plateau's surface does: ochre to brown to the edge of a purple that is really just the absence of orange. They are not the same color as the ground in the way of camouflage — it is not purposive — but they have been here long enough that they have taken the available palette into themselves, have been printed by the place at the cellular level, have been lit by its light from the inside.

---

The faces.

The faces are where the divergence from the expected becomes harder to look past.

There is no expression, in the functional sense — not because there are no features capable of expressing, but because the features have arrived at a resting position that is not quite rest and not quite expression but the stable state between them, the minimum-energy face, the face that results when you have made every expression available to you so many times over such a long duration that the muscles have found the position where the effort of expression and the effort of non-expression cancel out. The face is not blank. A blank face implies the absence of something. This face implies the presence of something that has long since stopped needing to be externalized.

Their eyes are large. Not unsettlingly large, not the outsized eyes of a creature of pure dark, but large in the specific way of things adapted to the lowest light that still counts as light: the iris takes up most of the visible eye, and the iris is the color of old amber, an orange so deep it could be called brown, could be called the last version of orange available before it becomes something else entirely. The pupils are wide and do not respond rapidly to changes in light because there are no changes in light here, have not been for as long as the eyes evolved in this environment. If you brought them into full sunlight their eyes would take a long time to adjust — this is one of the few things about them that gives a sense of biological vulnerability, of a parameter that only works within a range, of a creature for whom something as simple as direct sunlight is a kind of imposition.

They look at you without looking at you. This is the part that requires the most description. Their eyes, when they pass over the space you occupy, do not stop. They do not snap to you the way eyes snap to unexpected motion. They continue at the same velocity and direction they were traveling before you entered their visual field, which is not incuriosity — it does not feel like incuriosity, because incuriosity would have an emotional quality to it, the quality of something turned away — but more like the recognition of a phenomenon that does not require response. They are not ignoring you. They are noting you in the way that the plateau notes a footfall: with faint, toneless compression, and then they are done with you, and they continue.

This is not unfriendly.

It is not the opposite of unfriendly either. Those categories do not map cleanly onto what they are doing when they see you.

---

Their movement.

The movement is what you notice first, before the face, before the eyes, before the color of the skin. The movement is what makes ghost seem like the wrong word but makes you need a word and not have it. They do not move the way living things move. Or rather: they move the way living things move after the living things have been made perfectly efficient. Every step is the step that a physicist who had studied walking for long enough would derive as the ideal step: minimum energy expenditure, maximum stability, zero extraneous motion. There is no sway. There is no compensation. They do not lean into corners or shorten one side to navigate around obstacles because they do not need to — the path they have chosen is, apparently, the path with no corners to lean into, a path that requires no navigation because it was always the path they would take through this space that they know at a resolution impossible to convey.

They have walked these streets for so long that the streets are in them. Not metaphorically. The paths of the settlement, the distances between buildings, the locations of the pipe-vents that exhale from below — all of this has been absorbed into the motor cortex at a depth where it is no longer knowledge and has become something prior to knowledge, something the body does the way the body maintains temperature, the way the body keeps the heart beating without instruction. They do not navigate the plateau. They *are* the plateau's navigation. They are the plateau knowing where its own parts are.

This is why they move without hesitation. They are not going somewhere new. They have never gone somewhere new, not in any personal memory that is accessible to them, and perhaps not in any ancestral memory that informs them below the level of personal access. They are going where they are going, and where they are going is a place they have been an exact number of times that is very large, and the motion is the motion of a number that is very large, which looks from the outside like the motion of a mechanism, like a bearing that has worn smooth, because that is what a very large number of repetitions eventually produces.

---

Do they speak?

There is, occasionally, sound from them.

Not language, not in the sense of sequences of sounds arranged to carry novel information from one mind to another. What there is instead is more like the machinery's hum: a low, steady acoustic output that conveys condition rather than content. A sound that says: present, stable, functioning within parameters. Sometimes two of them will produce sounds that correspond to one another — not in a conversational rhythm, not in the alternating structure of communication, but in a tuning-fork sense, one emitting a frequency and the other producing a sound that harmonizes with it, not as response but as resonance. They do not look at one another when this happens. It is not a social behavior in any sense that social usually means. It is more like a distributed system confirming that its components are still in communication, still sharing a phase.

There is one recorded instance — if record is the right word for what exists here, where recording requires intention and intention is a concept with complicated application — of two of them standing close together and producing a complex overlapping pattern of sounds that lasted perhaps fifteen minutes and then stopped, and neither of them moved during it, and afterward they continued in their separate directions without any sign of conclusion, as if the fifteen minutes had been a ceremony only in the sense that it had a duration and then ended, not in the sense of meaning anything that could be extracted and stated.

What this was cannot be said with confidence.

What most things about them are cannot be said with confidence, which is not a failure of investigation but a feature of the encounter. They are not hidden. They are visible, in the brownish light, moving their minimum-energy walk through the streets they have internalized. They are present and they are available to observation. But they are present in the way that the plateau is present — fully, without concealment, without depth strategically withheld — and the plateau, observed fully, still does not tell you what it is.

---

Their number.

This is the question that requires the most care.

There are six visible at any one time, or fewer. This seems like very few for a settlement this size. It is very few for a settlement this size. The buildings — their scale, their number, their arrangement — implies a population that once required them, or that anticipated requiring them, or that was once much larger and has become what it now is through a process that cannot be identified from the current state. Attrition is one explanation. Consolidation is another — perhaps the six, or the fewer, are what many eventually become when the long duration of this place does its work, when enough of whatever makes an individual an individual has been worn smooth that the question of how many there are becomes a question about how you count.

They do not reproduce visibly. There are no young of any apparent kind. No structures that suggest nursery-functions, no scaled-down items that would indicate accommodation of smaller or less capable versions of the form. This does not mean reproduction does not occur — absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and the timescale on which reproduction might occur here is not necessarily the timescale on which observation occurs — but it is consistent with a population in a late phase, or a population whose relationship to continuity is organized differently than reproduction in the conventional sense organizes it.

Perhaps they have each been here for a very long time.

Perhaps each of them has been here long enough that the plateau knows their footfall the way they know the plateau's streets — has registered them, has incorporated their presence into its condition, has done with them what it has done with the boulders, the slow rounding of all angles to their final and preferred form. Perhaps they are done, in the plateau's sense: not finished as in ended, but finished as in finished, arrived at the minimum-energy state from which no further change is energetically favorable.

Perhaps being done, in this sense, is its own kind of permanence.

---

What do they do?

The machinery. The hum below the feet, the pipes running building to building, the processing facilities whose product is not visible. Someone tends this. Or something does, but the something has their dimensions and their deliberate movement and their single-function presence in the spaces between buildings. They move through the settlement and when they enter a building they enter it in the way of maintenance — not arriving, not visiting, but interfacing with a system that requires their presence at intervals. They make adjustments. Not large adjustments. The adjustments they make are the adjustments of a system monitoring itself and finding small deviations from the stable state and returning the deviation to the stable state. The deviation is always small. It has always been small. The monitoring has occurred long enough that nothing large is ever permitted to develop, because the small precursor of the large is caught first, every time, for so long that the large has not occurred in what is functionally forever.

They are the plateau's maintenance. They maintain the sustained process. They do not know what the sustained process is for, because knowing what something is for implies a perspective outside the system, and they have not been outside the system for longer than outside has been a meaningful concept for them. They maintain it because it is what they do. They do it the way the heart beats: not because the heart has decided to beat, not because the heart is working toward a goal legible to the heart, but because it is what the heart is.

---

One thing more, observed once, not repeated in the time of the observation.

One of them stopped.

Not to rest — rest implies the recovery from exertion, and their movement produces no exertion that would require recovery. They simply stopped, in the space between two buildings, in the warm dim air, facing slightly upward — not toward the haze-ceiling, not at any angle that would suggest they were trying to see through it, but at an intermediate angle, the angle of someone looking at something that is neither here nor quite beyond reach. They stood this way for a duration that was long. The hum continued. The pipes continued. The warmth continued.

And there was something in the stopped posture that was not stillness.

It was the opposite of stillness, which is not movement — it was the quality of something held very carefully in place, the quality of attention so focused that the body has been set aside as irrelevant, the quality of a mind that has arrived at the edge of what can be thought about its own situation and is standing at that edge not with anguish and not with acceptance but with a third thing that does not have a name in any language developed by creatures who have not been on this plateau for as long as these have been on this plateau.

Then they continued walking.

In the direction they were going. At the same pace. Without any sign that the stopped moment had been anything other than what it appeared to be, which was everything, or nothing, or the same thing again that is always sustained from below and never resolves above and will hum at this frequency until the frequency is the only memory the plateau retains of having been inhabited at all.
