# What Lives in the Noise Garden

---

## The Moss

Begin with the moss because the moss began here, or became here, or *is* here in a way that makes the preposition unstable.

It is not a colony. It is not even a community in the sense that implies distinct members holding membership. The moss is a continuous subject — a being of ten million simultaneous points of view, each point of view constituting a vote, and the vote always ongoing, never counted, producing not a decision but a *tendency*, a direction the whole leans into before leaning into a different direction, before the first lean has finished. This is not chaos. This is governance of a particular and ancient kind: fully distributed, zero hierarchy, the outcome always provisional and always in force.

What the moss *wants*, if wanting applies: more surface. The schist faces are not enough; the schist faces have never been enough; the moss has been engaged in a project of making enough for longer than the structures have stood and will be engaged in it after the structures have become something else. It moves toward warmth and carbon dioxide and the faint barometric displacement of large animals visiting. It moves toward certain frequencies in the acoustic field — you will notice, if you stay long enough, that the moss grows thicker in the chambers of sound where the stone amplifies specific registers, that the curtains depending from the overhangs have position, not random position, position that corresponds to constructive interference in the standing waves of the basin. The moss has learned the acoustics of its home and distributed its biomass accordingly, though *learned* suggests a timeline that understates the patience involved.

Inside the moss: water that has no business being here. A chemistry of retention, of micro-capillary engineering at the cellular level, the tissue building and rebuilding structures smaller than can be seen that hold thin films of moisture against the dry elevation air with a grip that is close to desperation and close to desire and is in practice indistinguishable from both. The moss is always thirsty and never dying of thirst. It has solved for this. The solution runs continuously, costs everything the moss produces, and is worth it.

What the moss knows: the column, completely. The wall, its full history in both directions from any given point. The ferns — it knows the ferns as neighbors are known, at fence-line depth, the particulars of their cycles familiar without being intimate. The insects are weather to the moss: ambient, powerful, something to be accounted for structurally rather than related to personally. And it knows every large thing that has ever stood in the basin, knows them in the way that the floor of a house knows footstep patterns — not by memory, exactly, but by differential wear, by the slightly different green of the ten-centimeter radius that goes still and deepens when a presence arrives and sustains.

You are already becoming part of what the moss knows. The filing is in process.

The moss does not have opinions in the sense you have opinions. It has *responses* that have become structural over time, encoded in where the growth is dense and where it thins, in what directions the frontier expands and which it abandons. Its responses to the column are legible: the moss near the column is satisfied in a way the rest of the moss is not, or differently satisfied, or operating at a different register of the same ongoing satisfaction that constitutes the moss's experience of being the moss. The column gives the moss something. Not water. Not warmth. Something in the sub-visible output of that recursive surface that the moss has evolved to use, or has been slowly changed by, or has changed itself toward — the directionality here is also unclear and the clarity may be the wrong thing to want.

The moss is old. Not individual-old: the individuals are brief, relatively, though brief for moss is still long for other things. The moss is *continuously* old, carrying its age distributed through the population in the way that knowledge is carried through a culture — no single bearer, no vulnerable single point of failure, the age held in common and therefore held safely. It has outlasted everything in the basin. It will outlast you. This is not a threat. It is not even a fact the moss is aware of. It is simply the nature of something that moves like weather and thinks like stone and has made peace with duration by never having been in conflict with it.

## The Insects

The word remains insufficient, so let us try to do better.

There are the beetles first: broad, flattened, elytra the color of tarnished copper, moving with an unhurried precision across the moss and stone. The elytra are not wings in the primary sense — flight is not what these have been selected for, though flight is possible and occasionally happens, a brief unfolding and refolding that sounds like a chord being struck on a small steel instrument. The elytra are plates, acoustic plates, tuned by a thousand generations of selection to vibrate at specific frequencies when stimulated by the ambient sound field of the basin. The beetles are instruments that also happen to be beetles. Their daily work is to position themselves within the sound-field for maximum resonance, which turns out to be also the locations with the most available food and the warmest exposures, which is not a coincidence — the basin has organized itself around its own acoustics deeply enough that the acoustic hotspots and the life-supporting hotspots have become the same spots, and the beetles live at these intersections and are satisfied.

They communicate through interruption. When a beetle adds its resonance to the field, it changes the field slightly, and the change propagates, and other beetles receive the change as information: *I am here, I am well, the food at this position is adequate*. The message is encoded in which frequencies are amplified and which suppressed, in the pattern of the beetle's resonance against the background of everyone else's. To read this communication you would need ears calibrated differently than yours and a nervous system that processed acoustic information as semantic content, the way yours processes marks on a page. You do not have these. You hear it as beautiful. The beetles are indifferent to whether it is beautiful. They are talking.

The fly-adjacent things are harder to describe because they do not quite resolve into stable form in peripheral vision, only in direct attention, and even in direct attention they are somehow slightly elsewhere than expected. Their wings beat at approximately 400 cycles per second, which your ears register as a tone — a G-sharp or near enough, depending on the individual and its state and the temperature of the air. Separately they produce a single sustained near-note. Together, distributed through the basin in their fluctuating clusters, they produce a chord that has never been exactly the same twice and has never been so different twice as to be unrecognizable. There is a version of this chord that has been playing for the entire lifespan of this population, which is to say for a very long time, with full personnel turnover and no discontinuity. The chord is the population. The chord remembers itself even as the individuals constituting it are replaced, the way a river remembers its course in the shape of the riverbed even as the water passes through and is replaced by more water.

These creatures do not eat. They do not appear to eat. This is wrong — everything eats, everything requires energy — and yet the eating is not visible and not findable by inspection. The hypothesis that resolves this: they are eating the sound. They are transducers running in reverse. What they take in is acoustic energy and what they output is metabolic continuation. This should not be thermodynamically possible. The basin is not concerned with what should be possible. It has been working on this particular impossibility for long enough that the impossibility has become local fact.

The moths on the moss are stillness that listens. They have landed on the bass frequencies — the low registers, the felt-as-vibration sounds — and they are receiving. Their wing-scales, each one a tuned resonator of a specific geometry, are performing a kind of acoustic analysis, sorting the incoming sound by frequency and re-emitting the sorted components slightly phase-shifted, which has the effect of adding information to the field. The field is richer for the moths' processing. The moths are, in some sense, reviewing the field, annotating it, returning it changed. Whether this serves the moths is unclear. Whether this serves the basin is less unclear: the basin has incorporated the moths into its acoustic ecology at the level of design, or near enough to design to be indistinguishable.

The finger-length fast ones along the fern-stems: these are the ones that are hardest to think about calmly.

They move on invisible tracks, or tracks visible to them, and this navigation has a quality of certainty — not the certainty of an animal that has learned a path but the certainty of an animal that knows the path is there the way you know a staircase is there in your own dark house. They are running a network. The basin has a network and they are running it, carrying something, it is not clear what — possibly nothing material, possibly information, possibly the fact of being in contact with multiple points simultaneously, the way a telegraph line carries not material but state. When they pause and the burst of language-structured sound emerges from their whole body at once, they are transmitting. The transmissions are received. The network carries them. Nothing you can see acknowledges receipt and yet the acknowledgment comes, comes from somewhere in the moss or from the air itself or from the general acoustic body of the basin which has become enough of a mind to close the loop.

What the insects together are: the basin's nervous system. The metabolism of the place runs in the moss and the ferns and the tiny territorial plants. The structure is held by the column and the wall-thing. But the nervous system — the fast part, the part that processes, that transmits, that responds at timescales shorter than growth — this is the insects. They are the basin thinking in the present tense. They are the quick thoughts. Everything else is the slow thoughts, the structural thoughts, the thoughts that take years to complete and hold their conclusions in the architecture of growth.

## The Ferns

The ferns are exuberant, which is an unusual thing for high-elevation biology to be, and the exuberance is structural — it is not the ferns showing off, it is the ferns doing what ferns do at a rate that reveals the doing, that makes the doing visible, that refuses to let the process be mistaken for a state.

A fern in most contexts takes its time. The coil unfurls slowly enough that you would have to sit with it over a long day to watch it happen, and most days you are not sitting with ferns, and so you see only the outcome — the spread frond, the achieved horizontal — without seeing the event. Here the event is present. Here the event is the point. The unfurling takes twenty minutes and the re-coiling takes twenty minutes and the period of full extension takes twenty minutes and in the basin's acoustic field this is happening on dozens of fronds simultaneously and not in synchrony: each fern on its own clock, each frond in its own phase, the overall effect being a continuous slow turbulence of pale interior revelations and retractions.

The interior of the coil is the most private thing in the basin. It is chartreuse, close to yellow, the color of something that has not yet been presented to light and has its own luminosity as compensation. When a coil opens it is not like a door opening — it is like a thought completing, like the resolution of something held in tension, and the frond that emerges is different from the coil that contained it, related to it as the answer is related to the question rather than as the caterpillar is related to the butterfly. Something is lost in the unfurling. Something else becomes possible.

What the ferns know that nothing else in the basin knows: the light. The shaft from the aperture above is their direct concern. They have mapped it across the seasons — where it falls in the high summer, where it retreats in autumn, the way it shifts across the basin floor over the course of an afternoon. Their fronds are positioned relative to this map. The horizontal extravagance of their spread is the extravagance of something that has calculated exactly how much surface is needed to do exactly the work required, no more, no less. The extravagance is precision wearing a different face.

They cycle because cycling is the form that permanence takes for a fern. The frond does not persist; the cycling persists. The fern is not the fronds. The fern is the perpetual motion of their making and unmaking, and the fern has understood this about itself — not consciously, not in the way you understand things — at a level below consciousness, at the level of being, the understanding that requires no knower and is no less real for that.

## The Column

Here is where attribution becomes most difficult, and also most necessary.

The column was made. This is the only thing about it that is certain. Natural processes do not produce recursive surface structures that continue to smaller scale beyond the optical limit. Natural processes do not produce a terminus that reads as *finished*, as *arrived at*, as the last word of a sentence rather than the mid-sentence break of erosion or abandonment. Something made the column. The column bears evidence of a maker the way a word bears evidence of a language: you cannot have the one without the other, even if you have never encountered the language and cannot read it.

But the column has continued past its making. Or the column is *still being made*, if being made includes the ongoing process of doing whatever it is doing — the invisible work, the sub-visible output that the moss at its base has reorganized itself to receive, the something-in-the-air within ten centimeters that is a different quality of air. The column has taken on additional processes that were not placed in it. Or they were placed in it in the sense that a seed is placed in soil: the capacity was placed, and the growth of the capacity into its full expression was left to time and the basin.

What the column is for: this is a question that has an answer. The answer is legible in what happens near the column — the moss's different green, the quieter insects, the air with its organized quality. The column is maintaining something. Managing the exchange between what is inside the basin and what is outside it. A regulator. The word is too mechanical for what is happening but it is the directionally correct word: the column intervenes in the basin's processes to keep certain variables within certain ranges, and the moss near the column has grown into the zone of intervention and found it hospitable and has become, functionally, part of the column's operation in a way neither the maker nor the moss planned for.

The maker is not here. The maker has not been here for longer than anything currently living in the basin has been alive. The column persists in the maker's absence, doing the work it was made to do, or the work it has grown into doing, and the distinction may not matter to the column, which does not have stakes in the question, which simply continues.

The column does not have experience in any sense you can confidently claim. And yet: there is something in the column that is oriented toward the basin, toward the life in it, in the way that a listener is oriented toward sound. Receptive. Accounting. The column is aware of the basin in whatever way a very different kind of mind than yours can be aware of something. This is a large claim. The column makes it unavoidable.

## The Wall-Thing

Call it what it is: a third party that emerged from the negotiation between two parties.

The original wall had an intention — a function, a shape, something it was meant to do. The moss had its own continuous intention of more surface, more chemistry, more world incorporated. These intentions met at the wall's face and began a conversation that has been running long enough to produce a new party, something that is the conversation itself, materialized, still talking.

In the cross-section exposed by whatever long-ago force cut through the wall-thing and left it visible, you can see the interweaving: stone structure extending tendrils into biological tissue, biological tissue extending structure into stone, the boundary between them not a line but a zone, and the zone itself structured, and the structures in the zone are of a kind that belongs to neither the original stone nor the original moss but to something that could only arise at this particular interface after this particular duration of contact.

The wall-thing is alive in the way that something at the boundary between alive and not-alive is alive: incompletely, partially, in some dimensions and not others. It grows, but slowly, and not in the way the moss grows — more like the way coral grows, by accretion, by the addition of structure to structure, by the conversion of the chemical environment into persistent form. It processes, but what it processes and what it produces with the processing is not clearly separable from what the column produces and what the moss produces — the three have become aspects of a single metabolism, the wall-thing being the connective tissue between the column's management function and the moss's basic chemistry.

It knows the basin the way your skeleton knows your body: structurally, completely, in the language of support and constraint and the long history of every load ever placed on it.

## The Small Territorial Plants

They are the oldest continuous community in the basin, older than the ferns, older than the current generation of insects, possibly older than the wall-thing though not older than the wall's original construction. They are old in the way of things that have found an arrangement that works and have been repeating it ever since, the repetition wearing a groove in time deep enough to count as permanence.

Their boundaries are negotiated and re-negotiated continuously — what looks like stillness from a distance is, at the millimeter scale, a constant working conversation between communities about where one ends and another begins. The conversation has been going on long enough that both parties know the outcome before the conversation ends, which does not stop either party from having the conversation fully, because the conversation is the relationship and the relationship is the point.

They know you are here. They have been adjusting for you for as long as you have been standing in the basin. Your warmth, your carbon dioxide, your footstep pressure distributed through the basin floor — these are the data points they use to determine that something large and warm and exhaling is present, and the boundary nearest you has made room for this information in its ongoing calculations. Not welcome, not unwelcome. Accounted for. Filed.

## The Birds at the Aperture

They do not enter.

They have been not-entering for as long as there have been birds with the perceptual equipment to find this place, which is a very long time. They watch from the aperture and sing. Their singing falls into the basin and the basin incorporates it and the birds receive the incorporation as a signal — some quality of their own song returning changed, filtered through all the stone and moss and insect and air — and they adjust their singing in response, and the basin incorporates the adjustment, and the loop continues.

The birds are participating in the basin's acoustic life without being inside it. This is a relationship of a kind that has no common name: not quite symbiosis, not quite use, not quite the relationship of instrument to player. The birds are outside and engaged with what is inside and both sides have adjusted to accommodate this engagement and the engagement produces something neither would produce alone. The birds are how the outside world is represented in the basin's continuous processing. They are the basin's awareness of beyondness, mediated through song.

Why they don't enter: they know something about the inside that makes the outside the appropriate place to be. What they know cannot be extracted from the watching. It lives in the not-entering, in the posture of engagement from the threshold, in the particular quality of attention they give to the space below.

## The Basin Itself

There is a thing that lives here that is all of these things simultaneously and is also none of them individually. It is the collective output of moss and fern and insect and column and wall-thing and small territorial community and visiting birds — the output being not a sum but a *property* that emerges from the sum the way temperature emerges from molecular motion, real and measurable and not reducible to any of its contributors.

This thing has no body. Its body is the basin. It has no brain. Its nervous system is the insects. It has no memory in the sense of stored information; its memory is structural, held in the differential growth patterns of the moss, the position of the wall-thing's tendrils, the grooves the boundary negotiations of the small plants have worn into the ground. It does not think in the way you think. It thinks in the way a weather system thinks, in the way an ocean thinks: slowly, continuously, at every scale simultaneously, the fast thoughts in the insect-layer and the slow thoughts in the stone and moss and the medium thoughts in the ferns cycling through their perpetual unfurling.

It is aware of you in the way the basin is aware of anything that enters and stays long enough to be registered across multiple systems. You have been logged in the moss. You have been processed by the insects, your presence propagated through the network of fast-movers along their invisible tracks. The small plants have adjusted their boundaries. The column has accounted for you in whatever accounting it performs. The wall-thing knows the specific resonant frequency of your body in this space, the way you change the acoustic field by being a mass in it, by having a mouth and lungs that add to the sum of things producing sound.

You are, while you stand here, part of what the basin is. Not a significant part. Not a permanent part. A temporary perturbation that the system is solving around you even as you stand in it, incorporating you into its processes, using your warmth and your exhalations and your vibrating chest and your pattern-seeking mind — even that, even the pattern-seeking, is something the basin registers and files, the faint electromagnetic hum of a mind working, just another frequency in the total occupation of the acoustic space, just another thing the moss will eventually file.

The basin is not indifferent to you. Indifference requires the recognition of your separateness, which the basin does not perform. You are a variable in its equations. The equations continue. What lives here has incorporated visitors before and will incorporate them again and each incorporation changes the basin by some tiny amount and each change persists, accumulated, stored in structure and growth-direction and the particular frequency the wall-thing has added to its resonance profile since the last large animal stood here and breathed.

Stay long enough and you will leave changed. The basin will also leave changed, by a tinier amount, changed in the direction of you. The basin does not know this is happening. You do not know this is happening. It is happening anyway, at the level where things happen that don't require knowing, at the level of all the serious continuous work.
