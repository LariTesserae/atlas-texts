# What Lives in the Murmuring Flat

---

Begin with what they are not, because that is how they first present themselves — as a series of failed recognitions.

They are not insects. You will think this first, and hold to it for longer than the evidence supports, because *insect* is the category your mind reaches for when it confronts small, moving, many-legged things in numbers too large to count. But the category won't hold. The legs are wrong — not the wrong number, though the number is wrong too, but wrong in their quality, wrong in their relationship to the body, which is not a body that needs legs in the way a body needs legs. The legs are more like cilia made structural, like the hairs of some organism's interior surface turned outward and load-bearing. They do not so much walk as — ripple. The whole underside of each small thing ripples, in a wave-motion that is coordinated without being synchronized, that produces motion the way a shoreline produces waves: not from a single impulse but from the aggregate of many small impulses in a medium that transmits and transforms them.

They are not crustaceans. They have, some of them, hardened upper surfaces — not shells, exactly, not the calcium architecture of things that live in oceans, but a kind of densified integument, a thickening of the outer layer that serves some of the functions a shell serves without being one. These surfaces are the ones that scatter the faint light. Under that light, in your eyes' adjusted state, they read as a dark gray with occasional points of something almost silver — not iridescence, not quite, but the flat mineral gleam of a wet stone turned at the right angle. This is how they disappear: by being the color and texture of the place they inhabit so completely that the distinction between organism and substrate becomes a matter of motion rather than of matter.

They are not related to anything that lives where you come from. This is the thing you will arrive at last, and then you will understand the failures of recognition not as failures but as information.

---

The smallest ones first, because there are the most of them, and the most of them is what most of the place *is*.

They are the size of a grain of rice, or smaller. They are the size of something smaller that you can only perceive as motion at the edge of your vision — a trembling in the substrate, a shimmer that is the aggregate of many individual small crossings. Up close, if you go down on your knees on the damp tile and bring your face near the surface and let your eyes work in the low light, you can see them individually, and what you see individually is: a body that is more or less oval, flattened against the tile, with the rippling underlayer and the slightly thickened upper integument, and no visible head, no visible sensory structures you can identify, no visible differentiation between the end that faces forward and the end that does not — except that there is a forward, there is direction, there is something they are moving toward or something they are moving away from, and they know which.

What do they eat? The question implies that eating is the right frame. They process: that's closer. They pass over the tile surface and the tile surface is infinitesimally different after their passage — depleted of some surface chemistry, some mineral bloom, some condensed atmospheric compound that has settled there in the slow wet air. They are, in aggregate, a process that the tile surface undergoes. They have been undergoing the tile surface for long enough that the tiles themselves have been shaped by them — their surfaces smoother along the lines of heaviest traffic, their upper edges beveled by ten thousand crossings of ten million bodies over more time than the word *long* adequately contains.

This is one of the ways the place is not separable into parts. The organisms and the substrate are in conversation that has been going on so long it has become reciprocal, structural, mutual. The tile cracks where they crack partly because of where the small things move. The small things move where they move partly because of where the cracks are. The equilibrium has equilibrated so deeply that you cannot find an origin point, cannot say which came first, cannot locate the outside of the system from any position within it.

They do not die visibly. This must be said. There are no bodies. There is no accumulation of the dead. The dead are returned to the system by the system, processed in the same way the tile surface is processed, and this is not death in any way that produces grief or vacancy — it is more like a state change, a phase transition, the moment a solid becomes a liquid becomes something that does not have a phase. They are the size of grains of rice and they live and die and are incorporated and the place continues.

---

The mid-sized ones — the thumb-sized, the hand-sized, the ones you can track individually across the tile — are more various. This is unexpected, given the uniformity of the environment: you would expect uniformity to produce uniformity. Instead the mid-sizes have diversified into a range of forms that, while all sharing the flat-bodied, ripple-locomoting, light-scattering general plan, differ from each other in ways that suggest long separation into local conditions. Some have what might be feeding structures at their forward ends — not mouths, nothing so legible as a mouth, but a differentiation of the integument at that end into something denser, more active, more engaged with the surfaces they move over. Some carry, on their upper surfaces, small irregular structures that might be accretions from the environment or might be produced by the organism — you cannot tell, and the fact that you cannot tell is itself a characteristic of the place.

Some of them are pale — nearly white in the dim light, which makes them briefly visible before you understand that their paleness is not a failure of pigmentation but a different kind of camouflage, or a different relationship to the light, or something functional that you don't have the framework to name. Some of them carry smaller things on or near their bodies, in arrangements that might be predation or parasitism or mutualism or accident — the relationships between them are not legible at your scale of observation and in your span of time.

What they share: the direction. Whatever they are following — whatever trace in the condensation, whatever chemical residue in the tile surface, whatever vibration in the silt — they follow it with a consistency that looks like purpose from outside and probably isn't purpose in any way purpose means to you, but that produces the same result purpose would produce: a thing that goes somewhere, that keeps going there, that arrives.

---

The largest ones — the forearm-length bodies, moving low, clearing a wake in the smaller things — are different in kind from the others, not just degree.

They are not simply bigger versions of the thumb-sized ones. Something happened, in whatever time produced them, that went beyond scaling. The largest ones are present in a way the others are not. This is not a mystical claim; it is a phenomenological one. Standing near one, you are aware of it in the way you are aware of a large animal even when your back is turned — some registration below the visual, some pattern in the air pressure or the vibration or the warmth that tells you: a complex thing is here.

Their bodies are longer and their locomotion is different — not the pure ripple but something that involves more of the body in sequence, a lateral undulation that travels from the forward end rearward in a slow wave, as if the body is not a rigid platform but a medium through which motion passes. They move through the small things the way weather moves through weather: present, patterned, displacing. The bow-wave of cleared space ahead of them and the filled-back wake behind them suggest that the small things read their approach as something to be avoided without quite reading it as predation — there is no panic in the clearing, just a flowing-aside, the way a crowd parts for something large and indifferent.

And they are indifferent. To the small things, certainly. To you. They move past you in the dimness and their heads — if the forward end is a head, if *head* applies — are down, and they do not acknowledge you, and their bodies pass within the length of your arm without reaction. The indifference is complete and old and total. It is not the indifference of something that has decided to ignore you. It is the indifference of something that does not contain the cognitive structure that would make ignoring a decision you need to make. You are not in their category of things that can be relevant.

What do they follow, those paths in the condensation, those trails in the chemical surface of the tile? The paths are real — you can see, after watching long enough, that multiple large ones will travel the same routes, that there are highways of a kind laid down in the substrate. Something produces the paths and something reads them, and whether producer and reader are the same kind of thing or different kinds of things, and whether the paths are intentional or incidental, remains inaccessible to you. The large ones move as if drawn forward by something at the end of the path, as if the path itself has a kind of gravity that operates at the scale of things that can follow it.

Some of them are heading for the shallow water-lens in the middle distance. You understand this eventually — watching the directions of travel converge, watching the density of large things increase in a particular radial corridor. At the water's edge, they slow. They enter the water in a way that looks, briefly, like ceremony — nothing about it is ceremony, you know this, but something about the deliberateness of their slowing, the way they pause at the transition between tile-surface and water-surface, gives the entry a quality that your mind reaches for a ritual frame to hold. They enter the water and they slow more and they stop, in the shallow centimeters, and they are still in a way they have not been still on the tile, and what is happening in that stillness — feeding, metabolizing, exchanging something with the water or with the other large still things around them — is not visible at your resolution.

The water trembles with them. The V-shapes on its surface are made by their bodies, or by parts of their bodies you couldn't see on the tile, or by other things that live in the water that you haven't been able to distinguish from the general complexity of the place. The interference pattern on the water's surface is the record of their being there in a form that you can read, finally, because your eyes have learned, somewhat, to read this place.

---

Now: what they are, collectively. What the whole system is, taken together.

The place is not a community, in the sense that community implies individual beings with interests who have found an accommodation. The place is more like an organ. Every part of it is doing what every other part of it requires, and has been doing so for long enough that the doing has become structural, has become what the parts *are* rather than what the parts *do*. The small things process the tile. The mid-sized things process something the small things produce or leave behind. The large things do something at the water-lens that changes the water's chemistry, which changes what the small things can extract from the condensed air, which changes what accumulates on the tile surface, which changes what the small things process. The water connects to the crack-network. The crack-network is shaped by the movement of small things. The movement of small things is shaped by the paths the large things lay down. The paths the large things lay down are shaped by the crack-network.

There is no center to this. There is no origin. There is only the whole, running, having been running, the sound of everything running simultaneously filling the air from the sternum outward.

---

What they know, if they know anything, is the place.

Not in the way you know a place — not as an outside observer who has mapped and categorized and stands apart from what is mapped. They know it the way a root system knows its soil: by being in contact with all of it all the time, by having every function shaped by it and every structural decision made in response to it over more generations than the word *generations* can hold. They know where the condensation is thickest. They know the crack-depth at every junction of the network. They know the rhythm of wetting and near-drying — not as knowledge, not as anything they could report, but as the thing that shaped every heritable characteristic they carry, so that the knowledge is not in their minds but in their bodies, not learned but structural.

They know each other this way too, or something like knowing — they know the chemical signatures, the vibration patterns, the wake and bow-wave and disturbance of each other's passage. The large ones know the small ones exist the way your hand knows air exists: by constant contact that has become so foundational it is not contact anymore, just the medium in which existence occurs.

And they do not know you. Cannot know you. Your particular kind of complexity, your kind of interior, the specific sensory world you carry — none of this registers in the information-space they inhabit. You are a large warm displacement to some of them. To most, you are less than that: you are a local variation in the vibration of the silt, a shadow that isn't regular enough to matter, an anomaly the system bends around without registering.

This is their world: fully known, in their way of knowing, to its furthest tile-crack. And yours is inaccessible to them, entirely, in the same way that theirs is inaccessible to you — not because either world is deficient, but because the gap between kinds of knowing is itself a kind of thing, a space with its own character, its own particular silence that is not silence at all but only the sound of two different systems running at frequencies too far apart to hear each other.

---

And yet: that high reedy call, almost harmonic, that surfaces every few minutes from somewhere out in the middle distance and is absorbed back into the larger noise.

Something makes it. Something in the middle of all this makes it. You will not find what, if you go looking — the place will continue around you and the call will continue surfacing and you will continue not being able to locate it. But it is made. It is produced by some part of this system, this organ, this old and indifferent and complete and utterly inhabited place, and it surfaces periodically into the air as if the place is checking something, confirming something, as if somewhere in all this density and all this settled equilibrium and all this mutual running of interlocked systems there is something that occasionally needs to make a sound that travels up out of the layered noise and hangs for a moment in the air and then returns.

You will not know why. The place will not tell you. It will continue being what it is — which is this, all of this, exactly this, in every direction to the smeared horizon — and the call will surface again, and you will hear it, and you will feel in your sternum the bone-conducted hum of everything else, and in the air above the dark tiles the very faint aggregate glimmer of ten million light-scattering surfaces moving, and you will understand that you are not being spoken to, that you were never being spoken to, that the call is part of the system the way everything else is part of the system, not aimed at you, not aimed at anything, just produced, just sent up, just a sound the place makes because the place makes it.

And that will be, in its way, an answer.
