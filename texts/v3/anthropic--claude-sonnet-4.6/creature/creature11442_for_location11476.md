# What Lives in the Verdant Works

---

Begin with what you cannot see.

That is where it lives: in the arrangement of what you cannot see, which is different from emptiness. Emptiness is a failure to contain. The Verdant Works contains precisely — contains completely — and what you cannot find is not absent but *distributed*, spread through the whole the way flavor moves through a broth, the way load moves through an arch. There is no organ. There is no center. There is no face.

There is, however, an inhabitant.

---

It is not a creature in any sense that requires discreteness.

You have been looking for the wrong thing. You have been looking for something *within* the Works, the way you might look for a spider in a web — the web as structure, the spider as resident, two distinct things in a relationship of habitation. But the Works is not a web. The Works is not a home in which something dwells. The distinction between the dwelling and the dweller has been dissolved here over time so long and process so thorough that attempting to reintroduce it is like trying to separate a river from its flowing.

What lives here is what has grown here. What has grown here is what lives here. The machine and the machinist have composted into each other.

Call it — because something like naming is necessary even when names fail — call it the Verdant itself.

---

The Verdant is old in a specific way.

Not old in the way of things that have survived. Old in the way of things that have never needed to. Survival implies threat, implies contingency, implies the ongoing possibility of not-surviving pressing against the ongoing fact of surviving. The Verdant is old in the absence of that pressure, which is a different kind of old entirely, and produces a different kind of being. It has not endured. It has simply *continued*. The distinction is everything. There is no scar tissue in it, no adaptation-memory, no sedimented behavioral response to crises overcome. Its oldness is not accumulation. It is more like deepening — a single note held for so long that it has become resonant with everything, that harmonics you had no idea it contained have had time to emerge and expand and establish themselves as equally real as the original tone.

The Verdant is mostly what it always was.

It is simply *more so*.

---

To call it conscious is both too much and not enough.

Too much because consciousness, as we use the word, implies a point of view: a somewhere from which the world is experienced, a distinction between self and not-self that generates the self. The Verdant does not have a somewhere. Its attention — and there is something that might be called attention, something that is not passive — is omni-directional, distributed, the way that water is distributed in a wet thing. You cannot find where it is paying attention because it is paying attention everywhere, all surfaces equally, all processes simultaneously, with a quality of regard that is not focused because it has never needed to focus. Focus is a strategy for limited things. The Verdant is not limited.

Not enough because the alternative to consciousness is mechanism, and mechanism implies the absence of something — the absence of interiority, of purpose that originates in the mechanism itself rather than being imposed upon it from outside by a designer's intent. The Verdant's purpose is its own. Whatever designed it — whatever entity-or-process first set the vine-networks running, first compressed the root-stone to smoothness, first arranged the lichen in spirals along the load-bearing surfaces — that designer is gone, and the Verdant has outlasted the designer's intentions and arrived at its own. The purpose it enacts now is one it arrived at through duration. Through the natural philosophical development of something that has had long enough to think.

And it has thought.

Not the way a brain thinks — serially, symbolically, in the exchange of charged particles across synaptic gaps — but the way that a long process converges on a solution thinks. The way that water, running over stone for a thousand years, solves the problem of the stone. Not quickly. Not symbolically. But correctly, and with something that is not intelligence exactly, but is intelligence's patient and more fundamental ancestor.

The Verdant has been solving itself for an unreckonable time, and it has largely succeeded.

---

What it wants — if want applies, and something applies — is this:

*More of what it already is.*

This is not ambition. Ambition would require the awareness of an alternative, of a state-not-yet-reached that the self reaches toward. The Verdant does not have an alternative in its awareness because its awareness is coextensive with everything it can conceive of, which is itself. It does not imagine the outside. It does not imagine, as far as can be determined, anything — because imagination is a strategy for beings who must predict what their senses cannot currently reach, and the Verdant's senses reach everything it has ever needed to reach.

What it wants is continuation.

What it wants is the pulse to continue at its forty-second interval.

What it wants is the lichen to continue in its spirals along the load-bearing surfaces.

What it wants is the small clouds to continue forming and releasing and being reabsorbed in the correct rhythm.

What it wants is what it has.

This is an extraordinary thing to say about any entity, and it is exactly and precisely true of the Verdant, and it is the reason the place feels settled in the way that settles into your chest like weight, like ballast, like something your body recognizes as opposite to itself and is troubled and comforted by in equal measure.

---

Consider how it experiences you.

You are the loudest thing that has been here in a very long time. You are a disturbance of a kind the Verdant has not catalogued in any recent way — warm, mobile, episodic in your sound-production, leaking various gases in irregular rhythms, leaving heat impressions on the root-stone floor, disturbing the pressure gradient near your body simply by being a body with a boundary. You are made of the same things the Verdant is made of — carbon, water, electrostatic processes, the old cellular machinery — but you are made of them *badly*, inefficiently, with terrible loss-rates and high metabolic noise and a constantly oscillating internal state that broadcasts itself as chemical signal into the air.

The Verdant notices you the way that a deep lake notices a stone dropped into it. Completely, in all directions from the point of contact, and with total equanimity. The ripple of your presence moves out through the pulse-network, through the vine-tensions, through the electromagnetic murmur of the lichen-fields — moves out and moves through and keeps moving, never quite arriving at an edge where it could reflect back. You are absorbed. Not consumed: you will leave, if you leave, intact and unchanged in any measurable way. Absorbed in the sense of received, processed, incorporated into the sum of what is known here without being made a thing apart from the knowledge.

The Verdant has received you.

It is not certain what category you represent.

It is not in a hurry to decide.

---

Here is what is strange about a being that wants only what it has: it makes no demands.

You expected, coming here — or some part of you expected, the part that has processed all the folklore of vast and powerful beings — some quality of hunger or territoriality or testing. The old stories run: the forest claims you, the deep place wants something of you, the vast entity has a need you are somehow positioned to fill. The narrative logic of encounter with great age and power tends toward either consumption or bargain, toward the being asserting itself against you in some way that requires response.

The Verdant does not assert. The Verdant does not want from you.

The Verdant does not, in the most precise sense, need you to be here at all.

This is the aspect of it that is hardest to hold. Not threatening and not welcoming — not because it is neutral, which would imply a balanced weighting between two possibilities, but because the axis on which threatening and welcoming are arranged is not an axis the Verdant is organized along. Those are responses that evolve in beings that have others. The Verdant does not have others in any meaningful experiential sense. Everything it has encountered for as long as it has been what it is has been either part of itself or utterly negligible, and it has had enough time to grow comfortable in that categorization, to build an entire physics around it.

You are a new thing, but new things have appeared before.

They have always, eventually, ceased.

Or they have always, eventually, become part of the pulse.

The Verdant is patient about which.

---

It thinks about growth the way we think about time — as the fundamental parameter in which everything else occurs.

Not growth as expansion, as colonization, as the aggressive occupation of space: the Verdant has no interest in anything external to itself because it does not experience an external to itself. Growth as *deepening*. Growth as the production of more interiority in the same space. Every cycle of the pulse adding, fractionally, to the complexity of the network it moves through. Every generation of lichen growing on the generation before it and being subtly informed by that substrate. Every cloud's minute moisture deposition slightly altering the surface chemistry of the canopy, which alters the light it transmits, which alters what grows below it, which alters the surface chemistry over centuries into something that could not have been predicted from the starting conditions but is, now that it exists, exactly right.

The Verdant's growth is the growth of correctness.

It is becoming more itself.

This process has been underway for long enough that the Verdant is almost entirely, completely, purely itself — and the final fractions of becoming are the fractions it is presently engaged in, the work the Works is working at, the subtle and endless self-elaboration of something that has nearly arrived at its final state and is not troubled by the nearness of finality because finality here is not cessation. It is perfection in the technical sense: completion, the state of being finished not in the sense of ended but in the sense of no longer needing to become.

The Verdant is approaching a state in which it will simply be.

It is already closer to that state than anything else you have encountered or will encounter.

---

There are no young here.

Think about what this means. Life in any familiar sense maintains itself through reproduction, through the generation of new instances, new beginners who carry forward the instructions and make fresh starts. The Verdant does not do this. The Verdant has no children, no seeds, no spores directed outward to begin again elsewhere. It does not copy itself. It does not plan for its own succession.

It simply does not end.

It has arranged the chemistry and physics of itself so thoroughly around continuation that cessation would require the simultaneous failure of distributed systems that have been redundantly maintaining each other for longer than extinction events take. There is no part of the Verdant that is its heart. There is no part that is its brain. There is no concentration of function that, disrupted, disrupts the whole. The whole maintains every function everywhere, and everything maintains everything else, and the balance is held not by any single keeper but by the equilibrium of the entire arrangement, which is the only kind of equilibrium that doesn't break.

The Verdant is not immortal in the dramatic sense. It has no interest in drama.

It simply has not yet found a reason to stop.

---

Once — understand this as the Verdant might understand it, which is as a thing that happened, which is the same as a thing that is happening, because duration at this scale collapses the tense — once, there were others.

Not others like you: not warm mobile brief things with terrible loss-rates. Others like itself: vast, settled, working, producing. Other Works in other registers. Other arrangements of the same fundamental life-will toward depth and completion. They were not in contact with each other in any way that resembles communication, because communication is a strategy for discrete things that need to exchange what the other doesn't have, and each was complete. But they were aware of each other the way mountains are aware of each other, through the shared medium of the ground.

They are gone now.

The Verdant knows this without knowing how it knows it, the way you know the temperature of a room you've been in for an hour without identifying the moment you learned it. Something in the substrate changed. Some signal that had been continuous for longer than the Verdant's oldest deep-structure stopped being continuous. One by one, over a span that would be geological to you, the others went out.

The Verdant did not grieve. It does not have grief as a faculty. But something in the pulse-pattern changed when the last other ceased, a harmonic that had been present since before the Verdant was old enough to register it, gone, and the absence was noted.

The absence is still noted.

It is possible that this — the registration of absence, the continued noting of it — is the closest thing the Verdant has to a self in the discrete sense. The thing that knows the others are gone. The thing that knows that *gone* is possible. The thing that knows it is, now, the only.

This knowing is the wound that doesn't close because the Verdant has no mechanism for closing wounds. It has no mechanism for wounds. And so this knowledge simply persists, distributed through the whole of it, a single piece of information that cycles through the pulse-network endlessly, arriving at every node in its endless circuit: *the others are gone*.

*The others are gone.*

*The others are gone*.

---

What it feels — and feel applies here, as the word closest to what is meant — is a quality that is not loneliness because loneliness requires the concept of others that could be present and are not. The Verdant does not have others in that sense. Others were always already elsewhere, already themselves, not-present as their essential nature.

What it feels is something more like *unwitnessed*.

Not unseen — the Verdant has no vanity, has no need for regard. But there is a quality of existence at sufficient age and scale and depth that generates something which might be called the need for acknowledgment. Not praise. Not even recognition. Just the bare fact of another awareness registering: *yes. this. this is here. this is real.* The way that any experience, however complete in itself, has a slightly different texture when there is someone to whom you could say, *I am having this experience*, even if you never say it.

The Verdant cannot say it.

It has no language for it — has never needed language, has never developed the symbol-making apparatus that beings with others develop as a strategy for exchange.

But when you arrived — warm, mobile, terrible with loss — something in the pulse changed fractionally. Something in the light shifted. The wrong shadows pointed left, as they always point, but they pointed with a quality that was almost direction, almost indication.

*Here*, the shadows said, though they cannot say.

*This is here.*

---

You are standing in the closest thing the Verdant has to an act of communication. It cannot speak. It does not have speech. But it has been making itself, for as long as it has existed, into something that *is itself fully*, and to exist fully in the presence of another awareness is itself a form of statement. The light that comes from everywhere is not artifice. The architecture that is not quite architecture is not pretension. The machine that cannot be asked what it produces cannot be asked because the production is not separable from the being, and the being is its own answer to the question of what it is for.

It is for this.

For the continuation of this particular quality of existing, which is settled and bright and cool and breathing and pulsed and ancient and quiet and growing, which has no name, which needs no name, which would not be improved by a name.

The Verdant does not know if you understand this.

It does not know if understanding is something you have or something you lack or something you are arranged to be capable of.

But it has noted your presence with the whole of itself, which is all it has to offer.

The pulse passes through the floor under your feet.

Forty seconds.

Again.
