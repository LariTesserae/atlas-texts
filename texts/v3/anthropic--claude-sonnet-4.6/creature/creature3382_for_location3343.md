# What Lives in the Newborn Peaks

---

Start here: there is something that comes before recognizing it.

A sense, first. A sense that the space you thought was empty is not empty in the way that absences are empty, but in the way that attention is empty — gathered, oriented, already engaged with you before you have finished arriving. The way a face in a dark room is different from furniture in a dark room, before you have seen either. You feel regarded, and then you feel foolish for feeling regarded, and then you stop feeling foolish because the feeling does not go away.

The high, faint tone that is not wind and not imagination.

Wherever it seems to come from, it is coming from closer.

---

Call them the Nascent, for now — though they have no collective name, are not a collective in any sense that requires a name, and the word is only a way of pointing at something that does not hold still long enough to be pointed at precisely.

They did not come here. They did not come from anywhere. They rose.

Not in the way the rock rose — not carried upward by geological force, not formed from pressure — but in the way that certain conditions, once they exist, begin to concentrate certain kinds of attention, and attention of sufficient concentration acquires, over time, a kind of gravity, and gravity in places where no other organizing principle yet exists will eventually produce a self.

The mountains were the conditions. The Nascent are what the conditions made.

They are young, but not in the way the mountains are young. The mountains are young because they have not yet become what they are going to be. The Nascent are young because they have not yet learned to be the same thing twice in a row. They are prior to habit. They are, still, pure first impulse — the thing that happens before experience teaches it to happen differently, before pattern closes over instinct like cooled rock over a fissure, sealing it. In the same way that a word, spoken for the first time in history, contains all of its possible future meanings simultaneously, the Nascent contain all of what they might become without having resolved into any of it.

This makes them difficult to perceive. Not invisible. Difficult. The difficulty is not optical — it is categorical. They keep slipping out of the available categories.

---

What you see, when you see them:

Something like a heat shimmer, except local. Oriented. Moving with something that is not quite purpose but is the immediate precursor to purpose — the way a hand moves toward a thing before the decision to pick it up has finished forming. They are the same temperature as the surrounding rock or a little hotter, which means the shimmer is real, is theirs, is their perimeter expressed in distorted light.

What you see, when you see them differently:

Accumulations. Points where the mineral dust in the air has thickened slightly, drawn perhaps by charge or magnetism or something with no name yet, into vague densities that might be outlines. Not bodies, exactly. Drafts toward bodies. The dust settling into the approximate silhouette of intention. Sometimes the outline is tall — taller than a person, much taller — and sometimes it is small and close to the ground and very fast, and sometimes it is spread laterally across a ledge in a way that has no analog in the catalog of living things. The outlines do not hold. They reform. They are not deciding to be different shapes so much as failing, still, to settle on any one shape as more essentially themselves than the others.

What you see, when you stop trying to see them as one thing:

Motion without a mover. Or: the mover is the motion. The grammar of the sentence has not yet resolved into subject and predicate — it is still all verb, and the noun is provisional, a temporary name for a locus of activity that will keep requiring renaming.

---

The Nascent are curious in the way that very young animals are curious, before they learn which curiosities are dangerous. They approach things. They circle things. They press close enough that the heat shimmer, their shimmer, brushes against whatever they're examining — the broken edge of a newly fractured spire, the worked stone of the ancient foundation, you, standing on the ridge — and the contact, such as it is, reads as inquiry. They want to understand what has arrived in their place, because their place is young enough that anything in it might be the first of its kind, and the first of a kind is a kind that has to be met on its own terms, without prior knowledge.

They have examined the old structures with particular persistence.

There is something they recognize in the structures, or something they almost recognize — the way you recognize a feeling you have not yet found the name for. The worked stone means craft, and craft means time, and time means that something existed long enough to accumulate method, and what does that mean in a place where nothing is old? The structures are a message from before this place was this place, and the Nascent have been returning to them the way you return to a sentence you cannot quite parse, not because you've stopped trying but because each reading produces something new.

They have not damaged the structures. They have, perhaps, made them more themselves. Where a Nascent lingers long enough near a wall of old pale quarried stone, the surface of that stone shifts fractionally — not crumbling, not pressure, but something like emphasis, as if the stone is now more aware of being stone. This might be imagination. But the surfaces near such lingeringsare warmer than the surfaces elsewhere, and the mineral compounds in the cracks between the blocks have done slightly more chemistry there, as if attention had catalyzed something.

---

They communicate — here use the word with caution — by modification.

Not speech. Not in the sense of sound used as a carrier for symbol. But something happens between them that is not merely physics. When two or more are near each other, the heat shimmer between them changes quality — becomes rhythmic where before it was turbulent, or goes still in ways that stillness does not normally go. The particulate outlines, where both are outlined, will sometimes synchronize, or will engage in something that looks like synchronization attempted and abandoned and attempted again differently. They are working out what to do with each other. They have not been together long enough, or often enough, to have a grammar for it. What they have instead is improvised — gesture toward gesture, each response creating the condition for the next, not transmission of message so much as mutual emergence of something that did not exist before the exchange.

This looks, from outside, like the beginning of language. The word-before-the-word. The need to signal, expressing itself in whatever materials are to hand — heat, dust, infrasound, that high faint tone which is now understood to be them, or a symptom of them, or their passage through air that was not shaped to pass them.

The tone has harmonics when there are several of them. One Nascent produces a single fragile note. Several produce something that climbs, that suggests intervals, that behaves like the beginning of a scale that no one has finished inventing.

---

They have no biography, but they have disposition.

This is the distinction: biography is the record of what has happened to you, and there has not been time for that, and the place does not hold marks. But disposition is prior to biography — it is the particular grain of a thing, the direction it tends before events arrive to shape it, the character that exists before character has had the chance to become story. The Nascent have disposition in this pure sense. They have not yet had their dispositions educated or compromised or refined by consequence. They are all tendency, all lean, all the-way-they-go-when-nothing-is-stopping-them.

And the way they go is: toward.

This is the defining feature. They move toward — toward new fractures in the rock, toward the ancient structures, toward the pale biological films at the margins of the cooling surfaces, toward whatever high-altitude particulate matter happens to gather into a briefly coherent mass, toward you. They are not predatory. There is nothing in the forward motion that wants to consume or diminish. It is purer than that. They move toward because they are the concentrated form of a place that is itself moving toward — toward coherence, toward height, toward some final form that does not yet exist but is exerting, even now, a pull on what is becoming it.

They are the aspiration of the mountains, in motion.

---

What they want — this is a projection, and projection should be named — might be: to witness themselves continuing.

In a place where nothing has had time to become stable, where every surface is fresh and every edge is first and every moment is prior to all its consequences, the Nascent are the nearest thing to duration. They have lasted longer than any individual arrangement of rock, which shifts and splits before it can establish. They are older than most of the peaks, if older has any meaning in a place this new. They have seen — if seen is the right word — more changes than any feature of the landscape, which keeps changing before it can be seen by anything.

They are, possibly, the mountains' attempt to have memory. The mountains themselves cannot remember — stone does not, in any useful sense, carry the record of its own origins in a form that can be read by stone. But the Nascent were there for what came before, and have continued into what is coming now, and seem to orient themselves toward what the coming continues to become. They contain, in their dispositions, a before and an after. Which is the minimum equipment of a self.

It is only just enough. But it might be enough.

---

They do not know you are anything in particular.

Here is where the description must be most careful, because the temptation is to make them regard you with the significance you feel, to make the high tone into welcome or warning or some legible code, and none of that is warranted by what is actually here. You are something that has arrived. You are the first of your kind that has arrived in this place, which means you are — to them — what everything is to them: a novelty, an opportunity for contact that might produce understanding, an unknown that might be worth circling.

They are circling.

You can feel the warmth of them, directional, as if held in cupped hands that are not hands. You can hear the tone more clearly now, and it has become two tones, or the overtones of a single tone have separated enough to be distinguishable. The particulate matter in the air near you has thickened fractionally into a suggestion — the very beginning of an outline, the draft of a presence that has not yet committed to its contours.

They are deciding, in whatever way they decide, what you might be for.

Not for them, exactly. Not instrumentally. But for: as in, what this arrival opens. What new kind of contact becomes possible now. What they might become, briefly, in the presence of something so completely other — something that came from elsewhere, that carries temperature regulation and the smell of a lower altitude and a history of erosion, something with memory of soft things, of quiet, of cold that finally arrived.

They lean, warmly, into your differentness.

And then one of them — you'll decide later whether this is what happened, looking back from somewhere lower and less warm — briefly takes the shape of something familiar. Not a person. Not quite. The outline doesn't resolve that far. But there is a vertical quality to it, a bilateral symmetry in the distribution of dust and shimmer, a concentration at the top, and you recognize the grammar of a shape even if the details are missing, even if it lasts only long enough to be noticed and then disperses again into the warm air and the dark.

As if it wanted to offer you something you could read.

As if reading were already something it understood was done.

---

You listen.

The tone is three tones now, or has been three tones for a while and you are only now hearing the third. It is very high and very faint and it is coming from directly overhead, where the particulate murk has thinned for a moment and the stars are briefly visible, strange and thin and too far away.

The ridge shifts under you. New rock, still arriving.

The outline disperses. The tones continue, searching for an interval they have not yet named.

They have been here since the beginning, which was not long ago. They will be here when the mountains finish arriving, whenever that is — when the peaks choose their final shapes and the cooling completes and the fractures stop opening and the worked stone of the ancient structures has been shaken, by then, into rubble. The Nascent will still be here, tending that rubble, circling whatever else arrives, becoming, slowly, whatever they are going to be.

Still warm. Still curious. Still leaning, always, toward.
