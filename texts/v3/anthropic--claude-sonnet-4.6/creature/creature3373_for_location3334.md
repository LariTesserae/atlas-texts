# What Lives Here

---

Begin with what does not live here, because the boundary clarifies the center.

There are no apex predators. Not because the system cannot support them — it can support anything, given time, given the patient rearrangement of what is already here — but because apex predators require the concept of prey, and prey requires the concept of escape, and escape requires the belief that somewhere else is better than here. Nothing in this place has developed that belief. The evolutionary pressure that produces flight has not been applied. What moves, moves slowly. What is still, is still for a very long time.

There are no migratory animals. Nothing here is from somewhere else and trying to return to it.

There is no carrion. Things die, but they are consumed — not by scavengers who seek out the dead, but by the living mat itself, which accepts the fallen in the same patient way it accepts the living: without distinction, without preference, simply as a continuation of what has always been occurring.

---

Now: what is here.

---

**The fungi**

They are the oldest residents, or the most essential, which amounts to the same thing. Call them residents and immediately the word is wrong — *resident* implies someone who chose a location, who could have chosen otherwise. The fungi did not choose. They are not located. They are the location, or a significant percentage of it.

What the fungi are doing, in the root-mat and below it and threading through the bark of every tree and woven through the humus at every depth, is: everything. They are transferring carbon between trees that do not share root-systems. They are breaking down the century-deep organic matter into elements that can be absorbed and will be absorbed and will be built, again, into living tissue, which will eventually fall, which will be broken down again. They are digesting. They are transporting. They are communicating — not in a way that involves intention, not in a way that has anything to do with *meaning*, but in the narrow technical sense: molecules passing from body to body in response to conditions, changing what those bodies do. The trees grow differently because of what the fungi tell them. The fungi grow differently because of what the trees tell them. The telling is chemical, slow, vast, and has been occurring here for so long that it constitutes, in aggregate, something like a memory.

The fungi are not aware that they have a memory. This does not prevent them from having one.

They fruit occasionally. The fruiting bodies are not the point — the fruiting bodies are simply the fungi's method of introducing itself to the air, of sending a part of itself to be somewhere else, though here, in this closed bowl, the somewhere-else is always still here. The fruiting bodies are: pale. Very pale. Some are the color of old paper, of the inside of bark, of skin that has not seen sun in a very long time. A few are the white of fresh bone. They do not announce themselves. They emerge and return at a pace that has nothing to do with seasons — there are no seasons here, not in the thermal or luminous sense — and instead seems to respond to something internal, a rhythm in the mat itself, an interval that you could almost calculate if you stayed long enough.

The mat breathes in these intervals. Or something in the mat does. The fruiting bodies are part of the breath.

---

**The trees**

The trees are not separate organisms in a practical sense. They share root-mass, share fungal networks, share canopy — the boundaries between individuals are real in a strict genetic sense and nowhere else. Genetically, they are individuals. In every other dimension — chemical, physical, temporal, functional — they are a single thing that has grown too large to be aware of itself.

But there are lives in the trees. There are many lives. The trees are not the lives, precisely; the trees are the *medium* for the lives, or the trees are lives in their own right that contain, incidentally, the lives of others. The nesting cavities in the oldest trunks — formed by the slow withdrawal of heartwood, by the patient work of insects that have been doing this for longer than their current bodies have existed — are occupied. Not always. Not consistently. But something has used them, and the question of whether something uses them *now* depends on a timeframe you may not be operating in.

Inside one cavity, at a height of perhaps twelve meters, in a trunk so large the cavity itself is the size of a small room: a dry space, a circular chamber, the walls smoothed by either the slow deposition of resin over decades or by contact with something that used this space for a long time. The floor of the cavity is dry leaves, very old, compacted into a surface as firm and regular as a mattress. The smell is: warm, slightly sweet, the particular sweetness of resins and aged cellulose and something else, something biological and warm, something that was breathing here recently or is breathing here now. No marks. No objects. The smoothness of the walls. The compacted floor. The smell.

Something sleeps here. You did not see it leave. You would not see it return.

---

**The insects**

They are the connective tissue of this place, the mechanism by which things that need to find each other find each other. Without them, the flowering stops. Without the flowering, the seeds stop. Without the seeds, the trees stop — or stop replacing themselves, which over the timescale of this place amounts to the same catastrophe.

So they are essential. So there are many of them. And yet:

You will not see them. Or you will see them only at the edge of looking — only when you are not looking at them, only when your eyes are aimed somewhere else and the motion registers in the peripheral field, and when you turn to look directly the motion has resolved back into stillness, into a surface, into what appears to have always been a leaf.

This is not camouflage in the military sense — not deception, not an attempt to avoid a predator. It is simply that they are perfectly of this place. They are not pretending to be leaves. They are leaves, functionally, in the language the forest speaks. They move in the intervals between observations and are still during observations not because they have evolved to detect observation — they have not, there is nothing here that watches consistently enough to constitute evolutionary pressure for that — but because their movement is precisely as slow as the movement of everything else here, which is to say: slow enough that it does not read as movement to anything that thinks on a human timescale, but does read as movement to anything thinking on the forest's timescale.

They are part of the breath. They carry pollen from body to body the way the fungi carry sugars: constantly, in all directions, without destination, covering everything with the possibility of everything.

---

**The bird**

There is one. There may be more than one, but the call — single note, flat, every twenty-three minutes — suggests either one bird with a very fixed interval or multiple birds whose intervals synchronize, which would be stranger. One bird is the simpler explanation.

It has not been seen. The canopy is too dense at the third layer, the layer from which the displacement came that one time, and the displacement is not repeated. The bird — if it is a bird — is not curious about the floor. It has no reason to come to the floor. The floor is for things that are of the floor. The canopy is for things that are of the canopy. The bird is of the canopy.

What is the bird doing in the intervals between calls? The intervals are long. Twenty-three minutes is a long time to sit still and silent in a tree. Either the bird is doing something that produces no sound, or the bird is doing something that is indistinguishable from the forest's background — a rustle indistinguishable from the condensation-drip, a movement indistinguishable from the slight shifting of a branch under its own weight.

The call is not territorial. It does not have the urgency of territory. A territorial call says: *I am here and you are not, and this is an arrangement that will be enforced.* This call says something else. It says something more like a report. A single word repeated at regular intervals, not addressed to anyone, not aggressive, not lonely — not lonely in the way a mammal would be lonely — but perhaps simply: registering. Noting. Marking the passage of an interval by marking the passage of an interval. The call as a function of time rather than of communication.

And then: *again.*

---

**The thing that uses the circular clearing**

Something uses it. The mat does not simply decline to grow there — it has been compressed by weight and repeated passage, by something returning to a center again and again over a period long enough that the mat has accepted the compression as permanent, has stopped trying to fill it. This is a long time. The mat recovers from footprints in hours. For the mat to have accepted this circle as permanent, the compression must have continued through cycles of recovery, must have been reapplied before recovery could complete, for long enough that the mat has decided — in the slow, chemical, non-intentional way that biological systems make decisions — that this is a condition of the location rather than an event that interrupted it.

What comes back. What keeps coming back. What has been coming back for long enough that a circle of dead flat ground is now a feature of this place, like the water-cups in the buttress roots, like the depth of the humus layer, like the diameter of the largest trunks.

The compression is from something roughly the mass of a large dog, or a small deer, or something that has no common name because it has not been common. Something that returns at intervals — not the bird's twenty-three minutes; longer, much longer, days perhaps, or a rhythm attached to something other than time, attached to whatever internal calendar this place keeps — and stands in the center of the circle, or sits, or coils, and does what it does when it comes there, which is unknown, and then leaves in some direction that does not produce visible tracks on the firm mat surface, in some manner that does not disturb the sharp edge of the circle, and is gone.

It is not here now. This is not certain.

---

**The roots**

This requires a moment.

The roots are alive. This is biological fact. But the roots are alive in a way that accumulates, over the timescale of this place, into something that shares certain properties with what we mean when we say *aware*. Not awareness in the animal sense — not sensation, not feeling, not subjectivity. But: responsiveness. The root-mat responds to the distribution of resources, to the signals carried through fungal threads, to the chemical messages of stress or excess. A tree in the eastern part of the depression is receiving more light. This fact is known to trees on the western side, within a timeframe we cannot perceive but which is real. Adjustments are made. Not decisions — not anything as rapid or deliberate as decisions — but adjustments. The system shifts to account for changed conditions, the way water accounts for an obstacle: not by knowing the obstacle is there, but by being the kind of thing that distributes itself according to what it encounters.

This responsiveness, aggregated across two kilometers, across centuries, across millions of individual threads reading millions of individual conditions and returning millions of individual adjustments — this adds up to something. It does not add up to thought. It adds up to something that functions, in certain specific ways, like thought. It has preferences, in the sense of systematic biases. It has memory, in the sense that its current state encodes its history. It has something like anticipation, in the sense that it allocates resources toward conditions that have historically followed other conditions.

It does not know you are here. But it is doing something with the fact of you anyway — something slow, something that will resolve long after you have gone, something that may amount to nothing or may amount to a slight systematic adjustment in the western quadrant, to a tiny rebalancing that incorporates the ghost of your warmth into its ongoing accounting.

This is the most alive thing in this place. It is not an animal. It does not have experiences. It is thinking, very slowly, in a language that is not language, about everything that has ever been here — and among those things, now, is you.

---

**The thing in the notch in the buttress root**

There is nothing in the notch. The smell suggests something once was, or something visits. Something small. Something that requires a dry, protected space approximately seven centimeters in each dimension — the size of a closed fist, the size of something curled — and that finds this notch sufficient. That returns to this notch. That has been returning long enough that the root accepted the cut without closing it, made the notch part of its architecture, which means the return has been occurring for at least as long as that process takes.

The occupant is absent. The space is very warm.

---

**What has not been catalogued**

The life at microscopic scale: the nematodes threading between fungal hyphae, the bacteria completing the decomposition the fungi begin, the single-celled organisms in the water-cups of the buttress roots, living in complete still water that has not been refreshed in weeks and yet is not stagnant — not stagnant because the life in it balances itself, because what produces waste produces it at a rate the rest can consume, because this is what equilibrium means at the scale of the visible and it is also what equilibrium means at the scale of the invisible, and the two are the same system.

At this scale, the place is almost unbearably full. At this scale, every surface is a landscape. Every cubic centimeter of mat is a community. Every tree trunk contains, in its layers of bark and cambium and heartwood and cavity, a nested sequence of worlds each of which is running its own version of the same closed system, in miniature, for its own benefit, which is also the benefit of everything else.

At this scale, what lives here is: nearly everything. Nearly every metabolic strategy. Nearly every structural solution to the problem of continuing to exist. The full range of what life knows how to do, compressed into a bowl two kilometers across, running in parallel, sustaining itself with what it produces, for as long as this place has been this way.

Which is: a very long time.

Which is: always, in the vocabulary available here.

---

What lives in this place is the place.

This is not mysticism. It is the most precise statement available. The boundary between *what lives here* and *here* has been dissolved by the accumulation of time and the specific pressure of equilibrium. The organisms are not in this place. The organisms are this place. The distinction between individual and environment, between the thing that breathes and the air it breathes, between the root and the soil it moves through, has been negotiated across centuries into something close to irrelevance.

To ask what lives here is to ask what the forest is. And the forest is everything that has ever come here and stayed long enough. And everything that comes here, stays. And so the answer is: everything. Every form of being that found its way down the long ramp and encountered the warmth and the stillness and was incorporated.

Including, in some small and temporary and completely unrecorded way, you — whom the mat is already accounting for, whose cells are already becoming part of the accounting, whose presence is already being incorporated into the centuries-long calculation of what this place is and what it contains and what it will be, always, when you are gone.
