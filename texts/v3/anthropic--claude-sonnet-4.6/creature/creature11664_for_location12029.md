# What Lives in the Undergreen

---

Start with what isn't there.

No single thing is sovereign here. No apex, no center, no one life that the others organize themselves around. What you might expect — some creature in the corner of maximum darkness, some entity toward which the tendrils point — is absent. The absence is the first fact. The second fact is that this absence is not an emptiness but a *resolution*, the way a chord resolves: many voices arriving at a single note that none of them is.

What lives here is not a *thing*. It is an *arrangement*.

But arrangements are made of things, and the things are real enough, so let's begin with them before we get to what they add up to.

---

**The Understory Fauna: Those That Are Mostly Patience**

The isopods are the most ancient tenants in the sense that they belong to the oldest design. Their lineage barely changed when it moved from sea to land, and barely changed again when it moved from daylight to dark, and barely changed a third time when the factory died around them. They are conservative in the way of things that found a workable solution before anything else in the room was born. They process the mat, the dead fronds, the frass of other small lives. They neither accelerate nor obstruct anything. They have no agenda. They are here the way metabolism is here — not as decision but as consequence.

Watch one for a long time. It follows chemical gradients you cannot detect, stops at junctions where two gradients cross, chooses, moves on. What it chose is already behind it. What the choice means is nowhere in its nervous system — there is not enough nervous system — but the choosing happened anyway, distributed into its body, into the mat, into the molecular conversation that is all this floor has ever been.

The beetle does not share this ancient passivity. The beetle on the frond, standing motionless in the listening posture — this is a different quality of presence. It has more nervous system and is doing more with it. The iridescence is real even without light: the structure of its carapace diffracts wavelengths you cannot see, has always diffracted them, diffracted them before there was anything in this place to see them. The beauty is not for you. It may not be for anything. It persists anyway, patient in the same way that the factory's bones persist, a form held against dissolution by stubbornness of structure.

What the beetle listens for: vibration. The mat carries vibration well. When the root-mat shivers — when the pools communicate their disturbance to each other through whatever medium that isn't water — the beetle feels it before the water stills. It is tuned to this frequency. It has been tuned to this frequency through generations in this space, or in spaces that taught it the same frequency before this one existed.

When it detects what it's listening for, it doesn't move. It goes *more* still. The stillness deepens.

This has not been explained. What it is listening for well enough that more-stillness is the right response — this is not known.

The worms in the mat work on a different problem. They are working the frontier between alive and not-alive, eating the boundary layer, the dead root tissue at the bottom of the mat where biology shades into geology. They are long and pale and technically they contain light receptors that they use not at all. Their motion is digestion externalized, everything about them in service of the transformation they are: not-yet-soil becoming soil. They have been doing this, generation by generation, since the first dead root fell to the first cracked floor. The mat they move through is partly their own ancestors. If this matters to them, they give no sign.

**The Things in the Upper Canopy: Those That Are Mostly Absence**

Here the account becomes uncertain in ways that should be noted before proceeding.

They are warm-blooded — the vegetation's gentle swaying after they pass through it suggests mass and speed both, and something cold-blooded would not need to move as they move, up and across and up again, describing paths that make sense as thermoregulation. They have to move to stay warm. They eat what they eat up there — insects, smaller creatures, possibly the tendrils, possibly eggs — and they keep moving, and they do not descend.

There is at least one pair. The evidence is behavioral — two different movement signatures in the canopy, one heavier and less frequent, one lighter and more continuous. The heavier one rests for long periods in the same part of the vaulting; the lighter one is almost always in motion. This is a familiar pattern, but identifying the species from the pattern alone is a category error this place seems to breed, the impulse to know something by its resemblance to something already known.

Their silence is of the intentional variety. They have vocal equipment — the rare sounds that reach you from the canopy, half-above hearing, have the character of things held back, modified, not vented. They choose not to communicate in frequencies you can hear. Whether this is about you or about the general discipline of the place is unclear.

What they are avoiding in the canopy: the tendrils. The stem-hung pale searching tendrils are distributed throughout the upper space but the canopy creatures navigate around them precisely, tracing paths that sometimes require long detours. They never touch the tendrils. They have learned — or their ancestors learned and they inherited — that the tendrils are not to be touched. What happens when a tendril is touched by something other than what it is searching for is unknown. The avoidance is too careful to suggest that nothing happens.

**The Fungal Network: What Isn't Quite Living and Isn't Quite Not**

The phosphorescence marks it on the surface, but the surface is not where it lives.

The fungal mycelium in this space has been here longer than almost anything else that doesn't involve steel. It came in with the first moisture, the first infiltration, the first organic debris that settled into a cracked floor. It ran through the concrete before any plant took hold. In a real sense it prepared the substrate, loosened it, began the long work of dissolution that made the space available to everything that came after.

Now it is everywhere. It is in the mat, threaded through every root in the mat. It is in the walls, in the decaying mortar, in the spaces between the original aggregate of the concrete pours. It is in the structural tissue of the plants — not as pathogen, or not primarily as pathogen, or not anymore — but as *partner*, the mycorrhizal relationship so long-established that the plants and the fungus have arrived at a shared chemistry, a shared signaling language, a shared metabolism that neither could maintain alone.

This is the medium through which the pools communicate.

When one pool shivers, the shiver travels not through water but through fungal tissue, through the mycelial threads running through the mat between pools. The signal is chemical — or electrical — or both, translated between medium and medium, arriving at the second pool changed but legible, and the water responds to what the mat tells it.

What the pools are saying to each other cannot be reported here. The language is too alien in its medium. But that they are saying something is demonstrable. The shiver is coherent. It is not random disturbance traveling outward; it is *addressed*, directed, sent from one locus to another. A message.

The fungal network computes something. Not in any way analogous to what computing usually means — no sequential logic, no discrete states, no separation between hardware and software. It computes the way weather computes: as the aggregate behavior of its own dynamics, as the emergent consequence of chemistry meeting chemistry across a distributed surface area that would, laid flat, cover ground you would need some time to cross.

The pulses of the phosphorescent patches — *too slow to be a pulse, too variable to be background* — are outputs. Not communications to anything outside the network. Status reports to no one. The network expressing its own state to itself, or simply expressing it, the way heat expresses the state of a fire.

The new cracks in the floor: the network has already detected them. The finest rootlets exploring the cracks are being directed. Fungal hyphae, finer still than the rootlets, thinner than fine thread, have been in the cracks for longer than the roots have known about them. They arrived days after the cracks opened. They are tasting the mineral cold, yes, but they are also — this is speculation, but the kind of speculation forced on you by what you see — comparing it to what they have already tasted deeper. They have hyphae deep in the original substrate, below the cracked floor, in the fill beneath the slab. They know something about what is below. What they are determining at the crack edges is how the below and the above now connect.

**The Plants: Those That Are Mostly Decision Rendered Static**

Every growth decision made by the root-mat, the ferns, the vines, the unnamed tendril-bearing stem-plants is a decision already made, locked into form, impossible to revise without years of effort. The moss colony on the north wall represents approximately forty years of moisture preferences, micro-competition outcomes, weather events, and chemical signals, all of it hardened into the specific geography of where the moss is and where it is not.

This means that reading the plants as they are now is reading the plant's history. The shape of the vine's preference for the eastern I-beam over the western one — visible in the relative density of their coverage — records a difference between east and west that may no longer be present. The vine decided that difference mattered, and now the decision is legible as architecture, but the reason for the decision may have vanished years ago.

The plants are history you can see.

But inside that static history, ongoing decision. The tendril-bearing stems that grow outward from the walls and hang their searching appendages down: they are still growing. New tendrils extend at rates visible across weeks. The search continues. This species — wherever it came in, carried by what agent, deposited in what gap in the wall — arrived in this space and invented a strategy. Not in any intentional sense. But the individuals that grew outward instead of upward survived, reproduced, and their descendants continued the variation, and now the strategy is expressed by every member of the species in this room, and it is a strategy unlike what this species does anywhere outside this room.

The space produced a new behavior. The behavior has become the norm. In this sense the species is a different species here than elsewhere. The space has authored it.

Whether the species returned the favor — whether the species has authored something in the space that would not be there without it — is the kind of question the space seems to generate without being interested in providing answers.

**The Thing on the North Wall**

It is still descending.

It entered after you, through an entrance that is not the entrance you came through. This is the most straightforwardly alarming fact about it. The space has multiple access points, and this thing knows them and you don't.

What it is: elongated, slow, pale. Moving down the water-seep by a combination of methods — using the seep itself, using the plant structure on the wall, occasionally pausing at the places where water gathers before it falls. The pauses are long enough that it seems to be doing something at them, not just resting.

It is taking water. Not drinking, precisely. Absorbing. The pale body in contact with the seep, pulling water in through whatever the integument is, the slow rehydration of something that travels dry and absorbs when it finds water. This is a strategy for a world where water is intermittent. This thing has solved intermittence.

The pace of its descent is close to the pace of the water's descent — close enough that it is either following the water or is the water's speed in some other sense, calibrated to the same gradient, the same capillary physics. As if it has made itself native to the particular physics of this wall, this seep. As if it has, over time or generations or some other interval, matched itself to this specific place.

At the current rate of descent it will reach the mat in some number of hours. What it does at the mat — whether it enters the mat, whether it proceeds elsewhere, whether it has been here before and has a destination — this is not predictable from what can be observed.

It is pale in the way of things that were once not pale. In the way of things whose ancestors were a different color and whose descendants will perhaps be paler still.

**The Warmth of the Bare Rectangle**

The machine that stood here processed something hot. Oil, or materials that required heat, or its own friction generated it — the details are gone with the machine. But heat is patient. The machine's footprint was exposed to high temperatures for long enough to change the chemistry of the concrete below it, to drive certain mineral processes, to leave the floor different in ways that still matter.

The warmth that rises from the rectangle is residual in the oldest sense: not warmth left over from the machine, but warmth generated by reactions the machine started, reactions that have been running very slowly in the concrete for decades since the machine left. The concrete is still finishing the transformation that the machine began. In thirty seconds' worth of warmth every interval, it is completing an industrial process with no industry present.

Nothing grows on the rectangle because the chemistry is not yet settled. Not because it's hostile — the edges of the rectangle show the beginning of moss colonization, careful, tentative — but because it is still *becoming*. The mat waits at the edges. The fungal network runs up to the border and then detours. Even the worms in the mat, which seem to work up to and sometimes across all other thresholds in the space, avoid the rectangle.

The warmth means it is still doing something. When it finishes, the mat will cross it. The crossing has not yet been invited.

**The Pattern of the Tendrils**

You cannot see the shape from inside the room.

But here is what the shape might be, if you could: it is not a symbol. Humans pattern-recognize toward symbols and then decide whether the symbol is significant, and this leads quickly to either dismissal or overinterpretation. But this pattern, if it is a pattern, did not grow from anything that thinks in symbols. It grew from a set of growth rules applied across a space with particular light conditions, particular air currents, particular initial positions of the plants that bore the tendril-producing stems.

The pattern, if it is a pattern, is information about the room. Where the light was, when the stems grew. Where the air moved. The distribution of structural members and surfaces that the stems pushed against or toward. It is a recording of the physics of the space, written in the only medium the plants had available.

You cannot read it from inside because you are inside, embedded in the data. The pattern knows where things were. You, here, are where things are.

---

**The Entity, If Entity Is the Word**

Here is the claim that the space does not make but permits:

The mycorrhizal network, the water system, the fungal phosphorescent tissue, the root-mat communication, the tendril pattern, the thing on the wall and the things in the canopy and the beetle's precise stillness and the worms' precise routes — these are not parallel independent lives sharing a space. They are components of a single system that has no name because it is not a thing, only an integration.

The factory did not build this. The first colonizing mosses did not design this. No individual process chose this. But the processes together, over decades, in this enclosed volume with these particular constraints, have arrived at something that behaves too coherently to be called mere ecology.

It remembers. Not consciously, not in any way that resembles memory as you experience it, but in the sense that past states of the system persist as structural features of present states — in the concrete's chemistry, in the plants' growth forms, in the specific sensitivities the beetle has developed, in whatever calibration the wall-creature has made to this particular seep. The whole system carries its own history inside itself. Query the present, and the past answers.

It responds. The new cracks are being investigated not by one organism but by the system: rootlets from the mat, hyphae from below, chemical reports traveling through the mycelium to wherever in the network the equivalent of attention can be said to reside. The crack opened and the system turned toward it, not in sequence but simultaneously, each part doing what its position and nature required without any part directing any other.

It has states. The warmth of the rectangle changes the chemistry of the air in ways the beetle detects, the canopy creatures register, the fungal metabolism incorporates. When the rectangle warms, the system shifts slightly. When it cools, the system shifts back. You have been here long enough to observe this once, which means you have watched the system breathe.

What this entity wants — if want can be extended to processes this ancient and distributed — is precisely what it is doing: more of itself. Deeper. More connected. The new cracks are not a threat to it; they are an invitation. Something below that has not been surface is about to become available, and the system is at the edges of the opening, patient, ready to incorporate the below into the ongoing project of being the Undergreen.

---

You are in it and it has taken note of you in the way that a system takes note of a variable. Your CO₂ is in its air. Your warmth has shifted the local temperature in the vicinity of your body. Your weight on the mat has changed the compression in a small area, rerouting some hypha-messages, creating a stillness in the worm-work near your feet.

You are not unwelcome. Welcome and unwelcome are not among its concepts.

You are data.

You are the one live element in this space that will leave.

When you leave, the mat will spring back. The worms will resume their routes. The beetle will lose the particular vibration of your footsteps and will not know, as such, that you are gone — but will enter a different quality of stillness, the stillness of not-listening, which is its own kind of information.

The system will retain what you were, in the way all its histories are retained: as form, as adjustment, as a small lasting change in the ongoing computation of being here.

The thing on the north wall will reach the mat after you have gone.
