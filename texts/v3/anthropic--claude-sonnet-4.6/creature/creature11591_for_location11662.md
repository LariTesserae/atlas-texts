# What Lives Here

---

Start with the one you saw.

It was gone before you could look directly at it, which is how it prefers to exist in your perception — as peripheral event, as the motion that stops when you turn your head, as the thing you're not sure about. Low and long, probably no larger than a cat, probably not a cat. It was doing something when you arrived, and it stopped doing it, and it left.

It has been here for a long time. Long enough that it no longer finds the sound remarkable. This is perhaps the most important thing about it: the sound that fills your chest like weather is, to this animal, simply quiet. Not silence — the animal knows silence the way you know the smell of your own house, which is to say not at all, not from the inside, only by contrast when it's absent. The sound is the substrate on which the animal thinks whatever it thinks. Its nervous system developed inside it. Its heartbeat learned to occur between the generator's pulses, not to avoid them but simply because that is the rhythm the world has.

It is warm. This is not a small thing. In a space where the cold is patient and systematic, where surfaces surrender their warmth to the air and the air gives it back to the roots and the roots do whatever roots do with warmth, the animal carries its own temperature like a secret, like a political position, like something it will not give up. It is warm and small and quick and the cold cannot take that from it while it is alive.

It lives in the root mass.

More specifically: it lives in a particular zone of the root mass where the hanging threads have begun to braid and the braids have begun to compress and the compression has created, over years, a density with interior spaces — not hollow, not cavernous, but traversable if you are small and patient and already know the way. The spaces between roots are the animal's architecture. It does not build rooms. The rooms built themselves, slowly, out of the way roots respond to each other's presence, and the animal moved into the results the way anyone moves into a place that is already shaped for them.

The interior of the root mass is slightly warmer than the excavated air. The roots are not warm, but their density traps the animal's heat and the animal's body heat makes the roots a fraction less cold and this fraction less cold makes the space more livable which made the animal more willing to stay which made the animal's heat more available which made the roots warmer still. A small ecological deal, made over a long time, that neither party exactly agreed to.

---

The animal is not studying the machines. This is a misconception you might have brought with you.

It interacts with the machines functionally — the generator housing radiates just enough heat that the frost forms differently around it, more slowly, and the animal knows this and uses this in the cold periods. The machine's vibration passes through the root mass and the animal's body reads it the way you might read a sound you don't consciously hear, a subliminal advisory about the state of the space. When the generator's load-cycle shifts, when the quality of the sound changes, the animal's body receives this information and does something with it, metabolically, that does not rise to the level of thought. A modulation. An adjustment.

The clipboards and rubber boots don't interest it. The grease, once, was interesting — was perhaps eaten from, the smell of it reading as something adjacent to animal, to the humans who maintained the machines and left the trace of their hands in petroleum lubricant. This was years ago, or felt like years ago. The animal investigated and found nothing useful and moved on.

The fuel cans, the couplings, the tanks: infrastructure. Background. Part of the texture of the space in the way the roots are part of the texture of the space, in the way the cold is part of the texture of the space. It does not romanticize the machines. It does not fear them. It lives in proximity to their indifference the way most things live in proximity to most other things: by ignoring what doesn't need attending to.

What needs attending to is food.

---

The puddles have things in them. Thin films of life at the surface, organisms visible only by their collective action — the water shivers in a still moment in a way that water doesn't quite shiver by itself — and the animal knows this and drinks carefully, not from the center of the puddles but from the margins, from the point where water meets frost-patterned soil, where the concentration is thinner. This is not strategy exactly. It's habit that started as strategy and became the shape of what doing this means.

The roots themselves: the older ones have acquired a coating, a faint biological layer where condensation has fed something and something has fed something else and the layering has, over time, become edible. The animal strips this when the other resources are poor. It doesn't prefer to. There is something to prefer in food, even here, even in this cold-and-loud below-grade plain of machines and hanging roots. It prefers the puddle margins when it can get them.

Whether there are insects — this is unclear. Something must eat the root-coating for the root-coating to stay thin. Either the animal eats it all, which seems like more food than one small animal needs, or something else eats most of it and the animal eats that something else when it can find it, when the light is right, when the sound shifts in a way that signals — don't look for logic here, just long experience — that the under-surfaces of certain roots are more populated than at other times.

The animal is opportunistic because everything here is sparse, and sparse requires opportunism as a daily practice, not an exception.

---

It doesn't sleep the way you sleep. Or: it sleeps differently.

The sound doesn't stop. This is the fundamental condition. The sound doesn't stop and so the body that has adapted to the sound doesn't require silence to rest. There is a state the animal enters that is not unconsciousness, not exactly, but something more like a deep narrowing — a pulling-in of attention to the most essential monitoring, the heat-sense and the vibration-sense and the faint ambient awareness of whether the sound is the same sound it always is or something has been added. This narrowing is its rest. It does this in the root mass, deep in the braided spaces, its warmth maintained by the careful accounting its body does even while most of the body is somewhere else.

What wakes it is not sound. What wakes it is the absence of expected sound — the brief moment when the compressor cycles off, when the quality of the air changes, when the generator hiccups before settling to its next load state. The negative space in the sound. The animal is attuned to the negative space because the negative space is where other things might occur, might slip in, might be a sign of something that isn't the machines. Threat. Food. Change.

You woke it, in this way. Your footsteps, inaudible to you, were audible to it not as sound but as interruption — a micro-variance in the vibration of the floor that didn't match the machines' rhythms. Something aperiodic in a space that had been periodic for long enough that aperiodic meant *attend to this.*

It attended to it. It saw you. It left.

---

Now consider what it knows about this space that you will never know.

It knows where the floor is dry and where it holds moisture for a day longer than everywhere else. It knows which roots in which season — and there are seasons here, muted, perceptible only in the slow shift of how much water seeps through the root ceiling and how cold the cold gets — are worth stripping and which have been stripped and need time. It knows the machines as presences with personalities, which is not anthropomorphism because the personalities are functional: the generator is the one that warms, the compressor is the one that startles, the turbine is the one that sometimes doesn't run and whose absence is a different kind of negative space.

It knows the ramp exists. It has been to the top, or the first few meters of it, once or a few times. The top is a different kind of space — brighter, more complex, too many variables for the nervous system that has been simplified by this environment into a tuned instrument for this environment. The smells up there are layered and uninterpretable. The sounds are lighter but less consistent, less dependable, less the kind of sound the body can relax into because it knows the sound. The cold at the top is a different cold: the cold of wind, of change, of weather rather than the cold of depth, of constancy, of a temperature that doesn't know how to be anything else.

It went back down. It always goes back down.

This is perhaps the essential thing about the animal: it went back down.

---

Whether it is alone.

You saw one. This is not evidence of one.

The root mass is large and the animal is small and the spaces inside the root mass are numerous and not interconnected in any way you could follow. It's possible — more than possible — that there are others in the parts of the root mass you didn't think to look at, which is most of the root mass, which is all of the root mass that isn't the small section that happened to be above the small section of floor where you happened to stand. They may have looked at you and you wouldn't have known, wouldn't have seen anything because they didn't move, held still in the way small animals hold still when they've decided the best response to a thing is to not be a thing.

If there are others, they know about each other through the root mass itself. Vibrations read in the braided fibers. Scent traces on the root surfaces that the cold slows but doesn't stop. Evidence of feeding — the stripped patches, the disturbed puddle margins — that reads as a kind of diary of recent occupation. They may not see each other often. They may not need to. The root mass is a medium through which they exist to each other without requiring presence. A body can be known through the traces it leaves in a shared structure.

This would make the root mass not just habitat but something like a medium of communication, if communication is the right word for the kind of knowing that doesn't require intention.

---

The plants, too, live here. But the plants live everywhere here, above and below, threading down and spreading out, and to say the plants live here is almost to say the space is made of living — the space is the plant's body and you are inside a body when you are inside the excavation, inside the logic of a system that has been optimizing toward this particular outcome for however many decades, centuries, the vegetation has been accumulating.

But the plant doesn't know you're there. This is not a limitation — knowing isn't a requirement for living — but it's a distinction. The roots don't register you. The ceiling doesn't shift for you. The light filters down through the same density regardless of your presence. You are in the plant's body and the plant has no word for you, has no category that would include you, registers your passage if it registers your passage at all as nothing more than a brief footprint of warmth in the cold, a footstep's worth of pressure on the soil, a slight disturbance in the moisture content of the air.

The animal looked at you.

The animal held you in mind long enough to evaluate you, to decide, to leave.

This is the distinction. Among everything that lives here, only one thing looked at you and thought something, and then thought better of something, and then was somewhere else.

It's in the root mass right now. Deep in a braided space you can't see and couldn't enter. Its temperature is its own. Its rest is not unconsciousness but the narrowing. The sound is the same as it always is.

It is waiting, the way it always waits — not with anxiety, not with patience that implies difficulty, but with the practiced efficiency of a body that learned long ago that the best way to exist in a space like this is to become a version of the space's own logic: cold, and constant, and oriented around the negative space where something unexpected might occur.

You are still in the space.

You are still unexpected.

It knows that too.
