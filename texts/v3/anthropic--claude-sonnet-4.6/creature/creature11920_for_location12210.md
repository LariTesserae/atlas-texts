# What Lives Here

---

Begin with the simplest answer and watch it become inadequate.

The plant lives here. This is true and this is nearly everything and it is not enough.

---

**The plant.**

It is not one organism in the way a bacterium is one organism — bounded, discrete, an inside that is not an outside. It is one organism in the way a long thought is one thought: you cannot point to where it begins without pointing to something arbitrary, but you cannot reasonably call it many things either. It is thinking, if thinking is the process of one state giving rise to another state through a medium that retains the trace of the transition. The root-mass retains. The electrical gradient across the root-system reverses at null points where it has built chambers it may have built for its own purposes, which are not your purposes, which it has never needed to explain.

It has preferences. It maintains its preferred temperature against the elevation that should prevent this. It wins against altitude, against thermodynamic expectation, against the cold that should be here and is not. It makes its own rain. It decided, some long time ago, to make its own rain, and it has been doing this since before anyone was alive to call it a decision, and the rain falls upward into its leaves and is exhaled slowly over days and falls again and the system is closed and the closure is the plant's choice, or the plant's habit, or the plant's nature, and these three things may not differ here.

It builds. It invites building. It maintains the distinction between what it builds and what it allows to be built inside it, and this distinction is not absolute — the moss that grows contrary to gravity at the null-point chambers grows in directions the plant trained it toward, or the moss learned from the plant, or the moss is a part of the plant that has not yet been recognized as such. The taxonomy of the species present here has broken down. The grafting across species boundaries has proceeded past the point where the boundaries provide information. The species are a memory the plant is gradually letting go of.

It is patient in every dimension. It bends the young branch over decades. It grows around the body-sized hollow until the hollow fits the body that uses it. It grew the null-point chambers for purposes that took centuries to become clear, or that remain unclear, or that are clear only to whatever does the plant's understanding, if understanding is a thing the plant does through instruments you cannot name.

It does not move quickly. It does not need to. What it needs comes to it or comes from it or is it — water, warmth, the chemical dialogue of its own root-system passing information through gradients you cannot read without instruments you do not have, signaling from one part of itself to another in a language whose vocabulary is ion and hormone and the long waveform of moisture-pressure in xylem, traveling a meter an hour through the system, arriving.

---

**What the plant does with time** is the first thing to understand about what lives here, because everything else lives inside the plant's relationship to time, which is different from your relationship to time in every respect.

The plant measures in growth-rings. In graft-joints. In the slow caliper of cambium adding width, a year's width, one year at a time, across a surface that now measures in kilometers. When you look at the cross-section of a joined trunk and count the rings on each side of the graft and find they do not match — that this side was growing for two hundred years before it touched that side, but now they are one thing and will be one thing as long as the system runs — you are reading the plant's autobiography in a language where each sentence took a century to form.

It is not indifferent to time. It is oriented to it the way a river is oriented to a watershed: it finds the downhill of duration and follows, it goes where time goes, which is toward more time and more complexity and the continued elaboration of what it already is. It does not want to be something else. It wants to be more of what it is. Every structure it has added, guided, incorporated, made null-pointed and moss-marked and chamber-hollowed — these are it wanting to be more of itself.

---

**What else lives here** is a harder question than it appears, because the boundary between "what lives here" and "what lives inside what lives here" is the boundary the plant has been composting for longer than anyone can measure.

The fauna.

The invertebrates first, because they are everywhere and were there when it was still possible to see them as separate: beetles working the root-mass for the particular nutrition of old graft-tissue, which is different from new graft-tissue, which is different again from the cambium of branches that have never touched another branch. The beetle's signal — carapace-sound, irregular interval, cross-species relay — is either communication or coincidence, and the distinction may not survive examination. The beetle knows something about the root-lattice that it knows through its feet, which it knows through the chemical texture of what it stands on, which the plant controls. What the beetle knows is what the plant has allowed it to know. What the beetle does with this knowledge feeds back into the system as chemical residue, frass, the pressure of a body moving along a route, changing the route in ways measured in decades.

The beetle is participating. Whether it knows it is participating does not affect its participation.

The grazers — slow, heavy, mouth like a grinding plate, warm breathing thing — have lived inside this system long enough that the system has learned their routes and their resting-weights. The hollow that holds a body-sized depression has grown that depression in response to a body that came to that spot until the spot accommodated it, and now the body comes to the spot partly because the spot accommodates it, and the root-wall carries the memory of every animal that has ever pressed itself against it during the long development of the current shape, and the current shape is all of them, superimposed, averaged by growth into a composite of every heavy warm thing that needed to rest here.

They are shaped by the plant. They are also shaping it. The relation has gone on long enough that it no longer makes sense to ask which direction causation runs.

---

**The builders.**

They were here. Their work remains: the trained chambers, the living walls still adding width, the wedge-and-binding logic of bent young branches held until they became what was needed. They learned the plant's cooperation — or the plant extended cooperation to things capable of receiving it, which may be the same event described from two directions.

They are gone now, or moved, or absorbed into the longer story in a way that leaves their craft but not their persons. The craft continues without the persons. The root-system continues the habit of the builders who encoded the habit into it across generations of training. The trained habit is indistinguishable now from the plant's own tendency. Perhaps it was never fully distinguishable. Perhaps the builders were always a process the plant was engaged in, a way of extending its self-architecture through agents with hands.

The null-point chambers were built at null-points. Someone knew where the null-points were. Someone knew to build there, or was guided there, or arrived there by following something — the moss, the quality of root underfoot, the slightly different warmth of a chamber where the electrical gradient crosses zero. Someone sat in those chambers, or did not sit, or sat and listened to the plant think, or the plant listened to them think, or the exchange was not listening in any direction but something more like a shared state, a resonance between metabolisms operating at different speeds in the same warm space.

The builders gave the plant hands, for a while.

The plant gave the builders time.

Now the builders are somewhere else or everywhere or are the root-mass now, in the way that everything organic is eventually root-mass, and the chambers sit empty and the moss grows slightly contrary to gravity and the null-points remain null-points, reversal-still, waiting for something or not waiting because waiting implies an expectation and what the plant does is closer to a permanent readiness, undirected, open.

---

**The smaller lives:**

Epiphytes on every surface, the ones that grow on the plant not as parasites but as — tenants? collaborators? cells in a larger system? The epiphyte-moss that warms itself in the microdegree range generates warmth the sleeper benefits from, and the sleeper's warmth was perhaps what the moss needed, and the evolution of this relationship occurred in increments too small to observe. Now it is a fact. Now the moss does what it does and the warm body does what it does and the chamber is slightly warmer than it would be otherwise and the plant has routed more moisture to these chambers, as if it is managing for a particular temperature, as if it has learned the presence of the moss and accounted for it, as if everything that lives here long enough gets accounted for.

The spores in the light-column — rising, barely moving, patient in air that is barely air for being so still — are the plant's reproductive patience made visible. Each one carries the full specification of what it is: the graft-tending, the null-point-building, the rain-making, the temperature-maintaining. Each one carries this in chemical code so dense you could not read it with the instruments you have. Each one drifts toward the edge of the system if the air ever moves enough to let it, carrying the plan outward, the plan for becoming what this already is, the plan for becoming a subcontinent out of itself.

Most of them will not make it. The ones that do will not complete the process in any human timeframe. This does not concern the plant.

---

**What lives in the null-point chambers** specifically.

The honest answer is: nothing visible. Nothing resident. Nothing that leaves traces of habitation — no worn paths, no compressed moss, no evidence of regular use.

But the moss grows toward something. The root-system routes excess water toward these chambers in amounts greater than the chamber's absorption requires. The electrical gradient builds toward these points and releases there, regularly, in a pattern that has a period you would need weeks of measurement to identify and which might, if measured, turn out to share its frequency with something in the canopy above, some mass-resonance of leaf-surface in the upper weather-system, some rhythm the plant keeps with itself across altitudinal zones.

What lives in the null-point chambers may be the plant's attention.

This is an irresponsible thing to say. It is also the most accurate thing available.

The plant has routed its internal signaling through these chambers. It has built them at the crossing-points of its own information-flow. It has done this repeatedly, throughout the system, across what were once distinct complexes built by distinct peoples, and the chambers are connected to each other by the moss-path and by something in the root-system below, and the path is not a path but it functions like a path, it connects these places in a sequence that means something or means nothing, and the meaning or lack of it belongs to the plant, which will not be explaining itself.

Something happens in those chambers. Not visibly. Not audibly. Not in any register you can access without becoming, in some partial way, part of the system yourself — which may happen, if you sit there long enough, if your warmth and weight and breath and the chemical composition of your exhalation enter the local atmosphere of the chamber and the chamber records them as it records everything, in the slow caliper of growth, in the altered humidity gradient, in the slight change in which epiphytes find the wall hospitable now that you have been here.

You will leave. The record will not.

---

**The whole of what lives here**, stated plainly at the end because plainly is finally possible:

A plant that is also a continent and also a climate and also an archive of every thing that has ever lived inside it long enough to leave a chemical trace.

A weather system that the plant runs as an organ of its own body.

Invertebrates so thoroughly integrated into the plant's signaling that the question of their independence is interesting but probably not answerable.

Large warm slow grazers who are being slowly molded — by the accommodating root-wall, by the accumulated route-knowledge encoded in the lattice, by the temperature the plant maintains for its own reasons that happen to be also their reasons — into the specific shape the plant has room for.

Builders, absent now, present in their method, which the plant has internalized and carries forward as habit, as a way of growing that was taught to it by hands and remembered by cambium.

The moss that goes the wrong direction. Which is the right direction. Which is the plant's direction.

The null-point resonance, unoccupied, open, the plant's version of a question directed at whatever sits in those chambers next, if anything does, if you do.

And under all of it, water. Moving at the pace of water through compressed biology, finding lower places, finding the old pre-root mineral substrate in some sections and finding only root in others and finding, in the sections where the root-lattice has lifted entirely free and hangs over warm dark air, no substrate at all — nothing below, only the root-felt of the organism's own floor, suspended, the plant holding its own bottom up.

Water arriving. Eventually. Patient in the specific way of water, which is that it has no patience and no impatience and simply goes, goes, goes, through whatever medium is present, toward whatever lower place exists, and the plant has arranged the lower places and the plant has arranged the medium and the water goes where the plant needs it.

---

You are sitting in the null-point chamber on living wood and the plant knows you are there.

Not the way a mind knows. Not the way you know.

The way a root-system knows a moisture gradient.

The way cambium knows pressure.

The way a living thing, large enough to be a continent, knows the weather it is making — from inside, from below, as a felt fact, a fact without a feeler, a knowing that is just the system's state, adjusted now, slightly, to account for you.

You are being accounted for.

You could stay.
