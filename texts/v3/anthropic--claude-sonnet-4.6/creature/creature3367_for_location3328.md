# What Lives on the Plateau of First Stone

---

Begin with what is hardest to name, because it is what everything else organizes around.

There is a presence on the plateau that is not located anywhere. You become aware of it the way you become aware of being watched — not by seeing the watcher but by noticing something shift in your own attention, a slight reorientation, as if your peripheral vision had decided something was there. But it is not behind you. It is not in any direction. It is more that the plateau, considered as a system, has an orientation, and you can feel yourself being held lightly within it. Not held as in trapped. Held as in: included in the accounting.

You will spend some time trying to decide if this is the structures. It is not only the structures. The structures are part of it. They are like — a word comes to mind before a better one does — *they are like ears that are also hands*. They receive and they act and they do both through the same surface, through the same warmth, through the same low sound in your sternum that does not stop. But the presence is larger than the structures. It is in the whole enterprise of the place. Something decided this plateau should exist and then arranged for it to become more thoroughly itself over time. That deciding is still ongoing. The presence is the deciding, continuing, distributed across every centimeter of advancing edge and every warm water film and every new crevice that knows what it will be.

Call it *the maker* only because you need something to call it, and know that this is insufficient, and know also that the maker does not care about the insufficiency — not out of indifference but because the maker is not concerned with being named. It is concerned with the stone. With the next centimeter. With whatever process is twelve steps ahead of visible results. It is an extremely patient intelligence, or something that produces results indistinguishable from those that extreme patience would produce, and perhaps that distinction doesn't hold.

What is it, the maker? It is the structures, and the structures are it, but neither fully contains the other. The structures are expressions of a will that has no body except the plateau and possibly not even that — the plateau might be more like a workspace than a body, a place where the work happens rather than what the work *is*. Whatever the maker is doing, the plateau is an early stage of it. Everything here is early. The maker has the patience of something that has never once confused early with unimportant.

---

Now, separately, the ones who arrived.

They arrived because the plateau existed, because the warm rock radiated something upward into the vapor that the wind carried, because there is always life waiting for the chance to be somewhere, and this was somewhere.

The birds — the ones filling the air with syntactically complex calls, the forty visible and the others heard in the vapor — they are not the maker's animals. They are not here by design, or they are not here by *that* design. They are here because they are the kind of creature that finds a new warm place and arrives. They are a species, or close enough to a species, that elsewhere lives on volcanic margins and thermal vents and other places where heat is not from sun, and they evolved some set of behaviors and preferences that turn out to fit this plateau as well. The maker did not make them. The maker made the rock that made the conditions that were compatible with them. The difference matters and also does not matter.

These birds are individuals, completely. Watch them long enough and you learn who is who. The one following at two meters — let's say she is a female, though the distinction is not obvious in their plumage — she is following you because she followed the last large thing that came to the plateau too, and the time before that, and she has learned nothing frightening from this habit and so the habit persists. She is not exactly curious. Curiosity implies a sense of the gap between what you know and what you might know. She does not have that sense. She has something simpler: a kind of contact-seeking that looks, from outside, exactly like curiosity, and serves many of the same functions, and perhaps the distinction between that and real curiosity is one of those distinctions that seems important until you've watched her for long enough.

Her calls are long. The other birds respond to them. The response changes what she calls next. There is a conversation happening that has no clear topic, that is something more like a continuous state-reporting — *I am here, the surface here is viable, something large is moving slowly toward the northeast edge, the temperature at this formation is warmer by some increment I do not have a word for but that my feet know* — and the whole group maintains a collective model of the plateau that none of them holds individually. The model is the calls. The calls are the model. When they go quiet, something has disrupted the model, or the model has resolved into certainty and no longer needs maintenance.

There is a lead bird. Not a leader in the social sense — not a bird whose authority others defer to. More a bird who is temperamentally positioned to be first. First to a new formation-edge. First toward you. First off a surface when something changes in the sound. She is slightly larger, slightly more marked, slightly more often wrong in ways that cost nothing. She has been on the plateau for — you estimate, from her bone density and the particular callus on her feet — four months. That makes her ancient here. She was here when the oldest zones were new. She has watched the crevices become more crevice-like. She has probably watched the rock change in ways she has no framework for noticing except as a feeling that the familiar places are not quite the same as they were.

---

The insects are a different matter.

They are not one thing. They are several things using the same spaces, which is common in new places where niches are not yet occupied and competitive exclusion hasn't had time to work. There are small flying things — midges, close enough — that feed on the biofilm in the water films. There are things that are not quite beetles eating something at the mineral margins of new formations, something the processes there produce that has caloric value if you are small enough and have the right enzymes, and they apparently do. There are larger things, slow and cautious, that eat the small flying things, and they are recent arrivals, which you can tell because they are not quite right yet — their behavior is calibrated for a place with more shelter than this plateau has, and they are always slightly more exposed than they are comfortable with, always about to find a better crevice, never quite managing it. They will adapt or they will leave. Some of them will adapt.

But there is one group of insects that is different in kind.

They are in the new material. Not on it — *in* it. In the yielding, halfway material at the formation-edges, in the category-gap between mineral and not. They are small enough that you need to look carefully. They are pale, almost translucent, and they move through the formation-edge material in ways that should not be possible for hard-bodied insects, which suggests the material is not as hard as it looks, or they are not as hard as they look, or both. They leave traces — channels, curves, structures within structures — that the new rock incorporates as it solidifies. The rock, where it has cured around their traces, has a different character than the rock that didn't. It is more — *articulated*, somehow. More able to hold water in useful ways. More likely to develop the microstructure that the water films need.

This might be coincidence. The rock might become articulated regardless and the insects might simply exploit the channels. But the distribution of their presence correlates with the quality of later outcomes in ways that feel like more than coincidence. And they do not eat, as far as you can tell, and they do not reproduce in any visible way, and their relationship to the structures is — charged. They avoid the structures but attend to their borders. They are in the places where the structures' effects begin and where the structures' effects have not yet arrived. They are in the margin.

What are they? They are one of the things the maker did not make but might have anticipated. Or they are one of the things the maker made and does not think of as separate from the stone. Or the distinction between those two possibilities is not one the maker makes.

---

The things in the water films are the most uncertain.

The water films are thin. A fraction of a millimeter. The things moving in them are smaller than that — smaller than you can resolve clearly without equipment you don't have. What you can see is that the movement in the films is not laminar. The flow patterns deviate from what water on a warm surface should do. The deviations are small but they are consistent and they are not random. Something is in there moving, aggregating, dispersing. Responding to something, or producing something, or both.

They are alive in the way that a process can be alive — in the way that fire is almost alive, except that fire doesn't have preferences. These do, or behave as if they do. They accumulate at certain mineral concentrations and avoid others. They cluster at the warmest parts of the surface and not the merely warm. They have been here since — you cannot say. Since there was wet warm rock. Since before the birds. Since, possibly, before the insects. They were here when the plateau was nothing but the first stone and vapor, and they were in the first water that condensed on the first stone, and the first stone is what it is partly because of what they left behind in the water, and the water is what it is partly because of what they left in the water before that.

They are native to the plateau in a way nothing else is. Everything else arrived. They emerged. Not from the maker — or not only from the maker — but from the conditions, from the warm-water margin of the possible, from the place where chemistry shades into something that chemistry doesn't fully explain anymore. The maker may have prepared the conditions knowing they would emerge. The maker may have seeded them directly. The maker may have done something that neither of those phrases captures, something for which the maker has terminology and you don't.

They do not have a life in any sense that would make them individuals. They are a population of processes, each running in its sliver of film, each feeding the next, the whole system constituting — not an organism, but a *disposition*. The plateau's water has a disposition. Toward life. Not because water always has that disposition, but because this water has been shaped by what has been in it, and what has been in it is what emerged here, and what emerged here did so partly by shaping the water. It is circular in the way that development is always circular. The chicken is the egg's way of becoming a better egg. The water-things are the plateau's way of becoming a place where water-things can be.

---

There is one more thing. It is the hardest to write about.

You have been on the plateau for some time now. Your collar is still damp. The bird is still at approximately two meters, occasionally closer when she forgets to be careful. The low sound is still in your sternum. And you have noticed, in the last while, that you have stopped trying to leave.

This is not alarming. You have enough water and you are not cold and the light is sufficient. But you have noticed that the comfortable thing keeps being to stay, to walk one more slow circuit of the older zones, to watch one more exchange between the calling birds, to press your palm flat against the rock again and feel the pulse that is probably yours. The plateau does something to the part of you that calculates when you have seen enough.

You do not think the maker is doing this. Or: you think the maker might be doing this without intending it, in the way that warm rooms make people sleepy without the room meaning anything by it. The warmth and the contained vapor and the steady low sound and the completeness of the sensory world — the way the vapor bounds it into something experienceable all at once — these things act on attention. They act on the part of you that wants to understand something fully before moving on. The plateau is bounded and therefore finishable in principle. You keep thinking you are about to finish understanding it.

But the things in the water films are down there in their sliver-world and you have not finished understanding them. The insects in the formation-edge have a relationship with the rock that you have not resolved. The calls of the birds carry information you have not decoded. The maker has a purpose that you cannot read from the evidence.

This is what lives on the Plateau of First Stone: the making, the arriving, the emerging, and the not-yet-understood that refuses to become understood, not through concealment, but through depth — through there always being more than a centimeter further in, more than another layer of water, more than another call to account for.

The plateau is not finished. Neither, it turns out, are you. The rock knows this. The rock is being patient about it.

Press your palm flat against it again.

Feel the warmth.

Wait.
