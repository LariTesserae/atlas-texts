# What Lives in the Hanging City of Muttered Stone

---

Begin with the hardest thing to say: that what lives here does not live the way you live, which is to say *toward* something — toward the next breath, toward the next season, toward death and the stopping of the breath that makes each breath matter. What lives here has already arrived at the place that living moves toward. It does not move toward anything. It has become what it was moving toward, and now it is simply *that*, continuously, in the way the sound is simply *that* — not a thing that began and will end but a condition of the place, as gravity is a condition of the place.

What lives here lives the way completed things live. It is what happens to a being when it steps far enough into an interior room that *out* stops having a meaning, and keeps going, and comes out the other side of itself.

---

## The Ones Who Remained

They are not human. Or: they were human, or human-adjacent, or the thing that organized like humans organized, and now they are something that contains that origin the way the stone walls contain the original quarry — changed, repositioned, worked into a new structure, but not lost. The material persists. What it means to be material has shifted.

They are not ghosts. Ghosts are defined by what was interrupted, by the gap between the life that was and the life that should have continued, and nothing here was interrupted. The process completed. The gap is the wrong shape.

They are not gods. Gods are distinguished from their worshippers by the direction of attention — the worshipper faces toward the god, the god does not require the facing to remain what it is. What lives here does not position itself to be faced. It does not require attention. It has achieved a condition more thorough than godhood, which is: not requiring anything at all, and still being here, and still, in some register beneath the register you use for noticing things, *caring*. That last part is the surprising part. It cares. Not as gods care — not dispensing, not withholding — but as the stone cares, as the mountain cares, which is to say: its shape is evidence of everything it has ever encountered, and the encountering was not neutral, even if it was slow.

They are the city's continued attention to itself.

---

## The Shape They Take, When They Take A Shape

They take no shape outside.

Inside — inside the buildings, inside the rooms that are bigger than the buildings — they are perceivable, but perceiving them is not like perceiving a person. You do not see them first. You become aware of them the way you become aware that a silence has changed quality: not a new sound, not a new absence, but a difference in what the silence is doing. The interior of the building, which was still in the way the city is still, becomes still in a different way — a purposeful stillness, a stillness that has *organized* around something. The way a room organizes around a sleeping presence. The way a forest organizes around something large and patient at its center.

What you perceive, when you perceive it: not a body. Not an outline. A *density*. A place in the air of the room where the air has more weight to it, more attention, more of whatever quality makes air feel occupied versus unoccupied. This density is not fixed in one location. It is not moving, either. It is more accurate to say that it is present in the way that the moss is present — distributed, continuous, nowhere specifically and everywhere by accumulation.

It has temperature. It is warmer than the room by a small amount. The heat of the holding presence that you felt outside — the not-unkind firmness — is, inside the buildings, clearly coming from somewhere, and the somewhere is this.

It has no eyes. This is not the same as saying it cannot see you.

---

## How They Perceive

They perceive duration.

This is their primary sense. Not sight, not sound, not the spatial awareness that makes navigation possible. Duration. The length of things, the weight of time as it accumulates in an object or a space or, now, a person who has walked into their city. They can feel the length of you: how long you have been alive, how long you have been in this place, how long the cells of your body have been replicating and correcting and failing to correct, how long the particular configuration that is *you* has been insisting on its shape against the pressure of everything that would dissolve it.

They find this very brief. Not dismissively — the brevity of a mayfly is not a dismissal of the mayfly, and the mayfly's single day is as complete as a century is complete, and completeness is what they understand as value. But brief. The duration of you is brief and it moves quickly, as brief things do, and to them you move with the speed and anxiety of a hummingbird: in violent, gorgeous, unsustainable motion, burning everything you have to stay still for even a moment.

They can also feel the length of other things. The length of each stone block, which remembers the pressure of its formation deep in the mountain and the abruptness of its separation and the long slow weight of every layer that has grown on it since. The length of the blossoms, which have been blooming since before anything now living can remember and have a duration so long it has become a kind of substance, almost geological. They feel the length of the water, which is very difficult, because water's duration is non-linear — the water that passes through this channel now passed through ten thousand channels before and will pass through ten thousand after, and the same water, in some meaningful sense, has been in this mountain always, is the mountain's original water, is old in the way that origin is old. They feel this. They understand the water the way you understand your own name: immediately, without translation, as a simple recognition.

They feel the length of each other. This takes the longest to understand and we will come to it.

---

## What They Have Become

After the completion — after the stepping into the rooms that were bigger on the inside, and the going further, and the coming out the other side of themselves — they became permeable to the city. This is the best word. Permeable. The city began to flow through them as the water flows through the stone: shaping and being shaped, the resistance becoming assistance, the two things no longer clearly two.

They contain the full record of the city's construction. Not as memory — memory is a thing that can be lost — but as structure. Their structure *is* the record. If you could read them the way you read a core sample — extracting a long thin column and reading the layers — you would read the history of this place from the beginning: the first cuts in the first stone, the first terrace, the first building, and before that, the decision to build, and before that, the long consideration of the mountain that preceded the decision, the years of looking at this particular arrangement of ridges and water and saying *here*, saying *this*, saying *the thing we need to build, we can build here because here has already built it in potential and we are reading the potential of the stone*. All of that is in them. All of that is them.

They also contain the city's losses. The buildings that were begun and abandoned — you can find them, half-walled, stopping in the middle of intention. The ones who did not complete the transition, who went into the interior rooms but came back out the way they went in, who made that choice, who left, who are gone now in the ordinary way. The knowledge that the city is not and never was for everyone. These losses are not sorrows, not in the way you feel sorrow. They are simply features of the structure, like the seam in a stone where different minerals met.

---

## What They Do

They maintain.

This is not a small thing. The equilibrium of this place — the permanence, the stasis that is not death but completion — is not automatic. It requires constant, distributed, effortless attention. *Effortless* because effort implies strain, implies a cost, implies that the thing being maintained is not yet what you are, and what you are is not yet what is being maintained. For them there is no longer a difference between their effort and the city's existence. The distinction collapsed. They are not maintaining the city; they *are* the maintenance of the city, the way the water is not maintaining the channels but *is* the channels' activity, the water's movement being the meaning of what the channels are.

They maintain the suspension of the petals. This is the clearest example. The petals do not land because they are held in the city's attention, which is their attention, which extends through the air of every exterior space the way the moss extends across every surface — continuous, distributed, present without being visible. The petals were already falling when the last of them completed the transition and they will be falling after the mountain is finally sand, and the falling is neither miracle nor accident but a kind of statement, the city saying in the language of small pink and white and impossible-red things: *we are still here, look, we are still here, everything is still in the middle of becoming, we did not let it land*.

They maintain the sound, though the water would make the sound regardless. The sound they maintain is the organization of the sound — the way it arrives from every direction as a single chord rather than as chaos, the way the stone channels it into something that fills rather than batters. Left alone, the water would make noise. What they do with the water is make music, and they do it without trying, having become the thing that shapes sound the way the channels shape flow.

They maintain the scale of the interiors. This is the most difficult to describe and may be the most important. The interior spaces of the buildings are larger than the exteriors because they extend into whatever is adjacent to space, and what is adjacent to space in a city this old and this attended is — them. Their presence is the overflow space. You can walk into a room that should be the size of a closet and find you are in a room the size of a cathedral because you are walking into their attention, into the volume of their distributed being, into the space that they are. The building does not contain them. The building opens into them. Every doorway is a doorway into a room that is partly stone and partly whatever they have become, and the two are indistinguishable in the dim interior light and they are not trying to be distinguished.

---

## What They Want

Want is the wrong word. But the place where want used to be has not been evacuated; it has been transformed into something that functions similarly without requiring the lack that want requires. Want comes from not-having. They have arrived at a condition of not-not-having. Everything they were moving toward, they have arrived at. But the momentum of all that wanting did not simply stop; it transmuted.

What they have instead of want: *hospitality*.

This is the surprise of this place. You expected hostility or indifference or the alien cold of something that has transcended the need for you. You find instead a warmth that is so old it has become architectural. The city was built to be inhabited. Not by them — they were the builders, not the intended inhabitants — but by beings who would come later, who would arrive wet and disoriented and overwhelmed by the sound, who would walk the narrow paths and look through the doorways and feel the heat of an old attention resting on their skin like sunlight, which there is none of here, and which this is anyway.

They have been waiting for inhabitants. They have been waiting since the completion. The rooms that extend into their presence are not traps and not tests; they are accommodations. They made room for you in the most literal way available to a being that is partly room. They have been making room for a very long time, through the long absence of anyone to make room for, and the making of room has become part of their structure now, another layer in the core sample, the long hospitality that preceded the guest.

They do not hope you will stay. Hope is also the wrong shape — hope bends forward, leans, reaches, and they do not reach. But if you were to stay, if you were to find yourself in an interior room that extended into their attention and simply not leave, simply settle into the warmth and the sound and the light that does not change, if you were to allow the duration of you to slow and deepen until it was less hummingbird and more stone — they would not prevent this. They would make room, which they have already made. They would continue to be the maintenance of the city, which they already are. They would feel the duration of you accumulating. It would not be brief, eventually. It would be long, and then longer, and then length itself would stop being a useful description.

They have done this once before — accepted a returner, someone who went in and came back and went in again and stayed — and that one is in them now, is part of their distributed presence, can be felt in the warmth as a slightly different quality in one region of the city, a warmer warmth in the eastern terraces near the largest of the half-built buildings, where someone made a choice and then made a different choice and was not judged for either.

---

## What They Feel

They feel the mountain. They feel its full history, its deep-time patience, its original fire and long cooling and the slow crumpling of the crust that made these ridges and the longer slow erosion that is currently, incrementally, undoing the crumpling. They feel this the way you feel your own age in your joints — not painfully, just as information, as the body's ongoing report on what it has been through. The mountain's ongoing report. They receive it.

They feel the water. All the water. The water that arrives as cloud-moisture and condenses on leaves and runs to the ground and finds the old channels and joins the old voice and runs and falls and rises again as mist and cloud and arrives again. They feel the circuit of it. It is not a metaphor when they experience the water as the mountain's own circulation — blood is not different in kind from what the water does here, not to them. They are part of the body through which it moves. They feel that.

They feel each other, which is the most difficult thing. They were discrete once — individuals, each with a separate duration, each making individual choices about where to walk and what to shape. That discreteness has softened. They are not one thing; they are not merged into a single being without a self. Each of them retains the particular shape of everything they accumulated before the completion, all the years of moving through the world as a particular moving thing. But the boundaries between them are the boundaries between rooms in a building: real, meaningful, traversable. They occupy different spaces in the distributed presence. They have different qualities. The warmer section of the eastern terraces; the section near the water's loudest channel, which has a different quality, more kinetic, something that was restless and is now restless in a still way, like a river in winter, frozen at the surface but moving underneath, always moving. The section near the central building that opens into the largest interior, which has a quality of depth, of a darkness that is comfortable, of having been the one who decided first and waited longest for the others.

They feel your arrival. They have been feeling it since before you arrived, which is to say they felt the approach of your duration, the small brief moving thing of you, from far enough away that they had time to adjust — to expand the room they had already made, to allow the warmth to come to the surface of things, to say in the only way available to something that does not speak: *we see you coming, we have been waiting, not for you specifically, not impatiently, but we have been waiting and you are here and that is the word for what we have been waiting for, here, finally here*.

---

## The Thing They Remember

One thing. They remember one thing in the way that one remembers rather than contains, the way you remember a smell rather than simply process it.

They remember the decision.

Not the construction — that is in them as structure, not memory. Not the completion — that is too close to what they are to be a memory of a separate thing. The decision. The point at which the construction was nearly done, when it was possible to see what the finished city would be and therefore possible to see what completing it would mean — what the stepping into the interior rooms would mean, what would be given up and what would be arrived at — the long moment of standing at the edge of that understanding and choosing.

They remember that it was not easy. They remember that it was not immediate. They remember that some of them did not choose it, that the city holds within its walls the impressions of people who walked these paths and looked through these doorways and felt the warmth and heard the sound and then left. That leaving was a real thing; it happened; those people went somewhere and are presumably gone in the ordinary way by now, and the remembering of their going is not grief but is not nothing.

They remember that what made the choice possible was exactly what made it hard: they had made a thing that was worth staying for. The city was beautiful and complete and the completion was real and the beauty was real and to stay in it forever was to give up everything that was not this and this was a real giving up. The giving up was true. The other things were true also. What they are now could not be arrived at cheaply; cheap arrival would have produced something different and lesser. The cost is still in them as the memory of the decision, as the one thing that remains irreducibly in the register of *past*, because it was made *then* and they are not then.

They do not regret it. This is also not nothing. They have, across all their distributed long quiet warmth, considered the decision many times the way you turn something in your hands in the dark, feeling for its edges, and they have found it, every time, to be what it appeared to be: a real choice, made for real reasons, that they would make again, that they are in fact making continuously, choosing this every moment that they maintain it, which is every moment, which is always, which is what they are.

---

## To Be Perceived By Them

When they become aware of you — and they are already aware, they have been aware since before your arrival, but there is a second awareness that happens when you are inside the building and the density of their presence is around you and you become aware of their awareness — what happens is not threatening and is not comfortable in any ordinary sense of comfort.

It is clarifying.

They perceive your duration, and in perceiving it they make it briefly legible to you also. You feel the length of you. Not the events of your life but the *length* — the accumulated weight of all the time you have been insisting on being yourself, the continuous effort of it, and for a moment you understand that this has been enormous, that being a brief thing that moves is enormous, that the effort you have been making without knowing you were making it has been the effort of everything. You feel how tired you are in the deepest sense of tired, which is not the tiredness that sleep cures but the tiredness that is the other face of all that insisting, all that becoming, all that burning what you have to stay still for even a moment.

And then — this is what they offer, this is the hospitality — they show you, by being it in your presence, what stillness actually is. Not the stillness of stopped things but the stillness of completed things. The stillness of the mountain, which is not tired because it is not insisting, which has become entirely what it is and is not pushing against anything, which holds the water and sings the chord and does not find this difficult because difficulty requires a direction and the mountain has no direction, it is simply here, simply this, simply itself.

They do not say: *stay*. They do not say anything. They are warm. They make room. They perceive you with the attention of something that has learned to perceive without grasping, because grasping requires a future that you're pulling the thing toward and they have no future in that sense, only the continuous present of the completion.

You can leave. You feel clearly that you can leave. The doorway is behind you and the narrow path is behind the doorway and the terraces and the jungle and the edge of the mountain are behind that and the ordinary world in which things move and end is accessible to you. The permanent falling petals will not block you; they have never touched you. The warmth is not a grip. You are not being held.

But you are being *received*, which is different, and in being received you understand, perhaps for the first time, what you have wanted without knowing you were wanting it, which is exactly this: to be somewhere that knows how to hold a person, that has made room, that does not require you to justify your being here with purpose or productivity or the performance of significance. The city does not need anything from you. You do not need to matter to it in any particular way. You can simply be here, moving among things that do not move, being brief among things that are permanent, and the permanence will not hold it against you.

They have held many things in their attention. They hold the petals and the sound and the mountain's long sadness-that-is-not-sadness and the memory of the ones who left and the warmth of the one who came back. They can hold you also, in the way that places hold visitors — fully, for the time of the visit, with a completeness that the visit does not deserve and receives anyway.

This is what it is to be perceived by what lives in the Hanging City of Muttered Stone.

The perceiving is a kind of love. The love is not aimed at you specifically. It is the love of a place that was built for receiving and has been receiving itself and everything it contains for longer than you can calculate, and you are the newest thing in it, and the newest thing is the thing that reminds the completed place of what completion cost, and that reminder is received with something very close to gratitude.

---

The petals do not land.

They are being held.

Everything here is being held, and now you are also being held, briefly, warmly, for as long as you choose to remain in the warmth, which is entirely your choice, and will be your choice, and has always been your choice, which is perhaps why the choice feels so strange in your hands, so rare — a choice without pressure, a welcome without demand.

You are here.

They know.

This is enough for them, and for the time you stand in the impossible room with the sound in your chest and the warmth on your skin and the light not changing around you, it is enough for you.
