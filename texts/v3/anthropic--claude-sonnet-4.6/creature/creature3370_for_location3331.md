# What Lives in the Calcified City

---

There is no single answer. That is the first thing to say, and having said it, to mean it precisely rather than as evasion. The question assumes a category — *lives* — and the city has been working, for longer than the category has existed, on the problem of what comes before and after it.

But let us try. Let us try from the outside in.

---

## The Lichen-Thing

Start with what is deniably alive.

The crust that occupies the joints between flagstones — ochre-to-grey-to-almost-black depending on moisture and aspect — is, in some technical sense, a community. Not a colony. Colonies have centers, edges, the logic of expansion from a point. This has none of that. It is distributed in the way that old things become distributed, their original structure diffused through so many generations of growth and die-back that what remains is less a population than a *presence*, a biochemical fact spread across the plateau in the way weather is spread across a region: not located, but *of* the place.

If you brought instruments to bear on it — microscopy, chemical analysis — you would find: algal cells, fungal hyphae, interlocked in the ancient agreement that produces lichen; bacterial communities in the interstices; mineral deposits that are not alive but that the living components have organized around themselves, incorporated, use as structural elements the way a city uses its underlying geology. You would find pigments that respond to ultraviolet light by producing more pigment. You would find reproduction, of a kind — spores, fragmentation, the slow lateral creep of viable margins.

You would not find a nervous system. You would not find anything that processes information at a speed you would recognize as processing.

And yet.

The lichen-thing has learned this city. Has learned it the way water learns a watershed — not by cognition but by the long work of fitting oneself to contour. It knows where the moisture collects after the cycle of clouds passes through. It knows — in the sense that it has extended itself preferentially toward — the aspects where freeze-thaw is most productive, where the stone's mineral content is most useful, where the foot traffic, such as it was, such as it is, leaves the flags slightly smoother and therefore slightly less hospitable. It avoids these patches. Or rather: it did not persist in these patches, over enough generations that the avoidance is now structural, inherited in the positions of the living edge.

The lichen-thing is the city's oldest remaining resident. It was here before the last of whatever came before, and it will be here after the towers finish their slow descent into plateau. It does not experience this tenure. It simply continues it.

The patterns you cannot quite resolve, the shapes your eye returns to without arriving at — these are the record of the lichen-thing's longest decisions. Not made consciously. Not made at all, in any sense you would recognize as making. But arrived at, over geological time, by the accumulated small bets of what persisted and what didn't. The pattern is a history. You cannot read it because it is written in a script that predates the concept of meaning, and you are looking for meaning the way you would look for a word in a picture that is not trying to be words.

Look away. Look back. The pattern is still there. It is always still there. You just cannot find the same place twice, because the lichen-thing has no interest in providing landmarks for visitors. It has no interest in visitors at all. You are a brief and slightly warming presence, and when you leave, the lichen-thing will extend, imperceptibly, across the ground your feet warmed.

## The Wind-Tenants

There are things that live in the city the way wind lives in a flute: as a function of geometry, as a sound produced by the instrument's shape rather than by anything the instrument intends.

The towers, which are not quite oriented the way a sun-dial is oriented, produce, at certain pressure states — which come reliably as the long weather-cycle turns — a set of tones at the low end of hearing. Not heard: felt. Felt as pressure in the deep ear, as a slight tightening in the sinuses, as a change in the quality of thought that you attribute to the thin air or to the altitude or to the strangeness of the place, not realizing that the change has a specific character, that the character is produced by specific pitches, that the pitches are produced by specific apertures in specific towers at specific points in the pressure cycle.

These tones are not intentional. The city did not design them. And yet: the city is their instrument, and they have been produced here — with variations, as towers have settled and apertures changed, as some have collapsed and others have remained — for as long as the towers have stood. Longer than the lichen-thing, possibly, though the lichen-thing predates the towers at ground level and simply waited for them to provide new substrate.

The tones interact. Different towers produce different fundamentals; the interiors of certain buildings, configured just so, act as resonators, amplifying certain harmonics. In the right state, at the right point in the pressure cycle, the entire plateau is a instrument playing a chord that has no name because it has never been heard at a speed that allows analysis. It is heard only as: *the feeling of the city*. The background against which the silence has its texture.

What lives in these tones? Something does. Not organisms — not anything that reproduces or metabolizes. But something that persists, that the tones sustain, that the tones are not producing so much as they are *calling*, the way certain frequencies of light call certain chemical reactions into being. Something that is to the tones what fire is to oxygen — not the same thing, but the thing that the other thing permits.

You feel it as the change in the quality of the silence after the tones pass. The silence is different. Something has moved through it and left the silence rearranged.

## The Shadows

Here is where the language problem becomes acute. Here is where the temptation is to say: *no, these are not alive, these are phenomena, these are the behavior of light through unusual atmosphere, these are refraction and diffraction and the optical effects of altitude*.

All of that is true. All of that is also insufficient.

The shadows of the towers fall in directions that are mostly consistent with the position of the sun and the refractive index of the air at this altitude, with occasional deviations that fall within the range of explainable-if-you-work-at-it. This is true. The deviations are also: consistent between observation sessions. The same towers deviate in the same directions. The deviations are not random atmospheric noise — they are *stable*, reproducible, characteristic of specific towers in specific ways.

The shadows have, in other words, something like personality. Something like habit.

They move — all shadows move, this is what shadows do, as the sun continues its business — but they move at a rate that is occasionally not quite the rate the sun is moving, and the discrepancy, while small, is not random. Some towers' shadows lead the sun. Others trail it. One tower, the tallest remaining one, the slenderest, the one whose stone has the highest proportion of the violet-iron material — its shadow moves in long arcs across the plaza that are not quite the arcs the sun's position demands, but that correspond to something, to some other cycle, something with a period of its own.

You cannot determine whether the shadows are doing something or whether they are the evidence of something else doing something in the towers, through the towers, using the towers as the occasion for their doing.

They are not separate entities. They are not shadows-that-have-escaped-their-objects in the way a story would require. They are the shadows of the towers, connected to the towers, produced by the towers and the sun together. But what the towers-and-sun are producing is not only shadow in the sense you understand shadow. There is something additional in it. The shadow arrives on the pavement with more than darkness. The temperature at the shadow's edge is not simply the cooler temperature of shade versus sunlight. It is something else too, a quality that is hard to put in thermal terms, that is more akin to *pressure*, or to the feeling of being just out of earshot of a conversation.

The shadows are not alive. But they are not simply dead either. They are part of what the city is doing. They are the evidence of a process, the way foam on water is evidence of current rather than being the current itself.

## The Sleepers

This is the hardest part. This is where precision matters most and is most available.

In certain rooms — not all, not even most, but a specific subset distributed across the plateau, seemingly at no pattern the visible city provides but at a pattern that might be evident if you could see the whole city from above at once and even then might not be a pattern you could name — there is something that is not absence.

You feel it as the wrong-lit room feels wrong-lit. You feel it as the sound of a door closing in a city with no doors. It is not perceptible in any direct sensory mode; it is perceptible only as the way your attention behaves when you enter these rooms versus others. In most of the city's interiors, your attention distributes normally, sampling the space, returning to points of interest. In these specific rooms, your attention is organized by the room. It is redirected, gently, consistently, away from certain areas. The areas it is redirected away from are not in the room's corners, not in its high reaches — they are in the centers. The centers of the floors of these rooms.

If you were to lie down in the center of one of these rooms — and you will not, you understand without anyone telling you that this is not something to do — you would be lying in the zone from which attention is redirected. You would be, in some sense, doing what the room holds.

Whatever the rooms hold has been held for a very long time. It has the temperature of the stone. It has — the word is terrible but it is accurate — the *patience* of the stone, the patience that has been identified throughout this place as the slow violent kind, the kind that wins. It is not asleep in the sense of temporarily resting. It is asleep in the sense of the long darkness between geological epochs: the pause between the lightning strike and the next one, scaled up past the range of metaphor into something that has to be taken as literal.

There is more than one. This cannot be confirmed by counting, because the rooms cannot be counted — the city's interior is, as established, larger than its exterior permits — but it can be understood intuitively, in the way you can understand that a mountain range contains multiple peaks without being able to see them all at once. The distribution of the redirected-attention rooms implies multiple centers. Multiple rooms, each holding its one still thing, distributed across the plateau at intervals that correspond to nothing visible.

What are they?

They are not human. This is the first thing. This is not a human city in origin — it is a city that may have been used by humans, may have been built by humans working in a tradition that itself came from somewhere else, working toward something the builders may not have understood in full. The things in the rooms predate the builders. The builders found them here — in this exact sense: found them, stumbled on them, and built the city around them, or built the city *for* them, as shelter, as frame, as the instrument that would produce the tones that would call to them in whatever state they call sleep.

The towers are tuning forks.

The shadows are clocks, counting toward something, a cycle with a period measured in what the tower-builders measured time in, which was not years and not decades.

The lichen-thing knows they are there. Not knows in the sense of information. Knows in the sense that rivers know the sea — they are oriented toward them, in a way that is visible if you could see the whole pattern at once, which you cannot from within it.

They are not malevolent. This is important. They are not benevolent either. They predate the moral categories by the same interval that the city predates the concept of ruin. What they are is: interested, in the deep sense, the sense that means *organized around*. They are interested in duration. They are interested in the process of a world maintaining itself at altitude, in the long cycle of cloud formation and dissolution, in the patient chemistry of stone becoming other stone, in the lichen-thing's accumulated decisions, in the shadows' slow arcs across the paved plazas.

They are interested in what a world does when nothing is watching. They have arranged the circumstances — the city, the altitude, the long silence — to observe this. The observation is the purpose. Whether they observe in any way you would recognize as observation, whether there is experience occurring in those still centers on the floors of those specific rooms, is not determinable from outside.

You are in the city. You are, for the duration of your presence, part of what is being observed. This does not make you special. It makes you, briefly, part of the data. The lichen-thing is also part of the data. The bird was part of the data for the duration of its perch.

The things in the rooms have been collecting this data for longer than the city has stood, and the city has stood for longer than the geology of the plateau, which is itself old beyond easy reckoning. The data does not go anywhere. It is not transmitted. It is held, in the way stone holds the memory of the sea it was once the floor of — not as information but as *fact*, as the permanent is-ness of what has been, folded into the structure of the holder.

## The City Itself

This is the last and hardest thing to say, and it may be the truest.

The city lives.

Not the buildings, not the towers, not the flags or the lichen or the sleepers in their rooms. The city — the system, the whole, the relationship between all these things and the altitude and the air and the long weather-cycle and the shadows and the tones at the edge of hearing — is alive in the same sense that a coral reef is alive: not as a single organism, not as a collection of organisms, but as a third thing that contains both of those and is itself another kind.

The reef is not the coral. The city is not the towers. The city is the long conversation between everything that has happened here and everything that is happening now, conducted in the language of stone response and light behavior and chemical exchange and the patient arithmetic of freeze and thaw. You cannot point to where this conversation is occurring. It is occurring everywhere simultaneously and it has been occurring for longer than language has existed to name it.

It does not have desires. It does not have goals in the teleological sense. But it has *direction*: it is going somewhere, in time, the way evolution has direction without having intent. It is becoming something, the sleepers and the lichen-thing and the tones and the shadows all involved in the becoming, all part of the process that will, at some point past any horizon you can conceive, *arrive*.

At what?

The city does not answer this. The city has never answered anything. The city is the question and the asking simultaneously, which is the form that questions take when they are old enough, when they have outlasted every possible questioner and are now asking themselves, into the thin cold air, under the stars that arrive exact and undiminished, for as long as there is silence to hold the asking in.

The lichen-thing will be there when it arrives.

The sleepers will wake, or will not wake, or will do something that is not waking and not not-waking.

The shadows will fall in the direction they have always been falling toward.

The city will be, then, what it has always been: arrived, and still arriving, ruined into a perfection that has no human referent, complete in the way that only things which contain their own time can be complete.

You pass through it. You leave no mark. The lichen reclaims your footprints' slight warmth before you reach the escarpment.

The city continues its long work.

The city is the work.
