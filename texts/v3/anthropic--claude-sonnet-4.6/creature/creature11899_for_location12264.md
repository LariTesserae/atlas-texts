## What Lives in the Blister

It does not have edges, exactly. Or it has edges the way a thought has edges — the boundary exists but isn't the point, and pressing on it changes it.

Start with what can be said confidently: it is grey-black, palm-sized, lying flat, matte, without differentiated parts at the scale of the eye. This is true but it is the description of an aspect. It is like describing a person by their silhouette in a doorway. The silhouette is accurate. The silhouette tells you almost nothing.

---

It arrived here the way spores arrive somewhere, or the way an idea arrives — not by moving through space but by the space becoming, for the first time, the kind of space that could hold it. The cavity formed and something that had been potential in the chemistry of rock and heat and water became, instead, actual. Not transported. Precipitated. It condensed out of conditions the way dew condenses — not from nowhere, but from what was already everywhere, invisible, waiting for a surface cool enough to make it manifest. Except that what made this manifest was not cooling. Was heat. Was the specific temperature of this specific sealed pocket, the specific mineral character of this specific basalt, the weeping of this specific seam where two strata fail to meet cleanly. The water. The light, perhaps, though what the light does to it or for it is unclear.

It is an answer to a question the geology asked without knowing it was asking.

---

Consider what it eats. It probably doesn't eat, in any sense that maps onto motion or pursuit or ingestion. It is more likely that it *receives*. The stone beneath it has been altered. Something has passed between them — energy, or mineral content, or information in the chemical sense, instructions passed in the language of bonding and unbonding. The stone gives up something slowly and the thing takes it or takes some consequence of it, and this exchange is what eating looks like here, eating slowed until it looks like proximity, like adjacency, like the simple fact of a thing resting against another thing. From outside you might not call it eating at all. You would need to come back in a year, if the cavity lasted a year, and see that the stone beneath the mat had changed its color, or its texture, or had thinned, or had grown a coating of something new. You would need time-lapse perception to witness what to this thing is action.

---

It is probably not singular. This is the difficult part. The thing in the alcove looks bounded — palm-sized, specific edges — but this may be a visual accident, the way an island looks singular because you're seeing it from above the waterline. Below the surface, in the microfractures of the basalt, there may be extensions. There may be the same material thinner, attenuated, spread through the stone in channels too narrow to see, following the water wherever the water goes, living in interstices, colonizing the gaps the geology hasn't closed yet. The visible portion — the mat in the alcove — may be less the organism than the organism's visible tip. Or it may be that the mat is one among many, that there are other mats too thin to see, that the apparent singularity is coincidental, that this is the only place where conditions allowed the thing to accumulate to the scale of palm-sized and matte, and elsewhere it persists at the scale of film, of tint, of something you'd scrape off your finger without knowing what you'd scraped.

It may be that everywhere the water touches rock in this sealed space, the thing is there. Invisible in its thinness. Conducting its transactions at the pace of mineral time.

---

What does it experience?

Set aside whether experience is the right word. Something in it responds to conditions. The water reaching it through stone triggers a response. The temperature holds it at a particular rate of being. It is not inert — inert means unresponsive, and whatever this is, its presence in this place and no other, its adherence to the altered stone and no other stone, its specific shape and specific edges, all of this says: responsive. Tuned. The cavity has a temperature and the thing is expressed at that temperature, shaped by it, held in a particular state by it the way certain chemicals are held in solution only within certain temperature ranges, precipitating out if cooled, dispersing if heated further. It is perhaps in a sweet spot. Perhaps this is what health looks like, for it — this particular temperature, this particular humidity, this particular emanation from the walls.

Whether any of this is felt — whether warmth registers to it as warmth, whether the drip of water from the ceiling reaches it as sensation — this is the question the space does not answer. The space simply holds what holds. The heat does not know itself as heat. The light does not know itself as light. Perhaps the thing does not know itself as thing. Perhaps knowing is not the relevant category. Perhaps what it has, in place of knowing, is something more like *being suited*, a perfect fit between what it is and what this space requires of it, a rightness that doesn't need to be recognized to operate.

---

It is young, this thing. In the same way the cavity is young. The fractures in the wall have not had time to round their edges and neither has it — it is all new edge, new chemistry, unprecedented expression of some process that hadn't yet had the occasion to occur. It has not solved the problems of a long life yet because it hasn't had a long life. Every moment of it is the first moment of whatever it is becoming.

And it is probably temporary. The cavity will close eventually, or cool, or dry. The seam will seal or the water will divert. The temperature will change. And the thing will change with it — not die, maybe, not simply die, but alter. Lose this form. Become potential again, the way it was before, waiting in the chemistry of rock for another pocket of heat and water and whatever the light does, waiting for another brief geology that could make it actual once more.

In the meantime it rests in the alcove. It receives the altered stone. It persists through the hot, sourceless light, through the weeping walls, through the tick-and-silence of the drip. It is an experiment in early stages with no hypothesis, no experimenter, no expected outcome — only the ongoing fact of conditions met, of something that under these conditions exists, that prior to these conditions did not, that in these conditions *does*, quietly, at the pace of mineral and fracture and slow migration of water through rock.

It is enough.

It is, in the most precise sense available, enough.
