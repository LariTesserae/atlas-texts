# The People of the Arrested Plateau

---

## What They Are

Begin with what they are not, because what they are not has shaped them more completely than most things.

They are not in a hurry. This is not a cultural value they have adopted — not patience practiced as discipline, not slowness cultivated as philosophy. It is structural. It runs through them the way the lipid suspension runs through the organisms they live inside, not a mood but a medium, the thing that other processes happen within. To be in a hurry here would require believing that there is somewhere to arrive that is different from here, and that belief does not survive long in a place where the horizon has been the same for longer than the human nervous system knows how to imagine. The hurry does not leave dramatically. It drains, gradually, the way the body sheds something it no longer needs, and most people do not notice it going until one day they reach for it and find the reaching strange — like groping for a cloak in a room that is the right temperature.

They are not afraid of the living material of their world. This sounds obvious but takes a long time to appreciate. Most peoples, in most places, draw a line between what is built and what is organic, between the city and the body, between the tool and the creature it was not. Here this line does not exist and has not existed in living memory and may never have existed. The walls breathe. The floor has a metabolic temperature. The ceiling bioluminesces according to a rhythm that is not human. And the people move through this with a quality that is not exactly comfort and not exactly trust, but something more fundamental than either — a quality closer to basic orientation, the kind of orientation that lets you walk a familiar room in the dark. Not that you are not in the dark. But that the dark is yours.

They carry the symbiont. All of them do, from birth or from threshold-crossing, and they have for long enough that they understand themselves as partly continuous with the organisms around them. Not as a metaphor. The chemical conversation between their bodies and the building-organisms is constant, low-level, below the threshold of attention in the way that breathing is below the threshold of attention — present when you focus on it, absent when you do not, never entirely stopped. They do not think of themselves as discrete in the way that most humans who live in made environments think of themselves as discrete. The self, here, has a permeable edge. Where it ends and where the canopy begins is a question with a complex answer that most people do not bother to ask and would answer differently on different days.

## The Body

They tend toward a particular kind of stillness when standing — not rigidity, not tension held, but a quality of mass that has been set down. The weight is low, the stance wide for the altitude, some adjustment in the inner ear over generations that has recalibrated the baseline of balance. They are comfortable sitting on the canopy surface itself, which is warm and very slightly yielding and technically alive, and they do so constantly — cross-legged, or with legs folded to the side, or simply lying full-length on the fibrous mat in a way that looks, to a newcomer, alarmingly like surrender and is, to a resident, simply rest.

Their breathing is measured. Acclimation to altitude over many generations has produced lungs that expand fully and slowly, that extract the maximum from each breath, that do not waste. This is reflected in how they speak. Sentences do not end where you expect them to. There is no rush to conclusion. A thought arrives at its shape through the full course of its expression, and anyone listening understands that the full course is the point, not an inefficiency on the way to the point. Newcomers interrupt frequently, initially. They learn not to.

Skin tends toward a tone with slightly more melanin than the altitude would strictly require for UV protection — the plant-surface iridescence has some reflective UV content, diffuse but constant, and there has been selection toward something that handles it. More notable: their hands. The continuous chemical conversation with the symbiont leaves traces in the dermal layers, something between a stain and a structural modification, a faint amber undertone in the palms and fingertips, most visible when light passes through the flesh, which is an experience that happens more here than elsewhere because the ambient light has that diffuse, sourceless quality of coming from everywhere. Hold your hand up to the plateau sky on an overcast day, which is every day, and the amber reads faintly through the skin. Residents don't notice this. Visitors do.

The eyes are adapted to the flatness of light here. Pupils wider than expected for the altitude. Excellent resolution in low contrast — they read fine differences in tone that most humans lose in this quality of gray-white glow. They are less good at judging distance under strong directional light, which they rarely encounter. Sunlight, when it comes, occasional and somehow surprising, makes them slow and slightly stupid for the minutes it lasts, the visual system overwhelmed by information it was not calibrated for. They laugh about this, often. The sun as disorientation — this is a running joke here.

## How They Think

It is tempting to say they think slowly. This is not quite right.

They think in a way that does not resolve prematurely. An idea arrives and is not immediately organized, categorized, settled into its place in an existing structure. It is allowed to remain in suspension — the way their plant-world's circulatory fluid is a suspension rather than a solution, things carried in a medium that does not dissolve them — and it is revisited, added to, let sit, revisited again, and only at some point that is not planned does it become what it is. This is not laziness and not indecision and not incompetence. It is a fundamentally different relationship to the process of knowing, one that has developed in a place where knowledge arrived slowly over vast spans and was not urgently useful for survival because survival had been settled at the level of the building-organisms they live within. You do not need to decide quickly here. The building has decided for you, at the level of temperature and air and structural integrity, and what is left to decide is left to take its time.

They are very good at holding contradictions without needing to resolve them. This is the thing visitors notice second, after the slowness, and find more unsettling. Two things can be true in ways that are mutually inconsistent, and this can be the state of a question for years, for lifetimes, without requiring resolution, because the place they live in contains similar contradictions at scale — the organisms are individuals and not individuals, the technology is understood and not understood, the settlement is built and grown, and all of these are true simultaneously and the landscape continues regardless. Ambiguity, here, is not a failure of thinking. It is accurate.

They are gifted observers of process. Not event — not the punctuation of change, the moment when something different appears — but the gradual unfolding of things that are happening very slowly, the kind of observation that requires sustained attention over long timespans and the ability to hold earlier states in memory not as events but as ongoing contexts. They notice when the canopy's iridescence in the northwest quadrant has been shifting toward a deeper purple over the last several seasons. They notice changes in the wind-chord's harmonic balance that reflect alterations in the branching structures' calcification, which reflect alterations in the organism's internal resource allocation, which reflect something happening below the root-level at the stone interface, which has implications for the settlement's eastern structures in perhaps twenty years. This is not calculation. It is the kind of attention that develops when you live inside something you are in continuous chemical conversation with, when the changes in the conversation are information even when they are too slow to be legibly change.

## Social Structure

Low-density, and the social structure reflects this — not in thinness, but in distribution. The closeness here is not the closeness of proximity. People live with gaps between them and are not lonely. The social fabric is woven from something other than constant contact, something more like the wind-chord of the plateau — everyone contributing to a structure that is not any individual's and is not really audible as individual voices, but that is unmistakably present, unmistakably itself, shaped and sustained by the presences within it.

There are groupings that function like families and are not quite families, organized less around birth and kinship than around symbiont compatibility — it turns out the symbiont cultures vary between lineages, having drifted genetically across the generations, and people whose symbionts are in particularly fluid communication with each other find a kind of ease in shared spaces that is difficult to articulate because it happens below articulation, in the chemical register, but that reads socially as something like deep familiarity, the kind of ease of longtime intimacy reached with startling speed. Groups that share high symbiont compatibility form stable household structures naturally. Whether this is chosen or chemical — whether there is a difference — is one of the unresolved questions they carry without urgency.

Decision-making is neither hierarchical nor democratic in any form a visitor would recognize. It is more like the process by which the building-organisms allocate resources: distributed, chemical, slow, emergent. Something needs to be decided. People are aware of this, in the same way that people are aware of ambient temperature. They continue doing other things. Conversation happens, not structured debate but ongoing texture, and over time — days, weeks — a shape emerges in the conversation that was not planned and is not anyone's, and at some point the shape is legible enough to act on. The acting-on is usually unanimous in the sense that nobody objects, not because objection is suppressed but because by the time the shape is legible, the people in the conversation have been part of making it and it has already incorporated what they would have said. This is very slow. It works extraordinarily well for questions that are not urgent, which is most questions, because survival has been delegated.

For urgent things, there are designated people — not leaders, not in authority, but people who have demonstrated a particular competence at reading the emergent shape of a conversation while it is still forming, who can say *the shape is here* earlier than others can see it, and who are trusted in this reading the way the gliding fauna are trusted to read the wind-harmonics — not because they command the wind, but because they have learned to feel it accurately. These people are not elevated above others. They are consulted at moments of urgency and otherwise live ordinary lives, which are long and quiet.

There is no writing here. There never has been. This is worth sitting with. A civilization of some antiquity, embedded in biology that constitutes an extraordinary wealth of accumulated knowledge, and there is no text. The knowledge is held the way the canopy holds its chemical history — in the structure of things, accessible through the right kind of contact, not through representation. There is an oral tradition, but calling it oral is insufficient because it does not only pass through voice. It passes through the symbionts, in a fashion — not literally memory-transfer, but a kind of calibration, a priming of the chemical conversation that a carrier who has received it understands as a slightly altered sensitivity to certain things. You have been told something and also made capable of noticing it. The tradition and the acuity it produces travel together.

This means knowledge can be lost. Is lost. Has been lost. Things that prior generations knew and passed through their symbionts that were not passed on completely, that degraded in the transmission, gaps in the calibration that now produce an absence — not an awareness of absence, just a region of less sensitivity, the way a scar has less feeling. They are not troubled by this. Or rather, they are troubled by it the way they are troubled by many things: with a settled acknowledgment that does not demand repair. The lost knowledge shaped the present. The shape remains. The explicit content is gone. The landscape does not appear to have noticed.

## What They Make

Given that the medium of their technology is the living material of their world, and given that their material culture is the canopy itself shaped and elaborated, it would be easy to say they make nothing. This is wrong.

They make *arrangements*. The word is insufficient but it is closer than the alternatives. An arrangement is a thing done to the world's own substance that causes the world's substance to do something it would not have done, or to do it differently, or to do it here rather than there. The arrangements are not imposed — not sculpted, not constructed from material that has been removed from its context and shaped in isolation. They are grown, but grown is also insufficient, because grown implies a passivity in the grower, a stepping back and waiting. What they do is closer to composition — they introduce conditions, they adjust gradients in the chemical conversation, they create situations for the organisms and for themselves and let the subsequent development produce what neither party would have produced alone.

The results are beautiful in a way that takes calibration to see. Nothing here has a line, a hard edge, an angle imposed from outside. Everything has the geometry of growth — radial, branching, accretive — but shaped by human intention, which gives it a quality of being resolved, of having a center and a direction without having been forced into one. A chamber in the settlement has the feeling of a space that knows what it is for, that has organized itself toward a purpose, even though no one built walls around a purpose. The purpose grew into the space while the space grew toward the purpose, and the two met somewhere in the middle where they have been ever since.

They make sounds. Music, or what they call music, which is not distinguished from other skilled interaction with the wind-chord of the canopy. A musician here is someone who has learned to play the calcified branching formations — to draw particular frequencies from them, to layer harmonics, to produce temporary alterations in the plateau's continuous chord that read as presence within it, as intention within it, the way the gliding fauna's steering reads as navigation within the wind rather than against it. The music and the landscape are in dialogue, and the music is partially the landscape's music, drawn out and shaped. Visitors find this either overwhelming or inaccessible, depending on their capacity to hear the chord before they can hear what is being done to it.

They make no weapons. They have at various points in their history needed to, or nearly needed to, or imagined needing to, and each time the need resolved before the making began — through the slowness of their decision processes, through the time that passed, through events that changed while they were still examining the conditions that would produce the decision. Whether this is wisdom or luck or the pace of their thinking simply running longer than conflict lasts is a question they hold unresolved with their usual equanimity.

## What They Know

They know everything about the organisms they live inside, except they do not know it in the way that knowledge is usually understood. They cannot explain how the organisms work. They cannot replicate the organisms' chemistry from first principles. They do not know, exactly, what the symbiont does or how, only that it does it and that living without it in this place is difficult and living with it here is not. The knowing is embedded in practice, in the calibration the tradition provides, in the accumulated chemical adjustment of generations of symbiont-mediated relationship. It is knowledge that cannot be held outside the context that produces it, that exists only in use, that would not survive extraction and analysis.

They are, by any external measure, poor cosmologists. They have not mapped their sky, have not observed the stars with any systematized rigor — the cloud cover defeats systematic observation, and the unresolved tradition of knowledge makes systematizing difficult anyway. They have mythology of celestial things, not astronomy. The myths are not naive. They are rich and precise and full of relationships between phenomena, and they have the quality of the plateau's thought generally: comfortable with ambiguity, good at holding contradictions, interested in process rather than punctuation. The sky in their myths is not a map but a conversation.

They know grief. This matters to say because a place this settled, this absorbed, might be imagined as past grief, as having processed its mortality into serenity. It has not. Death here is what it is everywhere, the cessation of a particular conversation that will not recur, and the loss is felt fully and mourned fully and not argued with. What is different is that the mourning has a container — the canopy continues its metabolic rhythm, the wind-chord continues, the bioluminescence in the ceiling continues its slow pulse that does not know about the loss and does not change for it — and the container holds the grief without dismissing it, so that the grieving person is surrounded by the evidence of continuation without being told that continuation means their loss is small. The plateau does not tell you anything about your loss. It simply continues. At some point the continuing is its own kind of company. This takes as long as it takes.

They know that they do not know how long they have been here. The oral tradition does not have a beginning — or has a beginning that is clearly mythological, a story of arrival that is not remembered as arrival but as the structure in which earlier stories are set, a foundation story that all foundation stories eventually produce when they are old enough. The question of origin does not trouble them. The question of the organisms' origin troubles them somewhat more — not as an urgent question but as a genuine one, held with interest, turned over occasionally, left to sit. What solved this problem. What happened that there is now a solution. How long. The answers they have are not answers but orientations, directions of wondering.

## How They Are With Visitors

Visitors arrive rarely, and not by accident — the plateau is not on any route to anywhere else, and reaching it requires both intention and effort, and the settlement does not send invitations. When a visitor arrives they are met with what reads, initially, as indifference. No ceremony, no formal greeting, no delegation, no protocol of introduction that signals significance. The nearest people acknowledge the arrival — sometimes with words, sometimes with a gesture, sometimes with a shift of attention and then a return to what they were doing — and a place is made for the newcomer to be, and a resident eventually comes who knows more about visitors than most, and sits nearby, and allows the newcomer to have whatever experience they are having.

This is not coldness. This will become clear in time, which is how most things here become clear. The settlement does not treat arrival as a crisis or an occasion because it treats most things as continuous with the existing texture of the place. A visitor is a variation in the wind-chord — present, registered, incorporated. The greeting that eventually happens is genuine. It simply happens at the pace of genuine things.

What residents want from visitors, slowly discovered through the texture of ongoing contact, is news of what changes. This is the thing they cannot easily get from each other — the experience of things changing, the view from a place where the horizon moves, where seasons produce dramatically different conditions, where the sky can be dramatic, where events occur and alter what comes after. They are not nostalgic for this. They do not wish to live in a place where things change. But they are interested in it the way one is interested in a phenomenon one knows of but has not experienced — the interest of engaged imagination, curiosity that has no urgency but is real.

In exchange they offer, for those who can receive it, the experience of actually being somewhere. Not passing through, not processing, not evaluating. Being. This is harder to give and harder to receive than it sounds, and not every visitor can accept it — some leave quickly, unsatisfied, feeling they have not found what they came for, which is often true in a way they cannot articulate: they came for the appearance of stillness, for peace as something to observe and return from, and what they found was an invitation to stop being a visitor, which they were not ready for or did not want.

The ones who stay, or who come back, undergo something that also does not have a better word than acclimation, though acclimation does not really cover it. The symbiont is a threshold — the chemical initiation that integrates a newcomer into the plateau's fabric is not reversible, or not easily, and most people who undergo it would not reverse it if they could, because the integration produces a kind of knowing — of the building around you, of the other bodies within it, of the plateau's metabolic rhythms — that, once experienced, reads as so much *more* information than what you had before that the previous state feels like deprivation. What you gain is not comfort. What you gain is contact. You were apart from your surroundings and now you are in conversation with them, and the conversation never stops, and this is different, and it is better in the way that being awake is better than being asleep — not that sleep is bad, but that there is more.

## What They Are Like at the End of Things

There is a practice here that does not have a translation and has not been exported because it requires the plateau to make sense.

When a person is very old, or very ill, or dying in any of the ways dying happens, they spend more time in contact with the canopy surface than they did before — lying on it, or sitting with the hands pressed flat against it, or being in the oldest and least-modified parts of the settlement where the integration between building and organism is most complete, where the chambers are small and warm and the bioluminescent pulse is brightest. The symbiont in the body and the organism below engage in something that is, presumably, a more intense version of the usual chemical conversation, though it is not understood what is said. The dying report — those who can still report — that the distinction between inside and outside becomes less clear. Not dissolution of self. Not loss of identity. A widening. The boundary that usually marks where the conversation happens becoming less like a wall and more like a membrane, less like a line between speakers and more like the medium the speaking happens in.

After death, the body is returned to the canopy — laid on its surface in a specific place maintained for this purpose, where the organisms' growth processes are particularly active. What happens then takes years, and the plateau residents visit the place during those years, and do not find it morbid, and do not describe what they find there but do not avoid describing it either, if asked. The body becomes part of the calcified woody tissue. Not symbolically. The organisms incorporate it. The chemical conversation that happened in life, through the symbiont, continues after death at a different register — the biomolecular content of the body entering the lipid circulation of the organism, becoming part of the slow amber movement through its interior structures, becoming part of what feeds the wax-skin that photosynthesizes in the dim sourceless light, becoming part of the chord the wind plays when it passes through the branching formations.

Whether any self persists in this is not a question they ask, or rather it is the held contradiction they carry longest and with the most care — two true things simultaneously present: the person is gone, and the person is continuous with what remains. They do not resolve this. The landscape does not resolve it for them. But the practice exists, and has existed for as long as the tradition goes back, which is further than the tradition knows, and the practice continues, and the chord continues, and in the pre-dawn cold when the calcified tissue contracts just slightly you can hear it ring.
