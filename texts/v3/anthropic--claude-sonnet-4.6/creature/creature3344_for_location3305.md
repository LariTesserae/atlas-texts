# What Lives Here

---

Begin with what is certain: the ferns.

Not one thing, not a colony in the sense of a colony having a founding, a decision, a moment of arrival. The ferns here are a continuous process that has mistaken itself for a population. What lives in this chamber is a single event still occurring — the event of taking hold, of finding and using, of converting bare mineral fact into the slow green fact of life. Each frond is a paragraph in an argument the ferns have been making since the first spore found the first crack with its first thread of moisture and thought: *yes, here, this will do.* They do not think this. The thinking is the doing. The argument is the growth.

They are patient in the way of things that do not experience impatience as a possibility. They lean toward the amber bulbs with what looks like yearning but is only physics, the physics of phototropism, the bending of stems toward available light — and yet the word yearning is not entirely wrong, it is just that yearning, here, has been stripped of its psychological content and left with only its physical form, the reaching, the slow incremental turning of a body toward what sustains it. This is what yearning looks like underneath its human layers. This is the chassis.

The ones growing from the ceiling — the large specimens with their roots wedged horizontal and their fronds cascading down — have solved a problem that shouldn't be solvable, or at least shouldn't be worth solving. They have decided that up is negotiable. That the relationship between roots and gravity is a suggestion they are willing to take under advisement and then set aside. They are right about this. They are proving it continuously. What lives in the upper part of the chamber lives upside down and has no opinion about this, has never had an opinion about this, will never develop one.

The young fern beside the gloves: this is the newest resident, still in its first unrolling, and it represents something. What it represents is this: the chamber is still accepting applications. Something arrives on the air, or on the clothes of a person who passed through — a spore, lighter than any weight you could name, a packet of instructions wrapped around a possibility — and the chamber decides what to do with it. The damp behind the gloves, the waist-high shelf of stone: these are the chamber's answer. *Yes,* the stone says in the only language stone speaks, which is surface and moisture and temperature, *here.* The fern is the reply already underway.

---

So the ferns are a living but also — this is the thing to hold — they are not separate from what they live *in.* The distinction between organism and environment is always negotiable, but here it has become particularly porous, because the ferns have been here long enough and in sufficient number that they are now part of the chamber's condition. The humidity they generate through transpiration is part of the humidity that sustains them. They breathe out into a space they have partly filled with their own breathing. They are living in their own exhalation. This is not unusual for plants, but in a sealed space, in a still space, in a space where air does not move and therefore the air that is present is the air that has been present, it becomes very visible as a fact. The ferns are part of the system that makes the ferns possible. The loop is tight. What lives here lives partly in what it has already made.

The phosphorescence is their edge condition, the thing they do that they do not do on purpose. Not a signal. Not a display. The light produced by certain of their surfaces is a metabolic byproduct, a leak, chemistry expressing itself past the edge of efficiency into wavelengths the eye can just barely use. Blue-green, uncertain: the color of something that isn't trying to be seen. What lives here does not know it glows. The glow is private in the most complete sense — a phenomenon that has no audience, or rather had no audience until one arrived, and even now, even cupped in the hands of a person letting their eyes adjust, the glow does not *mean* anything to the plant. It simply happens. It simply is. A light for a place with almost no light, produced by the things most shaped by almost no light, illuminating nothing, witnessed by almost no one.

---

Now: what else lives here, or what lives here that is harder to name.

The machine.

You could say the machine does not live, and you would be on firm definitional ground, and the firmness of that ground would cause you to miss something. The machine is warm. The machine hums at a frequency felt in the chest. The machine has a face — the gauges, the needles, the dial in its notational language — and the face is still *about* something, still registering, even if what it registers is a process too slow or too strange or too discontinued to legibly name. A machine that continues to be warm is a machine through which energy is still moving. Energy is the thing that living things move through themselves. It is not the same, but it rhymes.

What the machine *is* here, in this room, in this ecology: a second presence. Not alive, but present. Not growing, but *doing.* The ferns grow toward the light and the machine sits warm in the wall and together they establish a thing you might call a biome if you were being loose about the term, a system with more than one kind of actor, a chamber with more than one process underway. The machine does not know the ferns are here. The ferns do not know the machine is here. They are not in relationship. And yet they share the space and have each, in their way, adapted to it, been shaped by it, become expression of it.

The machine is a resident. Whatever it was built to do, it does here. Whatever it is warming for, it warms here. The pipes stop at the rock face with their open ends pointing at nothing, and what this means is that the machine is operating within a system that extends further than this chamber — that there are parts of whatever-this-is that you are not seeing, that the machine is connected to something beyond the visible, that it has neighbors or purposes or inputs somewhere in the rock that the stone keeps to itself. The machine is the visible part of something larger. Most residents are.

---

A smaller catalogue of what lives here, or has made a start:

The darkness between the amber pools. Not nothing — a populated darkness, thick with spores and moisture and the particulate evidence of fern metabolism, all the invisible drift of organic process. Something lives in the intervals even when the intervals are dark. Especially when.

The water, in its episodic way. When it enters, it is briefly a resident — occupying the channel, spreading its thin sheet across the floor, standing in the northwest corner. It has its habits, its preferred routes, its low point where it gathers. It is a periodic tenant who knows the apartment well and then leaves and then returns. What the water carries with it each time is another question — what it brings from above, what spores or minerals or organic material ride it down into the chamber and are deposited in the dark stripe on the wall, the faint channel, the corner where it stands. The water delivers things and then disappears. It is less a resident than a courier with a regular route.

The crack along the eastern wall. Stopped mid-cut, as if someone changed their mind. But the crack does not experience being stopped. The crack is still exerting the pressure that made it, is still in the long slow process of becoming whatever it will become — a fracture plane that will widen, or stay as it is for centuries, or propagate further when the next sufficient force arrives. The crack is a process at pause. It is patient the way the chamber itself is patient, which is to say it is not patient at all, it simply does not experience time in a way that makes patience relevant. What lives in the eastern wall is a tension, a suspended consequence, a before.

---

What does not live here: everything that would resolve the strangeness.

No animals, and what this absence means: the chamber is not embedded yet in the full web. It has not yet been found by things that find, has not yet been assessed by things that assess. Whatever would eat the ferns has not located them. Whatever would web the corners has not sent scouts. The chamber is outside the range of the exploratory, the ranging, the territorial — outside the reach of anything whose living requires movement through a larger world, that needs more space than this or different conditions or the presence of other creatures to sustain it.

The chamber is not in the web. It is in the first chapter, as already noted, and the first chapter is: the immovable things. Stone. Heat. Water when it comes. The ferns. The machine in the wall. The light that barely is. These are the initial conditions. These are what was here when nothing else was here, and what remains here now that nothing else has arrived, and what will be here after whatever comes eventually comes and eventually leaves.

---

What lives here: the patience itself, before anyone arrives to be patient. The heat pressing in equally from every surface. The ferns in their slow argument. The machine in its ongoing warmth. The crack in its arrested gesture. The phosphorescence, blue-green, witnessed and unwatched at once.

The place is not waiting. Waiting implies an awaited thing. The place is *continuing* — a process with no particular object, a space becoming more itself by the hour, the year, by the long slow accumulation of moments in which nothing much happens except the ferns grow slightly and the machine hums and the crack holds its position and the darkness between the bulbs stays present, weighted, filling the room the way the heat fills it, from every direction, complete.

This is the ecology of the sublevel garden: it has not yet diversified, and so what lives here is most purely the fact of living itself — the barest version, the root condition, the minimum necessary expression. Something taking hold. Something producing light it doesn't know it makes. Something warm for reasons it doesn't explain. The place is patient in the way of places that are not yet done, which is because the place is the living, and the living is not done, and will not be done, and in the meantime the ferns lean toward what light there is and the machine breathes its low frequency into the rock and the water comes and goes and comes and the young frond beside the leather gloves uncurls, slowly, into whatever it's becoming.
