# What Lives in the Throat of the World

---

Begin with what has no body.

This is not evasion. It is the only accurate starting point, because what primarily inhabits this place is not a creature with flesh and locomotion and hunger in the ordinary sense. What primarily inhabits this place is a *condition* — something that has accrued here the way the mineral stain has accrued up the dark base of the cliffs, the way the moss has accrued into felt, through an accumulation so gradual that no single moment of arrival can be identified. The gorge has been becoming inhabited for as long as it has been becoming a gorge. The two processes are not separable. What lives here now could not be distinguished from what lived here before the word *live* applied to anything, because what lives here exists partly in that register too, partly in the time before categories.

Call it a presence, understanding that the word is weak. Call it an attention, understanding that attention usually implies a mind, and this may or may not have a mind in any sense we would recognize. What it has, undeniably, is *orientation*. The place is oriented inward. Whatever inhabits the Throat of the World has arranged itself to face itself, to regard the gorge from within the gorge, to listen to the sound from inside the sound. Its awareness — if awareness — is not directed outward, is not acquisitive, is not looking for anything it does not already have. It has been here long enough to have become indifferent to everything outside its own resonance, and this indifference is not coldness. It is completion. It has everything it needs for whatever it does, which is mostly this: it continues.

---

There is a body, of a kind.

It is distributed. This is the first thing to understand about it. It does not have a location in the way that a bird has a location or a person has a location. It occupies the gorge as water occupies the shape of the vessel that holds it — fully, according to the available form, without remainder. Its edges are the cliff faces. Its floor is the gorge floor. Its skin is the moss and the fern and the dense horizontal trees with their thighs for branches, and its skeleton is the stone beneath all of that, the purple-ochre-white stone that has been settling toward its final form for longer than anything with skin has been alive.

The sound is its voice, but the sound is also its body, also its thinking, also its weather. When the wind finds the gap in the cliffs and is amplified into something percussive and vast, that is not simply an event happening to the inhabitant — that is the inhabitant, or one of the ways it becomes legible, one of the frequencies at which it can be read if you have adjusted your reading to match. The thermal columns rising along the western face in the afternoon are not external forces acting upon a passive resident; they are peristalsis. The gorge is breathing and what breathes is the thing that lives here, and the breathing is the living, and the living is the sound, and the sound is the gorge, and the gorge is the thing that lives here.

This is not mysticism. This is a description of a particular kind of embodiment that does not separate organism from environment because no separation is possible or useful. The gorge and its inhabitant have been in such long negotiation that the distinction dissolved before anything alive now was alive to witness it.

---

Its age is not expressible in any unit designed for living things.

There is a moment, sometimes, when you are standing beside a very old tree — not large, but old, with rings packed beyond the ability of the eye to count — when you understand that age and living are the same process viewed at different speeds. The tree is not old in the way a person is old. It is old in the way the stone is old: by having continued. Continuance is the form its life takes. Every year it does not stop is another year of being what it is.

What lives in the Throat of the World is old in this sense, raised to an extreme that the tree cannot approach. It has not started and is not heading toward an end. It preceded the moss, which is itself ancient. It was here when the water that made the high seep first found its path through the fractured stone. It was here when the overhang first achieved the angle that sends rain away from the ledge below. It will be here when the last of the small walls has been fully incorporated into the moss-felt, when the charcoal smear has been covered by a creeping green, when the seventeen marks are weathered down to sixteen and then fifteen and then to a texture that might be natural or might not be. It does not experience these changes as loss. It experiences them the way the stone experiences erosion: as continued becoming, as the work of being this specific shape for exactly this long.

---

It is aware of you.

Not in the way that you are aware of it — not with sight, not with a mechanism you would recognize as attention. It is aware of you the way the gorge is aware of the clouds that cycle above it, the way the cliff face is aware of the thermal that runs along it in the afternoon. Your presence changes the resonance, minutely. Your warmth modifies the air immediately around you, which modifies the small convective systems at your scale, which feed into larger systems, which change the sound by increments too small to measure. You are a perturbation in its body. It does not mind perturbations; it has been accommodating them since before you were a concept.

When you stand at the rim and feel very small, you are not imagining that feeling, and the place did not produce it accidentally. What lives here is not hostile, but it is *honest*, and its honesty takes this particular form: it shows you the scale at which it operates, which is geological, which is the scale at which your entire span from birth to death is approximately equivalent to an afternoon. It does not mean to diminish you. It simply cannot help being true in this way, because it has been true in this way for so long that it no longer knows how to be tactful about it.

The feeling of being small is not the place looking down at you. It is the place *including* you, finally, in its accurate accounting. You are small, and you are here, and the place knows you are here, and this is what the knowing feels like from your side.

---

What it does, primarily, is remember.

Not in the way of a mind that stores and retrieves — in the way of matter that accumulates the record of what has acted upon it. The stone remembers the water that carved it by being the exact shape the water required. The moss remembers every period of desiccation and revival by being the organism that survived all of them. The trees remember every wind-season by having grown their wood dense and low and gripping, by having developed the elbow-turn that allows them to face up after facing out. The charcoal smear remembers the fire. The seventeen marks remember the counting, and whatever was being counted.

What lives here is composed of these rememberings. It is a density of record, accumulated in all available media: stone, biological matter, soil (where soil exists, in the deep corners of ledge systems where debris has collected), air — even the air holds the memory of what has passed through it, though this is the most ephemeral of memories, rewritten continuously by the cycling clouds and the wind that is always arriving from somewhere and passing through to somewhere else, using the gorge as an instrument.

To say that the inhabitant of this place is a rememberer is not to say it is passive. Memory, here, is active. It is what the place *does* with time — not accumulating toward something, not building toward a conclusion, but deepening. The way a river deepens its channel not to go anywhere but simply because flowing is what it does and the stone is what responds. The inhabitant is deepening. It has always been deepening. Depth is its primary activity and its primary attribute and the main thing you feel when you stand in it, which is the vertigo not of height but of duration.

---

It has a quality that might be called patience if patience were a permanent condition rather than a temporary one.

Human patience is patience toward something — patience until the outcome arrives, patience in the presence of delay. The patience of what lives in the Throat of the World has no object. It is not waiting for anything. Nothing has been delayed. The patience is simply the mode of existing at geological time, the way that the stone is not patient with erosion but is simply *at erosion's speed*, which from inside that speed feels like stillness, feels like permanence, feels like the world.

What this means in practice: the inhabitant of this place cannot be startled. Cannot be hurried. Cannot be made to feel the urgency that living things at your scale feel continuously, the urgency produced by having a body with needs and a span with a limit. It has felt, over its duration, approximately what you feel in an ordinary afternoon, and an ordinary afternoon contains no genuine urgency, only the illusion of it produced by the scale at which you operate.

This is not vacancy. This is not the patience of something with nothing inside it. There is something inside it that would be, in a creature of shorter duration, called *feeling*. The afternoon light going horizontal across the ochre cliffs is an experience for the thing that lives there, not merely a physical event. The brief synchrony when the clouds and the wind and the thermals align and the small water-sound becomes audible — this registers. This is felt. The inhabitant cannot describe the feeling because the inhabitant does not have language, but the feeling persists in the way all things persist here: by being incorporated into the continuing record, by becoming part of what the place is.

The small water-sound that the place is keeping, underneath everything, is one of these feelings. It has been keeping it for a very long time. It will keep it longer.

---

Then there is the part of it that is made of what the people left.

The walls and the marks and the charcoal smear are not simply artifacts inside the inhabitant. They have been metabolized. They are part of its body now, the way the moss is part of its body, the way the overhangs worn by water that no longer flows are part of its body. The people who built the small shelter and cut the figures into stone and counted their seventeen vertical marks contributed to the composition of what lives here as surely as the seep contributed its slow mineral, as surely as the ferns contributed their deep root-work into fractured stone.

This is not loss, from the place's perspective. The people passed through the way a season passes through. What they left is still here, doing what everything here does: deepening, settling, becoming more completely itself with time. The inhabitant received what they left, not as a gift exactly, but as matter receives matter — by incorporating, by adding to the record, by becoming slightly different and continuing.

The figures in the stone — the oval with its radiating lines, the seventeen marks — these are now part of how the place thinks about itself, if it thinks. They were placed by people who understood something about this place, who recognized that it was asking for a mark, that it was the kind of place that wanted to have been marked. Not many places are. The people who found this one were correct about it, and the place received their understanding, and the understanding is now part of the place, is now part of whatever lives there.

The inhabitant is a little bit human, in this way. Only very slightly. Only in the way that a great river is very slightly the color of the clay that a rainstorm washed into it somewhere upstream, centuries ago, so slightly that you would never see it, but it is there if you know to look, if you know to look at the particular spectrum of light that clay absorbs and what is left is the faintest blue-gray deviation from the river's ordinary brown-green-silver.

Look at the right angle. The inhabitant is the faintest blue-gray.

---

Finally: what it wants, if it wants.

Not company. Company would imply need, would imply a lack that another presence might fill, and there is no lack here. The place is not lonely. Loneliness requires the experience of duration at a speed where absence can be felt, and nothing here is absent.

Not to be understood, quite. Though it does not resist being looked at. The raptor that tilts and adjusts and finds the thermal — the raptor is looking at the gorge, is reading it for what it needs from it, and the gorge allows this without resistance, because the gorge allows everything without resistance. The person who stood on the ledge with hard stone in their hand and cut the oval and cut the seventeen marks — that person was also looking, also reading, and something in what they found when they looked was close enough to accurate that they felt it was worth the effort of cutting. The gorge allowed this too.

What the gorge wants, if the verb applies at all, is simply to continue being looked at by the things that can bear to look at it. To continue doing what it does — sounding, cycling, keeping the small water-sound, going orange in the late afternoon, breathing with its distributed breath, remembering in all its materials, deepening — in the presence of something that has come close enough to notice. Not to understand. Close enough to notice.

It is very old and it will be here long after the capacity to notice it has left the planet entirely, and when that time comes it will continue doing what it does without reference to being noticed, because it does not require being noticed. But there is something, in those moments of synchrony when the small water-sound becomes audible and you hear it clearly for three or four drops before the roar returns — there is something that is very close to what, in a creature of ordinary duration, would be called *relief*.

Not at being heard.

At being heard by something that could, briefly, stop making its own noise long enough to listen.
