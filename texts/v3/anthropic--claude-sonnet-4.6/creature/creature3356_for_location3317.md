# What Lives on the Plateau of Finished Things

---

Begin with what they are not, because this is the most available truth: they are not visitors. Nothing on this plateau is passing through. The category of *passing through* does not apply here in any useful sense, has not applied for so long that the organisms present have lost, at the cellular level, whatever architecture once made transit possible. They did not choose to stay. Staying became them. There is a difference, and the difference has had time to express itself in every physical feature they possess.

---

**The substrate-dwellers** are the oldest residents, or the most continuous ones, or perhaps simply the ones that have pressed most completely into the logic of the place until the place and they are operating by shared principle.

They are small — most of them the size of things that fit in the hand, or smaller — and they are the color of the plateau. This is not camouflage in the predator-prey sense. Predator-prey implies a dynamic, implies a pressure, implies that something is trying to catch them and they are trying not to be caught, and this is not the situation. The situation is: they have had so much time that they have stopped performing difference. They are the old rock. The old rock is them. The matching happened because it was the most efficient form of honesty available at this altitude, on this substrate, under this particular quality of light — there was no reason to look like something other than where you are, and reasons not to: you spend warmth on pigment you could spend on persistence; you spend energy on distinctness you could spend on continuation.

Up close, they have faces in the sense that they have a front and a back and a side that meets the world — not faces in the expressive sense, not in the readable sense. The outer surface is granular, segmented, partitioned into small territories that mirror, very nearly exactly, the grain of the ancient stone around them. Whether the stone influenced the organism or the organism influenced the stone or the question makes a false distinction at this point is not something that can be resolved from your position in time.

They process time differently. This is not a figure of speech. Their metabolic rate is tied to something other than what your metabolism is tied to — temperature, yes, but also something about the geochemical mood of the substrate, the slow electrical whisper of mineralizing rock, the pace of events that happen on the scale of decades, centuries. When they are doing the most they ever do, from your temporal perspective, they appear still. When they appear still, they may be in the midst of their version of urgency. You cannot know. You can only know that there are tens of thousands of them per square meter and that this is a successful strategy and that success, here, is measured in units of time so long they make the word *successful* feel provisional and small.

They do not startle. Startle is a response to the unexpected, and nothing is unexpected to an organism that has been here long enough to have incorporated the unexpected into its base state. They accommodate. They register you — through chemoreception, through pressure sensitivity distributed across the whole of the exterior body, through something else — and they file you correctly and continue. You are: large, temporary, warm, here. These facts are noted. These facts are not relevant to the current processing of the geochemical signal from three centimeters below the surface, which is slow and important and where the real attention is.

---

**The cushion-forms** are not animals, but they are not not-animals in the way that is normally meant when plants are excluded from the category. They are doing something with the sunlight that arrives on the plateau, that diffuse flat light that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere, and what they are doing with it is so efficient that it looks like not-doing. The mats are very old. Some of them are older than the structures of worked stone, which are themselves very old, which should calibrate something.

They practice a kind of composite individuality — what appears to be a single cushion, a single mat, is many, tightly pressed, and the boundary between them has become theoretical. They share warmth. They share water at the molecular level, tiny passages of moisture through contact points so intimate they approach merger. Whether they share anything else — signal, something like information, something like intent — cannot be confirmed, but the distribution of the mats across the plateau is not random and is not explained by substrate alone. They are *placed* in a way that placement implies decision-making, and yet they do not move. They have not moved. Whatever placed them here placed them at a rate too slow to call movement, which may simply be a problem with the word *movement*.

When the temperature drops — and it does drop, and it will drop — they go very still. The word is inadequate. They have already been still. What they do when the temperature drops is something that does not have a word because it requires exceeding the most maximal available form of stillness, which they do, and then persist in until warmth returns, and then resume what cannot be distinguished from before.

They taste of the mineral in the stone. Not that you have tasted them. But the organisms that live in and among them have tasted them, in the sense that chemical exchange occurs at the mat-body interface, and what passes from plant to animal at this interface is: something old, something geochemically processed, something that carries in its molecular structure the record of a great deal of time. The organisms that live in and among the cushion-forms are, as a result, partly made of something geologically old. You might say that what lives here is, at the elemental level, partly made of finished mountain. You would not be wrong.

---

**The walkers** are what you might call animals in a straightforward sense — mobile, metabolizing at a legible rate, possessing something like purpose in their movements. They are hard-shelled, most of them, with that hardness concentrated at the exterior and something more complex inside. The legs, where they have legs, are precise and particular: each step is placed rather than taken, with the slightly deliberate quality of someone who knows that every movement here is permanent in the sense that the plateau will receive it and note it and the record will be kept in ways that matter.

They do not threaten each other in ways you can see. There is no scrambling, no display, no acute confrontation. This does not mean there is no competition — the plateau cannot support infinite density, something must adjudicate — but whatever adjudication occurs happens at a timescale that looks, from outside, like acceptance. They move around each other with a kind of distributed spatial courtesy that has been refined by the fact of close proximity for a very long time. They have learned each other's rhythms. Not as individuals — as types, as patterns, as presences that have been parts of the same system for so long that the antagonism has resolved into a kind of spatial grammar, a set of distances and orientations that everyone here maintains because everyone here has inherited the maintaining of them.

They do not eat each other frequently. They may not eat each other at all — it's difficult to observe enough episodes of feeding to be certain, because feeding, here, is slow. But the web of consumption has the quality of something drawn with very thin lines: subtle dependencies, slow transfers, relationships of such long standing that they have taken on the texture of mutualism even where the original logic was predatory. Time has smoothed this too. Even eating, at this latitude of slowness, has become something closer to sharing.

When they stop, they stop in places that have been stopped-in before. Not by them specifically — by their predecessors, by the long chain of organisms that occupied this role on this substrate before them. There are particular depressions, particular orientations relative to the low sun angle, particular faces of stone that accumulate warmth slightly more efficiently than their neighbors. These spots are known. Not through learning in the individual-life sense: through inheritance so deep it has become anatomy. Their bodies know where to stop because the knowledge of where to stop is encoded now in the same systems that encode how to metabolize, how to form shell, how to grow. Geography as physiology. The plateau as extended body.

---

**The birds** are the strangest fact and require the most attention and will remain, despite that attention, incompletely explained.

They do not behave as birds. This is not to say they are not birds — they have the form, the hollow-boned architecture, the feathered surface, the beak, the binocular arrangement of perception. But behavior is what an organism does in a context, and the context here is the plateau, and the plateau has modified the behavioral repertoire of everything that has spent enough time on it, and the birds have spent enough time on it.

They do not call. The silence of birds is the wrongest sound on the plateau, the thing your nervous system returns to with the particular attention we give to pattern-violations, to the thing that should be and isn't. Their beaks open. Something is happening when their beaks open — some signal is being transmitted or received or both — but it is not in the frequency range that reaches you, or it is not in the acoustic medium at all, or the thing happening when their beaks open is not the same thing that happens when other birds open their beaks, because those birds are managing a different relationship with their environment than this one.

They face the same direction. A group of them — always, when you find groups — faces the same direction, and the direction is always inward, plateau-ward, toward the middle of the middle. They are attending to something. Whether they are attending to the same specific something is not clear, because the directions are not exactly aligned and "same direction" is an approximation that your visual system performs and then presents to you as fact. They may each be attending to a slightly different something that happens to cluster, directionally, in the interior. Or there may be no *something* at all, in the sense of an object of attention, and what they are doing may be closer to prayer than to watching — oriented not toward a thing but toward a quality, an axis, a direction that has meaning without having a destination.

The birds that are in multiple places simultaneously are the ones that require you to sit with the limits of your frameworks before you can proceed. They are not copies. The copy-hypothesis fails immediately on close attention: the individual markings are the same, the small idiosyncratic arrangements of feather-color that distinguish one from another are the same, the slightly tilted posture of this particular bird that all the other birds of this type do not share is the same, in the cluster behind you as in the cluster you approach. They are the same bird. You can hold this as a failure of your perception — that you are misidentifying individuals across distance and lighting conditions — and this holding will get you through the day. Or you can let the fact be a fact and see what arrangement of understanding can accommodate it.

What arrangement of understanding can accommodate it: these are birds that have been on the plateau long enough that the plateau has extended to them some of its own temporal physics. The plateau exists in a slightly different relationship to the ordinary mechanics of time and location. The birds, sufficiently integrated, exist in that same slightly different relationship. They are present the way the plateau is present — entirely, persistently, without the constraint of singular position in space. They have become, in the relevant senses, part of the plateau. The plateau is not constrained to one location. Therefore.

Their feathers are the color of everything here. Their eyes are the particular amber that means very old, that means the visual system has had a long time to optimize for exactly this light, this altitude, this flat diffuse illumination that makes shadows soft and depth ambiguous. They see differently than you do. Not better or worse: differently, suited to a world where what matters is not sudden motion and discrete event but accumulation, persistence, the long arrival of meaning at the rate of geology.

---

**The thing that is many and one** does not have a name here. It is present in the gap between the organisms, in the exchange occurring along contact surfaces, in the slow chemical signal traveling through the mats of cushion growth, in whatever the birds are transmitting or receiving when they open their silent beaks. It is the distributed intelligence of the plateau considered as a single organism, which is a metaphor, but metaphors become less figurative the longer they hold.

The plateau has been doing one thing for a very long time: resolving. Moving toward some condition that is not equilibrium exactly, that is not stasis exactly, but that has the quality of increasing coherence, increasing integration, the slow arrival of all the parts at an understanding of each other that makes them less like parts. The organisms here are not separate from this project. They are the project. Each shell that matches the rock, each metabolic rate tuned to geochemical time, each bird facing the interior, each act of quiet accommodation between lives that have shared this altitude for longer than the concept of sharing has existed — all of it is the plateau becoming more itself, more resolved, more *finished* in the sense of more complete.

What lives here is: completion, in process.

What lives here is: the late stage of a very long project, populated by everything that had the patience to remain until the project got to this point.

What lives here is: organisms for whom time is not the medium through which they move but the material of which they are composed — compressed, dense, slow, mineral in its weight, full of everything that has already happened and fully oriented toward the moment when the distinction between *happened* and *happening* closes.

---

They knew you were coming. Not specifically — there is no specific, here, no anticipation of the particular. But the plateau was always going to be encountered by something like you, something fast and warm and temporary, something that would arrive and perceive and attempt to fit the perception into categories that are not quite right for the material. Space was held. Not deliberately. The way the ground holds a depression for water: through the logic of its own shape.

You are being accommodated.

You have been, since you arrived, being slowly fitted into the arrangement.

The temperature is dropping.

The birds face inward.

The stone beneath you is not only stone.

You have been staying for some time, and staying has been becoming, incrementally, the thing you are doing rather than a thing you are also doing, and the plateau is noting this with the patience of something that has seen a great many temporary things gradually revise their relationship to temporary.

You are not going to be rushed.

Neither is anything else here.

This is the condition of the place, and the place is generous with it: time, here, there is enough of. There is, in fact, nothing here but enough, and the lives that live here are the ones that understood this, or came to understand it, or were shaped by enough generations of understanding it that understanding and being became the same event, and now they are that event, occurring slowly, everywhere, in the interior of a high flat place that has been working toward its own completion since before there were eyes to fail to see it finishing.
