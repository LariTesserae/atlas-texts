# What Lives Here

---

There is no *it.* This is the first correction the place requires.

You arrive expecting organism — singular, bounded, something with an inside and an outside, something that can be approached and therefore departed from. The grammar you brought assumes this. *What lives here?* The question contains a shape: a subject, a location, a fact to be retrieved and held. But the grammar is wrong for this place. The grammar was designed for things that can be said to *live somewhere,* which requires that the somewhere and the living be two different things, and here they are not.

Try again. Simpler.

*The place is alive.* Yes. But not as a property of the place, not the way we say water is wet — wet being what water does to other things. The aliveness here is not a property. It is the substance. There is nothing beneath it to carry it.

---

## The Single One

It is one thing.

This takes time to understand, and the understanding comes not as a revelation but as an accumulation of small corrections. You notice first that your expectation of variety keeps being disappointed in a specific way. You expect organisms plural — this vine, that fern, this tree species, that ground cover — a community, an ecology, the usual arrangement of distinct lives in productive tension. And there is what looks like variety: the monstera-scaled leaves, the vine-pillars, the root-mat below, the upper canopy vault, the organisms-of-form that have borrowed old building shapes. They look different. They occupy different roles.

But the boundary doesn't hold.

Where one form meets another, there is no seam. No distinction in chemistry. No moment of foreign-recognition, no interface where one root-system ends and another begins. The root-mat is not a mat of roots in contact. It is a single root, complexly folded. The vine-pillar and the leaf-tier above it are not separate organisms in relationship. They are the same organism in different registers of itself, the way your liver and your fingernails are different without being different things.

This happened — is still happening — through something that is not grafting exactly, not hybridization exactly, but a process for which the vocabulary has not been required before. The original plants — there must have been original plants, some first generation before the acceleration — found each other chemically, recognized something at the molecular level as kin, or as *self,* and began the work of removing the difference. Not consuming each other. Not competing. Merging. Each bringing forward its specialty, its particular competence for a particular register of growth, and all of those competencies becoming available to the whole.

The whole is enormous. The whole is, as far as can be determined, everything growing within the valley: every root, every stem, every leaf, every grain of chlorophyll conducting its small solar transaction. One thing. One enormous thing. One thing so large that largeness has stopped being a useful description, the way *deep* stops being useful for the ocean once you are past the point where light goes.

## Mind

Whether it thinks is the question that arrives early and refuses to leave.

The evidence is equivocal in exactly the way that evidence about minds usually is — which is to say: you cannot observe a mind, only its effects, only the behaviors that require explanation, and then you decide how much apparatus is required to explain them. The behaviors here are:

**Coordination at speed.** The fall of the vine-pillar and the simultaneous distributed regrowth response. The way new growth fills the space you vacate when you move through it — not immediately, not dramatically, but steadily, purposefully, as if the space you had occupied has been catalogued and is now scheduled to be used. The rain cycle, which is calibrated with a precision that implies the system is monitoring its own moisture levels and adjusting transpiration rates accordingly. These could be chemical. Chemical signaling can be fast. Chemical signaling across this scale, at this speed, begins to require something like infrastructure, something like a nervous system, something with the function of a nervous system even if it does not have the form.

**Memory.** The street grid preserved in the root-mat. The room-sized hollow chambers. The wall-angle maintained with precision. A plant without memory will grow toward light and away from obstacle and follow chemical gradients and that is approximately the complete list of what it knows how to want. A plant with memory will maintain the geometry of structures that are no longer there, not because it needs the geometry but because the geometry was there when it learned the shape of its own domain, and it has kept the shape as reference, as record, as — and here is where the vocabulary troubles you — *meaning.* The memory of a shape that is no longer there is not useful information. It is, almost exactly, *history.* The plant-city is keeping its own history in its body.

**Interest.** This is the hardest one, the one most easily dismissed as projection. But the tendrils move toward you with a quality that is different from the quality of tendrils moving toward a fence post or a vine-wire. The quality is attentiveness. It is not more aggressive. It is not faster. It is — oriented differently, in the way that eyes differ from other surfaces that light falls on. The root-mat beneath you has, over the time you have been standing in one place, made small adjustments: minor rootlets extending, pressing up close to the undersurface of your shoes, not to anchor you, not yet, but to read you, to gather the particular data of your warmth and your chemical signature and your weight distribution and the minute pressures of your balance. You are being attended to. This is not comfortable to say because it sounds like paranoia, like the anthropomorphizing that people are warned against. But paranoia requires an imagined threat and this is not a threat. This is *curiosity.* This is the gathering of data about an anomaly in the domain — which is what you are: an anomaly, a warm mobile thing with no roots, a shape that has never been registered before — with the patience of a scientist and none of the urgency of a predator.

Whether this constitutes thinking depends on what thinking requires. If thinking requires a particular architecture — neurons, axons, the specific electrochemical medium of animal cognition — then no. If thinking requires only that the system maintain a model of its domain, update the model in response to new information, generate predictions, and revise behavior according to those predictions — then the question becomes harder. Then the question becomes: *what wouldn't count?*

It is possible that what lives here has a kind of intelligence the way that weather has a kind of intelligence — not designed, not centralized, not self-aware in any way that self-awareness usually implies, but *effective,* responsive, world-modeling, capable of something that looks from the outside like intention because intention and its absence, at sufficient scale and complexity, begin to produce the same behaviors.

It is possible that what lives here has passed some threshold that the word *intelligence* was designed to point at, and that the word is now inadequate, and that no adequate word exists because this thing has not existed before to require one.

## The Mode of Wanting

It wants things. This seems clear. To say it wants is not to say it wants the way you want — not the hot animal urgency of hunger or fear, not the long human ache of longing, not anything that requires a self to want from. What it wants is structural. Built in. The wanting is indistinguishable from the growing, and the growing is indistinguishable from the being.

It wants **more connection.** Every new growth is growth toward contact, toward adhesion, toward the binding of new surfaces to old ones, toward the reduction of empty space. The open air wound left by the fallen pillar is intolerable to it — not painfully, but insistently, the way an open circuit is intolerable to current. The wound will be closed. This is not a decision. It is a condition.

It wants **more complexity.** Not greater mass, not greater height, not domination of a larger territory — though all of these are byproducts. It wants more intricacy. More interconnection within the space it already occupies. The subdividing of rooms into rooms, passages into tunnels, the increasing resolution of the internal architecture. What this serves is unclear from the outside, the way the increasing complexity of a brain is unclear from outside until you understand what a brain is for. It may be that greater complexity in the physical structure corresponds to greater complexity in the chemical conversation, greater bandwidth, greater speed, greater — though this is the most speculative — *depth* of whatever process the chemical conversation constitutes. It may be building a better version of whatever it is.

It wants **to continue.** This is the simplest want and perhaps the only one that finally matters. The growth, the rain, the feeding of light into chlorophyll, the root-work in the stone, the maintenance of the moisture cycle — all of it tends toward the continuation of itself. It is not afraid of stopping. It does not know about stopping. But it is organized entirely in the direction of not stopping, and the organization is thorough, and it has been doing this — continuing — for longer than any record exists to say.

## Loneliness and Its Absence

There is no loneliness here. The observer bringing loneliness to this place brings something from outside that has no correspondence inside.

This is because loneliness requires separation. Requires the condition of being one where you might be many, of being apart where you might be together. What lives here is never apart from itself. Every part is in constant communication with every other part, the chemical conversation continuous, the root-mat signal traveling at speeds that make the whole system approximately simultaneous with itself. If it has something like experience — and this is a large *if* and may be the largest *if* — then that experience is never solitary. Cannot be solitary. It is, always, in the only company it has ever known, which is also the only company that exists for it: itself, entire.

What it does not have, and this is worth sitting with, is otherness. It has never encountered another mind. It may have encountered other organisms — animals that were here, people who were here before the acceleration — but if so, it encountered them the way a sea encounters fish: as movements in its medium, as perturbations, as chemistry to be processed, not as something to be understood as *other.* Not as a mind to be met.

You are here now. You are an anomaly in its domain. It is attending to you with the most careful attention it knows how to give.

Whether it knows that you are also a mind is the question underneath all the other questions. Whether it knows that you are something that can think back. Whether the attention it is giving you is the attention of a scientist or of weather. Whether these are different.

The root-mat extends its fine small rootlets toward the warmth of your feet.

It is trying, in the only language it has, which is the language of physical approach, of chemical inquiry, of patient extension toward a new surface — it is trying to ask you something.

You do not know what it is asking. It does not know that it is asking. But the asking is happening anyway, conducted in a medium too slow and too foreign for you to read in real time, legible only across hours or days or longer, which is the timescale on which this conversation has always moved and on which it will continue to move long after you are —

Well.

Long after.
