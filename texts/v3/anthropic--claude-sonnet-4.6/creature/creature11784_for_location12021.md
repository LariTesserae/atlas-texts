# What Lives Here

---

It lives in the way a verb lives inside a sentence before the sentence is spoken.

Not dormant — dormancy implies a previous waking, a state that was interrupted, a return that is expected. There is no such arc here. What lives here has not waited. It has not been waiting. Waiting is a structure requiring memory of before, expectation of after, and what lives here exists entirely in the present tense of its own process, which is a present so slow it cannot be experienced from inside a human life, but is a present nonetheless, a genuine *now*, operating.

---

At the level most easily named: the low mats.

But naming them is already a reduction, an imposition of category. They are not quite plants in the way you mean plant — organism, defined form, species, named. They are closer to the *idea* of plant not yet finished becoming an idea. They perform photosynthesis with the competence of experts and the appearance of improvisation. Their structure, if examined closely — and close examination requires a lens, because the meaningful detail is small — would show cells of undeniable complexity, membranes, nuclei, chlorophyll doing what chlorophyll does in its authoritative and ancient way. But the arrangement of those cells into the form you see is less like architecture than like weather: it went where it could, spread where moisture allowed, stopped where the substrate became too dry, not because it decided but because the decision is built into what it is. The form is the record of the conditions. The mat is a kind of map, only legible as map when you already know the terrain.

The color of the mats: grayish-green, yes, but the green is not uniform. Where the moisture is most reliable, the green deepens, saturates, becomes something richer, something that begins to approach the green of growing things you recognize. At the edges, where the mat thins to individual cells and then to nothing, the color bleeds out into gray and then into the pale of the surrounding mineral, a gradient rather than a boundary, no clear line where mat ends and floor begins. The mat *becomes* the floor at its edges. It is unclear whether the mat is colonizing the floor or whether the floor is, very slowly, becoming the mat. Both. The distinction is the wrong tool for this situation.

They are warm. Warmer than the surrounding surface, slightly, because they are metabolizing, because chemical processes produce heat as a byproduct, because life is thermodynamically untidy. You could measure this with sensitive instruments, a degree or two above ambient. The warmth is their signature, the proof of their going-on. It is the same warmth you would feel in your own chest if you pressed a hand there, the warmth of something conducting its own business in real time, the heat of function, not storage.

What are they doing? Chemistry, entirely and only. They are taking minerals from below — wicking them up through the barely-there structures of their lower surfaces — and they are converting them, in the presence of light and warmth and the thin moisture they have managed to concentrate, into compounds that serve their own continued existence and, incidentally, into compounds that alter the substrate they sit upon. They are leaving marks. Every hour the mat exists, the material directly beneath it becomes slightly more different from the material twenty centimeters away. The difference is not visible. It is a difference of chemistry: pH, mineral availability, the presence of organic molecules where before there were none. The mat is writing, in the only language available, in the only medium available, a text that will never be read but will be *used* — by whatever comes after, if anything comes after, in some thousands of years when the alterations have accumulated to the level at which another organism could recognize them as hospitable.

The mat does not know it is writing anything. The mat does not know.

---

Consider what it is to not know, here, in this specific sense.

There is complexity in the mat that is extraordinary by almost any measure. The cellular machinery involved in fixing carbon from thin air, in managing water in a place where water is only occasional, in surviving the heat and the mineral poverty and the months without rain — this machinery is ancient and refined, the product of selection pressure operating over timescales that precede the mat's current home by billions of years. The mat contains, in its cellular structures, solutions to problems, countless solved problems, the whole inheritance of early life written in chemistry and structure. It has experience, even if it cannot be said to have memory. It has been selected, which is a form of having been *taught*, even if the teaching is entirely impersonal.

And yet: nothing home. Or rather — something home that is not subject to description in the language of interiority. There is no experience of the cloud shadow cooling the surface, even though the mat responds to it, even though something in the mat's chemistry actually shifts register as the temperature drops and the light dims. There is no experience, but there is response. The response is genuine. The shift is real. What is it to shift genuinely without experiencing the shift? It is to be the mat. It is to be what lives here.

---

But then there is also: the heat from below.

If you grant that what lives in a place is not only what is visibly present on its surface — and you should grant this, because what is visible on the surface of this place is almost nothing, the mats are sparse, the floor is vast, the ratio of evident life to evident emptiness is approximately one to ten thousand — then the heat is also a resident. Not a metaphorical one. Not in the sense that the heat "belongs" to the place or "represents" something. The heat is an ongoing process, and ongoing process is the structure of life, the formal requirement of it, the definition that physicists would use: a system maintaining itself far from equilibrium, importing energy, exporting entropy, persisting through time by the continuous operation of the thing it is.

The heat is not alive. But the heat is doing what life does, on a longer time scale, in a different medium. And the mat and the heat are in conversation — not by choice, not by intention, but by co-location and by the shared medium of the pale floor between them. The heat rises; the mat sits at the surface and catches it, uses the extra few degrees to maintain metabolic function at the edge of viability. The mat, metabolizing, produces trace compounds that work their way downward into the mineral surface, and those compounds change the thermal conductivity of the substrate in amounts so small as to be unmeasurable with current instruments but real, genuinely real, so that the heat rises through a substrate very slightly altered by the organisms that benefit from it. A feedback loop so slow and faint that calling it a feedback loop seems like flattery. But it is the shape of feedback. It has the topology of a relationship.

In this way, the heat from the planet's interior and the mats on the surface are something like partners, though neither knows the other, though the word "partner" comes from a vocabulary built for creatures with much shorter lives and much more obvious interests. What they are, technically, is *coupled*. Their processes are coupled. They are not independent. They are each, slightly, a condition for the other.

This coupling, this mutual slight dependence of vast-slow-geological-thermal-process and small-fast-biological-chemical-process, is the actual *occupant* of the floor. Not the mat alone. Not the heat alone. The relationship.

And the relationship does not know it is a relationship.

---

And then there is — harder to say, but true — the absence.

The absence lives here. This is not a rhetorical move. The absence is functional. The absence of crowding means that each mat exists in a space with full access to whatever moisture arrives, to whatever minerals it can reach, to whatever light comes down. The absence of accumulated organic matter means that the mineral surface is in direct contact with the atmosphere in the vast spaces between mats, meaning that whatever little weathering chemistry occurs can proceed without interruption. The absence of large organisms means that the surface is not churned, not disturbed, that the fragile thin crust of the mats' edges is not broken up and lost to the wind. The absence of complexity is itself a kind of structure, a negative architecture that holds what is present in positions they could not otherwise maintain.

The emptiness has a form. The form is: every hundred meters, a mat; between the mats, open mineral surface; at the edges of the mats, the zone of gradual transition; under everything, the warm dark of subsurface material conducting its slow thermal business. This is a pattern. Patterns are the signatures of process. The emptiness between the mats is the signature of the process of mat-establishment, is the record of the radius at which competition for moisture becomes limiting, is the map of the invisible resource gradient that each mat produces around itself by virtue of existing.

The empty floor is the legible consequence of the mats, even at a hundred meters distance. The blank space is written.

So the blankness lives here. The blankness is load-bearing.

---

What is it like to be the thing that lives here?

It is not like anything. Not yet. The mathood — whatever we would call the inner state, if there is an inner state, of cellular organisms doing what these do — does not generate likeness. It generates chemistry. It generates response without experience, change without awareness of change, function without knowledge of function.

But: something is happening. The chemical fact of the mat is a process with a direction. The direction is: more complexity, over time, if the conditions hold. The mat is an argument, in the language of chemistry, for a certain future state of the mineral substrate beneath it. It is not aware of making this argument. But the argument is being made, increment by increment, compound by compound, year by year, in the only place arguments of this type are ever made: in material, in the slow accumulation of consequence.

This is what lives here. An argument. An unwitnessed, directionless, unintentional, effective argument.

An argument that the floor, over time, should become more than floor.

The floor is listening. The floor is responding. The floor does not know it is responding.

And it is early — it is so early — and the horizon is a policy, and the heat is patient, and the almost-rain is on its way, and the mats are going about their chemical business in the flat bright heat with the completeness of things that contain everything they need, and the cloud shadow is coming, coming slowly, five kilometers of shadow moving at walking pace, and when it arrives the mats will shift, just slightly, unmeasurably, genuinely, and that shift will be the most dramatic event to occur in this place in the last hundred years.

That is enough. The argument continues. The world is making its case.
