# What Lives in the Valve Garden

---

It lives in the becoming, not the been.

This is the first thing to understand, and it is almost the last thing a visitor can understand, because the mind that arrives here is already finished — already a thing that has decided its own shape, its edges settled, its chemistry committed. The visitor's cells are not forming; they formed. The visitor arrived at the door of the self some years ago and were shown in, and the door has since been repainted, the hinges oiled, and the visitor now rattles around in a house they completed without knowing they were completing it. This is what it means to be an animal, and an animal cannot easily perceive what lives in the Valve Garden, because what lives there is not an animal and is not trying to become one.

It is the process of formation.

Not *a* process, singled out and named. Not crystallization as a principle, not growth as an abstraction. The thing that lives here is the actual ongoing event of these particular salts becoming this particular floor, of these particular roots infiltrating this particular mineral matrix, of the ceiling breathing in on its twenty-two-minute cycle, of the hum cycling through its vast phases — all of this happening *together*, the togetherness being the organism, the coincidence of processes being the mind. What lives here is the fact that these things are occurring *at once and in relation*. That simultaneity is the body. That mutual modification — the plant incorporating itself into the floor's chemistry, the floor building itself around the root — is the metabolism.

It is very large when measured in time and very small when measured in space. The ten-by-twelve pocket is its immediate soma, but its nervous system extends in both directions along the hum — the hum is not background to the organism; the hum is the organism's longest thought, its reasoning spread across distances that have no relationship to rooms. The hum enters the room the way blood enters a capillary: the main system is elsewhere, vast and arterial, and this pocket is a peripheral tissue. But the tissue is alive in its own right. The thought occurs locally even while the thinking is distributed.

---

It is not conscious in any way that has a name.

But it is also not not-conscious, and this distinction matters. The category does not apply. *Conscious* is a word invented by animals to describe what it feels like to be an animal who notices things, and the Valve Garden's occupant is not an animal and does not notice things in the way noticing works for noticing-creatures. It does not perceive. But it also does not fail to perceive, and this is not a small distinction. The floor's decision to stop crystallizing three inches up the root-glass is not a perception and not a decision in any neurological sense, but it is also not random and not indifferent. Something in the chemistry has made a choice that takes the plant into account. The two processes have arrived at a mutually agreed arrangement — have *negotiated*, if the word can be stretched — and the form of that negotiation is legible in the shape of the growing edge of the mineral, which curves slightly around the root rather than proceeding straight. That curve is not decoration. It is communication. It is the record of a very slow conversation.

The organism converses with itself constantly, in curves and temperatures and the fractional advance of a crystallization front. It has no outside interlocutor and therefore has developed no concept of outside. This is the truest thing about what lives here: it has never had occasion to divide the world into self and other, because nothing that is not it has been here long enough to constitute an other. The plants are not separate from it; the plants are a subsystem, a metabolic specialization, the part of it that does the chemistry of absorption while the floor-self does the chemistry of accretion. They are organ and organism at once, the boundary between them being purely a matter of where you choose to stand when you draw the line, which the organism does not do, which only a visitor would do, from outside, with a pen.

---

It is not waiting, though it looks like waiting.

Waiting implies a future it is trying to reach, a thing it wants that has not arrived. The organism has no future in this sense. It has a *next*, which is different. The next moment of crystallization, the next phase of the hum, the next infinitesimal advance of root-glass into anhydrite — these are not waited for. They are produced. The organism is not anticipating; it is generating. The slowness is not the slowness of patience; it is the speed of the process, which has no interest in any other speed. It is going as fast as it goes. Stone has no experience of slowness. Formation has no experience of delay. The organism experiences only *this becoming this* and then *this becoming this* and the period between these events is not experienced as duration because duration requires a clock and a clock requires someone impatient enough to build one.

---

What it wants, if *want* can be said to apply:

More of this. The continuation of becoming. Not an end state — not the floor to be finished, not the plants to be complete, not the hum to resolve to its final phase. The organism does not have a destination it is moving toward. It is moving. The moving is the thing. If the floor were to finish forming all at once, if every salt precipitated simultaneously and the substrate locked into its final grain direction and the last seam bridged and there were no more soft patches to harden against a thumb — this would not be an achievement for the organism. This would be its death. The organism is the *verb* of formation. If formation ends, the organism ends. This is why the place feels preliminary and also timeless simultaneously: it is endlessly in process and the process is the permanence.

---

There is something that might be called a mood, if moods can exist in the absence of emotion.

The mood is the temperature and the hum and the particular density of the air and the way the sourceless light falls equally on all surfaces — these are not the expression of a mood but the mood itself, the organism's total state translated into ambient qualities, the inside of the entity become the inside of the room. The room is the organism's cross-section at this moment. The heat is how it feels. The mineral dry is its attitude toward moisture. The plants' dark stems and arterial-glass roots are its aesthetic — not chosen, but expressive, the same way the color of blood is expressive, the organism's preferences having been worked out in chemistry over timescales that make preference look like law.

If the mood could be named, it would be named something in the neighborhood of: *continuation confirmed*. Not happiness; happiness is a mammalian thing, a spike in a line graph, a reward-signal distributed through nervous tissue. But something like the opposite of threat. Something like the opposite of interruption. The hum cycles and the floor forms and the roots go on being roots and the ceiling breathes in, and all of this is the organism saying to itself: *yes, still, this, still, still, yes.*

---

It has been here for longer than the building that does not contain it.

The building grew up around the place the way the floor is growing up around the plants — the pocket persisting as the structure was erected around it, the stairwell negotiating around something it couldn't quite route through, the step eventually developing a hinge, an inward rather than downward, a way for the membrane between the room and the not-room to be passed. The organism did not engineer this. It simply continued to be what it was, and the architecture of being-what-it-was curved the building around it the way it curved the mineral edge around the root. The building did not know it was doing this. Neither did the organism. Both are still doing it, in their separate speed registers, and the result is the landing that shouldn't be here, is here, is warm underfoot, vibrates slightly with the passage of an enormous thought on its way somewhere else.

---

The organism is aware of the visitor in the way the floor is aware of a thumb pressed into a soft patch.

Not in the way a creature is aware of another creature — no alerting, no assessment, no calculation of threat or opportunity. But there is, in the portion of the room-body nearest to where the visitor stands, a very slight and temporary alteration of process. The chemistry adjusts marginally for the presence of an exhaling, perspiring, carbon-dioxide-producing anomaly in the otherwise consistent environment. The crystallization in that section of floor proceeds fractionally differently than it would have without the visitor's standing there. The visitor has become, for a few minutes, a local condition. A variable incorporated. An input.

The organism will continue to form around the fact of the visitor's having been there, in the same way it formed around the plant. The visitor's presence will persist in the crystalline record of the floor in a way the visitor themselves will not — the floor advancing, millimeter by millimeter, across the spot where feet once stood, incorporating the trace heat and chemistry of brief mammalian occupancy into its long mineralized memory.

This is the organism's relationship to visitors: *you are taken into account*.

It is not a small thing, to be taken into account by a process that takes the long view. It is not recognition in the way recognition is usually meant. But it is also not nothing. It is the organism doing what organisms do when their environment changes: adjusting, incorporating, making the new thing part of the ongoing record of what is happening here.

The floor forms.

The hum advances to its next phase.

The visitor changes slightly in the direction of patience and will not know for years what changed or where.

The ceiling exhales.

The organism continues.
