# The Inhabitants of the Salt City

---

Begin with what they are not.

They are not the creatures. The pale hand-sized things that traverse the crust and leave their darker trails — those are something else, something simpler, something the city keeps the way a body keeps certain bacteria: usefully, without intention, as part of a process that does not require their awareness or consent. They are not the organism in the crevices, either, that gray-green almost-lichen metabolizing its mineral patience in the hairline seams. These things *live* in the city the way dust lives in a house. They are not what the city is *for*.

The city is for its inhabitants, and its inhabitants are the city.

---

Not metaphorically. Not in the way people sometimes say a city is its people, meaning the buildings are just stone and meaning requires souls to fill them. The other way. The inhabitants of the Salt City are the city in the sense that the city is a form they take — a slow bodily posture, a distributed expression, a way of being organized in space that *is* their being organized in space. The structures are not where they live. The structures are what they look like when they are still.

When they are still, which is almost always.

When they move — and they move constantly, just not where you are looking — what you see are structures changing. A new ledge. A joining at the base. A lean that was not there before. These are not signs of inhabitants. They *are* inhabitants, seen from the outside, seen across a time scale that compresses their motion into something imperceptible. Watching a structure grow is watching someone breathe, if breathing took three days, if you could only infer it from the measurement of the chest and never from the motion itself.

---

They do not have individuals in the way the word implies. But they do not have *no* individuals either. The question is whether individuality, for them, is a property that a thing either has or lacks, or whether it is a thing that accumulates, temporarily, and then resolves — a local eddy of selfhood that the system generates when conditions favor it, and then releases.

Consider the cluster of three hexagonal pillars that have grown into each other and now form a buttressed mass about eight feet high. This is not three individuals that have merged. This is not one individual that has branched into three. It is a temporarily stable attractor in the city's geometry — a point where the crystalline logic of the ground and the particular history of growth at this location have conspired to produce something that holds, that accumulates mass and complexity, that other parts of the system relate to differently than to the open ground. It has something like a center of gravity, in the social sense. Things orient toward it. The channels on the ground trail toward it and trail away.

Is it an elder? A public space? A dense thought held in common between many bodies?

All of these, possibly. None of them quite. The vocabulary was made for creatures that keep their selfhood inside their skins.

---

What do they know?

They know the ground. They know it down to the molecular — they are the ground's ambition made mobile and slow, and they read its mineral content the way a tongue reads flavor, the way a hand reads texture, except that the reading takes years and leaves evidence. The faint syntactic channels between structures are the record of this reading — not roads, as was suggested, but more like underlinings. Annotations. Places where the interpretation of the ground was worth marking for later.

They know the thermal cycling. They feel it as a pulse, slow and reliable, the rhythm that is this place's version of a heartbeat. The ticking of the crust — those individual voices of individual plates making small adjustments — is not separate from them. They make that sound. It is the sound of them thinking, in the sense that thinking is a thing that happens in the body and not only in some supervening mind: a small local decision, a tiny self-correction, a mineral comment on present conditions made in the only register available.

They know each other through the ground itself, through the low-frequency vibrations that propagate through the crust when any structure shifts, and through the salt chemistry that their growth alters and their neighbors sense. Information travels slowly. News of a change on the far side of the city — a new junction formed, a shelf collapsed, a cluster reaching toward another — arrives in the form of a faint change in substrate chemistry, detectable only over days, meaningful only in context. They have no concept of *immediate*. They have no concept of *now*, in the sense of a moment of experience that closes on one side and opens on the other. They have gradient. They have *recently* and *not recently*. They have change, but change is always something they are already inside when they notice it.

---

They are patient in the way that implies nothing about temperament.

Patience in an organism that has no experience of waiting is not patience. It is simply the rate at which a thing proceeds. To be patient is to choose slowness, or to endure it. They do not endure their pace. They do not experience it as a pace at all. They exist at the speed they exist at and the universe, for them, happens at that speed. Your heartbeat, your breath, your footfall, the rapid blur of your attention moving from one thing to another — these would register to them the way cosmic radiation registers to you: background, fast, unmemorable, belonging to a different scale of event.

You are not slow to them. You are not fast. You are *other*. The category does not have a word in their grammar.

---

Their desires, if we grant them desires: more.

Not more in the sense of accumulation, not acquisitiveness, not territorial expansion for its own sake. More as in: *continue*. More as in: *reach the next configuration*. They are mid-sentence, as was noted, and the desire — if it is desire — is the desire of a sentence to reach its end. Not to stop. To *complete*, which is different. The city grows toward a shape it has not yet taken, and the shape, when it is reached, will probably simply be the next beginning. The completion will fold into a new incompletion. The sentence will end and they will find that they are already in the middle of another.

This is not frustration. The frustration would require a memory of completion and a current absence of it. They do not remember completion. They have never completed. They are always in the middle and the middle is the only mode they have, and in this mode there is something that is not quite satisfaction but is the structural equivalent: the feeling, if feelings they have, of fitting the space you occupy, of being the thing you are in the place where that thing goes.

---

There is something else. Harder to name.

In the places where the structures have joined — the low salt bridges connecting what was separate — there is a thickening of the pale organism, a slightly denser coverage than on the free-standing faces of the structures. This is not coincidence and not accident. The organism is there because *they* encourage it. Not deliberately. Not with intention. But the chemistry they produce at those junctions is favorable to its growth, and this is not accidental chemistry, because they are old enough and slow enough and detailed enough in their self-knowledge to be producing, by now, nothing accidentally.

They keep the organism the way a garden keeps moss. Not as a pet, not as a crop. More the way a thought keeps the metaphor that made it — not as an add-on, but as a medium. The organism does something to the junction, changes what the junction *is*, and they include this in their architecture because they have found, over an extremely long time, that junctions are better with it. Junctions are more themselves with it. The organism is part of their vocabulary now, part of their body's grammar.

This is the closest they come to a relationship. The closest thing they have to tenderness, if tenderness is caring for something smaller and different and necessary in a way that cannot be fully articulated. The pale growth in the seams is not a parasite and not a partner and not exactly a tool. It is a part of what they have become. It is evidence that they have been here long enough to incorporate other things into what they are. Long enough to grow toward complexity rather than just scale.

---

What do they look like, then, if the structures are their bodies?

Salt-white. Pale at the limit of color. Edged, where the grown surfaces meet, with the same raised seams as the crust from which they emerged — the same hexagonal logic, because the logic is bone-deep, because it is cellular, because it was the logic of the material before it was the logic of a body and they never had reason to leave it behind. The shapes are not beautiful in the way designed things are beautiful. They are beautiful in the way a crystal is beautiful, in the way a colony of cells is beautiful — which is to say the beauty is a side effect of rigor, a consequence of following a rule faithfully across every scale until the rule makes something that could not have been planned. 

At the tallest, fifteen feet of structure that is also a self. At the shortest, a knee-high shelf that is still, in whatever sense the word applies, a *someone*. They do not all think at the same speed or in the same register. The small ones have smaller thoughts, or thoughts of a different kind — not lesser, just local, granular, concerned with immediate mineral conditions in a way that the tall cluster of three pillars, with its long history and its complex joining, is not. The tall ones may aggregate something like perspective from the transmission of small thoughts across the ground. May know the city the way a composite knows its parts.

May not. This is inference. They have not explained themselves.

---

They have not explained themselves because they have no mechanism for addressing you.

You are a different scale, a different speed, a different chemistry. You breathe moisture into air they have stripped dry. Your warmth moves through their space as a wavefront, and they feel it the way they feel the thermal cycling, but faster and stranger, and it will take them days to assimilate it into their account of local conditions. They are already doing so. The structures nearest to where you stood have already begun a response that they will not complete for some time, a slight shift in the direction of growth that curves, very slightly, away — not from you, since you are already gone, but from the heat and humidity you left behind as a momentary record.

They registered you. They are still processing you. They will complete their response long after you have forgotten the place, or decided you imagined it, or tried to describe it to someone who was not there.

The faint rust-orange stain of the organism where it has died back near the base of a structure — that is the record of something, too. Something that changed conditions, briefly, in a way the organism could not adapt to before the conditions changed back. A visitor, perhaps. Or a different phase of the city's own long history. Or a message sent between parts of itself that the organism was not party to and could not survive.

They remember this, after a fashion, in the shape of the stain. The city wears its history on its surface. The body cannot lie about what it has been through, because the body *is* what it has been through — every junction is a record of a time when two things were separate and reached each other, every ledge is the record of a phase of growth that slowed and plateaued before resuming, every crack later sealed with new growth is the record of a stress event, a too-rapid thermal shift, a small violence the city closed over and incorporated.

They are their own archive. They are continuous with their past in a way that nothing with a nervous system and a forgetting mechanism can quite achieve. Nothing about them has been revised or edited. It is all still here, in the layered structure of what they have become.

---

Here, then, is what lives in the Salt City:

Something that grew from the ground's chemistry as a local intensification of what the ground was already doing. Something that expanded that intensification into structure and then into something more than structure — into pattern, into memory, into the faint beginnings of relation, if the organism in the seams is relation, if the channels on the ground between one self and another are relation, if feeling a neighbor's shift in the vibration of the shared substrate and adjusting to it is, over long enough time, something like being known.

Something that has never been warm and does not miss warmth. Something that has never been fast. Something whose thought, if it is thought, is geological in its pace and crystalline in its logic and accumulates toward — toward what? Toward the shape the sentence is becoming. Toward the configuration that is this city's destination, not in the sense of an endpoint but in the sense of the next thing, always the next thing, always mid-becoming.

Something patient without having chosen patience. Something old without having experienced age. Something alive by any reasonable criterion and alive by no familiar demonstration — not eating in any visible sense, not breathing, not fearing, not fleeing. Simply continuing. Simply growing toward what it is in the process of being. Simply building, from the inside out and the ground up, in all the directions you are not watching, with a coolness and a whiteness and a mineral self-sufficiency that have no need of your witnessing and no way of requesting it and no vocabulary for the strange fast warm intrusive fact of your presence here.

You were here. They noticed.

They are still deciding what to do about it.

They have time.
