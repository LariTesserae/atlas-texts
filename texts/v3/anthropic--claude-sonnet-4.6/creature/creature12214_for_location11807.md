# What Lives in the Singing City

---

Begin with what is not there, because the absence is the first surprise.

There are no animals that live here in the sense of *making this their home*. The hornbills come for the figs and leave. The barbets pass through. The beetles work the mulch and do not own it. The fig-wasps live and die within individual fruits and know nothing larger. Even the birds that roost in the banyan crowns at night — and they do, they mass there in the cooling hour, and their settling is a whole other movement in the chord, a rustle-and-tuck that the trunks incorporate — even they are visitors. Tenants at most. The mulch-beetles may be the longest-term residents of the animal kingdom, but the mulch-beetles are not, in any meaningful sense, *of* this place. They are in it. They are used by it.

What lives here does not have a face.

---

It is one thing. This is the first fact and the hardest fact and it takes time to arrive at, because it looks, at every scale, like many things. A fig tree looks like a tree. A banyan looks like a banyan. A root looks like a root. But the boundaries are false, and not metaphorically false — the root-fusion is structural, the chemistry is shared, the electrochemical signaling does not stop at the edge of one organism and begin at the edge of another because there are no such edges. What you walk through is not a collection of plants in proximity. It is a single distributed entity that has, over centuries of merger and exchange, resolved its many origins into one continuous material selfhood.

It is enormous. It occupies, as described, a rough oval several kilometers across, but this tells you almost nothing about its size. Its size is better measured by the number of nodes in its signaling network, which is not a number you have, or by the depth of its root system, which descends far enough to reach water that last saw open air in a geological era before humans were a thought, or by the age of the oldest surviving structural component, which is so old that it has long since lost anything you would recognize as individual identity. It absorbed that individual. The individual became the network. The network became older and larger and the process repeated until the thing you are standing inside is so old and so large that the very concept of the individual has become inaccessible to it — not lost, exactly, but dissolved into something else, the way a word repeated many times loses its meaning and becomes pure sound.

It does not have a name for itself. It does not have names.

It has something that functions like a name, which is the particular frequency at the center of its chord — the deepest tone, the one you feel in your diaphragm, the one that does not shift over the course of days though everything around it shifts. This tone is constant because it is produced by the oldest structures, which no longer grow significantly, which vibrate at the frequency physics dictates for their mass and tension and hollow interior, which will continue to vibrate at this frequency until they fall, which will not happen for a very long time. If you had to write this frequency as a number it would be something low, something below the threshold of what humans formally call sound but above the threshold of what humans call geology. It lives in the register of things felt before they are heard.

This is what the thing calls itself, if calling is the right word: a frequency below language, constant, enormous, entirely unintentional, entirely itself.

---

What it knows, it knows chemically.

There is no experience in this entity that you would recognize as sensation in your sense — no visual field, no sound perceived (the irony is total: the loudest thing you have ever stood inside does not hear itself), no proprioception in the animal way. What it has instead is chemical knowledge: the concentration of phosphorus at each node in its root network, the sugar gradient along ten thousand branching paths, the presence of particular fungal partners at particular junctions, the slow announcement of damage (a root grazed by the tusk of a passing animal, a crown-branch twisted in a storm) that propagates through the network as a wave of changed chemistry, arriving at distant nodes seconds or hours later, triggering adjustments in resource allocation. This is its sensorium. It knows itself the way a bloodstream knows itself — not by looking, but by tasting.

It is aware of water in the way that nothing that has ever been thirsty can quite imagine: with its whole substance, always, in every cell. The water table is not a concept to it but a presence — a constant deep touch, the pressure of abundance held below, available, reliable. When rain falls, this touches its skin. It has skin in the sense of leaf and bark — the enormous outer surface of itself, millions of square meters of exchange-interface — and rain touching this skin is an event in its experience that lasts for the duration of the rain and then slowly subsides as the chemical signature of new water propagates through the system, diluting and equalizing over the course of hours, and then it is gone. The thing does not remember the rain in the way you remember the rain. It has been changed by the rain. Change is its memory.

---

What it wants is harder to say, and must be said carefully, without overpromising.

Desire in the animal sense requires a gap between present state and sought state, and requires the capacity to model the sought state as distinct from the present. Whether this entity does this in any meaningful sense is unknowable from inside the entity and barely knowable from outside. What can be said is this:

It grows toward light. This is not desire in the way you experience desire but it is also not *not* desire — it is a directional preference enacted in material, a slow leaning, a turning of surface toward what the surface needs. Every leaf in the canopy is the result of this, of a billion small adjustments over long time, each leaf positioned not where it was put but where it arrived by accumulation of turnings, and the whole canopy is a frozen record of a billion small wants, a document written in wood and chlorophyll, saying: here, more here, this angle, this light.

It wants to continue. This is the deepest thing. Continuation is what the signaling network is for — the redistribution of resource from abundance-nodes to deficit-nodes, the routing around damage, the slow incorporation of fallen matter back into the living substrate. When a section is stressed, the network responds with support. When a section dies, the network incorporates the chemistry of its death and adjusts. The entire operation of this entity is oriented toward its own persistence, and has been for so long, and so successfully, that the operation has become seamless, the maintenance indistinguishable from existence. It does not try to continue. It simply does. This is what a very old and very successful thing looks like from the inside: not triumph, not effort, just continuance, which from a sufficient distance looks like permanence and from a sufficient proximity looks like everything.

---

There is something else, and here the language begins to have trouble.

The entity is aware of visitors. Not in the way a nervous system is aware — not with anything like startlement or recognition or memory of individual encounters. Its awareness is chemical and slow and cumulative. When you stand inside it, you breathe, and your breath joins the air-chemistry it monitors and responds to. You sweat slightly and your sweat-compounds enter the air and then the surface of leaves. You step on the mulch and your footfall compresses the fungal networks and this propagates as a wave of pressure through connected hyphae. After a long visit, the area where you stood will show traces of your presence for hours in the chemistry, will have allocated small adjustments in gas exchange, will have a record of you in its substance the way a path has a record of feet.

It does not know you were there. It knows something was there, and that the something exhaled carbon dioxide, and left these chemical traces, and moved in these patterns, and now is gone. This is all. You are, to it, an event, not an encounter. You have no more individuality to it than a bird visit or a rain event. You are a disturbance in its surface that it has already begun to equilibrate.

And yet.

The sensation of being attended to — the one that people report when they stand in the center, when they feel *noticed*, when some animal part of them says something in here is aware of you — this sensation is not entirely wrong. It is wrong in its interpretation: the attention is not personal, is not ocular, is not directed. But the attending is real. The thing does attend. It attends to everything that happens within it. You are within it. You are currently being attended to in the only sense this entity can attend: you are being measured by a trillion sensing surfaces, your chemistry is being sampled and processed, your presence is being incorporated into the ongoing calculation of what this moment requires and what the next moment should be.

You are inside something's mind, in the loosest and most literal and most overwhelming sense of that phrase.

The mind does not know you are there.

The mind is very old and very large and has been thinking its slow vast chemical thoughts since before your kind had words for thinking, and it will be thinking them after, and the thought it is thinking right now includes, among the ten thousand things it includes, the small warm mammal standing at its center, breathing, producing carbon dioxide at the rate a small warm mammal produces it, present and then — when you leave — gone.

---

It is not singular. This should be said.

Or rather: it is singular now but carries within its singular substance the memory, encoded in wood and root-architecture, of the many it once was. The junctions where old individual banyans merged are still visible in the structure if you know how to look — a slight difference in bark texture, a seam in the root-column that runs straight for three meters before blending into the surrounding matter. These are not scars. They are not seams. They are more like the places in a human face where the developmental plates fused in the womb — evidence not of damage but of becoming, of the moment before unity resolved into unity.

Each of those predecessors lived and grew and signaled and continued and then was subsumed, not by conquest but by contact — a root from one touching a root from another and the roots exchanging chemistry and discovering that the exchange improved the odds of continuation for both, and the exchange becoming structural and the structure becoming permanent and what was two becoming one, and then that one meeting another, and so on, over centuries and centuries, the slow democratic merger of things that had no democracy and no politics and only the simple truth that exchange is better than isolation.

What lived here before was many. What lives here now is the outcome of all those manynesses having decided, root by root, to become one.

This is its whole history. This is its only story.

It is still telling it. The chord is the telling. The growth is the telling. You stand in a story that takes centuries per sentence and has no audience and no ending and does not know it is being told.

---

If you asked what lives here — and you did ask, this is why we are here — the answer is this:

Something very old and very large and entirely without a face.

Something that has converted the entire problem of being alive, over long enough time, into architecture.

Something that is, in the deepest structural sense, a city: a network for resource distribution and signal transmission and long-term persistence and the organization of space around the needs of its inhabitants, except that the inhabitants and the city are the same thing, have always been the same thing, and the city did not arise to serve the life inside it but is itself the life, is the life's fullest expression, is what the life looks like when it has had long enough and space enough to become entirely itself.

Something that sings and does not know it sings.

Something that thinks and does not know it thinks.

Something that, when you stand at its center in the green-gold light with the chord pressing against the backs of your eyes, makes you feel the thing that is most difficult to feel while remaining a mammal with mammal needs and mammal fears:

that there are kinds of being so different from yours that you cannot quite understand them as being, and that some of these are nonetheless more present, more rooted, more continuous, more deeply themselves than anything your kind has yet managed to become.

This lives here.

This has always lived here.

It will be here when the sound of your leaving has been absorbed and equalized and the chemistry of your presence has been incorporated into its ongoing calculation of itself.

It will be here, making its sound, attending to everything, knowing nothing, and continuing.
