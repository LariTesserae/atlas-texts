I would be one of the pale crickets in the crack beside the moss.

My body would fit where the cement has parted from the silt. I could turn around inside, though only just, and there would be a bend beyond which the light could not follow. When I backed in, my feelers would remain outside for a while, resting against the wall and crossing the paths of passing animals.

I would know the chamber by what touched me. A pill bug’s back would be cool and ridged; the stem’s hanging root would be soft enough to catch against a foot. I might discover the moss by climbing over the snails that were eating it, and the entrance grass by following a current of air that grew drier as I went.

The first time the pump started, I would retreat so quickly that a grain of silt came with me into the crack. Later, I might wait at the mouth. The wire would quiver, the animals on the floor would move aside, and soon afterward there would be water on the metal. I could climb partway up the wall to reach it. A beetle climbing down might make me turn sideways and hold on while it passed.

The other trembling would find me wherever I was. Even deep in the crack, with my feet pressed against the broken sides, I would hold still. I would stay there after it ended, until the first loose grains began to fall and something brushed past the entrance again.

There would be nights when I ventured farther out. I might cross the open silt to the hanging roots, or reach the grass and stand among its blades while warmer air moved over my back. From there I could go onto the plain. Some nights I would take a few steps in that direction, then turn and follow the cooler air back beneath the slab.

The crack would change as the chamber settled. One day the bend might narrow, or a pebble might roll against the entrance, leaving me to find another way in. By then there might be young crickets sheltering where I had sheltered, their feelers reaching out beside mine while we waited for the floor to quiet.
