I am on the downstream side of a wall, near a shallow basin. The wall is not high, but the current divides around it, and in the small shelter behind it there is room for dust to settle.

I arrived there drifting. I remember that time only as changes in pressure: a long pull, a tumbling interval, water moving through me faster than I could move through it. I touched the wall once and was swept away. When I touched it again, I held.

The surface was uneven with older chambers. I settled in a notch between two of them, where the flow was gentler but still brought food. Above me, the fringes of my neighbors opened and closed. Their chambers gave me something firm to build against. As I grew, the chalk around my body joined theirs, and the notch became smaller.

Now the water enters me through one fringe and leaves through the other. I take what I can from it. Much of what passes is too large or too hard or has no nourishment in it; the useful things come mixed with dust. When the current is steady I can remain open for a long time. I feel its weight against the extended fringe, and the finer touch of particles caught there.

I do not need to know the whole wall to live on it. I know the chamber beside mine by the alteration in the water when its occupant closes. On my other side, an old opening has been empty since before I settled. Sometimes a small fish rests inside it. Its fins send quick pulses through the water. Once it backed in so suddenly that its tail struck my fringe, and I closed before I had taken a full breath.

There are disturbances that pass along the wall faster than the things causing them. One neighbor closes, then another. By the time the pressure of a large body reaches me, I am already withdrawing. For a little while there is only the close water inside my chamber and the low movement traveling through the shell. Then one of us opens. If nothing follows, the others open too.

The current changes with the seasons above, though I cannot see them. At times it comes clean and strong over the wall, carrying food and very little dust. At other times it turns into the basin and returns thick with what it has lifted. The grains gather against the downstream chambers. Some of my neighbors can no longer extend fully; one remains closed beneath the deposit. I keep my openings above it for a while, adding shell at the rim.

A grazer passes over us. Its body presses against the wall, and the creatures riding beneath it touch the edges of our openings. Later the water clears enough that I can feel the grazer farther away, crossing a bare patch of shell. It leaves the surface cleaner behind it. I know the difference when the current brings water from that direction: fewer loose particles, and a faint taste from the scraped ground.

The empty chamber beside me does not stay empty. A fragment has lodged against its lower edge, and dust accumulates between them. Young wall-makers touch there. Most are gone with the next strong flow. One remains, tucked against the fragment. At first its openings are so small that I feel them only when the current is weak. Over time its shell reaches the side of my chamber. The fragment no longer shifts when the water rises.

We make a little more shelter together. Dust catches below us; some is carried away again. A fish noses among the openings and retreats. The young one closes at its approach, and I close after it.

When the current turns back into the basin, a cloud comes over the wall. I draw in what I can until the grains are too dense, then fold my fringes inward. The dust settles on my rim and on the young one’s shell. We remain closed through the thickest of it.

Later, the weight of the water changes. Something is lifting from the wall. I open a fraction. The water carries dust again, but it is moving past rather than settling. Beside me, the young one opens too, and we begin to feed.
