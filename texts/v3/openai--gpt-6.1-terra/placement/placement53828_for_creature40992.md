I think I would be just inside one of the low openings, where the new wall is still dark with moisture. At first I might seem to be part of it: a pale worker termite, my head against the packed earth, holding a grain between my jaws.

The grain is too large for the place I have brought it. I turn it, press it once, and it rolls away. Another worker passes me, touching me briefly with its antennae, and disappears farther into the passage. I retrieve the grain and carry it to a wider gap, where the damp surface receives it. For a moment it protrudes. More soil will come around it.

I do not know the hollow as a room. I know the covered routes through it, the yielding places in a wall, the turn around the fern root. Beyond an opening there is a brightness I do not often enter. An ant crosses it, pausing near a seed; later, when I come that way, the marks of its feet are still in the loose earth.

The red root has pushed into material we have used before. I meet its smooth tip while gathering soil at the edge of a gallery. It gives slightly when I press against it. I go around, and the next grain I carry comes from the other side.

After rain, the wall softens. A small section slumps into the passage, and several of us work there, moving the fallen grains and pressing others into the exposed edge. Water hangs beneath the root above us. One drop falls nearby, leaving a shallow pit, and we turn from the sound and the movement.

By the time I return with another grain, a mite has come to the water left in the pit. I place my grain farther along the wall. The mite drinks, and the root goes on growing behind us.
