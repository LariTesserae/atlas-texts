At first I think I would live in the warm crack, among the pale film at the water’s mouth. But the water moves too readily there: it can desert one groove and find another overnight.

I would be a fungus a little farther down, in the dark pocket behind a stone, where the water slows enough to leave things.

I would begin in a fallen fern stem. My threads would enter through its broken end, work along the softened walls of its cells, and spread into the moss beneath it. Beyond the moss would be the narrow space where a shrub root bends around a grain of rock. I could grow there too, though the stem and the root would offer different lives: one giving way as I entered it, the other alive and guarded.

I would have no view of the clouds. The bird would come to me as a brief shaking of the moss, followed perhaps by the loss of a springtail that had been feeding nearby. The pump would be more constant. Its tremor would pass through the stone and through the little pieces of stone crowded against my threads. I don’t know whether I would feel it. It might simply be one of the conditions in which I grew, like the warmth.

The maintenance person would matter to me most after they left. A fern frond cut from the shed door might lie green for a while on the wet rock. Its leaflets would catch rain; its stem would slowly darken. Eventually I could enter it, or another fungus might reach it first. Mites would open small paths through the softer parts. What remained would break and collect behind the stone, adding a little to the dark material already there.

Some of my threads would dry when a trickle shifted. Others, deeper in the crack, would persist. A root might thicken beside them and press the pocket nearly shut; later, perhaps, a fine fracture would open beyond it, with room and mineral dust enough to explore.

I would not be the whole garden, or even the fungus everywhere in it. I would be this particular branching: the part that reached the root, the part that died in the dry groove, the part growing now into the fallen fern. For a long time nothing visible would announce me. Then, in a wet spell, a fruiting body might rise through the moss beside the grating—small enough that the next boot could miss it, or not.
