You would find me below the bats, on the mouse’s ledge, where a flake of stone leans against a rounded rib. I would be a small spider in the space behind it.

From the open side, my web would be difficult to see. It would cross the narrow gap between the flake and the rib, anchored at several points to the warm, dry stone. Farther back, there would be room for me to wait with my legs on the threads.

The ledge would give me a great deal to attend to. Crickets pass along it, touching the ground ahead with their antennae. Mites move among the crumbs the mouse leaves behind, though most are too small to trouble my web. Dust settles on the strands; droplets sometimes strike them and make them sag. Above, the bats shift in their folds, and now and then something falls from among them and bounces down the rock.

At first I imagine myself patient in the dark, waiting for a single clear signal. But the threads would rarely be still. I would have to distinguish the fine movements I could use from the ones that meant only grit, or water, or the immense disturbance of the mouse.

One afternoon, while the blade of light is high on the opposite wall, a young cricket comes through the gap. It strikes the web and kicks. I feel the sharp pulls, approach, and find it tangled near the outer edge.

By the next time the mouse visits, little remains of the cricket. The mouse noses beneath the flake, lifts it, and tears away half my web. I retreat deeper into the crack while it searches. When it moves on, I come out to find the flake resting at a different angle. Two of my anchor points are gone.

I would not put the web back exactly as it was. I would fasten new threads to the exposed underside of the flake and to a rough patch farther along the rib. The gap would be wider now, and some of the strands would have farther to reach.

At dusk, the bats would begin to leave. Their departure would pass overhead in a clatter, then thin out through the opening. I would remain on the ledge among the crickets and the ticking water, in a web stretched across a path that had changed while I was living on it.
