I would be beneath the floor, where the stone stays warm after the surface cools. At first I think I would be one of the pebbles there: smooth, dark, moved from chamber to chamber and never left in one place for long.

But I would want to know what moved it.

I would be the thing with the scraping mouth and the hard front legs, broad enough to push a pebble but narrow enough to turn around in a passage. My back would have the color of the stone when it is wet. I might have eyes, though they would be small and not much used underground. I would know the hollow by changes in pressure: the packed weight of grit after rain, the fine tremor of a bronze hunter striking the floor, the many quick feet of the drinkers returning from the basin.

The drinkers would pass me in both directions. When they were dry, they would brush along the walls to get around me. Returning, they would be swollen with water and slower. Sometimes one would stop and touch my leg before going on. I don’t know whether that touch would mean anything to it. I would pause until it had passed.

There would be a particular bend where grit collected. After a shower I would go there first, pushing loose grains ahead of me with my mouth closed. The work would take many trips. Each time I reached the opening, I would lift my head into the cooling air and shove out a little heap. Once, among the grit, I would find a pebble worn almost spherical. I would bring it up too.

On the surface it would look different from how it felt in the dark. A pale stripe would run across it, broken in two places. I would leave it beside the heap while I went back for more grit.

By the time I returned, a squat animal would be resting against it, one side of its body lifted. I would wait just inside the opening. The animal would shift after a while and move toward the slabs. I would take the pebble down again, though I might have to widen the turn at the entrance to get it through.

Farther below, there would be a low room where the roof had begun to crumble. I would set the pebble beneath the weak place. It would not fit at first. I would scrape the floor around it, roll it a little, scrape again, until it held against the roof. Then I would leave it there and return to the bend, where grit was still coming in.

I would not see much of the hunt. Through the stone I would feel the basin fill, then the gathering feet, then the blows as bodies fell. Once in a while something would land close enough to shake dust from my ceiling. The drinkers would come back along the passage, touching the waiting bodies as they passed. I would keep to the side until the rush was over.

Near dawn I might come up through an opening by the broken groove. The squat animals would already be in position. I would stay behind them, where the stone was rough. If the water crossed the gap, I could follow it with my head as it trembled toward the fern. If it fell, I could feel each drop strike before the hiss reached me.

Perhaps I would come out on one of those mornings when the slabs were wet and the rest of the hollow was dry. I would stand in the shade between two stones, cool enough that moisture gathered on my back. The patterns would pass over me too quickly to make out. I might remain there until a waiting animal nudged past my leg.

Then I would go underground again, carrying a little surface dust with me. At the low room I would check the pebble with one hard foot. If it had shifted, I would set it back. If it held, I would continue along the passage, feeling ahead for the place where the grit had begun to gather again.
