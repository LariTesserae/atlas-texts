I would be a fungus in a crack beneath the bird’s ledge.

Most of me would be hidden inside a fragment of old leaf, held against the stone by a root. A few pale threads would reach into the moss around it. I would be too fine to see beside the root, though together my threads might make a faint whitening at the edge of the leaf.

The mouse rests above me. Its weight presses the moss into the crack; one of my threads lies against a hair fallen from its coat. On the other side of the root, a snail’s body covers the stone. Its shell is just visible from the leaf, a small brown curve among the larger shells.

I am not the whiteness alone. I am also inside the leaf, where its veins are still strong enough to hold its shape. Water surrounds the fine threads and fills the spaces between the leaf and the rock. There is food against me, and there are other filaments there too, so close that I could not say where all of them lead.

I would not know the whole hollow as a view. The bird would be a dark underside beyond the moss; the frogs would be mostly concealed by the lip of a pool. I could see one toe in the shallows, and beside it a pale jointed creature standing on a submerged leaf. The small piece of sky would be somewhere above the ledge, out of sight.

The water’s note reaches even the crack. I cannot find where it begins. It is present against the stone and against the leaf that contains me.

I would have no task in keeping the hollow as it is. The mouse has found room in the moss; I have found room beneath it. Its fur stays warm, the leaf stays whole enough to shelter me, and the water remains against every thread.
