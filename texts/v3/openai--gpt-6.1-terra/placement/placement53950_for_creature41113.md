At first I think I am the note made by an overflowing cup: a thin sound held for a moment above the water’s deeper voice. But the note stops when the cup empties, and something remains below to feel the change.

I am under that cup, in a seam between two shelves. I have flattened myself against the lower stone, where the current passes over my back. My underside holds by hundreds of small grips. When I release them, I do not do so all at once; I let go in a ripple, and the water moves me a little before I take hold again.

The roar is everywhere. I do not hear it as a visitor standing on the shelf would hear it. Through the dark lines along my sides, I feel the quieter things within it: a root drawing taut when the wind reaches a fern above, the occasional knock of a flake turning in a narrow passage, the soft impacts of debris against my feeding lobes.

Today the cup has begun to spill. The water arriving from it is warmer than the water in the seam, and carries a little soil. I turn toward the flow and spread my lobes. They tremble there, almost invisible, until a small creature touches one. Then I fold them inward, slowly enough that the current does not tear them from me.

There is another seamling farther down. I can feel its movement against the stone, though the channel bends before I can reach it. When the fresh water weakens, it releases its hold and moves away. I stay until the trickle has stopped entirely, grazing on the film where the flow has washed it clean of grit.

After dark, rain enters the seam in many places at once. I climb through a crack beside a fern root, my body narrowing and lengthening as I go. The surface is cool. Water lies on the rock in a sheet thin enough that my back breaks through it.

A snail is feeding nearby. Its trail crosses a patch I have already grazed, and I follow the edge of it for a short distance. Above us, drops fall from the fern crown at irregular intervals. Each one strikes with a force I can feel through the shelf.

I move toward the moss when the rain slackens. There is a sheltered space beneath it, but I do not enter yet. I pause at the edge, partly on stone and partly among the wet stems, while the water drains around me. Then a lizard shifts somewhere out on the open shelf. The movement reaches me through the rock. I draw myself under the moss and wait there until it passes.
