At first I would be hard to distinguish from what I live in: one of the faint branches moving through the pearly wall.

In the morning I gather with the others on the side facing the pool. The ridge is there, held in our surface, its black shape clear even when the long-legged creatures come between us and the water. I do not know whether we are keeping its reflection or whether the ridge is simply very patient.

Below me, a filament has found a root. For some hours neither moves. Then the root makes a small turn around it, and the filament shifts enough to leave room. The root continues growing. By evening they are touching again.

I can follow the filament only as far as the wet stone. Beyond that I feel changes rather than places: warmth where a pool has thinned, the cool press of water arriving, the slight resistance when a track closes. I send myself toward these things, or something in the structure sends me. I am not always certain where my choosing begins.

When the surface clouds, I draw close to the other branches. Across the basin, the solitary plant turns its pale undersides toward us. The creatures wait. I have seen one fold down beside the ferns with a young one beneath it, both so still that the seed-sized animals came out and began grazing around their feet.

The pools are quiet then. I attend to them, though I cannot say that my attention holds them still. Sometimes a drop rises over the ridge and hangs there so long that I have time to follow it. It comes down without making a ripple.

Later the wall begins to clear. The branches loosen; I find myself extending into a narrow place I have not reached before. Water beads higher on the rock, first one bead and then several, until a thread joins them. The waiting creatures rise. One places a foot beside a closing track and tests the stone before putting its weight down.

I think I would live mostly in these intervals: gathered, then spreading; uncertain, then briefly able to follow the water. I would come to know the creatures by the care with which they cross the basin, the plants by the order in which their leaves fold, and the roots by how gently they grow around what they encounter.
