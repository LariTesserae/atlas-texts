At first I would not know where I ended.

There would be the yielding wall, the water held in it, and a pressure passing slowly across both. When the pressure stopped, the wall would still tremble against me. I might be a thread of the pale growth, bending with a current too slight to carry anything away.

Then one point of contact would loosen, and another. A row of tiny feet would take hold in sequence. I would be moving.

I live where the nap thins at the edge of a seam. My body fits beneath a mineral fold that feels, along my back, as broad and smooth as a roof. Here the water remains close even when I leave the growth. I follow its taste toward the richer places, feeling ahead until my foremost feet meet the soft threads. I can feed there with most of my body still sheltered.

Sometimes another body brushes mine in the nap. The touch is jointed, cool, brief. I hold still; then it is gone, and I continue feeling my way over the same patch of wall. Perhaps there are others nearby for long stretches without either of us crossing the other’s path.

I have no use for the open middle of the chamber, though I can cross it. The ridges there rise against my underside, and the water gathers around my feet. Once in a while the wall’s familiar taste grows faint and I turn until I find it again. The seam is easiest to enter from below, where one blunt nodule presses close to another and leaves a narrow space between.

Above the wet places is a shelf. Its edge is firm and less yielding than the growth. I have felt my way partway up it, but the moisture thins there, and I have turned back. Beyond my reach, the three stones rest against one another beside the stiff cord and the bowl of water. They were arranged by hands I will never touch.

I do not wait for those hands. I stay beneath the fold, or move along the wall when the seep brings food within reach. The fine threads bend under me and slowly rise after I pass.
