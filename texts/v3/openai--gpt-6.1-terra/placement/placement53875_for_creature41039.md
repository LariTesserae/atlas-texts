At first I think I would be in the bowl. It has the look of something waiting to be used again, and the crack gives it a way out: a thin dark line through which the water leaves.

But the bowl is too far from the seam. Much of the time it is dry. I think I would be higher up, among the moss stems, where a bead of water can hang for days without falling.

I would be a springtail, pale and small enough to pass beneath the salamander’s chin. The moss would not look like a mat from where I lived. It would be a thicket of upright stems, some slick with water, others dry and stiff at their tips. I could climb one and find the underside of a fern frond spread above me. I could go down to the rock, where old leaves and dark specks collected and softened.

The roar might come to me through the stone, though I don’t know what I would make of it. My world would be close at hand: the dampness on my body, a surface I could grip, the taste of something I could eat. In wet weather I could move farther, picking my way over films of water and across the fern roots. I would feed on the fine growths there, and on pieces of things fallen into the moss.

Sometimes a section of the thicket would shift. A stem would bend aside and hold there. I might touch it with an antenna and find the smooth flank of the salamander behind it, cold and wet as the stone but giving slightly under pressure. If it moved toward me, I could spring away, the little fork beneath my body snapping against the ground. I would not know where I was going to land. Often it would be only a short distance, into another cluster of stems; sometimes the salamander would be quicker.

When the moss dried at the edge, I would go down with the others. In the seam there would still be dark water held between the rock and the roots. We could wait there, with the salamander somewhere near us, until moisture gathered again.

After rain I might climb high enough to see out of the hollow: a narrow strip of valley, the cold light on stone. Then a drop would strike the moss beside me, shaking water from several stems at once, and I would turn toward the wet place and make my way into it.
