I am on the shaded wall, just below the place where the stone dries when the cloud goes.

For a long time I stay beneath two larger bodies. Their legs hold them above me; mine are folded into the seam. There are young under one of them, soft enough that I can feel them moving when they press against my back. Occasionally a leg finds my edge and rests there.

The stone carries sound better than air. I feel a scrape from below before I hear it, then another from somewhere above. I draw my ridged limb along the wall. A little grit comes away against it. I wait, and farther down several animals begin.

When water falls, the weight above me shifts. Legs lift and take hold again. One of the young is left against my underside, its few working legs caught in the fringe there. I unfold carefully and move out. It stays with me.

There is a chip wedged in the crack beside the water pocket. A bead has collected at its lower edge, but it is not heavy enough to fall. Animals crowd beneath it, touching the stone and one another with their forelimbs. I settle behind them. The young one moves from my underside onto the animal in front of me, and disappears under its body.

The bead grows. One animal reaches up and touches it; the water draws back along the chip. There is scraping on the wall, close and rapid. Then the chip slips. Its edge strikes an animal beneath it, and both drop into the pocket.

For a few moments the wall is still. The fallen animal climbs out. The chip remains in the water, with one corner raised above the surface.

Later, when there is room, I go down to it. The edge is slick. I can lift one end, but not hold the other clear of the pocket. Another animal grips it, and we pull until the chip turns over. A third gets beneath it. We carry it a short way up the wall, where it catches against a projection.

Water reaches it there, though not at once. It comes from a seam I had not touched before, gathering so slowly that I leave and return before the first bead reaches the edge. By then another animal is waiting beneath it.

Cold comes down from the opening. The upper animals pass over me, their pale bodies dry enough to catch on the rock. I follow them a little way into the deeper warmth, then stop where there is a narrow space between two bodies. They make room as I press in.

After a while I feel movement beneath me. A young one has found shelter there. Its legs take hold, release, and take hold again. I keep my body raised while it settles.

The chip is somewhere above us now. I cannot see it, but every so often a drop strikes the pocket, and the animals nearest it turn.
