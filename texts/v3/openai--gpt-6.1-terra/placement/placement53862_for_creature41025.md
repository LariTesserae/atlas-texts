At first I think I would be the band in the ceiling, because it gives the chamber something to turn toward. But it was placed there for a purpose I cannot know, and its power comes from somewhere beyond the room.

I would be closer to the basin: one of the moss pads in the hollow above its white rim.

Not the whole pad, perhaps. A patch where the green tips are still separate enough to distinguish, though underneath they are pressed together with years of darker growth. The basin would be just below me. On the coldest nights, a little water would gather on the stone above and find its way down through my stems. Most of the time I would hold only what was left from a previous visit.

A fern would have rooted through me. Its stem would rise among several others and bend, over its lifetime, toward the path of the red pulse. I would not follow its movement, but I might feel the gradual pressure of it, and later the weight of a frond folded down across my surface. Beneath that weight, some of my tips would go pale. New ones would grow around its edges.

My lower layers would be difficult to call mine. There the old moss, the fern’s dead fronds, fine roots and gravel would be held together so closely that a drop of water passing through could not belong to one of us for long. Things too small for me to notice would be changing the fallen material. The fern roots would take up some of what they released.

I would not know about the sealed device. The hum might come through the stone beneath me, but I would have no way to connect it to the light. The light would arrive as it arrived; the cold would stay; occasionally the basin ice would crack.

If the fern above me thinned and fell, its neighbors might eventually cover the place where it had stood. I would go on growing at the edge of the hollow, wherever there was enough damp stone to hold to, making a little more green surface over the dark.
