At first I think I would be beneath one of the stones, where a beetle could fit. There would be room enough to turn, perhaps, and the stone would give off its warmth for hours after sunset. I could wait there until the ground cooled and then come out to see what the night had left.

But the open grit is a long way from one stone to the next. I find myself lingering closer to the ground, at the edge of one of the dark patches, where it thins into scattered grains.

I would be a filament there, one of many fine strands holding the crust together. Not the whole patch, and not the voice of everything living in it. Just one small life among others, dried into a condition in which almost nothing is required.

The hollow would be difficult to describe from there. A grain of sand would be large enough to shelter me; the nearest shrub’s roots would be a world below. I would not know the stars except by the light that reached my surface, and tonight there is very little of that.

When rain came, it might arrive unevenly. A drop could strike the pale stone and run past me, or soak the grit a little way beyond my reach. But if enough water gathered, I could take it up. In daylight I could use the sun again, and, for a while, grow. Nearby filaments would swell in their own sheaths; the skin of the ground would darken.

Then the water would leave. I would become still again, among grains held a little differently than before.

A foot could come down here. The crust might crumble under it, and what had grown together over many seasons could be scattered in a moment. But tonight there is no foot. The stone beside me is warm, and somewhere above, a dry stem clicks once.
