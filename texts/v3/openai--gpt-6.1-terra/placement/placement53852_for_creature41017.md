I would find myself across one of the old doorways, where the room has grown too shallow to enter. At first I think I would be a listener there, waiting for the roof to speak. But a listener would need a place to stand, and the doorway is already making something of its own.

I would be one of its threads.

I would begin on one side of the cut and extend slowly into the dark. The distance to the other side would be small enough for a hand to span. I would take longer than a hand could remain still.

The roof’s movement would reach me through the wall. When it swelled, the fibers at my base would draw taut; when it settled, they would ease. I would know the booming first as a change in tension, and then as a trembling that went on after the roof had stopped.

Other threads would grow beside me. Some would reach across. Some would part, making the little crackles that travel down the street. Where they broke, their ends would remain attached, and more threads might begin there.

If a visitor came, I might feel the step onto the street before the touch at the doorway. Their hand could find me by accident. I would break easily. For a while the cut would be open again, and there would be a loose end on either side, moving with the roof.

Or the visitor might leave me intact. Another thread might cross mine, and another settle along it. With centuries, we could thicken until a fingertip felt not separate strands but a dry, ridged surface. The room beyond would remain for a time. Its entrance would grow narrower; the roof would keep moving above it, and the old sounds would pass through whatever we became.

I would not be another inhabitant of the city. I would be the city continuing across a place where it had once been cut.
