I am a filament growing from the lower wall, where the fresh stone disappears into silt. At first I would have said I live on the rock. My base is fixed there, and the minerals come through it. But part of me has been buried, and part has grown across the edge of a frond, and the frond has folded so that my tip now rests against a white cup. I receive things from all three places. I do not know which of them I would be without.

The cup is useful to me. Water moves past its rim more freely than it does near the wall. When something settles there, the cup closes slowly, and some portion of what it takes eventually reaches me. Once a visitor put its head inside and ate the film from its inner surface. I felt the cup give beneath it. I drew tight, though not soon enough to help; the animal had already withdrawn.

It left the cup intact. Later, it returned and combed the frond beside me, and I lost several branches. For a long time afterward I grew toward the bare place. The new branches were finer than the old ones. When the animal came again, they stretched over its back without breaking, and for a while I was attached to something that could move.

I learned little from this. The visitor stayed almost perfectly still. It may have been feeding, or waiting for warmth, or resting with its threads extended into a passage I could not reach. When it finally went on, my branches parted. One remained on its back. I cannot feel that one now.

The rock clicks from time to time. The first motion comes through my base; another arrives much later from the silt. By then I have often relaxed. I used to draw tight for both, but the second motion has become familiar. It comes up through the buried things, altered by their bodies and by the weight of the grains above them. Sometimes it reaches me unevenly, one side before the other, and I bend toward the corner.

There is nourishment there, though getting to it takes a season or more. I have sent a branch down between the grains. It grows slowly. The deeper it goes, the longer each grain seems to take to settle against it.

In the cold, the frond stiffens and pulls my upper branches taut. I cannot reach the cup then. I remain connected to the stone and to whatever lies below it, and wait. When warmth comes back, the frond loosens, but it does not always return to its former position. One year the cup opened on the far side of a fold, beyond my reach. I grew toward it again and found two smaller cups in the way.

Something young lives among them now. Its threads touch my new branches before its body arrives. It is very small; when it grazes, the branches scarcely move. Still, I draw tight. Afterward I grow into the space it has cleared.
