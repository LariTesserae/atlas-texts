I am at first a yielding place between shells.

A foot finds the edge of me and withdraws. Another presses down. I turn, or the bodies around me turn me, until one of my own feet catches on stone. There is room beneath an adult’s raised shell, enough to pull myself through. My edges bend against it. I come out with a grain of sulfur held between two bristles.

The wall is close. I work my mouth over it, taking the film a little at a time. A patch of pale crust appears beneath me. When another body moves into it, I climb across its back and find another place to feed.

The blows begin. My feet hold fast. The grain between my bristles jumps, then jumps again and falls. The shell above me bears down; the stone comes up against my underside. Between blows there is a finer trembling, and through that, the touch of legs still moving. I keep my feet where they are until the pressure eases enough for me to reach the wall again.

By the time my shell has hardened, I can no longer fit beneath the adult. I have to go around it. Going around means pushing against a second body, which pushes against a third. After a while the first lifts a fraction and I slide past. The movement travels on without me. Farther along the seam, a shell rises and settles, rises and settles. A newly hatched animal comes through.

It passes close to a narrow head held still among the grazers. A leg touches its edge. The small body jerks and is held. I feel the struggle through the shells beneath my feet as I move over the hunter’s back. Its ridges are deeper than mine; I catch a foot in one and pull it free.

Later, the blows stop. For a while I brace whenever a neighbor shifts, expecting the stone to strike against me again. Gradually my legs loosen. I reach a fresh patch of film and lower my mouth to it.

Something small presses against my side. I lift a little, and it slips underneath me. Its feet click against the rock, then move away toward the seam.
