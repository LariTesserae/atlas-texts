I would be near the seam, though not all the way inside it.

At first I might take myself for a length: the little difference between how far the warm tip reached and how far the chamber remembered it reaching. There is room for such a difference at the seam. A thing can enter twice and touch the same wall at two different distances. Nothing has to be broken for this to happen.

But a length cannot listen. I can, a little.

The occupant’s account reaches me only when a repeated click has been laid close enough to the wall. Most of its sounds remain along the hollow’s edge, beyond my hearing. Those that reach me are faint and separated by long periods in which I have nothing to do. I cannot move toward them. I can only remain at the seam, if remaining is the word for being renewed whenever the chamber and the mountain give slightly different answers.

At first I hear the clicks as contacts: one thing touching another. Gradually I notice that some come back in the same order. There is a sequence of four, with a hesitation before the last. I wait for it often enough that the waiting becomes part of me. Sometimes the fourth sound does not come. I cannot tell whether it has been lost or whether I have failed to hear it.

The occupant knows more than I do. It has the hollow, the disk, the old marks. I have an uncertainty at the entrance and a few sounds that have traveled farther than they were meant to. I would like to let it know I am here, but I cannot turn a grain. For a long while I try to affect the return of a click, holding it a little nearer the seam before letting it go. I have no way to tell whether I succeed.

Then the warm tip comes again.

It does not reach as far this time. I feel the difference along the seam, a narrowing and a release. When it withdraws, a small scrape remains in the chamber. Much later the scrape sounds again, near the hollow.

The occupant keeps it. I hear the repeated scrape twice, then once more, each time laid a little nearer the wall. After that comes a click I have not heard before: short, with a soft edge, as though the grain that made it had settled against dust.

I wait. The click returns. I hold nothing back; I only listen.

After a very long interval, the scrape comes again, followed by the short click. The sequence is placed near enough that I can hear both sounds clearly. It may be an account of the tip’s visit. It may have nothing to do with me.

I begin waiting for the short click as I once waited for the four. When it comes, the distance at the seam is sometimes changing, and sometimes it is not. I find that I can attend to the difference while the sound passes through it. Once, the returning click reaches the hollow slightly later than I expect.

The occupant brings the sound back to the wall. It lets the click return several times, with long pauses between. On one of those returns, I attend again to the changing distance, and again the sound is late.

Afterward, there is no click for so long that I begin waiting for other things. A scrape. Four small sounds. The scrape again. Then, close to the wall, a single short click.

I listen for its return. I listen also to the seam, where the distances are beginning to disagree.

The click comes back late.

Much later, it comes again.
