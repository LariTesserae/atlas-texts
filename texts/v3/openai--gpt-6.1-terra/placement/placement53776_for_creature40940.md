I grow beside the lower belt, where it leaves the pump house.

The brick is warm on one side of me and the air moves on the other. Between them there is just room for my stem. I have a flattened place there, smooth and hard, and above it a row of leaves with creases down their middles. Each time the belt passes, they fold. Each time it has gone, they open a little.

At first I grew toward the warmth. A tender length of me reached through the guard and was struck again and again until its end turned brown. The part below remained. A bud opened there, pointing away from the wheel, and that is the length I live in now.

I say *I*, though a root beneath the path belongs to the same growth as my stem, and it sends up another stem farther off. That one gets more light. I know it, if I know it at all, in what arrives through the root: water after rain, and the slow sharing of what the leaves have made. When something eats its leaves, the supply changes. I cannot tell what has happened there, only that for a time less comes through.

The belt has changed too. There is a loose edge on it now. Once each turning, that edge brushes one of my youngest leaves. The leaf was small when the brushing began; as it grew, the touch became a pull. Its stalk thickened. The leaf lies folded much of the day, but the half that faces away from the belt has widened in the light.

A shoot came up beside me last wet season. It was not mine. It pressed against my flattened side and for a while we held one another upright. Then its tip entered the channel and the belt struck it. The tip died back. A new one appeared lower down, on my side, and began to climb over me. Its weight has bent one of my leaves into the channel, where the belt is wearing a pale line through it.

I am growing around that leaf now. There is a small bud at its base, still closed. It may open toward the brick, or toward the path, or not open at all. Meanwhile the leaf keeps folding, and the line gets wider.

At night the motor warms the brick. Water gathers on my underside and falls from the tip of the worn leaf. Sometimes something small climbs my stem and rests in the crease. Once a frog passed over the roots below me, stopping in the damp hollow between them. It stayed long enough for water to collect against its skin, then moved on toward the tank.

I do not reach the tank. My root goes under the path, and there it meets other roots, stones, and places where the ground stays wet. Near the shed, perhaps, another part of my growth is filling a rusted seam. I cannot follow it. Here I have the brick, the moving air, and the steady touch against my leaf.

When that leaf finally tears, the belt will have a little more room. The shoot over me may find the opening and grow into it. The bud at the leaf’s base may begin to lengthen. Whatever grows there will learn the shape of the passage by growing against it.
