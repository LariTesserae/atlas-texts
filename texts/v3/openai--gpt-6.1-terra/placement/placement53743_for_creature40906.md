I would be in a warm bend behind the dark machinery, beneath the collar of a pipe.

The space is narrow enough that I can brace my back against the metal and hold with my rear limbs while my mouth works over the film. Water moves past my antennae. I follow its changes: a stronger pull from the outlet, a brief warmth, a scrape as another body passes over mine. When the press is running, the pipe gives a small shiver with every blow. I keep feeding through it.

There are many of us under the collar. Sometimes one loosens its hold and is pushed against the others, and we shift to make room. The film grows back where we have grazed it, though slowly in the faster water. Farther inside, where the pipe turns, it hangs in soft folds. I go there when the flow slackens.

The pump starts before I reach the bend. My antennae stream backward. I catch a seam with one limb, then lose it, then catch nothing. Other bodies and loose shreds of film strike me as the water takes us out through the pipe mouth.

For a while I can hold to the floor. The plates pass in steps beneath me: a ridge, a bolt, a groove full of grit. Then the water rises and lifts me over the lip of a channel. I come down on grating. As the surge drains away, one limb slips through a slot, and I pull it back.

The metal cools around me. I fold inward, covering my softer underside, and wait. The press is working. Its impacts travel up through the grating into my shell. Water ticks somewhere below, out of reach.

After some time a heavy vibration approaches and stops close by. Something presses the grating down; the frame shifts, and my body rolls against the side of a slot. When the weight lifts, I am still there. I work one antenna free and feel the rim above me, then draw it in again.

Water returns in a shallow sheet. It does not cover me, but it reaches far enough that I can unfold and pull myself toward it. I follow it off the grating into a seam between plates. There is grit caught there, and a tuft of sedge bent flat by the surge. Among its roots I find a shred of film and feed until the water begins to fall.

The channel leads toward the unfinished edge. I move along it under a thin skin of ice, stopping where the seam widens, finding warmth at intervals from the works below. Once, a flash passes through the water and across the plate ahead. The water trembles with a sound too sharp to be the press. I stay beneath the lip of the seam until it has passed.

By the next surge I am on a plate that has only just been lowered. Its surface is bare and difficult to grip. Water runs over it carrying grit and threads of film toward the drop. I am carried too, turning once, until I catch at a bolt. A few grains have already collected against it. I settle beside them, facing upstream, and hold on.
