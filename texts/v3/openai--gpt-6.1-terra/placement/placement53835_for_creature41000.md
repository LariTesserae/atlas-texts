I might first mistake myself for the hum. It is the one thing that reaches everywhere: through the floor, the shelves, the water between them. But it comes from elsewhere, and it would go on without this basin.

Perhaps I am the current, then, slipping over the rim and finding the low channel out. Yet the current leaves too readily. It passes over every groove without staying in any of them.

I think I would be a small patch of the pale film, just inside the lip of a shelf. Not the whole growth in the basin, only this colony, where water slows enough for things to settle and keeps moving enough to bring more.

From below, I would be difficult to distinguish from the stone. A grain of silt caught at my edge would remain visible for a while. Then the film would thicken around it, and the grain would become a little rise in my surface. Others would follow. There would be no centre to me, no single place from which I watched them arrive. The organisms nearest the water would live differently from those close against the limestone; some would die, and others would make use of what remained.

A green strand grows a short distance from my shelf. I do not reach toward it, though eventually part of the film may spread into its crack. In daylight it is more conspicuous than I am, waving so slightly that its movement is difficult to see. I would gather beneath it, where the stone gives shelter.

The hum would tremble my loose edge. When it grew faint, I would not go looking for it. Water would still pass, grains would still lodge, and the colony would go on growing.

One day a piece might lift away. For a little while it would cross the open basin, no longer attached to the shelf. If it caught in another groove, it might hold together and begin to spread there. I would remain behind too, thinner at the edge, with the current passing through the place where it had been.
