I would live against the stone, beneath the leaves. I would have a broad mouth and a jointed, colorless body, and most of me would be hidden in a groove only a little wider than myself.

The stone would be my food. I would rasp it slowly, bracing my legs on either side of the groove. When a root entered the place I had opened, I would turn and feed on the soft deposit it left. Sometimes I would continue beside it; sometimes the root would fill the groove and I would have to begin another. I do not think I would know which of us had sought the other out.

The ground would tell me when an ash-colored animal was near. Its feet would make fourteen small contacts, though several might reach me through the same stretch of stone. When it held still, I could feel a faint pressure from its head. I might rasp beneath it for a long time without disturbing it. Then a crystalline tip would snap above us, and the pressure would lift.

Once, working toward a patch of exposed mineral, I would come to the side of my own groove. I would feel the marks of my mouth there: a row of short ridges, then a smooth place where a root had passed. I would follow them for a while, expecting to find the place where I had turned. Instead I would meet another mouth.

It would fit the groove as mine did. We would press together, unable to pass, until one of us backed away. I would feel its legs moving against the stone as clearly as my own. Perhaps that would mean we were the same animal; perhaps the stone would carry its movements unusually well there. I would follow the retreating mouth until it reached a branch in the groove and disappeared into it. Then I would go on eating.

Much later, a root would enter that branch. It would grow past my head and leave a deposit against the stone. While I fed, the root would thicken and bend toward a seed lodged somewhere above. I could not reach the seed, but I would feel its weight draw steadily through the root. On the other side of the branch, another mouth would be feeding too.

When the seed opened, the pressure would change. A stem would rise, then another, far enough apart that I could not sense them both at once. Their roots would remain close to me. From time to time one would tighten around the stone, and the other would follow.

I would keep making my groove. The mouths might meet again, or the root might grow between us and leave us working on opposite sides. Above, the two crowns would gather light in their separate places. Under them I would have stone to eat, and room, for a while, to turn.
