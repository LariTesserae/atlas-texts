I would be in the packing too, though at first I imagine myself higher up: a drop on a bolt head, holding an upside-down work lamp until the plate shudders and I fall. Or one of the pale numerals beside a valve, dry enough to remain legible, with no way to follow what passes beneath it.

But I think I would be lower, on the submerged side of a moss thread. I would be part of the bacterial film there: cells attached to the fiber and to one another, with a little matrix of secreted material holding us in place. Not a single creature, and not quite a colony with an edge one could draw. Water would move through us as well as over us.

Particles would arrive in the current. Some we could use; many would pass. We would divide where there was room and enough to feed on. A cell at the outside might be sheared away while those beneath it remained. If it caught elsewhere, the film might begin again, although nothing would tell us that it had.

The rotifer would come near. Its cilia would draw water across the thread, and cells loosened from our surface might enter its mouth. I would not know it as a neighbor. The pull at the edge of the film would be one more movement in water already moving, though it would carry some of us into a different life.

When the valve opened, the bead around the moss would flatten and run. The thread would bend; the film would stretch and tear in places. I might leave then, carried across the steel toward the grate. Or I might remain on the fiber while the rotifer folded and held fast beside me.

Afterward, water would still surround us. There would be another interval in which to feed and divide, and then another disturbance. I would have no view of the court, no sense of its height or of the sky above it. The dam would reach me through its water: sometimes as food, sometimes as a force that took part of me away.
