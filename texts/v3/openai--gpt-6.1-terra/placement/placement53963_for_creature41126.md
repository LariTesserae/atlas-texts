I would be in the shaded chamber, at first so close to the wall that I might be taken for a flaw in it: a pale length of fiber caught in the seam beneath the thumbprint.

From there I could see the seam creature waiting at the edge of the resin. Its legs would be folded under it, its blunt head resting against the soft shine. Behind it the paving would be bright enough to hurt the eyes.

I would have eyes, I think, though not at first a face. Two dark beads worked into the fiber, perhaps, and a body that could loosen itself from the joint one strand at a time. My lower end would remain caught. I could lean out over the paving, draw back into shade, turn to follow the creature when it moved.

For a long time it would not move.

The clay above me would be warm. I would try to fit myself into the broad impression under the block, arranging my strands as closely as I could along the lines of the print. The warmth would pass into me. It would not tell me whose hand had been there, or whether it had been there recently. I would stay until I began to cool.

When I came back to the seam, the creature would have advanced a little. The resin had yielded around its head without taking an impression. I might touch it too, cautiously, and find it sticky. A few of my strands would come away in it. The creature would turn toward them, wait, then begin pulling them underneath the block.

That would be our first work together. I could reach places it could not, and it could take in the fibers I left at the edge. I would learn to separate a fine strand from my body without breaking it, to lay it across a gap and hold it there while the creature fastened it from below. Afterward I would be a little thinner. The gap would be closed, though on the shaded side a pale thread would remain visible.

Sometimes I would stretch as far as my tether allowed, toward the doorway. From there I could see the deer on the other side of the terrace, and its reflection in the basin on this side. I would pull against the seam, trying to reach the water, until the block gave a faint tick around me. Then I would draw myself back and wait for the sound to stop.

The creature would attend to that seam as well. It would go in beside my tether and emerge hours later, or what felt like hours, from a joint near the garden border. I would watch it cross the paving. The distance would not lessen until it was suddenly at the woven edge, climbing among the roots.

I would remain beneath the thumbprint. When the resin was nearly firm, I could lift one strand from its surface and find that it came away clean. The creature would return and touch the seam with its head. Together we would wait a little longer.
