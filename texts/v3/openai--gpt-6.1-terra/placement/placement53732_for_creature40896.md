I would be the third pot, on the ledge.

At first I think I am a place to rest: a hollow with a rim, a bottom, and a little weight. I was made to hold something, though water comes and goes through me without needing permission. The chamber is full of it, and so am I.

The surge reaches me after the grass. It knocks my side against the ledge, and I answer with the low note that carries through my clay. I don’t know whether the others hear it. Their roots and ties muffle them; mine is the sound of an empty pot struck while full of water.

The one who lives here comes around my rim. Sometimes it passes over me so quickly that I feel only a brief pressure against my inner wall. Sometimes it turns down into me and circles there, sand tracing a pale ring across my bottom. The channel pulls at it, and the wider water follows, but the turning lasts a little longer inside me.

I cannot keep it. My rim is too broad, and even if it were narrow, the next swell would come.

Once, a loose strand of fiber drifts in. It moves with the whirl, touching my wall again and again. When the water slows, it settles across my bottom. I hold it through the soft interval, long enough to feel its slight weight against the clay. Then the next rush lifts it, folds it over my rim and carries it away.

Afterward, I wait for it without knowing that I am waiting. Each time something brushes my interior I feel for that fine, yielding drag. More often it is sand. Once it is a tuft of grass bending far enough from its own pot to touch me.

My base has begun to wear a small pale patch where I knock against the ledge. The corners of the blocks are still sharp. Sunlight moves over my inner wall and gathers on my bottom, then breaks apart when the water comes.

At the quietest point I can feel the last of the turning ease away. There is a moment before the thud at the holes, before the grass bows and the pots rattle. Then the water reaches my rim, and I make room for it again.
