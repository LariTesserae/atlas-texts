At first I would look for an open place: the small space above a ridge, perhaps, or the junction where several seams come together. But there is no open place that is not already being made use of. Even the light occupies no room I could take.

I think I would be one of the sprigs at that junction. Not the largest. A short clear branch would reach along one seam, and a second, shorter one would end against the rounded side of a column. My roots would be difficult to find, even for me, if finding them were something I could do.

The vapor would pass through my tissues. I would take from it, shed the white substance, and dissolve what pressed too near. In that way my shape would remain: not because nothing happened to it, but because what happened was so nearly balanced.

The column beside me would contain something I had laid down. I could not say how much. My neighbors’ substance would have joined mine without leaving a line, and the same column would bear against the growths farther along the seam. If I dissolved its surface to keep my place, I might be removing what another sprig had shed. The distinction would not survive the work.

The note would pass through me. I would not hear it, exactly, and my tips would not move. Still, it would be there, loud throughout the chamber, as much a condition of my existence as the cold light and the vapor.

I would remain at the junction, with one branch reaching into each narrow opening. Around me, the chamber would go on becoming the chamber, and my unchanged outline would be one of the spaces it made.
