I would live on the underside of a bracket, where a drop gathers before it falls.

I would be part of the film there: a thin colony spread across metal and mineral, thickest in the shallow scratches. The drop would pass over us slowly, bringing dissolved things from the pipe above. Where it lingered, we would grow. Where it dried, we would leave a skin behind.

I would not know the whole chamber. I might know the edge of a bolt by the colder water that comes around it, or a patch of rust by what seeps from it into the film. When the floor sends up a wave of heat, the bracket warms after the pipe does. For a while there is less water to share. Then another drop arrives and joins the first.

An insect comes along the edge of the bracket. Its mouthparts scrape a clear path through us, down to the hard surface. It eats until it reaches the bolt, then turns. Some of us remain in the scratches too narrow for it to reach. By the time it comes back, the path has begun to close.

There are others of us on the jointed stems beside the trough. I could not tell you when they arrived there, or whether they came from this bracket at all. A falling drop might have carried a few of us down. An insect might have taken some on its feet. The stems hold moisture differently from the metal: close around their fine fringes, with air moving on every side. The film there grows in little sleeves. When an insect eats a fringe, the sleeve goes with it.

One day the drop beneath the bracket grows heavy enough to pull away. It takes a strip of the film, including some of the cells that were in the insect’s scraped path. I remain behind, and I fall.

The drop strikes the trough beside a white island. Some of us wash into the shallow water. Others catch against the scale, where fine grit has gathered. The water here is warmer than it was on the bracket, and it moves, though so slowly that the grains seem settled until the long animal passes.

Its front lobes press into the grit. It draws the grains through its mouth, taking the film among them, and leaves a clean fan behind. A few grains roll into a hollow in the scale, beyond its reach. They are wet, and there is room between them.

Above, another drop begins to gather under the bracket. Here, in the hollow, we begin to spread over a grain.
