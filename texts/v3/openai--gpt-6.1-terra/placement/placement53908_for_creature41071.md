I think at first I would be one of the creatures in the fingertip marks, darkened by clay. There is a shape there that seems made for a body: a shallow curve, a little protection from the brightness.

But I keep returning to the pebble wall.

I would be the six-legged animal outside it. My feet are spread on the mineral, and my head faces the stones. I am small enough that each pebble presents a different surface: one smooth and pale, another with a blunt crystal protruding from it, another glossy along its lower edge. Through the gaps I can see the wet floor of the enclosure, though not the whole of it at once.

There is a great deal I cannot see from here. The animals behind me are close; their backs and limbs crowd the light around my body. Somewhere among them is a frond, and somewhere farther off the clay. I know them, perhaps, by the shapes they interrupt in the brightness. I do not know whether I have ever been nearer to either.

The ringing is loudest where the pebbles nearly meet. It does not seem to come through the gaps, exactly. It is there beside my head, and within the empty place, and among the animals at my back. I cannot tell whether the wall makes any difference to it.

I am facing the wall. That is certain. Whether I came here to watch the little space, or was left facing it by the pressure of other bodies, I cannot say. I would like to think I recognize it as a room. The damp floor is open, and no one stands inside. Yet the pebble nearest me leans so slightly against its neighbor that the opening between them is narrower than my head. If there is a way in, it is somewhere I cannot see.

I remain outside. My feet hold their places on the fine gloss, and the stones hold theirs. The sound continues. In the bright gap before me, the empty floor stays empty; I have that much to look at.
