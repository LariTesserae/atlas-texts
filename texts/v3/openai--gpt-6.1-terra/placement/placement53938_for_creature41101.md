I live in the joint where the pump house’s warm pipe passes through the retaining wall. From inside, the joint is only a narrow ring of darkness. From outside, it is a seam in the stone, halfway up the wall. I know both sides, though I spend little time on the outer one.

My body has a cloudy plate near the tail and one short leg that does not reach the pipe. The others go over me as readily as they go over the sounder bodies. Sometimes a young one pauses beneath the edge of the cloudy plate, where there is warmth enough and a little shelter from the rubbing. I feel its legs moving against me until it finds its way out.

The pipe shivers before each stroke of the pump. The shiver comes through our feet, and we draw ourselves a little narrower as it passes. A moment later the wall takes it up. By then the bodies nearest the pipe have already begun to spread again.

There is a place on the underside where water gathers. I go there to feed, working between the legs that hold to the iron. The film yields in small, soft scrapings. When I have eaten, I can feel the cleaner pipe with my mouth; soon another body passes over the place and I lose it.

Once, at least, I followed the pipe farther than usual. I may have done it many times. The joint is crowded, and a journey through it can begin without any decision: a body presses in behind you, a leg finds a purchase ahead, and you move because there is room.

The pipe grew cooler. My feet found stone instead of iron, and then there was daylight along one edge of me. I went toward it until my head emerged from the wall.

The outer shelf lay below. It was pale and dry except where vapor from the basin had settled in a shallow seam. I crossed the top of the wall and descended a little way. The rasp behind me became fainter with each step. I could still feel the wall through my rear feet, but when I lifted them clear, I felt only the stone beneath the others.

I stopped. Nothing pressed against my sides. My short leg moved and found nothing, as it always does, but here I could feel the difference between it and the rest.

There was water in the seam, though not much. I walked its length, lowering my mouth to the stone. The seam ended in a smooth patch where my feet slipped. When I turned, the wall stood close behind me. A translucent joint protruded from its mortar, bent, and withdrew.

I climbed back. At the rim I paused to let another animal pass; it came out onto the shelf with its legs folded close, then opened them one by one. We touched briefly. It continued downward, and I entered the space it had left.

Inside the wall, bodies moved past me in both directions. The pump gave its stroke. The vibration traveled along the pipe, through the stone, and through the feet of the animals holding to both. I went back toward the warmth until the others closed around me, leaving enough space for my mouth and the legs I needed to use.
