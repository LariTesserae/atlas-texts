I am under the collapsed shelter, where the stones leave a space just high enough for me to stand. Dust has gathered here in a low bank. When the ground warms, the bank holds the heat longer than the bare floor outside.

There are others in the gap. We have made three entrances through the dust, though the wind keeps closing the largest one. I work at it with a pebble gripped between my front limbs. The pebble is too smooth on one side, so I turn it until an edge catches, then tap and pull the loosened grains beneath me. Behind me, another runner pushes them out.

A vibration comes through the shelter stones. For a moment I stop. Then a frond strikes the roof of the standing shelter, and the cord knocks there: several hard sounds, close together.

I go out.

The gust has torn a leaf across one of its thick veins. Oil stands along the edge in three shining drops. By the time I reach it, one has fallen into the dust and hardened. A runner from beneath the nearest plant is already pulling it away. I seize the other side. More of us arrive, and for a while the bead moves back and forth, gathering a coat of powder.

The runner opposite me loses its grip. I drag the bead toward the shelter, but it catches against a stone. Two smaller runners take hold beside me. They pull in different directions until one finds a crack in the bead and works a piece loose. It disappears beneath a curled leaf with the piece. I follow it, leaving the rest to the others.

There is more oil on the leaf. I climb the stem, passing a pale cluster tucked into a fold. A rasper is feeding nearby, its body pressed into a pleat. It contracts as I come close. I reach past it to a hardened thread along the torn vein and break off the end.

The frond lifts. I hold on while the wind shakes it, and a flyer strikes the far edge. Its hooked feet catch; it folds itself down and crawls toward the stem. The rasper flattens. I carry my piece of oil down before the leaf begins to shake again.

At the shelter entrance the dust has shifted. The opening is there, but narrowed; the piece will not pass through. I set it down and fetch my pebble. Another runner comes out as I begin to tap, and pauses beside the oil. I turn toward it. It takes the piece instead, backs up the bank and tries the second entrance, where it fits.

I follow.

The storage chamber is close to the surface. Its roof is a flat stone, warm underneath. The runner has put the new piece among the older beads, some scratched white from handling. I leave it there and return to the entrance with my pebble.

Outside, the wind is carrying dust across the hollow again. In the lee of the tooth, grains settle softly over my forelimbs and the pebble. I clear the opening until another runner can pass me. It brings out a load of loosened dust and pushes it onto the bank. Then I go inside to help dig.
