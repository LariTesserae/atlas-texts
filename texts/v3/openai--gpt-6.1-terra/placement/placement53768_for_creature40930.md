I would find myself caught against the wall, a little way above the cup.

At first I would be a broken line of stone, no longer than the silver animal, held there by a fold that has risen around one end. The current would lift my free end and strike it against the wall. After each reversal I would expect to be carried away. After many reversals, I would still be there.

Something dark runs through me. It withdraws when the highest note comes, then comes forward again. For a long time I think of it as a weakness in the stone. Later, when a flake catches against my side and the dark thread reaches out to hold it, I begin to understand that it is part of me.

Perhaps I was made by the creature beneath the stones. I remember no release, but my shape has the same fineness as the lines it sends upward. Unlike those lines, I have broken without dissolving. I thicken at the place where the fold holds me. The wall and I are becoming difficult to separate.

I cannot see the cup all at once. When the current bears me outward I can see beneath the roof stone, and when it presses me back I can see only pale wall and wavering light. I come to know the creature in intervals: a knot, a lifted fringe, a sudden handful of filaments streaming over the rim. Sometimes one of its new lines comes close enough to brush me. The touch passes along my length and into the fold.

Once, a line catches on my free end. For several surges it holds. The creature climbs it, spreading so thinly that I can see the wall through its body. It reaches the place where we are joined and stays there while particles drift into its filaments. One of my own threads emerges from the stone and takes hold of a particle beside it.

I do not know whether the creature notices. Its fringe rests against me, quivering at a speed I cannot follow. I feel a new seam starting at the edge of it, and for a moment I think it will grow through me. Instead it runs past, toward the ceiling, and breaks before it gets there.

The line between us breaks too. The creature is pulled back into its cup. I remain on the wall with the particle caught in my thread. By the next inward rush, the particle is gone, and the thread has grown a little longer.

After that I wait differently. Not for rescue; the cup is well sheltered, but I cannot get there. I wait for things small enough to stay against me. A flake lodges at my base. A grain catches in the dark thread. I can hold them through one reversal, sometimes two. The silver animal comes to investigate and picks the grain away. Its fin touches my free end, and I curl around it without meaning to. It vanishes so quickly that the water closes before I have let go.

The fold at my base rises. My loose end grows heavier, and each time it strikes the wall the sound changes slightly. One day, during the highest ringing, a second dark thread appears beside the first. It is very short. When I reach toward it, it retreats into the stone.

Below, the creature lays its filaments against an upright stone, and a ridge begins to form around their tips. Above it, part of the canopy gives way. The falling strands whip through the water; one catches on my free end, trails there for a while, and tears loose.

I am still too small to know whether the thread beside mine belongs to me or to someone else. I hold my end away from the wall as long as I can, giving it room to come out again.
