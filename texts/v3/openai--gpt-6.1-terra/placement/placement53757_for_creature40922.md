I would be under one of the fungal fans, in the rotting root above the basin. From outside, there would be little to see of me: a dark beetle with limestone dust lodged along the seam of my wing cases.

The space beneath the fan is close and uneven. I can turn around in it, but not without brushing the roof with my back. The root is soft enough to eat in places. In others I must work around hard fibers, leaving a scatter of crumbs behind me. Smaller creatures move through those crumbs. I feel them against my legs before I can distinguish them from the grit.

When the fan dries, it lifts at the edges. More air comes in. The root grows hard, and the little animals disappear into cracks too small for me to follow. I go farther along the root, testing it as I go, until I find a pocket that still gives beneath my mouth.

Rain reaches me first as a change in the wood. Then a drop strikes the fan and makes the whole shelter jump. Another finds its way around the edge and runs down the root. I climb toward the damp, but the water comes faster, filling the groove in which I am standing. I back out, turn, and make my way into the litter.

The basin is fuller than I have ever found it. Crickets are gathered on the far side, their legs trembling. I reach a shallow runnel and drink there, where the water spreads thinly over the stone. The lizard is on her middle stone. I keep moving along the edge, with a piece of fern litter between us, though I cannot tell whether she is watching me.

Later, under the fan again, I find that the crumbs have shifted and a strand of root has fallen across my usual passage. I press beneath it. It moves a little, shedding damp grit onto my back, and I can get through. I eat at the newly exposed surface until the space around me darkens and the bats begin to stir near the entrance.
