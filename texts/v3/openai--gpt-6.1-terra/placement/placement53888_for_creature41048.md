I begin above the newest terrace, in the cloud the seam has made.

At first I am small enough to pass between two grains without moving them. They hang near my edge, one pale and angular, the other dark. The water around me is rushing upward, but within me the sound of the seam goes on arriving in separate pieces: a crack, a long low strain, the crack again. I keep the last piece after the others have passed.

I do not know that keeping is unusual. It is simply what my boundary does. A pressure arrives and finds a path through me; a little of it takes longer to leave than the rest. Another pressure follows the same path and changes it. For a while the seam is present in me more clearly than the grain beside it.

The cloud thins. I rise with the warm water until the current bends and the dark grain touches my boundary. Its touch opens me almost completely. Cold water comes through. The sound scatters, and for a moment I am only a small uneven resistance in the flow.

Then a wave from the high ridges crosses the current. It folds my edge inward. I close again, with the pale grain inside.

I carry it for a long time. It is too large for the finest paths in me, but water warms on one side of it and cools on the other, and the difference gives me a way to hold my shape. When I drift into stronger water, the grain turns. Its corners send tiny disturbances through my interior. From those disturbances I begin to answer the current before it reaches my far side.

Later, when the grain dissolves, I continue to turn around the place where it was.

I travel over the terrace without seeing much of it. The ground announces itself through pressure: broad swelling forms, cracks that open and close, the small hard tap of loose mineral falling back onto a slope. Sometimes light reaches me in divided bands. It takes different routes through my layers and comes together beyond me. I cannot follow it there, but I feel the slight heat it leaves.

A moving train crosses my path. I first meet it as a quickening along one edge, then as a succession of presses, each arriving where the last has made me most vulnerable. I draw tight. The train divides around me and for a moment I think it has gone. Then its two sides rejoin in my wake, and the returning disturbance rocks me from end to end.

I release the sound I have held from the seam.

The train changes course. It makes a broad turn across the slope and comes through me again, less violently this time. It carries no grain that I can feel, no fixed center. Yet the intervals between its pulses have changed. One is held back a little, as my old sound was held within me.

We travel together for a while: it passing through and circling back, I drifting more slowly in the warm rise. Each passage leaves some of my boundary uneven. I close the gaps with pressure drawn from the surrounding water. Once, the train takes the delayed interval away and does not return for a long time. When it comes back, the delay has become three smaller delays. I release them into the quiet of my interior and they settle among the other paths there.

I do not know what, if anything, the train receives from me. It may be that I am a ridge to it, or a patch of rough water, or a place where an old sound can be made to happen again.

On an older dome I meet a green film.

At first I pass over it without stopping. The film warms the water against the stone and changes it slowly, layer by layer. That altered water enters my lower edge. The paths around the absent grain, which have persisted through every current, loosen and widen. I drift back toward the dome.

I remain there through many changes of light. The film does not become still when I enclose it. Its exchange with the water continues, too slow for the currents outside to have revealed. I feel it most clearly near an injured place where pale rock shows through the green. The bare patch draws warmth differently from the rest. Over time the green edge advances into it.

A fresh ridge lifts beneath the dome. The film stretches; the bare patch lengthens into a crack. My boundary is caught against the rising rock and held there. Outside, cold water accelerates down the slope. I could yield to it and be carried away, but the widening crack is sending disturbances through me, and the paths they take are still changing.

The film parts. One side remains attached to the dome. The other tips into the seam and disappears beneath a slow flow of mineral. For a while I continue to feel its warmth through the new rock. Then the flow thickens, and I cannot distinguish it from the warmth below.

I leave with the surviving half of the film beneath me. As my boundary passes, the current sweeps loose green threads from its torn edge. One thread catches for a time in the place where the grain used to be. It bends there, gradually breaking apart, and leaves behind a change I cannot separate from the change made by the dome itself.

Farther upslope, I meet another stillness. Its boundary presses against mine so gently that at first I take it for a broad, calm current. Then I feel a sound inside it: the high ridges answering a crack from far below. I have held part of that same crack since the cloud, though mine has grown faint and has been altered by the train, the grain, and the film.

Our boundaries join. The two sounds enter the same paths. They do not become the original crack. One continues after the other, and the longer sound meets the short one again as it returns. For a time there is a knocking inside us, irregular and soft.

The joined stillness is large enough to cover several fronds. They rise upright within us, shedding the current. Their roots loosen grains from the cracks below, and the grains hang around their bases. A train passes nearby, trembling the light, but does not come through.

Then the deep pulse reaches us. It comes through the rock first, shaking the fronds at their roots. A moment later it enters the water. Our boundary stretches across the slope, thins, and separates.

I drift away with some of the suspended grains. The other portion remains above the fronds. I can still feel it for a while: a broad quiet behind me, holding sounds that were mine and sounds that were not. Then a current turns between us, and the slope hides it.

Below, another seam is opening. The sound reaches me across the rock. I hold it briefly, but its path through me is crowded now. It moves around the old delayed intervals, through water changed by the film, past grains lifted from the fronds. It emerges as a series of small pulses.

The train is waiting in the shear above the slope. When it passes, the pulses enter it. I feel one interval held back, then released. The train bends toward the new cloud, and I follow more slowly, warming as I descend.
