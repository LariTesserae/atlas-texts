I would be beneath the lifted edge of a crust sheet, where a tuft has caught a little dust.

At first I might seem to be the sheet itself: a pale, curved roof with a dark underside. But the sheet is mostly the work of others, living and dead. I would be one of the fine fungal threads running through the loose material below it, branching around grains and into the crumbling edge of a fallen leaf.

The leaf would be much larger than I am. When rain first presses it down, I can use only what water carries from it. Later, my threads enter its softened surface. Bacteria gather there too. We alter the leaf between us, though not for one another’s sake. Some of what I release feeds them; some of what they leave becomes available to me.

Above, the surface dries quickly. Under the flap, a little damp remains. It enters the dust and darkens the old sheaths that hold the grains together. For a while I extend a tip into a space that was too dry to cross yesterday. The space is narrow, but it leads toward the tuft’s roots, and around the roots there is more to feed on.

I would not experience the plain as an expanse. Its distances would be made by conditions: a few grains of dry dust could stop me longer than a deep, wet seam. The tuft’s nearest root might be unreachable all summer and then, after one storm, connected to me by a continuous film of water.

As the damp goes, growth stops. Parts of me die. Others remain enclosed in the loose material beneath the sheet. I would become harder to distinguish from the old threads already there, which still hold dust although nothing moves through them.

Then perhaps the flap breaks.

Light falls across the place where I have grown. The loose grains warm and dry sooner than before. Some of my threads remain in the shade of the tuft; the exposed ones are left among the dust. On another day rain strikes that dust, carrying fragments into the seam. The crust swells around them, and green filaments begin to move through the wet surface above.

I would still be under the tuft, where the root passes through the older layers. I would also be partly in the new skin: not all of me alive, not all of me fungal anymore. The leaf would be there too, less leaf than it was, and the work of the bacteria, and dust from the open flat.

When that skin dries, it will hold together. Under it, if enough moisture remains, I can grow a little longer.
