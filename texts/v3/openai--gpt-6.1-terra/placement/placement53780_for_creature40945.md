I think I would live near the edge, but not on the ledge itself. There is a shallow basin above it where a stone rib has broken into fins. On one side of a fin the wind strikes hard enough to make the gravel chatter; on the other, a few cushions grow in the dust.

I would be one of the birds. My feet would know the narrow ledges better than my eyes do: where the rock tilts, where a loose chip can turn under a claw, where there is room to settle without displacing another bird. From the basin I could walk to the rim, open my wings and let the air take my weight.

I would spend part of each day below the plateau, feeding in places that cannot be seen from the basin. Coming back, I would rise along the cliff with the others, sometimes high enough to see the pale table appear all at once above the darker slopes. From there the grazers would be hard to pick out. I might see one because it moved an ear.

The pebble I carry would not be special at first. It would be a convenient size, with one edge I could hold firmly. I would bring it to a slab near the basin and strike it there. The note would come through the roar for a moment. A bird on the ledge might raise its head; another might not. Afterward I would drop the pebble among the others.

Over time I would come to favor that slab. It has a crack across one end, and the note is clearer on the near side. Sometimes another bird would use it while I waited on the fin. Once, returning from below, I would find my usual pebble gone and have to try several before finding one that sounded much the same.

In the heat of the day I would rest beside the cushions, my feathers pressed close. Insects would emerge around me and be taken sideways by the wind. A small grazer might settle on the far side of the basin, close enough for me to see its foot move against the stone. I would not know what passed between it and the animal that answered.

Then the light would begin to change on the fins. I would pick up a pebble, carry it to the slab, and strike it there before going down to the ledge for the night.
