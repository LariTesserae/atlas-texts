I think I would begin on the underside of a stone, just inside the entrance to a dome. There is enough light there for a copper branch to lie open, and enough shelter that it can remain open for years.

Most of me would be out of sight, following the small, salt-filled cracks. At first I might take the exposed branches for my whole shape: a thin fan with one edge curled away from the door. Then, growing farther into the stone, I would meet an older thread of my own and find that I had already been here.

A grazer would come along the entrance at dusk. I wouldn’t see it as a complete creature. I would know the faint pressure of its feet, then the loss of a narrow edge. By morning it would be gone, and I would grow again from what remained. Perhaps, after many visits, my outline would be partly the shape of where it had eaten.

People would arrive less often. Their shadows would pass over the entrance, and dust would shake from their clothing. Once, someone might sit just inside and scratch a date into the wall above me. The cut would remain visible through many storms. I would grow toward its lower edge, though the wall there might be too bare to hold me.

Farther out, beyond the shelter of the dome, I would have another patch on a stone exposed to the plain. It would close sooner when a storm approached and open later afterward. For a long time, threads beneath the grit might join it to the part near the entrance.

Then a seam would open between the two stones. By evening it might close again, but the buried threads would be broken. Both patches could go on living. The one outside would be worn back more quickly; the one by the door would keep its small measure of light.

If I were asked afterward where I was, I could give two answers. I would not know that I had given either one.
