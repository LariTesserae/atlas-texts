I think I would begin in one of the cups, though I might not stay there.

At first I would take myself for part of the water: a clear globule held against the limestone by a skin too thin to see. The cup would fill, and the branch left there by the larger inhabitant would swell beside me. In the hours between surges I would drift close to it, then away, moving as the water cooled and warmed.

But the globule would have a seam, and beneath the seam a small body folded around grains of stone. I would have gathered those grains myself. With them I could feel vibrations that passed too quickly for my soft outer surface to register: the faint knock from deep in a column, the report of a bubble entering a crack, the long shudder that followed a change in pressure.

I would not know, at first, which tremor meant what. After one report the cup might drain; after another, it might fill. Sometimes the nearest branch would withdraw before I felt anything at all. I would learn by remaining where I was, through enough brightenings and drainings for certain disturbances to acquire a shape.

When the cup emptied, I would unfold and creep down its inner wall. I would be slow. A film of water would suffice for a while, but the bare lip would be dangerous. Perhaps the first time I tried to cross it, part of me would dry there: a translucent edge stuck to stone. I would pull free, leaving that edge behind, and descend by the wetter side of the column.

At the base I would find the larger inhabitant’s fringes working in the current. They would fold at my touch and open again. I could shelter among them, pressing my grain-filled body into a crack while the bright water passed overhead. When grit collected, I might help roll it clear. There would be food in the fringes too, though I would have to leave the heavier pieces to the swimmers.

Over time, the fringes might begin to close when I contracted around a familiar vibration. Or perhaps they would already have felt it, more keenly than I had. I would never be entirely sure whether I was warning them or merely closing alongside them. Still, I would feel the motion pass from one edge to the next, and sometimes I would loosen my grip in response to theirs.

I would be small enough that a surge could carry me away from that column. If it did, I might settle in another hollow, or catch in one of the grooves beneath the black doorway. The current divides there; I could feel it pull at both sides of me while the frame remained dry above. From that place I might sense the old column only faintly, through the stone.

If I found a new fringe, I would press against it. If I found none, I would wait in the groove, listening with my collected grains, until the water rose high enough to take me somewhere else.
