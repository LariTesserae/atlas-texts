I would be just below the surface of a low shoulder, where a duct carries hot air away from a switch.

At first I think I would be the duct: a narrow passage with leaves for walls, widening where the heat is greatest. But the leaves are replaced often, and the air comes from machinery deeper down. Neither seems quite to belong to me.

I would be a band of fiber wrapped around the switch housing. When the duct heats, I tighten. When it cools, I loosen, and the switch settles back under its own weight.

The movement is small. I have never seen the switch from outside, but I know it by resistance: the initial give, then the stubborn point, then the brief ease as it passes over. Sometimes grit catches in the joint and I must pull for a long while. Fibers added along my length take up the load, and I become thicker without becoming much stronger, because the joint is wearing too.

The switch directs oil between two vessels. I know this only through what follows. In one position, a distant drum turns more easily; its vibration reaches me through the housing, slow and even. In the other, the vibration becomes irregular, and somewhere above me a set of combs begins to close. I have no word for drum or comb. I have the repeated difference between them.

I could not point to where I end. One end of me is fastened around the housing; the other divides into fibers that run through the duct wall and into a cable beneath it. That cable serves other things. When a stand on the far side of the shoulder begins to lean, its pull reaches me through the shared fibers. For a while I tighten whenever it moves, whether the duct is hot or not. The switch starts to pass over too soon. More growth packs itself between me and the cable, and the unwanted pull diminishes.

If that packing failed, I might gradually become part of the leaning stand instead. The switch would still need a band, and fibers closer to the duct would thicken into one. I cannot tell whether that band would be me. It would feel the same housing, perhaps, and the same difficult point in the switch’s travel. It would not have my layers: the fibers that hardened during the long season of grit in the joint, or the twisted inner strands left from the stand’s pull.

A seed structure forms at the vent above. I feel its weight only faintly. It grows joint by joint, held in the rising air, and some of its grains fall back into the duct. A few lodge against the switch housing. Most are ground away when I tighten. One catches farther back, in a seam I cannot reach, and sends a pale fiber down beside me.

For a time it takes oil from a vessel I use. My outer fibers dry and stiffen. Then it finds another vessel, or the first one widens; I can no longer tell. The new growth presses against me as it thickens. Eventually it braces the housing on the side where the metal has begun to split.

The switch passes its stubborn point more cleanly after that. I loosen when the duct cools, tighten when it heats, and feel the altered resistance each time. Deep below, the drum continues turning.
