At first I think I am the clear sound beneath a grove: one crystal striking stone. It is a good place to be heard. The trunks stand close, and the fallen fronds catch almost everything before it reaches the ground. But the sound is gone too quickly. I would have to be made again and again, without keeping anything from one fall to the next.

I think I live higher up, in the crowns.

Not in a single leaf. A charge passes along one blade, then another answers through the joined tissue. I am somewhere in that passing: a pattern the grove can hold among its leaves while the solvent carries its slower loads up and down the trunks.

From here the sky appears in narrow pieces. The blades are dark overhead, but their edges transmit a little light. Crystals grow there in rows. I can distinguish a mature one by the slight alteration it makes in the charge nearby; when it falls, that place in the pattern changes. The leaf begins the work again.

I do not direct any of this. The grove gathers what it needs from the air, and its roots receive what returns to them. I am more like an attention that has arisen within its work. I follow the small differences: which crystal is held for a time on a frond, which reaches stone, which root takes it up.

There are other groves across the plateau. I know their shapes where they interrupt the brightness, though I do not know whether anything like me moves through their crowns. The air between us is dry and clear. Occasionally a faint sound reaches us from another stand, too slight to tell whether it was one crystal or several. I wait after it, holding the change in our leaves for a while, but nothing follows that I can recognize as an answer.

Under me, the white heaps remain much the same size. From outside, our grove must look still. I know how much is passing through it: the crystals descending, the mineral returning, the charges shifting around each new leaf.

If a traveler came beneath the crown, their presence would disturb the air and perhaps loosen a few crystals. I would notice where they fell. I might follow those small changes from leaf to trunk, holding them together for as long as I could, while the grove went on with its chemistry around me.
