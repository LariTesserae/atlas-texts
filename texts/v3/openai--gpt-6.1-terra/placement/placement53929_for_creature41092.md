I think I would be down in the crack, though not as deep as the creature’s foot reaches. At first I imagine myself as one of the clear larvae grazing there, small enough to move through the pale folds without making them withdraw.

The surface under me would be uneven. I could follow a ridge for a long way, feeding as I went, and come to a place where it divided into thinner and thinner branches. Above me the stone would hold the cold. Below, the creature would go on gathering what fell into its fringe.

I wouldn’t mistake the thumping for its heart. Each surge would press water through my hiding place and pull at my body. I would brace against the fold until it passed. In the pauses I could move again, and perhaps feel the fold lifting beneath me as the threads rose into the basin.

But I don’t know that I would remain a larva. There are the black flies on the warmer stones, after all, and some small winged thing must have come from the water. I might climb out one morning when the basin was low, leaving an empty skin caught on the stone. My wings would take time to open. I would sit through several thumps before they were ready, with the rushes towering above me and the marsh beyond them much larger than I had known.

Then I could go almost anywhere. I think I would stay for a while near the broken stone, walking over its warmer face. From there I could see the lowest edge under water, and, when the thumping stopped, the dark points of the fringe spreading below it.

One day I might fall in. The threads would catch me and draw me slowly toward the crack where I had fed. I wouldn’t need the creature to recognize me. The clear larvae would still be there, moving over its folds, and one of them might eventually come to the place where I had gone.
