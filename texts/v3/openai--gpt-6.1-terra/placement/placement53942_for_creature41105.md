I would be a growth of the level skin, at the foot of a young ridge. Above me the tall ones stand close enough for their sides to touch. Below me, my roots enter the mud at different depths, though none reaches as far as the roots of the tall ones.

I know the ridge by the weight it puts on me. Its growths draw water through my channels, and where two of their trunks press together, a little more condensation runs down between them and enters my surface. I take it in before it can collect.

One of my roots lies around the base of a narrow plant. Its leaf sends back sap altered by its work. I pass some of it toward a bud farther out on the flat, where there is room to rise. The bud is still pale and closed; it draws steadily. At first I supply most of what it needs. Then a root of its own enters the mud, and the pull on my channel lessens.

I continue to feel it there. Our roots remain joined, and from time to time it takes water from me or sends something back.

Deeper down, one of my oldest channels has begun to narrow. The mineral seam beside it is yielding less than it did. I send a new tip through the mud, around a broad root belonging to another growth of my kind. The mud is warm on every side. The tip advances where the differences in the ground are strongest, divides, and advances again.

For a long while the new channel carries little. Then it meets a richer seam, and the flow through it increases. Above, a small pool changes its outline as I thicken one part of its rim and let another part be taken apart. The change is too gradual to disturb the water. A pale edge of moisture remains on the root where the pool used to reach.

The tall growth beside me draws through the new channel too. Its need varies slightly as its folded walls take up sap. I feel those variations in the water entering my pores. Another root presses against mine in the mud, and for a while we both widen around the same source. When it begins to diminish, its root turns one way and mine another.

I keep a place firm enough for the bud to open. Its first leaves separate slowly, their pale seams appearing between black, wet surfaces. Water gathers in the center. Some enters the young growth; some passes down to me.

Much later, when the channel I first sent toward it has grown thin and stopped conducting, I take the channel apart. Its material returns to my roots. The young growth draws from the mud and from a neighbor now. Its leaves still touch mine at the surface, yielding when the water presses them together, then moving back.
