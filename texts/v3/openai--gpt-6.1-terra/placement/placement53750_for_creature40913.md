I would begin beneath an overhanging roof, in the last house before the paths loosen into the marsh. At first I might seem to be only the pale lining of a crack in one of its beams. Water gathers there each morning and reaches me before it falls.

But I would also be in the basin below, where the light reaches through the doorway. There I am bronze at the surface, pale underneath, and folded over a shallow ridge of silt. A thread runs from the basin beneath three paving stones to a low tree. The thread is so fine that a dry week could break it; in the damp it has endured for several seasons.

I know the house by its rates of wetting. The western wall becomes cool first when rain approaches, though rain sometimes fails to arrive. The forked pole beside it releases water slowly, long after the stone has stopped dripping. Under the threshold is a hollow that stays damp through nearly everything. When the basin falls low, I draw back toward that hollow and leave a thin skin on the clay.

I would not know the whole of myself at once. Something enters the water beneath the tree, and much later my margin in the basin thickens. A seep opens in the far wall, and tissue there begins to take what it can from it. Between those places are lengths that have little to give or receive, though they keep the others joined.

Another inhabitant meets me in the channel beyond the house. Our seam passes a pebble, follows the edge of a laid stone and bends around a reed root. Once, after a long rain, I grew past the pebble and held that place for part of a season. Then the water changed course, and the seam moved back. The pebble remains at the boundary. I have come to it from both sides of the year.

Farther out, where the path lies beneath a sheet of water, there is a patch of pale growth I have not yet reached. It may be mine already, connected through a crack too deep for the surface to show. It may be another. I am extending a narrow film along the sheltered edge of the path; the water there carries little, but it carries enough. If the film crosses the next joint between stones, it will enter a basin I cannot yet feel.

Meanwhile, beneath the roof, the old beam is settling. A split has opened where it rests on the pole, and drops now fall closer to the wall. My lining follows their new course. Below, the basin receives less water than it did. I am binding sediment at its outlet, building a small ridge that will hold what remains for longer.

The ridge is not finished. Each rain may add to it or wash part of it away. The path may dry before I reach the basin beyond it. Still, under the eaves, on the wall, in the channel and under the tree, I continue wherever the water allows.
