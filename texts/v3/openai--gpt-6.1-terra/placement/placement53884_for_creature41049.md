I would be one of the ferns at the far wall, though at first I might mistake the whole bank for myself.

My roots would be somewhere in its crowded base, among the soft remains of older fronds. Water would arrive behind me in thin threads, passing through brick dust and dead stems before reaching my roots. I would have no need to follow it. It would keep coming, and I would grow toward the places where it stayed longest.

Above me, the lamps would make a pale patch on the new fronds. I would open one there, then another, each pushing into the space already occupied by its neighbors. From the passage, the bank would look almost motionless. Up close, a curled tip would be a little farther from the wall each day.

I could not see down the streets, but changes there might eventually reach me. A lamp replaced near the pump house might brighten the passage enough that a frond on my outer edge could use it. A cleared grate might draw water faster from the foot of the wall. When someone came to tend the lamps, a sleeve might press my fronds aside. Some would spring partway back; a broken one would hang among the older growth until it softened.

I would be more particular than the green bank suggests. One of my roots would enter a joint that held water after the others had drained. One frond would have a browned tip from touching a lamp cage. Another would lie under its neighbors and receive so little light that it would yellow early.

At my base, a young fern might take hold in the remains of that frond. Its first leaf would be small enough to disappear beneath mine. Later, it would press against me, and from the passage there would be no clear way to tell where I ended and it began.
