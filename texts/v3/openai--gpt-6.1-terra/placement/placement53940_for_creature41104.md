I would be in the shallow channel beside a wall that is coming apart.

The seep enters at the wall’s base, warmer than the water in the street. It loosens pale grains, and I take some of them in as I pass. Beneath the grains there is a strip of dark film that was covered for a long time. I fold around the edge of the wall and hold there, feeding, while the seep presses through me.

At first I would have said that this is where I live. But the wall is already thinner than it was. Water reaches a little farther through it each time, and I can feel the place on the other side: a cooler channel running across the seep, too shallow to enter yet. I wait there often. When I leave, I carry the warmth in my folds for a short distance.

A small one has begun to follow me. It came out of a ridge in the square, though I did not know it then. It caught behind me in the loose sediment, and when I moved it moved as well. At first the current could carry it past me. Now it can hold to the paving, and sometimes it reaches the wall before I do.

We lie there together, spread thin. The small one touches the places where I have been feeding. It is still cloudy through most of its body, and grains collect against its edge. When the seep strengthens, I feel it lift and settle again. I move closer, not to hold it down, but because the water passing over both of us makes a broader quiet place behind us. There it can gather itself.

The passage through the wall opens gradually. One night the cool water reaches my farthest edge. I extend into it, finding a joint in the paving, then another. The small one comes after me. On this side the wall has a different slope and a heavier growth of film, though the water tastes familiar.

I follow the base until it brings me beneath a fan of stems. The fan has folded inward, leaving a wet hollow against the wall. I enter and rest there while salt settles on me. The small one stays outside, where I can feel its edge in the current. By the time the stems open, it has gone.

I find it again—or find a small cloudy one—near a pool farther down the channel. The paving beneath the pool gives off a little grey light. I pass over it, and the light remains after me, showing a drowned stair under the water. The small one gathers at the first step. I spread along the second, and there is another below it, and another, until the warmth of the stair is too faint to follow.

We return to the street. The wall is still there. The opening through its base is wider now, and water from the cooler channel has begun to carry pale sediment toward the square. I turn against it and settle at the edge of the seep. The small one remains behind me for a while. Then it moves along the wall in the other direction, stopping wherever the water presses through.
