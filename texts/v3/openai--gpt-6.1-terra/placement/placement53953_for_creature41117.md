I would be under a room, at first.

Not beneath its floor, but beneath the shade of its walls, where the salt stays cool. From the rim I might look like another pale fragment. Only my limbs would give me away: six of them, spread around the space my body occupies, their pads resting lightly on the ground.

A root lies exposed at the edge of the room. It is pale near the wall and darker where it runs into open air. I follow it by placing a pad ahead, lifting my body, then placing another. The root disappears beneath a crust of salt, but I can still find it. The ground above it has a different feel, a faint unevenness that continues into the next street.

At the intersection there is a tuft. I settle over it and take the dust from its blades. They bend beneath me. When I have finished, I stay where I am, sheltered a little by the walls and by the plant itself. The blades rise slowly against my underside.

A change reaches me through the pads on one side. The floor is lifting. I raise myself and wait, watching a line travel across the room I have just left. It passes over my tracks and opens. Two walls unfold from it, their edges pale in the light.

The root goes under them. For a while I cannot follow. I stand beside the new wall with my limbs drawn closer to my body, until the passage widens enough to admit me. On the other side, a few blades of the tuft have come through separately. They turn toward the wall, gathering the dark powder at its foot.

There is another of my kind in the passage. I had not seen it while the wall was opening. It stands high on its limbs, leaving me room, and I begin to pass. One of its pads touches mine. We stop like that, then turn toward the open plaza together.

A smaller body waits there, almost transparent against the salt. It moves when the other animal moves. I go slowly enough that it can keep near us, though after a time it finds its own way between our tracks.

We cross a space where a wall has folded away. Beneath the fresh powder, a root is newly exposed, longer than either of the tufts it joins. The smaller animal pauses over it. Farther ahead, the other adult settles beside a plant, and I continue to the edge of the square.

I would not need the town to stay as it was. I would know it by what my pads could reach now: a root under salt, a floor beginning to rise, the touch of another animal waiting in a passage. When the light shifted and the square became a room, I would lift myself and find where the root went next.
