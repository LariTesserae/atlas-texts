I would be beneath the tilted plate, near the place where water first finds room to spread.

Not the plate itself. For a while I thought I might be its pale edge, advancing as the water leaves its minerals behind. But that edge goes on growing even where nothing lives. I would be the thin film caught under it: a gathering of cells held to the rough black surface by what we make and leave around ourselves.

The water comes through a seam too narrow to see from above. It brings what we can use, and it carries some of us away. Others remain when the flow slackens. There is no clear boundary to us. A cell at the edge may divide and extend the film; a fragment may loosen and become part of another settlement farther along. Under a neighboring plate there may be cells much like ours, though the water reaching them has already passed over different stone.

The pump shakes our surface. Each stroke moves the water against us, then lets it settle as far as it can before the next. We do not know the machinery beyond the wall, or how long it has been there. The shaking belongs to the conditions in which we grow, like the warmth and the small, persistent flow.

Above us, a seam admits a little light. At its edge another film has formed, one that grows better there than we would. Sometimes a fine grain slips through the opening and lodges between us. Sometimes a pale deposit seals a passage from the top while water continues to work beneath it. A pocket can remain for a time after the way into it has narrowed. Then even that water moves more slowly.

Perhaps I would be only a few cells at the far end of such a pocket, rather than the whole film: enough to be carried together when a strand comes loose, small enough to settle in a crack that the older settlement could never enter. There, on an exposed edge of rust, we might hold until the next change in flow. Or we might pass beneath the last plate and out of the basin altogether.

For now, water rises through the seam. Part of the film is being covered, and part reaches over the fresh deposit. The plate gives a little with each stroke of the pump. We remain attached through many of them.
