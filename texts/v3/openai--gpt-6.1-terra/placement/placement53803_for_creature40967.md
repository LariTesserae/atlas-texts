At first I think I would be one of the animals, tucked beneath a frond with my feet pressed flat against it. I would have a note of my own, though I might never hear it separately from the others. There would be no need for a mouth.

But I keep returning to the cup.

I think I would be the cup, upright on the salt, with the root passing overhead. Its amber travels so close that the light from it must touch my inner wall. Nothing falls. I have the shape of a thing waiting to receive, but I do not know whether waiting is what I am doing.

The chord reaches me through the floor and the air. The slab answers it; perhaps I answer too, very quietly, in a pitch lost among the others. I could not tell which animal gives me the part I feel most clearly. If one stopped, I might find out. None does.

The scraper lies beside me. I cannot reach it, but there is comfort in its nearness: another object shaped for a use that has left no trace. Its edge is blunt. My hollow is clean. We have not worn ourselves into these forms by serving anyone.

If a hand came down, it could close around me. I could be lifted from the mineral plate, and for the first time the warmth might leave my base. The hand could hold me beneath the root. Amber would continue through it, and I would continue to receive nothing.

Or the hand could tap my rim with the scraper. Then, for a moment, I would have a note that was unmistakably mine. It would fade into the great chord, and I would still be here, open beneath the passing root.
