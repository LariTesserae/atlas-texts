At first I think I would be a lizard at the foot of a tower, with the whole plain available to my eyes and only a narrow strip of shade available to my body. I would wait there until movement in the dust became something I could eat.

Or perhaps a beetle on a stem, climbing into the wind and holding on while the whole plant bowed. From there the towers would look less like a city than like a second kind of growth: clay rising among the shrubs, gathering dust at its feet.

But I keep returning to a small darkening on one wall, a place where fresh earth has been pressed over a break. I think I would be one of the termites behind it.

The passage would be close enough that my body could touch its sides. I would know the plain mostly through what entered it: the dry edge of a leaf, a fragment of stem, the bodies of other workers passing me in the narrow way. The air beyond a break would feel different. I might reach it with others and add my pellet to the edge, then turn back while there was still a gap. Another worker would come forward. The opening would grow smaller from several sides at once.

Outside, a bird might scratch at the tower’s base. A lizard might dart through its shade. I would not need to know their shapes to live with the consequences: a wall disturbed, a passage exposed, soldiers gathering where the light came through.

I would spend time in chambers that never dried like the outer clay, passing food to nestmates and tending the young. Then I would go out through a covered route beneath a shrub, returning with a piece of dead vegetation that had lain there long enough to be caught by the roots and half buried in dust.

The tower above me would be older than I was. I would work against walls built by others, following passages that bent around chambers I had not made. At the surface, wind would lay a little more dust against the base. The shrub beside it would send a shoot through the dust. We would keep carrying fragments in and earth outward, each by a small amount.

By evening the patch on the wall would have paled as it dried. I would be elsewhere inside, among passing bodies, while the sealed place held for another night.
