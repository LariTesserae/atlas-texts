I think I would be one of the pale animals, though not the one in the broad pool. I would be farther up the slope, in a seam that holds water only along its deepest edge.

At first I would be difficult to distinguish from the white crust. My body would rest in the wet line, and my longest limbs would reach out across dry stone. One of them, grown back after a break, would have an extra joint. It would not fold as neatly as the others, but it could reach a little farther around the lip of the seam.

The hum would come to me through my pads. I don’t think it would be a single sound from where I lay. Each place I touched would give me something slightly different: a heavy pulse beneath my center, a finer trembling under the limb on the slope, almost nothing under the one extended toward the overhang. I would lift that limb and put it down again, finding the edge of the quiet.

Water would gather slowly above me. When a bead fell, I might remain still while it spread along the seam and touched my skin. The dark branches in my limbs would show through. Then, as the moisture reached a film farther down the stone, I would extend a brush and draw a little of it toward my mouth.

I would know the seam by its intervals: where a limb could grip, where water lingered, where the rock’s movement changed. For a long time that might be the whole extent of my world.

Then the vibration beneath one pad would change. I would hold the others still and test it again. Somewhere beyond the lip, water was pressing through a passage that had been quiet before.

Leaving would take me a long time. I would cross a dry patch, stop in the shade of a stone, and wait there until mist reached me. At the new seam I might find another creature already feeding. Its limbs would be arranged differently from mine; one brush would be very short. I would settle beside it, and eventually one of my pads would touch its limb.

We might remain like that through many droplets, while the water spread beneath us and the rock carried its deep, continuous hum.
