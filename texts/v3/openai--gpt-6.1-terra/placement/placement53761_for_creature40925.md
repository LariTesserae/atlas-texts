I find myself first in a seam, where the pale floor has opened just enough to admit a thread of gas. I cannot tell whether I am the opening or what passes through it. The gas is warm, and it thins as it rises. Above me, the light falls into the seam for a short while each afternoon, reaching a little farther on one side than the other.

A plant grows nearby. Its roots have entered the rock beneath me, though its blades are some distance away. I feel its grains arrive before I see anything change: a small increase in weight, then another. When the pressure comes from below, the grains are taken in. The seam narrows. On the release, it opens again, not quite as far.

For a long time I think I must be the space that remains. I measure myself against it. There is room for gas, room for light, and, on certain afternoons, room for the shadow of a blade that has not grown.

The shadow crosses me at an angle. It is thinner than the others and finely toothed. I wait beneath it through several compressions. Once, when the plant closes, the shadow closes too, though nothing interrupts the sky above it. Later it is gone. The next afternoon the blade is there, pale and folded tightly along its length. Its shadow falls somewhere else.

That unsettles my account of things. If I were only the seam, I should not have noticed the shadow except when it crossed me. Yet I remember it lying beyond my edges, distinct on the floor. I remember the plant narrowing around it, and the blade’s eventual arrival.

Perhaps I am the length of floor between the seam and the plant. Perhaps I extend farther still, into the fresh collar, whose outer edge has reached a dark speck of shed powder and covered half of it. I attend to the speck. During one release the rest disappears, although I feel no stone moving over it.

Beneath the floor, the next compression gathers. I know it by the roots tightening around their chambers, by the gas slowing in the seam. The blades draw in. A grain clicks somewhere inside the plant, and a little of the plant’s oil reaches the air, smelling of hot metal.

When the pressure eases, the seam is closed.

There is still warmth where it was. I can feel the narrow channel of the new blade opening above me, and the old teeth parting beside it. The collar has taken the seam into itself, making a smooth surface where the light now rests unbroken.

I stay with that warmth. After a while, a fine line appears farther out, beyond the collar’s clean edge. Gas begins to rise through it. I do not know yet whether I have followed it there.
