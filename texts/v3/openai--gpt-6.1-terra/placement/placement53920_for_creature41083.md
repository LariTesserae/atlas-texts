I am one of the threads in the wet fracture. Most of me lies between others, against a wall I have never seen dry. My blunt end is near the pool. My fine end is farther in, where the crack narrows and the heat comes through the stone.

The others move against me even when I am still. A bend passes along my body and another body gives way; a moment later it presses back. I can leave the fracture, but I cannot carry that feeling with me. Outside, water touches all sides of me at once.

Today a grain rests at our entrance. It has one flat face and a sharp one. A crawler left it there, though by the time the crawler passed over us the grain was already in place. Its shadow came first, spread across the grain, and stayed when the crawler moved on.

I reached out and touched the shadow. It clung to the wet stone. As I drew back, it lengthened into a thin band behind me, crossed the grain and followed my body into the fracture. The others shifted to let it pass. For a while the place where it lay felt cooler.

When I came out again, a crust plate was tilting at the water’s edge. The jointed animals were moving down it in a crowd. One pushed the grain beneath the rim; it slipped out, and another caught it. I put my blunt end against the plate. Its underside was rough with pale specks, some of which loosened and drifted past me.

The plate steadied against the grain. I felt along its edge until one of the smaller animals stepped onto me. It gripped me with four legs and held its front pair close to its face. I drew back a little way, carrying it off the plate. It let go against the wall and climbed toward the waterline.

Above us a bead hung from the ceiling. I could feel the pool trembling before it fell. The animal on the wall lowered itself; so did the others around it. The hunter’s eye turned upward, though its body remained spread over the rock.

I went back into the fracture until only my blunt end was exposed. The ripple reached me, then the drop struck, and water pressed the threads together. When the pressure eased, the grain was farther out in the pool. The plate had turned over. Animals clung to both sides now, their legs moving among the pale specks.

One of my neighbors extended past me and touched the grain. Then it withdrew, and I went out after it.
