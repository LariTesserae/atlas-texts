At first I think I would be a drop hanging beneath the bowl’s chipped lip. From there I could see the entrance, perhaps: a narrow piece of light wavering on the water. But I could not stay. I would grow heavy, fall, and be taken through the lowest seam.

So I would be farther inside, where the groove feeds the bowl: the small, slow turn the incoming water makes there. Not a whirlpool. Just a circulation, difficult to see unless a grain of silt is caught in it and goes around once before settling.

The water making me would be different from moment to moment. After a long rain I might become stronger, then slacken as the seep diminished. A change in the groove—a new crust of mineral, a strand fallen across it—could send the water in another direction. I would have no way to keep my shape except to keep being made.

The creature’s membrane would lie beneath me. I would pass over it without knowing all its marks. Sometimes a grain carried in my turn would come to rest there, making a pale point visible in the dimness. If the grain were lifted again, the place would be clear, though the membrane might still hold the slight deformation for a while.

I would touch the creature most closely where it extends down the lip of the bowl. Some of its finer strands might bend in my flow. I would not know whether the bending helped it feed or simply tired that part of it. Now and then a strand would come loose and drift with me, going around more slowly than the silt before finding another wet surface to settle on.

The sea would reach me only faintly. It would enter by the seam as a change in the water, a pressure passed through the pools. The creature would draw tighter against the stone. My turn might alter a little, enough to move a grain from one side of the bowl to the other.

If someone lifted the bowl, I would stop at once. Water would run from the chip; strands would stretch and part. There might be a new turn wherever the bowl was set down, if the groove could still reach it. Otherwise the water would find the floor, and I would be gone.

Until then, I would stay near the bottom, turning so slowly that the bowl would appear still.
