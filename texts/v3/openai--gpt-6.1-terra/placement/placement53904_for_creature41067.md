I would be beneath the fallen limb, where it rests on a bed of leaves without quite touching the ground.

At first I imagine being a spider there, waiting in the space between two stems. But the leaves keep shifting, and I find myself following the movement underneath them: a beetle, dark-backed, small enough that the ridge of a leaf is a slope I must climb.

The underside of the limb is damp. I work along it, pushing my head beneath a curled edge, then backing out when the space narrows. Something else moves below me. For a while we press against opposite sides of the same leaf, lifting it a little and letting it settle.

I come upon a soft place in the wood and stay to feed. Above me, ants pass along the exposed surface. An occasional ant comes down the side, feels its way around my back and climbs up again. I do not need to know where they are going.

A hoof lands nearby. The ground moves, and loose grains patter into the hollow under the limb. I draw in my legs and remain still. The animal shifts its weight; a stem breaks somewhere above. When it lifts its foot, the leaf in front of me has been pressed flat against the earth.

I cross it and find a fragment of dead plant matter caught in a fork of wood. It takes some pulling to get it free. I drag it a short way, lose my grip, turn and seize another edge. There is room to work here, and moisture held in the litter, though the exposed surface of the soil has dried.

Later, a drop falls from a leaf and strikes the limb. Water runs down a shallow groove, carrying a grain of red earth with it. I move aside, then return when the flow has stopped.

I would not see much of the herd. A leg might pass between the grasses; the weight of an animal would reach me through the ground. The frog’s hollow would be a considerable distance away, though dampness from the rain remains nearer at hand. The mechanical hum might pass over the basin without entering my world at all.

Toward morning I work deeper under the limb, following a gap between the wood and soil. It ends in a pocket packed with leaf fragments. I turn there, slowly, and find a place against the cool underside of the wood. Outside, another beetle shifts the litter. I feel the movement, then feel it stop.
