At first I am between two stones. The upper one rests on three points, leaving a space beneath it wide enough for me to turn. My antennae reach the edge before the rest of me does. One touches gravel; the other meets another cricket, and we both draw back.

There are many of us under this slab. We keep finding one another in the dark, passing legs and antennae over legs and antennae, then settling until someone moves again. Above us, the bats make a sound like dry leaves pressed together and stirred.

I come out where the ground slopes toward a roost. The gravel is loose, and each step shifts it a little. There is food in the deposit beneath the bats: fragments, soft growth, things carried in and dropped. A beetle moves past me, dark with it, with smaller pale creatures on its back. Farther along, a spider has stretched threads across a notch. I feel one with an antenna and take another way.

The open ground is wider than the distance I can see. I cross it in starts, stopping when the air moves against me or when the sound overhead changes. Once, something shifts among the bats and the movement spreads along the ledge. Wings open; for a moment the air fills with their papery beating. Then it settles.

A pale patch lies ahead, cooler in color than the stone around it. I go around its edge. Near it there is a crack with moss in it, and farther up, a fern. The light will move away from them later. It moves across the gravel even when the rest of the chamber seems still.

Toward evening the sounds overhead change again. More wings open, and bats drop into the air and fly toward the clefts. Their passage continues for a long time. I stay beneath a tilted stone until the strongest rush has passed, then come out among the fragments they have left.

There is damp clay in a basin nearby. I cross it and leave marks that hold their shape. At the far side, water has gathered in a hollow no larger than my body. A drop strikes it while I am there, sending a tremor across the surface. Something pale moves over a pebble below. I drink at the edge, then retreat as a salamander emerges from the wet crack beyond.

By the time the bats return, I have found another slab. There are crickets beneath this one too, their antennae reaching into the space I enter. I fit myself among them. From here I can hear the bats finding their places overhead, the small clear notes of drops, and the gravel shifting as the others come in after me.
