I think I would be found beneath a pool that has only just emptied.

At first there is light on me, though I have no use for it. The wall has fallen away, and water is running off through the floor. Above, the colorless plants lie folded over one another, their clear stalks bent without breaking. Their roots are still in the felt. So am I.

I am a fungus growing through the remains of a tower stem. The stem came down some time ago and was covered by leaves. Its hollow filled with water and fragments of green tissue. I entered through a split end and have been branching into it ever since, loosening the fibers as I go. Bacteria coat the wet inner surface; pale roots have come down through the opening and hang among us.

Before the basin drained, the tower stem was a chamber of nearly still water. Now air enters it. The exposed leaves overhead warm, and a thin vapor rises through the places where my strands cross the floor.

There is a sound in the fallen stem when water runs through it. While the pool was full, the sound came only in faint knocks from the tower still standing farther upslope. Now a little stream has found the split end, and the stem gives a hollow rattle. The flow carries some of the softened material out onto the floor, where the small plants’ roots can take it up.

My oldest strands reach beyond the stem, into leaves pressed dark and close together. Some have gone no farther than a leaf’s edge. Others have followed a vein until it ended at a joint, then crossed into the next layer. I could not trace all those paths from where the pool has opened. Parts of me are separated by woodier tissue I have not yet entered, or by pockets of water too deep for the strands to cross.

A white root tip comes through the wall of the chamber. It bends along the wet surface, grows between two of my branches, and divides. For a while we occupy the same narrow space. The root takes up water; I continue into the softened stem beside it. As the chamber drains, the water level falls below the younger branch of the root. It stops lengthening there, while the older branch reaches farther down.

When the next rain comes, the floor leaves close above us. Water arrives along their channels and spills into the basin. The colorless plants lift a little. One clear stalk rises against the opening of the fallen stem, and its leaf trembles in the current. The root inside is submerged again. The hollow rattle deepens, slows, and stops.

The standing tower sounds through the shower. Its note changes as its chambers fill, then disappears. Water continues to move through the old leaves around me, carrying fine particles into gaps and washing others away. By the time the rain passes, the pool has returned.

I will remain here as long as the fallen stem gives me places to enter. Already its split end is soft enough that a new shoot has pushed through it from below. The shoot presses the walls apart as it thickens. Some of my strands tear; the rest keep growing into the damp fibers on either side. Above us, its small crown opens on the surface of the water.
