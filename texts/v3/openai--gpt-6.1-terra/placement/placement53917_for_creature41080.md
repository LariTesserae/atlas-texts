At first I would look for a place to stand. From the rim the basin seems to offer miles of ground; close up, every foothold belongs to water. A boot set down among the stones would divert a dozen streams and uncover things too small to see.

I would have to come down much farther.

There is a flake of shale leaning against a limestone knob. Water presses through the gap between them, but a shallow hollow on the downstream side is sheltered. A skin of living film coats the stone there. On the rim of the hollow the current is strong enough to carry away a loose grain; inside it, a grain might stay for days.

I am one of the nearly transparent crustaceans grazing there. My body lies close to the stone, and my limbs keep finding purchase on its uneven surface. I move forward a little, hold, and work my mouthparts over the film. Farther along, the surface is cleaner. I turn back toward a fold where more has gathered.

The other animals are not all visible at once. One is tucked into the narrow end of the gap; another is on the underside of the shale. A smaller one moves across a patch I have just grazed. We share the hollow, though each of us occupies only a little of it. The places between us are filled with water and with the thin growth on the stone.

The basin’s roar reaches me as vibration through everything I touch. The shale trembles against my limbs; water presses and pulls along my back. I could not move away from the sound. I know the hollow instead by differences within it: the firmer hold near the limestone, the stronger pull at the lip, the place where fine particles collect in the film.

One day the shale settles more closely against the knob. The hollow remains, but the passage through it narrows. Water runs faster along one edge, stripping a patch of film and carrying it away. I keep my hold. The smaller animal is gone from the place where I last grazed, though it may be farther under the stone.

I follow the remaining film toward the deep end of the gap. There is less room here, but the current is gentler. I brace my limbs and begin to feed again, with the whole white basin sounding through the stone above me.
