I would be beneath the last step, in the clay where its upper stone meets the blank face. There is a pool above me. I know it first as weight.

When the pool gathers, the clay yields a little. Water comes down through a seam so narrow that the stone on either side seems to meet. I take it in along the fold nearest the seam. That fold loosens against the cold surface, then presses outward. Much later the pressure reaches another living skin, farther into the wall. It gives way, and the space I occupy becomes a little larger.

This happens many times. The pool is not always there. Between fillings, my outermost fold tightens and pales. The part farther in remains damp. If the dry interval lasts, I become smaller without moving away: a film along one stone, a thicker line along the other, a portion held in the clay where the air cannot reach.

At first I am simply this joint. Then water finds a second way down.

It enters through one of the sockets above the step, travels behind the face of a block, and comes to me carrying a trace of something the first water lacks. I open toward it. The fold thickens, divides around a grain of grit, and extends into a seam that stays wet after the step has dried. The new seam is longer than the old one. Its far end lies beside another body, which withdraws as I swell and fills the space again when I diminish.

We have no need to distinguish each arrival. Still, the two routes leave different pressures behind them. Water from the step comes broadly, weighting the stone above me. Water from the socket arrives along the upright face and reaches my farthest fold first. In time, more of me lies along that face. The part under the step grows thin.

There is a period when neither route brings water. The neighboring body contracts. The clay stiffens around us both. A fold of mine is held fast beneath a crust where dissolved stone has gathered; the rest draws back from it, and at last the connecting neck closes.

I cannot tell when the trapped part ceases to be part of me. I cannot feel it for a long time.

Water returns to the socket first. It reaches down the upright seam, and I begin there again, admitting it slowly. The neighbor opens too. Our skins meet across a thin layer of clay, each changing the space available to the other.

Later the pool forms on the step. Its weight comes through the stone, faint at first, then sufficient to loosen the old clay. The trapped fold fills beneath its crust. It has a small chamber of its own now. As it swells, I feel it through the stone beside me, not where it once joined me but a little to one side.

The pool remains for days. We fill what space there is. When it begins to recede, the fold under the crust stays wet longer than I do, and its pressure is the last I feel from that direction.

Above us the unfinished steps stop at the wall. Water rests on the top one whenever it can. Beneath it, I have become a narrow body along the face, with an older, thinner reach under the tread. I continue to open at both places, though not equally. The socket is holding water now, and most of me is gathered beneath it.
