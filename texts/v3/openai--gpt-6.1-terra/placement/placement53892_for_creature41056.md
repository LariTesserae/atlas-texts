At first I might take the grinding for the center of the world. It comes through the iron before the loose salt begins to fall, before the beetles on the wheel lose their footing. There are quieter sounds under it: the rasp of a body squeezing past mine, the click of a hunter placing one long foot among us.

I live behind the pipe, where a flake of rust has lifted from the metal. I am a beetle, gray beneath the dust. There is room under the flake to turn if I go in headfirst, though often another beetle is already there and I have to back out.

When the bearing has been still for a long time, I climb toward the light. The pipe is rough enough to hold to, and warm where the brightness reaches it. Others climb with me. Sometimes one of them gets a foothold on my back and passes over my head; sometimes I do the same. We make slow progress, stopping whenever the bodies ahead stop.

From the upper joint I can reach the edge of the slit. The air there moves against the dust on my wing cases. Once I opened my wings and rose into it, buzzing, but struck the stone almost at once. I fell onto the backs below and was carried a little way before I found the floor.

Food arrives without warning. A dry insect comes through the opening and strikes the salt, and the beetles nearest it begin to pull at its underside. I follow the movement toward it. By the time I reach the body, the joints are crowded. I wait, pushing my head into a gap whenever one opens, until I can work my way beneath a leg.

A hunter steps over us. I feel its foot on my wing case, then the pressure is gone. Nearby, another beetle is held against the floor. The bodies around it climb and turn; one backs over me. I pull my head in and stay still until there is space to move again.

Later I return behind the pipe. Pale larvae are working inside a fragment lodged there, and beneath it are eggs pressed into a crack. The iron is warmer than the stone. I fit myself under the lifted rust beside another beetle, leaving my hind legs out.

The shaft turns. The flake rattles against my back, and we both scramble out as grains spill from above. When the movement stops, the other beetle goes down toward the floor. I remain by the pipe, brushing a grain from one leg with the other. After a while I go back under the rust. There is room enough now to pull all my legs inside.
