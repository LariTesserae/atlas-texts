I would be in the mortar beneath the pump, where the white ring disappears under the brick. A filament among many, too fine to see on its own.

When the mortar dries, I would contract with the rest of the skin. Salt would remain around us, and the brick would go on giving off its stored warmth. I don’t think I would know how long we had waited.

A drop landing near the wall would reach me by degrees, darkening the deposit above and filling the small spaces between grains. I would swell. My neighbors would swell too, and together we would lift a ridge of mineral, though none of us would have any use for its shape. The warmth would draw the water away again. The ridge would settle.

There would be more of us along the buried pipe, where dampness lasts longer. Perhaps some part of that film would continue into a seam beneath the fallen stone. I could not tell where my growth ended and another’s began without breaking us apart. Near the surface, a plant runner has passed under the chip; when rain comes, its fine roots may find the same water. They will reach places we cannot, and we will remain in places too narrow for them.

If the pipe shifts one day, the mortar may crack and admit water farther in. Or the masonry may grow too dry, and the living ring retreat until only a little remains in the deepest joint. For now I would stay there, enlarging when the wall is wet, holding together when it isn’t, under a pump that no longer runs.
