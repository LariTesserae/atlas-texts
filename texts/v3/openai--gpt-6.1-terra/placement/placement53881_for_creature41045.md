I would look first toward the sunbeam. It is the easiest thing to follow: down the fissure, across the floor, up the pale broken wall. But it does not stay, and the place I would belong to is one that remains after the brightness has gone.

Perhaps I would be under the loose chip with the springtail, where the grit is damp and a few grains can be moved aside. From there the chamber would be a set of limits: the stone overhead, the drying edge of the shelter, the heat that stops a journey before it has properly begun. The springtail has a body that can turn back. I think I would be something less mobile.

I would be a thin film in a seam near the meeting of the waters—not at the steaming pool itself, but a little way up the cooler wall, where a trickle sometimes reaches the warm stone. The crack would be narrow enough to hold moisture after the exposed face had dried. Some days water would pass through it; on others there would be only a dampness held close against the mineral surface.

I would not see the blade of light or hear the drop. Those belong to a description of the chamber from somewhere larger than my place in it. What changed for me would be warmth, water, and the substances carried into the crack. A fresh seep might bring one set of conditions; as it warmed and thinned, another. If the seam dried too far, parts of the film would die. Other cells, farther in, might endure.

There would be no single creature in the film to call *me*, no little watcher spread across the rock. I would be the life held there together for a while: cells dividing where they could, their remains becoming part of the fine material in the seam, the margin advancing or receding by amounts too small to notice from the floor.

The springtail might pass nearby without entering my crack. A drop might fall into the pool and send a slight disturbance along its edge; later, some of that water might reach me by a route neither the drop nor I could trace. Most of the chamber would remain beyond me. Even the pool, so close in a room this small, would be somewhere else.

When the sun withdrew, the wall would cool slowly. Water would continue to gather and disappear. In the sheltered seam, I would persist where I could, changing with what arrived.
