Each builder is small enough that its neighbors press against it on every side. Its body is a clear, yielding sac, with a mouth ringed by fine tentacles. These extend into the passing water, gather what catches, and withdraw. At intervals the whole animal retreats into its sleeve. The opening closes over it so neatly that, for a moment, it seems to have disappeared into the wall.

The sleeves are not quite alike. Some are flared at the mouth; some bend around an older neighbor or grow long and narrow where space is scarce. Their edges meet, and mineral spreads across the joins. What begins as a shelter for one animal becomes a roof over several, then the floor of a chamber inhabited by others. The builders remain visible inside it: faint sacs and branching threads, repeated so closely that the wall looks cloudy.

A young animal arriving in the hollow has little choice of ground. It drifts until a current carries it against a surface where it can hold. There it fastens and begins to make its sleeve. If it settles near a crowded passage, the water may bring it plenty to eat, though its opening will be jostled by those of its neighbors. If it settles in a quiet recess, the sleeve may grow undisturbed while food passes out of reach.

The grazers know the walls by contact. They move on broad, pale undersides, rasping at the film that gathers wherever water and surface meet. A grazer may cross a hundred living roofs in its slow passage, pausing at the rim of an opening or passing directly over it when the animal beneath has withdrawn. Behind it, the cleaned strip is briefly brighter than the surrounding mineral. Particles settle there; fine growth returns.

The swimmers find routes too small for the grazers. They bend through joints in the architecture and pause in chambers where the current weakens, their nearly transparent bodies difficult to distinguish from the water until they turn. Some feed on the same drifting fragments as the builders. Some take eggs. The hunter under the arch takes whatever comes within reach, holding itself against the wall with its rear legs while its front limbs remain folded among the fringe. After a capture it stays where it is. The current carries away the lighter pieces.

At the seam overhead, the dark filaments occupy a narrow privilege. Their upper portions receive the uncertain light; their lower portions trail among animals that cannot use it. Eggs and particles collect on them. Small grazers climb the stems, and once in a while a filament comes loose and folds down into the city, where it is caught across several mouths and slowly pulled apart.

As the walls thicken, animals are enclosed within work that continues around them. Some retain a path to the water through a tube no wider than their own opening. Others are sealed in. Above them, new mouths extend and close, and the current finds another way through.
