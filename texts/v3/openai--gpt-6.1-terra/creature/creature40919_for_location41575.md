The most numerous inhabitants are small enough to live in the spaces the rock has not yet closed. Midges gather at the edges of pools and in the thin air above them; some have spent their earlier lives in the cold water or soft mud. Spiders wait in cracks and under the lips of stones, catching what the wind brings within reach. Beetles move through the damp grit beneath loose plates, where the temperature changes more slowly than it does in the open.

The plants hold the basin in scattered points of green. Moss makes dense cushions in sheltered seams, soaking up meltwater and drying slowly when the sun comes through. Wiry grasses send roots into pockets of grit. The low flowering plants spread close to the stone, their leaves crowded together, their stems short enough to stay below much of the wind. A cushion may occupy a crack no wider than a hand, with bare rock on either side for several yards.

The birds are the basin’s most conspicuous visitors. They come up from the valleys in flocks and work along the pool margins, picking insects from the mud and water. On a calm day they spread out; when a hawk crosses the ridge, they rise at once and wheel over the basin before settling again. The nests are harder to see, tucked onto ledges where the rock receives some warmth. Adults fly back and forth between these shelves and the water, carrying food to their young.

Small mammals use the rockfalls in much the same way the beetles do, slipping into the sheltered gaps and emerging for brief periods to feed. A movement between two blocks may be the only sign of one. They leave the open ground to the birds and cross it quickly when they must.

Even around the warm seep, life stays close to the water and the stone. Damp surfaces hold films of growth too fine to distinguish at a distance; insects visit the softened edges. At evening, as ice forms around the colder pools, the birds return toward their ledges and the mammals disappear beneath the rocks. Under the new ice and in the unfrozen mud, smaller lives continue through the night.
