The most conspicuous inhabitants are the jointed ones. Their clear backs show pale organs folded so tightly together that it is difficult to tell where one animal ends and the next begins. Some have broad, flattened limbs laid against the mineral; others have legs as fine as the white hairs on the fronds. Their bodies are fitted to the seams, though several are caught in postures that would require more room to complete: an extended leg, a lifted head, a back arched beneath the low ceiling of a nodule.

The soft-bodied creatures occupy smaller spaces. They are nearly the color of the stone, and at first their curved bodies look like additional nodules, pressed and faceted among the rest. Only at the edges can they be distinguished: a translucent fold, a dark internal thread, a narrow mouth resting against a wet surface. One lies with its length wrapped halfway around a bead of water. Another has its head and tail so close together that it makes an imperfect ring.

The six-legged forms are the smallest and most numerous. They stand along the rims of the water in ranks several bodies deep, their feet finding purchase on the fine gloss. Those nearest a bead are reflected in it, each reflection rounded and bright. Farther back, they stand on the backs of the jointed animals and among the sparse leaves of the fronds. Their heads differ slightly in shape: some blunt, some drawn to a point. It is possible they are several kinds of animal, or several ages of the same kind.

The fronds hold a little distance around themselves. Where a leaf extends into a crowded seam, the animals are arranged along its edges and beneath it, leaving most of the thick upper surface bare. A few cling to the white hairs at the tips. On one leaf, a soft-bodied creature rests with its open mouth against the skin; there is no visible mark beneath it.

Beside the pebble wall, the crowd is thinner. The enclosed space is empty except for a sheen of moisture, and the stones lean against one another without mortar. A six-legged animal stands outside it, facing the wall. It may be attending to the space, or it may be positioned there as one of the pebbles is positioned there.

The clay fragment has inhabitants too. Several tiny bodies lie in its shallow marks, following the curves where a finger might once have pressed. They are darker than the animals on the mineral, though whether from the clay or from some difference in their own substance cannot be seen.

All of them remain in their places while the chamber sounds around them. The ringing seems closest in the narrow seams, where their bodies are packed together, but no wing, throat, or limb can be seen to produce it. The fronds are still; the water keeps its roundness. In the bright stone, each raised leg and open mouth remains as distinct as if the animal had only just arrived there.
