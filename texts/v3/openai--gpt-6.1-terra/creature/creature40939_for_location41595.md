The people who live here know the chamber by its pauses. When the pump stops, they can hear whether the belt is running true, whether a valve has closed, whether someone is still working behind a shut door. They know, too, which pipes will be cool enough to touch after a run and which railings stay warm through the night.

They are machinists and pump workers, builders still finishing the place, and people whose work is less easy to see: someone cleans the settling basin, someone keeps track of the water drawn from the tap, someone mends shirts worn thin at the shoulders. They pass close to one another in the service passages. A person carrying a meal may have to wait while a ladder is moved; a conversation at one doorway is easily heard at the next. Privacy depends on small acts of courtesy—a shutter left closed, a glance turned away from a lit window.

The shrub has several caretakers, though none need claim it. One person straightens its wire guard after a length of pipe catches on it. Another lifts a trailing stem clear of the channel before the tank is drained. The machinist who opens the workroom shutter sometimes checks the tin cup beneath the nearest elbow, tipping a little of its water toward a dry patch of liverwort. The plants are useful as signs of where moisture gathers, but people look after them even when there is no work to be learned from them.

The plants make use of what escapes attention. The liverwort holds to the dampest mortar; the jointed stems reach out over a cup and shed pale fragments onto the floor. The shrub follows its crack upward, putting out leaves in a narrow strip of weak light. Its roots are already prying at the new brickwork. Sooner or later someone will have to repair that seam, and decide how much of the root can be left.

When daylight reaches the basin, it may fall across hands scraping mineral crust from its edge or catch in the steam from a cup held near a doorway. Nobody stops the pump schedule for it. Still, people notice the patch moving: from one drill-marked section of wall to another, down toward the little pool, and then away.
