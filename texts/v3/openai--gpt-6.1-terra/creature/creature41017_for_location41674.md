The city has one inhabitant, and it is also the city itself.

Its body forms the floor of the street and rises without a join into the walls and roof. It is dry to the touch, finely grained and hard enough to hold the marks of a knife for ages. Beneath that surface, waxy sap moves slowly through fibers drawing what they need from the air. The roof gives a little when the air outside changes, and the movement passes through the whole body.

The old rooms remain open, though the plant has been growing into them ever since they were cut. A threshold thickens by an amount too small to notice in a lifetime. A notch narrows. Across a cut that has been left alone, a few dry threads appear, delicate enough to break with a touch; over centuries, they may become part of a solid wall.

The handprints belong to the people who made the city, not to anyone living there now. Their benches and troughs are still joined to the ground, their outlines softened by growth and wear. The plant has grown around what they left, almost closing the oldest marks at the end of the street.

In the darkness it can be heard more readily than felt. Its roof booms as it swells and contracts; a thread parts with a small crackle somewhere down the street. The sounds continue after the movements that made them, filling the empty rooms and crossing back over one another. Under a visitor’s hand, the floor moves almost imperceptibly, in time with the roof.
