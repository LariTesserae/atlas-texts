Most numerous are the termites, though only a fraction of them are visible at once. Workers emerge from the low openings with soil or wood held in their jaws, disappear behind a partition, and return for more. Their bodies are pale and soft, suited to the covered passages they maintain. Where a wall is newly built, they press grains into the damp surface until the separate pieces become difficult to distinguish. A damaged edge is visited again and again, gradually losing its ragged shape.

Deeper in the galleries are termites that seldom or never cross the open floor. Soldiers, with darker, heavier heads, can block the narrow passages if the colony is disturbed. The queen is more sheltered still, attended by workers who feed and tend her. The settlement above is only part of their home; routes continue into the soil and through the wood on which they feed.

The ants have no such visible home in the hollow. One crosses the pale earth, pauses over a fallen seed, and moves on. Another follows the edge of a root, testing the ground ahead with its antennae. They are travelers here, leaving tracks that may outlast them until a crumb of earth falls or a termite gathers the surface for a wall.

Under a leaf, the spider keeps to its small patch of shade. The frog occupies a broader one, resting against the clay with its limbs folded close. Neither needs the whole chamber to belong to it. The spider waits where something might brush the leaf; the frog waits where insects pass. After its sudden movement, a few grains cling to the damp skin of its underside.

The snail remains drawn into its shell while the air is comparatively dry. Around it, mites move among moss stems and soil grains, where the spaces are still smaller and the moisture lasts longer. The fern and moss hold some of that moisture near the ground; the tree supplies shade, fallen fragments, and the living red root tip, which will keep lengthening through the loose floor. None of these lives is quite separate from the others. By the time the snail ventures out, a route it used before may have narrowed against a new termite wall.
