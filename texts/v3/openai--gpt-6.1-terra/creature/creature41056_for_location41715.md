The beetles are small enough that one can climb onto another’s back without stopping it. They have dull gray wing cases, dusty at the edges, and blunt heads that disappear beneath their thoraxes when they pull themselves tight. In the light they loosen and extend their legs, testing the salt and the bodies beneath them. A dead insect falling through the slit can draw them from every ledge. They gather first at its joints and softer underside, working their heads into gaps, while others wait on top or push underneath.

Their larvae are pale and soft where the adults are armored. They stay out of the open floor, among the fragments that collect behind the engine and in the corners. There they hollow dry bodies from within, leaving the pieces in small, orderly heaps. Adults visit those heaps too. Some eat; some press eggs into the cracks beneath them, as far from the shifting air as they can reach.

The hunters are narrower and higher on their legs. They can stand over several beetles at once, feeling their way through the crowd. When one catches a beetle, the struggle spreads outward in a brief ripple: neighboring bodies turn and climb over one another, then settle while the hunter holds its prey down. The mites are smaller still, thick in the seams and under the loose rust. Some gather on the remains after the hunters have left; others cling to passing bodies and are carried from one crowded place to another.

The chamber never has quite the same inhabitants at its edges as it does at its center. Near the slit, beetles follow the moving warmth and occasionally open their wings, making a short buzz before dropping back among the others. Deeper in the cracks are eggs, larvae and animals resting with their legs folded close. Around the pipe the hunters find both shelter and prey. When the bearing turns, a tremor passes through the iron and the packed floor breaks apart. Beetles tumble down the flywheel; mites vanish into seams; the hunters raise themselves above the scattering mass.

Afterward they return, though not to precisely the same places. Dust shaken loose settles over their backs. A few grains continue to slip between them as they gather against the warm metal, and soon the narrow spaces they had opened with their flight are filled again.
