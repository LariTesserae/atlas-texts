The deer are made around a light framework of cane. In the hollow of each chest there is a little bundle of fiber, tied tightly enough to spring back when pressed. Their hides have been fitted in pieces so fine that the joins follow the natural lines of the body: beneath the jaw, along the belly, in the dark folds behind the knees. The eyes are wet and exceptionally clear. They look as if they have seen the person who made them and are still waiting for that person to step away.

The frogs are heavier. Their feet have been pressed into the leaves, and a little clay has risen between their toes. Inside their extended throats are chambers of thin membrane. The held insect note passes through these chambers without changing; if a throat is cupped in both hands, the sound can be felt there as a delicate trembling.

In the seams of the paving live creatures small enough to pass beneath the sealed edges of the blocks. They are the color of the resin, with blunt heads and many short legs folded close to their bodies. In a clear joint of water one may be seen as a darkening, no larger than a seed, moving slowly against the scored clay. Elsewhere they reveal themselves by the soft ticking they make inside the terraces.

They tend the joins. A loose fiber is drawn in; a bubble in the resin is pressed flat from underneath. They work without leaving a new mark on the surfaces above them. When they come to the edge of a garden, they climb through the woven border and pass among the roots, where the tied strands make a kind of forest for them. The knots at the roots are not all alike. Some are old enough to have darkened; others are pale, their ends tucked in neatly.

The birds are hollow too, though less simply made than the deer. Fine threads run from the tips of their wings into their bodies and are fastened there to small clay weights. Their long tails are composed of narrow strips, each one joined separately. One bird stands with its beak touching the water in a leaf. Beneath the surface, its reflection has its beak lifted. The difference is slight, and remains slight however long one watches.

There are places beneath the cane frames where the paving is scored with rows of shallow marks. They are thumb-width apart, and beside some of them lie tiny coils of unused fiber. The seam creatures gather there, sometimes several hundred in a row. They remain in place while the note sounds. From time to time one disappears into a joint; another emerges nearby and takes its place.

No maker can be found in the gardens or on the terraces. But the clay retains the warmth of a hand for a long while. In one shaded chamber there is a block whose upper edge bears four parallel impressions and the broad print of a thumb beneath. The resin at its seam has not quite set. A seam creature reaches the edge, touches it with its blunt head, and waits.
