Below the surface, the ground is opening.

A broad chamber stretches for miles beneath a lid of pale stone. The ceiling is thin in places, broken by long, jagged skylights through which ordinary daylight pours. From below, the openings look almost flat and white; from above, they would be narrow cracks in an otherwise level plain. The light reaches the chamber floor in shifting bands, bright enough to show the warm pink and yellow of newly exposed rock.

The floor is not level. It rises in ridges and slumps into troughs as pressure moves through the soft layers underneath. Some ridges stand for a day, split along their crests, and fold outward into shelves. Others are still growing, their surfaces crazed with fine fractures. There has been little time for weathering: edges are sharp, fallen slabs fit the hollows they broke from, and heaps of rubble retain the exact pale color of the fresh scars above them.

Animals fill almost every open space.

There are six-legged runners the size of hands, with translucent backs and blunt heads suited to pushing through crowds. Larger creatures, low and broad as carts, travel slowly among them on padded limbs. The small ones pour around their feet and sometimes climb onto their backs, where they ride until brushed off by the ceiling or shaken loose. In the high air, pale-winged animals circle the shafts of light. When a skylight narrows, they gather beneath it in a dense, turning column.

The animals seem to live chiefly on one another. Wings and shed shells collect briefly in the troughs before being carried off. The broad creatures scrape films of tiny, moving things from damp stone with rows of soft mouthparts. In turn, smaller hunters gather at the folds of their legs. There is no grass or moss, not even around the water; the colors are those of rock, wet clay, and bodies.

Water appears irregularly. A seam may bead with condensation all morning, then release a warm trickle that runs down a wall and vanishes into a crack. Sudden showers fall through the skylights, striking the chamber in narrow, slanting curtains while other parts remain dry. The animals crowd toward these wet places so quickly that their movement can be seen from across the chamber: a darkening, converging flow over the pale floor.

At one pool, runners press shoulder to shoulder along the rim while the larger animals wait behind them. Every few minutes a heavy body pushes through, sending hundreds of smaller ones skittering over the wet stone. The pool clouds with fine sediment and clears again wherever the pressure of feet lets it rest.

The noise is continuous but uneven: feet ticking against stone, wings rattling, deep bodies rubbing past one another, and the occasional broad crack of a ridge giving way. A collapse brings a rush of sound as animals scatter. Afterward, the fallen rock is explored almost immediately. Some creatures work into the gaps underneath; others climb to the top and stand there until the next movement of the floor.

Scattered among the ridges are small human-made shelters, built from loose slabs leaned together. Their entrances are low and partly blocked with fitted stones. Near a few of them lie chipped cutting tools, shallow bowls and lengths of cord made from some tough animal fiber. One shelter stands on a shelf that has risen above its neighbors, exposing a ring of old postholes in the floor beneath it. Another has been dismantled or crushed, its stones mixed with fresh rubble.

People move through the chamber in short groups, carrying poles to test the ground ahead. They wear wraps against the warm, damp air and masks against the fine mineral dust. When a shower comes through a skylight, they spread skins to catch the water, keeping a watch for the animals it will draw. Their paths are marked here and there by stacks of three stones, though a marker may tilt or disappear as the ground shifts.

By evening the shafts of light turn amber and then withdraw up the walls. For a while the high-flying animals remain visible against the openings, still circling. Below them the crowded floor continues to move and rustle in the dark, and from deep under the chamber comes the slow grinding of another ridge beginning to rise.
