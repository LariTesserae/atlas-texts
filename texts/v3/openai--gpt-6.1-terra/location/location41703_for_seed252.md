The chamber lies just below a bare, sun-struck slope. A narrow fissure opens onto it from above, admitting a blade of white light that falls across the floor and strikes the walls in hard reflections. For a little while each day the pocket is dazzling. Then the sun moves, and its brightness withdraws up the rock.

The chamber is scarcely larger than a small room. Its ceiling has split recently: the broken faces are angular and pale, with dark seams running through them. A few sharp flakes lie where they fell. There has not been much time for water to round their edges.

Heat rises through the floor. In a shallow hollow, clear water gathers over a bed of glittering grains. It is hot enough to send a faint vapor into the sunbeam, though the water seeping down the outer wall is cooler. The two meet at the pool’s edge, where a trembling line of condensation appears and disappears. Every so often a drop swells on the ceiling, hangs there, and falls. The sound is small but distinct in the stillness.

Nothing green grows here. Thin films of heat-loving microbes occupy a few protected seams, too sparse to color the rock noticeably. Near the cooler fissure, under a loose chip of stone, a pale springtail sometimes moves through the damp grit. It has a short distance to travel before the floor becomes too hot, so it keeps to the margins, where moisture arrives from above.

The place is active without seeming busy. Water enters slowly, warms, evaporates and beads again on the ceiling. Tiny crystals form at the pool’s rim as it shrinks; the next seep may dissolve them. A faint tick comes from the rock as the sun heats one face and leaves another in shade.

There are no paths into the chamber, no marks on its walls. Far above, instruments able to read minute changes in the rock could map the pocket and follow its water without disturbing it. From inside, there is only the brightness, the heat, and the occasional falling drop.
