High above the surrounding plain, a fresh ridge of black mineral holds a basin no wider than a village. Its walls are sharp-edged, their facets still bright where they split from the earth. There has been too little wind, and no rain at all, to soften them. Warmth rises slowly through the stone.

The basin is crowded with growth. Pale, fleshy fronds unfold in tiers, their surfaces furred with fine red threads. Low cushions of jointed greenery press against them, climbing wherever they find a little light. None of it is wet. The plants draw thin mineral vapors from cracks in the ground and store them in waxy bladders; a broken stem gives off a faint resinous smell and seals itself with a dry amber skin.

Among the fronds stands a settlement, though no one has built it. Colonies of small, many-limbed creatures have raised their young for generations in the same branching growths. The plants thicken around occupied hollows and narrow the passages between them, so that each season leaves another layer of rooms and covered ways. At the center, several trunks have grown together into a vaulted chamber, open above to an ordinary pale sky. Its floor is packed with shed skins, seed husks and the fine dust of the ridge.

The inhabitants move through the settlement almost without sound. Broad pads carry them along the walls; folded membranes let them descend between levels, drifting a short distance through the warm air. Smaller creatures graze on the red threads or wait beneath the cushions for falling seeds. There are many of them, but they move patiently, making room as they pass. A frond settling back into place may be the only motion visible for a long while.

At the rim, the growth thins and the sharp young rock shows through. Beyond it, the land drops away in terraces of salt-colored stone. The basin keeps its warmth after the light has faded, and its crowded chambers remain faintly visible: pale leaves, dark openings, and now and then the slow turning of a creature at home.
