The city occupies a shelf high above the cloud line, where the mountain has been worn into a broad, tilted bowl. Its far rim stands so high that the horizon is stone. Beyond that rim, other shelves can be seen through gaps in the rock, each carrying the same dense green growth and the same pale, standing shapes.

It is hot enough that the wet ground gives off a mineral breath. Water lies close beneath everything. It gleams in seams of black rock, gathers in shallow courts, and hangs from the undersides of leaves in beads that do not fall. The stone is old and rounded, its channels deepened and smoothed over a very long time. In places it has the soft, fluted look of something pressed by a thumb.

The city has no masonry. Its walls are living trunks fused together, their roots forming foundations thick as streets. Chambers open inside them, cool and dripping; corridors run between folds of bark. Great leaves overlap to make roofs, while pale fungal growth lines the inner surfaces and gives off a mild, milky light. The buildings crowd together in neighborhoods, separated by passages wide enough to walk through. Some passages end at pools. Others open suddenly onto terraces from which the whole basin is visible.

There are no tools, and no marks where tools have been used. The roots have grown in regular courses around the pools and along the streets. At each crossing they rise into arches, nearly identical in shape. Within the largest chambers, smooth root-banks stand at the height of a seat, facing inward toward bare patches of floor. Nothing sits there now. Moss grows over the banks and stops precisely at their edges.

The sound is immense. It fills the basin with the force of a waterfall, though the water is almost still. The noise seems to come from the leaves, from the stone, from the wet air itself. Close to a wall it has a heavy, wooden resonance; beside a pool it becomes a high, packed hiss. On the open terraces it is so loud that the body feels lightly held in place. Yet every leaf is motionless, every reflection steady.

There are no insects in the flowers, no birds among the upper branches, no tracks in the soft margins of the water. Vines climb the living towers and flower profusely. Their blossoms face inward, toward the city’s empty rooms. Ferns fill the shade beneath them, and mats of tiny plants cover the flat roofs, each rooted in a film of water.

The streets are longer than the basin allows. From the rim, a passage may be followed with the eye from one side of the city to the other; at ground level it continues through grove after grove, always beneath the same strip of visible sky. Returning along it takes no more time than entering. The pools have a similar depth without distance: the roots visible on their bottoms belong to trees growing several terraces away.

Nothing moves through the city, and nothing needs to. Water remains suspended in the air, leaves remain green in the heat, and the tremendous sound holds its note. Above the roofs, clouds press against the stone rim without spilling over it. The place has the settled fullness of a city in the middle of its life, preserved at an hour that never passes.
