High on a drowned mountain, just below its summit, there is a hollow no larger than a room. It is full of cold, clear water. Beyond its narrow mouth the seafloor falls away so steeply that the blue seems to have no bottom.

Light reaches the hollow from above. It passes through a long flank of nearly transparent stone and enters in broad, wavering bands. The stone is new: pale, faintly warm, and smooth as the inside of an eggshell. Along the floor, thin ridges are lifting out of it. They are not deposits of sand or the remains of older rock. They grow directly from the surface, pressing upward in slow folds that have not yet hardened.

The water is almost empty. A few threads of dark vegetation cling near the entrance, each separated from the next by a long stretch of bare wall. Now and then a small silver animal appears among them, holding itself in place with transparent fins. It feeds, perhaps, on whatever the water brings from above. When the current reverses, it vanishes into a crack too fine to see.

The current reverses often. Water thrusts into the hollow, wheels around its far wall, and rushes out again. Loose flakes of newborn stone shoot past and are sucked back before they can reach the open sea. The light twists across the floor. A ridge may bend nearly flat in one surge and stand up again in the next.

The noise is enormous. The hollow catches the pulse of the ocean and gives it a hard, continuous voice: a roar with a higher ringing inside it, like a stone bowl being struck over and over. There is no quiet side of the room. The sound seems to come through the water and through the walls at once, pressing against the body.

In the back of the hollow stands a small construction of three flat stones. Someone has balanced them upright against one another, with a fourth laid across the top. Beneath it sits a shallow cup, roughly chipped from the same pale material as the walls. The stones tremble violently but do not fall.

Fine lines rise from the cup toward the ceiling. At first they look like cracks. Watch longer and they can be seen forming: wherever the highest ringing note passes through the water, a hair-thin seam of stone appears. Most break apart in the next surge. A few join the ceiling and thicken there, making a delicate canopy above the little shelter.

From outside, the hollow is only a bright slit in the mountain. Inside, the sea is busy making a room, though it cannot yet decide what shape the room should be.
