The plain is low and nearly level, but it is not smooth. Fresh drifts of pale mineral dust lie against ridges of packed earth, and a fine crust gathers wherever the ground has been left undisturbed. There are no streambeds, no pools, no dark seams of dampness. In the afternoon warmth, the soil breaks underfoot with a dry, papery sound.

Plants grow thickly across much of the plain. Their leaves are small, stiff and gray-green, crowded along woody stems; nearer the ground, mats of needle-fine foliage fill the spaces between them. In sheltered places the growth rises waist-high and folds over itself, making a dense cover through which small animals pass unseen. Elsewhere, the plants stand farther apart, with bare salt-colored ground between them. Their roots and fallen stems catch the moving dust, gradually building hummocks around their own bases.

Among the hummocks are towers of clay, thousands upon thousands of them. Some are rounded and no higher than a knee; others rise taller than a person, buttressed at the base and fluted with narrow channels. They are termite nests, joined below ground and crowded so closely that, from a distance, they give the plain the appearance of a vast low city. New walls are being added to old ones. Fresh earth, slightly darker than the sun-baked surface, marks the work. There are no roads or tools here, but there are passages, chambers and colonies laid out over a distance too great to see across.

The place is busy without being loud. Beetles climb the stems; pale lizards wait in the strips of shade cast by the towers. Birds move through the scrub in loose groups, pausing to pick insects from the ground. Beneath them, termites carry fragments of dead vegetation through their narrow passages. A hunting animal’s approach may send a whole patch of leaves trembling, though the hunter itself remains hidden.

Wind moves across the plain in long, uneven breaths. It combs the foliage flat, lifts loose dust and drops it again against the plants and towers. The larger gusts travel for hours, raising faint veils that soften the far horizon. By evening, new ripples have formed on the exposed ground, and some of the lowest leaves are already half buried. The plain is still being made: dust caught by growth, walls raised by insects, shelter gathering around shelter.
