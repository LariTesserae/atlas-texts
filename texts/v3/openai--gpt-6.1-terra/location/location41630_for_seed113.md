The city occupies a hollow just below the ground, a chamber no wider than a small square. Its ceiling is a shallow dome of dark earth, held up without columns. There is room to walk around the whole place in a minute, though the path is interrupted in several places by water.

The construction is precise and almost entirely vacant. Pale curbs describe streets too narrow for vehicles; doorways open onto platforms with no rooms behind them. A dozen levels of transparent pavement hang in the air, each one following the plan of a different city. They are spaced far apart, and most contain nothing but a drain or a lit line marking an edge. From below, the lines overlap like a map drawn and redrawn on the same sheet.

The streets are new. Their corners are sharp, their surfaces unworn. At the lowest level, black stone rises through them in angular ridges, as if the chamber had been built around a fresh fracture in the earth. Water gathers along the ridges, then spills into the gutters in clear, fast threads. It is warm enough that the exposed rock sweats.

Ferns grow wherever a gutter slows. Their fronds lean across the empty thoroughfares, glossy and deep green in the scant light. Pale mats of liverwort cover the undersides of the suspended pavements. Farther up, roots descend from the ceiling and stop a handspan above the highest street; droplets collect at their tips and fall through every level of the city.

The drains are louder than their size suggests. Water strikes them with the sound of rain on a broad roof, and a low mechanical note moves from one side of the hollow to the other. Sometimes the pavements answer with a brief ringing. There are no calls or wingbeats in the chamber, no insects on the fern leaves, no tracks in the wet sediment.

Every few minutes the street plans change. A pavement brightens along one edge, loosens into a veil of droplets and reforms a little higher or lower, carrying its ferns and pooled water with it. The drains adjust before the water reaches them. Whatever machinery governs this is hidden in the thin margins of the streets; the earth beyond them remains dark and close.

After each shift, the falling droplets find slightly different routes. For a while the city is filled with small impacts, then the gutters settle into their deeper, steady roar.
