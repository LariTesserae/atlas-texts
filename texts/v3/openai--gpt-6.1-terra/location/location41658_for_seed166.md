The basin lies beneath several metres of clear, cool water. It is not large: from one side, the opposite wall can be seen through the blue-green distance. Pale limestone rises around it in rounded shelves, enclosing a floor of fine grey silt and scattered stones. Long exposure has softened the rock’s edges, leaving shallow grooves and hollows where grains have settled.

Daylight reaches the bottom evenly. It gathers on the upper faces of the stone and fades beneath the ledges, but even the deepest recesses remain visible. Here and there, a few dark-green strands of aquatic growth cling to cracks in the limestone. Most of the basin is bare.

There are no fish among the stones, no insects at the surface, no shells in the silt. The water moves so slowly that the plants hold almost the same position for hours. A faint current passes over the rim, crosses the floor and leaves by another low channel, carrying little sediment with it.

The quiet is not quite complete. From somewhere beyond the basin comes a low mechanical vibration, too distant to have a clear source. It travels through the rock and water as a steady hum, more felt than heard. No pipes, cables or machinery are visible here; the basin itself contains nothing made.

The light shifts a little through the day, but the place otherwise seems settled into its form: cool water over stone, a few rooted strands, and the broad, empty spaces between them.
