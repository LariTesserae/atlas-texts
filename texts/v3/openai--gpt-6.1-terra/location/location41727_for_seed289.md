Below the ground is a chamber no larger than the space beneath a cupped pair of hands. It is packed with pale mineral nodules, each pressed so closely against its neighbors that their soft surfaces have flattened into facets. Between them lie thin seams of cool air, too narrow for a finger and bright as daylight.

The light comes from the stone itself. It has no visible source and casts shadows only where one nodule touches another. In those dark points, beads of water sit clear and round. Most of the chamber is dry; the moisture is confined to these small, cold holdings and to a fine gloss on the mineral faces.

The rock has the look of something just beginning. Its surfaces are smooth and yielding, its crystals blunt, their edges not yet drawn out. Nothing has worn the chamber into shape. Its boundaries are the first boundaries the material has taken, and they remain exactly as they are.

A few colorless fronds occupy the wider seams. Their leaves are thick, widely spaced, and tipped with white hairs. Around them, in extraordinary numbers, are tiny animals: jointed creatures with translucent backs, soft-bodied ones curled into the angles between stones, and minute six-legged forms standing shoulder to shoulder along the wet rims. Their bodies crowd every available surface. None moves. A leg remains raised over a bead of water; a mouth rests open against a leaf.

The chamber is loud. A deep, densely layered hum fills it, with a sharp ringing above, as if innumerable small bells were sounding behind a closed wall. The noise does not pulse or fade. The animals seem neither disturbed by it nor responsible for it. Even the water holds its perfect surface under that weight of sound.

Near one edge is a fragment of clay, bearing three shallow marks that might have been made by fingertips. Mineral has formed around it without covering the marks. In another seam, a short wall of pebbles closes off a space scarcely larger than a seed. Whether it was placed there by a creature, or simply arrived in the shape of a wall, is difficult to say.

The light stays bright. The chamber stays cool. Its inhabitants remain poised among the unfinished stones, and the great, steady sound continues to fill the little space.
