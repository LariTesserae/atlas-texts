High in the wall of a mountain-spanning factory, behind a bolted inspection plate, there is a chamber scarcely larger than a cupped hand. The mountain itself is made: stacked furnace blocks, riveted trusses and insulated conduits rising above the clouds. Inside the chamber, every surface has been cast or laid by a machine. Even the grit under the water consists of tiny, identical ceramic teeth.

Cold seeps through the metal. A shallow black pool covers the floor, held just above freezing by a heater buried in the masonry. Moisture beads on the ceiling and gathers along the edges of the bolts. The light comes from a single orange indicator behind ribbed glass, too weak to reach the corners. Near it, a few pale threads of engineered moss cling to the grout, their tips dipped in water.

The pool is crowded with animals. Translucent swimmers pack the spaces between the ceramic teeth; blind, jointed things cover the walls in layers; minute snails seal themselves around the bolt heads. In the dimness their bodies make the chamber look furred. They feed on the factory’s mineral condensate and on one another, though no gap opens among them for long. Each has its place in a population so closely fitted to the chamber that the removal of one would leave a visible hollow.

The noise is immense. A compressor directly behind the wall pounds through the metal, and thousands of tiny shells ring against it in answer. The water carries the vibration into every body and every seam. There is no quiet spot: the chamber is too small for sound to diminish, and the cold walls return it all. The animals have no ears in the ordinary sense. They sense the machinery through their feet and keep their grip by leaning against its beat.

The place is unfinished by design. Along one wall, a row of cast ledges ends in clean square edges, as if the next section were waiting to be attached. Droplets stand beneath the ledges, fat and bright, but never fall; an upward pulse of air holds each one against its own weight. Water enters through a pinhole at exactly the rate it leaves through another. The heater, the compressor and the crowded animals maintain a balance so precise that the chamber remains wet, new and deafening, with all its little lives in motion and its shape unchanged.
