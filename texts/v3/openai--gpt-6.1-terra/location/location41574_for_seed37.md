Beneath a thin shelf of stone is a chamber no larger than a pantry. It has no entrance that can be found twice. The ceiling is low, the floor almost level, and the whole place is brightly lit, though there is no opening to the surface. The light seems to come from the stone just before the stone finishes becoming solid.

The walls are pale and faintly warm at first touch. A moment later they are cool. Their surfaces are smooth not from wear but from having only recently formed: here and there, soft ridges are still lifting, and a seam of chalk-colored material slowly closes around a pebble. The pebble protrudes from both sides of the wall. If you touch it on one side, the other side grows cold.

Most of the floor is dry. In its shallowest depression lies a skin of water, thinner than a leaf. It gathers for a few hours, drawing moisture from the air, then disappears into the floor without leaving a stain. During its brief existence, animals come to it from everywhere.

There are hundreds of them. Small jointed creatures the color of unglazed clay move between the ridges, some carrying translucent young beneath their bodies. Pale larvae crowd the edge of the water and bend down to drink. A soft-bodied hunter no longer than a fingernail waits among them; every so often it folds around one and becomes still. Tiny winged things cross the chamber in looping flights. Their wings make no audible sound.

Nothing here has far to travel, yet the animals seldom meet except at the water. They pass through one another’s shadows and emerge at different times. Sometimes a creature leaves a trail ahead of itself: a fine line of dampness appearing across the floor, which it follows with evident care. A few never quite arrive. Their trails remain, and the others step over them.

Vegetation is limited to a little tuft of colorless moss growing from a crack near the ceiling. It hangs downward when dry and points upward when wet. Occasionally a clear drop forms at its tip and rises into the stone. The larvae gather beneath it anyway.

In one corner, several flat chips have been leaned together into a shelter. It might have been made by the larger animals, though none is seen entering it. The chips enclose a space slightly bigger than the chamber itself. Looking through the gap between them, one can see the floor and the bright walls continuing farther than they should, with more animals moving quietly in the distance.

The chamber is most active just after the water vanishes. New seams appear. Old ones close. Eggs laid in the damp patch hatch on the dry floor; the young scatter before their shells have settled. The light increases a little, then a little more, until every small body casts a sharp shadow.

There is almost no noise. Now and then a grain of new stone falls from the ceiling and lands with a delicate tick. The animals pause, their shadows still moving beneath them, and then go on.
