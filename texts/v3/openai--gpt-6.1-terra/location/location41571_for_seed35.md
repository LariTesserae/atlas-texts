The chamber lies below the desert, reached through a narrow shaft whose walls are warm all the way down. It is no larger than a village square. Above it, black stone hangs in low folds; below, the floor rises in sharp mineral ridges, as if it had only lately been split open. The edges are perfectly clean. Nothing here runs or rains or blows hard enough to wear them down.

Between the ridges lies a fine gray powder that does not cling to the skin. There is no dampness, not even in the deepest seams. The air tastes of hot iron and something faintly sweet, like insulation warmed around a wire. A few pale growths stand in the powder: jointed stalks with narrow, metallic leaves. They are far apart, but each has a crowded ring of roots exposed above the ground, gripping the stone like small hands.

Animals occupy nearly every other surface. Thumb-sized creatures with translucent shells pack the undersides of ledges, their legs moving in overlapping rows. Larger ones, flat and many-jointed, sit upright in the cracks with their faces turned toward the ceiling. Along one wall, a colony of soft-bodied things forms a thick, breathing mat, though it is difficult to see what enters or leaves them. They feed on the mineral dust and on one another’s cast-off shells. The pale stalks draw heat and trace elements from the rock; the smallest animals graze their leaves without ever stripping them bare.

The noise is enormous for such a confined place. Shells rasp, legs tap, and the upright creatures produce a steady metallic whine through slits in their backs. The whines combine into a chord that fills the chamber from floor to ceiling. It is loud enough to feel in the teeth. After a while, the separate sounds become distinguishable, and it becomes apparent that each creature keeps precisely to its own note.

Three iron frames stand among the ridges. They resemble the remains of a small processing works: a hopper, a toothed wheel, lengths of pipe ending in open mouths. One wheel turns continuously, driven by a belt that disappears into the stone. Its teeth pass close to a line of the upright animals, which add a dry clicking to the general chord. The machinery is dusty but unworn. Dim amber bulbs set into its frame illuminate the nearest shells and leave the rest of the chamber to glimmer by reflected light.

The place is busy, but it does not seem to progress. A creature leaves its ledge and another takes its exact place. Powder sifts from a ridge while, at its base, an equal quantity gathers back into stone. The wheel completes its rotation and the same marked tooth comes around again. Even the chord holds: when one animal falls silent, another begins the same note elsewhere.

The inhabitants do not hurry. Their closeness is not the press of a crowd arriving, but the fit of things that have long had room for one another. Deep below the desert, in the warm and ceaseless din, the chamber remains sharp-edged and whole.
