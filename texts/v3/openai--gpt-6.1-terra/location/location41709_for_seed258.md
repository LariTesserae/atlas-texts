The valley is high enough that its rim lies among the clouds, but the air within it is warm. A broad floor of living green stretches from one side to the other, almost empty of anything that rises above it. The emptiness is deceptive: the ground itself is a continuous body of plants, a close-laid surface of leaf and root, resilient underfoot and faintly warm.

Here and there, at long intervals, a column lifts from the floor. Each is formed of stems grown tightly around one another, widening overhead into a flat, open crown. Their shadows are far apart. Between them there is room to see the whole valley: low green embankments, shallow basins bright with water, and the clouded heights beyond.

Everything here has been made by growth. The embankments have seams where thick roots meet; the basins are lined with overlapping leaves that hold their edges against the water. Great ribs of pale wood support the slopes beneath a skin of moss. There is no stone showing, no bare soil, no ruin of an earlier place. Even the watercourses have soft green walls, bent into shape while they were young and held there by the bodies growing around them.

The valley looks newly finished. Its ridges are plump and unweathered, its channels shallow, its edges still rounded like the edges of a folded leaf. Yet it does not proceed toward any later form. New shoots appear in the small spaces left by old ones; a leaf loosens and another takes its place before the gap can widen. The growing and the giving way are so evenly matched that the landscape keeps its first shape.

Water moves almost everywhere, though seldom in deep pools. It seeps over the floor in clear films, gathers under the embankments and spills through ranks of hollow stems. The noise is immense. Each stem has a different voice, and together they produce a long, steady rushing, pierced by notes like wind across the mouth of a bottle. The sound fills the open spaces without seeming to come from any particular distance.

A few animals live among the crowns. Small pale gliders cross from one column to another, traveling a surprisingly long way on stretched membranes before disappearing into the leaves. Their calls are lost in the water’s roar. Near the pools, patient insects cling beneath the broad leaves, where droplets collect on their backs and eventually fall.

The light is ordinary daylight, softened when a cloud passes over the rim. Under it the valley’s green has many shades: the dark, wet green of the channels; the yellow-green of new growth; the almost white green inside a stem where it has parted around another. From a distance the whole place seems serene and still. Standing within it, one feels the floor gently yielding, hears the innumerable pipes, and sees that its stillness is something the living valley continually maintains.
