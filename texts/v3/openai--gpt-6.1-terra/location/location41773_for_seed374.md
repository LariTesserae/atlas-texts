The town occupies a shallow coastal basin, spread across a floor of dark, unweathered stone. Low ridges divide it into districts, and between them run streets paved with flat pieces of the same rock. The slabs are sharp at their edges. Rain gathers in their joints but has not yet worn channels through them; in places the ground looks as though it has only just cooled.

Water is everywhere without becoming a lake. It stands ankle-deep in courtyards, shines on walls, and wells slowly through cracks in the paving. The air is warm and wet. Under a low ceiling of cloud, the town is dim even at midday, its distances reduced to overlapping roofs and the pale sheen of standing water.

There are no trees or grasses. The houses are built from stone, clay and shell, their roofs weighted with more stone. They crowd close together, leaving narrow passages through which people carry baskets or push shallow boats when the water rises. Walls have been repaired so often that the old courses and the new are difficult to tell apart. Stone hammers hang beside doorways; fish bones and shells serve as needles, scrapers and hooks.

Animals fill the spaces the buildings leave. Crabs pack the wet foundations, climbing over one another in the dark. Small, blind-looking fish move through the drains. Insects gather beneath eaves in thick, trembling layers, and waterbirds stand shoulder to shoulder along the basin’s outer pools. Where warm mineral water seeps from the ground, pale films of bacteria coat the rock. Snails graze on the films; crabs take the snails; fish and birds take what they can. The people harvest from the same crowded margins.

The noise is immense. Frogs call from the flooded lanes, insects rasp under every roof, birds cry from the ridges, and countless claws tap against stone. The hard walls throw the sound back and forth until individual calls disappear into a continuous roar. People speak close to one another’s ears. They have learned to recognize a neighbor by touch on the shoulder before they turn.

From the higher roofs the town seems to go on for a long way: wet stone, low houses, packed pools, and the gray light caught on each surface. The animals move constantly, the water seeps and gathers, meals are caught and walls repaired. Yet the whole place keeps the same aspect year after year. There is no great current here to cut the rock or carry the town away, and the sound never falls far enough for anyone to remember what silence was like.
