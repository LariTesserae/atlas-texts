A stair descends through a square opening in the ground, its iron treads warm even near the surface. At the bottom is a chamber no larger than a village hall. The ceiling is low at the edges and rises in the middle, where dark rock has split into a fan of sharp, clean faces. The break looks recent. Chips still lie beneath it, their edges unworn.

Heat gathers here. It presses against the skin without the relief of a breeze. Most of the floor is dry, a pale crust over black mineral, but there are shallow hollows where water appears after rain above. Elsewhere, droplets collect on the coldest iron and hang for a long time before falling. Around each damp place the crust has softened into a narrow margin of ochre mud.

Plants occupy these margins in separate, luxuriant stands. Thick green fronds unfold over themselves, glossy as wet leather; pale runners cross the dry floor between them, then disappear into cracks. They are not evenly spread. There is room to walk among them, and long stretches of bare mineral remain untouched. Some of the runners lead upward through the roof, toward light and roots beyond the chamber. Others gather beneath a few dim electric lamps, where their leaves turn a deeper, almost black green.

The lamps belong to an old pumping installation. One low brick housing stands against the wall, its door removed, its machinery visible through a square opening. A flywheel rests inside under a skin of rust. Two pipes leave the housing and vanish into the rock; a third ends abruptly above a basin, as though the rest had been taken away. Now and then a faint current passes through the lamps. They brighten too slowly to notice at first, then hold for a while before sinking back to a dull red glow.

The plants have grown around the installation but not over it. A hand’s breadth of bare floor surrounds the brickwork, marked by a fine white deposit. When condensation falls there, the deposit darkens and briefly rises into little ridges, as if drawn toward the warm wall. By the next visit it is flat again. No tracks disturb it. Nothing calls from the fronds, and nothing moves within them.

In the quiet, the place seems motionless, though it is not. A leaf opens over several days. A droplet lengthens beneath a pipe and finally releases. After rain, a shallow pool forms in one of the hollows, holds the red reflection of a lamp, and very gradually gives itself to the roots.
