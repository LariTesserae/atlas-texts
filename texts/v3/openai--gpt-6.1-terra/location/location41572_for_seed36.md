Below the surface there is a country of leaves.

Its ceiling lies far overhead, supported by pale columns of fused roots. Between them, a continuous sheet of living tissue gives off a white-gold light, bright enough to show the fine veins in every frond below. There is no visible source within the ceiling and no shadow deep enough to hide in. Even the undersides of the leaves are lit by the glow rising from the ground.

The country is broad: a valley-sized chamber, perhaps several chambers joined together, with long views through avenues of upright growth. The avenues are crowded rather than clear. Fans of foliage overlap at every height, and mats of small, thick leaves cover the floor. Some stand rigid and translucent as mica; others are soft, dark, and folded around pockets of warm air. Trunks rise in close ranks, their surfaces made not of bark but of tightly packed scales, each scale green at its edge. Where they join overhead, they form low arches, high galleries, and walls so dense that a person would have to turn sideways to pass.

The heat is steady. The air has the dry warmth of a stone left in the sun, though there is little exposed stone here and no sun. Moisture gathers in particular places: a clear bead held at the tip of a leaf, a shallow cup where several stems meet, a damp seam beneath a bank of roots. There are no streams. The little pools have clean, sharp edges, and the plants around them remain plump while those a few paces away have narrow, leathery leaves.

The ground looks newly made. In places the living mat parts to reveal pale mineral nodules, still rounded and smooth, embedded in a soft-looking substrate. There are no gullies, fallen blocks, or banks worn down by water. Bulges rise beneath the vegetation as if hills were about to form, but their slopes go nowhere. Throughout the valley, things have the unfinished shapes of beginnings: roots gathered around empty centers, leaves furled around buds that do not open, small ridges awaiting a direction.

Here and there are structures that seem almost deliberate. A cluster of stems has grown into a chamber with an arched entrance. Several such chambers stand apart from one another along an open stretch, each roofed with broad overlapping leaves. Their interiors are bare except for the same fine green covering found everywhere else. It is difficult to say whether anything made use of them.

Animals are scarce. A few tiny, pale creatures rest beneath the water cups, their limbs spread against the leaf surface. They are so still that they might be part of the plant until one is seen more closely: a dark eye, a seam where a folded limb meets the body. There is little sound beyond a thin, continuous hum within the foliage and, occasionally—or perhaps always, too faint to notice until one comes near—the minute ticking of water held against leaf edges.

Nothing here visibly grows or withers. The brightness does not shift across the valley. A bead of water hangs from its tip, the curled leaves stay curled, and the pale creatures remain beneath them. The place has the richness of a flourishing wilderness and the stillness of a landscape held at the moment before it finishes forming.
