The land is almost level, though not quite flat. Long swells of pale stone rise a little above the water, rounded so thoroughly that their slopes can scarcely be distinguished from the plain. Between them lie broad shallows, warm and brown over beds of sand. In places the water gives way to dark mud, then appears again a few paces farther on, filling some depression too slight to see until the light catches it.

Rain has polished the exposed rock and left beads in its hollows. Even under a clear sky the air feels damp, and a faint haze softens the far edge of the plain. The sun is bright enough to make the pools shine, but its warmth is held close to the ground. Water seeps slowly from beneath the stone shelves. It gathers in narrow runnels and spreads into the shallows without any visible current.

Plants grow where they can find a lasting grip: separate clumps of sedge, low salt-tolerant shrubs, a few small trees rooted on higher patches of gravel. Their shadows fall a long way across bare ground. Much of the plain is open stone, wet sand and water, with mats of fine green growth just below the surface. Along the pool margins, snails graze among scattered blades, and small fish shelter in the thin shade.

The open ground is busy with animals. Long-legged birds walk through the shallows, pausing to strike at fish. Smaller birds gather on the exposed stone, and frogs crowd the muddy edges beneath the sedges. Their calls carry across the plain in overlapping bursts. A flock takes off with a loud clatter of wings; farther away, unseen birds answer from another stretch of water. Wind passes through the sparse grass with a dry hiss, while, from beyond the low horizon, surf provides a continuous heavy sound.

Little changes quickly here. The pools expand after rain and contract through warm stretches, leaving curved lines of silt and shells. A channel may shift over many seasons as sediment settles in it, but its water moves so slowly that the surface usually lies still. The stone has been worn down over a span too long to read in its remaining contours. Across the whole wide basin, its rounded backs sit just above the shallows, wet at their edges and warm in the sun.
