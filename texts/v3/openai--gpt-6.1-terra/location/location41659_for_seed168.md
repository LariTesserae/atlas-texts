At high elevation, a pale plateau opens for many miles beneath a clear, bright sky. Its surface is made of newly exposed mineral: broad plates of cream-colored stone, black seams, and low ridges with edges still sharp enough to cast thin shadows. There are no channels cut into it, no dunes, no deposits gathered at the foot of the slopes. Even the loose fragments look freshly broken.

Vegetation grows here, though it is widely spaced. A traveler could cross an hour of bare ground before reaching the next stand, then find it thick enough to shut out the sky. The plants rise on pale, jointed trunks and spread into dense crowns of dark green blades. Their leaves are heavy and slightly translucent at the edges, with a soft luster like polished wax. Beneath each crown, the ground is covered in layers of broad, overlapping fronds. Nothing moves among them.

The plants have no need of water. They draw carbon and reactive minerals from the dry air and circulate a slow, oily solvent through their tissues. At the tips of the leaves, tiny crystals form in precise rows. As they mature, they fall into white heaps around the roots, where the plants take them up again. In the still air, a single crystal can sometimes be heard striking stone.

Across the plateau, whole groves carry on this quiet chemistry. Their pale trunks separate and recombine minerals in long columns of living tissue; faint electrical charges pass through their leaves. The work is continuous and immensely orderly, but there are no machines, buildings, or paths between the stands. Under the vegetation the stone has a faint metallic smell, strongest where the roots meet the open air.

Far away, the plateau drops out of sight behind a line of raw peaks. Their faces are smooth and unweathered, as though the range had only recently emerged, yet nothing in the landscape appears to be progressing toward another state. The crystal heaps remain the same size. The groves keep their distance from one another. In the warm daylight, the sharp shadows hold steady over the bare stone.
