High on the mountain there is a hollow no wider than a small grove. Its rim is fresh black rock, sharp enough to pare a hoof, as though it had risen only moments ago. Inside, the ground lies nearly level. Water stands between cushions of moss and among widely spaced ferns whose fronds are thick with silver droplets. Every surface looks newly made, but nothing wears down.

The air is cold and dim. Cloud presses just above the rim, and the light that comes through it has the greenish cast of deep water. A few pale trees rise from the hollow, far enough apart that each has a circle of open ground around it. Their trunks are furred with liverwort; their roots disappear into shallow, clear pools. Beneath the trees, the moss is dense and soft, growing over stone without hiding its edges.

Animals fill the place without crowding it. White insects hang in the air, each keeping a precise distance from the next. Dark birds occupy the fern stems. Small, many-legged creatures cling to the undersides of leaves, and in the pools transparent fish face into currents that never arrive. Their abundance becomes apparent slowly: an eye in every cup of moss, a breathing shape beneath every stone, a fine adjustment of attention wherever one looks.

There is a steady industrial noise, loud enough to feel in the teeth. It comes from the rock itself: a low motor-hum under the ground, a measured clacking within the ridge, the thin electrical whine of the cloud. No machines are present. The fern tips vibrate in exact rows, condensing water with such regularity that the drops resemble products moving along an invisible line. They do not fall. Each hangs at the point of release, full and trembling, while the rock continues its work around it.

Nothing in the hollow passes from one state to another. The birds sing, and their notes remain audible, neither beginning nor fading; the insects hover without spending any effort. A ripple is visible on each pool, complete from its center to its edge, though the water is perfectly still. Looking into one pool, you can see the underside of the cloud. Looking into another, a few paces away, you see the same cloud from above, with the hollow beneath it like a dark seed.

At the rim, the machinery of the sound stops all at once. Beyond the sharp rocks there is only ordinary mountain wind. From there the hollow looks quiet and very small, a patch of green held high in the cold. The longer one watches, the more animals can be counted inside it, though none has appeared or moved.
