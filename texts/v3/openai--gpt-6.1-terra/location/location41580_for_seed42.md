The town lies underwater on a high mountain shelf, in a lake so clear that the broken surface is visible from its floor. Daylight reaches the streets in broad, wavering bands. The water is cool; even at noon, the upper leaves feel cold.

There is very little bare stone. A thick mat of plants covers the shelf, drapes over its edges and descends into the deeper lake beyond. Rootlets bind old silt into a springy ground. Low fronds overlap like shingles, and here and there a pale stem rises several body-lengths before spreading into a small, flat crown. Between these taller plants there is room to see: long, open lanes of clear water, with a wall, a doorway or a flight of steps standing alone in each one.

The buildings are low and close-set, though much of each has been swallowed by the green. Their walls were laid from lake stone and held together with wooden pins and cords of twisted root. Roofs are woven from broad leaves weighted with pebbles. There are no enclosed rooms that keep the water out. Doorways lead through to courtyards, and narrow channels run between blocks toward a central basin. The channels were once dug for travel or drainage; now they carry the gentlest of currents. At their crossings, worn stone posts stand in pairs, linked by handrails of polished wood.

Everything has been softened by time. The steps have shallow hollows where feet found purchase for generations. Corners have rounded, cords have thickened with growth, and roots pass through cracks without bringing the walls down. A grove grows directly from one roof, its roots threaded through the wickerwork and into the floor below. The house remains standing, though its door has become a curtain of fine leaves.

Animals are everywhere in the spaces the plants leave open. Small fish shelter beneath the fronds, then emerge in quick, coordinated clouds. Snails graze the old masonry in slow bands. Long-bodied crayfish occupy the drains, and a pale, broad-headed fish rests beneath the central steps with only its gills moving. Farther up, among the flat crowns, water birds dive from the surface and disappear briefly into the brightness before plunging down again.

The town is loud. Water carries the scraping of snails across stone, the clatter of crayfish in the channels, and a constant low drumming made by fish calling beneath the shelf. When wind moves across the lake above, the plant crowns rub together with a sound like heavy rain on a roof. These noises overlap without hurrying anything. A leaf slowly uncurls against a doorway; silt settles on a handrail; a fish holds its place in the faint current. Above the shelf, the lake’s surface shifts and flashes, while the streets below remain in their cool green light.
