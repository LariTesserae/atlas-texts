Behind a maintenance panel high in the city, there is a hollow no larger than a room.

The panel opens onto a shelf of black stone, newly exposed where a bore cut through the mountain. Its corners are still sharp. Pale veins run through it, and droplets gather along them before falling into a shallow basin in the floor. The basin has no visible outlet, but it never fills: water passes through a seam at one edge and returns, after a few minutes, as beads on the ceiling.

The air is cold enough to sting the nose. Beyond the hollow, the city climbs in terraces around the mountain’s upper slopes. Transit lines thread between towers, and heat exchangers comb moisture from the thin air. Most of their work is silent. Here, through the open panel, it can be heard as an occasional low pulse in the stone.

Light reaches the hollow through a narrow shaft fitted with clear, dustless panes. During the day it shifts across the basin and catches the fresh facets of the rock. At night, the city supplies a softer light: a strip set into the shaft brightens when maintenance traffic passes, then fades again.

There are no plants. Nothing green grows in the basin or along its wet rim. A fine grey deposit marks the highest reach of the water, and tiny crystals form there each time the level rises and falls. Some loosen and slide into the basin; others grow around grit carried in on a worker’s boot.

Once in a while, a small pale arthropod emerges from the seam. It crosses the wet stone on long legs, pauses beneath a droplet, and retreats if the panel is opened. There are few enough that a worker might visit for years without seeing one.

Water is always moving, though slowly. Condensation thickens overhead; a drop lengthens, trembles, and falls. A thin skin of ice forms at the basin’s edge, breaks when the level changes, and is drawn into the seam. Above it all, the city regulates its warmth, carries its people, and bears down on the mountain. Inside the panel, the hollow keeps its own cold, and the next drop begins to gather.
