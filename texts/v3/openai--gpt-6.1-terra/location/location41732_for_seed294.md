The plateau lies high beneath a cold sea, its broadest ridges still far below the surface. Dim light comes down through the water in long, wavering bands, enough to show the shape of the ground but not its color. The distances between ridges fill with blue-gray shadow.

Everything under the water was made by animals. The plateau is a mass of old shells and their fillings, laid down over such a length of time that the separate generations are visible only along fresh breaks. No bedrock shows. The oldest surfaces have been rubbed almost level; they roll gently for miles, with shallow basins where water gathers and turns. Here and there a pale escarpment rises, its edge worn into a blunt lip.

The water is never still. Currents meet over the plateau and fold down into the basins, lifting clouds of fine shell dust. They strip loose material from one slope and lay it against another. In the narrows between ridges, the flow can bend a passing shoal into a crescent; a moment later the fish break apart and scatter into clearer water. Farther out, the same current barely seems to move until a veil of sediment begins to travel across the floor.

Life is plentiful but widely spread. Small silver animals pass in schools above the ridges. Soft, translucent grazers cling to the shell surfaces, and long-bodied predators wait in the sheltered hollows. On the open flats, colonies of shelled creatures build low walls around themselves, adding a little chalky material while the water takes a little away. Many walls are abandoned, their makers gone, their openings rounded and widened by centuries of current. Others are crowded with living bodies, opening and closing in the flow.

There are no leaves or fronds to mark the passing water. Its movement appears in the drifting dust, the flicker of a shoal, the sudden rush around a ridge. The sound is subdued: a low wash, an occasional clicking from a colony, shell fragments tapping briefly together before they settle. Above it all the sea continues to churn, while the old plateau holds its shape, losing its edges and being built over again.
