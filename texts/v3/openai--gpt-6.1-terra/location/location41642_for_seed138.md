The place is a pumping yard no larger than a city block, enclosed by factory walls and the dark undersides of elevated tanks. Beyond it, the town continues, but here the view ends at brick, pipework and a pair of corrugated gates. The ground is almost level with the canal on the other side. Water stands in the seams between concrete slabs and gathers in shallow basins around the machinery.

It is hot even in the shade. The yard is roofed in places by gantries and broad ducts, and the light that reaches the floor is weak and brown, reflected from wet walls. Moisture beads on the cold-water pipes and falls from them at a steady rate. The concrete is comparatively new: its corners are square, its drainage channels clean-edged, its surfaces only lightly stained. Where the slabs meet the brick, thin black lines of water show how near the groundwater lies.

Three pumps stand far apart on raised plinths. They are boxy, painted green, and fitted with heavy bolted covers. Their motors produce a continuous low roar, crossed by the sharper clatter of a loose guard and the hiss of water passing through valves. In the narrow spaces beside the buildings the sound becomes almost solid. A person speaking must stand close; a dropped tool can go unheard.

There is little vegetation. A few stiff grasses grow beside the canal gate, and pale moss has taken hold in a groove that never dries. Most of the open ground is bare concrete or shallow water. Life gathers in the wet margins instead: small fish hold in the drainage channels, frogs shelter beneath the plinths, and insects crowd the warm surfaces of the lamps. Geckos wait above them on the walls. Pigeons nest along a high beam, and now and then one comes down to drink from a puddle vibrating with the engines.

The pumps run at a nearly constant rate, lifting canal water through the yard to the factories behind it. Water enters, water leaves, and the puddles remain. Workers cross the yard to check gauges and grease bearings, then disappear through the steel doors. The gates stay shut except for deliveries. At any hour the same lights are on, the same motors are audible, and the same damp heat occupies the space between them.
