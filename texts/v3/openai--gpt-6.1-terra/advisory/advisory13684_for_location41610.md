Prepare for a cool, quiet working town, not a wilderness campsite or an empty ruin. It is small enough to see across, but the pale growth beyond its rim may make the route out harder to follow than the view in suggests.

**Bring the essentials with you.** There are no visible fields or obvious shops, so carry drinking water, food, any medicines you need, and a light layer against the cool air. A compact rain shell would be useful even if most showers pass the basin by. Ask before counting on a house for lodging or a meal.

Wear sturdy, flexible shoes with good grip. The road is springy rather than hard, and the ground is alive; a walking stick may help you judge your footing, but avoid digging its point into the mat. Bring a small light for the evening, when the works closes, and a bag for anything you would otherwise drop. A washer can disappear into the ground slowly enough that you might not notice it being taken.

**Arrange a reliable way back.** Before entering the basin, note the gap in the bank and the appearance of the surrounding stems. If your means of travel requires a fixed return point, choose one you can find again without cutting or marking the vegetation. Don’t wander into the growth beyond the rim without local guidance.

Once in town, use the existing paths and ask the maintainers where visitors may go. The low open spaces are deliberately left alone; treat them as part of the town rather than vacant ground. Don’t cut shoots, pull leaves from damp seams, or fasten equipment to walls and frames. The buildings are both structures and living things, maintained by people who know how much growth to remove.

The works is active, though it may sound almost silent from outside. Stay clear of belts, rollers, the lifting roof, and the spools unless invited closer. If you arrive in the morning, you may be able to watch the roof open; by evening the machinery stops and the town becomes quieter still.

Expect to move slowly and pay attention. Changes here happen at the edge of perception: a roof turning toward the light, leaves opening around damp iron, a crack narrowing in the square. Give yourself time to sit somewhere permitted and watch. Leave with everything you brought, and check your clothes and gear for hitchhiking insects or plant material before traveling onward.
