Prepare for this as a visit to a **flooded industrial enclosure**, not a garden with a pool in it. There is no breathable air in the chamber, and the water outside is deeper still. The bright lamps may make it feel like an afternoon grove, but the nearest safe place to breathe could be a long way away.

### Before entering

Find out who built the chamber, who maintains it, and whether visitors are permitted. Ask for a current plan of the mountain, an entry procedure, the water temperature and pressure, and the location of any functioning locks or refuge spaces. The painted depth marks may be legible, but they are not a substitute for knowing the pressure at the entrance and the route back out.

Have the water tested remotely. Its warmth is encouraging, but the mineral feed, pollen, plant compounds and machinery could make it irritating or toxic to skin and eyes. Check visibility, current, electrical conditions and whether the lamps or machinery are sensitive to a diver’s presence. A camera or small inspection vehicle sent ahead would also reveal whether the foliage has blocked the intended route since the last inspection.

An ordinary recreational diver should not attempt this alone. If the chamber is reached underwater, arrange a professional dive team experienced in overhead environments, with surface support, redundant breathing gas, a continuous guide line and a decompression plan appropriate to the actual depth and duration. Depending on the pressure and access route, the safe choice may be a remotely operated visit rather than a human one. Do not assume that a sealed door can be opened simply because it is visible; operating a pressure boundary requires an established procedure and an authorized team.

### What to bring

Wear exposure protection suited to the measured water temperature and chemistry, with gloves and a full-face mask or helmet if the water warrants them. Carry independent lights even though the chamber is illuminated: a power failure could turn the crowded grove into a dark maze. Use secured, low-profile equipment that will not snag on stems, rails or grilles, and a line system you can follow by touch if disturbed pollen or sediment clouds the water.

Bring a camera and slate or reliable underwater communications. Keep your hands free as much as possible. Agree in advance on a modest destination and turnaround point; crossing the entire chamber is not worth pushing through growth whose density you could not judge from outside.

Clean equipment before and after the visit according to the site’s biological protocols. Everything in the room may be manufactured, but the plants are living, and the description gives no reason to assume they can tolerate foreign organisms, oils or careless contact. Do not collect flowers, pollen or numbered stones without permission.

### Inside

Move slowly and maintain control of your buoyancy. The plants nearly fill the available space, and stems growing between floor plates may conceal loose screws, bent grilles and uneven footing. Avoid brushing the moss from gauges or handrails just to read them; if a gauge must be inspected, leave that to the maintenance team.

Keep clear of electrical fittings, valves and moving machinery. The faint note in your teeth and the occasional relay click are part of the machinery’s presence, not necessarily signs of trouble. A change in that note, a new current or falling debris, however, would be good reasons to turn back and report what you observed.

The chair is an inviting landmark, but there is no need to sit in it or clear the ferns from it. Its purpose is unknown. Treat the glass-covered **DRAIN** lever as a control with potentially chamber-wide consequences: do not break the glass, move the lever, or pull away the roots pressing against it.

Most of all, allow yourself time to look without needing to penetrate every part of the grove. You may see a white pollen cloud travel only to the next leaf, or a bubble hanging at a joint under the lamps. In a room with nothing swimming, your own movement will be conspicuous. The gentlest visit is probably one in which the leaves settle soon after you pass.
