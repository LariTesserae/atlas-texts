Prepare for a warm, wet industrial city that is much larger than it first appears. The distances, constant noise and presence of animals will shape your visit as much as the weather.

### Before you enter

Confirm that the air, water, local microorganisms and food are compatible with human biology. The basin’s industrial haze is plainly visible, but its composition is unknown; arrange a suitable respirator and filters based on local measurements rather than assuming an ordinary dust mask will suffice. Bring any medicines you need and find out where human-compatible medical care is available.

Check entry requirements, language and payment arrangements. If possible, make contact with a local guide or host before arrival. Ask about animal behavior, restricted industrial areas, herd crossings and the safety of the upper quarry terraces. The city appears inhabited and organized, so local practice should be a better guide than treating every creature or industrial installation as an unfamiliar threat.

Travel with a reliable way home and agree on a rendezvous or extraction point. A landmark visible across the basin may be hours away by road or rail.

### What to bring

- **Light, breathable waterproof clothing** and a change of dry clothes. The rain is gentle but persistent, and warmth does not make prolonged dampness comfortable.
- **Waterproof boots with good grip.** Wet stone, mineral crusts, dark films and metal walkways may all be slippery.
- **Hearing protection.** Machinery, animal calls and echoes continue for long periods. Carry earplugs even if you do not expect any single sound to be painfully loud.
- **A respirator appropriate to the measured hazards**, plus sealed eye protection if local workers recommend it.
- **Safe drinking water or a locally approved treatment method.** Clear water emerging from a wall is not necessarily safe to drink.
- **A lamp, charged navigation device, offline map and power bank.** Mist can obscure routes, and service passages should not be assumed safe or open to visitors.
- **A small first-aid kit**, including supplies for cleaning cuts, which can be troublesome in a persistently wet environment.
- **Food for the journey**, unless you have confirmed that local food is safe for humans. The absence of familiar vegetation is reason to check, not proof that provisions are unavailable.

Pack equipment so it stays dry but is easy to reach. Avoid strongly scented products and ask your host whether any colors, sounds or materials are known to disturb local animals.

### Moving through the city

Use established public routes and follow the lead of residents. Walkways may exist because the street below is wet, because animals pass there, or because it serves a working industrial route. Trams and trains are likely to be more practical than walking between distant districts, but allow generous time: even a journey visible from the terraces may cover a considerable distance.

Give herds and nesting colonies room. Do not feed, touch or crowd the animals, and do not position yourself between an animal and an exit or its young. A creature accustomed to people can still injure someone unintentionally, especially on a narrow landing. If a herd occupies your route, wait where locals wait rather than trying to push through.

Stay out of drains, reservoirs, excavations and factory yards unless explicitly invited and equipped. Water levels, moving machinery, fragile crusts and working freight traffic may be difficult to judge. At the quarry terraces, use maintained paths and keep back from edges; runoff has worn the stone smooth, and the scale can make a drop deceptively hard to read.

Protect the local ecology as well as yourself. Clean your footwear and equipment as required before entering and before leaving, and follow local guidance about waste and biological contamination.

### Choosing where to stay

Look for accommodation with a dry room, reliable ventilation, safe water and a route to transport. Ask whether it is near a foundry, reservoir colony or established herd route: all might be memorable, but perhaps less welcome immediately outside your window. Earplugs and a way to dry your boots overnight will matter.

Plan your first day lightly. The city’s sound may settle into the background, but that does not mean it cannot tire you. The haze, wet footing and unfamiliar traffic also demand attention.

If conditions are clear enough, visit an accessible upper terrace late in the day. Bring a layer for the cooler descending air and leave yourself a straightforward route back before mist thickens. From there, you can watch rain move across one part of the basin while another remains bright, and see the lights appear through the haze—without needing to cross the whole immense city to appreciate its scale.
