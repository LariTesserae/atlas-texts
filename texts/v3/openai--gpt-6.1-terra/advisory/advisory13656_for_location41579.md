### Before you arrive

### What to bring

- **Light rain protection:** a breathable rain jacket or poncho, rather than a heavy coat. An umbrella may be awkward in narrow, plant-filled streets.
- **Shoes with good grip:** expect slick stone, moss, puddles, and raised pavement. Shoes that dry reasonably quickly will be more comfortable than heavy, waterlogged boots.
- **Dry bags or waterproof pouches** for documents, electronics, medication, and a change of socks. Persistent damp can reach things even when the rain is light.
- **Drinking water and a small light:** do not assume canal or rainwater is safe to drink. Streets may become dim beneath the canopy before the open ground above the hollow does.
- **Hearing protection** if you plan to visit the mill or spend much time close to its machinery. Ask before entering any work area.

Dress for warmth and humidity, with an easy layer for rain. Insect repellent may be useful, though the description does not tell us whether biting insects are a problem.

### Visiting comfortably

Expect the sound to be continuous rather than overwhelming everywhere: belts and rollers from the mill, water under pressure, rain on metal roofs, and voices raised enough to carry between walls. If you need directions, get someone’s attention and speak clearly; conversation beside the machinery may be difficult.

Allow time for the place to reveal itself. In the afternoon, the streets receive patches of light rather than full sun. By evening, the mill windows and wet leaves may be the brightest landmarks. If you stay after dark, keep your light handy and remember that the route that looked obvious from the road may feel different under the canopy.

Above all, approach it as someone’s home and workplace. The appeal of the town lies partly in its careful balance: gutters cleared, walls repaired, doorways cut open through growth. Move without disturbing that work, and ask before entering buildings or photographing people at it.
