The power first became aware of the town through the note in the stone. It followed the note down to the shelf and found that it did not end there. It passed through the clear animals and the pale lines, through the rooms and the spaces held thin between walls, and through the inhabitants, who altered it by attending to it.

The power remained at a distance while it learned what it could. This took longer than it expected. It had known bodies made of water before, but not these bodies, nor the particular ways in which they shared their thoughts. At first it mistook an answer held in the basin for an old remnant of construction. Later it understood that the answer had been made for someone who had not yet asked.

It also found a change taking place beneath the shelf. One of the broken faces of the cliff extended far into the rock. Along it, two great pieces were settling against one another. The movement was too slow to disturb the water, but in time it would change the note. The lines in the western part of town would no longer carry their alterations as they did now. One chamber, folded into a wall, would be drawn apart.

The chamber was occupied.

The power considered holding the pieces of rock still. It could have done so without touching the town, and no inhabitant need ever have known. But the note would have remained unchanged as a result. The power could not tell how much of the inhabitants’ lives depended upon changes it had not yet understood.

Instead, it made a small pressure at the edge of the open court, where the inhabitants sometimes met. It took care not to imitate any alteration passing along the lines. The pressure lasted a little while, then withdrew.

Several years later, the water at the court’s edge became distinct. Its boundary approached the place where the pressure had been and waited there.

The power made the pressure again, more gently.

The boundary changed. It moved through a series of shapes, some so slight that the power had to attend closely to distinguish them. After a time it understood enough to answer. It answered badly at first, pressing where it meant to release, and once caused the boundary to close altogether. It waited until the inhabitant returned.

There were others listening through the pale lines. They helped to make a place where the power’s unfamiliar pressures could be received without entering anyone’s body. They used a vacant room, enlarging it until the power could make a representation of the shelf within it. The representation was crude: rock, fault, chamber, and the slow movement between them.

The inhabitants examined it. They found errors. The power corrected those it could.

What they discussed afterward took a long time. The power could feel alterations passing to the basin and back, sometimes years apart. It did not follow them. At one point the inhabitant of the threatened chamber opened a narrow boundary into the vacant room and held it there while the others were present. The power sensed a complexity in that boundary it had not noticed before.

When the inhabitants answered, they did not ask for the fault to be held still. They asked whether its movement could be slowed, and whether the power could show them the places where the stone was already beginning to separate.

It could.

For a long time afterward, the western cliffs held marks visible only under certain pressures. The inhabitants followed them into the rock. They moved several pale lines, leaving their old courses faintly visible in the streets, and made another entrance to the occupied chamber. They changed the chamber itself, though not all at once. Its inhabitant remained there throughout, extended into the wall on either side of the work.

The animal near the cliff had settled directly above one of the marks. The inhabitants left that part of the rock until it moved, which it did after a considerable interval. It crossed a distance shorter than its own body and fixed its limbs again. When the work reached its old place, the transparent ridge it had left behind was lifted in one piece and set beside it. Some of the shed sections came loose. The animal took no notice.

At last the inhabitants sent a change along the lines to the open court. They were ready for the rock to move at its former pace.

The power released it gradually. The note deepened by a fraction. Along the basin’s rim, the fragments of clear shell caught the light at slightly different angles. In the altered chamber, the inhabitant held its boundary against the wall and remained intact.

The power stayed nearby for a while, in case it had misunderstood. The town gave it a place to wait: a small extension of the open court, with no line leading into it. From there it could perceive the note and the pressure of the surrounding sea, but nothing the inhabitants wished to keep among themselves.

In time the power withdrew. The place remained. On occasion, an inhabitant crossed the court and rested at its edge, and the water there held a shape for a while before becoming part of the ocean again.
