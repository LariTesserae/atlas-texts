The power came first to the rim and remained there through a change of weather. It could have stood in the square without displacing the air, but from the rim it could feel the whole hollow at once: the deep water, the growing seams, the weight of each house borne by stone that had been soft not long before.

One seam interested it. The seam ran beneath a finished street, where there were no markers and no wicker frames. The ground there had hardened unevenly, leaving a thin pocket of water under a row of chambers. In time the pocket would enlarge. The floor of the lowest chamber would sink; a drain would break; water would enter before the household understood what had happened.

The power could have closed the pocket. It could have made all the stone in the hollow sound, and kept the rain from ever overwhelming the drains. For a while it considered the lives that might then be lived without the work of listening for faults. It listened longer. The keepers knew the water intimately, and the unfinished streets were held in memory by many households. The power did not know what else might change if it made the ground still.

At dusk, a keeper came out to clear leaves from a channel near the threatened street. She worked with a slender hooked tool, drawing each blockage onto the paving. When she had finished, the power made a small movement in the stone beneath her feet: three taps, a pause, and three more.

She stood raised for some time. Then she put a forelimb flat against the paving and answered with a question used for finding another worker in the dark.

The power repeated the taps beside the hidden pocket.

The keeper followed them to the edge of the street. There she pressed her limbs to the ground in several places, moving slowly from one to the next. She called another keeper, and together they tested the paving until full dark. They found a difference too faint to be certain of: a slight yielding in the beat returned by the stone.

For the next several days they came back with others. They opened a section of drain and tasted the water in the soil. They struck the paving with mallets and set their limbs against the neighboring walls. At last they uncovered the pocket, braced the chambers above it, and dug a narrow channel to carry its water toward a cistern. The work took most of a season. People from three households helped, including young residents who carried away the wet stone in baskets.

The keepers continued to test the street after the channel was finished. They also returned several times to the place where the taps had first been felt. One evening, two of them laid their forelimbs against the paving and waited.

The power tapped again. It tried the question the first keeper had used.

Their answer came quickly, though the power did not yet understand it. They repeated it in a different rhythm, then brought a third resident, who tapped more slowly. The power made mistakes. Once, attempting to ask about the cistern, it gave the name of a household that had ceased to occupy the street many years before. Those who felt the message passed it on, and several residents came up to examine the paving. They remained there a long time, clicking to one another.

In the seasons that followed, the power learned enough to tell them where it had found another weak place. This time they checked it against what their keepers knew, and left it alone: the water there was feeding a seam they had already framed. The power attended while they explained, through many repetitions, how they expected the seam to rise and where its water might go. It had felt those movements but had not known their plan for them.

After that, it asked before altering anything. Sometimes a household requested help with a weight too great to lift or a wall in danger of falling before it could be braced. The power held stone steady while wicker was set beneath it. It lifted a slab once, and kept it raised through the night while a damaged drain was rebuilt. At other times the residents thanked it and continued their work themselves.

On still days it joined them in the square. It had learned to give its voice a place on the paving, so that a resident who wished to speak could stand near and feel the reply. When the wind returned and the gutters began their long call, conversation grew difficult. The power could have quieted them, but the residents were already gathering their tools, making use of the last clear moments before going below. It let the wind sound.

Much later, beside the street where the water pocket had been found, a new wall rose from a framed seam. The household nearest it kept a record incised on a slab: the shape of the wall, the course of the drain beneath, and the repairs that had made the street safe. At one edge they added a small group of marks for the taps that had led them there.

As the years passed, the wall dried and took its place among the older walls. Moss grew over the bottom of the record slab. From time to time someone cleared it away, so that the marks could still be felt.
