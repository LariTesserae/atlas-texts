The power became aware of the chamber by degrees.

It noticed the red pulse first, passing beneath the split stone. Then the pale fronds, each bent a little differently by the light it had received. Then the moss, and the water held between its threads, and the lives working slowly in the dark beneath it.

It did not enter all at once. There was very little room.

For a long time it watched the cycles of cold. It followed each droplet from the ceiling to the basin or the moss, and found that in some years almost none arrived. It watched the rosettes crowd around the fitting. It watched old fronds fall, and the fine patient work that returned them to the bed.

Deeper in the rock, the sealed device was wearing out. It had lasted far beyond the span intended for it. A small flaw had developed where two of its parts met, and each time the light passed along the band, the flaw grew a little.

The power mended it. The next pulse came at its usual time.

It did not make the device last forever. Instead, it made a second path for the energy, so that when one part failed, another could take up its work. It left a way to mend the new parts, too.

Then it attended to the water. It opened no stream through the chamber; a stream would have torn the moss from its hollows and drowned the bed beneath. Far above, where a seam in the rock carried moisture past the cavern, it made a channel no wider than a hair. In the coldest seasons, one more droplet would find its way down.

The droplet gathered slowly. When it fell, it landed at the edge of a moss pad and vanished.

The power stayed to see what happened. A fern root found the dampness. The basin acquired a little more ice. Some of the moss spread across a patch of gravel, and some of the fern stems there had less room to grow. In time, the power narrowed the new channel.

It could have filled the chamber with warmth and brightness. It could have raised the ceiling and made space for forests. But these plants had grown here, among these stones, under this passing light. The power wished to give them a better chance to go on, not replace them with something it preferred.

When it withdrew, it left its attention resting lightly on the device, the seam, and the chamber below. If the stone shifted or the light faltered, it would know.

The red pulse moved along the band. A fern continued its slow turn toward it, while beneath the living moss an old frond gave itself, little by little, to the roots.
