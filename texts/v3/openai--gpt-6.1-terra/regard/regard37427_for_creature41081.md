The power attends to the two animals for a long time.

The broken feeler does not cause the first animal to turn in circles or miss its food. It touches the ground with the others, and when a root changes the shape of the space beneath it, the animal lifts its head. The second animal does the same. Their movements are close enough in time to be difficult to separate, but the sound of the snapping tip reaches each by a different route.

The power could make the feeler whole. It leaves it as it is.

Beneath the bare patch, a root is growing into a fracture in the glass. It has entered a narrow place and begun to widen it. The white bodies inside move away from the advancing tip, feeding as they go on the films it leaves. In time the fracture will open through the ridge, and a thin blade of glass will settle across the bare ground. The animal with the broken feeler will be beneath it.

There are intervals in which the animal might move. The power follows several of them. In some, it does. In others it remains beside the leaf impression until the blade comes down.

The root is already exchanging instructions with the plants around it. The power does not know, at first, how much of that exchange concerns the fracture. It waits through a small change in the ground, then another. It finds the part by which one root makes room for its neighbor, and there it offers a change of its own: a little more space beside the fracture, enough for the growing tip to turn.

The root enters it. For a while the surrounding plants make no visible adjustment. Then the gap beneath the black leaves lengthens, and a copper-centered seed, suspended there since before the root began to grow, descends a little. The root turns toward it.

The blade remains where it is. The white bodies continue along their cracks.

The animal on the bare patch lifts its head. Its clear feelers rest against the ground, all but the broken one. After a time it crosses the patch and disappears beneath a cushion. The second animal is still among the blue needles when the first settles; it has found a tip to eat.

The power watches the seed. Its root arrives, and the transparent coat opens. Two crowns rise at a distance from one another, joined below by the root. An animal comes to rest near one crown. At the other there is no animal, but a silver passage advances through a thick leaf, following a copper vein.

The power remains until the little creature inside reaches the leaf’s edge and continues into another. Then, having made room where it was needed, it leaves the rest to grow.
