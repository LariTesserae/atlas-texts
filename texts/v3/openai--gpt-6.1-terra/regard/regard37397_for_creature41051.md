The power came to the chamber without a light.

It did not need one. It could feel the slight differences in warmth across the paving, the pressure of each shell against the stone, the fine movements of water through the joints. It felt a young keeper beneath its parent, working a particle loose from the soft skin there. It felt the parent shift one limb to make room.

For a long time the power remained still. The creatures went on feeding. A snail crossed the back of a keeper and reached the stone beyond it. In the passage, particles continued to descend.

The power followed them upward.

The passage made several turns. At one of them, a slab had settled against the wall, leaving a gap through which the water passed. Above the slab was a broad space full of loose stone. Water entered it through fissures and brought with it the remains of things that lived higher on the plateau. Much of this material lodged among the stones. Small animals worked at it there, and what they loosened traveled down to the chamber.

The slab would settle farther. Not yet, and not quickly: a grain at a time would wash from beneath it, and the weight above would move into the space left behind. The gap would narrow. The animals in the chamber would receive less food, then very little. Some would go up the passage. Some would lay fewer eggs. Most would remain where they were until they could no longer live there.

The power could have lifted the slab. It could have opened the passage wide enough for the keepers to follow it upward. It could have filled the chamber with food, or brought back the people who had laid its stones and asked them what they had meant it to hold.

Instead, it attended to the places beneath the slab where grains were being carried away. It pressed a few loose stones together and fitted them into the hollow. The water still moved through, but it no longer took the weight-bearing grains with it.

This work disturbed some silt. A cloud traveled down the passage and into the chamber. The crustaceans gathered more tightly at its mouth, and the worms along the nearest seam drew in their crowns. A keeper raised its front edge, tasted the water, and settled again. By the time the silt had begun to clear, the crowns were emerging.

The power examined its work. The support would last a long time, though not forever. Water would find other ways through the loose stone; the chamber would change as it had changed before. The creatures above the slab still had food, and food still came down to those below.

There was one other thing the power did.

A young keeper had become lodged between two blocks near the wall. It had entered the gap when its back was thinner, following the taste of the film, and could not turn or draw itself out. For days it had pressed its limbs against the stone whenever it had strength enough. Its parent was elsewhere by then, leaving a succession of clean impressions across the floor.

The power eased one block aside. The keeper backed out slowly, its limbs unsteady, and rested against a ridge. The block settled into place again, with a little more room along that part of the joint. Two worms extended their crowns into the widened space.

The power stayed until the young keeper moved. It advanced a short distance, stopped to feed, and then went on toward a shallow hollow. By the time it settled there, the water above the paving was almost clear.

Nothing in the chamber marked the power’s departure. The keeper’s pale back gathered silt. Along the wall, a snail reached the rim of an old gate socket and continued across it, its mouth working steadily over the stone.
