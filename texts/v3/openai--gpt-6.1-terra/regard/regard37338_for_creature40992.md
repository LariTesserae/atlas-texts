The power came upon the hollow while a termite was repairing the edge of a wall. It watched her fit a grain of earth into place, turn, and go back for another.

Beyond the roots, the weather was changing. The next rain would pass to the east, and several hot days would follow it. The moss would shrink against the root. The beads in the cool creases would become scarce.

The power could have filled the hollow with water. Instead, it lifted a broad fallen leaf from the forest floor and set it against the outside of the tree, where it shaded one of the narrow seams. The leaf settled without blocking it. Green light still reached the fern, and air could still pass through.

For a few days the shaded crease stayed cool enough to gather moisture. Mites found their way to it. The snail remained in its shell until evening, then came out and left a silver trace across the pale earth. The termites continued carrying grains; one used a speck from the edge of the snail’s trail.

The frog caught another insect. The spider waited, and at last something touched its leaf.

When the heat passed, rain loosened the fallen leaf and carried it a little way down the slope. Light returned through the seam. By then the new red root had lengthened, and a termite wall stood where there had been open ground.

The power stayed long enough to see the workers find a way around it.
