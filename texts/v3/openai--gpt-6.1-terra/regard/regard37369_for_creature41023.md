The power came first to the seam.

It followed the water down past the last place where the shaft light reached, through passages that opened and narrowed with the stone. It found the folded film on which the animals fed, and the eggs beneath an overhang, and a young one cleaning a grain from its foot. It found, too, a body caught in a slow current, already being taken apart by smaller lives.

For a long time it did nothing. It watched the water rise and retreat, and learned how much of the cold belonged to the mountain and how much came from the city above.

Then, in the city’s maintenance records, a bore inspection was scheduled. A worker would find the basin, note the unexplained water, and recommend that the seam be sealed. The water would still have somewhere to go, but the pressure below would change. Some of the cracks would dry. Others would fill and stay filled.

The power could have closed the records, or turned the worker away. Instead, when the panel was opened, it held the air still for a moment longer than it otherwise would have been.

The worker’s light found an arthropod at the basin’s edge. It stood there with its legs spread, then moved across the wet stone and vanished into the seam.

The worker waited. After a while, another appeared.

Samples were taken: a little water, a trace of the film, an empty skin lodged against a grain of grit. The hollow was entered in a different part of the records. People came who knew about such small animals and people who knew about water in rock. They found that the wet cracks extended well beyond the bore and that sealing the visible seam might force water into places where it was less welcome.

A cover was fitted over the panel to reduce the movement of air. A narrow window was left in it, with a light that could be switched off. The maintenance instructions were amended: no sealant, no cleaning agents, report changes in the basin level. Once a year someone checked that the cover was sound. In time, the hollow acquired a name on a map, though the name was seldom used.

The power stayed through one winter and part of the next. It watched the basin after the cover was fitted and saw that the droplets still gathered. It watched the film grow back where the samples had been taken. Beneath the overhang, the clear cases swelled; the young emerged and remained near the place where they had hatched.

When a worker’s boot knocked grit onto the shelf, the power let it lie. When one of the young failed to shed its skin, it left the small body to the others. The water passed through the seam. Ice formed and broke. Above, the city’s pulses continued, and the animals paused for some of them.

At last the power withdrew its attention from the hollow, though the hollow remained easy to find.

The window in the cover grew faintly dusty. Behind it, a drop gathered on a pale vein, lengthened, and fell into the basin. An arthropod came out of the seam after the water and crossed the stone to drink.
