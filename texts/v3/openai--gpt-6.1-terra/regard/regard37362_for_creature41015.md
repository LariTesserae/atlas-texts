The power came first as attention.

For a long while it did nothing that could be seen. It followed the warm water from the pump, along the pipes and out among the leaves. It waited for the mark on the flywheel to return. It watched the white fish turn into the current and settle again.

The fish entered the workshop and rested against the tool rack. Above it, a loose leaf passed between two dark panes of glass. It caught for a moment on a bracket, then came free and went on.

At the outer wall, the shelled animal withdrew the threads it had placed in the crack of the valve housing. A little mineral silt emerged. The threads came out again, touched it, and closed.

The crack had lengthened since the previous turning of the seasons. Inside it, iron had softened almost through. One day the housing would give way, and the warm water would escape beneath the bank faster than the pump could draw it back.

The power considered the valve wheel. The shell had grown around its spoke; to turn the wheel would tear the animal loose. It considered the pump, the pressure engines, and the channels through the roots. It waited until it understood the paths by which water and food reached each part of the settlement.

Then, below the valve, where the pipe passed through stone, a second passage began to form. Grain by grain the stone shifted, leaving a narrow bore around the old housing. Fine sediment came out of it and drifted over the roots. The shelled animal caught some on its threads.

The bore widened slowly. Water entered it and made a faint new current along the wall. The fish came out from beneath the pump, felt the change, and followed it to the bank. After a time it returned to its usual place.

As more water passed through the new channel, less pressed against the crack. The shell remained fast to the wheel. Its feeding threads opened in the current from the bore.

Only when the old housing carried almost no pressure did the power close the broken section. It fitted sound stone around the pipe and left a small opening beneath the shell, so that water could still reach the animal’s threads. Later it made a second channel beside the first, against the day when roots or silt might close one of them.

The pump continued to turn. Warm water rose through the basin, and the copper-colored animals opened around the buried pipes. Their neighbors opened later, as the warmth reached them.

The power stayed to see what followed. Roots grew into the new channels; one narrowed, while the other carried a little more water. A film formed on the stone inside the bore. Some of its offspring were swept into the streets, where a few found dark seams in which to grow.

The leaf that had passed the workshop lodged at last in the root bed. Its tissue clouded. Small animals fed on it, and its strands caught silt. Much later, a shoot pushed up through the mound. Its first leaf unfolded in the warm current, thin enough for the light from a caged lamp to show through it.
