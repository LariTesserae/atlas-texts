The power begins by listening.

It has heard valleys before, but does not assume this one is calling. It listens to the roar and the higher note, to the small movements within the cushions, to the opening and closing of mouths against stone. It listens long enough to notice that the bone-collectors sometimes turn from one seam to another, and that some of the frost-filled buds once held eggs.

Then it comes close to an animal resting beside a wall.

The animal has carried a fragment almost as long as its body. Its sides are moving quickly. When the power draws near, it presses itself flat among the stones, and the power goes no closer.

Instead it makes the fragment a little lighter.

The animal takes it up again. It travels along the wall, pauses, and sets the fragment down against a gap. A short while later it returns to the gap, noses the fragment aside and goes through.

The power leaves the weight of the other stones alone.

It follows a bone-collector over a plate where the distance has been uncertain. The animal reaches the edge and disappears; the power finds it beneath a cushion some way off, with a pale object still in its mouth. It has not been hurt. It puts the object down and begins to feed on the damp leaves.

Later, another crosses that plate toward the enclosure. The wall remains no nearer after many pauses. Its sides move faster. It puts down the object and rests, then takes it up and continues, and still the wall remains where it was.

The power lays a narrow path through the uncertain distance. Nothing marks it on the stone. The animal walks it with frequent stops and reaches the wall. There it puts down its object, a piece of another animal’s jaw, and lies beside it until its breathing slows.

The path remains. Other paths are made: from a seam to a dry place, from a cushion to a bud, between the shelter of a ledge and the nearest enclosure. They do not run straight unless a creature’s own way is straight. They are only as wide as they need to be, and away from them the plates and pools still keep their old arrangements.

At a cushion the power holds its attention until the tiny creatures within are accustomed to it. There are eggs in the damp chambers and leaves enough for the creatures now living there, but the cushion is badly eaten. Nearby stands a bud full of frost. The power warms one scale by a fraction. A bead forms inside it and runs down to an egg that has been held there a long time.

The egg does not hatch. The power waits and warms another bud, one that still contains living creatures. There, after a long while, an egg changes. A jointed white body emerges and presses its dark mouth against the scale.

The frost is left on the pools. The cushions are given a little more room to grow along their seams.

When the power turns its attention to the sound, the high note alters for the first time. The little animals lift their heads. One drops the stone it was carrying. Another withdraws into a wall and does not come out for some time.

The power restores the note. After a while the first animal picks up its stone again.

It leaves the sound as it is, but makes a hollow beneath an enclosure where the roar is softer. The hollow is dry, and a bone-collector can enter by three gaps between the stones. The first to find it stays only briefly. Later, two lie there apart from one another. One sleeps.

The white animal remains on the ledge.

From one place the power sees it on two ledges, facing inward. From another it sees only one. It does not try to decide which view is true. It goes to the foot of a ridge and waits where the animal can see it.

The flank has a hollow in it. After a time the hollow fills, then appears again.

The power puts a little moisture in a crack below the ledge. It sets no food there; it does not yet know what the animal eats. It waits longer, and the animal remains still, its ears laid along its back.

At last, on one ledge or both, an ear lifts slightly. The power withdraws.

The paths stay open. The little hollow stays dry. The warmed bud holds its living brood, and the cushions spread so slowly that a long time passes before their new edges can be seen.

The power does not leave entirely. From time to time it returns to listen, and to see what has made use of what it gave.
