The power came to the plateau without a body. For a long time it did not make one.

It attended to the sounds in the stone. At first the herds seemed to produce a single continuous pressure, with the louder voices passing through it like weather. The power stayed until it could distinguish the pulse of an animal feeding from the pulse of one resting, and the altered rhythm of a limb under repair from the rhythm of a limb that had never borne weight.

It learned that a silence could mean many things. A young animal, its mouth pressed to stone, sometimes stopped grinding for a season. An adult might cease moving because another had come into contact with its plates. The power did not fill these silences.

After a time, it felt a repeated pressure beneath a shelf of black glass. An animal had fallen there. One of its rear supports had entered a fold in the ground and come down far from the rest of its body. The limb was still attached, but its weight had twisted the living sleeves around the bearing. Each attempt to lift the foot sent a strain through the animal’s back. It had opened two of its sealed pockets; material was flowing toward the damage, but the joint could not settle into a position where new surfaces would hold.

Another animal stood nearby, with its face against the fallen one’s shoulder. Their plates carried a slow exchange of tones. When the fallen animal tried again to lift itself, the other leaned in, but could not reach the misplaced foot.

The power made a place for itself in the ground beneath them. It did so gradually, taking on weight until its presence could be felt through the stone. The standing animal broke contact and turned its head. The fallen one remained still for some years. Then it sent a vibration into the shelf.

The power answered as closely as it could.

The animal sent another pattern. The answer was not quite right. They continued for a long time, while the other animal stood beside them and the shelf grew warm and cool under the ordinary sun. The power learned which tones belonged to the hurt limb, and which accompanied the animal’s efforts to move it. At last it made a small upward pressure beneath the stranded foot, then let the pressure fade.

The fallen animal waited. It sounded a long note through the ground, followed by a pattern the power had come to recognize. The other animal lowered its head again and braced its shoulder against the fallen one.

The power lifted.

It could have unfolded the distance all at once. Instead it raised the foot slowly, allowing the joint to turn within its sleeve. When the animal’s breathing changed, it held the foot where it was. The companion adjusted its stance. A plate along the fallen animal’s flank opened, releasing a spill of hot grit, and the limb moved a little farther.

The work took eleven years. The foot emerged beside the body, on a patch of red stone exposed beneath the glass. The power kept it there while the damaged bearing softened and new tissue grew around it. Through the animal’s hide, fresh metallic rings could be seen forming, dull at first and then bright as they began to take the load.

The companion remained until the fallen animal could stand. Then both rested, touching at the shoulder. The power eased its weight out of the ground. Before it had fully withdrawn, the animal sent a tone through the new bearing. The power felt the tone pass into the shelf and onward across the plateau.

Later, in another part of the herd, an old animal came to rest for the last time. Its companions gathered around it. One leaned against its side until the inner rings had stopped. The power stayed nearby as the body cooled. After some years a young animal began feeding at the edge of a loosened plate. The power felt the small jaw working, and left the plate where it lay.

It remained attentive to the plateau. At times an animal seemed to notice the extra weight beneath the stone and sent a sound toward it. The power would answer, if it could. Sometimes the animal answered in turn; sometimes it went on feeding.

The herds continued across the shelves. Their paths crossed and doubled back through spaces that opened under their feet. The old trenches filled with grit. New ones appeared. In the evenings the smaller animals moved toward the long shade of the larger bodies, while the cooling plates ticked and the grinding carried on through the ground.
