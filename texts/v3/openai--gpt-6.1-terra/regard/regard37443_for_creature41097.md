In the afternoon, beneath a ridge of woody moss, a grazer was trying to turn around.

She had followed a tender root into a narrowing passage. Behind her, part of the roof had settled, pressing her hindquarters against the stone. Each time she pushed forward the passage grew tighter; each time she pushed back, loose stems filled the space she had made. Her family had gone on ahead. Their movements reached her through the mat, growing fainter.

The power that had come to the plateau found her there.

It could have lifted the whole ridge and set her in the open air. The grazer had never been in the open air, however, and the ridge held a chamber full of beetles and a bird sheltering with a damaged wing. So the power attended to the stone beneath her instead.

A flake of it had come loose from the floor long ago and lay tilted under her belly. The power eased it aside. There was a little more room then. The grazer drew one hind foot under herself, pushed, and backed out into the wider passage.

She remained there for a while, breathing quickly. Then she began to groom the grit from her fur.

The power stayed with her until she found her family. They were eating at the edge of a damp basin, where fine new roots had grown between the stones. One of them moved over as she approached, and she put her head down among the others.

Near dusk, a party of people came along the rim of the basin. One walked ahead, probing the mat with a pole. The power saw that the ground beside their route had thinned over a fissure; the roots still held together, but not for much longer. It considered closing the fissure and left it open. Birds nested on its shelves, and the falling water reached it by that way.

Instead, it brought a rain shower up the plateau wall.

The people hurried to a shelter while water gathered in the sedges and ran along the roots. Where the mat was thin, the water pulled it downward. From the shelter entrance the travelers could see the shallow, widening dip. After the shower, one of them crossed to the nearest standing stone and cut a mark into its base.

The power watched them fill their vessels. It watched the grazer’s family move on beneath the shelter floor, raising a seam that passed between the travelers’ feet. As the light faded, moths rose from the wet troughs, and the noise swelled until the people leaned close to speak.

The power did not quiet the plateau. It remained through the evening, attending to the innumerable lives moving over and under its surface, and to each one it could reach.
