The power came to the plateau without making a footprint. There was hardly any place to put one.

It did not at first know whether the stillness was an injury. A jaw held against a shell might have been an agony, or a meal being eaten at a pace for which no visitor had stayed long enough. A column might have been a burden, a dwelling, or the work of several lives. The power attended to the animals until it could tell something of the difference.

The broad creature at the bottom of one column had not chosen its place. It had grown around the weight until the sockets in its head fit the feet above. The animals standing on it were alive, and wanted to remain alive; they knew almost nothing of what held them up.

The hunter with its mouth against the grazer was hungry. The grazer, where it could still feel its softer edge between the hooks, was afraid. The fear had lasted so long that it no longer rose and fell. It was simply there.

The plants were not being eaten by the dark fringes around their leaves. The small animals were keeping the leaves open. The plants supplied them with salts, and in return the animals held the leaves against a tendency to curl. Neither could have continued for long without the other.

The power listened farther, through the folded gray roots. It heard the dry tap, and found that it was a shell settling against a stone. The sound had begun a very long way off. It was still on its way.

There were many such things on the plateau: motions not absent but delayed, lives that had adapted to the delay, and others caught in it. The power could have ended the delay at once. It understood that the columns would fall, the jaws close, the leaves fold, and that whatever had sustained the animals in their stillness might fail them in their sudden movement.

So it began with the bowl.

Three animals sat inside it, over the pale patches made by their feet. The power lifted the bowl and held it in the doorway of the shelter. It gave each animal a little more time than the bowl and the shelter and the plateau around them, enough that an intention could reach a leg.

For a while nothing happened.

Then one animal drew a foot from the clay. It set the foot down again on the same pale patch. Another turned, very slowly, until it faced the doorway. The third remained where it was, its amber light steady.

The power waited. The second animal crossed the lip of the bowl and descended onto the grit. There it paused among its neighbors. The first followed a little later. The third stayed in the bowl even when it, too, was able to move. The power left it there.

Outside, the two animals began taking apart the salts beneath them. The powder in their tracks was soon being fed on by smaller creatures. Their movement was slow enough that the power could see how the ground received it: which grains shifted, which legs found purchase, which neighboring animals needed room. It widened that room by degrees.

The hunter and the grazer required longer. The power placed a seam of loose salts within reach of the hunter, salts it could take in through the plates behind its hooked mouth. It eased the hooks apart without tearing the grazer’s edge. When the grazer was free, it stayed where it was for a time, and then went under a larger shell. The hunter remained beside the seam and fed.

At one leaning column, the power supported the upper animals while the broad one underneath withdrew its legs. The animals above kept their places, feet in sockets, until a piece of black stone was fitted beneath them. Later, one climbed down the stone. The others remained.

The work proceeded unevenly. Some places changed while others remained as they had been. When a root needed the ring of animals around its leaf, the power left the ring intact. Where a broken column lay scattered, it gave each member room to right itself, if it could. It made no water. It carried away no animal to a greener country.

At the shelters, the power found the scratches made by human hands. It examined the drawings of the grooved shell and the hands reaching toward it. It could see the marks left by the makers’ tools, and where a hand had rested against the stone while another drew. It could not tell from the drawings whether the people had left willingly, or whether they had found what they came to learn.

It did not call them back. It repaired the cracked bowl with filament cord, threading the old holes. Beside each doorway it cleared a narrow place in the animal cover, so that a visitor could kneel without crushing anything. It left the flint and the sorted shells where it had found them.

The black island remained on the horizon. The power followed its image from one shelter to another and found that the plateau had a way of folding the distances between them. The folding was old, and many things had come to depend on it, including the roots beneath the grit. There was no need to undo it. The power made one addition: a person who walked toward a shelter and wished to reach it would arrive.

Much later, a sound came into one of the stone rings. It was a dry tap, followed by another. A small animal had crossed a patch of grit and struck its shell against the lowest stone.

In the repaired bowl, the third animal lifted a foot. It held the foot above its pale patch for a long time before putting it down again.
