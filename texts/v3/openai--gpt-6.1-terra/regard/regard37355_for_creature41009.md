The power came at the end of a long interval without rain.

The basin was still damp, but its pools had drawn apart. In one of them, a parent lay beneath the reflection of a tree whose leaves had fallen two days before. Four young held to the underside of its fringe. The water covered them, though the parent’s back showed through the surface whenever a faint movement passed over it.

From there, a seam led toward a deeper pool. It had dried halfway across. The parent had traveled as far as the damp earth, then turned back.

The power felt the cool water under the stone and the warmth stored above it. It felt the mites among the moss, the larvae in the plant cups, the fine roots entering each widening crack. There was life in the dry seam, too, too small to be seen from the rim.

It waited until evening, when the stone began to give up its heat. Then it lifted one rounded hump by less than the width of a leaf.

A little water ran out beneath it. The first trickle darkened the earth and stopped. Another followed, gathering grains as it went. By morning a narrow thread had reached the parent’s pool.

The parent moved into it, taking the young along. At the place where the seam bent around a root, it rested for most of the day. Sediment settled against its fringe. One of the young let go and remained beside it until the parent moved again; then it clung beneath the fringe once more.

They reached the deeper pool after dark.

The lifted stone made a new path for the water. Over the following days it carried some inhabitants of the moss into the pools. Others remained where they were, and some were lost. A root grew toward the dampness and, in time, made the opening wider.

The power stayed through the first rain. It watched the drops strike the stone, saw the pools fill and join, and found the parent beneath a reflection that showed a tree in yesterday’s light. The four young were still there, with a little more space between them.

When the rain stopped, the power went away. The water continued through the channel it had opened, slowly wearing the edge of the stone.
