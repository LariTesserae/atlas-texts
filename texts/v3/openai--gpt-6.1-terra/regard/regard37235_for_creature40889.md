The power came in the long warm stretch, when the broad pools had separated into smaller ones and the birds had followed their edges inward. It had been listening to the frogs. Each evening there were fewer places from which they called.

It looked beneath the water as well as over it. A snail had reached the end of a wet track on the stone and was drawing itself into its shell. A fish lay in a hollow so shallow that its back broke the surface. Nearby, a young bird stood with one foot raised, waiting while its parent searched the mud. Beneath the shelves, other fish still had water enough. In the sedges, a spider had caught a fly.

The power understood how much was living here, and how much depended on other lives. It did not ask the birds to go hungry, or the snails to stop grazing, or the shallows never to dry. But the fish in the hollow was struggling, and the young bird’s parent had found very little that morning.

It brought a bank of cloud over the basin. Rain fell gently at first, darkening the stone around each pool, then steadily enough to run down the low slopes. The hollows filled. Brown water spread across the sand and joined from one depression to the next.

The fish swam out from its hollow and found the shadow beneath a shelf. The snail extended itself again and continued along the stone. Farther off, the adult bird found small creatures washed from the mud and called its chick over to feed.

The rain passed before evening. Water dripped from the sedges, and the shallows shone in the returning light. Fish crossed places that had been dry since morning. A long-legged bird stood watching them, and after a while it caught one.

That night the frogs called from several pools at once. The power listened a little longer, then went on attending to the world. It would remember this place, including the small lives nearest the stone.
