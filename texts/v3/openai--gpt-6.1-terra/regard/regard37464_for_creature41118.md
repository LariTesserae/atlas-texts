The power came to the valley without crossing the ridge. It was able to enter the smallest spaces there: the seam beneath a lifted mat, the dark between a moth’s folded wings, the narrow water below the ice. It could also see the whole basin at once, and the slopes beyond it, where weather gathered before passing over.

It did not at first make itself known.

Through a season of drying, it watched the scales close against their stems. It followed water as it moved beneath them, thinning from a slow sheet to separate threads. In the deepest pocket, the ice lost its white surface and became clear. The grazing animals remained beneath it, moving over a film so thin that the stone showed through.

A crawler was trapped when the roof of its gallery settled. The power could have lifted the roots. It held them still for a moment, long enough to feel the small animal pushing against them. Then it let them settle. The crawler found a seam between two fibers and worked along it toward the colder ground.

Elsewhere there were animals that did not find a way through. Their bodies softened among the stems. The power felt these deaths too. It did not turn away from them, but neither did it draw the dead out of the places that would receive them.

At the end of that season, a bank of cloud formed beyond the western ridge. Ordinarily the wind would have carried it north. The power drew the cloud lower and let it travel slowly over the valley.

Rain touched the upper slopes first. The scales opened where the drops reached them, exposing surfaces that had been folded away for weeks. Water ran along the ribs of the plants and fell into the moss-lined hollows. Farther down, the leaves of the jointed stalks began to pass it from one to another, their hairs catching grit loosened by the rain.

The power kept the cloud there through the afternoon. It could have filled every basin, but it knew how much weight the mats could bear. When the channels were running and water had begun to reach the dry ground beneath them, it released the cloud to the wind.

That night a section of canopy slid on the lower slope. It covered a hollow of moss and broke two young stalks. By morning, water was gathering against the fallen stems, and a crawler had entered the damp space beneath them. The power watched it go in.

In later years it returned its attention to the valley. Sometimes it found nothing to do. Once, after a winter of little snow, it brought another cloud across the ridge. Once it loosened a stone that had fallen over a spring, leaving it where rootlets could still grow into its cracks.

The routes through the valley continued to open and close. The moths emerged from their stalks, rested until their wings were dry, and flew low over the channels. From the ridge there was still the broad green basin, with its dark seams reaching down from the heights.
