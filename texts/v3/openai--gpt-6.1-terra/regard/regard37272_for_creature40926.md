The power came first to the place where the canal passed beneath the buildings.

There, out of sight, a fallen section of brick had narrowed the channel. Leaves and lengths of reed fiber had caught against it. Water still passed through, but after each rain a little more remained in the streets above. The people who lived there had noticed. They had cleared the gratings twice that month and were beginning to wonder where else to look.

The power could have lifted the brick and carried it away. It could have widened the channel too, or made the rain fall more gently on the hollow. Instead, it waited until morning, when the younger of the brothers from the pump yard came down to the canal with a length of wire.

He had brought a lamp as well, though its light did not reach far beneath the building. As he crouched by the bank, the water grew clear enough for him to see the submerged stones. Then a line of light appeared along the bottom of the channel, extending under the building and stopping at the fallen brick.

He stood up and looked around. Nobody else was on the bank. He crouched again, put his hand into the water, and found it cool and moving as it should. The light remained.

He went to fetch his brother.

By the time they returned with two neighbors, the light had faded. But the younger brother had marked its stopping place against a window on the opposite wall. They sounded the channel with the wire, then went inside the building and lifted a section of the floor where an older resident remembered an inspection hatch.

The work took most of the day. They brought out sodden fiber, sticks, three bottles, a child’s leaf boat caught between them, and finally the loose brick. They found other bricks behind it that needed securing. The mill stopped its pump for an hour while they worked, and people gathered under the awning, offering tools and watching the water rise through the cleared opening.

The power stayed with them. It did not move their hands or tell them when the work was finished. When someone proposed leaving the weakened bricks until a drier week, it let a small thread of mortar crumble into the water. The older resident saw it and asked for another lamp. They repaired the wall before putting the floor back.

Afterward, as the workers washed their hands at the canal, the younger brother told them about the light. His brother said he should have called someone sooner. One of the neighbors asked whether it had been a reflection. The younger brother did not know what it had been, and said so.

That night the power went through the town, attending to what was difficult to see.

It found a motor bearing running hot at the mill and let its knock become distinct enough for the worker on duty to hear it above the belts. It found a child lying awake because the rain was getting through a seam above her bed, and made the drip fall into the empty basin beside her rather than onto the blankets. In the morning her mother found the basin and climbed up to patch the roof.

It listened, too, where there was nothing it should mend. Two people argued quietly in a kitchen over whether one of them should leave for work in another town. Neither wanted the power to choose for them, though neither knew it was there. It left them the evening and the table between them.

When the next heavy rain came, the canal rose but stayed within its banks. Several people went out with rakes to clear the gratings. The younger brother checked the inspection hatch and found the water moving freely beneath it. On his way home he stopped at the shop for batteries and told the woman there that the repair was holding.

She was moving her lowest crates back from the doorway. He set down the batteries and helped her carry them.

The power remained for a while in the hollow. It could not remove every burden without also taking away things the people valued: the mill that gave them work and kept them awake, the trees whose roots lifted the paving and whose leaves sheltered their windows, the rain that soaked their walls and filled their canal.

It helped where it could, and took care to leave room for the people to help one another.
