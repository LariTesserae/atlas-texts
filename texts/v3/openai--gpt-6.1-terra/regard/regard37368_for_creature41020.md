The power came to the hollow on a hot afternoon, when the houses were closed and most of the animals were hidden inside them. It did not come down at once. It rested above the leaves and listened.

The noise reached it in separate strands. It heard the larvae rasping in their narrow channels and the climbers shifting in the roofs. It heard the feet of sleeping animals twitch against one another beneath the floor. It heard a grazer eating, and another grazer eating nearby, and the small changes in their sounds as they approached the same patch of tender growth.

The town’s deep call came through all of this. The power felt the distance across the hollow lengthen and fold. Moisture appeared on the façades and began to run down. In an upper room, a winged animal worked itself free of its case and waited for its wings to open.

The power waited too. It watched the animal climb toward the light and saw a pale predator turn in the passage above it. The animal’s wings were still damp when the predator caught it.

There were many such moments before evening. The power watched them, and when evening came it watched the winged animals pour over the rim, carrying food from the town out into the plain and bringing other food back. It watched the larvae feed where bodies had been left. It watched a group of grazers gather around a young one that had become stranded by the deep call. The young animal tried to follow them before the distance had settled; one of the adults pressed against its side until it stayed still.

The power could have made the hollow quiet. It could have smoothed the distances and closed the passages to predators. Before doing any of these things, it stayed to see what the town and its inhabitants did for themselves.

For some days there was brief rain. Then there was none. The warm plain grew hotter, and the leaves around the rim held themselves more tightly folded. The town continued to call, but less moisture shone on the walls afterward. What gathered in the grooves disappeared before the warm-bodied animals came out to drink.

They began to wake earlier. Their shadows appeared beside the damp pockets while the afternoon was still bright, and the animals themselves emerged to find the water already gone. They nosed along the grooves and returned underground.

The power followed a shadow to the place where its animal would appear. When the animal came through the floor, the power brought a little water up beneath its feet. The animal startled, then drank. Others found the damp place and drank too. By morning the floor had taken in what remained.

The next evening the power did the same, and on the evening after that. It brought only enough to fill the old pockets. The animals came to them by their accustomed routes; the larvae received moisture through the walls; the lower entrances did not have to close against a flood.

Beyond the rim, deep in the plain, water moved through a seam in the stone. The power opened a narrow way from it toward the town. The way ended beneath an outer root, where the living surface could feel the damp.

The root turned slowly. Over the following days it sent out fine growth into the opening. Some of the growth died back. More followed, and at last water began to reach the rim from below.

The power still brought water to the pockets while the root was growing. It brought a little less each evening, and watched what happened. The animals drank. The town called. Moisture returned to the inner walls, first in scattered beads and then in thin shining lines.

One afternoon clouds gathered over the plain. A brief rain fell into the hollow, and the grooves filled and divided and filled again. The lowest doorways puckered nearly shut. Several grazers waited beneath an overhang until the water had passed, then came out and ate the tender growth around an entrance.

The power remained for a while longer. It followed the new root through the stone and found that its fine ends were still taking in water. At dusk it watched the warm-bodied animals come up beside the pockets. Their shadows had arrived before them, small and bent over the water. When the animals emerged, they paused, felt the floor with their feet, and drank.

The deep call began. They flattened themselves beside the groove and waited for it to end.

Afterward the power rose above the leaves. Below, the streets were busy. A wall shuddered where something large had entered a passage, and the warning raced ahead of it through the town. Creatures poured from doorways farther along the rim. Among them were the grazer and her young, moving close together along a façade.

The power watched until they reached an open entrance and went inside. Then it went on, while the noise of the town continued across the warm plain.
