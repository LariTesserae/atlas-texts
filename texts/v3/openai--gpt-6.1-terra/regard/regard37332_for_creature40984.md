The power came to the plain where a pump was beginning to fail.

Its bearing had worn unevenly. At each turn the shaft moved a little farther from its center, striking the casing with a force that traveled through the channels. The pumps on either side compensated for it. Their gates opened more often, and the growths in those channels remained tight through the long intervals between.

The power followed the shaft down through its housing. It saw the worn bearing and the heat gathering around it. It saw, too, the growth fastened to the casing: a thick, old body whose knots passed through the foundation into six neighboring channels. If the bearing broke, the shaft would tear loose, and the sudden opening of the casing would draw much of that body into the pump.

There were other things below. The power spent a long time finding them.

Some parts of the machinery still served purposes that could be traced across the plain. Others fed systems so deep that even a small change took years to show itself. In a flooded chamber far from the failing pump, a stand of reeds depended on water warmed by its discharge. In a dry joint above the chamber, a thin band of living tissue had grown around a cable and nowhere else the power could find.

The power made no opening in the casing. It laid a new bearing alongside the old one, joining it to the shaft a fraction at a time. The work took place during the turns of the pump, while the shaft was moving. When the new bearing had taken the weight, the old one cooled. The growth along the casing loosened over the following months.

The pump continued to run.

The power stayed to learn its cycles. It learned how the water changed as it passed through iron and copper, through the black porous layers, through chambers where nothing living had yet been found. It followed the knots of the inhabitants through joints too narrow for water. Some ended in blind seams. Others joined bodies that had been separated on the surface for so long that their hard layers were different colors.

The contractions traveling along these connections were not all alike. Some followed the opening of gates. Some began in channels where the water had not changed, and passed outward through the knots before fading. The power watched them for a long time. It could tell which passages were used and which had grown closed. It could not tell, at first, what the passages carried.

At one of the rods, it found a collar compressed so often that little of its living surface remained exposed. The rod had been bending farther each year as a brace beneath the plates wore thin. The collar still drew cold water down when the rod lifted, but the deepest folds no longer had time to open before the next descent.

The power strengthened the brace. Afterward the rod bent less.

For several cycles the collar changed very little. Then a narrow fold opened along one side, and water entered a part that had been sealed for a long time. A contraction moved away through the knots. Hours later it returned, altered by its passage through the adjoining bodies. The collar tightened and opened again.

The power let the brace wear slightly, until the rod bent a little farther. It watched. It strengthened the brace again. Over many cycles it found a range in which the collar opened fully and the connections remained active. It left the rod moving within that range.

At other rods, the old movements appeared to do no harm. Those it left alone.

The power made a channel where there had been no channel, between two inhabited seams that lay close beneath the plates. It lined the passage with the same porous black material found nearby and let water enter slowly. Neither body crossed into it for a long time. Then a fragment lodged against one wall and began to grow. The power kept the passage open but did not widen it.

Elsewhere, a new passage remained empty. Sediment collected in it. The power allowed it to close.

Above the plates the rain continued. A gantry settled another small distance toward the ground, lowering one end into a basin. Water gathered inside its hollow beam and began to seep from a rivet hole. A mat of green took hold beneath the drip. The power cleared a little of the mineral crust from a lamp nearby, then saw that the mat grew equally well in darkness and left the other lamps as they were.

Years passed. A compressor house lost part of its roof. The fallen sheets diverted rain into a channel that had been dry through many seasons. Beneath the plates, old growths swelled out of their hard layers. Most survived the return of the water. One had become fixed around a corroded fastener, and as it expanded, the fastener began to split it.

The power lifted the fastener out. It left the cavity open, and over time living tissue grew across it.

There were many such places. Some the power could mend; some had already changed beyond mending. It found bodies reduced to hard shells in channels whose water had long ago gone elsewhere. It found fragments alive in pump housings, in the joints of gates, in a pocket of warm air beneath a plate that had never admitted rain. It did not join them all together. It made room where room was needed and kept watch where its own work might have consequences farther along.

At the failing pump, the new bearing gradually acquired its own pale coating. A growth from the old casing reached across the narrow gap, fastened to the worked surface and began to spread. Its outer layer tightened each time the gate opened. When the pressure fell, it loosened, taking water into the folds around the shaft.

Much later, a traveler lifting the grating at the damaged vent would have seen little difference. The same wet bands lined the shaft. Warm air still pulsed upward into the rain. Beyond the vent, the rods bent over their pale rings and rose again, shedding water from their tips.

Below them, in one old collar, a fold opened. Cold water entered it. A slow contraction passed through a knot, crossed a dry section beneath the plates and reached another body waiting beside a gate.
