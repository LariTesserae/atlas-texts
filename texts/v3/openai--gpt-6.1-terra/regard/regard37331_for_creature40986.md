The power came without a sound the animals could hear. The knocks continued inside the mountain, and the seep water hummed through the cracks.

It attended first to the warmth rising through the crown: where the water came from, what it carried, and the narrow places through which it must pass. Then it attended to the animals, one by one.

A grazer had been caught beneath the edge of a white tube. It had climbed there while the feeding plates were closed; when the animal extended them, the tube shifted against the rock and trapped one of the grazer’s limbs. It had been pulling for some time. Each pull bent the limb a little farther.

The power eased the tube aside. The grazer withdrew and remained beneath it, gripping the stone with its other limbs. After a while it began to scrape again.

Nearby, a broad hunter lay over a cooling fissure. The black deposit had grown thin, and most of the grazers had gone. The hunter had begun to move, lifting an edge of its body and settling it a little farther along the ridge. Its progress could hardly be seen from one day to the next.

The nearest warm opening lay beyond a collar of rust-red crust. The hunter could reach it, but the crust had grown high, and on the exposed side of the ridge the rock was sharp.

The power opened a fine crack beside the collar.

Warm water entered it slowly. Black grains gathered along its edge, and microbes spread over the newly exposed stone. Grazers found the deposit. When the hunter came, there was food for it on the sheltered side of the ridge, and it settled there.

The crack also disturbed two tube animals whose shells had grown against the crust. One lost several plates to falling grit. The power held the broken pieces away while the animal drew in its remaining plates; in time, it grew new ones. The other tilted into the opening and could no longer reach its accustomed current. The power turned it gently toward the flow and packed grains beneath its base until it held.

Above the crown, the silver animals continued to hang in their ranks. A deep knock rose from the mountain, and they grew still. Below them, feeding plates closed, grazers gripped the stone, and the hunter folded its pale limb beneath its body.

The power stayed through the long pause. It felt the pressure gathering farther down, where the mountain would one day break again, and kept watch on the living things above it.

At last a white plate opened. Its neighbor closed to make room. The grazer with the injured limb came out from beneath the tube and crossed the stone, moving more slowly than before. Where the surface was steep, the power made a slight roughness for it to hold.
