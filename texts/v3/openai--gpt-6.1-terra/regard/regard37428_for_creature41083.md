The power has noticed the hollow because the bead is about to fall three times.

The first fall will send a ripple across the pool. The second will strike the plate beneath it and break it against the wall. The third will enter the crack, where it will meet water rising under great pressure. Afterward the crack will widen, and the pool will empty faster than it can fill.

The animals are already bracing for the first fall. None has yet encountered the others.

The power considers catching the bead. It would be easy to hold it against the ceiling until the pressure below subsided. But the water would find another way through the stone, and the hollow might grow hotter, or dry, or fill with mineral crust. The power can see many such endings. Seeing them does not make the choice among them painless.

It does not know what the animals make of their lives. It knows that they keep living them: the clear ones feeding at the bubbles, the crawlers bearing passengers away and sometimes eating them, the threads drawing old shadows into the fracture. It knows, too, that the mountain has lately risen and is not finished moving.

The power goes down to the cliff. It makes itself small without giving up its sight, and enters among the steam.

The bead hangs above it. Below, the ripple spreads.

A hunter gathers itself into a deeper shape and glides toward a crawler whose back has just opened. The power leaves them both alone.

At the lip of the hollow, one of the translucent flakes has loosened from the rock. It is sharp, thin enough to bend, and held at its base by a strip of stone scarcely wider than a thread animal. The power touches the strip. It softens, just enough.

The flake falls inward. One end comes to rest above the pool; the other settles into the wet fracture.

The first drop falls. Its ripple meets the one already traveling outward, and for an instant the water is still.

The second drop falls. It strikes the flake and divides. One half runs along the brown edge into the fracture. The other lands gently on the plate, which tilts but does not break.

The animals clinging to its rim feel their way around the obstruction. Several step onto the flake. One pauses there, its independent eyes turned in different directions. It begins cleaning its mouth.

The third drop falls into the crack.

Deep beneath it, the pressure rises. The crack widens as it would have done, but the fallen flake presses across its mouth. Water gathers behind it and, for a little while, drains more slowly. The pool sinks. A crawler stranded on a high patch of rock unfolds its back to the steam. Two clear animals leave its seams and hurry down the slope.

The power waits.

The water below forces its way through the mountain along another fault. Farther down the cliff, where no hollow has yet formed, a thin jet escapes into daylight. Stone flakes away around it. The pool here begins to rise again.

The flake will not hold forever. Its edge is already dissolving. The power could make it permanent, but the animals have begun to use it, and a permanent thing placed here might close more paths than it opens. Instead, it attends to the water below. For a time, when the pressure rises, it eases the new outlet wider. When the flow steadies, it withdraws.

A thread extends from the fracture and touches the underside of the flake. Another follows. A clear animal approaches along the upper edge carrying a grain of stone. It sets the grain down, retrieves it, and continues.

The hunter has caught the crawler. Its clear body is folded around it, and the crawler’s pale back opens once more inside. The power sees the small animals scatter from the seams. One remains caught near the narrow mouth; when the hunter releases the covering, it pulls itself free and climbs onto the wall.

Steam thickens. The animals on the exposed stone press close together in the damp. Above them, another bead forms.

The power stays until it falls.
