The power came first to the hunter holding a beetle against the iron.

It did not make the hunter release its grip into emptiness. It quieted the hunger that had brought it there and eased its legs open. The beetle lay still for a moment, then drew itself together and climbed beneath a flake of rust.

The same mercy moved through the chamber, reaching into the places no light entered. Larvae stopped feeding within the bodies of other larvae. Mites loosened from living joints. Eggs, safe in their cracks, went on developing, but those that had already died were left as they were. The power did not ask anything to return.

Aboveground, the wheel stopped. Its bearing no longer ground against the shaft, and the tremors in the chamber ceased. The metal kept its warmth.

For a time the animals explored the changed floor. The hunters moved on their long legs among the beetles without grappling. Some found the bodies of those that had died before the power came, and fed there. Others probed the seams and the pipe joints, searching for what they had always found.

The power made food in the sheltered spaces: soft films along the stone, small dry clusters in the corners, drops held deep in the iron where they would not evaporate. Each animal could take what sustained it. The hunters found food suited to them behind the pipe; the larvae found it among the husks. The mites gathered where a little moisture gleamed. No creature had to tear open another to live.

The pantry-sized room was still crowded. So the power widened the seams below it, making passages with cool stone and warm iron, with places to rest and places where food could be found. Some beetles entered almost at once. They felt along the new walls and laid eggs there. Others remained near the slit, following the patch of light.

The power attended to the eggs as well. They continued to be laid, but only as many as the chamber and its new passages could hold in comfort. When there was room for more, more hatched. No creature had to go hungry to bring their numbers down.

At the slit, dust continued to fall from the basin. Once in a while an insect came with it, and the beetles gathered as before. The pale stone still admitted a narrow blade of daylight. The iron still smelled of rust and salt.

The chamber grew quieter. There was rasping, and the faint shift of grains underfoot, and sometimes the buzz of a beetle opening its wings. In the deeper passages, animals rested without having to press their bodies against the walls. A hunter passed over a beetle and went on.

The power remained near enough to know if anything was wanting.
