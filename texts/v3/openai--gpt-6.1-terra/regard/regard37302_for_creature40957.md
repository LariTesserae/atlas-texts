The power came looking for the being who lived on the plain.

There was no single place at which to find it. The power attended to a tuft and followed its roots down among the cells feeding there. It followed a film of water from a hollow into a seam, and the cells it carried came to rest beside others already dormant. Farther away, a lifted sheet of crust sheltered a community that had never touched those in the seam.

The power stayed long enough to see rain fall on them all, though not at the same time.

It could have filled the hollows and kept them full. It could have drawn a river across the plain. But the cells under the crust were fitted to long dry intervals, and the reeds had roots deep in ground that would change beneath standing water. A river would break open layers that had taken many seasons to form and carry them away. Some of the life it uncovered would flourish; much would not.

So the power watched the clouds.

One year, a storm gathered over the plain after a long, dry stretch. Its winds grew strong enough to lift sheets of crust and send them tumbling across the flats. The power eased the winds before they reached the ground. The storm passed slowly, dropping rain into hollows and seams. It left some of the lifted sheets where they lay, while wetting the dark ground beneath them.

The rain still fell unevenly. In one pool it was deep enough to cover the reeds. Elsewhere, droplets softened only the upper skin, waking cells close to the light. Far beyond the storm, the plain remained pale and dry.

The power did not command the buried cells to wake, or make the dead live again. It let the rain sink as far as it could, and when the clouds moved on, it let the plain dry.

Before leaving, the power attended once more to the life spread across it: to the cells dividing in the sheltered wrinkles, to the roots taking up water, to a filament growing through a narrow space between grains. There was more happening than could be seen from the surface. The power had not needed to give it a name in order to care for it.
