The power found a beetle beneath one of the stones.

It was alive, though it had not moved for some time. A grain of grit had lodged against one of its legs when the stone last shifted. The beetle had tried to pull free. The seam was narrow, and there was little room to turn.

The power moved the grain.

The beetle remained where it was. Later, when the temperature had fallen a little, it drew its leg in and worked it slowly against its body. It stayed under the stone until near morning, then came out and made its way toward a shrub.

The power did not follow it with a light. There was enough starlight to see by, and the beetle had other ways to find its way.

Above the hollow, clouds were gathering along the edge of the desert. They might have passed without rain. The power brought them a little farther, and toward dawn a few drops struck the sandstone. More followed, at first far apart, then steadily enough to darken the grit.

Water collected in the shallow places and sank. The crust swelled against the ground. Drops hung from the shrubs until their weight drew them down the stems. Under one plant, a seed took in water through its coat.

The rain ended before the sun rose. By afternoon the exposed stones were dry again, though moisture remained beneath them and deeper in the soil.

The beetle found shelter in a new seam. The seed began to open. Not every seed in the hollow would grow, and not everything that grew would last the season. But there had been enough rain for the crust to work for a while, and for some of the waiting roots to drink.

The power left no mark upon the hollow. The moved grain lay among the others beneath the stone.
