The power came without a shadow. A shadow over the pools would have sent the swimmers to the bottom, and it wanted first to see them as they were.

It attended to the water coming through the stone, to the films that fed on it, to the swimmers grazing there. It felt the slow tremor of the basin and the quicker ones made by limbs on rock. It watched a scavenger ease a plate upward and hold it for a long time while water ran from its edge.

Under one of the old piles, eggs were still alive. Mineral growth had closed the gaps around them. The air inside was damp, but the young, when they hatched, would have no way out.

The power loosened a single crust at the foot of the pile. A flake no larger than a swimmer’s body fell inward. It left a passage narrow enough to hold the damp and wide enough, in time, for the young to crawl through. The power stayed until water had run over the pile and past it without entering.

Elsewhere, it found a young scavenger caught in a trough. Its last molt had left it large enough to climb, but the nearest dry stone had a lip it could not grip. Each attempt ended with the animal sliding back into the water. There were movements below it, small and quick.

The power lifted a sliver from the bottom and set it against the lip. The scavenger drew itself into a seam and remained there. It did not emerge for a long while. When it did, it tested the sliver with its front limbs, then climbed over it and went on between two fins of stone.

The power could have made broad paths across the plain. It could have stilled the roar, or raised the basin out of the reach of water. But the films needed the water, and the swimmers needed the films, and the scavengers knew their way by the trembling stone. Even the pools that looked bare were inhabited. So it made no such changes.

It did not leave everything as it found it, either. Where a fissure was about to close and starve a long chain of pools, it held a small opening in the rock. Where a pile had been built on a plate that would soon slip, it settled the plate more firmly. It did these things at times and in ways that disturbed as little as it could. It watched afterward, because a kindness in one place could become a hardship in another.

Toward evening, mist thickened over the ridges. A scavenger came to the loosened pile and paused, its front raised. The crust beneath it gave no tremor. After a while the animal lowered itself and went on, leaving crescent marks across the mineral film.

The power remained a little longer, attending to the eggs beneath the stone and to the life moving through the dark water below. Then it went, leaving the basin to its rain and its immense, unbroken sound.
