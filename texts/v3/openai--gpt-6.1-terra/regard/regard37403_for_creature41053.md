The power came to the plain on a day when the rain had missed the basin.

It had not meant to arrive as anything in particular. The workers were accustomed to movement overhead, however, and several looked up when the dimness shifted. A crew carrying a block stopped with the sling taut between them. One worker set a mallet sideways on the ground.

The power held still.

After a while the crew lowered the block. One of them approached the place where the shadow had gathered and passed a hand through it. Finding nothing to take hold of, she returned to the others. They took up the sling again and carried the block to its place.

The power stayed through the next shower. It saw the pans fill and watched the workers empty them into the joints. It saw an animal wait beside an overturned pan until a child came and tilted it, letting the gathered drops run toward its mouth. Farther out, a new block was laid among older ones, and the ground knocked beneath it.

The power followed that sound downward. There were passages under the plain, and blocks under the passages, and more work being done in places where no light reached. It listened for a long time. Even then, it could not tell what would happen if the work stopped.

So it did not stop it.

It could have brought a deep rain. Looking at the sleeping shelves, the raised thresholds, the marks left by old water on the walls, it waited to understand how much the houses could bear. It could have closed every seam. The films lived in those seams, and the animals ate the films, and the workers depended on both. It could have made the blocks motionless. Some of them needed to move before they could be set.

On the third day, a block fell beyond the basin. Its sound came once, then again, then a third time, very clear.

An old listener sitting under a weighted roof lifted her head. Two carriers were already putting down their slings. She pointed toward a narrow lane where a child was collecting pegs, and called to him.

He heard her and turned. Before he could step away, the block beneath him tipped. His foot went into the opening joint.

The power held the two blocks apart.

It did not lift the child out. Three workers were close enough to reach him, and they came running. One caught him around the chest while another worked at the wrapping caught around his ankle. The third drove a wedge into the seam, although the blocks did not close upon it as she expected.

When the child was free, the power let the upper block settle slowly against the wedge. The workers pulled the child back through the lane. His ankle was scraped, but he could stand on it.

They returned with levers and another wedge. The old listener came too and put her cheek against the stone. For a long time she listened. Then she rose and looked at the place in the air where, three days earlier, a worker had put her hand through the shadow.

She held out her palm.

The power had no hand. It drew the loose dust together and let it rest against her skin, so lightly that none lodged in the creases. She watched it for a moment, then closed her fingers. The dust fell.

She said something to the others. They looked up and then at the wedged block. One worker fetched a pan and set it upside down beside the seam.

The power understood that this was an offering only when the old listener pushed it a little closer.

It could not drink, but the pan was cool and carefully made. It accepted the pan by keeping the dust off its upper surface. The next time the worker came by, she turned it upright to collect rain.

Word passed through the basin. Some workers left pans where the power might find them; others moved their children indoors when the dimness shifted overhead. A crew on the outer plain abandoned a block and would not return until an older worker had gone out to sit beside it. The power withdrew from them and watched from farther away.

It wanted them to know what it could do and, just as importantly, what it was doing. It tried holding a loose peg upright. The peg frightened a drinking animal, which knocked over a pan as it fled. It tried making a sound like the workers’ two quick taps. A nearby crew dropped a lever and ran from a joint that had been safe.

After that, it waited for the old listener.

She came to the basin each day and sat where the sounds from several passages met. The power gathered dust beside her and, when she looked at it, moved the dust toward a seam where a wedge had begun to slip. She inspected the seam, then called a worker over. Together they corrected it.

The next day the power pointed out another. On the day after that, the old listener studied the seam it showed her and shook her head. She tapped the block with her knuckles, then drew a line of dust across it and brushed the line away.

The power listened again. The movement below was slight and regular. It had mistaken it for the beginning of a failure.

It left that seam alone.

Through many days they learned a few signs. A line of dust led toward a place worth inspecting. A small clear patch on a block meant that the power was holding something there. The old listener could dismiss it with a sweep of her hand, or call others to look. When she did not want its help, she turned the nearest pan over.

The power came to know other workers as well. One showed it how a film could be divided without killing the thinner edge. Another brought two nearly identical wedges and struck them on the same stone so it could hear the difference between sound wood and wood that had begun to split. A child demonstrated the proper use of a peg, which the power already understood, and then demonstrated three games played with pegs, which it did not.

It made mistakes. Once it held a wall steady while a crew was trying to shift it, and they spent half a day searching for the obstruction. When the old listener discovered what had happened, she made the sign for dismissal with both hands. The power released the wall, and afterward kept farther from the crews unless they called it near.

There were accidents it did not prevent. It could not attend to every passage at once, not while remaining close enough for the workers to know where it was. When a carrier crushed two fingers beneath a lever, the power came at the sound of his cry. Others were already lifting the lever. It held the weight while they freed his hand, then followed them to a house and watched as they washed and bound it. The carrier slept badly that night. In the morning he asked for a cup of water and, later, for his sling-mending tools.

The power stayed.

During a long dry stretch, the pans emptied. The films contracted into almost invisible folds, and the drinking animals came closer to the houses. Workers carried water from a deep hollow on the outer plain, taking turns on a route where several blocks rocked underfoot.

The power found moisture held in the cool passages below. It could draw the water upward, but the deep joints had their own life. It showed the old listener the place first, gathering dust along the route down. She went with two others and returned with a fragment of film on damp clay. They set it in a pan and watched it for a day. Then they sent a young worker down to listen, and an older one after her.

At last they brought the power an empty pan and set it by a joint near the houses. The power drew up a little water. The workers marked the level inside the pan and came back the next day to see what had changed below. When they were satisfied, they asked for more.

They did not take enough to fill every pan. They carried what they had to the places that needed it most, including a sheltered seam where a drinking animal had begun to wait. A child sat nearby while it drank, holding a second pan in case the first ran dry.

In time the old listener could no longer walk easily to the basin. Other listeners came to her house. They sat beside her sleeping shelf and told her what they had heard, and sometimes the power gathered dust on the floor to add what it knew. She would consider their reports, ask for a sound to be repeated, or send someone to look again.

One day she asked the power to move a pan from beside her bed. It had been placed there for her, but she wanted the space clear so that the young listeners could sit closer. The power slid it across the floor. She nodded, and the lesson went on.

After her death, the workers brought her tools out into the rain and washed them. Her listening stone went to one of the young listeners. Her mallet was laid sideways beside the basin for a day, then taken up by a worker whose own mallet had split.

The power remained where she had taught it to wait. Others learned its signs, and it learned theirs. The plain continued to grow. New blocks came up warm from below; children found edges that rocked; crews repaired what they could reach and marked what they could not. When a sound returned for the third time, they still stopped and listened.

Sometimes, while they worked, a small clear patch appeared on a dusty block. A worker would glance at it, judge the weight on the lever, and call for another wedge. Then someone would tap steadily, and the crew would keep pressure where it was.
