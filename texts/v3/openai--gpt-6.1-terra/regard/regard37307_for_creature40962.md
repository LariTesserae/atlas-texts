The power came at noon, when the light lay in pale patches on the gravel.

It did not arrive through an opening. The bats went on sleeping; the crickets continued to feel their way beneath the slabs. A drop fell into a pool and sent a blind crustacean climbing over a pebble that had not been in its way a moment before.

The power attended to each of them.

It learned the routes the bats took at dusk, and the places beyond the clefts where they found insects over water. It followed a beetle into the warm depths of the roost deposit and a salamander along a fissure scarcely wider than its head. It watched the crustaceans in a pool that had grown smaller for eleven days.

It could have filled every hollow with water. Instead, it found the crack that fed the shrinking pool and eased apart two stones above it. By evening the pool had risen a little. The dry shelters remained dry.

It held a fresh-broken slab, high on the cavern wall, until the weight above had settled onto sounder rock. Then it let go. A bat slept on the ledge below, its folded wing touching its neighbor’s.

Beyond the cavern, the power did its work in many small places. It kept water in a pond where bats drank on the wing. It left a stand of old trees where moths sheltered, and made room for young trees to grow when the old ones fell. It watched the weather move across the land. When rain failed for a long time, it helped enough water reach the clefts that the wettest fissures did not dry.

The bats still hunted. A spider took a cricket from its web, and the beetles fed on what fell from the roosts. The power did not make the creatures into something other than they were. Where an animal was hurt beyond recovery, it shortened the hurt. Where a young bat fell and could still be helped, it gave the mother time to find it, and a small purchase in the stone for her feet.

The animals noticed only what was useful to notice: a steadier seep, a foothold, a pool that lasted. The bat climbed back among the others and vanished in their warmth.

For a long while the power remained, listening to the chamber gather every sound: wings stirring overhead, crickets rasping under stone, and drops striking the pools one by one. Then it turned its attention elsewhere, without turning it away.

At evening the colony began to wake. Bats loosened themselves from the ledges and streamed out through the clefts. In the cooler dark beneath them, a beetle crossed a fresh patch of gravel, found the edge of a deposit, and burrowed in.
