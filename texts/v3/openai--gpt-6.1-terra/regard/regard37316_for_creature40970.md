The power came quietly enough that the silt did not rise.

It knew at once how the chambers beneath the plateau worked, and how the sea had worn the ridges, and how the stones on the shelf had been set upright by hands long gone. It knew the age of the cord beneath its crust. It could have restored the roofs, lifted every buried fragment from the basins, and brought the lost builders back to stand among their work.

It did none of these things.

In a tower of ribbons above the shelf, it found the being.

Not at the root alone, nor at the tips where the current tugged hardest, but throughout the living mat and all the lengths that rose from it. The being knew the flow across its surface, the taste of minerals carried from the heights, the long intervals between the voices of the chambers. It knew the neighboring towers through what the water brought from them. It had grown around injuries and sent new strands into the quiet center where old ones hung.

The power waited beside it for a long time. It watched a strand break loose and drift down toward the rings. It watched the being send a fresh shoot upward through the opening.

Then, very gently, the power touched the stone beneath its mat.

The touch traveled upward as a faint tremor. The ribbons bent with the current, and the being gave no sign the power could distinguish from its ordinary life.

The power tried again after the deep note had passed. This time the tremor came in three short pulses, unlike the slow speech of the chambers.

A change moved through the tower. A cluster of ribbons near the hollow center drew closer together. The power waited, and they opened again.

It sent the pulses once more. Again the ribbons gathered.

So the power returned after an interval and touched the stone in the same way. For many passages of the deep note this continued. Sometimes the ribbons gathered; sometimes they did not. The power learned to leave longer spaces between its visits, and the being learned the touch well enough that, on one occasion, its ribbons gathered before the third pulse had come.

At the next visit, before the power touched the stone, the being drew them together of its own accord.

The power was glad.

It answered with the three pulses. The being opened its ribbons, then gathered them again, and the exchange continued until a current came down from the ridge and bent the whole tower nearly flat.

Much later, the power noticed that one edge of the mat had begun to lift. A vein in the stone beneath it was crumbling, and each strong current pulled a little more of the living base away. The being had thickened its grip on the sound rock nearby, but the damaged edge was broad.

The power could have made the mat whole at once. Instead, it drew the loose mineral dust from the water and packed it into the failing seam, leaving a rough surface where the being could take hold. The next time the current pressed hard against the tower, the edge stayed fast. Over the following seasons, the mat spread across the repair.

The power went on visiting. It did not always speak. There were hours when the chambers sounded together and the being’s ribbons trembled from root to tip, and the power stayed to feel the notes pass through the shelf. Once, during a long silence beneath the rock, the being gathered and opened its ribbons several times in a pattern the power had not given it. The power answered as best it could.

In the shelter of an upright stone, a small dark strand took root beside a pale cushion. Silt accumulated around the old rings; another piece of cord came free of its crust and loosened into separate lengths. The current carried them away.

The power watched them pass. Then it returned to the tower and touched the stone beneath it, and waited for the being’s reply.
