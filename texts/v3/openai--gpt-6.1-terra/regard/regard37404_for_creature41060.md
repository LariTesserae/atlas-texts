The power came without entering. There was room enough for it in the water already there.

For a long time it did nothing. It followed the seep backward through the limestone, where the water moved in darkness, and outward along the lowest seam to the sea. It attended to the tiny lives the water carried and to the pale snail resting on a stone. It felt the creature draw itself together at a wave’s approach and loosen afterward.

It could not tell, from this alone, whether the creature welcomed the sea or endured it. So it did not close the seam.

It noticed the bowl. A fine crack ran from the chipped lip partway down one side, too narrow to let water through. The creature’s strands crossed it. In time the crack would widen; perhaps the bowl would break, and the water would find another path over the shelf.

The power laid a little more clay within the crack, grain by grain, leaving the lip as it was. It did not lift the bowl. The strands remained where they had grown.

Then it turned its attention to the land above. Rain fell there, and roots followed the fissures in the stone. There were people, too, whose paths and drains and discarded things might one day alter what reached the hollow. The power could not make their choices for them. When the time came, it could make the clean course easier to notice: a clear spring worth keeping, a channel worth leaving open.

In the bowl, a drop fell. The membrane held its trace for a while.

The power stayed through many more drops, until it knew the intervals between them and the small variations in the seep. Once, it sent a tremor through the water, no stronger than a snail’s passage. The folds closed and opened. A mark formed in the bowl and began to fade.

It sent no second tremor. The creature had the water, the stone, and all that arrived by their own courses; there was no need to add a message for it to keep.

When the power withdrew its attention, the groove continued to whisper. Water spilled over the chipped lip, crossed the shelf, and returned to the pools.
