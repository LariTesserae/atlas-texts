The power watched for a long time before it moved.

It watched a fog pass over the plateau without descending. It watched the plants draw upon the water in their stems, and a line of hinge-backs alter its course toward a seam where the rock was still warm. The animals arrived at intervals, each pressing its jaw to the leaves where the one before it had paused.

The last to arrive was smaller than the others. One of its rear legs extended poorly; it had to brace itself on the other five before it could lift it clear of the mat. Beneath its flank, the leaves had been worn pale by the dragging plates.

It reached the warm seam after the others had gone. There it folded and remained through the dark hours. When it rose, the damaged leg caught between two stones.

The hinge-back pulled. A plate shifted over its neighbor, and the animal folded again. It waited until the ground cooled, then unfolded and pulled a second time. The leg did not come free.

Beneath the mat, a hunter felt the disturbance and turned toward it.

The power could have lifted the animal out. Instead, it entered the narrow crack as warmth, slowly enough that the roots along its sides did not burst. A little frozen water melted. The stone, warmed unevenly, shifted against the stone beside it.

The movement was slight. The hinge-back pulled once more and came away.

It did not hurry. It folded where it was, then unfolded, tested the leg and began to feed. The limb still dragged, but it could be lifted over the stems.

The hunter reached the place some hours later. It found disturbed roots, a trace of blood and no animal. It rested with its chin against the ground. When the hinge-back moved again, the hunter followed.

The power stayed nearby. The hunter would need food, and its young were waiting under a shelf of stone. Farther along the seam, the hinge-back found a depression among the roots and settled there, its plates drawing together. The hunter searched the mat around it until a smaller animal stirred beneath the leaves. Then it went after that.

On the next fog, water entered the widened crack. A branching colony, dormant there for years, began to grow. The roots of a pale plant also entered, and in time they held the stones a little farther apart.

The power attended to these things, and to others across the plateau. It did not make itself known.
