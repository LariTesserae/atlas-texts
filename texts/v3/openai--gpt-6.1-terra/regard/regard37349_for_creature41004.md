The power noticed the plateau first as a light moving under a forest. It followed the light through roots and crystal, down into chambers where the gatherers tended their eggs, and farther still, into a warmth that moved slowly enough to be mistaken for stone.

There was attention there. The power could not at first tell whether it belonged to one being or many. It waited, watching the blue-white pulses cross the land and feeling the long pause before one ridge answered another. Then it sent a small question into a vein of crystal.

For three days nothing happened.

On the fourth, a seam walker lifted its head beside a pool. The others lifted theirs in sequence, and the leaves beyond them closed. The response traveled out of sight and returned by a different path, carrying sensations the power had not asked for: the cool weight of water in porous rock, the pressure of eggs against a chamber wall, the strain of a forest holding together while its ground rose.

The power understood some of it. Much of it remained beyond understanding, so it continued to listen.

In time it learned that the distances troubled the plateau too. They had always changed, and the herds had learned their ways, but lately a region beneath the stalk forests had begun to hold several paths open at once. A pulse sent through it could come back before it had gone. Gatherers entering their tunnels emerged in unfamiliar colonies. Herds crossed a basin and found the same pool waiting ahead of them, smaller each time, with less water and more tracks.

One such herd had been walking for days. Its adults still brought their feet down carefully, but the young stumbled, and the gardens on their backs had begun to dry. Behind them, on a ridge that kept appearing and vanishing, a folded hunter waited.

The power could have lifted the herd out. It could also have closed every path in that region and made the ground stay where it was. The plateau showed it what would be lost: chambers full of developing eggs, roots drawing water through cracks that would no longer meet, the routes by which unfamiliar animals sometimes arrived and found a place among the trees.

Together they searched for a smaller change.

The power laid a thread of coolness along one edge of the basin, deep enough that the rock would not close over it. The plateau sent a pulse down the thread. When the pulse returned, it came back in the proper order.

The eldest seam walker turned toward it. The herd followed, stepping into the prints ahead. This time the basin narrowed behind them. They came out among low trees, where condensation had gathered on the leaves. The adults drank first, then stood around the young while they drank. The hunter arrived some hours later and lay down beneath a warm ridge.

The power left the thread in place. The plateau could open it when paths began to multiply and let it close again when the strain had passed. A seam walker crossing the basin afterward might feel nothing more than a coolness through its feet.

Elsewhere the land went on rising. Pools filled and vanished. Flyers brought water to their nests, and the gatherers carried dust into the stone. The power remained for a while, long enough to receive another pulse and answer it, then withdrew its attention.

From the highest ledges there was no visible mark of its visit. Under one forest, leaves closed in sequence, reopened, and were still.
