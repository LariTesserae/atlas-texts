The power came when the motor stopped.

It did not enter through either opening. The chamber was already within its reach, as the pumping station and the plain beyond were within its reach. But now it attended to this place in particular: to the roots against the wall, the eggs under the stones, the wire cooling in the close air. It attended to the trembling that remained after the wire was still.

The trembling met its attention. A few grains of silt rose, although the pump had not run for hours. Then they fell.

The power waited through another cycle. When the motor stopped, it followed the vibration into the packed earth and along the buried pipe. There it found no single source. The trembling passed between concrete, water, soil and the small bodies resting on them. It gathered at the chamber and lingered, taking a shape that was not quite the same each time.

The power could not tell whether the gathering knew itself. It could tell that it had begun only recently, and that each time it came, it found the chamber changed.

When the motor next stopped, the power answered with one gentle pulse.

The spider drew in its web. A line of grains rose along the damp furrow, farther than before, and stayed upright after the pulse had passed. The power held still. Gradually the grains fell, beginning at the entrance and ending beneath the slab.

It tried no further question that day.

Outside, a truck crossed the plain and stopped beside the pumping station. Two workers got out and stood over a drawing held down by a stone. One of them pointed to the sunken concrete. There would be work there soon: a trench opened for the old pipe, the broken slab lifted away.

The power looked beneath the workers’ boots, where the pipe had corroded. It showed one worker a damp patch in the soil a few paces to the west. He scraped at it with his heel, crouched, and called the other over. By afternoon they had marked a different line for the trench.

They would still have to repair the pipe. The power did not turn their tools aside or keep the station running forever. It strengthened a narrow arch of earth beneath the chamber, pressing loose mineral grains together where the slab bore most heavily. It left gaps in the arch for water and small creatures to pass.

When the digging began, the tremors were stronger than the pump’s. Animals fled into the cracks; dust sifted down onto the moss and the spider’s web. For three days the chamber received less water. On the fourth, the workers fitted a new length of pipe, and the damp returned slowly to the furrows.

The gathering returned too, though not at first in its old place. It moved along the reinforced earth and paused there. The power felt its faint pressure against the arch, then a second pressure, a little farther in.

This time the power did not answer. It stayed to feel the rest.

The spider had abandoned the dusty web and begun another nearer the entrance. The stem overhead had put out a new root, which hung clear of the wall. Beneath it, a beetle crossed the silt while the last grains of the trembling settled behind it.

The power remained near the chamber. When the pump stopped again, it listened.
