The power first noticed the town because of the long distance left open through its middle. It had seen other settlements fill every available space, and others abandon a space they could not use. Here, people had made a room for the air.

It listened for a long time. The roar made listening difficult even for a power accustomed to hearing through stone. It learned the intervals of the machinery, the small adjustments of the doors, the patterns sent along the walls. It learned that a stillness at a threshold might mean a child was hunting, not lost; that a hand held against another wrist might be an introduction, a question, or simply a way of resting.

Deep behind the chamber, one of the pressure mechanisms was wearing unevenly. A collar around a moving shaft had cracked. It would probably hold for some years, and then one day it would slip. The machinery would struggle to compensate. The first sign in the town might be a pulse strong enough to scatter the grit in the square; the second might be much worse.

The power could have replaced the collar without anyone knowing. But there were other worn parts, and the town had people whose work was to understand such things. It waited until one of them was tracing a troublesome vibration along a seam. Then it made a small movement in the stone beneath her hand: a pattern she had not felt before, followed by the vibration she had been tracing, repeated more clearly.

She lifted her hand. When she put it down again, the pattern returned.

She fetched two others. They lay together in the street, feeling the seam. The power showed them the crack as best it could: its position, the way its edges parted when the shaft turned. It took many attempts. Some of what it showed them was mistaken for a fault closer to the chamber, and they spent a day opening a junction that proved sound.

When they understood that something was answering them, they became cautious. A message went around the town, interrupted by the pulses and begun again where it had been lost. People came to the square and placed their hands on the stone. Some felt the strange pattern for themselves. Others trusted those who had.

They discussed it for several days. The power did not press them. It learned a good deal from their questions, including how little use a precise description of the collar was to people who could not reach it.

At last they gave it an answer. If it could make the passage safe, they would go and look.

There was an old service way, narrow even by their measure, closed for a long time by a warped gate. The power eased the gate without breaking its fittings. On the other side it held a loose section of stone while the workers secured it. Four of them went through, carrying wedges, bindings, and lengths of cord. Others waited at the entrance to receive what they learned.

They found the crack. They also found that a nearby channel had narrowed with mineral deposits. Clearing it would change the flow of air through several rooms. That part was within reach of their tools, so they returned to ask the people who lived there when the work could be done. Screens were moved, sleeping hollows covered, and the channel was cleared during a period when the pressure usually ran low.

The collar was another matter. The workers could feel its movement but could not stop the shaft long enough to mend it. They asked the power to do that. It held the mechanism while they bound the collar, and afterward showed them where the binding would need to be checked. They marked the place in a pattern on the service-way wall and taught it to the younger seam workers.

The machinery resumed its heavy breathing. Its pitch had changed a little. For some time people paused to feel it when they came out of their rooms, until the new sound became familiar.

The power remained near enough to answer, though the town did not always call. Once it was asked to hold a door while its hinge was replaced. Once a child tapped the strange pattern into the square and, when the answer came, ran to bring her friends. They took turns making movements against the stone. The power tried to follow them and was corrected often.

Much later, after a fine condensation, a winged creature crossed the chamber. People at several doorways felt the air stir against their faces. A seam worker, on her way to the service gate, stopped with one hand on the wall. In the brief lull she felt the creature land somewhere above her, and waited to see whether it would cross again.
