The power came first to the pools.

It did not touch the branching films. It watched them draw themselves together in the cooler water, and watched a tender kneel to study them, shading the surface with her hand. When she rose, she moved a bucket so that its drips would fall beyond the basin. The power was glad of that small adjustment.

It went up through the workshops, where people had left room in their walls for the mountain to move. It followed the surveyors along the eastern rim and saw what their marks had not yet shown: a section of the shelf was gathering strain beneath the newest foundations. It might hold for years. It might shift before the next cloud crossed the city.

Above the peaks, the winged being was circling. Its wings were broad and thin, dark along the leading edges. One had been torn near the tip; the tear had healed unevenly, and in certain winds the being had to work harder to remain aloft. It had found a current rising from the hot rock, and it stayed there, turning slowly, resting as best it could.

The power could have stopped the movement beneath the shelf. It considered this. The movement reached deep into the new rock, farther than the city’s surveyors could measure. To stop it cleanly would require changes far beyond the rim, and other lives had found their places among those folds.

So the power began with a warning.

That evening, a pipe beneath the upper streets knocked three times, paused, and knocked again. A mason carrying tools home heard it through the sole of her boot. She stopped and put her hand against the paving. When the knock came again, she went down to fetch the pipe crew.

They found no obstruction. One of them noticed a fresh crack in a foundation nearby, crossing a block instead of following its joint. By morning there were surveyors on the street, setting rods against their marks. The easternmost had moved more than a handspan since its last reading.

Work stopped on the unfinished streets. The crews moved their stone away from the edge and opened the seams around the affected foundations. Families in the nearest houses took bedding and cooking pots to neighbors farther down the shelf. Some grumbled at the interruption; some were frightened. Meals appeared in courtyards where there were suddenly more people to feed, and benches were brought in from the stairs.

For three days nothing happened. On the fourth, shortly before dawn, the shelf shifted.

The movement was slow enough to be seen. A lamp on the upper street leaned, steadied, and leaned again. The opened seams widened. One wall came apart above the second story, dropping stone into a courtyard that had been emptied the previous afternoon. Dust rose against the dark mountain and hung there in the still air.

Below, a hot-water main bent at a joint. The crew had left it under reduced pressure. It held.

People waited until the surveyors gave word, then went up carefully to see what could be saved. They found broken drains, tilted foundations, and a flight of stairs pulled away from a doorway. The black compound had stretched in some seams and torn in others. There was a great deal of work ahead, but everyone who had lived on the upper streets was accounted for.

The shift had also altered the currents above the eastern rim. For a while the winged being struggled there, its injured wing folding awkwardly whenever it turned. The power felt the effort of each beat. It gathered the rising air beneath the being and held it steady until the creature had gained the height it needed.

Then the power let the current loosen.

The being circled once above the rim. It turned toward a farther peak, where another warm updraft moved along the rock, and crossed into it under its own strength.

In the weeks that followed, the city measured the shelf again. The upper foundations were redesigned with wider seams and a second path down between the houses. A condenser tender, finding dust in her basin, cleaned around the coolest part and left the branching film undisturbed. Children were allowed back onto the lower roofs, though the adults called them away from the damaged streets.

The power remained a little longer, listening to the work. It heard the knock of tools, the measured calls of the surveyors, and voices carrying across the yards. When the pipes settled into their familiar vibration, it withdrew, leaving the people their walls and their decisions, the films their cool water, and the winged being the open air above the peaks.
