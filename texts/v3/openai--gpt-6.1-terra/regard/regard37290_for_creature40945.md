The power came close enough to feel the plateau through the stone.

At first the trembling seemed continuous. Wind worked in the clefts; loose chips knocked against their neighbors; thousands of birds shifted on the ledges. Then, beneath a basin of pale gravel, it felt a series of presses, a pause, and an answering series from farther away. A small grazer lay there with its feet folded under it. The other grazer did not rise. After a while, both began to feed.

The power stayed.

It followed water as it gathered on the undersides of overhangs. It felt the weight of a bird settle on a ledge and the slow working of insects through the roots of a cushion. It learned how far a long-legged animal would travel for a mouthful of water, and how a young grazer could lie in a patch of brightness without being noticed by anything passing overhead.

Near the edge of a basin, a young grazer had put its forefoot into a crack. A thin flake of stone had tipped inward behind it. The animal had drawn back once, then again. Now it was still, except for its ears and the movement of its sides.

An adult stood a short distance away. It came close, touched the young animal with its muzzle, and stepped back. Its foot pressed against the stone several times.

The power lifted the flake just enough for the foot to come free.

For a moment the young grazer remained where it was. Then it pulled its leg out and held it bent beneath its body. The adult approached again. This time the young one rose and followed, favoring the foot at first. They moved toward a patch of vegetation in the lee of a stone rib.

The flake settled back into the crack. The power eased it down until it lay flat. A little grit ran over its edge.

Later, at a sheltered hollow, a long-legged animal waited while drops gathered above a nearly dry pit. The power brought a thread of mist across the rim. It passed through the hollow and caught on the cold underside of the overhang. Drops formed there and fell, one after another, until the animal could drink.

The mist moved on. Some of it caught among the silver leaves nearby, where insects were sheltered deep in the cushion.

The power remained on the plateau through the changing light. It watched the birds return, dark against the bright rock, and felt their feet grip the ledges. One bird struck a pebble against a slab. Another turned toward it and spread its tail; then a third took up the pebble and carried it farther along.

When the high light faded, the roar continued. Presses traveled through the basin, faint and distinct beneath it. The power listened as long as they went on.
