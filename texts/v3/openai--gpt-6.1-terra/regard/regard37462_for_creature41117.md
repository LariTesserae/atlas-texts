At first the power did nothing that could be seen.

It watched a wall rise through a tuft and found the roots beneath both halves. It followed the dark powder from the living films to the blades, and from the blades back to the salt. It watched a small animal settle over a plant and remain there until its body cast a shade in which the blades stood longer and straighter.

It watched the smaller animals follow the larger ones. Some grew opaque and wandered away. Some did not. The power attended to these, too, and to the slow work by which their bodies returned to the crust.

It did not stop the walls from falling. It did not give the animals voices or make them doors. It stayed until it could distinguish a pause from an injury, a buried root from a dead one, and the ordinary ending of a life from something that need not happen.

Deep beneath the basin, beyond the dark films, a seam was opening. The changes that raised the streets were drawing it wider. In time the crust would split along it, and the warm mineral vapors would escape below the reach of the roots. The town would remain visible for a while: its last rooms open to the sky, its final walls whitening at their bases.

The power followed the seam into the depth. There it eased the two sides together, not closing them entirely, but setting between them a vein of yielding mineral. The pressure would still move through the crust. The streets would still rise and fold. The seam would no longer open all at once.

The work sent a fine dust up through a plaza. The blades at its edge turned toward the disturbance. After a time they bowed and released what they had gathered, and the powder settled among their roots.

Later, an animal was resting with a smaller one beside it when a ridge began to lift beneath their room. The larger animal stood. The smaller one tried to rise but had placed two pads in a fissure that was closing.

The power held the fissure open.

The small animal withdrew its pads and stood for a moment. Then it followed the larger one through the widening passage. Behind them, the fissure closed, leaving a pale line in the floor.

The power remained near the basin. It did not promise that every animal would reach the next passage, or that the town would keep any particular shape. But it kept watch on the pressure below and on the translucent rim above, where fractures sometimes began.

When the light shifted, the two animals crossed a square that had not been there before. The smaller one paused at a dark tuft. The larger waited while it fed, holding itself high on its six limbs.
