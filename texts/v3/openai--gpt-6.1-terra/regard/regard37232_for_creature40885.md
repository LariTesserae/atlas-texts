The power came to the marsh in rain.

It could have lifted the settlement out whole: bridges, sleepers, fern and all. It could have set the chamber inside a stone that no foot would ever trouble. But the builders ate from living roots, and the fern made their new ground as it closed the old. A chamber kept unchanged would not remain a home for long.

The power stayed to learn how water entered.

For many nights the rain was gentle. Beads gathered on the walls, grew heavy and fell. The builders cleared a crossing, renewed the dry fragments near the hunter’s tunnel, and went on working. The power followed each drop down through the moss and the living floor. It found the narrow places where water drained away, and the places where silt was beginning to seal them.

Then a foot came down above the chamber and remained there.

The roof bowed. Water spilled from the roots in a sheet. One of the sleepers pulled the sling, and the youngest climbed out over the older builder’s body. Along the advancing side of the fern, a bridge twisted under the flood. A carrier let go of a bundle of grass and held fast to the edge.

The foot lifted. The roof rose partway, but the water kept coming, pressed from the ground around the hollow.

The power loosened a thread of silt beneath the floor.

Water took it away. More silt followed, and a narrow passage opened among the roots. The flood slowed in the lanes, then began to sink. The carrier found a stem with a reaching limb and climbed it. Others came tapping from their shelters. One builder did not answer until a companion reached the moss fold where it was caught; together they freed its walking limb from a binding that had tightened around it.

When the lanes were clear, the builders recovered the floating grass. They found that one bridge had pulled free at both ends. They cut away its softened knots and carried the sound strands forward.

The power kept the new passage open for a time. It did not make it so wide that the floor dried, or take the water away from the springtails beneath the beads. It only drew out a little silt whenever the opening began to close. Roots grew around it. Their fine tips held the sides apart, and after a while the water could find that way without help.

The fern went on unfolding. One night its tip pressed down a sling whose occupants had already moved. In the opening behind it, a builder laid a strand of grass across a wet place and bound it to a pale stem. Another followed, feeling the first knots, then added a strand alongside so that two could pass.

Far above, rain gathered again. Some of it would reach the chamber; some would bead on the walls, and some would pass through the new channel into the marsh below.

The power left the roots to hold it.
