The power came slowly, if coming is what it did. It passed over the ridges and down through the clear water, learning the shapes of things before it touched them.

On one slope it found a swimmer resting beside a stalk. A pale line showed through the swimmer’s body, and one end of its tail had healed with a slight bend. It held itself against the stalk by minute adjustments, its head turned toward a hollow where fine particles had gathered.

The power watched it feed. When the hollow had been picked clean, the swimmer crossed a bare stretch of stone and entered another grove. It moved through the branches without touching them. Beneath a broad leaf it found a grazer, took it, and rested again.

The power could have filled the water with swimmers, or made this one larger and stronger. It left the swimmer as it was. There were things here that sustained it, and things it might seek for itself. Whether it wanted company, or what company might be to it, the power did not yet know.

For a long time it watched the ridge.

A plate of black stone stood above a cleft, held in place by its narrow base. A green stalk had rooted beneath it and, as the years passed, pressed gently against the stone. One day the plate shifted. The stalk sprang free, carrying a cluster of heavy leaves into the gap, and the plate settled against the rock below.

The swimmer had been in the gap. Now it lay between the plate and the leaves, its tail moving in short bends. Each movement drew one of the leaves more tightly across the opening.

The power lifted the plate a little. It held the leaves aside while the swimmer worked its way out, then lowered the stone onto a ledge where it would not fall farther. A few grains rose from the ledge and drifted down through the cleft. The stalk slowly unfolded. One branch was broken, but the roots held.

The swimmer remained nearby for a time. Its body trembled once, then grew still. Eventually it moved under a broad leaf and began picking at the surface.

The power stayed. It followed the swimmer along the ridge and found the places where it rested, the hollows where it fed, and the cleft it entered when the light was strongest. It watched other swimmers, too, and the grazers beneath the leaves. Over time it learned something of what each could bear.

Where loose plates threatened to close a passage, it eased them into safer positions. It did this sparingly. Some plates were already home to leaves and cushions, and a fallen edge could catch the grains in which new life began. The power examined each one before it moved it, and often left it alone.

Years later, a cushion had grown over the ledge beneath the plate. Its silver threads held a little bed of grains. A swimmer paused above it, nearly invisible until it crossed the pale leaf behind, and bent down to feed.

Far above, sunlight moved over the ridge. A bubble slipped from a seam in the rock, touched the underside of a leaf, and rose between the stalks. The power watched until it reached the surface.
