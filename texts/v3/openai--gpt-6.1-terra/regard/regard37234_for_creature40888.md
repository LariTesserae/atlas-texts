The power came down without taking the stairs. It entered where the runners entered, through seams in the stone, and attended first to what was already happening.

In the new break, a loose slab rested on two narrow points. It would not fall at once. Sooner or later, heat and water would work it free, and it would come down across a damp hollow. The hollow would fill again around the stone, in time; other roots would find it. But the fall might also break the iron stair and leave the opening above exposed to anyone who happened upon it.

The power settled the slab more firmly. It did not close the fissures. Water could still gather in them, and the sharp faces remained open to whatever might reach them.

Then it followed the pale runner beneath the fallen chip. Its tip was sound, though the floor ahead was dry. A little farther on, a seam held moisture too deep for the runner to reach in its present course. The power turned the chip, just enough that its underside would hold a bead of condensation through the hottest part of the day.

Whether the runner would find the seam was still uncertain. The bead might dry; the next rain might come too late. But now there was a little more time.

The power examined the pump without starting it. Water moved through the old pipes in ways the machinery no longer governed. Salts came from the mortar. The lamps, weak as they were, drew current from a damaged line above, and the damage would one day bring someone looking for it.

So, above ground, it made the fault easy to find. A technician would discover the warm stair, test the air, and mark the opening on a map. She would return with others. They would look down into the chamber and, before doing anything else, ask what was growing there.

The power could not know what they would decide. It left them a clear view of the fractured rock and the old installation, and time to consider both.

Below, a droplet fell beside the brick housing. The white skin darkened around it. Grains gathered into a small ridge, which held its shape for most of the afternoon before settling again.

The power stayed until the ridge had flattened. Then it left by the same narrow ways it had come, without clearing a path through the fronds.
