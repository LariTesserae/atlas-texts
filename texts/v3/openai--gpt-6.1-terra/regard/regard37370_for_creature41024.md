The power came first to the entrance, where a frog sat facing the mist.

It did not step inside. Its shadow would have covered the frog’s ledge, and the frog was watching for food.

It listened long enough to distinguish one insect’s call from another. It watched a beetle rise for air and return to the roots. It followed the water out beneath the marsh and back through the channels, taking the measure of the warmth it carried and the lives it sustained along the way.

The pumps were drawing more water each year. In a dry season not far ahead, one of the old seepage paths would fail. The pools would shrink; the living walls would pull away from their roots. The factory would still need water, and people would still need what the factory made.

So the power went there, too.

A worker found a loss in a valve that had been difficult to locate. Another, studying the warm discharge, saw a way to recover some of its heat. A plan to deepen a drainage channel was delayed when someone asked for a better survey of the marsh. None of these people knew that anything had visited them. They argued, checked their figures, and made changes slowly.

The power stayed through the dry season. Where the old seepage path narrowed, it loosened a little silt and let water find a second way among the roots. It did not make the pools immune to weather. It gave them a chance to endure the change.

Over several years the returning water cooled. Some larvae that had thrived in the warmth did less well; others survived that once would not have. The insects’ chorus changed by degrees. A frog lost its ledge when a root softened and fell, and found another near the entrance.

At last the power withdrew from the hollow. It left no mark that the animals could find, no blessing sealed into the moss. Water still arrived and left. The pumps could still be heard beneath the calls.

On a mild morning, a drop gathered on the ceiling. A beetle passed below it, trailing silver, and the drop fell after it had gone.
