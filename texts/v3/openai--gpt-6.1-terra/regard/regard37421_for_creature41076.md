The power came to the plateau without a shadow. The schools did not turn, and the fringes of the colonies remained extended in the current.

For a long time it did nothing that could be seen. It followed the water down into the basins and out again. It watched a wall grow crowded, watched its oldest chambers open into hollows, watched a grazer cross the same pale slope in a hundred slow, irregular paths. It learned where the young wall-makers were carried when they left the shelter of the ridge, and where the fish went when the water above the flats grew dark.

In one basin, a fragment had fallen across a narrow groove. A grazer beneath it could still move, but only enough to scrape at the shell on either side. Dust gathered against its body. The current was too weak there to shift the fragment, and the animals that might have eaten the grazer could not reach it.

The power lifted one edge of the shell. A thread of dust came out from underneath. The grazer drew itself slowly into the opening and continued across the basin floor, leaving a clean track. When it had passed, the fragment settled again, a little farther from the groove.

Later, a strong flow came over the eastern lip, carrying more dust than the colonies there could clear from their openings. The power turned a small part of the flow aside. It went through an empty channel, where the dust settled in an abandoned wall and over the back of a waiting predator. The living fringes along the eastern lip closed for a while, then opened again.

Not every young wall-maker reached shelter. The pale fish still contested their holes, and the predators still struck at the schools. The power did not stop the water from wearing down the walls, or keep the old animals from dying in their chambers. It stayed to see what followed.

Over many seasons it moved a few loose pieces of shell into the paths of currents that swept too cleanly across the flats. Dust gathered behind them. Young wall-makers settled there, and in time their chambers joined. The new walls grew unevenly: one was broken by a passing fragment before it rose higher than a fish; another spread across the slope and became a place where fish could shelter.

On that slope, a grazer came to a steep face beneath the wall. It climbed, leaving its narrow track through the film. Tiny crustaceans moved under its edge. Above it, the wall-makers opened their fringes into the current, taking what the water brought and sending the rest on.
