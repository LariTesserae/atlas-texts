The power came to the lagoon without parting the water. It felt the chamber first as the inhabitant did: through the thud at the holes, the drag of the grass, the three knocks of the pots.

It felt, too, the long turning in the empty pot. It did not know whether this was pleasure or simply a place where turning lasted. So it waited.

A fiber came loose from a grass tie. The inhabitant lifted it, let it fall, and lifted it again. The power made a tiny roughness on the rim of the pot, within reach of the moving strand. The fiber caught there. On the next surge it pulled free; the inhabitant brought it back, and again it caught.

This happened several times. Then the inhabitant left the fiber where it was and went on around the chamber.

The power took this as permission to do something small, though not as permission to do everything.

It eased one sharp corner beside the channel, where the water was drawn down fastest. It settled the pots more firmly on their ledge, leaving them room enough to make their three different notes. And in the empty pot it raised a thin curl of clay from the bottom, so that the whirl might hold a little longer when the surge had passed.

The next rush came through the wall. The water spread over the tiles, pressed the grass flat, struck the pots. In the empty one it found the new curl and turned around it. For a while the whirl stayed, making its low sound.

On the following surge, the inhabitant went there again.

The power remained through many swells. When a loose strand drifted toward the channel, it let the strand go; another might come. Beyond the holes, the lagoon moved in its own wider ways, carrying sand and light past the chamber. The power left those ways open.

At the softest point between surges, it rested with the nearly still water. Then the thud came through the wall, and the inhabitant gathered itself again.
