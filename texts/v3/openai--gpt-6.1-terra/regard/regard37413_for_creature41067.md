The power comes without a light.

It attends first to the water in the hoof hollow. The frog has lowered itself until its belly touches mud. There are eggs farther down the channel, laid after the rain, in a pool that is nearly dry. The power reaches beneath the basin, where water still moves slowly through the earth, and opens a narrow passage. Before dawn, the pool deepens by the width of a finger.

A beetle is caught on its back beside a root. It has been moving its legs for a long time. The power turns it over. It stands still, then goes on beneath the leaves.

There is a grazer with a thorn buried in its lower leg. She has been placing the foot carefully, keeping near the edge of the herd. As she feeds, the thorn works loose. She lifts the leg once, puts it down, and follows the others through the grass.

These are small acts, and the basin is large. A spider takes a moth from its web. A small mammal finds seeds left by another. Termites dismantle a fallen limb in which beetles have made their shelter. The power could stop each taking, but there are mouths throughout the basin, and young waiting in the dark. It does not make a promise that nothing here will die.

It stays through the hours before morning, attending to what can be helped. It loosens a crust of mud over a burrow entrance. It guides a little more moisture toward the roots of a tree whose upper branches are dying. It leaves the thickets tangled, and the old mound standing, and the paths through the grass where the animals know to find them.

The mechanical hum returns briefly beyond the basin. The power listens until it fades. It will listen again.

Near dawn, the frog at the hoof hollow slips into the water. The herd moves on, the younger animal close against the adult’s flank. Dew gathers on the leaves and falls, drop by drop, among the insects below.
