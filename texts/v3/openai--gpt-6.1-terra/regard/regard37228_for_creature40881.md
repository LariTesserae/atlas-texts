At first the power listened.

It heard the calls of the larger animals, and the places where those calls were lost beneath the pumps. It heard the silver fish strike the intake lattices when the current changed too quickly. It heard the grazers moving, too, though no human instrument in the basin could have separated that sound from the rest.

There were people beyond the water who depended on the machinery. The power listened for them as well. It learned what the pumps carried, where the water went, and which changes the people could safely make. Then it waited for an inspection vessel.

The vessel came with two people aboard. Its lamps passed over a tower, a seam of fish, a patch of fresh rust. One of the inspectors was already making a note about the rust when the power drew her attention to a grazer on the adjoining plate. She watched it cross a weld and begin on the other side, leaving its narrow clean track.

She called her colleague over. He watched too, then took a photograph and wrote down the location.

The power did not need them to know it was there. Over the following weeks, it helped them notice other things: a large animal lingering near an intake, unable to make itself heard; eggs gathered in a gap that would be scoured when the pumps reversed; a strain in a mounting bolt that added a hard, repetitive knock to the station’s hum.

The bolt was replaced. The quieter machinery made it possible to hear a second loose fitting, which was repaired in turn.

The people studied the animals near the intakes. They changed the speed of the pumps gradually where they could, and fitted finer barriers in places where fish were being drawn through. They left gaps and sheltered routes elsewhere, after checking that these led away from the blades. The work took time. Some of the first barriers clogged, and one diverted fish into a current stronger than the one it was meant to protect them from. The inspectors returned and altered it.

At one station, where a broad support stood between two bright stretches of water, they suspended a row of heavy baffles. Behind them the pounding was less severe. A large animal began to rest there. It came and went, sometimes alone and sometimes with another, and its calls carried farther into the basin.

The power visited that sheltered water often. Once, while the animal rested, it made itself known: a presence beside the animal, patient and still. The animal turned toward it, then swam a short distance away. The power stayed where it was. After a while the animal returned to the lee of the support and settled again.

Above them, algae continued to grow in the seams. Fish gathered at the edge of a lamp, and a hunter waited beyond the light. When it struck, the shoal broke around the tower, each fish turning as space opened before it. The hunter caught one and carried it away.

The pumps went on. The basin remained young, its floor mostly bare. But in a few more places grit lodged, and algae took hold, and small grazers found their way along the steel. The people came back with their lamps and notebooks. The large animal’s calls passed through the quieter water, and sometimes another call answered from farther off.
