The power attends first to the scraping.

It listens through several cold periods, while water gathers and falls and gathers again. It listens when the walls are crowded and when mist passes through and the animals spread out. It follows the pauses after a stone falls, and the calls that begin again.

It finds no single creature making the sound. It finds the young carried beneath bodies that did not produce them, the animals waiting at the water, and the dead held for a time among the living. It sees how a chip can bring water within reach, and how many bodies can be caught beneath a larger fall.

The fissure will not last as it is. The mountain is settling. One wall is moving toward the other so slowly that, during the cold periods, the movement is almost lost in the swelling of the stone. In time the deepest chambers will narrow. Some seams will remain open; others, now full of animals and young, will be pressed shut.

The power could hold the mountain apart. It does not know what that would do to the warmth below, or to the water that has only just found its way through the fresh stone. Instead it waits for a mist to enter.

As the animals loosen their grip and move across the wet walls, the power eases the weight on a long crack at the bottom of the fissure. The crack opens a little, then continues downward into older stone. A thread of warm water follows it. The power holds the sides apart until the mountain has settled around them.

The change makes a sound. The rasping stops throughout the lower fissure. A few chips skitter down the wall and land among the crowded bodies. One animal is struck and loses its hold. Others shift away from the place where it comes to rest.

The crack lies still. The fallen animal moves a leg, catches the edge of a pocket and pulls itself beneath an overhang. After a while, one of the others climbs to the new opening and feels along it with its forelimbs. Another comes behind. Neither enters yet.

Above them, the power loosens a shelf of stone that is beginning to tilt away from the wall. It lowers the shelf until its weight rests securely on the rock beneath. The animals on its underside cling there through the movement. When it stops, several leave, carrying young.

There are other loose pieces. The power leaves the small ones where they are. The animals have uses for them, and the mountain will go on making more. It clears only the stones that would block the new crack if they fell, setting them in a dry recess away from the water pockets.

The thread of water reaches the crack’s mouth and spreads over the stone. It is little more than a damp line at first. An animal approaches and drinks; another settles beside it. In the days that follow, animals enter the crack singly and return with wet legs. Then some remain inside. The scraping reaches farther down, thin at first, with long intervals between calls.

The power stays through another cold period. It watches the new passage as the surrounding walls contract. The passage narrows at one bend but remains wide enough for the largest animals to pass, and the water continues to seep along its floor. Green growth appears where the damp stone receives a little of the gray sheen, though it grows more slowly there than near the upper pockets.

Animals still die. Growth is eaten away in some places before it can recover. A useful chip falls from a seam, and for a time the animals gathered beneath it have less water. They probe the seam and the fallen chip. Eventually two of them work the chip back against the wall, where it catches a bead and carries it outward.

By then the young in the lower fissure have begun to climb. Some move into the new passage beneath adults; others stay in the places they know. The power leaves the opening as the animals have found it, neither widening it further nor directing them toward it.

When it turns its attention elsewhere, the scraping continues behind it. Water falls in the upper pockets. Deep below, where the mountain is still warm, the calls from the new passage answer those along the old walls.
