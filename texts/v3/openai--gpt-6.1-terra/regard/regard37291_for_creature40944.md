The power does not send rain.

It comes to the rim and finds the hollow already full. Beneath the slow movement of its surface it attends to the smaller movements: the pressure of a leg against a back, a mouth scraping, an egg turning as the bodies around its shelter press inward. It follows the warmth down through the stone to the places where gases gather, and waits through several of the hot breaths.

On the third, a section of shelf grows too hot before the animals beneath it have begun to climb. The warning passes upward through the crowd. Most get away. A few remain caught in a narrowing crack, their fringes working against the stone.

The power opens the crack just enough for them to pass.

After the heat has fallen, it follows the survivors back down. It has learned something about how quickly the stone can change, and how little warning reaches some of the places nearest the vents.

It begins there. Deep below the hollow, it eases the channels through which the hottest gases rise, widening one and narrowing another. The next breath comes a little more slowly. Warmth still reaches the fissures, and the smallest animals gather to feed, but the animals at the lower shelves have more time to climb.

The power watches what follows. Where the flow has weakened too much, a colony thins; it opens that channel again. Where yellow dust collects beneath an overhang and does not disperse, it makes a seam for it to escape. It works in increments, waiting between them while the inhabitants descend and the hunters follow and the builders return to their walls.

The shelves offer fewer places of shelter than there is room for. In several of the warmer ones, the power hollows shallow recesses with entrances too low for the largest hunters. It leaves their floors rough enough for small bodies to grip and guides a thin current of gas through each. For a long while the recesses are empty. Then a granular mass begins to form in one of them. A soft-rimmed animal follows. Later, two builders carry grains to its entrance and make a curve there.

The power does not keep that wall standing. It lets the animals work on it and, when passing grazers break it, work on it again. But it strengthens the shelf above, whose loose edge has already fallen twice, burying eggs and the animals tending them.

There are hunters in the hollow, and they must eat. The power makes no command against their hunger. In the cracks where they travel, it coaxes a dark film from the minerals and the warm gas, something a hunter can scrape and swallow when it finds it. Some pass over it; some feed. The power watches their bodies and their young through many cycles of heat before it gives the film a place in other cracks.

Still there are struggles. A grazer is drawn between stones; its neighbors lift and press outward, carrying the warning across the shelf. The power feels the animal’s efforts diminish. When its injuries are too great for it to live, the power lets its distress end swiftly. The hunter feeds, and the space above closes.

At the rim, the air remains thin and cold. No light has been made brighter. The hollow is still crowded, still warm, still almost silent.

After another hot breath, the animals descend. In one of the new recesses, a builder presses a grain against the broken end of a partition. Another approaches along the shelf and passes over its back. The builder waits under the weight, then carries the next grain into place.

The power remains close enough to feel it.
