The power comes to the city without entering from above. There is no useful above for it to enter from. It is present first in a narrow chamber beneath an old street, where the floor is lifting toward the ceiling.

A plate is in the chamber. It has followed a seam down from a shelter whose entrance closed some time ago. There are others behind it, too deep in the wall to turn around, and young among them. The film along the seam has been grazed pale. Ahead, a dark patch remains on the ceiling, but the gap beneath it is narrowing.

The plate reaches the end of the seam and presses its underside against the stone. It stays there while powder sifts from the ceiling and gathers along the edges of its back.

The power could hold the floor where it is. It could open the whole chamber and leave a broad, permanent passage for the animals. But the chamber lies within a district that is folding slowly together; the pressure has opened new walls elsewhere, and those walls are beginning to darken. The power attends to the movement for a long time, until it can distinguish the changes that will come from this one.

Then, where the ceiling meets the far wall, a pale line appears. It widens a little at each end, making a shallow passage around the rising floor. The crack is narrow enough that the stone above it remains supported, and it leads into another seam in which film has grown.

The plate does not enter immediately. It moves along the crack, touching it at several places. Powder has caught at one end, and the animal grazes there before drawing its plates close and slipping inside. It emerges a short distance away, stays still, then begins to feed on the dark seam.

The others follow over the next long interval. The smallest arrive with dust on their backs. One turns into an adjoining fissure and comes out much farther away, in a chamber the power has not changed. It finds film there too.

The power remains until the old chamber closes. Then it follows the plates through the district and watches them settle in several pockets, some already occupied. At the entrance to one pocket, the residents draw back to admit the newcomers. In another, a plate leaves a cluster of disks against the wall. The clear substance around them hardens slowly.

Across the city, passages are closing and opening. Some closures happen over intervals long enough for the inhabitants to leave. Others come quickly, when a terrace slips or a folded wall bears down on a hidden seam. The power finds animals caught in both kinds. It opens a way where there is a place for them to go, and where an opening will not bring the wall down upon them. Sometimes it eases a movement enough for a group to reach a neighboring crack. Once, beneath a street that has already fused, it is too late.

The power does not turn every narrow seam into a passage. Many hold the dark film close against the stone, or shelter the young from passing grit. It leaves the branching crust undisturbed, and the empty chambers, and the broad streets where powder gathers in level bands. It waits to see what grows on the edges it has opened. When a new passage dries a seam and the film recedes, it lets that passage close and finds another way.

At the outer district, an animal comes out onto a smooth floor while the air is still. It moves toward the edge, stops, and turns back. Far across the drop, a street is visible between two hollow walls. The power brings a fold of mineral up beneath the floor, extending it into the distance, then waits.

The fold rises as the city rises: grain by grain, over a long succession of changes. Film takes hold on its sheltered side. Crust grows beneath one edge, catches powder, and thickens. Plates come from the inner chambers to graze there. By the time the fold meets a seam on the neighboring height, animals are living inside it. Some pass through and emerge among walls they have never touched before. Others remain where they are.

The distant street folds out of view. Later it returns, and a pale track is visible along the base of one wall.

The power watches the bridge through several shifts. When a section narrows, the plates move into cracks beside it. When another section opens to the grit, they withdraw to its sheltered face. Film darkens the grazed patches again, slowly, from their margins.

At last the power’s attention loosens. The city continues to buckle through itself. In a chamber beneath an old street, the stone bears the faint outline of a crack that closed long ago. On a nearby wall, several young plates rest together in a patch of dark film, their small backs almost the color of the mineral around them.
