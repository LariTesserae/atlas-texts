The power came at dusk, when the hands on the rooftops were still visible but the first voices had begun to carry across the channels.

It stood for a while beside a cooling outlet. A broad grazer rested there, its edge lifting and settling as water passed beneath it. The grazer had a notch in one side and three names, depending on which household was asked.

The power could have made the water sweet. It could have cooled the stone beneath the streets and filled the approaches with trees.

It watched the grazer feed. It watched a hunter take a smaller animal from the shallows. It followed the cooling water back through its channel and felt the warmth of the engines it had carried away.

Then it went among the people.

A woman was sitting on a doorstep, drawing a scraper beneath her nails. The power sat down on the step below her, leaving room between them.

After a while she looked up.

It raised a hand and signed, *I am here.*

She studied its face, then signed back, *I see you.*

The power asked whether there was anything she needed.

She glanced at the scraper. “A better light, perhaps.” Then, after a moment: “My brother has been ill.”

The power asked if she would take it to him. On the way she told it what she knew: the fever had lasted four days, he could keep water down, and the town’s physician had visited twice. She had asked the physician to come again in the morning.

Her brother was awake. The power sat beside his bed and told him what it was, as well as it could in words he would understand. It offered to ease the fever.

He asked whether he would still remember being ill. The question seemed important to him, so the power answered carefully: yes. He said it could go ahead.

When the heat had left his skin, his sister brought him a cup of water. He drank it slowly. The power stayed until he slept, then accompanied her back outside.

At the pump house, a worker had his palm against the wall. The rhythm beneath it had changed. He had noticed it that afternoon and again an hour ago, but the instruments showed nothing unusual.

The power laid its own hand beside his. Deep below them, a bearing was beginning to fail.

It told him what it had found. Together they went to the workers on the evening shift. They stopped the pump, opened the chamber and found the damaged bearing just as the power had said. By midnight a replacement had been carried down, and the old one lay on the floor for everyone to examine.

News traveled. People came with questions, ailments, disputes, small devices that no longer worked. The power helped where it could, asking before it touched a body or altered a thing. It showed a mechanic how to make a seal last longer and listened to two neighbors who had disagreed for years about a drainage channel. It could not settle the disagreement for them, but it helped them measure the flow, and afterward they had something new to discuss.

Before leaving, it went again to the flats. Rain was crossing the far rim, whitening the distance. The hunter at the crossing had lifted one leg; there was a cut above the joint, clogged with mineral grit. The power cleaned it with a little water. The hunter watched, then stepped away.

By morning, rain had passed over the town. The repaired pump was running, and fresh tracks crossed the wet dust on the causeway. Beneath the cooling outlet, the grazer with three names had moved a short distance. Someone had set a vessel under the ledge beside it, and drops were falling into it one by one.
