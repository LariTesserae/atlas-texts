The power came in the season when the water in the western basins was lowest.

There was still rain. Drops gathered under the eaves and fell, and the channels carried water between the houses. But a crack had opened beneath one of the long pools, and more water was leaving it than the narrow feeder could replace. Along the nearest wall, a film that had once reached the timber had withdrawn to a thin line at the base of the stones.

The power followed the crack down through the plateau. It went far into the rock, too far for the growths to bridge. The water entering it would not return to the town.

The power could have closed the crack. Before doing so, it looked at what was living along its edges. Threads had already followed the water down, and in a sheltered pocket, well below the pool, a small new mat was beginning to spread. It was separate from the growth above. Its rounded bodies had not yet appeared.

The power waited through another rain. It watched the new mat draw nourishment from the water, and the older inhabitant at the surface send threads toward a notch that had long been dry. Those threads reached only a little farther before the pool fell again.

Then, deep in the crack, the power moved a stone. It turned slowly, without breaking, until it rested across the narrowest part of the opening. Water could still pass around it, enough to reach the sheltered pocket below. Above, the pool rose by the width of a finger.

The change was small enough that, for a while, it could have been mistaken for the work of the weather. The water reached the old notch. A dark line appeared there, then a pale one. Under the eaves the film thickened, and after a long time its edge found the timber again.

The new mat below the pool continued to grow. Some seasons less water reached it than before, but it had room to follow the dampness into the rock, and it did.

The power remained on the plateau. There was much to attend to. One of the forked poles beneath an overhang had begun to lean; if it fell, the clay roof might choke a basin. The power eased the pole upright and settled its foot more firmly among the stones. It left the cracked roof as it was, because rain entered there and ran down a wall into a place where a young growth had taken hold.

At a seam between two inhabitants, one was slowly gaining ground. The power watched them both. Neither was failing, and the water beyond the seam was open to them. It did not move the seam.

In time, grains from the western pool passed through the marsh. Most came to rest in places where they could not grow. One lodged beneath a block on a distant path. There was a little soil there, and shade, and water that came and went with the rain.

The power found it but did not carry it elsewhere. It had already seen water remain beneath that stone through a dry season. It waited to see whether the grain would open.

When it did, a thread emerged and lay against the wet ground. By the following season there were several, almost too fine to see.
