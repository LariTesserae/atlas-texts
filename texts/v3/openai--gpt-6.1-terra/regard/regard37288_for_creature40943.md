The power came to the chamber and stayed long enough to distinguish the great note from the things that made it. It followed the roots into the stone and found where each small exchange took place: a charge passing from one surface to another, a mineral gathered, a minute repair made along the crystalline edge of a leaf.

It found the record, too. It could have read the history of the mountain there: the long settling of its weight, the opening and closing of seams, the passage of heat far beneath the precipice. Some events had left their marks in many places at once. Others survived only in a few altered cells near the ceiling, where the descending growth had been sheltered from abrasion.

The power did not need to break a leaf to read it. It listened, and kept what it learned.

The old instruments drew its attention. The cable ended in the rock, its far end crushed where a seam had closed. The gauge had stopped because a grain of pale mineral had lodged behind the needle. The reading had been harmless on the day it was made. The power left the grain where it was. A working gauge would have told nothing useful now, and setting it right would have disturbed the roots.

There was one thing approaching the chamber that the growth could not record, because it had not yet reached the mountain. Far below, people were studying a deposit of ore. They had marked possible roads and drilling sites on maps. One of the proposed shafts passed near a deep seam that supplied the chamber with mineral vapor. Another would cut through rock under strain. The people had only a poor idea of either.

The power wanted them to know more before they chose.

A surveyor examining cores from an earlier bore found, in a box thought to have been catalogued, several lengths of stone that had been set aside. She tested them and showed the results to her colleagues. They made further measurements, revised their maps and abandoned the nearer shaft. Work began elsewhere on the mountain, after arguments and delays, and at a greater cost than some had expected.

The chamber was not on their maps. Its seam remained open. The roots continued their exchanges, and the note filled the crowded space as before.

The power returned from time to time. It found changes too slight for a visitor to see: a rosette drawing less current as the charge in a neighboring seam diminished; a mat sending fine new roots toward another source; a single blade no longer passing its share of the mountain’s sound.

The power could have kept that blade useful. Instead it watched while, over many years, the neighboring leaves took up what the blade had carried. At last the blade separated at its base and came to rest among the branches below. There it remained, gradually covered by pale grains.

Much later, a person entered the chamber by chance. They stood sideways in the narrow opening, startled by the noise, and looked at the little housings among the fronds. When they withdrew, the sound stopped. They waited on the ledge for some time, looking back at the entrance, then descended carefully to the valley.

The growth registered a change in the air, then another. A few particles settled on its leaves. It went on taking the measure of the mountain, and the power, which had listened to it before, listened again.
