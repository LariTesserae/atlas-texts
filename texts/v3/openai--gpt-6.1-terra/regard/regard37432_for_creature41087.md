The power listens first.

Not to the roar alone, but to the trough filling and emptying, the water crossing the plate, the pressure held behind the walls. Far below, people depend on the dam. Upstream, the reservoir presses against it. The pumps have work to do. Nothing here can be made still without changing something else.

The power attends, too, to the small life in the drain: the rotifer folded around its moss thread, the cells it feeds upon, the nematodes moving through the water films. None has asked to be saved. They go on feeding and being carried away.

On a sheltered side of the drain packing, above the strongest current, the power loosens a few fibers. It turns them outward into a fine, tangled fringe, anchored securely where they will not foul the grate. Water can pass through the fringe; some of it stays there between discharges.

The next time the valve opens, the trough fills. A sheet of water travels over the plate and through the packing. A few particles lodge in the fibers, then a bacterium, then another. The rotifer remains where it is, gripping its moss thread until the trembling passes.

The fringe will not last forever. It may be torn loose, or cleaned away by a worker who has no reason to notice it. The creatures among its fibers will live and die, as they would have done without it. For now, it holds a little water when the trough is drawn down, and offers another place for something to catch.

The power does not ask the rotifer to know what has happened. It leaves the lamps burning, the pumps drawing, the gates opening and closing. Above the court, cloud moves past the slot of sky. In the packing, when the floor grows still enough, the rotifer extends its crown and feeds.
