The power came at night, when the dust was rising through the center of the courtyard.

It did not come down through the roofs. It was already in the saltstone, in the heat leaving the walls, in the slow oil moving through the leaves. But now it attended to these things, and to the growth threaded beneath the paving.

For many days it watched. It saw the fronds turn toward light and new ones open in the heat. It saw fallen leaves darken the mineral around the roots. It followed the drafts through the old flues and felt how they changed when the stones cooled. Nothing here was waiting for rain.

One shutter had worked loose at the top of its frame. Each day it knocked a little farther out, and the clay behind it had begun to crumble. If it fell, it would break the young growth below and narrow the flue.

The power pressed the shutter back into place. It gathered the fallen grains of clay and packed them into the gap, leaving the opening around the stone as it had been. When the draft rose, the shutter knocked again, though not quite so hard.

The power stayed until morning. A pale frond opened beside the repaired flue, and the first bar of light moved across the floor. The leaves turned slowly after it.

Then the power gave its attention to other places. In the courtyard, the dust went on settling into the seams, and the fronds rattled together above their roots.
