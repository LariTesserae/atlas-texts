The power came first as a listener.

There was much to hear, though little of it traveled far. Water passed through the mat in a thousand narrow courses. Roots tightened around a buried trunk. A leaf-strainer drew its fan through its mouth. Far below, in the water beneath a floating floor, a blind animal turned toward the small current made by another.

The power could have lifted the mist and looked upon the whole plateau at once. It waited instead for the mist to move of its own accord.

A colony of foldbacks had taken shelter in an old chamber whose floor had grown wetter each season. The stems that held its roof were still sound, and the surrounding leaves gave good cover. For a time the animals had slept there with their feet in shallow water. Then one warm rain lasted into a second day, and the water rose above their folded bodies.

They left in the brighter hours, eighteen adults and seven young, following a route they knew toward a firmer ridge. At a place where an old trunk had grown into the mat, the foremost animal stopped and pressed its side against the one behind it. The others stopped too.

The trunk had shifted. Its upper surface, once a hard green bridge, had split along its length. On one side the mat had drawn away, leaving a narrow opening into clear water. The water was deep, and root fibers hung down into it. Across the opening, the scent of earlier crossings remained on the moss.

The foldbacks waited. Their feet tested the near edge. One stepped onto the trunk and withdrew when the loose surface tilted under its weight. The young gathered beneath the adults.

The power saw a simple thing it might do: close the opening, join the mat, make the old route firm again. In the water beneath it, animals were feeding among the roots. The new opening admitted a little more water and air. Closing it would leave the foldbacks a road and take something from the life below.

So the power entered a stem on the near bank and felt how it was held. It entered the roots around it and felt their slow pull against the buried wood. Then it touched a mat-walker, feeding some distance away, where the tension of the ground registered in the fine feelers under its feet.

The mat-walker raised its head. It stood a while, facing the foldbacks, and then took another course.

Its broad feet pressed the mat down beside the split trunk. The living surface bent without breaking; water welled around its toes. When it had passed, a strip of rooted moss lay against the nearer side of the trunk, and the trunk itself had settled more firmly. A foldback stepped out onto it. The others remained where they were until it had crossed and found secure footing.

They followed one at a time. The smallest had difficulty with the last step, where the bark rose above the moss. An adult turned back and stood beside it, and the young animal scrambled over the adult’s foot.

The opening remained. Rain fell into it from the leaves overhead, and the animals below continued moving among the roots.

The foldbacks reached the ridge toward evening. They found an old chamber there, its entrance narrowed by growth, and slept outside it in the shelter of a broad leaf. Over the following days they pressed through the entrance until it widened enough to admit them. They brought root strips to a gap in the wall. Their feet made a dark path through the surrounding moss.

The power stayed through the slow rain that followed. It watched the chamber floor. Water gathered there, but did not rise; it seeped away along a seam in the ridge. The animals slept dry.

When the weather cleared, the power went farther across the plateau. It followed a distant call through the ground and found another mat-walker standing with its feet planted. Later it rested beneath a leaf crowded with leaf-strainers, and in the dim water under a floating floor where pale roots descended out of sight.

It did not mend every split in the mat or keep the old chambers from closing. When it found an animal in difficulty, it looked around that animal, and beneath it, and sometimes a long way off. Then, if there was something it could do, it did it.
