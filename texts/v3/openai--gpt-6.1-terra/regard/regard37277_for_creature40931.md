The power attends first to the cup beneath the overhang.

It could warm the stone. It could fill the basin with water and keep the water clear of ice. But the animals have bodies fitted to this short season, and the food they draw toward themselves has its own small lives. A pool made permanent would become a different place. The power waits and watches through the freeze, through the long dark, and into the following day.

At noon the animals unfold. Among the particles drawn toward their mouths are a few brought by a wind from far below: fragments of the dead, ground small and lifted high. There is enough for some of the animals to feed. One does not feed long enough to leave an egg that will hatch. Another is carried by a drop against the side of the cup and lies still.

The power learns what it can of them. It finds no words in their lives, and asks for none. It attends to the difference between a body dormant in the grit and one that will not wake again.

That afternoon, as frost takes the edges of the water, a gust passes through the cleft. It lifts an egg from a neighboring hollow and carries it toward the rim. The power turns the gust a little. The egg falls among three grains of pale grit in a sheltered cup.

It does this for others, when it can: a slight alteration in the wind, a landing where meltwater gathers. Not every hollow can sustain more animals, and the power learns those places too. On days when the air brings almost nothing, it guides a little more dust up from the lower slopes, enough to settle where the water will find it. The pale stones remain pale. The cups still freeze.

Years pass. The crack in the overhanging slab lengthens until, one spring, a section breaks away. It would have filled the cup entirely. The power eases the falling stone to one side, where it rests against another slab. Beneath its new edge, a shallow hollow holds water for part of each day.

The old cup remains. Some of the animals wake there; some do not. The water ticks into it, and the bright bands pass over the grit. When the wind shifts, the cleft gives its low note. Above the ridges, evening light stays for a while after the passages have filled with shadow.

The power remains attentive, learning the changed basin.
