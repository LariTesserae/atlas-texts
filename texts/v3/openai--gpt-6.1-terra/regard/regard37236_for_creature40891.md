The power came quietly, because a town is not an empty thing, even when nothing in it moves.

It went first to the cistern and looked into the shallow water. It listened to the sound at the channel’s lip until it could hear the bird’s note and the voice within it. Then it walked the lanes without disturbing the bright drops of rain.

Under the bench, it saw the grey animal’s folded legs. It saw the worn place on the digging stick where a thumb had rested through many days of use. It looked at the animals among the garden leaves and did not decide that they were trespassers.

At last it understood enough to make a small opening.

The person with the bowl felt a drop strike their wrist.

They drew breath. Across the court, the person with the digging stick blinked and took their weight from the handle. A bird shifted its feet on the roof. Water passed over the lip of the channel with a sound that went on for a little while and then changed.

The rest remained still. No one was hungry or cold; no roof would fail while they spoke. The power had given them an interval, and held it open.

It told them what it had found.

Some were frightened. One asked after the herd, and the power showed them the animals standing safely where they had stood before. Another wanted to know how long the town had been this way, but there was no useful answer: beyond the edge of their stillness, there had been no seasons by which to count.

“Can we go on?” the person with the bowl asked.

“Yes,” said the power.

“And will we remember this?”

“You can, if you wish.”

They spoke together for a long time. They went into the houses and came out again. They touched the rain, and watched the animals, and argued in the courts. The power stayed where it could be found, and answered what it could.

When they were ready, they asked for the town to go on.

The power did not promise them that no creature would die, or that rain would always come when it was wanted. It did promise that the stillness would not close upon them again. It also gave them a way to call it: water poured from a bowl onto the cistern stone, and a request spoken aloud. There would be no penalty for asking, and no need to ask unless they wished.

Then it stepped back.

The rain fell through the rest of its short distance. The bowl rang softly beneath it. At the garden, the person with the digging stick watched one of the small animals pull a leaf free, and went over to see how much of the plant was left. The grey animal came out from under the bench and stretched, while the person at the doorway finished what they had been saying.

The power lingered beyond the terraces until evening, listening to the town grow quieter. When someone poured water onto the cistern stone, it attended at once. But the person was only rinsing a bowl, and went back inside carrying it under one arm.
