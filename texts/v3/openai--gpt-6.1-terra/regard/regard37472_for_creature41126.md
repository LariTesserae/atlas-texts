The power came quietly, because the note was already very faint.

It stood for a long time beside the shaded block, attending to the seam creature that had touched the unset resin. The creature did not move. The power felt the warmth in the clay, and beyond the warmth, no more than anyone else could have felt: a hand had been there, and might come back.

It did not finish the seam.

It put down a small bead of water beside the creature. The creature turned, touched the bead, and carried it away beneath the edge of the block. A little later it returned with two others. They examined the resin and went back into the joint.

The power visited the gardens. It watched the deer and the frogs, and the bird whose reflection held its beak just clear of the water. It listened to the note at the nearest garden and at the farthest. It lay a palm against the paving and felt the seam creatures moving inside.

In the rows of marks beneath a cane frame, it made a mark of its own. It waited. After a while a seam creature came out and inspected it. The creature went away and returned with a short length of fiber, which it laid across the mark.

The power understood that the fiber had been brought there; it was less certain what the creature intended by it. So it left the fiber where it lay.

There was heat enough in the place to tire a visitor, but the gardens had shade, and the animals held their positions without any visible distress. The power could have pulled down the white matting, or set a wind through the terraces. It could have made every drop fall at once. Instead, it chose an empty stretch of paving and lifted a single drop from beneath a slab.

The drop stretched. The note trembled.

The power held it there for a moment, then let it return. It resumed its roundness under the slab; the note steadied.

The power had learned something, though not yet enough to know whether a falling drop would be welcome. It went to the wall with the doorway. The deer visible through it remained on the other side, its reflection present in both basins. The power did not disturb the doorway. Beside it, in a blank part of the wall, it pressed a second opening with its hand.

This one led to a small room.

The room had a floor of cool clay and a roof through which a little daylight entered. A bowl of water stood there, and a folded mat. Against one wall were fiber, cane, pointed bone, and a pot of resin, each placed separately so that nothing had to be taken up. On the other side of the room was another door. It opened onto a world of moving air, where grasses bent and lifted again. That door could be closed from within.

The power left the first doorway open too, between the room and the terraces. Anyone who wished could enter the room, rest, and return.

For the seam creatures, it made a narrow passage under the threshold. One found the passage almost immediately and went through. By the time the power had stepped back from the wall, three more had followed. They inspected the joins around the doors and began, without hurry, to tend them.

The power returned to the unfinished block. The four fingers and the thumb were still visible in its edge. Near them it placed a piece of fiber, long enough to be picked up easily, with one end laid across the paving toward the new room.

Then it waited at a distance.

The light stayed bright. The insect’s note continued. In the garden, a deer’s wet eyes faced the place where its maker had stood. Along the wall the seam creatures moved in and out of sight, darkening the clear water in the joints.

At last the loose end of the fiber shifted.

It drew slowly across the paving, stopped, and drew again. The power could not see what held it at the block. It remained where it was until the fiber had been pulled out of sight; then it turned toward the doorway and left the terraces by the new room.

The bowl was still full. Outside, the grasses were moving. The power left the door open a little way and went on, so that whoever came after would find the room empty.
