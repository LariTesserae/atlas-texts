The power first noticed the plateau by its heat.

From a distance the warmth appeared steady. Closer, it resolved into thousands of narrow fluctuations: a drum slowing under a fresh burden, a duct opening as its fibers contracted, a current of oil diverted through a vessel that had formed around a crack. The heat moved through the growth and returned to the machinery, changed by its passage. A machine could no longer have been lifted out without taking living material with it.

The power waited through the settling of a factory mound. It watched the roof descend, the roots stretch, and a new bridge take hold across the widening seam. Under the fallen roof, a pocket of heat remained. Fine fibers entered it. In time they would find the exposed teeth of a stalled gear.

There was damage throughout the plateau. Some of it was being repaired; some was being incorporated into the foundation. At one edge, however, a buried drum was wearing through its support faster than the growth could reinforce it. The fibers reaching into the gap were repeatedly sheared away. Oil gathered beneath the bearing and ran into an old, sealed chamber, where no comb or vessel could recover it.

If the drum fell, it would pull several ducts closed. The heat beneath them would rise. A broad section of the living surface would burn, and the machinery under it might stop.

The power could have lifted the drum into place at once. Instead, it made room beside the failing bearing and held the weight there. This took pressure off the torn fibers without moving the drum farther than its ordinary travel.

The roots entered the new space. Their first ends were cut by the turning axle, but the pressure had lessened, and the next layer held. The power kept the drum steady while fibers wrapped around it, hardened, and wore smooth. Oil began to pass through them. When the new bearing could carry the load, the power let it do so.

The sealed chamber still contained the lost oil. The power opened a narrow seam into it from below. A root found the seam and followed it down. Over a long interval the chamber emptied, and the recovered oil went back into circulation.

These were small repairs. There were other places where machinery was failing, and the power attended to those it could reach without pulling apart the structures that depended on them. It cleared a blocked air channel beneath a bed of seedlings. It strengthened a cracked drum wall from the outside, leaving the living housing undisturbed. In a hot pocket where the growth had repeatedly sent fibers and lost them, it cooled the metal just enough for the fibers to hold.

It did not make the machinery immune to wear. The growth had taken its shape around heat, strain, and the slow opening of gaps. Too great a change would have closed some of the places it used and starved others of warmth.

The power also looked beyond the plateau. The ground there was colder and contained little that the grains could use. It laid a length of warm mineral seam across the stone beyond the last escarpment, with sheltered hollows in its surface and oil held deep enough that it would seep out slowly. It joined one end to an existing channel of heat beneath the plateau.

For a time nothing grew there. Dust collected in the hollows. Then a few grains from a vent settled along the seam. Most did not open. One sent a fine fiber into a crack, followed by another. The first shoot remained low, drawing warmth through the stone while it built a collar around itself.

The power left the rest of the seam bare. The shoot would grow as far as conditions and its own fibers carried it.

Back on the plateau, a repaired drum turned beneath its living bearing. The bearing thickened unevenly where the load was greatest. Roots crossed the chamber that had held the lost oil, fixing themselves to its walls. Above them, a stand of ribbed stems leaned into another and began to pull.

The power remained until the repairs had passed through several cycles of heat and strain. Then it withdrew its hold from the last supported axle.

The tremor continued through the packed layers. Near a vent, pale seed structures loosened grain by grain into the rising air.
