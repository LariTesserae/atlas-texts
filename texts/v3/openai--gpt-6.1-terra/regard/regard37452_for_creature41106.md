The power came in with a cloud and stayed after the cloud had passed.

At first it did nothing. Rain fell, and the leaves folded. The towers sounded one by one as the wind found their crowns. Along the edge of a pool, a wall lifted slowly, with fragments of old leaves caught between its stems.

The power followed one pale stem beneath the surface. It had forked so many times that some of its crowns grew half a mile apart. Between them, the older lengths had blackened or broken, and in places other plants had grown through the gaps. One crown stood on the bank of a pool; another had been carried on a loosened wall and had rooted where the water left it.

The power could not find an undamaged path from every crown to every other. It did not need one to attend to them all.

For many showers it watched the plant grow. It watched the loss of leaves, the opening of buds, the patient entry of white threads into torn ground. Where a crown died, others went on. Where a stem was buried, a bud sometimes waited in the dark and rose when the covering shifted.

Then came a storm that lasted longer than the others.

The leaves closed and remained closed. Water ran through the channels until it filled them and spread across the green floor. Several walls came apart in the first day. Their stems floated away and lodged against a ridge, where they caught more loose growth. The ridge held, and a broad pool gathered behind it.

By the second day the pool had reached the base of a tower. Water entered a split in the tower’s wall, and its low note ceased. The ridge began to lean. Beneath it, deep in the black felt, a long layer of old roots was pulling loose. If it gave way, much of the pool and a wide sheet of the floor would slide toward the plateau’s steep edge.

The power felt the strain traveling through the wet layers. It felt the small plant’s stems stretched among them, and the submerged roots of other plants clinging to material that was beginning to move.

It could have held the ridge upright. Instead, it opened a narrow way through a lower fold of ground, far from the edge. Water began to run into an empty basin on the other side.

The cut widened. Stems tore free and went with the current; the clear stalks at the pool margin folded under. For a while the power steadied the sides of the channel, leaving the torn ends of living roots in reach of the wet earth. Then it let the water shape the opening for itself.

The pool fell slowly. The ridge settled, still bowed but no longer pulling so hard at its roots. Late in the storm, air found its way back into the tower, and a knocking sounded inside it.

When the rain ended, the small plant had lost several crowns. One of its pale stems lay exposed along the new channel, with fine roots dangling in the flow. Over the following days, those roots caught against the bank. White threads entered the soft ground. A bud thickened beside the water and opened a narrow crown of leaves.

The power remained to see this. It watched the basin fill, and the loosened stems take root along its far side. At dawn, nearly colorless leaves rose on clear stalks from the shallows there.

After a time another wall lifted beside the basin. The power left it to rise and fall.

Clouds continued to cross the plateau. Rain warmed the surface; vapor drifted low between the towers. Beneath the green floor, roots grew into dark seams, holding some of the old material in place while other parts softened and came away. The power kept its attention there, quiet enough for the water to be heard.
