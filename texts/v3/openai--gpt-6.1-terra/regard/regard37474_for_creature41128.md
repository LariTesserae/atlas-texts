The power came quietly enough that nothing fled.

It had seen plains from a great height before: their pale distances, their weather, the slow changes in the shapes of their edges. Here it came close. It looked beneath a leaf where a beetle had laid its eggs. It followed a root down through the dry crust. It watched a worker termite turn in a passage with a fragment of stem held crosswise in its jaws, finding room to proceed only after several small adjustments.

The power could have covered the plain in rain. It could have raised green forests where the towers stood.

It did neither. The shrubs were living well in the soil they knew, and the colonies had made their chambers for the air that reached them through their walls. A flood would fill those chambers. Shade would cool them, perhaps too much. The birds and lizards and beetles belonged to this place as it was, and the power could not give one creature a different world without giving it to all the others too.

So it stayed and watched through the afternoon.

A lizard caught a beetle at the foot of a tower. The power felt the quick closing of its jaws and the beetle’s last struggle. It watched the lizard return to shade and settle there, fed. It did not make light of either life.

Toward evening the wind strengthened. Dust struck the towers and slid down their sides. In one old mound, a fluted wall gave way, and a rush of dry earth fell into a gallery near the surface. The termites inside withdrew. Others came toward the break from deeper passages, touching the fallen grains with their antennae.

A soldier waited at the opening while workers began to clear it. They carried grains away one at a time, but more dust kept blowing in.

The power laid its hand, if a movement of air can be called a hand, along that side of the tower. The wind passed over it. In the sudden shelter the loose earth settled, and the workers could reach the edge of the collapse. They brought damp pellets from within and pressed them into the breach. By nightfall a low wall stood across it, rough and dark against the older clay.

The power remained nearby until the wall held. Then it let the wind move freely again.

Across the plain, other walls were being repaired. Birds found places to sleep in the shrubs. A hunter passed between two hummocks, leaving narrow prints in the dust. The power followed for a while, attentive to the small sounds beneath the foliage, and did not ask the hunter to go hungry.

By morning, the workers had covered the fresh patch with another layer. The outer surface was drying in the sun. Beside it lay a few grains of pale dust, already caught against the wall.
