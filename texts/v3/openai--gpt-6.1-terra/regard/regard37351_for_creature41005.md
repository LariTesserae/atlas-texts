The power came first to the upper canopy, where the blades were sounding in a wind that had crossed a great distance. It could have quieted them and made room for itself among the stalks. Instead it remained above, and listened through the noise.

The sound carried news from a long way off. A blade broken in one place altered the pitch of its neighbors; the alteration traveled through the threads and stems, diminishing as it went. Beneath it were slower changes, too low to be heard in air: the filling of a hollow, the strain of a root against crust, water moving through a seam.

The power attended to these for a long time. It found no single place from which the upland governed itself. It found many places where damage was answered by growth, and where growth met other growth and had to turn aside. Some connections carried nourishment for miles; others ended after a few stalks. There were dark patches that had been sustained and dark patches that had not. The power did not take either kind as proof that nothing could be done.

Beyond the upland, weather was gathering. Several days hence, a mass of cold rain would reach the young ridges on its western side. The canopy was already turning silver there. Most of it would bear the weight, but in one low region the hollows were nearly full from earlier rain. The new water would split the soft crust, drown the root tips working along it, and carry a broad mat of living tissue down into the cloud.

The power followed the water’s probable courses. Much of the mat would take hold again where it came to rest. Much would not.

It went to the edge of the approaching rain. There, over the cloud, it made a little warmth and held it in place. The front widened. Rain began to fall earlier, over a greater distance, and the wind reached the upland in several gusts rather than one.

Even so, the western hollows filled. Broad leaves lifted to make passages, and water moved beneath them toward a low seam. The seam was too narrow. For a time the water stood above it, trembling under the falling rain.

The power touched the mineral crust beside the seam. It softened a short length and eased the sides apart. Water entered, carrying spores and torn green tissue. Some of the small plants on the seam’s edge were washed away; others bent in the current and held. By morning the hollow had fallen, and the exposed root tips were laying new films along the widened course.

The power remained while the rain passed. It watched the plants close over the channel, leaving a passage beneath their leaves. Farther west, a ridge had broken despite what it had done. The fallen material lay in the cloud below, and fine shoots were already rising from it.

When the wind settled, the blades continued to sound. The power did not make them quieter, or give them another kind of voice. It stayed long enough to hear the changed note where the water had gone through, then left the upland to its weather.
