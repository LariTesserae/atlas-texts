The power came to the hollow on a day when the basin was dry.

It found the lizard on the middle stone. She had been waiting there a long while, her body flat against the warmth, her eyes turned toward the fern litter. Beneath the dead fronds, a cricket was cleaning its legs. A beetle moved through the softened heart of a root. Near the entrance, the bats slept.

The power listened to the stone as well. Water was moving above the hollow, left by rain that had fallen beyond the thorn trees. Most of it would pass through the limestone and continue downward, out of reach of the roots.

The power opened a small way for some of it.

A seam darkened in the ceiling. A bead formed at the tip of a root, swelled, and fell onto the crescent of stones. The lizard lifted her head. Another drop followed, and then another, until a thin thread of water found the old channel in the wall.

It ran for the better part of an hour. The basin filled only shallowly, leaving the rough lip exposed. Along the floor, the damp reached the fern roots and the pockets beneath the fungal fans. A cricket came out to drink and vanished when the lizard shifted. She waited, then lowered her own head to the water.

The power stayed through the night. It watched the bats leave and return, and watched the spider draw in a sagging strand of web. By morning the thread from the ceiling had stopped, though water remained in the basin.

Before leaving, the power loosened a little more of the stone along the new channel, so that future rains might find it too. The opening was narrow. In dry weeks it would give nothing; after rain, it might give the hollow a little longer.

The lizard returned to the middle stone the following day. A pale cricket stood at the far rim of the basin. She watched it for a time, then crossed the crescent on her sure feet, and it disappeared beneath the ferns.
