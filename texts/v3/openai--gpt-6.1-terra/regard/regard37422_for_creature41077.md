The power came after a rain, when the walls were dark and the gullies still running.

It listened first. The cicadas did not pause for it. A monkey shook a branch, and a spray of water fell through the leaves onto a skink, which ran beneath a root.

In a damaged wall, a worker termite had been caught under a grain of earth. Other workers passed through the chamber around it, carrying pellets toward the breach. The power lifted the grain. The termite drew its legs in, then moved away along the gallery.

Farther down the slope, a frog was swallowing a beetle. The power felt the beetle’s struggle and the frog’s hunger. For a time it stayed beside them, attending to both. Then it reached back through the hours and opened a small cavity beneath the bark where the beetle had rested that afternoon. The beetle found it and remained hidden until the frog had gone.

The frog ate a moth instead. The power made the moth’s dying quick.

It moved through the ridges slowly. Where a colony’s fungus beds were failing, it let a little more moisture seep into the chambers. Where a root had blocked a passage, it softened the earth on either side, and the workers opened a new route. It left the ants their roads and the bats their roosts.

In one hollow, the smallest of the pools was nearly dry. Tadpoles crowded the brown water, stirring the silt with their tails. The next rain was days away. The power drew water up through the ground until it lay a finger’s depth over the leaves, then shaded the hollow with the fallen branch of a tree whose upper limbs had died.

The branch made a bridge as well. Ants began crossing it before morning.

The power stayed through the dry interval. Some of the tadpoles became frogs; some were eaten. A snake found a skink asleep under bark. A bird carried a termite to its young. The power could feel each life close, and it did what it could for them without leaving the hungry to hunger. It did not finish the work. There were always more bodies moving in the litter, more chambers under the ground than it could attend to at once.

When the rains returned, a shower opened a long seam along a ridge. Water entered the exposed galleries, and soldiers gathered at the torn edge. Workers brought damp earth to them.

The power held the heaviest water back for an hour. The workers filled the seam from both ends, leaving a rough, new wall. By dawn, droplets had gathered on it, and a snail was grazing along its lower edge.

The power remained nearby. It learned the paths to the pools and the hollows beneath the roots. It learned where the ground dried first and where young trees were most likely to fall. When another rain came, it was there to see what it might wash open.
