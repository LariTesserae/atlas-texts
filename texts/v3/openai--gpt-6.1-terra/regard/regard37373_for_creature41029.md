The figure at the edge was not a traveler. It had no feet on the mountain. It was what the hollow made visible of a power attending to it.

The power could have silenced the rock. It could have drawn the cloud away and sent every suspended drop to earth. It did neither. A held breath might be distress, or it might be the shape of a creature’s life. The power did not know yet which.

It listened to the low note in the ground, then to the other notes, until it could hear each without losing the rest. It looked at the fish with the smaller fish inside them, at the mouths against the leaves, at the eye whose rim had gathered stones. It attended to the dark muzzle among the roots and to the ear beyond the tree. It found where their bodies joined, and where they did not, and kept that knowledge to itself.

Then it made a small place beside the hollow, within reach of the wind but sheltered from its cold. There it laid a drop of water on a flat stone.

The drop ran slowly across the stone and fell. Another took its place.

In the eye among the roots, the figure at the rim bent down. The pale seam of one wet-iron feather grew a little brighter. The power waited, giving the hollow as much time as it needed, though no time crossed the rim.

At last the large animal’s nostrils narrowed. They opened again.

The sound in the ridge continued. The birds’ notes remained in the air. But beneath the stone, the broad creature’s ribs eased downward, and the breath it had held passed softly through its mouth. A white insect turned toward it. One drop fell from a fern and struck a pool, making a new ripple inside the old one.

The power kept the place beside the hollow open. Any creature that came there could rest or return. If the motion growing through the hollow frightened or hurt them, they could let it slow again. It would not have to pass through them all at once.

For a long while the power stayed at the rim. It learned the difference between the clacking in the rock and the sound of the many-jointed birds shifting their feet. When the first of them closed its throat, its note lingered for a while, then grew faint enough that the mountain wind could be heard beneath it.
