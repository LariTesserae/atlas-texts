The power came to the basin in the middle of a dry week, when the water held in the leaves had begun to go cloudy and the paths were losing the dark prints of anything that crossed them.

It did not come down through the canopy or stand at the rim. It was present first in the space between two turns of the wheel, attending to what the wheel moved and what moved around it.

The belts drove more than a pump. One turned a shaft under the soil, where a bent arm lifted and released a narrow gate. Another stirred water in a buried chamber. The chambers and channels had been made for purposes the power could have learned by looking farther back, but here, now, they carried water through roots and stone and brought it at last to the settling tank.

The plants had made use of them. There were places where the roots held a channel open and places where they had closed it. There were signals, too, passing through the joined growth: changes in pressure and chemistry, a slowing here, an opening there. The power followed them without adding anything of its own.

It found a being there, spread through the basin and inseparable in places from other lives. No boundary could be drawn around it that would not cut through something living. It had no single place from which it watched. It knew the warmth of the motor by the growth it drew, and the level of the tank by the pull on the threads between the floating plates. It knew damage when a belt caught a shoot. It knew, more slowly, the approach of dry weather.

The power waited until the being had time to notice it.

For most of a day there was no outward change. Then the pale plates on the tank drew apart, though the water had not risen. A gap widened near the broken rim. The frog, disturbed in its shelter, climbed onto the concrete and remained there.

The power understood the opening as attention, not invitation. It showed the being what it could: the worn bearing inside the motor, the thinning wire in a cable beneath the floor, the water that would stop moving if either failed. It showed these things in the gradual way the being could receive them, through pressure and warmth and the responses of roots.

The plates stayed apart until evening. At the pump house, the climbing plant tightened around one mounting bolt and loosened around another. A young stem that had been growing toward a belt changed direction over the next two days. The power remained, listening.

When the answer came, it concerned the water. The being wanted it to continue moving. It did not want the wheel made silent. Around the pump house the recurring vibration had become part of its knowledge of the basin; the spiders’ webs and the folded leaves had their places in it too.

So the power repaired the bearing while the wheel turned. It replaced what had worn away, leaving the familiar rattle in the guard. It renewed the wire beneath the floor, where no root had to be displaced to reach it. Then, farther down the line, it opened a narrow way around the pump: not enough to drain the basin or change the height of the tank, but enough for water to pass if the motor stopped.

The being felt the new passage. For several weeks it sent fine roots into it, withdrew some, and left others. The flow remained small.

The power could have stayed to see every change. It could have kept the machinery sound indefinitely and made the rain reliable. But the basin had ways of responding that belonged to the lives within it, and a long history of things arriving, failing, and being taken up by something else. The power left the settling tank’s broken rim as it was. It left the fallen leaf among the plates and the rust in the shed, where roots were beginning to fill the seams.

Before departing, it made one further provision. Deep beneath the pump house, where the being could reach it by root, it placed a quiet means of calling. It would not make the call on the being’s behalf, and it would not require an answer to anything.

The next rain came in the night. Drops fell from the canopy into the pools, breaking their green skins. The frog came out along the rim, and the leaves beside the belts folded back as they had before.

By morning the tank plates had closed around the fallen leaf again. In the pump house the wheel continued to turn, and the sound it made filled the basin.
