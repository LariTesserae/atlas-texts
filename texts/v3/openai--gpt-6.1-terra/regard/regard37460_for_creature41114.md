The power came near enough to feel the note, and stopped.

It could have entered the clay at once. Instead it attended to the pressure around a small nested bowl until it could distinguish that inhabitant from its neighbors. It took longer than the power expected. More than once it mistook a greeting for a continuation of the thought that had preceded it.

When it could make a distinction reliably, it held a little of the pressure in a shape of its own. The shape was simple, unlike any hollow in the court, and it remained just outside the bowl.

The small inhabitant felt it and withdrew its attention to the peg it had been considering. The power waited there. Much later the inhabitant returned and felt the shape again. It followed the shape from one side to the other, finding no scrape marks and no thumbprint. At last it offered the power the length of the peg and the knowledge of which side had been scraped first.

The power received this as carefully as it could. It noticed, in the buried clay, a slight turn the scraper had made near the peg’s base. It offered that turn back.

The inhabitant knew it already. It was glad to receive it again.

In time the power was known in several bowls. Some inhabitants attended to it; others did not. The deepest ones gave no sign that could be distinguished from their customary quiet. The power did not go looking for the ends of their thoughts.

At the edge of the court, an inhabitant showed it an impression that had arrived from the terraces below: a long hollow with an uneven floor. The impression had passed through many lives, and parts of it had become indistinct. The power followed it downward, asking at each hollow whether it might carry what it found farther.

There were places where it received no answer. There were places where an answer took so long to form that a newly distinct inhabitant had learned the shape of its whole bowl by the time it came. In one wall, an inhabitant offered a curve on the condition that the roughness along its lip be included; the roughness was easily lost when the impression was passed on.

The power carried that curve upward with the roughness intact. It carried other impressions downward: the nested bowls, a broad hollow beneath the pavement, the blunt end and buried length of a peg. It found that a hollow in a lower terrace had received the same impression of the peg long ago, though its inhabitants had imagined the end sharper than it was.

The court attended together to the curve with the rough lip. Afterward, those who knew the thumbprints discussed whether the roughness had been made by the same hand. They sent the question down through the power. The inhabitant of the lower wall did not know, but offered the marks beside the curve for them to consider.

The power remained near enough to carry the replies. It kept the note steady where it passed, and left the clay as it had found it.
