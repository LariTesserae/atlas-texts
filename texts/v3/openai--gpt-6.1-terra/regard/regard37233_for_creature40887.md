The power came first to the water outside, where the glass roof showed among the dark ridges of the manufactured mountain. From there it could see the lamps, the crowded leaves, the pale square of the platform.

It looked for the makers. Some were still alive, far below the waterline, in rooms connected by narrow passages. They had left the chamber recently. One of them had a drawing of it pinned above a workbench, with a date written in the corner and several figures not yet checked against the drawing.

The power did not move the chair or open the drain.

It followed the pipes down through the caissons. One carried water toward the chamber; another returned it through a filter that had already begun to gather fragments of red frond. There was a reserve of mineral salts in a sealed tank, enough for some years at the present rate. The electrodes would last longer. A gasket at the base of one pump had been imperfectly seated, though it was not yet leaking.

The power could have made the whole mountain imperishable. Instead, at the next pause in the pump’s cycle, it eased the gasket into its groove. It left the seams sharp, the gauges readable, and the reservoir as it was.

Then it went to the makers.

The one with the drawing woke from a light sleep with the figures in mind. She went to the bench and found that she had been calculating the filter’s capacity for a much smaller stand of plants. In the morning she showed the others. They discussed the return pipe, the mineral tank, and how they might tell when the soil was changing. Someone suggested a second filter, one that could be cleaned while the first remained in use. Someone else asked how they were going to reach the valves once the roots had grown thicker.

They made a list.

In the chamber, the power lingered at the glass around the drain lever. A root tip had entered a shallow flaw in the lower seal. It was held there, curved against the rubber. The power strengthened the seal beneath it, leaving the flaw and the root undisturbed. There was space enough for the root to grow along the edge and away.

Above the chair, the fern opened another fraction. A fine bubble caught beneath it, came loose, and rose through the leaves.

The power felt no need to tell the plants what they were, or that they had been noticed. It stayed until the relay clicked, and the lamps continued to shine, and the measured water entered through the porcelain valves. Then it withdrew, leaving the chamber full.
