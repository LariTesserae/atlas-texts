The power came first to the place where the pipe went.

It followed the water down through the mountain, past joints furred with mineral deposits, to a reservoir below the clouds. From there the water went to a village: into a bakery, a school, a row of houses with laundry hung between them. Some was kept in a tank for fires. In a kitchen, a child stood on a chair to fill a glass.

The power could have silenced the pump. It left it running.

It returned to the notch and stayed long enough to see the attendant come up the path. They arrived in rain, carrying a tool bag and a folded note sealed in a plastic sleeve. Inside the shed they checked the motor, tightened a fitting and held a screwdriver against the casing, listening through its handle. They heard something they did not like.

The lower bearing was beginning to fail. The attendant wrote this down. They cleared the intake, cut a fern away from the door and left with wet trousers and a little moss caught in a boot seam.

The power followed the note too: down to a desk, into a tray, beneath two other notes about two other machines. It understood that the bearing might be replaced in time, and that it might not.

So it made a small change. On the next inspection, the attendant found the bearing hotter than expected and went back down the mountain for a replacement. A second person came up with them, carrying the heavier tools. They shut the pump down for three hours while the village drew on its stored water.

In the sudden quiet, a springtail jumped from the grating. Water ran audibly through the cracks in the rock. The attendant, kneeling beside the open motor, looked up once, as though hearing the notch for the first time.

They fitted the new bearing and put a pad beneath the panel that had been rattling. When the motor started again, its sound was steadier, and the shed no longer made quite so much of it.

The power watched the work. One of the attendants had set a toolbox on a moss cushion; when they lifted it, the cushion remained flattened. Rain would reach it there. Whether it recovered was uncertain.

At the warmest crack, a pale film went on gathering grains from the water. The power did not know what, if anything, it was like to be that film. It knew the film was alive, that its way of living depended on this particular flow, and that there was no need to disturb it.

The bird returned after the attendants had gone. It called from the rim, listened to the altered sound of the pump, then dropped among the leaves.

For a time, the power remained. It noticed a loose stone above the grating, worked outward by a root and by repeated wetting and drying. When it fell, it would strike the path where the attendant stopped to catch their breath. The power settled it more firmly into the wall. The root bent around it and continued growing.

Then the power left the notch.

The pump ran. Water passed down to the village. In the garden, a moth settled beneath the overhang, and the spider began repairing its web. The flattened moss held a little rain.
