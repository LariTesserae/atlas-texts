The power came to the hollow without entering it. There was no space among the leaves that was not already occupied, and no need to make one.

It attended first to the hum. In the long interval between two notes, it followed the roots up through frozen soil, and down through the pale floor, and out along the transparent leaves. It found the old plant joined to itself in more places than its shape suggested. Some of the finest connections passed through other living things: threads that entered roots and came away carrying water, films of growth that bound a broken stem to its neighbor. There were places where the plant’s tissue ended and began again, and places where that distinction could not easily be made.

The power listened through many hums. It followed the burrower through a waking and watched it sleep again. A grazer was caught beneath its flank, where the fur pressed it into the pelt. The animal worked its legs for a long time, making little progress. The power lifted a single hair. Water moved into the space beneath it, loosening the strands, and the grazer came free.

Above the hollow, winter extended through the ground. The roots were still growing into it, keeping their tips alive in thin films of moving water. Some met stone and turned aside. Others entered cracks that widened by a grain each year as mineral matter gathered around them.

The power found another hollow some distance away. It was smaller and held only ice, old soil, and the dark remnants of roots. A narrow fracture led toward it from the living hollow, but the fracture was dry and closed in several places.

The power could have opened it at once. Instead it warmed the ice around the nearest obstruction until a little water ran through. When the water froze again, the crack widened. It did this repeatedly, leaving time between, until the passage held a seam of damp soil.

Then it waited for the burrower to wake.

The animal emerged slowly from its cavity, pulling free of the growth that had gathered around its back. It fed along a mineral rib, breaking off soft pieces with its worn claws. Near the new seam it stopped. Its muzzle moved over the damp ground. It began to dig, pushing loose soil beneath itself and turning now and then to tamp it against the walls.

The passage took shape over much of its waking. Spores rubbed from its coat into the damp places. When the animal reached the smaller hollow, it pushed its head through, rested, and withdrew. It did not seem ready to go farther. It slept beside the entrance, where the floor was warmer, and in its sleep its breath kept a patch of the passage wet.

A curled blade grew there. In time it carried two glassy beads into the passage, and one broke against a stone. The living speck inside found enough moisture to hold. The power kept a little water moving around it through the coldest part of the season. Once its first vein had lengthened and begun to warm itself, the power left it alone.

Years passed in the hollow beneath the rise. The burrower used the passage often, widening it where its shoulders rubbed. Grazers entered and made smaller tunnels in its walls. The young growth spread toward the second chamber, though it died back twice where the soil dried. Each time, some remained.

The old plant sent a root into the passage too. It grew alongside a trail the burrower had worn smooth. At one bend it met a mineral nodule and divided around it; beyond the bend, its two tips joined again.

The power did not know whether the root would reach the smaller hollow before the passage changed. It could see the growth of the ceiling and the slow gathering of stone, but the animals also dug, and water chose new courses, and the plants answered one another in ways that took time to become visible. The power continued to listen.

When the ceiling hummed, the note traveled a little way into the passage. The animals there stopped and held fast. Then the sound faded, and they resumed their feeding.
