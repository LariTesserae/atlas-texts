The power comes at a time when the structure is clouded. It does not enter the basin in any visible form. The long-legged creatures remain where they are, and the small animals in the ferns do not tick.

There is water beneath the stone, more than there has been in some time. The filaments have followed it down through a narrowing seam. One by one, they withdraw, leaving tracks that close slowly. The last remains in the ground. Around it the stone is warm enough to dry a fallen leaf.

The branching shapes gather at the wall facing the pool. They remain there through the afternoon. The solitary plant folds, and then the plants by the seep. An adult stands in the runnel with two young beneath it.

The power attends to the water and to the filament held beside it. The seam has shifted in the heat; it will not open again as it did before. Other seams will form, but not soon enough for the shallow pools.

There is a softer place in the rock a little way uphill. The power loosens it, grain by grain, until a thin crack joins it to the seam below. No water enters the crack yet. The end nearest the basin opens beneath a patch of bare stone, away from the roots.

The trapped filament moves. It draws back a short distance, then extends toward the new opening. For a long while nothing else happens. The creatures in the runnel settle against the wet stone, and the adult continues to stand over them.

By evening, the filament has entered the crack. The surface of the structure begins to clear, first in a small patch and then in a band facing the wall. Water appears there, one bead at a time. It travels down rather than up, collecting in a hollow that has not held a pool before.

The adult leaves the runnel and approaches the water. It folds its legs and drinks. One of the young comes after it; the other remains among the ferns until the first calls, making a sound too low to carry across the basin.

During the night, the structure sends two more filaments toward the crack. They travel on either side of it, pausing where the stone is thin. In the morning a thread of water reaches the old pool, and the leaves by the seep begin to open. A shelled animal slips from one leaf into the damp beneath it.

The power stays through another clouding and another clearing. The water takes the new route each time. Some reaches the old pools; some remains in the hollow uphill. The solitary plant turns one leaf toward it, and a root begins to grow in that direction.

When the power leaves, the crack remains. The filament that first entered it still moves there, following the water as the stone warms and cools.
