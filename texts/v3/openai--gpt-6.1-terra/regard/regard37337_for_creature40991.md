The power came in cloud, though the cloud was there already. It settled over the notch and stayed through a night of hard frost.

By morning it had found the seam of blue rock. Water had entered a crack high above the settlement and frozen there. A broad slab, still attached along its lower edge, leaned a little farther out each winter. When it fell, it would strike the outer trees first. The smaller stones beneath it would follow into the lanes.

The power held the slab for a while and listened.

There were roots in the crack. One had grown thick against the stone and would tear when the slab came away. Farther down, moss occupied a hollow beneath it, and a spider had sealed itself inside a fold of moss for the winter. The power could not leave the rock where it was forever without also stopping the root, the water, and the patient widening of the crack.

It waited until the middle of the day, when the canopy was wet and the old wood less brittle. Then it eased the slab away.

The root tore. A tree shuddered from its crown down to the place where its trunk joined the others. Snow slid from the branches in a soft rush. Several smaller stones fell ahead of the slab and disappeared among the trees.

The power guided the great stone down the outside of the notch, where it came to rest with one blue face tilted toward the sky. Some moss was crushed beneath it. The hollow where the spider slept remained on the upper side, exposed now to the cold, and the power drew a loose fold of moss back over it.

For a time, water ran freely from the torn root. It darkened the rock, gathered at the edge of a ledge and fell through the canopy. By evening the flow had slowed. The wet surface froze, and the tree held fast among its neighbours.

The fallen slab left an opening in the mountain wall. Light reached a chamber that had been dim for many years. Drops formed on the pale growth there and shone until the sun passed behind the ridge. One small tree, long pressed between two older trunks, had a little more room above it.

The power stayed through the next thaw and the frost after it. The settlement continued to move too slowly for most eyes: moss lifting with water, a root tightening around stone, needles settling into the hollow of a branch. At the torn place, a fine white edge began to grow over the wood.

The power loosened its hold. The slab remained where it had come to rest.

Cloud thinned over the notch. A bird arrived in the upper branches and shook a little snow into the new opening. Then it flew away, leaving the branches swaying after it.
