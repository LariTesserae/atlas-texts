The calls from the back reached the father as a vibration in the floor. He turned. The entrance was narrowing; a woman with two vessels was trying to lift one through before the roots closed on her arm.

He drew the child back. The red wall followed her hand a little way, then let it go.

A stem came down across the square. People scattered, except for those who could not move quickly enough, and the stem stopped just above their heads. Its leaves opened flat. Water spilled from them into the upturned vessels and over the people beneath. For a moment everyone stood looking at it. Then the woman pulled her arm free, and several hands reached up to hold the stem.

It was heavier than they expected. They shouted for help. More people came, setting their shoulders under it while the woman passed her vessels out. When she was clear, they let the stem settle. It lay across the square, leaving a passage beneath its leaves.

The opening streets began to arrive.

One came from the north, another from among the low roofs to the west. A traveler at the square’s edge felt for the cord at her neck and found its weight hanging straight down. She lifted it by the knot. The weight stayed still while the floor moved under her feet.

The streets did not cross the square. They opened along its crowded sides, each wide enough for people to walk two abreast. Those nearest them began to leave, carrying water and calling to the others. Someone waved a strip of leaf overhead until the people in the passage saw it and made room.

The father had knelt beside his daughter. She was breathing quickly, her face pressed to his shoulder. He had been carrying her since morning, and his arms shook when he lowered her.

A coolness touched the back of his neck. A fold of leaf had opened beside them. Inside it was a pool no larger than his two hands. He put a finger in and tasted the water. It was clean. He filled his cup and held it to the child’s lips.

She drank a little. When he took the cup away, she reached for it and drank more.

The coolness remained around them. The leaves above the square had turned their pale undersides toward the light, and drops were forming there, close together, quicker than dew. People held out cups, then spread lengths of growth-skin between them to catch what fell. The red bloom continued to pulse, but the roots no longer tightened around it.

The father sat with his daughter until her breathing eased. She looked up at the leaves and asked why they had turned over. He told her he did not know. After a while she wanted to stand. He helped her to her feet, and she walked a few steps before asking to be carried again.

By then the square was less crowded. People had taken water into the new streets, and others were returning with empty vessels. The traveler with the cord went out along the western avenue. She found that it led through three districts and opened beside a house whose doorway had been closing for days. The family inside had cut through two walls already and were resting before they tried the third. They came out carrying their mats and a pot of pins.

The avenues remained open through the night. In the morning their sides began to grow inward, slowly enough that people could see what was happening. Families who wished to use the new chambers along them moved in; others cut the young growth back to keep the passages clear. For several days there was water in the folds above the square. People collected it, shared it, quarreled over vessels, and carried news of it outward.

The child slept through much of the next day. When she woke, her father took her to a woman who knew illnesses. The woman listened to her chest with an ear against her back, felt her forehead, and told him to keep giving her water. He did. In time the child grew well enough to run ahead of him through a passage and hide in a leaf fold, waiting for him to find her.

The woman who had seen her brother walking away in two directions also came to the square. She stood beneath the turned leaves and asked, aloud, whether he was living. The roar of the city went on around her. Water gathered on a leaf tip and fell into her open hand.

She stayed until her hand was full, then drank. At home she left his place by the water garden as it was.

The power had been present at the red flower. It remained attentive as the city changed around the people: as houses traveled, veins broke, children were found, and people raised their strips to call one another close. It did not close the place into a shape that would last. When a floor lifted beneath a sleeping room, the old person at its doorway woke and roused the others. They gathered their things and moved into the chamber next to it, where the walls were still growing wide.
