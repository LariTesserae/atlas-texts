The power came first to the salt plain. It stood over one of the narrow seams and looked down through it. In the green far below, a herd of shinglebacks was crossing a leaf broad enough to roof a house. Their feeding marked its surface with a slow amber stain.

The power followed the seam into the stone. There it found a crack running outward from the chamber roof, much longer than the crack visible below. Each heave opened it a little farther. The next great movement of the floor would bring down a wide shelf of stone, closing two water seams and burying the dwellings beneath them.

There was time to move the people. There was also time to hold the shelf.

The power entered the chamber near a dwelling on a stone rib. It took a shape that could stand on the path: a person in leaf-fiber wrappings, carrying a pole. The pole had no cuts on it. A child noticed this and called to an adult, who came out of the shelter with her own pole held across the entrance.

The stranger told her about the crack.

She looked up at the roof, then at the pale bar of daylight crossing the path. She asked how the stranger knew. The power showed her a small stone held in its palm. A line appeared across the stone, then lengthened and branched, until it matched the crack above them.

The woman called to the neighboring shelf. Others came, bringing poles and lamps. They asked where the shelf would fall and how soon. The power showed them the edges of it, tracing the boundaries in light along the roof. One edge passed directly over a shelter. A man from that shelter went inside and began handing out water cups.

The power said it could hold the stone in place.

“For how long?” someone asked.

“As long as it needs holding.”

They discussed this among themselves. The man with the cups wanted the shelf held at once. Another person wanted to move first: she did not know what a stranger might do to the roof, or whether the floor would heave while they were all watching it. They sent two people along the rib to warn the next dwellings, and others began carrying water and tools toward a shelf outside the line of falling stone.

The power waited. When asked, it went with them, carrying bundles and testing the route ahead with its unmarked pole. At one crossing it stopped them: beneath the leaves, a burrower had been feeding, and the floor was hollow for several paces around it. They went by a longer path, cutting through a curtain that had grown across an older way.

By the time they reached the far shelf, the people from seven dwellings had gathered there. They could hear calls farther off as the warning traveled. Some had brought lamps, though it was still day. One child had a shallow cup of shingleback eggs cushioned in leaf fur.

The woman who had first met the power returned with it to the edge of the marked area. From there they could see the shelves below the threatened roof. No one remained on them.

“Hold it now,” she said.

The power reached into the stone. It found the long crack and the smaller fractures joining it. Without closing the seams that admitted light and cool air, it drew the broken edges together and set them against the weight above. Deep in the roof there was a grinding sound. White dust sifted down for a while, settling on leaves and shoulders.

The light along the boundary went out. The woman stood listening. Hammering continued in the stems around them, steady and widely spaced.

The power showed her the crack again in the stone on its palm. It was still there, but its edges had been pressed close.

When the people returned to their shelves, they found that the two water seams still dripped. A few cups had been left beneath them, and these were full. The man who had carried his cups away put them back at his doorway. Others went to see the place in the roof where the dust had fallen.

The power stayed through the next heave. The floor rose; the curtains swung; the paths shifted out of line. The held shelf stayed where it was. Afterward the people came out and called to one another, then began finding their routes again.

Before leaving, the power told them it would hear if they called for it. They asked how, and it taught them a sound they could make with the heel of a pole against stone. Several tried it until they were satisfied they had it right. Then they went on clearing the path, because a stand of new shoots had opened across it, and people farther along the rib were waiting to pass.
