The power found the city by way of the water leaving it.

At the basin’s edge, a thin flow passed under a shelf of silt and emerged somewhere beyond the shelf’s length. It carried salt and mineral and a few dark fragments of film. Among these were three young inhabitants. They had caught in a seam where the paving ended, and the water was passing over them into the ground.

The power slowed the flow there. Silt settled around the seam, raising its downstream edge, and the water spread sideways. Two of the young came loose. They drifted apart into shallow channels, where they lodged again. The third remained in the seam. Its cloudy body held to the stone at several points; after a time it began to spread.

The power left the silt in place.

It followed the outward water back into the city, though following was not always a matter of moving upstream. Some channels returned it to places it had already passed. In one court it found the same fan of stems twice, first folded and then open, with no interval it could measure between. It waited against the wall until the fan folded again. An inhabitant lay in the hollow behind it, taking salt from the wet stems.

The power could feel the exchange through the water. It also felt the inhabitant withdraw, gathering itself toward the narrowest part of the hollow. The power lessened its presence there. After a while the inhabitant spread out again.

It stayed in the city for a long time, small enough in its attention that water could pass through it undisturbed. It learned where the warm seeps rose and which courts held salt longest. It learned that a new wall could be an obstruction, a feeding place, and a shelter, sometimes within the same interval. It learned to distinguish an inhabitant resting in a channel from one held there by a current too strong for it to cross. Even then it was not always certain.

At the square, the power lay beneath the paving while several inhabitants met across the sheets of water. Their edges lifted into seams. Mineral gathered; the seams thickened and sank. When the inhabitants came apart, the power felt the small changes each carried away, and the ridge they had left behind.

It could have made the ridge firm at once. It could have drawn the cloudy young from it and placed them in safe joints, with warm water passing over them and film growing nearby.

Instead it watched the ridge dissolve at one end and grow at the other. Some young washed away. Some remained. An older inhabitant settled downstream, and several caught against its edge. It held there until the current shifted, then moved on, with two of the young following in its wake.

Beyond the square, a wall was dissolving along its base. Inhabitants came to feed on the newly exposed film. The opening beneath it widened, and a channel found a shorter route through. Water that had once reached a salt court began to bypass it.

The court did not dry. A seep in its floor kept the paving wet, and the fan continued to fold and open. But each time it opened, less water passed behind it. Salt accumulated in the hollow. An inhabitant resting there left a white stream as it emerged and spread into the channel. The stream remained close to the wall, scarcely diluted.

Over many visits the power felt the water grow more saline. Inhabitants still came, but stayed for shorter periods. One approached the hollow, touched it along the wall, and withdrew without entering.

The power followed the old channel upstream. A new ridge had grown across it, directing the current toward the opening under the dissolving wall. Young inhabitants lay within the ridge, and more water was depositing mineral around them. The power did not break it.

A little farther back, where the channel divided around a low block, there was a joint in the paving packed with old sediment. The power loosened a few grains. Water entered the joint, carried more sediment free, and passed beneath the block. It reached the salt court in a narrow seep.

At first the seep made little difference. The power kept the joint open until the flow had found its own course. Then it withdrew.

The court changed slowly. Salt still collected behind the fan, but when the stems opened, fresh water passed through the hollow and carried some of it away. An inhabitant returned and remained there through a folding. Later, two waited against opposite sides of the fan. One left when the stems opened; the other stayed until they folded again.

The new seep also changed the channel beneath the block. Film grew along its cooler edge. Mineral gathered on the downstream side, making a low lip where there had been none. The young in the ridge continued to develop, though the current passing them was somewhat less strong. One came free and lodged against the new lip.

The power remained long enough to see these changes, and longer still to see what followed them. It had not restored the old route. The court now received water by a way that had not existed before, and the route might close or widen as any other did.

In time a wall rose across the court entrance. Water collected against it, then found the space between its base and the paving. The inhabitants that came there spread along the wall, touching it in several places before passing underneath. The power let the wall stand.

At the basin’s edge, the silt it had gathered was wearing down. Water again approached the seam where it had found the three young. By then the seam was empty. One of the channels beside it had deepened, and most of the outward flow went that way.

The power eased a little more water into it. Then it left both channels to the basin, and turned its attention elsewhere.
