The power came first to the inspection window.

It could have taken in the whole plain at once: every animal, every channel, every turning pump. Instead it stood beside the glass until a swimmer crossed the circle of light. It watched the water after the swimmer had gone, and waited for the next one.

At the interval when the next swimmer should have appeared, the power laid one finger against the pipe. The pressure changed by a small amount. The swimmer arrived, hesitated, and turned back the way it had come.

The power restored the pressure. A little later the swimmer crossed the window again, moving in the original direction.

This was not yet an answer to anything the power knew how to ask. It went on watching.

It watched a worker lift over a rivet, and another follow the same lane. It watched a broad-winged animal open its wings into the space made for it, then fold them on the next beam. It watched the large animal at the basin edge drink, and watched the adjustments pass from body to body across the plain.

The power found that it could stop the pumps. It found that it could break the foundations, release the water, unmake the plates and the animals and the halls beneath them. It left all of these things alone.

It went down through the galleries to the distillation halls, where the water was made. There it added a second set of pumps beside the first, with their own pipes and their own supply. It made them without interrupting the ones already turning. Where the galleries had no room, it made room, and held the foundations steady while it worked.

Above, a little way from the basin, it lifted a section of the plain. The animals standing there remained level while the plates rose beneath them. The power fitted new plates between the old ones, extending the seams and the pores that supplied the clear film. It filled the added channels with warm water.

The animals were no longer quite so close together. The gaps between their flanks widened. For some time they stood as before.

Then one of the larger animals moved a foot sideways. Its neighbor made no adjustment, because none was needed. The first animal moved its foot again, farther this time, and turned its narrow head toward the open space.

It did not enter. After a while it turned back and drank.

The power made other spaces. It widened a pipe at one junction and left the adjoining pipe as it had been. It placed a broad shelf along part of a basin wall, where the workers could pass without scraping. It raised an empty beam near the condenser towers, close enough to reach from the ranks and far enough away that an animal could spread its wings there without touching another.

The power did not know what any of them wanted. It returned to the inspection windows, the basin walls and the towers, and watched what they did with what it had made.

Some swimmers entered the wider pipe and continued through it. Some turned back. A few moved repeatedly between the wide place and the narrow one, until the power lost track of which were which.

The workers crossed the shelf. Most went on to the next lane of riveted metal. One remained at the edge for a time, its ridged mouth still, before returning to the wall.

For a long while the empty beam stayed empty. Then, during one of the great rustlings, a winged animal landed on it. Its feet closed around the metal. It opened one wing partway, then folded it. At the next movement of the ranks it went back among the others.

The animal beside the basin entered the open space on the following day. It crossed three seams, stopped, and lowered its muzzle to a channel. Nothing prevented it from continuing. It remained there until another animal came and stood near it, with a gap between their flanks just wide enough for a hand.

The power left the new pumps running. It made a way to maintain them if one failed, and another way if that failed in turn. It left the old channels supplied and the old beams in place.

By afternoon the clouds stood in bright towers on the horizon. On the added plates, the water had gathered in a seam. One of the small creatures had settled there, its clear lid open to the flow.

The power stayed close enough to see it take in a bead of film. Then the lid closed, and the creature was difficult to distinguish from the wet ceramic.
