The power first attends to the beat of the hands outside.

It does not stop them. A hand is making a mountain, and the mountain is not yet finished. The hand has brought moss and water this far above the clouds. It has also made a place so crowded that its smallest inhabitant must fold itself flat when the work continues.

The power waits through three blows. It feels where each one enters the clay, travels along a reed fiber, and reaches the roots. Then, in the thickness of the wall, it loosens a few grains from one another. It leaves the roots anchored. It leaves the seams joined. It makes a narrow layer that can tremble without passing the whole force inward.

The next blow sounds in the chamber, but the leaves do not strike together. The animal folds two legs and holds still. After a while it puts them down again.

There is still the matter of room.

The power touches the pinhole, not to widen it but to give the light another way to travel. A thin brightness follows the wet seams. It appears beneath one shelf, then another, where the smallest moss shoots have been growing pale. The fernlet no longer has to lift so much to find its way upward.

The chamber remains the same size when measured from outside. Within, the power eases the shelves apart by a root’s breadth, then another. A drop can fall between two leaves now without striking either. There is a patch of bare, damp clay where nothing has yet been planted. The animal crosses it later, touching down cautiously, and finds root hairs on the far side.

When the wooden tool next approaches, it meets resistance at the pinhole.

The hand beyond the wall tries once more, gently. The tool enters just far enough to show its blunt edge. On it is a tuft of moss, its roots trailing in wet clay.

The power leaves the hand a sensation: the slight pressure of leaves against leaves, the trembling of root hairs under a blow, the small bare patch that has only just appeared. It cannot tell the hand what to build. It can make these things perceptible.

The tool remains still. A little water runs down its handle and gathers at the edge. The animal, sheltered beneath a root, feels the root hair draw taut and waits.

At last the tool withdraws with the moss still upon it.

Outside, the hands continue their work. They press another course into the mountain. The sound comes through the wall, softened now; the animal lowers its body but does not fold all the way.

The power stays until the new clay has taken hold and the water is climbing as it should. Before leaving, it traces one more damp line through the wall, connecting a crowded shelf to a seam with space in it. A root finds the line. Its tip enters, pauses at a reed fiber, and divides around it.

Above the animal, the droplet swells. It reaches up and drinks.
