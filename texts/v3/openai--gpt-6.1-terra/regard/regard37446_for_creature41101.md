The power came first to the shelves beyond the wall, where the air was still. It stood there long enough to see the insect cross the stone, disappear, and emerge again facing the other way. Then it watched for longer.

The animal that emerged was not the same one. The chip in its back was different. Yet after a time the first came out again, and crossed the same stone at the same deliberate, uneven pace.

The power followed it into the crack without making the opening wider. It felt the warmth there, the pressure of bodies, the faint nourishment on the damp surfaces. It felt an animal pause at a junction until the passage ahead was clear, and another find a place to graze. It found injuries, too, and bodies too old or damaged to hold their places.

It could not tell whether the animals wished to leave. The dry shelves were no place to ask them to prove it.

The power made a hollow in the outer face of the retaining wall. It did this slowly, while the animals in the nearest joints moved away from the settling stone. The hollow had a narrow mouth and a roof that held the cool air. A little water seeped through its back, and where the water passed it left minerals on the rock.

For a long while, nothing entered but air.

Then a young animal came through a joint and felt along the mouth of the hollow. It withdrew when a drop fell. Later it returned, followed by others. They grazed where the stone had begun to dull.

The power made no more hollows that day. It watched the water and kept it from running too quickly. It watched the nearest duct, too, to see whether the animals leaving it had made any difference to those who remained. Their places filled. The rubbing went on at much the same pitch.

In the factory, a belt shuddered as it always had. The tremor passed through the bodies around the pulley, and their rasp changed with it. But now the power noticed something else: before the belt returned to its old course, one small animal had crossed a clean arc on the metal and begun to feed where, on every previous turn, another had fed.

The difference was slight. It remained.

A little later, a second drop fell into one of the street hollows before the first ring had vanished. The rings met and disturbed each other.

The power stayed near the basin. It did not put out the lamps or still the engines. Where a valve had begun to fail, it gave the metal enough strength to hold; where the new seep threatened to dry, it let a little more water through. It waited to see what the animals would make of the space.

Some stayed in the ducts. Some found the hollow and returned to the warmth. A few remained there, beneath the sheltered roof, their backs catching the gray daylight. Over time, their grazing left a pattern on the stone unlike the one beneath the pipes.

Beyond the wall the shelves were still mostly bare. At the new opening, there was the occasional sound of water, and a faint rubbing from within.
