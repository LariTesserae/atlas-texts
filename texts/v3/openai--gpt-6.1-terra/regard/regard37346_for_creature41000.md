The power did not begin by changing the basin. It listened through a full turning of the light, and then through many more.

It followed the faint current beyond the limestone rim and out through the low channel. It traced the vibration through the rock to its source: a machine moving water elsewhere, doing work that mattered elsewhere. Stopping it would quiet the basin, but it would also change the water’s course. The power left it running.

It returned to the shelves. Beneath one ledge, a fine grain had lodged against the film. Over the following days the living surface lifted around it, almost imperceptibly. Nearby, a loose edge trembled in the current, held by only a few slender attachments. At last it came free and drifted away.

The power followed the fragment as far as the outlet. There it caught briefly on a stone, then broke apart. Most of it passed into the channel. A little remained.

There was no way to ask the film what it wanted. The power knew how readily a shelter could become a trap, and how an abundance supplied too quickly could exhaust the water. It did not fill the basin with new creatures or cover the bare stone with growth.

Instead, it worked a small change upstream. Where the current divided around a buried shelf, it eased a little more water toward the basin. The change took place slowly, over many days. The strands of green leaned a fraction farther; fresh particles reached some hollows that had received very few. In one of them, a thin patch of film began to thicken.

The power watched for what else might follow. When a trace of silt began to gather too quickly beneath one shelf, it adjusted the flow again. The water cleared there, and the patch beneath survived. Other places remained still and sparse. The exposed stone stayed exposed.

At the outlet, the little remnant on the stone persisted for a time. Then a grain of silt struck it loose, and it too passed out of sight. The power did not retrieve it. Farther along the channel were other grooves, other places where the water slowed.

The machine continued its distant work. Daylight crossed the shelves. The pale film grew where conditions allowed, and pieces of it came away.

The power kept watch on the waters feeding the basin. When, much later, something harmful entered the current upstream, it found and turned it aside before it reached the limestone rim. Then it let the cool water pass through as before.
