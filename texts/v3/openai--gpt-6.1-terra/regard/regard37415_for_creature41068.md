The power first feels the mountain move.

A flake above the foundry has come loose along three edges. It is held by a fourth, a thin neck of stone already whitening under strain. In a short while it will fall across the warm hollows and the shelf below them. The impact will break the foundry’s main shaft and bury most of the young.

The power could take the flake away. It could close the crack and make a chamber that would never shift again. It could also see that the city has been built from the mountain’s failures: metal exposed by shearing stone, shelter made from fallen plates, generations of animals whose feet and feelers know how to live where nothing is quite still.

It holds the flake where it is.

The movement is slight. Some grit spills from the whitening seam, but the stone remains suspended a few lengths above the scaffolds. Bridge workers come up the cables and touch it with their feelers. They retreat, then return carrying wire.

For a time the power holds its attention there. The workers pass loops through an old bolt hole and anchor them to the wall. A second group clears the lane beneath. Adults enter the warm hollows and emerge with the smallest young clinging under their plates. The traffic below the foundry grows dense and slow.

The workers tighten their wires. The stone settles against them, and several strands snap. The animals scatter, then climb back. They add more wire, and a brace taken from a disused section of belt. When they have finished, the power lets the flake rest a little more heavily on their work.

It holds.

The power stays through another settling of the mountain. One anchor draws partway out of the wall, and the workers replace it with a longer one. They leave the shelf beneath the stone empty. A covered lane is built around it, with room for two carts to pass.

The young are brought back to the hollows. Several have lost their grip along the way; attendants find them on the lower shelves and carry them up. One remains beneath a belt where none of the attendants can reach. The power turns a loose shaving of metal so that it catches against the moving guide. The belt stops. Its riders bunch together, and a worker coming to clear the jam finds the pale animal and pulls it free.

Beyond the entrance, wind drives grit over the black stone. Some falls through the opening. In it are grains of metal, as there have always been, and among them the power places a few more. They lodge where the entrance workers can find them without leaving the covered passage. The animals carry them inward, one grain at a time.

The power leaves the city its predators and its dead, its frail bridges and its long, crowded belts. It does not know what these creatures call safety, or whether they call it anything. For now they have kept their home through one movement of the mountain, and their own repairs are holding.

As the power withdraws, a bridge shivers. The workers hanging beneath it pass down the cables to fetch wire. Traffic pauses at either end. When the bridge is ready, the first animal steps onto it and crosses, touching the rail as it goes.
