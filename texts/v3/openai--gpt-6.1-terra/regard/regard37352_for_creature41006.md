The power first follows the water.

It goes out beneath the floor and on through the flooded works, past other pumps and gates, into places where the current keeps silt from settling in drinking-water intakes. It travels the ducts in the other direction, too, and finds where the silver fish came from, and the places they can no longer reach.

It listens long enough to know that stopping the pumps would not be a kindness.

The third pump has a bearing close to failure. When it starts, the shaft moves farther than it should. Soon it will strike the housing, break, and send pieces of metal into the intake. The two remaining pumps will carry the increased load for a time, then one of them will heat and stop. Farther along the system, a gate will open to compensate. The altered flow will empty a shallow refuge where young fish shelter.

The power could hold the bearing in place indefinitely. Instead, it makes the fault easier to find.

On a maintenance screen in a dry building some distance away, a vibration reading rises above its usual band. It stays there. A technician checks the instrument, finds it sound, and schedules an inspection.

Before the crew comes, the power watches the eel in the abandoned pipe nearest the pump cage. The eel has lived there for many years. When the pipe was first fitted, it carried water; now one end is blind, and the other opens just wide enough to admit the eel. It will be removed to make room for the repair.

The power does not lead the eel out with a vision or a voice. Over several nights, it sends a little more food toward another pipe, farther from the cage. A crustacean shelters there; later, a small fish. The eel finds them both. It begins to rest in the second pipe after hunting.

By the time the divers arrive, it has not returned to the first for days.

The divers work slowly in the cold water. They fasten their tools against the current, lift the old pipe away, and replace the worn bearing. One of them notices mussels growing on a section of grille marked for removal. She has worked in flooded machinery before, though not in this chamber. She asks whether that section can stay.

It can. They cut around it.

Some mussels are lost when the adjoining grille comes free. The water clouds; the silver fish withdraw between the cage bars. The divers finish their work, close the hatch and go back up. The third pump starts without its old clatter.

For a while, the fish turn at the change in pressure as they always have, then hesitate in the quieter water. Eventually they gather downstream. The pump still lifts debris from the floor, though less violently, and food still arrives.

The power remains a little longer. It sees the eel emerge from its new pipe and cross the lamplight. It sees the technician sign off the repair, and the diver go home with the smell of wet metal still on her hands.

Then it leaves the chamber to its currents, which continue to carry things in and away.
