The power came quietly, and at first did nothing.

It followed the tide through the kelp, past the schools of fish and the seals that hunted them. It went down the drowned slopes and waited in the deeper water, where a loose blade turned slowly above a terrace of stone.

An octopus lived there, in a crevice beneath an overhang. Outside its den lay the shells of crabs and mussels, some bleached white, some still dark and shining. One arm emerged and drew a shell closer. The octopus turned it over, then let it fall.

The power stayed through several tides. It watched the octopus leave its den to hunt, its skin shifting against the rock, and return with a crab held beneath its arms. It watched a seal nose into the crevice after it, then move on. Once, when a surge sent sand spilling over the ledge, the octopus pulled a stone into the entrance and waited until the water cleared.

The power could have made the den safe from everything. But the octopus needed to leave it, and the crabs needed their own chances, and even the seal had young to feed.

So the power looked farther.

At the turbine, a length of net had caught on the housing. It streamed out into the current, nearly invisible except where scraps of kelp clung to it. A fish was tangled there, still moving. The power loosened the net from the iron and brought it, with the fish inside, to a stony beach on the distant shore.

A woman walking there saw it. She freed the fish in a pool, though it was badly hurt, and carried the net above the tide line. Later she reported where she had found it. People came to examine the turbine. They brought lights and cameras and learned how often animals passed through the narrow channel. Afterward, they changed when the machine ran and fitted guards around its moving parts. They marked the site more clearly on their charts.

These things took time. The power did not resent the time they took. It helped where help was wanted: a clear image on a monitor, a conversation remembered, a question asked again when the first answer was not enough.

In the deeper water, the octopus continued to hunt. One day it found a mussel larger than any it had brought home before. It worked at the shell for a long while, then carried it beneath the overhang.

The power watched an arm reach out for the stone at the entrance. As the stone scraped into place, a small crab slipped from beneath it and scuttled away across the terrace.

The power let it go.
