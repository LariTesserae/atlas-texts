The power first came to the basin during a shower. It had noticed the rings and, later, the shadows that crowded into them. It might have made the rain fall evenly, filled every dry groove, and kept each pool clear. Instead it stayed where the rain was already falling and watched what happened when the weather changed.

On the third day a fin broke near the roofless shelter. Its upper half slid against another fin, then dropped across a narrow channel. Water backed up behind it, spread over a shelf, and began to drain through a different seam.

Two shadows were traveling along the channel when the stone fell. One reached the spreading water. The other remained on the far side, where the channel had already become a row of separate drops.

The power held the drops in place. They hung just above the stone, close enough for the shadow to cross, and for a moment nothing else in the basin moved. The power had not intended to stop the wind or the rain, but it had done so.

The shadow did not cross. It drew back toward the broken fin. Farther along the channel, the other shadow came to meet it, and the two gathered on opposite sides of the suspended drops.

The power released them. Rain struck the rock again. The shadow on the dry side moved slowly beneath the fin, following a film of water that had been hidden from view. It emerged farther downhill, where the channel still ran, and joined the other.

For a while both remained there. Then one traveled back to the fallen stone. The other went toward the shelter and returned with a movement so slight that at first the power saw only the hammerstone shifting in its niche. After several showers, the inhabitants had turned the broken channel into a shallow reservoir. They left the fin where it had fallen.

The power went on watching. There were long intervals in which it did nothing. It learned that a shadow held in a crevice after rain could be resting, or waiting, or attending to something at the other end of a connection. Sometimes another inhabitant stayed nearby; sometimes none did. The power could not always tell whether the shadow would climb out again. Once, when a pool dried, a reflection remained in its bottom through the evening and was gone by morning. Others returned to that dish after the next rain and gathered around it for a time.

In the shelter, the shallow hollows did not all serve the same purpose. Chips were moved from one to another as the light changed. A hollow that held water longest was sometimes left empty, while several inhabitants occupied a shallower one whose water would soon be gone. The power could see their movements more clearly than before, but not all their reasons.

When it wished to make itself known, it chose an open shelf where water passed after rain. It made a small mark in the stone: three short grooves leading into a dish. The next shower filled them. One inhabitant came to the dish, spread along the grooves, and withdrew.

On the following day the power deepened one groove. The inhabitant returned and placed a chip across it, diverting the water into the dish by another route. The power left the chip where it was.

They continued this way through several showers. The power made small changes; the inhabitants left some and blocked others. Once they carried chips to a second dish and arranged them in three lines. The power copied the arrangement in the stone beside it. An inhabitant entered the dish and held the reflected ridge steady there until the rain stopped.

After that, when a groove silted up or a dish cracked, the power could offer help by marking the nearby stone. Sometimes the mark remained untouched. Sometimes chips appeared beside it. Then the power widened a channel or fitted a loose edge back into place, working slowly enough for the inhabitants to move away.

Near the end of a dry season, water stopped reaching the shelter floor. A few inhabitants remained beneath its wall, where dampness lingered in a seam. Others crossed the basin by the deeper pools. The power found water below a shelf not far from the shelter. It could have opened the rock at once, but first it made the three grooves in the dusty channel leading toward the wall.

A shadow appeared at the mark the next evening. It stayed there until dark. After the next brief rain, two chips lay in the channel, with a gap between them.

The power opened the seam below the shelf. Water emerged in a thin line and followed the old channel, passing between the chips. It reached the shelter wall and darkened the floor. By morning, one hollow held a little water. A shadow spread into it from the seam, then another.

The flow lasted through the dry interval. When rain returned, the power narrowed the opening until it ran no faster than the other seeps in the basin. The inhabitants later altered its course, adding a lip of stone that sent some of the water beneath the shelter and some around it.

The power remained attentive after that. It did not keep every fin standing, or every connection wet. It could move stone more easily than the inhabitants could, and sometimes they made use of that. Sometimes, when it approached a mark and found the chips gathered tightly over it, it waited.

In late afternoon the basin was bright with separate glints. Water ran through the lip beside the shelter. Beyond it, several shadows crossed a pool and gathered beneath a cliff that was reflected close by. One stayed in the pool while the others climbed into a seam. The power watched until the light changed and it could no longer distinguish them from the dark water.
