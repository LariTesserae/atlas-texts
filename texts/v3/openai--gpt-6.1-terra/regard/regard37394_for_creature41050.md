The power listened before it touched anything.

It heard the great calls from the flanks, and the smaller knocks of shell against shell, and the clicks by which the swimmers kept their places. It heard, too, the burrower working in the felt: the push of its blunt head, the tightening of its muscles, the faint pull as it drew a strand across the passage behind it.

The burrower had entered an old chamber where the walls had grown close together. A sheet of leaf had folded over its back, pinning two of the blind creatures against its skin. When it pushed forward, the sheet tightened. When it pulled back, the roots at the entrance closed around it.

It rested for a time. The blind creatures crept toward the moving water at its head. Then it tried again.

The power could have opened the chamber at once. But above it a grazer was feeding, and the roots that held the chamber also held a long section of the terrace. So the power eased one strand free, then another, allowing water to pass through the wall. The loosened leaf lifted. The burrower drew itself backward, turned in the chamber and began to make a new passage.

One of the blind creatures had been left beneath the leaf. It moved its hooked feet against the smooth surface, finding no purchase. The power bent the edge of the leaf until it touched the burrower’s skin. The creature crossed over and held on.

The burrower went on fastening strands behind it. Before long the chamber was another seam in the felt, with a little water moving through it and roots beginning to grow across it again.

The power remained. It learned the routes the great animals took to the terraces and the places where their weight had pressed the floor thin. In those places it guided loose fibers into the breaks, where grazing limbs and the slow growth of roots could bind them in. When a frond tore, it made room in the water for its young edge to spread.

It did not quiet the plateau. The grazers continued their rough work; the swimmers answered one another under the canopies. Some animals were caught and eaten, and others died in passages that closed too soon. The power attended to them also.

For a long while its presence could be known only by small things: a strand settling where it was needed, a current turning gently through a crowded hollow, a lost creature finding a surface on which it could hold.
