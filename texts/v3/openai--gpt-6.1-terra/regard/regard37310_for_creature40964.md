The power comes to the hollow without entering it. There is no room inside that is not already occupied by water or flesh or the work of flesh. It attends instead to the pressure on the stone, the slow arrival of food, the places where water has ceased to pass.

Beneath a new terrace, one builder has been closed in. Its tentacles open into a space that holds almost nothing. They withdraw, and open again. The animal does not know the shape of the wall above it. It knows, if this can be called knowing, that the water no longer brings enough.

The power could lift the terrace away. The terrace holds others, and its underside shelters a swimmer carrying eggs. So the power does something smaller.

A grain caught in a seam between two sleeves shifts. The seam parts a little, then farther, along a line where the mineral is still soft. Water enters. It moves over the enclosed animal’s tentacles, carrying a fragment fine enough to be taken. The animal draws it in.

The opening remains narrow. It will close again if the builders continue as they have been. The power follows the passage upward and finds another place where a little yielding will let the water through. Then another. It makes no straight channel, and leaves the roofs standing.

For a while the currents change. A swimmer is carried from one chamber into another; a grazer turns away from a ledge where its hold has loosened. Beneath the arch the hunter unfolds its front limbs and catches a passing animal. It eats. The power does not take the prey from it.

At the seam overhead, the stone is pressed very gently apart. Cold water enters in a broader sheet, bringing particles and the eggs of animals from elsewhere on the seabed. Some settle on the filaments, some are drawn into the city, and some pass through and out again. The light reaches a little farther down one wall. There, after a time, a dark filament begins to grow.

The builders keep making their sleeves. New animals fasten where the current brings them. Some find good places; some do not. A grazer crosses a living roof, and the animal beneath withdraws until it has passed. In the older quarter, one builder dies inside its sleeve. Its neighbors continue to feed, and the mineral around them continues to thicken.

The power remains attentive as the passages change. Where a crowded roof begins to shut off the water beneath it, the soft stone gives a little. Where the city grows against the side of the hollow, the side recedes by an amount too small to disturb the animals anchored there. The hollow becomes larger, though it is still a small place on the seabed, and the water through it still carries only what the sea can spare.

The builder under the terrace feeds for a time. Eventually its tentacles grow slow, and one day they do not extend. The opening above it stays clear. Water passes through, bearing fine grains into a chamber beyond, where a young animal has settled.

It holds to the surface. Around its clear body, the first delicate edge of a sleeve begins to form.
