The power came to a feeler on the northern side of a ridge. It had eight limbs, one shorter than the others, and had made its place in a crack beneath a leaning sheet of mineral. Inside the crack was a clutch of eggs.

The feeler drew food from the warmer water and carried it to its mouth. At intervals it turned and reached farther into the opening, brushing the eggs with its foremost limbs. Some had loosened from the stone. These it pressed back into place.

The power stayed with it long enough to learn the shape of its attention: the pressure of water against the hairs, the scrape of a hook finding purchase, the particular warmth that rose from the crack. The great roar was part of all of it. The feeler did not hear silence behind the noise, or wait for the noise to end.

The leaning sheet was slowly shifting. A thin edge of it rested against the feeler’s back. As the current pressed on the broad face above, the edge wore a groove through the animal’s covering. When the sheet moved again, it would close the mouth of the crack.

The power lifted it, very slightly.

The feeler withdrew at once, pulling its sensory limbs inside. The current passed over its back without resistance from the mineral. For a while it remained motionless. Then one limb emerged and touched the stone where the edge had been.

The sheet settled a little farther out, against a firm shoulder of rock. Water could still pass beneath it. The crack remained open.

The power might have closed the wound on the feeler’s back. Instead, it cleared a flake of mineral from the groove and brought a little more warm water through the fracture. In time, if the animal ate enough, its next covering would form whole.

It looked in on the nearby machines. One tower had stopped, and its scavengers were leaving the cooling casing. The power followed the cables down through the rock and learned what the tower did, and where the energy it gathered went. Finding no harm in its work, the power loosened a bearing that had seized. The plates began to tremble again.

The casing warmed slowly. Several scavengers returned before it was fully warm, pressing into the narrow space beneath it. Others had already gone too far to feel the change. They drifted over the ridge, and a silver school took some of them.

The power remained while the feeler’s eggs opened. The young came free one by one, small and almost clear, and the current carried them through the mouth of the crack. The feeler reached after the first few, then resumed feeding.

One young animal caught briefly against the shifted sheet of mineral. It worked loose and rose into the pale water. The power followed it until it was among the other drifting things, too small to pick out by sight.

The roar continued through the faults. Light moved across the ridge. Under the mended tower, the scavengers fed and laid eggs, and their young gathered in the warmth.
