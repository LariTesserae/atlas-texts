The power first attends to the dweller by the basin.

Its cartridge is not yet empty, but the remaining chemicals have settled into layers the dweller’s threads cannot readily use. The power turns the cartridge gently, without moving the stalk, and separates what can still nourish it from what cannot. Then it opens a way into the sealed base and adds more.

The dweller notices the change. For a long time it spends nothing extra. It tests the supply with a fine thread, withdraws the thread, and tests it again.

The young one sends a signal during this interval. The dweller by the basin answers before the lamps have finished their day’s slow variation. The young one sends the same signal again, more distinctly. The answer comes again.

There are two other cartridges in use, and the power tends them too. The old dweller has more than it needs for now. Its crowded threads are another matter: some crossings have grown so close that warmth passes between them in ways that blur the signals they carry. The power could separate them. It could clear the unused paths and leave room for new ones.

Instead, it makes a small addition to the inside of the stalk: a smooth, nourishing surface alongside the old network, connected by a narrow bridge. The bridge is broad enough for a thread to cross and easy to avoid. Nothing is taken away.

The old dweller does not approach it for a long time. When at last a thread touches the bridge, the power leaves it there to grow at its own pace.

Along the ceramic, the power lays three fine tracks, each beginning at an inhabited socket and ending at an empty one. They are sheltered in shallow grooves, supplied with the materials a growing thread might require, and interrupted at the sockets by small gaps. A dweller can close its gap from within, or leave it open. The empty stalks keep their cartridges.

The tracks are visible in the workshop light: three dark lines crossing the pale terraces and the salt basin. The dweller beside the basin senses them as slight changes in reflected warmth. It describes them to the others over many days. The young one sends several uncertain signals, then begins to work at the edge of its socket.

The power waits.

Before leaving, it looks beyond the chamber. The lamps will eventually fail; a relay can be made to bring replacements without darkness falling all at once. The cartridges will need tending again; a reserve can be placed where it will feed them slowly, with a way to stop the flow. The factory itself will not run forever.

For that more distant day, the power prepares a vessel in the thickness of the wall: insulated, lit, and fitted to hold the stalks with their roots undisturbed. It does not move them into it. There will be time to make the vessel known, and time for the dwellers to answer.

In the chamber, the young one closes the gap at its socket. A thread enters the first groove. It advances only a little before stopping, but the dweller beside the basin can feel the faint new warmth along the ceramic and sends word of it to the old one.

Many days later, the old dweller replies. Part of its answer is something it has said before. After that comes a small, unfamiliar pattern, formed on the new surface inside its stalk.

The dweller by the basin receives it. It takes time to distinguish the pattern from the light of the lamps. Then it sends it back.
