The power came at an hour when the plateau cast almost no shadows. It might have arrived in any form. Here it was a coolness moving along the underside of the ledge, too slight to interrupt the winged things in their circles.

It watched the groove first.

The break looked easy to mend. A grain of stone lifted from the floor and fitted into the gap would have carried the water to the fern. The animals waiting below would have had less to drink, but the power could have given them water elsewhere. It could have filled the old pool.

It waited.

The next morning the silver line held. Water passed across the gap and reached the roots. On the morning after that it broke, and the waiting animals caught the drops. Their bodies crowded together so tightly that one was pushed out into the sun. It crawled back beneath the ledge, leaving a dark, damp track.

The patterns moved on the slabs. The power watched them through heat and darkness, through the fall of a short, hard rain, through the long drying afterward. It began to see where one movement ended and another began. The three stones were not three beings, nor was the pattern merely something that happened to them. Together they attended to the hollow: to the weight of water in the groove, to the warmth held in the floor, to the scrape of the animal working below.

The slabs had been there a long time. They had not always been cool. Long ago, when the hollow held a deeper pool, their lower edges had stood in water. The fern was younger than they were. So were the animals beneath the gap, though the groove was older than those animals’ kind.

The power made a shadow pass over the stones. In it, the shifting lines slowed.

*May I help?* it asked.

The slabs did not answer at once. They had felt the power watching; they had also felt the grain of stone lifted and put down again.

At last, condensation gathered on the inner faces, though the air was dry. It ran into the groove, crossed the gap in a trembling line, and reached the fern.

The power understood only part of this. It waited again.

That night the scraping below the floor came nearer. A small heap of grit appeared beside a crack, followed by a polished pebble. The slabs cooled around it. By morning, a fine fracture had become visible across its surface.

Far below the hollow, a layer of stone was giving way. The chambers would hold for a time, perhaps for many seasons. Then one wall would fall into another, and the cracks by which water and air entered would close.

The being in the slabs could sense the strain but could not reach it. The animal below had been carrying grit out for many nights. It could not reach the failing wall either.

The power went down through the rock. It found eggs in the dry pods, pale bodies pressed together in their chambers, and the small worker resting beside a heap of stone dust. Deeper still it found the fracture. It held the two sides apart while loose grains settled, then joined them as stone joins under immense pressure, along a seam no wider than a thread.

Nothing moved in the hollow above. No creature was driven from its shelter. The repair would last longer than the chambers as they stood, though not forever; the power left the stone around it free to shift.

When it returned, dawn was near. The squat animals had taken their places beneath the groove. One had pushed another aside and now stood with its mouth open to the sky.

The power asked the slabs whether they wanted the break mended.

For a little while the patterns ran quickly. Then a shadow crossed all three stones at once, though nothing passed above them. The patterns became still, and the water fell through the gap.

The waiting animals drank.

The power stayed until the basin beneath the overhang received its first drop. Pale bodies came through the crack, and the hunters descended after them. One drinker was caught; another reached the shelter with water darkening its body. The noise rose and filled the hollow.

The power left the slabs as they were: cool in the sun, with their shifting patterns and their broken groove. Before departing, it went once more beneath the floor and showed the small worker where the stone had been joined. The worker touched the seam with its mouthparts, scraped at it, and carried away a loosened grain.

By evening the heap beside the crack was gone. A different pebble lay there, newly polished, catching the last light.
