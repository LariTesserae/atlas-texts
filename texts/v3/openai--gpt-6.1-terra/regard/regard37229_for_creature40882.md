The power came down through the cloud without parting it. For a time nothing changed. Water rose through the seams; the ice clicked between breathing backs.

Then the pulse of one bank slowed.

An animal lay beneath the others where two pools had come together. The cartilage around its mouth was caught in a ridge of old ice, and each time its neighbors settled, the ridge pressed farther into the soft folds behind the ring. It had been pumping against the obstruction for a long time. Clear water came from it in weak spurts. The young that had sheltered on its back were gone.

The power touched the ice. The ridge softened into water, and the animal drew its mouth free. It lay still afterward, held between the warm bodies on either side. Presently its rings opened a little. Water passed through them, and a few small, transparent jaws gathered above its back.

The power remained near it until the bank began to pulse again.

The hunters felt that pulse beneath the ice. One turned toward it, braced its limbs against the stone and found the underside of a basker. The basker thrashed once, breaking the bridges to its neighbors. The hunter held on. When the basker’s pumping stopped, the power stayed with it through the last movement of its body.

The hunter fed, and the gray creatures came to the torn edges. There were young hunters beneath the ice as well, folded into a hollow in the rock. The hunter carried pieces back to them.

Across the basin, the cloud descended. The power went with it, moving among the suspended droplets. In each drop were the smallest baskers, turning their jaws in water that had grown too thin to feed them. Many would have fallen when the cloud rose; others would have remained caught in the still sheets, pumping until their bodies emptied.

The power gathered water around the ones left behind. The sheets grew heavy and sank slowly. Where they touched the ice, tiny bodies slipped through the seams and found the warm plumes below.

Afterward, the power followed the pulse along a passage that had no visible water in it. The passage had become narrow over a great span of years. Animals entered it as they had always done, but some were delayed there, and a few never reached the pool on the other side. Their sounds continued to arrive among the living long after their bodies had gone cold.

The power widened the passage. A line of baskers moved through, each appearing in the far pool as its pumping sounded there. Behind them came a hunter with one limb missing, and behind the hunter a drift of dark grains.

When the cloud lifted, the pools had changed places. The bank around the freed animal had divided into three, and the animal itself lay in the shallows beside an unfamiliar ridge. It had a deep mark behind its mouth ring. As it breathed, water moved over the mark, and the fine branching threads within its pumping chambers slowly covered the damaged flesh.

The power watched for a long while. It found other places where ice held too tightly, where a pool had closed around a living body, where the young hung in water too sparse to sustain them. It worked at these places without haste. In time the banks rose and settled more evenly, and fewer sounds went on alone after their makers had fallen silent.

The power did not leave the flats. When the cloud lowered again, it moved within it, attending to the droplets. Beneath, the hunters scraped along the ice, and the baskers drew mineral-rich water through their widening mouths. A pulse began at the edge of one pool and traveled outward, passing through water, flesh and stone. The power felt it reach the farther banks and took care that the way remained open.
