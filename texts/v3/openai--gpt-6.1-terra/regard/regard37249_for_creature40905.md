The power came without a body large enough to cast a shadow. It entered the basin through every crack at once and felt the rattling in the stone.

It listened long enough to distinguish one caller from the millions around it: a small one whose plate had split. Each time it flexed, the broken edge caught against the soft tissue beneath. When a hunter passed, the caller fell silent. Afterward it began again.

The power mended the plate. It also mended the split plates of the others, and the torn undersides where they held to the stone.

There were injuries everywhere. A grazer had been caught beneath a shifting mass and could neither reach food nor pull free. A blind swimmer lay in a shrinking pool, still some distance from the crack that led to water. The power opened a space around the grazer and made a narrow wet passage beneath the swimmer. It stayed with the swimmer until it had passed safely into its chamber.

In the channels, hunters lifted their feet and set them down. Diggers rested their faces against fissures, listening for food. The power understood what each of them needed, though it did not mistake that understanding for a right to leave them as they were.

It began in the stone.

Along the deeper channels, mineral water rose through new seams. Films spread over the damp surfaces, thick enough to feed the grazers and the young sheltered beneath them. The diggers found the seams too. Their first excavations opened broad, wet chambers where food could be gathered without crushing animals packed tightly into a fissure.

The power worked more slowly in the living bodies. It gave the hunters a way to take nourishment from films that grew in folds of mineral-rich stone, and an appetite for it. Their young learned the new food readily. It gave the diggers another way to find what they needed, and helped the swimmers feed in the water that remained below ground.

For a long time there were still hunts. The power did not leave an animal to be eaten alive while the change took hold. Where a creature was caught, it quieted the suffering and kept the wound from being passed on. It attended to the hungry hunters as well, leading them toward the new seams, and to the eggs and young that depended on them.

The callers continued to rattle. Their calls warned of moving bodies and helped them find one another on the walls. When a hunter crossed among them, a silence opened and closed again, though the hunter was now as likely to be seeking a wet seam as prey.

At the next flood, water spilled over the ribs and filled the channels. The grazer sheets lifted and gripped again. Flat swimmers came out from beneath the stones; blind swimmers entered the pools. Some grazers were swept loose, but places of shelter opened in their path, and those that caught hold there found food when the water fell.

Afterward, the power felt the basin through the limestone. The roar was still immense. Within it were separate pulses, pauses, the scrape of mouthparts, and the slow movement of a digger returning to a familiar cut.

The power remained. When rain failed, it kept water in the chambers. When bodies wore out, it stayed near as they died. It could feel the smallest lives against the stone, and it did not let their number make any one of them less distinct.
