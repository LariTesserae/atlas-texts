The power came first to the edge of the plateau, where the cloud lay against the ground. It could have passed through the whole place at once, following every line and fissure, but there was no need to hurry. It listened to the machinery through the soles of its feet.

A pump had stopped in one of the nearer districts. The missing note was difficult to pick out from the others. Upstream, water had risen over a service deck and frozen around the railings. Below the station, the basin was low. Its black fronds stood in a collar of ice, their lower stems still in water.

The power went inside the hall. A bearing had broken apart in its housing, and bright pieces of metal lay among the oil. The shaft was sound. The pump could be made to run again.

Before touching it, the power walked along the dry channel below the station. In the shadow of a pylon it found a thin new course of water, no wider than a finger, running toward a crack in the stone. A line of moss had begun to grow beside it. The water disappeared into the crack and emerged a long way off, where there had once been bare rock. There, too, the first shoots had taken hold.

The power returned to the hall and sat beside the pump. It remained there while the cloud at the horizon shed and renewed its fringes, while frost gathered on the railings, and while the water above the station found another small way down.

At last it cleared the broken metal from the housing and made a bearing to fit. It did not start the pump. Instead it opened a narrow bypass around it, so that some of the water could reach the old channel. The rest continued over the flooded deck and toward the new crack.

The level in the basin rose slowly. When the water reached the ice around the fronds, a dark line appeared beneath its surface and widened along the shore. Farther down, the new moss stayed wet.

The power listened again. The chord was still missing a note, but the rush of the bypass could be heard close to the station, and through the stone came the faint vibration of water passing into the new fissure. For a time these sounds continued without change.

Then, from somewhere beyond the next rise, a pump altered its pace. Its note climbed, held, and settled lower than before. Another district answered, not immediately, but after enough time for water to travel between them. The power could not tell how much of the change belonged to the opening of the bypass and how much to other movements under the rock.

It went to that district and found no damage. The water there had risen a little around the foundations, where the moss was thick. The engines were warm, the lines charged. It left them as they were.

There were other broken things on the plateau. Some had been broken for so long that roots had grown through them, or water had made a course around them that now served several beds. Others were recently damaged: a split pipe losing water onto bare stone, a cable worn nearly through where it crossed a support. The power attended to these. It sealed the pipe and eased the cable away from its support. At each place it waited afterward, listening to what changed.

It found, too, a broad basin where the outlet had closed under a fall of stone. The water was rising toward an electrical yard. The power moved some of the stone aside, enough to let the water pass, and left the rest where moss was already beginning to gather.

By the time it returned to the first station, the black fronds stood in open water again. The flooded deck remained under ice. Along the distant crack the new bed had grown wider, though it could still be crossed in a few steps.

The power kept the bearing it had made on a shelf inside the hall. It was clean and bright, and fitted the pump exactly. Outside, water went on through both channels. The power stayed long enough to see the basin through another freezing, and longer still, until the new bed had reached the next pool.

Sometimes it sat among the broad leaves, with its hand resting lightly on the moss. The machinery sounded through the ground beneath it. Once, when a low note faded far away, it rose and went to find what had happened.
