The power came without a shadow.

It found a fly in a crack, waiting for warmth. Beneath the fly lay a cushion of leaves, and beneath those were the remains of older leaves, and among them a mite moving slowly through the dark.

The power could have warmed the whole basin at once. It could have opened every flower and melted every shelf of snow. But the snow was a roof as well as a weight, and the water beneath it was not yet needed everywhere.

So it waited with the fly.

When the sun reached the crack, the fly climbed out and stood on the stone. Its wings opened a little. Presently it rose and went to a flower.

Near the basin’s lip, a loose plate of rock rested on a narrow point. It would fall one day, perhaps in the coming thaw. Its fall would bury one of the oldest cushions and send grit over several others. The power knew this, and knew also that the fallen stone would make new shelter in time.

Still, it put a little grit beneath the plate, enough to hold it through another winter. It did not make the mountain permanent.

Then it attended to the places no eye could easily follow: the thin films of water, the spores lodged in their seams, the roots finding their way around grains of stone. It gave no command. It asked nothing of them.

A bird came down over the ridge and took the fly from the air.

The power did not turn the bird aside. The fly had warmed itself, visited a flower, and flown. The bird carried it away in its own small body, over the lip and out of sight.

As the light shifted, the power went with it across the basin, leaving the snow to melt at its edge and the open stone open to the sky.
