The power first attends to the enclosure without entering it.

The film advances along the seam, darkening the clay. Beneath the central plate, the small chamber widens and takes a pellet from the bed below. At the gate, three more joints appear beside the first, not all at once, and the low wall acquires the beginning of another course.

In a dark interval, a copy of the pellet appears in a chamber far beyond the enclosure. The new chamber holds it against a wall while the first keeps its pellet near the center. When the distant wall shifts, a faint groove appears on both pellets. The power follows the groove back and forth between them for some time.

There are other things to attend to. A film has been left dry on a high course of brick, too far from any seam for the usual rains to revive it. A chamber is narrowing under the weight of a roof that has not yet risen; it keeps returning a fallen tile to the same place, although the tile now scarcely fits. At the end of a wall, an impression has held its shape through several light intervals, waiting where the clay is too firm to take another brick.

The power does not know whether these things are in need. It knows that each continues where continuation is possible.

It sends a little rain to the high course. The film comes back in fine branches, crosses a molded face, and stops. The power waits, then sends no more rain there.

It eases the weight on the buried chamber by lifting one edge of the roof. Water enters through the opening and fills the room. The tile rises, turns slowly, and comes to rest against a wall. Afterward the roof goes on becoming upright.

At the end of the wall, the power softens the clay beside the waiting impression. A new print forms there during the next interval. The old one remains, its thumb still pressed against the last brick.

These are small acts, and the plain is immense. Elsewhere films dry, chambers fold, and impressions disappear beneath completed courses. The power could hold every chamber open and keep water at the edge of every film. It could prevent a wall from ever settling or a pool from leaving its bed. It sees, too, the chambers that form in newly settled walls, the films that need a dry interval, the prints that travel onward when their work is done. It cannot preserve every condition in which something lives.

It makes a place in which conditions may change more slowly.

Beyond the low gate, where the paving gives way to a long unfinished wall, it raises a shallow bank. The bank is made of clay from the plain, pinched up a little at a time. On one side, water gathers. On the other, the paving remains only damp. The bank has several gaps, and through them the water may pass when it is deep enough.

Beneath it the power leaves a hollow, broad and low, with room to yield under pressure. It places no object inside. At one end of the bank it sets a row of empty molds. The other end it leaves rough, the bricks unaligned and their upper faces soft.

The place is not enclosed. Nothing is brought to it.

For several intervals, the water rises and sinks. A film reaches the bank from the wet side and branches across it. Where the film passes, the clay takes on its delicate stain. At one gap, the stain holds a shallow print. Later another print appears inside the nearest mold, and a brick forms there. It is not removed at once.

The hollow remains empty longer. Its roof settles twice and rises again. Then, in a dark interval, a small chamber forms beneath it, keeping a broken piece of paving near one wall. The larger hollow changes shape around the chamber and leaves it room.

When the next disturbance begins, rain hangs in rows below the vapor. The pools show different arrangements of the wall; in one, the bank is already broken, and in another it extends far beyond the gate. The power holds its attention there. The water in the gaps draws back and returns, draws back and returns, before the rain strikes.

A brick slips from the rough end of the bank. The chamber beneath the hollow flattens around its piece of paving. The film disappears under a sheet of water. For several minutes the bank bends and its courses loosen, but it does not fold. When the trembling passes, one gap has closed and another has opened farther along. The chamber gradually rounds itself. On the bank, the film emerges from the retreating water, broken into several separate patches.

The disturbance has made an enclosure elsewhere, and within it a roof is beginning on the ground. Around the roof are fresh molds, and beyond them a district has settled into a broad, shallow pool. The power attends to these in turn.

At the bank, a narrow impression forms over the place where the brick slipped. It closes around the brick’s edge. During the next light interval the brick is set back into the course, a little apart from its neighbors, leaving a space through which water can pass.
