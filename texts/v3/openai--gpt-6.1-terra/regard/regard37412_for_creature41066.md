The power notices first the line of frost crossing the third socket. It can see the fitting that will follow: a narrow support for a conduit, with two feet and a channel down its center. One foot will cover the green film. Water will run from the conduit onto the exposed edge of the tuft, bringing more sealant with it.

This is not the worst thing the power has seen. It stays.

Behind the plates, it follows the mechanism through a cycle and waits through the long interval before the next. It follows the wire from each socket to the contacts above the warm panel, where the moss becomes three small readings on a screen. Someone looks at the screen at the beginning and end of a shift. The readings help keep the air in another room safe for people who work there.

The power considers moving the moss. The mesh beneath each tuft is delicate, and the sockets are part of what makes the readings comparable. It considers stopping the rain. The rain carries salts the moss needs, and clears certain vapors from the air. It considers warming the room, then waits until the valve opens again and sees how quickly the ice leaves the pipes intact.

Instead it goes to the fitting, which has not yet been brought down from storage. The power changes the drawing from which it will be made: one foot is a little shorter, the channel ends in a small turned lip, and the base will leave a narrow gap beside the socket. The change appears on the plans, with a note that the clearance protects the monitor beneath it. A person checks the revised drawing against the neighboring parts and approves it.

The power also finds the pipe above the second tuft. It leaves the pipe sound, but gives the bead a place to gather farther along its underside. There, when the drop grows heavy, it falls onto the tile and reaches the socket by a seam.

For the first tuft, it does less. The mineral dust has already crusted its lower threads; a sudden washing might tear them loose. The power opens a hair-fine path through the deposit, enough that some of the next rain can reach the mesh beneath. Then it lets the water find its own way.

The mechanism engages. A plate shifts, and sealant rises along its edge. Vapor reaches the nearest socket, and the reading above the panel changes. At the end of the shift, someone sees the trace, checks it against the construction log and adds a second check to the following morning’s work.

Days pass. The new fitting arrives. Its shorter foot settles beside the green film. When the valve opens, water runs down the conduit and hangs briefly from the turned lip before falling clear of the socket. Some reaches the moss along the tile, carrying a little salt.

A fresh line of frost forms around the base. One thread of moss lies beneath it. When the light brightens, the thread is visible through the ice; afterward the ice thins, and the thread remains where it is.

The power does not make it grow faster. It keeps watch long enough to see the next tip appear, pale at first, then green.
