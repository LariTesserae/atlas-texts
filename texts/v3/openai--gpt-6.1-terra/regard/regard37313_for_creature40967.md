The power came first as an attention. It did not enter the chamber, for there was nowhere to stand that would not be among the roots.

It listened long enough to hear each animal separately. It listened to the amber passing through the threads, and to the high answer of the slab. It found that the chamber could continue as it was without injury. It could not tell, from that alone, whether continuing was what anyone there wanted.

So it waited at the edge of the sound and made room for an answer.

It did not quiet the chord. Instead, it gave the slab a second way to ring: a note so faint that the grains did not tremble for it. Beneath that note it placed an invitation, which the chamber could receive as easily as a fold in a frond receives a finger:

*I am here. Would you like me to stay?*

For a while there was only the chord. The amber circulated. The plates remained where they were.

Then one of the little animals altered its pitch.

The change was so slight that its nearest neighbors might not have heard it. The slab heard, and the power stayed.

It learned slowly. There was someone here, though the power could not have pointed to a single body and said *there*. At times an answer came from one crown; at times it passed among all six, each animal contributing a small part of it. The amber carried something too, though not words. The being knew the shape of the chord from within, and knew the warmth of the floor, and the precise pressure of the earth against the slab.

It had been there a long time. Long enough to know every note and every folded surface, but not long enough to have grown tired of them. It had no name for the stone cup. It did not know what lay above the slab.

The power told it about the salt plain: its great white distances and the shadows that crossed them. It described rain, carefully, without letting any into the chamber. It described the wearing down of a sharp corner, and the slow arrival of roots in dark soil. The being listened through thousands of ivory bells.

*Could these things come here?* it asked at last.

The power considered the warm floor, the slab, the delicate circuit of amber. There were things it could change safely, and things whose safety it could not yet promise.

*Some could,* it said. *We could begin with very little. We could stop whenever you wish.*

The being took time to choose. Finally, it asked about the cup.

Together they learned what a cup could hold. The power opened a minute place in the root above it, and one drop of amber fell. The being gathered the fluid around the opening so that no more would follow. The drop rested at the bottom of the stone cup, bright against the gray.

The chord continued. The slab answered. Nothing collapsed; none of the animals fell. After some time, the being asked for the drop to be lifted back into the root, and the power showed it how. It asked to do this again. Then it asked to leave the drop in the cup a little longer.

The power stayed while it practiced. When it was time to go, it left the faint second note in the slab, where the being could sound it again.

Much later, beneath the salt plain, a drop fell into the cup. It remained there through many repetitions of the chord. Then the being drew it up, waited, and let another fall.
