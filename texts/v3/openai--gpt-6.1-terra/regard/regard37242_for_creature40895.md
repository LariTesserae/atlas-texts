The power came to the ridge while the water was clear.

It found a child pressing both hands against a wall of new stone. The child had been told to watch this place, where a seam had begun to swell beneath a path. Every so often she took her hands away and laid them flat again, as if she might feel a change more clearly by beginning over.

The power could feel what she felt: the warmth traveling through the crust, the fine tremor of water moving inside it. Farther along the seam, beneath the roof of a sleeping chamber, the water was already finding a way upward.

It could have closed the seam. It could have held the roof in place until the stone around it hardened. But the seep would find another course, and the people would be left to discover where.

Instead, the power made the seam knock.

The first sound was small. The child lifted her hands. It came again, three dull taps beneath the stone, then a pause, then three more. She called to an older person working nearby, who came and put a palm against the wall.

They followed the sound along the path, taking turns feeling for it. Where the seam passed under the sleeping chamber, they could hear it without touching the ground.

The household was gathering food when the warning reached them. They came back and listened at the threshold. One went inside for the leaf bedding; another carried out a small stone basin and the smooth pieces they kept in it. The others began lifting the mineral nodules from the roof.

By then, several neighbors had arrived. They moved the heavier stones together, passing them down the terrace and setting them among the stalks. The roof slab was still difficult to shift. Four people worked a clay wedge beneath one edge while another held a rope around it. When the slab finally rose, warm white water slipped from the gap below.

The power steadied the stone as they carried it clear. None of them saw the help, though one later said that the slab had seemed strangely well balanced.

They moved their sleeping hollow into a flooded room nearby. The following morning the seam opened fully. Water welled through the old floor and filled the chamber with pale sediment. For several days the knocking continued, growing fainter as the new channel widened.

The child returned often. She sat with her hand on the path and listened to the sound changing under the rock. When it had stopped, she still felt a faint shiver there, too small to hear.

After a time, the household built again a little farther down the terrace. They used the old roof slab, which had not cracked, and made a basin for the warm water that now rose through their former floor. Worms gathered there. The youngest child of the household liked to watch their tips emerge when the water was still.

The power remained near the town for a while. It watched the people clear their paths after a clouding and shake the sediment from the field leaves. It watched the scarred grazer pass, and the neighbors come out afterward with lengths of living fence.

Before leaving, it touched the ridge once more. Wherever water was gathering enough force to break a settled crust, the stone would knock softly. The sound would not tell the people what to do, and it would not warn them of everything. But it could be heard through a palm resting on the ground.

The child who had first heard it grew older and taught others to listen. Sometimes they moved a path. Sometimes they waited and found that the seam had quieted on its own. They kept their hands against the stone long enough to tell the difference, as well as they could.
