The power came to the plateau by way of the lower slopes. It took a long time over the ascent, stopping where the stone changed beneath its hand.

It could feel the heat there, moving slowly outward from the city. It could also feel how much remained.

At the first retaining wall it sat down. A small jointed animal had fallen into a crack between two stones and could not climb the smooth sides. The power laid a finger in the crack and waited until the animal had climbed onto it. Then it put the animal down beside a warm vent.

It stayed by the wall through many turns of the machinery. Once, a loaded conveyor passed beneath the paving and the pressure in the stone changed. Farther into the city, a press stopped, rested, and began again. Nothing approached along the street.

The power went on.

In a storage building it found the stacks of tiles. It examined them without disturbing the animals between them. Some showed alterations already made. Others showed streets that were still as they had been when the tiles were pressed. Several bore plans for the same district, each different from the last. One opened a square; another kept the buildings and made a narrow covered passage between them. The tiles were stacked in no order the power could discover.

It walked to the district and found a seam cleared of grit where the passage might begin. A tool had been left in the seam: a thin bar of dark metal, worn smooth near one end. The power did not touch it.

For a long while it listened to the machines and followed their shafts under the streets. It found bearings close to the end of their use, and others newly packed. It found a press whose frame had cracked and been bound with bands cut from some older piece of equipment. Beneath one hall, where a shaft passed close to the heated stone, a section of wall had been opened and carefully closed again. The repair had been difficult. The stone there was cooling.

The power could have warmed the entire plateau. It could have filled the streets with light and made the presses quick and silent. It could have drawn the city’s remaining heat into a single bright source that would last longer than the ridges.

Instead it went back to the retaining wall and waited.

After a time, someone came for the tool.

The power saw only the movement of a small figure between two buildings. When it reached the cleared seam, it paused. The tool was where it had been left. The figure picked it up and stood there, facing the place where the power waited.

The power raised one hand. In its palm lay a piece of stone, black and roughly the size of a tile. A faint warmth came from it.

It set the stone on the pavement and stepped back.

The figure did not approach at once. When it did, it carried the tool in both hands. It touched the stone, then drew away. After a while it touched the stone again, holding its hand there longer.

The power showed what it had made. It turned the stone on its edge, and the warmth diminished. It laid it flat, and the warmth returned. Then it lifted the stone clear of the pavement, and the warmth fell away almost completely.

The figure watched. It turned the stone several times itself, then carried it toward the open seam. There it set the stone down and worked beside it with the bar.

The power stayed where it was. By the time the figure had loosened the first paving block, the stone beneath it was warm.

For some time afterward the power found the stone in different parts of the city. Once it lay beside a bearing that had been taken apart. Once it was propped against a wall near a cluster of animal eggs. Later it disappeared into a passage beneath the workshops.

The power waited at the retaining wall. When the stone was brought back, the figure set it on the pavement with a tile beside it. The tile bore the plan of the hall where the cracked press stood. A shallow mark had been scratched into one corner, beside the wall that had been opened and closed.

The figure waited while the power looked. Then it picked up the stone and led the way.

They worked under the hall. The figure removed the old masonry around the shaft while the power held the weight above it. Animals came out of the opened bearing, and the figure gathered them on a scrap of cloth and placed them near another motor. Together they fitted the warm stone into the wall and built around it, leaving its upper edge exposed. The figure tested it before closing the last course: flat for warmth, on edge for less, lifted free when none was wanted.

The press continued to move overhead. Dust sifted down with each stroke and settled on their hands.

When the work was finished, the figure took the marked tile away. The power returned to the retaining wall. It remained there for a while, but the figure did not come again, and eventually the power went down the broad slopes.

The machinery kept turning. Under the hall, the stone warmed the shaft and the new packing around it. The animals returned when the housing was closed. From time to time the stone was turned, and the warmth in the wall grew stronger or weaker.

Much later, a visitor found the tile of the hall in a storage room. A small square had been pressed into its scratched corner, and beside it someone had drawn a line that ended at the edge.
