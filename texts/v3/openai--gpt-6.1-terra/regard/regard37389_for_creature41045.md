The power attends first to the seams.

It follows the water as it descends through hairline cracks, warming as it goes. It finds the films of microbes and the springtail beneath its chip, and the narrow band of damp grit between the drying air and the heat of the floor. It takes the measure of what a change might do.

The split in the ceiling will widen. One morning, a flake will fall across the seep above the chip, diverting the water onto the hotter stone. In the days afterward, the springtail’s shelter will dry.

The power waits until the flake loosens. As it falls, the power turns it slightly. It lands beside the seep instead, with a sound like a fingernail tapping glass. Water continues down the same channel. The flake makes a little more shade over the grit.

Nothing else in the chamber needs changing. The pool remains hot; crystals gather and dissolve at its rim. The springtail moves out from beneath its chip when the conditions are right, and returns when they are not. The microbes continue their slow work in the sheltered seams.

The power keeps its attention there for a while. Above, the sun passes over the slope, and the blade of light withdraws up the rock.
