The power came down through the rock, though it had no need of the shaft. It found the engine first, by its heat and its steady knock, and followed the water upward through the pipe to the town. There it found a woman filling a kettle, a boy washing grit from a scraped hand, and a row of small gardens that would be watered in the evening.

Then it returned to the chamber and attended to what was smaller.

In the channel a larva bent away from the shadow of a bat. The bat could not reach it there, but would take a midge before morning. Beneath the housing a cockroach pulled at the soft body of another that had died. The shoots under the shaft had grown toward a patch of light so narrow that their leaves touched one another.

The power could have widened the shaft, cooled the engine, made the lamps shine like the sun. Each change reached further than the place where it began. More light would warm the channel and alter what grew in it. A wider shaft would bring rain and cold. Stopping the engine would leave the chamber quieter and the town without water.

For a long while it listened.

There was a bearing beginning to wear. The worker had heard it on his last visit, but only briefly, while the strainer was in his hands. The power did not mend it. Instead, when he came down the following morning, it held the other noises back for a moment: the draft, the rustling, even the splash of water at the pipe’s mouth. In that brief quiet the bearing gave its thin, uneven cry.

The worker set down the strainer. He listened, put his hand against the housing and frowned. Before leaving, he marked the bearing with chalk.

The repair took place two days later. The power went with the workers’ hands as far as they would let it. It kept a loose tool from falling into the channel. When one worker looked up and saw the bats, the power stayed the other’s raised arm until he looked too. They finished the work without disturbing the roost.

Afterward, the engine ran more evenly. The midges returned to the lamps. A few had been caught on the oily rag left beside the housing; a cockroach found them before the worker came back for it.

The power lingered near the shoots. Their roots had almost filled the crack, and the next dry spell would reach them quickly. It opened a hairline path through the limestone so that a little seep water ran beneath their roots. It was a small alteration, and the power watched it for some time. The channel below received slightly less water, though not enough to leave its stones dry.

At the next maintenance visit, the worker noticed the damp patch under the shaft. He crouched to inspect it, touched one of the leaves with a dirty finger, and decided the seep was too small to report. On his way out he moved a length of discarded pipe away from the crack, where it had been pressing against one of the shoots.

The power followed him as far as the hatch. When it closed, the chamber was dim again. The water ran under the roots and on into the channel. Above it, a bat shifted in its joint, settled against its neighbors, and slept.
