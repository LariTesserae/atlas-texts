The power came without a lamp.

It could distinguish a grazer resting against a pillar from a grain of grit lodged beneath its shell. It found the young beneath the adults, and the animals in the grit, and the small lives among the folds of their bodies. Beyond them were more pillars and more chambers, some the width of a hand, some large enough for the warm air to gather and circulate for years.

The chipped blade had fallen from a maker’s grasp. The low wall had been built by another, much later, who had needed a place to rest. Neither maker was near enough now to be called back. The power left their work where it lay.

Under the edge of a glass blade, a shelf-dweller had come to rest with one of its legs trapped. The leg had bent beneath it on landing. Each time the animal pulled, the edge cut farther into the delicate mesh inside its body.

The power lifted the glass. The animal drew its leg free and lay still. Around it, the tiny creatures that had gathered at the base of the blade shifted into the opening. The power set the glass down a little farther away, leaving room for them.

It eased the injury in the shelf-dweller’s leg. When the animal was ready, it folded itself and began to climb the nearest pillar. Partway up, it stopped at a film of mineral and fed.

Elsewhere a grazer was dying. The grazer had lived long enough for its plates to thicken and wear, and there were young in the shelter of the congregation around it. The power stayed with it until its body was quiet. It lessened what pain it could, then left the shell for the lives that would come to it.

In another chamber, warmth had begun to fail. A narrow seam beneath the floor was closing, and over many seasons the films on the pillars had thinned. The grazers there spent longer and longer over each patch. Their young had grown slowly; fewer of them had reached the age when they could leave the shelter of the adults.

The power opened the seam enough for warmth to rise again. It did so gradually. The air moved a little more freely between the pillars, and the films began to spread. One by one, the grazers found them.

There were other places where warmth was failing, and places where the air had become too dry. The power attended to these also, making changes small enough for the lives nearby to endure. It watched their effects, and where it had done too much, it altered its work.

A long time passed. On a shelf above the chamber where the seam had been opened, a cluster of animals had grown crowded. One released its hold. Its body unfolded in the warm air, and it drifted down toward the waiting limbs on the floor.

The two touched. The animal below raised its other limbs. For a while both remained still. Then the shelf-dweller folded its body, found purchase on a limb, and climbed away. The animal on the floor settled again into the grit.

The power remained in the country. There was always more to notice, and time enough to notice it.
