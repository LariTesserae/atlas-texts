The power listened first from the room above.

It could have heard every movement at once, but that was not the same as understanding them. It stayed through the cooling of the room and the long damp hours afterward. It learned the difference between a hinge-back grazing in a joint and a builder scraping one clear. It learned that a pebble placed in a passage was not necessarily lost, and that a roof flake lifted at one edge might be a door.

The water troubled it. A single drop could kill an animal in the basin, and the reports went through the sleeping chamber as readily as through the rest of the settlement. But the builders stirred at some changes in the sound and slept through others. They used the basin to know what was happening at the seam. The power could not tell, merely by listening, what they would wish changed.

It looked beyond the chamber. The water came through a long, narrow course in the rock. Higher up, a root had entered an old fracture; in time it would thicken and loosen a stone beside the seam. The stone would not fall for many seasons. When it did, it would close part of the passage and send the water by another route. The basin would empty.

The power could keep the stone in place. It could also remove it, or move the root, or open another seam. It considered all of these and began with the root.

Far above the chamber, it softened the earth on one side and made a little space where the root could grow. Then it loosened the root from the fracture without tearing it. Water continued down its accustomed course. Nothing in the settlement shifted.

That was the first thing it did for the people below, and they never knew of it. Afterward the power remained uneasy about doing more without their knowledge.

On a dry patch of the shelf, away from the doors and the basin, it laid a flake of slate. The flake was thin and sound, with one straight edge. Beside it the power placed three small pebbles. A builder came out after a time, touched the slate with its feelers, and withdrew. Later, two builders returned. They turned the flake over together, struck it with a tool, and took it into the masonry. They left the pebbles.

The next day the power set down another flake, and a third. The builders took one and used it to replace a roof. They leaned the other against a wall near the basin, where it stayed.

The power touched the rock beneath that wall: one gentle pulse, then two. It waited. A builder emerged and put its hands on the wall. The power repeated the pulses. The builder stayed there a long time, then tapped once with a flake.

It took many days to establish even a few exchanges. The power made mistakes. Once it answered a sequence that had not been meant for it, and the builders stopped working until the strange vibration ceased. After that it learned to wait for a hand against the particular stone beside the basin. Even so, much of what passed between them remained uncertain.

The builders understood that something outside their passages could feel their taps and answer them. They discovered that it could bring pieces of slate. One of them tried asking for a flake with a notched edge; the power brought three, and the builders chose one. Another time they asked for nothing the power could understand. It put a pebble beside the wall as a sign of its uncertainty. A builder carried the pebble away and did not repeat the request.

The power tried to tell them about the root. It found no way to convey the fracture, the seasons of slow growth, or the stone that would have fallen. The builders understood only that the water’s course had been touched somewhere beyond their reach. For a while several of them kept their hands against the basin rim, feeling the familiar impacts. One brought out the thread and gauge. The water stood at the lip.

They began to check it more often.

The power regretted frightening them, though it did not regret moving the root. It kept its hands, as it thought of them, away from the water after that.

In time the builders found uses for their distant neighbor. They asked for flakes, pebbles, and once a length of fine root that had died and dried in a crevice. They worked the root into thread. The power brought too much on the first occasion; the heap obstructed a passage, and the builders had to move it before a hinge-back carrying young could get through. From then on it brought one piece at a time.

They did not ask for the falling water to be quieted. The power still wondered whether some part of the sleeping chamber could be sheltered from the hardest reports. It brought a hollow chip of stone, light enough to be moved, and left it on the dry shelf. The builders examined it and eventually placed it over an opening used to store scraped mineral. It kept the heap from washing away when water rose beneath the house.

The room above remained abandoned. A crack widened in one wall; dust gathered in the corners. When rain entered through the roof, the power guided it away from the floor seam, taking care not to dry the rock below. Years later, when people came to inspect the room, it turned their attention to a sound in the adjoining stairwell. They went there first and found a loose board that needed repair. By the time they returned, they had forgotten the faint noise beneath the floor.

Below, the builders continued to lift roofs, clear joints, and lay scraps at the thresholds. Their neighbor became one more thing they knew how to reach, though they did not reach for it every day. Sometimes a flake remained beside the basin for months before anyone had use for it.

The power listened when they called. At other times it listened less closely, leaving them the privacy of their difficult, noisy home.

The basin stayed full. The drain carried the overflow under the houses, and now and then a bead pushed up between the paving stones. A builder who needed to cross that place went around it, feeling the way with one hand along the wall.
