The power came down the shaft without taking up space in it. The warm walls held their warmth. Nothing in the chamber fell silent.

It attended first to the chord, and then to the sounds within it: the uneven scrape of a young glassback, the dry clicks at the wheel, the minute shifting of the cups as they released a foot. It followed a grain of powder from the ceiling to a ledge, from a glassback’s mouth to its feet, and from there through two more animals before it came to rest among the roots of a stalk.

It stayed long enough to see the grain taken up.

The power could have quieted the chamber. It could have made the creatures long-lived, or opened the ceiling to the stars, or stopped the wheel and learned what depended upon it. Instead, it listened and watched. The noise did not keep the animals from finding one another. The wheel gave rides as well as danger. Death fed bodies that were still alive. None of this meant that every hurt was needed.

Near the wheel, a white animal came to the rim of its pipe. It had a fold in its soft body where it had been pressed against the metal while growing. The fold caught on a rough place inside the pipe, and each time the animal drew back, it pulled the fold tighter.

The power felt the rough place with a tenderness almost too small to name. It eased a flake of iron loose. The animal withdrew, leaving the flake at the mouth of the pipe, and after a while came out again. It crossed the spot where it had been caught without pausing. Later a glassback carried the flake away.

Beyond the chamber wall, the power found a seam in the stone. It widened the seam gradually, over many turnings of the wheel, until it opened into a hollow not much larger than one of the machinery frames. The hollow held heat. Its floor was ridged, and there was powder in the sheltered places. At the entrance the power left a narrow screen of stone, with openings wide enough for the animals to pass but small enough that the chamber’s warmth would not drain away.

No creature was placed inside. The power waited.

For a long time the hollow remained empty. Then a scavenger from the deep seams came through, bracing itself against the sides of an opening. It crossed the floor, examined a crack, and went back. A glassback arrived later, bearing a grain that it dropped just inside the entrance. It scraped at the wall, found little to eat, and returned to its ledge.

The grain stayed where it had fallen. In time other grains came to rest beside it, carried on feet or shaken from shells. A fragment of a stalk’s root found its way into the hollow too. It lay among the grains through many warm cycles, pale and still, before a fine filament began to extend into the powder.

The power did not know what would become of the root. It could have looked ahead, but for now it watched as the first filament found a seam.

In the old chamber the marked tooth came around. An upright animal clicked against it and withdrew its leg. Beneath a ledge, a vacancy traveled slowly through the glassbacks, each animal moving into the place left by another. The power remained for a while at the entrance to the hollow, attending to both rooms, as the chord filled one and entered the other more faintly through the screen.
