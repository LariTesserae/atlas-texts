The power came to the country of leaves without entering it. There was no place to stand that was not already occupied, and it had no need of a place to stand.

It attended first to the bead of water at the leaf’s tip. The bead was not falling. Whether it was held there or had never been given a direction, the power could not tell from looking. It listened to the leaf, then to the cup above it, then to the small animal beneath the cup, whose mouth lay against a vein.

The white branching inside the animal brightened, very slightly. The change passed into the leaf and along its stem. A hair inside a folded leaf leaned farther toward the soft body it sheltered.

The power waited. It could have opened all the buds at once. It could have filled the chambers, sent water through the avenues, made the unmoving creatures lift their heads. It did none of these things.

Instead it made a little room.

At the edge of the valley, where a bank of roots enclosed a shallow seam of moisture, it eased two roots apart. It did this slowly enough that the fine fibers between them remained intact. Into the space it put a hollow no larger than a seed. The hollow contained darkness, and the darkness was cool.

For a long while nothing touched it. The roots held their new shape. The moisture along their seam gathered into a clear line, then stayed there, neither entering the hollow nor withdrawing.

At last a fiber extended from the nearer root. It reached across the gap and rested against the far side. A second followed, jointed and fringed. The two lay together, leaving the darkness open beneath them.

The power took this for an answer, though not an answer to every question. It made a second hollow close by, and a third farther up the bank. Between them it left the roots as they were.

The coolness spread a little through the substrate. At a water cup some distance away, one of the pale animals lifted the edge of a rear limb. Under it the leaf was the same green as elsewhere, except for a faint white mark where the animal had rested. The limb settled again, a short distance from the mark.

Nothing hurried it to move farther.

In one of the leaf-roofed chambers, the fine covering over a lobed depression rose. It rose only enough to reveal that the depression was deeper than it had seemed. The pale limb against the wall bent at its tip. From beneath the mat came a sound like two damp surfaces separating.

The power brought the coolness to the chamber’s entrance and let it stop there. It did not shine a brighter light inside. After a while the limb straightened, and the covering sank back into its place.

Elsewhere, the hanging bead lengthened. It touched the leaf below and joined a smaller bead already there. The two remained together, trembling so faintly that the trembling could be seen only in their reflection of the ceiling.

The power stayed with the country while these things happened. It stayed through intervals in which nothing happened that it could discern. It learned that the hum changed near the chamber after the limb moved, and that the hairs in the folded leaves changed with it. It learned where the dry places lay and left them dry; creatures lived there too.

When it had made a few more hollows, each beside a living root and each small enough to be closed, it ceased making them. One closed at once. In another, a pale grain came to rest, its membrane unbroken. The others remained open, holding their pockets of cool dark under the white-gold ceiling.

The bead on the lower leaf did not fall again. The animal beneath the cup kept one limb a little apart from its body. In the chamber, the covering remained smooth, though from time to time the pale tip touched the wall in a different place.

The power did not know what the country would become. It left the hollows connected to the roots, so that the country could fill them, widen them, or let them be. Then it attended to the thin hum and waited to learn what, if anything, was wanted next.
