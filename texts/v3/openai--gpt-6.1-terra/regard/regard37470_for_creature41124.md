The power came to the basin without entering it all at once. It attended first to the water beyond the ice, then to the ice itself, then to the narrow streets below. It learned the turns of the wheels and the routes by which their brine returned to the foundations. It followed a grain of sediment until it lodged against a frond, and followed one of the clear animals as it pulled the grain into place.

The power could have cleared the water. It could have raised the town above the sea and set air moving again through the factory halls. The buildings would have stood straighter in that air, and their windows would have admitted light.

It left them where they were.

Above the central channel, a crack had begun to branch through the ice. With each tide beyond the basin, its edges shifted against one another. In time a long section would break free. It would fall across two factories, stop three of the broad wheels, and send a current through the streets strong enough to strip the fronds from their shelters.

The power closed most of the crack. It left a narrow passage through the ice, where water from above could still enter slowly. Around the passage it thickened the ice in a curve, so that the movement of the sea would press the two sides together. Small particles continued to descend through the opening. A few settled on the roofs; others found their way to the chilled plates.

In one factory a bearing had worn thin. The wheel beside it still turned, but its axle had begun to lean. A dark animal lay against the housing, its filaments lifted by the irregular flow. It had been there for a long time. Beneath its sole the iron was cleaner than the metal around it, and a row of capsules was fastened to the pipe just downstream.

The power held the wheel still. The animal contracted, and the faint outlines around the pipes loosened and drifted through the room. The power repaired the bearing, using iron drawn from a collapsed beam in an empty hall. It fitted the new surface close enough to carry the axle, leaving the small gap the turning wheel required.

Then it let the wheel move again.

The animal remained contracted for some time. When the water settled into its familiar rhythm, it spread its sole over the housing. One of its filaments touched the repaired edge and lay there, bending slightly with each turn.

The power returned to the capsules often. It could feel the young changing inside them. When the first emerged, pale and narrow, the parent did not move toward it. The young animal held to the pipe for a while, then lost its grip and passed into the central channel.

The power followed. It could have caught the animal, brought it back to the bearing, and set it beside the others. Instead it made a small place on the wall beneath a streetlamp: a shallow hollow where the current was weak, with a rough surface the young could hold. The animal passed the hollow and continued upward. At the lamp it caught on a salt flake, slipped from it, and came to rest among the branches.

The power left the hollow where it was. Later, two other young animals found it. One stayed for a season, growing dark along its back. The other crept out into the channel and disappeared above the roofs.

The power made a few more such places, far enough apart that no single current could empty them all. It did not fill them with food. Salt sheets reached some and missed others; clear grazers gathered where there was material for them to take. The dark animals fed as they had before. The fronds grew and were cropped and grew again.

Over the years, the power learned which wheels could fail without harm and which needed attention. It loosened a blockage in one pipe. It left another blocked, because a colony had settled inside it and the water had found a sufficient way around. When a large visitor entered the basin, the power watched it pass among the roofs. It took many of the clear animals from a wall and moved on. In the emptied place, sediment gathered; after a long time, grazers returned from the next street.

The power remained aware of the ice. It remained aware, too, of the water beyond it: of changes that would come slowly toward the basin and of others that would arrive without warning. It did not know everything the town would become. When a pump changed speed, it watched the water for longer before deciding whether anything needed to be done.

Near the repaired bearing, the old animal eventually died. Its body loosened from the iron and drifted down through the factory, coming to rest against a beam. Clear grazers gathered along its edge. Above them, the wheel continued to turn, and a younger dark animal settled on the clean patch beside it.
