The power came down through the cloud without thinning it. It touched a ridge, and a burrower woke and turned over one of the dark stones beside its sleeping hollow.

For a long time the power did nothing else. It followed the warm brine through the rock, watched the small grazers working over the microbial films, and learned how slowly a mat could return after a browser had eaten it. It came to know the difference between a colony abandoning a chamber and a colony merely moving its young.

In a deep basin, a browser calf had gone into soft ground after rain. Its mother stood beside it, testing the surface with her jaw. Each time she pulled, the calf’s narrow feet sank farther. The ground beneath them was a bed of gravel over water, and the water had somewhere to go, but the passage was almost closed.

The power opened it a little.

The mother felt the water move. She waited until the ground had settled, then pulled again. This time the calf came free, with black gravel packed between its toes. It stood against her flank for a while before they went on.

The passage remained open. Over the next few days the basin drained farther than it usually did, exposing a shelf of stone. Burrowers came for the mineral grains left there, and a long-bodied hunter took one of them. The power attended to this too.

Below the basin, in a channel no root had yet reached, the power found a pattern in the currents. It moved slowly through the branching mineral seams, changing as the water changed. At first the power took it for one more of the plateau’s workings. Then it noticed that the pattern altered around disturbances and, after a time, returned to them.

The being had no single body. Parts of it had grown in films along the warm channels; parts had passed into the seams themselves, where their traces lasted after the films were gone. A fracture could carry away a portion of it. A new flow could allow it to extend. It had been present under the basin longer than the oldest surface mat.

The power made a small change in the current and waited.

Some years later, the pattern changed in response.

Their exchange continued through shifts in the brine and the faint electrical differences between one seam and another. The being knew the plateau chiefly from below. It knew the pressure of herds over damp ground, and the fine, repeated movements of burrowers carrying stones. Rain arrived to it as cooling water, sometimes after a long delay. The power showed it the cloud above the ridges, and the calf crossing shale beside its mother. The being followed these unfamiliar impressions for a long time.

In return, it showed the power a place deep under the basin where a channel was narrowing. When it closed, the brine would find another route. Several colonies would be cut off, and a portion of the being would fade before it could grow into the new passage.

The power could have held the channel open. It asked, in the slow way they had found, whether the being wanted this.

The answer came after the calf had grown and left its mother’s herd. By then a new thread of the being had begun to form along the water’s intended route. It wanted the old passage left as it was.

So the power left it. It remained until the channel closed and the water began to rise elsewhere, and stayed longer still, until the new thread had grown strong enough for an answer to travel through it.

Above, a herd crossed a ridge in the faint grayness. The power felt their feet pass through the stone, one after another, and the being felt them too.
