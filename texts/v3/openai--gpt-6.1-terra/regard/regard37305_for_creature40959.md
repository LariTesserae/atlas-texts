For a long time the power does nothing that can be distinguished from attention.

It follows the warm water into the covered drains and the cool water beneath it. It feels the pressure against the submerged walls, the slight give of the hinges, the places where a mineral crust has made a pipe seem sounder than it is. It attends, too, to the chambers above: the sleeping ledges, the pools, the metal strips that a youngster has learned to set trembling with a flick of its tail.

At the lowest reservoir, a wall is beginning to bind. Its upper edge still moves freely, but a footing deep in the silt has caught against an older piece of masonry. The strain is too small for the gauges. In time, the wall may break. A sudden release would send water into several lower drains, including one where young grazers shelter, and might carry away a chamber at the foot of the nearest tower.

The power could loosen the footing without disturbing the silt. It almost does. Then a flat-bodied animal leaves the wall and rises toward the surface, and another follows. The inhabitants of the tower have seen this before. They may already be attending to the change.

The power waits.

Two of the basin dwellers come to the reservoir. They lie with their hands on the stone for a long while. One goes back to the tower and returns with a third, carrying a coil of cord. They lower it into the water beside the wall and draw it slowly along the masonry. At the place where the wall has caught, the cord quivers. They mark the spot with a white deposit scraped from a grille.

They cannot reach the footing. The water there is deep, and the channel below the wall is too narrow for them to enter. They return the next day with a long jointed tool, but it catches on the upper masonry. One lies at the edge until its skin cools, feeling for a change through the stone.

That night, in the exchange hall, the power touches a metal panel.

It begins with a single pressure, held long enough to be noticed. Several of the inhabitants turn toward it. When one approaches and places a hand against the panel, the power gives another pressure, then withdraws.

The inhabitant taps once. The power taps once.

Others gather. Their feelers move over the panel’s lower edge, looking for a loose fastening or a flow of water behind it. One brings a lamp. The power continues only when a hand is resting on the metal, and stops when the hand is lifted.

Over the next several days, they make a means of speaking. It is slow. At first the power can convey little more than attention to a place, or a pressure increasing and easing. The inhabitants teach it the patterns they use for water, strain, blockage, and danger. They disagree among themselves about what may be touching the panel. Some stay away from it; one sleeps beside it and wakes whenever the metal moves in a draft.

At last the power tells them what it has found beneath the reservoir wall. It tells them it can free the footing. It also tells them, as well as it can, that it does not know how long the wall will remain caught.

The groups send people to examine the drains. They find the young grazers in the seams above the waterline, and farther along, mineral plates belonging to a neighboring chamber. They move the plates. They carry the grazers, a few at a time on wet scraps of cloth, to a sheltered drain upstream. At the tower they clear the lower chamber and make room on a dry ledge for those who have been sleeping there.

Then they return to the panel and ask the power to loosen the wall.

The power presses upward beneath the footing, gently enough that the silt rises in a narrow cloud. The trapped masonry moves. For a moment the wall holds still; then a line travels across the reservoir, and a bridge settles far away. The inhabitants at the edge feel the change and hold their hands there until the smaller movements have passed.

They keep the lower chamber empty through the next heavy rain. Afterward, they inspect the drains and return some of the grazers to their old seams. Others have already settled in the upstream drain, where the water is warm, and are left there.

The panel remains in the hall. People come to it with questions, and sometimes with news. The power answers when it can. When it cannot, it says so. Once, asked about a pulse felt across the northern causeway, it attends to the stone for a long time and finds nothing it can name. The group waits outside anyway. Later, one of them checks the grilles.

In the wet season a youngster discovers that the panel can be made to ring by striking it near the corner. The sound carries farther than the ordinary hand-presses. For several days it interrupts every conversation in the hall, until an elder moves the lamp and shows the youngster where to strike more softly.

The power listens to that, too.
