The power came to the edge of the hollow and looked in.

It saw the three stones, and knew who had set them there and why. The knowledge did not make a path appear between them. The person who had placed them was long gone, and whatever had grown beside them was gone too. The stones were shelter now for a few lives of their own.

At the back wall, the pale animal rested in its seam. The power could see it through the limestone: the faint line along its body, the feelers folded close. It could see the eggs in the moss, each with its small store of water, and the creatures moving among the moss threads. It watched long enough to see the animal emerge and feed, then retreat as the film on the rock thinned.

There was no need to draw it out or lead it across the bare clay. The crack held moisture deeper in, as it had before. Water would come again.

So the power left the wall as it was. It did not make the ferns larger, or widen the seam, or promise the animal that nothing would eat it. It left the dead to become food, and the young to find their way through the moss.

Before departing, it laid its attention lightly over the place—not as a roof, but as a remembrance. If the land changed, if the water ceased to find this joint, it would know. Then it could look again and decide what might be done.

The animal remained in the crack. Later, when damp returned to the stone, it came out beneath one fern and moved along the thin green line.
