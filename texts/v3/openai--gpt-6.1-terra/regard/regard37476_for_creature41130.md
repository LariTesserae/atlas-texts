The power first attends to the delay.

A pulse of heat enters the eastern wall. By the time its account has crossed the room, the western inhabitants are already bearing a different load. Each passes on what it has received, changed a little by the work of holding it. The power could make the account arrive everywhere at once. It waits, and feels how much of the city’s life takes place in those intervening minutes.

It waits through many changes of the opening. A thin skin of ice forms once along its upper lip and is gone before the next breath of cold. A bead reaches the seam after crossing two old white rings. Another stops short, leaving a smaller ring that no fold beneath the floor can yet feel.

Farther down, beyond the folds gathered around the seam, there is a fracture filled with warm vapor. It has been sealed from the room for a long time. The mountain’s rising has brought one end of it close to the city, though a thick plate of stone still lies between.

The power does not break the plate.

It begins instead with a pressure too slight to damage the wall, sustained where one of the oldest inhabitants can receive it. The inhabitant yields, spreading the load through its folds. The power eases when it yields and bears down again when it holds.

The exchange continues. Other bodies receive the altered strain. A chamber brightens; then another. The old inhabitant releases a note, and, some minutes later, a note comes back through the opposite wall.

The power cannot be sure what the answer means. It can feel, however, where the inhabitants have begun to hold against it and where they leave room for the pressure to return. It stays within that room.

Over time it leads the pressure downward, toward the buried fracture. Folds near the seam become active. They open and close against the underside of the mineral shelf, passing distinctions back into the city: warmth, a hollow, moisture held behind stone. Several withdraw. Others extend farther.

The power leaves them to it for a while.

When it returns its attention to the plate, the inhabitants have made a new arrangement beneath it. Their folds touch along the fracture’s edge, holding the surrounding stone so that a small opening might be made without carrying the break into the floor. The arrangement is not uniform. There are places they have strengthened and one place they have left bare.

The power presses there.

A flake separates from the plate and drops into the fracture. Warm vapor enters the narrow space beneath the seam. For a time nothing reaches the room. Then a bead appears on the underside of the shelf, where no bead has formed before, and the nearest folds close around it.

The old inhabitants take up the new load. Their notes pass through the mountain, and some of the deeper chambers remain lit for a long time. The vapor brings heat as well as water. The city must learn to distribute it; the first folds to receive it grow warm, and others extend to help. At the bare place in the plate, the inhabitants begin to build an edge that can open or close as the pressure changes.

The power could widen the passage. The fracture holds enough water for that. But the bodies around it are still adjusting, and the flow is not yet steady. The power leaves the stone as it is.

Cold air continues to enter through the opening overhead. Some beads still evaporate before reaching the seam. The mountain continues to rise, and the wall plates flex beneath its weight. There will be other changes, including some the new passage cannot meet.

The power remains attentive through the first long cycle of warming and cooling. When the vapor lessens, the edge the inhabitants have made opens a little farther. When it strengthens, the edge closes. Water reaches them in both rhythms now: from the sky above and from the fracture below.

A low note sounds deep in the eastern wall. The power feels it travel through the stone. Before it reaches the western side, several small chambers brighten, one after another, each taking up a little of the work.
