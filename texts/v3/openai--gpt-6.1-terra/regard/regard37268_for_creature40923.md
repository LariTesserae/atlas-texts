The power listened for a long time before it touched anything.

It listened to the rails when hands closed around them, and to the low calls passing between houses. It listened to the fish beneath the shelf, and to the hollow in the old woman’s wall where a crayfish had made its home. It followed the faint current through the channels and out over the edge, where the plants trailed into deeper water.

There, beneath the green, one of the shelf’s great stones had begun to move.

It moved so slowly that silt settled on it between shifts. A root stretched across the widening seam, its fibers pulled taut. In time the stone would fall away, taking a length of the plant mat and part of the outer channel with it. There were houses above.

The power could have held the stone in place. It could have made the shelf whole and left no mark that anyone could find. Instead, it went back through the town to learn how the people cared for a thing that was failing.

It watched them ease a bowed wall upright. It watched a child fetch stones for a roof, rejecting several before finding one that lay flat without crushing the leaf beneath it. It watched the keeper of the grove loosen a root from the wickerwork and guide it toward a sounder place. She stopped often to feel the strain with her hands.

When the power understood enough to ask, it settled into one of the rails at the central basin.

A young person came along the channel, holding the wood as she passed. The rail gave three small taps beneath her hand.

She stopped. After a moment, she tapped back.

Others came when she called them. They crowded around the basin, touching the rail and one another, passing the strange message from hand to hand. The wood tapped again, slowly, in their own manner of speech:

*There is a stone moving beneath the outer channel. May I show you?*

They spoke among themselves for a while. Some were afraid of the voice in the rail. One person asked whether it had put the stone there. It answered no. Another asked what it wanted, and the answer took longer:

*I would like to help you keep your homes.*

At last the grove’s keeper took hold of the rail. She asked the voice to wait while they went to look.

They found the seam. The oldest among them remembered a narrow crack in that place, but it had grown wider than any of them knew. They felt the stone shift under their hands. They followed the roots up into the mat and saw where the ground above had begun to sag.

Back at the basin, they talked through the day. They knew which houses stood nearest the edge and which channels would be affected if they moved the stone themselves. They sent a swimmer to look farther beneath it. They considered wedges, ropes, and the long labor of cutting a new channel around the weak place.

At last they asked the voice what it could do.

*I can hold the stone while you work,* it said. *I can bring it gently back. But the seam will remain unless you mend it.*

This seemed to them a useful offer. They made their plans, and the people in the nearest houses gathered their roof stones and cords and moved for a time to stay with neighbors.

The work began the next morning. While the power held the great stone, people cleared silt from the seam and fitted smaller stones into it. They drove in wooden braces and bound them with root cord. A few went down with the grove’s keeper to guide living roots into the spaces where they could take hold without forcing the stones apart again.

The work disturbed a drain. Two crayfish came out, waving their claws, and were given time to leave before a new brace was set in place. Small fish gathered around the workers’ ankles to catch what rose from the silt.

When everything was ready, the people put their hands against the great stone. The power moved it so slowly that they could feel each change in pressure and call for it to stop. At their direction, it settled the stone against the new supports.

For many days afterward, the people checked the seam. They tightened cords, replaced one brace that had slipped, and watched the roots begin to take hold. The power remained quiet in the rail unless someone addressed it.

Then, one evening, a child came to the basin and laid both hands on the wood.

*Are you still there?* she tapped.

*Yes.*

She thought for a while.

*What do you do when you aren’t here?*

The answer came so softly that she leaned closer.

*I listen.*

She stayed with her hands on the rail. After a little while, she began to tell it about the pale fish beneath the steps: how still it could lie, and how once it had opened its mouth so wide that a snail fell out.

The power listened to that, too.
