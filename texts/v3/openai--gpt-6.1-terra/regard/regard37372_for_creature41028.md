The power came to the basin without casting a shadow.

This was not because it feared disturbing the creatures. Shadows crossed the pools often enough, and the closing of shells was one of the ways the basin knew itself. But the power wished, at first, to hear what was already happening.

It listened to the rain and the shells and the blind swimmers striking the water. It listened beneath those sounds, to the seepage moving through fractures in the stone. It felt the small effort of a grazer crossing a bare patch in search of food, and the pull of a hunter working a shell loose. At the end of that struggle the hunter fed, and afterward it carried fragments to the gravel bar.

The power knew it could stop the hunting. It could fill the pools with food so that nothing need pull another creature from the stone. But it could not see how to do this without changing the lives of everything in the basin: the grazers, the films, the shells, the hunters, and creatures farther along the water’s course that it had not yet come to know.

So it watched.

Near the upper escarpment, a slab had fallen across one of the seepage cracks. The fall was recent. Pale grit still clouded the water below it, and a narrow run had begun to dry at its edges. Shell-creatures lay exposed there, closed against the air. Some would be reached by the next rain, but others were already too far from the water.

The power lifted one end of the slab. Water came through underneath, carrying the loose grit into the run. It reached the nearest shells first and went on, slowly, until the last of those still living lay submerged. For a while nothing happened. Then one shell opened, followed by another. The power left the slab propped against two smaller stones and watched the water until it was clear.

On a rise nearby stood a shelter with a sound roof and one wall beginning to lean. The power gathered the fallen stones around its base and set them where they would support the wall without closing the entrance. It did not touch the grooves cut inside. Water had collected in them, and while the power worked, the surface of each thin pool trembled with the clatter outside.

No one came to the shelter that day. The power remained through the night. Ice formed in the still places and broke; the rain returned before morning.

When the light strengthened, the power went back to the seep. The water was still running beneath the slab. A pale-backed hunter had found the restored channel and was moving among the open shells. The power watched it carry one away, then followed the water down to where it disappeared into the gravel.

There was more of the world beyond that place, and more to learn before acting there. For now, the shelter stood ready, the seep flowed, and the basin continued its clattering under the rain.
