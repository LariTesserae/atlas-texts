The power came at the hour when the ridge was losing its light. It did not arrive as a shape in the sky. The sky remained pale, and the creatures moving between the fronds continued on their accustomed paths.

For a long while it attended to what was there.

It followed the warmth upward from the stone and the vapor through the narrow cracks. It noticed where the red threads grew fastest, and where they had been grazed almost bare. It watched the small seed-eaters disappear beneath the cushions and found the pale tips of seedlings in places no larger creature could reach.

In one hollow, three young rested against the curved wall. An adult returned, touched each one, and settled beside them. Later another adult passed the entrance without entering. The power watched until it could distinguish these movements from one another: a crossing, a pause, a return.

The hollow had been occupied through several seasons. Each passage of a padded body had encouraged the plant to firm its entrance. Now a new fold of tissue was growing inward from above. It would narrow the opening gradually. The adult might find a way to keep it open by passing often; the young might be ready to leave before it closed. But the fold was thick, and the smallest of the young had only lately shed its skin.

The power could have opened the chamber to the sky, or made the young strong at once, or arrested every growth in the basin at a convenient size. Instead, as warmth rose through the trunk, it turned a little of it toward the underside of the fold.

There the tissue softened and lengthened. Over the following days its weight drew it outward, away from the entrance. When the adult brushed beneath it, the fold yielded. Light reached farther into the hollow, and a narrow passage remained.

The power stayed to see what followed. A grazer came to the exposed edge and ate some of the red threads there. The plant sealed the grazed places with amber skin. One of the young ventured to the entrance, put two pads on the outer wall, and drew them back. Later it came out again.

The power looked for other hollows, other narrowing ways. Some had second entrances, hidden in the growth. Some were empty, though their smooth walls showed long use. It left those alone.

At the rim, where the warmth escaped most quickly, the power eased a hairline crack a fraction wider. Mineral vapor rose through it in a thin, steady breath. Seeds already lodged nearby would have a better chance of sending down roots. The power could not know which would take hold before the wind arrived, so it left the seeds where they lay.

Then it withdrew its attention a little, though not so far that it could not return.

The basin darkened. The adult left the hollow and moved along a trunk to feed. Behind it, the three young rested under the fold, which had begun to grow firm again in its new position. Beyond the rim, the terraces cooled. In the central chamber, a creature crossed the floor, lifting faint prints among the skins.
