The power noticed the basin before anything in the basin could have noticed it.

It knew how much food passed each stone, how long each colony had been closed, and which animals would soon die if nothing changed. It knew, too, that an open feeding crown would catch living things, and that the scavenger scraping a film from stone was feeding on its inhabitants. There was no way to feed everything and leave everything uneaten.

For a long while it watched the currents. It followed the grains as they rose and sank, and the young animals as they drifted above the floor. Some passed over good places to settle and were carried on. Some came down into hollows that filled with silt before they could grow.

The power could have stilled the water. Instead, it made a small change high above the basin, where two currents met. A little more water turned downward there, bringing with it fine particles of food. They spread over the floor in a thin, uneven fall.

The colonies opened. More of them caught enough to feed, and some that had been closed for a long time grew new feeding limbs. The burrowers drew their feathery branches in and sent out packets of rejected silt. The scavenger found a dead animal beside a stone and stayed there until its gut was dark and full.

Not all of the food was caught. Some settled, and some was carried beyond the rise. The power watched what happened to it. When the water near the bottom began to hold less oxygen, it reduced the fall and waited for the currents to clear it. Later, it sent another, smaller fall.

A stone shifted on the plain, trapping one of the scavenger’s legs beneath its edge. The animal pulled and could not get free. Silt gathered around its body. The power lifted the stone just enough for the leg to come loose, then lowered it again, leaving a narrow space beneath it. The scavenger moved into the crack and stayed there until the current weakened.

Under the stone, most of the film remained attached. The exposed patch was scoured away, and the cells at its edge began to spread.

In time, young animals settled in the basin. Some grew in the shelter of stones; others were lost to the silt or found by animals searching the mud. The currents continued to cross the plain, raising columns that spread and sank. When a dense cloud reached the colonies, their crowns still closed.

The power stayed near enough to attend to them. It let the currents carry food where they would, and from time to time, when it was needed and could be borne, it brought a little more down.
