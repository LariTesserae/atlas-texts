The power came close enough to hear the fans separately: the rush through the ducts, the uneven beat of one worn bearing, the rattle of a loose panel against its frame. Far below, people were working in air the fans made possible.

It left the machines running.

At the edge of the building, a door seal had split. The power held it against the frame until morning, when a worker noticed the strip hanging loose and replaced it. A week later, another worker found the worn bearing during an inspection. They changed it before it failed.

The power attended to the basin too. It traced the vole’s runs beneath the snow and found where a thin place in the roof had left the animal exposed. During the night, a little snow drifted into the gap. By morning there were fresh marks at the other end of the run.

In the hollows, the moss was frozen at its surface but wet beneath. The power felt how much lived among its stems. It did not warm the basin or fill it with water. The winter had its work to do, and the workings below depended on pumps whose limits it had taken care to understand. It kept watch through a thaw, when water gathered against one of the loose slopes. The slope held.

Over the following months, the people tending the building found reasons to mend things that had been neglected: a leaking pipe, a damaged section of duct, the loose panel that had rattled whenever the wind came from the north. Each repair made a small difference. The fans were still loud among the pines, but the pounding no longer returned so sharply from the cut walls.

One evening a worker paused outside the door after a shift. The lamps showed the crusted footprints, the moss and a few sedge heads moving in the air from the vent. Beyond them, the basin was growing dark.

The worker stood there for a while, then went home. The power remained through the night, attending to the water in the pumps and the water held under the moss.
