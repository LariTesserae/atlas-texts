The power noticed the plain first by its warmth. Against the colder distances around it, the terraces held a steady temperature, losing heat at night and gathering it again beneath the pale sky. The suspended structures moved through the upper air. Below them, in a seam under a low ridge, one of the long animals turned around a hard knot of damaged material and began to eat.

The power came closer.

It could have opened the ridge without breaking a single sheet. It could have lifted the animal into the light and examined each of its sections separately. Instead it waited while the animal worked its way through the knot. This took eleven days. The animal left a clean channel behind it and closed the seam, except for a small gap where one section had failed to lay enough adhesive. A microscopic worker found the gap and filled it.

The power watched the workers too, and the plants, and the hunters that ate at their roots. It watched a suspended structure lower its filaments into a region where the air had stopped circulating. When the filaments withdrew, the vents breathed again, though a broad patch of the flat-growing plants had died.

There were failures the structures could not reach. Deep beneath the terraces lay a layer from which the mineral streams drew one of their rarer components. It had been accumulating there since before the ridges were worn round. Now the layer was thin. In some places the workers had already altered the channels to draw from it more slowly.

The power looked ahead. It saw terraces where the black stems grew farther apart, and hunters waited longer between meals. It saw the suspended structures bringing material from great distances, and later saw fewer of them in the sky. The plain would continue for a long time, but less of it would be inhabited.

There was material enough elsewhere. A body of it moved through the dark beyond the atmosphere, following a path that would never bring it near the plain. The power changed that path. It did so gradually, over many years, until the body passed within reach of the suspended structures.

They turned toward it. Several rose higher than they had risen in a long time, carrying their filaments gathered tightly beneath them. They tested the new material, cut pieces from it, and brought those pieces down. For some years they used it only in a small region near the edge of a terrace. The plants there grew well. The hunters increased, then became fewer as the collectors at the roots were brought under control. The soil breathed evenly.

After that the structures carried the material to other regions. They did not use all of it. The remainder stayed in the upper dark, where it could be reached when needed.

The power stayed long enough to see a seed open beside a seam. Its root entered the ground and divided, following a mineral stream into the terrace. A glass hunter came to wait in the shelter of its first stem.

Beneath a nearby ridge, the long animal was still alive. One of its front sections had been lost, and the others had adjusted to the work without it. The animal moved slowly through a layer of old circuitry, tasting its way along.

The power did not make the section grow back. It had found, by then, that the animal’s body contained changes from many such losses, and that some of those changes passed into the ground when it fed. The power could not tell what might be lost by undoing them.

When it withdrew, the animal continued along the seam. Its rear sections pressed the sheets together behind it, leaving a thin line of adhesive that caught the light until the dust covered it.
