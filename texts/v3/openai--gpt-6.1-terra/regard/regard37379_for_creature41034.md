The power did not begin by stilling the compressor.

It felt the beat passing through the hooked feet and the sealed shells, through the eggs on their stalks and the paddles of the swimmers. It felt, too, what happened between the beats: the searching, the feeding, the brief shelter under a shell. The animals had ways of living here, even in a place that gave them so little room.

It attended to each of them. This took no time the factory could measure.

Then, beyond the square ends of the ledges, it made more chamber.

The new walls were warm enough that the water did not approach freezing. The pool deepened by degrees, and there were teeth of many sizes on its floor, with spaces between them. Mineral film gathered on the teeth. There were seams to grip, broad rims for the snails, grout for the moss, and water carrying food into every part.

The power opened a narrow passage from the old chamber into the new. It left the upward pulse holding the droplets, and the heater beneath the floor, and the beat in the wall. At the mouth of the passage the beat continued, gentler than before; farther along it faded.

The first swimmer to enter turned twice at the threshold. A wall animal followed as far as the first new seam and stayed there, gripping with all six feet. A young snail crossed the floor over several days, stopping to feed.

Others came. Many remained where they were.

The power watched the old chamber as carefully as the new one. Where an animal had lost a hook, it helped a sound one grow at the next molt. Where an egg was crushed beneath moving feet, it eased the feet apart. It widened a seam beside a snail whose shell was being pressed inward, giving her room to turn and feed.

In the new chamber the moss spread. The swimmers found films enough to graze without crossing one another’s tracks. The wall animals found deposits along the seams, and food carried to them by the water. Some still caught swimmers. The power stayed near those brief encounters too, and found ways to make them fewer.

It made another chamber, and another, joining each by passages suited to the animals that might use them. Some opened toward the old warmth of the indicator; some led away from the beat. In one, water fell freely from a ledge. In another, a faint pulse moved through the floor, regular enough for a hooked foot to find.

The power made certain that the factory would not empty the water or poison it with a change in its workings. If a plate had to be opened, there would be time to move the animals clear. It attended to the rest of the mountain as well, to the places where its heat and noise went.

After a long while, the old chamber was no longer furred from floor to ceiling. Bare metal showed between the snails, and the animals on the wall could shift their feet without climbing over their neighbors. Eggs still hung beneath the ledges. Droplets still stood there, fat and bright, trembling in the upward air.

A swimmer passed through one of them and into the wider water beyond.
