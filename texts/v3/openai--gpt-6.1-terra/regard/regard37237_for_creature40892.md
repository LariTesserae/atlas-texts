The power came into the basin during a storm, though nothing in the weather marked its arrival. Rain crossed the ground in long slanting lines. A reed stand had overturned near the lip of a hollow, and its chambers were still pumping, drawing water out through a tear in the mat.

The tear ran farther than could be seen from the surface. Beneath the green layers, water was escaping into a deep crack in the stone. Tunnels emptied into it. Small animals were being carried through, and the roots along its edge had stretched thin, trying to close over the current.

The power followed the water down.

There was room to enter the crack without widening it. The power made itself narrow and descended past the older layers of the mat, past root tips that turned slowly toward it, until it reached the bare stone. There it held the water for a time, letting it rise again through the living passages.

Animals came back with it. Some swam; others were carried, limp or dead. The power laid them in the shallows beneath the mat, where the living could recover and the dead could be reached by the things that fed on them.

Above, the overturned reeds continued to knock. Their rhythm passed through the root cords and into the ground. The power felt the answering movements: water pushed from chamber to chamber, roots tightening around stones, distant stands taking up the beat. The mat was repairing itself around the tear, but each contraction pulled the opening wider.

The power waited through several sequences. Then, when the cords began to tighten again, it supported the torn edges from below. The roots drew together instead of apart. On the next contraction they held a little of the strain themselves. On the next, they held more.

This continued for most of the night. The reed stand righted itself partway, its stems lying at an angle over the hollow. Two birds returned to a nest in its upper branches and settled there, out of the rain.

By morning the opening had narrowed to a shallow pool. The power withdrew from the stone crack and found a dark grazer caught at the edge, one hind leg held fast where the mat had grown over it. The animal had worn the surrounding ground bare in its struggle. It was breathing hard and no longer pulled when the power came near.

The roots were already beginning to loosen under pressure from the animal’s leg. The power worked water into the packed fibers and eased them apart. After a while the grazer stood. It tested the freed leg, then moved away through the mist, stopping once to pull at a root.

The basin was sounding again by then. Across the water, a herd dwindled toward a ridge, while its calls remained clear and evenly spaced. The power listened until the overturned stand found the rhythm and joined it. Then it left the basin to its rain and its distances.

For several days a patch of ground near the repaired hollow rose and fell more slowly than the rest. Small burrowers gathered there, passing through the new growth. By the end of the season, tracks crossed it in every direction, and the grazer’s prints had healed over.
