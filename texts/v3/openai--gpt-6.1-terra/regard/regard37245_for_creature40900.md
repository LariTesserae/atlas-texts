The power came first to the edge of the hollow and remained there through a long succession of cracks.

It did not still the water. The weed depended on its rush, and the bulbs on the warmth brought up through the stone. Beneath the fins, the jointed animals shifted together when the low note passed. The white creature kept the shapes of the blows. There was much to learn before anything was changed.

When a slab split, the power felt what passed into the ring. It was a brief thing, neither sound nor substance: the difference between stone being whole and stone being two. The white creature took it in, and for a time its clear stalks lifted into the current.

Then a newly shed animal was brought to the ring. It was held through the low note and put down among the grains. The adult waited. One of the young animal’s limbs remained visible for a while, opening and closing.

The power could not tell whether the creature in the ring was keeping it, or whether it wished to be kept. The adult carried the next young one away to a crevice. There, the soft animal held to its parent and began, slowly, to harden.

The power waited until another slab was about to split. At the instant of separation it caught a little of what the creature fed on, and carried it into a sheltered crack beneath the nearest fin. It laid it on the stone beside a newly shed animal.

The young one touched it with the pale underside of a limb. Its body tightened. After a time it touched the place again, then pressed itself against the stone and remained there while the low note passed.

The power made more such places. Each held a little of a breaking, enough for the young to feel and return to as they wished. Some recoiled from the touch. Some lay against it until their shells were firm. The adults found the places gradually, carrying their young into the cracks and standing over them through several surges. They still brought some to the ring.

The power could have closed the ring to them. It could have drawn the white creature out grain by grain, or placed a wall around it that no current could carry away. Instead, it waited beside the bed until the white surface had held the shapes of many cracks, and then offered it one shape more: the small pressure of a young animal choosing to withdraw.

The grains did not brighten. For a long while nothing happened that the power could distinguish from the ordinary trembling of the bed.

A newly shed animal was brought to the edge. It slipped from the adult’s limbs and entered the white surface. The adult waited as before. After several surges, a vacancy traveled across the grains toward the ring’s edge. The young animal followed it, pulling itself free with slow, uncertain movements. The adult lifted it and took it back beneath the fin.

Later, another entered and did not come out. The power waited near the ring until the limb visible among the grains had folded and unfolded many times. When it touched the limb, the grains around it closed gently over the place. Nothing else could be learned that day.

The adults continued to mend the ring. The power helped only once, when a current took three stones in quick succession and one struck an animal crossing the hollow. It eased the stone aside and held a fin steady while the animal dragged itself underneath. In time the others replaced the missing stones.

On a day when the ridge rose toward the copper light, the white creature made its shallow mound. The power followed the ridge as far as it could. The water grew lighter and warmer, and the fresh stone narrowed beneath it. Beyond that point there was a silence the power could not enter.

It returned to the hollow before the ridge turned downward. A few grains were already falling from the far stone. The power caught one, intending to bring it quickly to the ring, but the grain pressed against its touch. When released, it resumed its slow descent through the rushing water.

So the power left the others to fall in their own time.

The creature held its quieter shape. The jointed animals waited in their crevices, their young beside them, while stones went on striking the bottom. In one sheltered crack a soft animal touched the place where a breaking had been laid, then drew back and rested against its parent.

The power stayed until the first grain in the ring began to tremble again.
