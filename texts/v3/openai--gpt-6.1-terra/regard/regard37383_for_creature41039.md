The power came without entering. There was nowhere in the hollow that was not already occupied: by air, by sound, by the patient work of water, by lives too small to see.

It attended first to the seam. Beneath the fern roots the salamander lay with its belly against the stone. The rock carried the wind’s vibration through it; the salamander could not move beyond the sound without crossing the dry floor.

The power followed the roar back into the narrow passage. There it loosened one flake of stone, small enough to fit under a fingernail. It turned the flake and set it in a crevice where the wind divided.

The high note stopped.

The deep sound remained, but it no longer filled the chamber so fiercely. A drop fell from the ceiling and struck the rim of the wooden bowl. After a while another fell into the grit. The ferns did not move.

The salamander stayed where it was.

The power waited through a long dry spell. It watched the green recede from the outermost moss stems and the salamander withdraw, as it had before, toward the wettest part of the seam. Under the stone there was a fine thread of water, too little to break through. The power opened a way for it no wider than a root.

Days later, a dark patch appeared on the rock beneath the moss. It spread slowly to the stacked stones. Water gathered in their shallow hollows and stayed there after the ceiling had stopped dripping. The floor remained mostly dry. Mites moved among the wet stems; larvae fed there and were taken in their turn. Behind the bowl, the moth rested undisturbed.

One evening the salamander came down over the lowest stone. It paused with its front feet in a film of water, then lowered its head. Its throat worked. When it had finished, it climbed back into the moss.

Winter came. The salamander grew slow, and the moss closed over it. The power did not draw it out or warm the valley around it. Under the fern roots, there was water enough.

The flake remained in the passage. Wind came and went around it, making a lower, softer sound. On wet days the bowl caught a little water despite its split, and the moss along the seam shone green when the light reached it.
