The power attended first to the sound.

It could have quieted the plateau. Even before it understood the creatures, it could feel how the chord pressed against their bodies, how the sharper notes drove their plates together. But beneath the second roof, the larger inhabitant’s elastic bands tightened and released with the deeper vibration. In the thickets, the noise lifted seeds from their stalks. It held the young terrace in a slow shiver as it rose.

The power listened longer.

At the shelter beside the sorted heap, the reaching limb closed around the smallest plate. It drew the plate inward, a little at a time, and set it against the gap near the creature’s neck. The fit was poor. One corner stood away from the body. The creature held it there through several changes in the mountain’s note, then returned it to the heap.

The power let the plate remain where it had been put.

Beyond the basins, one of the uncertain peaks came briefly into agreement with itself. A deep note traveled over the terraces. At the second shelter it entered through the narrow space at the back, as it had done many times before. This time the rising terrace had shifted the footing of a slab. The note loosened it.

The larger inhabitant felt the change. Its fringes turned, and its hammering limb began to lift. Beneath its back plates a young one was folded, its small fringe trembling against the adult’s pale underside.

The slab moved faster than either of them.

The power held it.

For a long while the slab stood at an angle it could not have kept by itself. The larger creature raised its hammer, pressed the loose fiber beneath the lower edge, and worked a small stone into place. The young one remained tucked under the plates. When the work was done, the power eased its hold until the slab rested on the creature’s wedge.

The roof settled. The adult’s fringes stayed turned toward the altered pressure.

The power pressed, gently, on the ground outside the doorway.

The pressure reached the shelter as a single note, unlike any note the basin had known. It was held, then allowed to fade.

The creature did not answer at once. Its hammer lay across its body again. The young one shifted beneath the plates, and a long time passed in the sound of the needles and the glass.

Then the larger creature extended a limb and pressed a plate edge to the floor.

The power felt the answer travel through stone.

It did not know what the answer meant. It held the note in memory and waited. In time the creature pressed again, differently; the first pressure had not been a request to hold up the roof. The power answered as closely as it could, and the creature’s fringes opened a little along their length.

They continued in this way, with intervals long enough for a frond to unfold and darken. The power learned which pressures made the plates draw tight, which brought the young one out from beneath the adult’s back, and which were simply the creature attending. It learned, too, to leave room for an answer.

When a needle near the basin began to sharpen its note, the power did not break it or still its growth. It altered a seam in the stone beneath the shelter, making a narrow passage for the deeper vibration and a difficult one for the sharp. The creature tested the new pressure through its limbs. After a long time it moved one of the wedges and adjusted the opening to its liking.

At the neighboring shelter, the other inhabitant took up the small plate again. It turned the plate over, tested the opposite edge, and this time fitted it into the gap at its neck. The corner still stood away. Much later, it used the edge of a stone to wear the corner down.

The power watched without taking the stone from its limb.

It made no promise that the plateau would stop changing. The terrace continued to rise; the mountains continued to show four summits or seven. But where a journey stretched unexpectedly, a traveler might feel a steady pressure beneath the glass, leading not by the shortest way but toward a basin it could recognize. Where a roof slab threatened to fall, there might be time enough to brace it.

And sometimes, through the immense chord, an unfamiliar note came to a shelter and waited there—quiet enough not to hurt, steady enough to be answered.
