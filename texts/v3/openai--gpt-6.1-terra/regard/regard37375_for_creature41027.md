The power came at night, when a pulse was moving toward the western escarpment.

It felt the vibration through the root beds and followed it, attending to the things disturbed along the way: the animals closing their breathing openings inside the columns, the seeds gathering behind the slits, the travelers who had moved their hearthstones away from a hollow they knew might open. It attended, too, to those who had no warning.

In a fold of the land, a woman lay beside a cold hearth. She had crossed an old root shelf that afternoon, and it had broken under her. She had climbed out, but her ankle had swollen in her boot. Her water was nearly gone. The companion who had been traveling with her had set off at dusk for a camp beyond the ridge.

She had told him to go. She knew the route, and she knew the camp. Still, the ridge was a long way off, and she had heard no answering calls.

The power could have brought her companion back at once, or carried her to the camp. It waited, because she was awake and might have something to say about it.

She heard a voice near the hearth.

“Your companion is still walking. He has reached the far side of the ridge.”

She pushed herself up on one elbow and looked around. There was enough starlight to see the empty fold, the dark column above it, and her bowl where she had set it against a stone.

“Who is that?”

“Someone who can help.”

She listened. The clicking of a herd passed beneath the hearth, thin and fast, then changed pitch as it reached the root shelf.

“Where are you?”

“Here,” said the voice, though there was no place she could point to.

She took up her digging stick. She had a stone blade as well, but it was across the hearth and would have meant reaching with her injured foot.

The voice said, “I can make your ankle whole. May I?”

She held the stick across her lap. “Will it hurt?”

“No.”

She considered this for some time. The column overhead gave a low roar as the wind entered its branches.

“Can you bring my companion here?”

“Yes.”

“Then do that.”

The power found the man walking in the dark. He was tired and had begun to favor one leg where the broken shelf had scraped it. He heard the same voice and stopped. When it offered to bring him back, he agreed, though he asked first whether the woman was alive.

A moment later he was standing beside the hearth.

They held each other. Then they sat close together and spoke in low voices. The man looked often into the dark beyond the fire ring. At last the woman asked whether the voice was still there.

It was.

She put down her digging stick. “You may mend it.”

The swelling left her ankle. The torn tissues joined; the small fracture closed. She moved her foot cautiously, then stood, leaning on her companion until she was sure.

The man asked if his leg could be mended too. The power mended it. He pulled up his trouser leg and touched the unbroken skin, looking both pleased and uneasy.

Their bowl held a little water, and the man had nearly emptied his own. The power offered to fill both. They agreed. Clear water rose in the bowls without being taken from the column or from any sleeping animal.

The woman drank, then held out her bowl to her companion, who drank too. He said that if they left now, they could reach the camp before the sun was high.

They packed by starlight. The woman checked the ground where she had been lying, making sure she had left nothing but the hearthstones. She picked up the digging stick and tested her weight once more. The ankle held.

As they started toward the ridge, she stopped.

“What should we leave you?”

“Nothing,” said the power.

They walked on. At the top of the rise they looked back, but the fold was dark and ordinary, and the voice did not call them.

When the sun rose, the power was still on the plateau. A climber with a young one against her belly had caught a foot in a split in a column’s bark. Others had moved up toward the dripping grooves, leaving her hanging below them. The power eased the split open enough for her to pull free. She climbed quickly after her family, pausing once to lick her foot.

Along the escarpment, seeds released in the night had settled into cracks among the old roots. Some would grow; most would not. The power remained for a while, watching the first animals come to drink.
