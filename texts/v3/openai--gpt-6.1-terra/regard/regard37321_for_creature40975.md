The power came to the chamber during a reversal, when the grit was rising and the three gardens bent together.

It attended first to the swimmer. Beneath the grate, the animal was making its way into the machinery, touching the ribs ahead of it as it went. One of the passages it used had narrowed. A ceramic facing had come loose and settled against a metal brace, leaving a gap through which the swimmer could still pass, but only by folding its limbs close to its body. The pale wear on its arms came partly from this place.

The power lifted the facing and set it securely into the wall. The gap opened. A small drift of particles came away from the brace, and the swimmer stopped until they had passed. Then it went on.

Farther down, the power followed the water through the pumps. Much had changed since the machinery was made. Openings had filled with growth; seals had worn away; chambers meant for air had held water for ages. Life had spread through these places. The reversals carried it from one level to another, along with food and silt and the fragments from which the gardens sometimes took root.

One pump was close to failing. Its shaft had worn unevenly, and with every turn a bearing shifted a little farther out of its housing. When it came free, the intake would draw hard through the passages beneath the chamber. The swimmer’s usual refuge would not be far enough.

The power held the shaft still and repaired the bearing. It strengthened the housing around it, leaving the opening and the flow much as they had been. Then it went up the narrow shaft above the chamber, where the loose plate knocked, and fastened the plate in place.

The change in the noise was small. The pumps still hammered through the walls. But a slower sound could now be heard within their pounding: the movement of water through the ribs, and the faint tapping of grit against the ceramic.

By the time the power returned, the intake had ended. The fish were feeding in open water. Beneath the stairs, grazers moved over the tight sheaf of weed, while a line of newly hatched ones clung to the underside of a leaf.

The swimmer came back through the widened passage. It paused beneath the grate, then reached an arm between the ribs. A fish turned sharply away. The arm remained there, opening and closing in the current, until a piece of torn weed settled against it. The swimmer drew the weed down.

The power watched another reversal and another afternoon. It found places in the machinery where water could still travel but animals had difficulty following. In a few of them it cleared a way, wide enough for small bodies to pass and sheltered from the strongest flow. It left the gardens where they grew.

When it had finished, light continued to come through the old lenses. Grit settled a little farther along the floor. The grazers fed, and the fish waited at the edges of the leaves, and the swimmer moved through the passages below.
