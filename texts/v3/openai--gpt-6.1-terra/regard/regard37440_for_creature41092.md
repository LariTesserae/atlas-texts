The power came to the hollow at night, when the marsh was covered in a thin new frost. It did not come in a shape that the frogs could see, or with a light that would draw the flies. It came as attention, and for a long while it did nothing else.

It followed the water down through the narrow crack and learned where the air entered it, and where the pressure gathered before each release. It felt the many-branched foot against the stone, the minute movements of the grazers, the slow closing of the tracks they left. It attended to the beetles under the rushes, and to the vole, and to the roots the vole had been eating.

The creature had no name for the hollow, nor any name for itself. The power could tell when its tissues were damaged, when food reached them, when the water carried the right substances for a new thread to grow. Whether any of this was felt as pleasure or pain, it could not discover by looking for feelings like its own.

So it stayed to learn more.

Toward morning the frost gave way. Water ran off the higher ground, bringing dead grass and fragments of reed into the basin. A short length of stem was drawn against the mouth of the crack. It turned there for a time, tapping the rock with each surge, until a mass of fine roots came down and caught behind it.

The thumping grew uneven. Once, after a long pause, the water gave a single heavy blow; then the basin began to fill.

The creature extended its fringe along the drowned edge. The new colonies opened too, and for a while the crowded water brought them more than they could gather. But the level went on rising. The lowest rushes bent beneath it, and the water spread into the grass without finding another way down through the stones.

The power waited until it could see what was holding the stem. Then it eased one end free of the rock. The next surge turned it sideways and carried it clear, with the roots streaming behind. Water rushed after it. The basin dropped, the thumping returned, and the stem came to rest among the rushes, where a beetle climbed onto it.

The creature withdrew from the falling water. One of the new colonies had lost several threads; another had grown a little. Silt lay over part of the old foot, and the clear larvae began to graze through it.

The power watched until the basin had returned to its usual depth. Beyond the rushes, morning light reached the frost still clinging to the higher ground. A frog dropped from the grass into the water, changing the note of the thump, and settled on the lowest stone.

The power remained near the marsh for a long time. It did not keep the crack clear thereafter. Stems passed through it, and silt gathered and was washed away. The stones shifted by small degrees. When the thumping stopped, the fringes lifted; when it began, they bent with the water, and the small lives among them went on moving.
