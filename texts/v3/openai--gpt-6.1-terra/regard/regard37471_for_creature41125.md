The power attends first to the water.

It follows the current through the three mouths and out into the buried city: through turbines and cooling jackets, past valves sealed by age, into a reservoir where the surface trembles beneath a grating. It finds people still working above, and other small lives below. It does not stop the pumps.

Behind the lamp cage, the shrimp grips the brick. The third pipe begins its familiar warning through the stone.

The power waits until the shrimp is deep in its crack. Then, while the flow is still running outward, it changes the shape of the cage.

The wires bend slowly, without scraping the wall. Mineral fur remains on them; the films in the joints remain wet. A little arch forms over the crack, open at both ends. Beneath it are hollows of different sizes, some no wider than a thread, with rough floors where legs can hold.

The pipe reverses.

Water strikes the new arch and divides around it. A little passes through, carrying particles from the pipe crusts. The shrimp feels the change and braces, but the current does not reach far enough inside to dislodge it.

The power listens through the walls. It eases a loose bearing in one pump and settles a gate that has been striking its frame. Elsewhere, it strengthens an old seal before the quieter running can send a new vibration into the masonry. The heavy beats continue, but less of their force comes through the chamber walls. The pipes still sing.

For a time the shrimp stays where it is. Its longer antenna moves along the edge of the arch. Later it comes out as far as one of the openings and picks at a flake caught there. When the current changes again, the flake rolls away. The shrimp follows it a short distance, then returns.

The warm pipe continues to feed its colonies. Some flakes still break loose; some settle in the sheltered hollows, where other minute lives take hold. Water moves through the chamber, and the city draws what it needs.

The power remains long enough to see several reversals. It watches the new eddies and the slow settling of grit, and makes one more opening near the bottom of the arch, where too much silt has begun to gather.

During the next quiet interval, the shrimp emerges. It crosses the brick beneath the lamp and pauses in the open, both antennae extended into the water.
