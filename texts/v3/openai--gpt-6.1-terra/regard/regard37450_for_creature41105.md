The power came quietly enough that the charge in the hollow walls did not change.

It could have lit the lowland from end to end. Instead it learned the place through what was already moving: the passage of salts through a root, the fine transfer of sap between two pressed surfaces, the current descending into the mud. It followed these exchanges farther than any one of the growths could reach, and then returned to the small differences on which they depended.

A pale-edged bud stood on a flat among older plants. Its connection to the common root was narrow, and the tissue inside that connection had begun to break down. Sap still passed through, but less each day. The bud had sent two new root tips into the mud. Both had met a layer poor in the minerals they needed, and both were turning slowly along it.

The older growth beside it was making an adjustment. A channel in its broad root had widened toward the bud; more water and concentrated sap were beginning to move that way. The adjustment had come late. At its present rate it would keep the bud alive for a time, but would not supply enough to open its folded leaves.

The power looked also at what depended on the older growth, and at the ground beneath them. A mineral seam lay below the poor layer, thin but sufficient for several young roots. It was separated from them by a dense band of mud.

The power loosened a small part of that band.

Water entered the opening first. Then a little of the mineral-rich mud rose into it, making a gradient the root tips could follow. The opening was no wider than it needed to be, and did not drain the seam or disturb the channels already reaching it.

One root tip turned downward. The other continued along the poor layer and eventually stopped growing. The widened channel from the older plant remained in use until the new root made contact below. After that it narrowed again.

The bud opened slowly. Its first leaf had a slight unevenness along one pale seam, where development had been interrupted. The leaf worked well enough. Water gathered in its base, and a neighboring root drew some of it away.

Elsewhere, the power found older leaves being taken apart from within, their useful substances returned to the network. It left them to the work. It found young growths with no path to sufficient minerals, and places where new channels had already begun to form. It learned, each time, as much as it could of the surrounding roots before touching the mud.

For a long while it stayed among the low ridges and flats. Where a small opening would let the living system do what it was already attempting, it sometimes made one. Where an opening would only move a shortage from one patch to another, it did not. The lowland continued to renew and consume itself, and its surfaces remained clean-edged in the dark.

At last the power withdrew its attention from the bud. Its root held in the mineral seam. A second leaf was beginning to separate from the first, carrying a fine, even layer of moisture on its black surface.
