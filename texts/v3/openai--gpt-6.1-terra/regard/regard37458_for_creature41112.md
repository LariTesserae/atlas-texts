The power came in with the wind, though the wind did not know it.

It found the room by its sounds: the bats murmuring in the crevice, the fine rasp inside the stems, a beetle’s feet slipping in the powder. It listened long enough to hear the silences too, each lizard’s pause before a dash.

There was a stone above the back wall that had begun to move. Not quickly. Each hot day expanded it; each cold night drew it in. A root had entered beneath it, and grit had worked into the space the root made. In a few years, or perhaps many, the stone would drop. It would bring part of the ceiling with it and close the low passage behind the room.

The power put a grain of stone where it would bear the weight. It chose the grain carefully, from a place where its absence would not loosen another stone. Then it waited through a day and a night, feeling the slab expand and contract. The grain held.

The bats woke towards evening. One by one they crept to the edge of the crevice, released their feet and flew through the opening. The smallest of them had a torn wing. It had caught the membrane on something outside, and the tear had widened when the bat pulled free. It could fly, but not well; twice it struck the wall before it found the entrance.

The power followed it out over the plateau. In the dim light, the bat flew close to the ground, making short turns after insects. When it landed on a rock to rest, the power held the edges of the tear together until they joined. The bat groomed the wing with its tongue, stretched it once and flew off.

The power returned to the room. A spider was repairing a web across an alcove, drawing a fresh line past a moth caught in the old silk. The moth’s wings moved less and less. The spider approached, paused, and approached again. The power watched without touching either of them.

Later, a lizard came out from beneath a clay fragment and lay in the last of the light. An ant crossed its forefoot. The lizard lifted the foot, set it down again and remained where it was until the bright band had gone from the floor.

By morning the bats had returned. The small one squeezed into the crevice between two others, causing a brief, sharp quarrel. Then they settled, and the room grew quieter.

The power listened once more to the stone above the wall. The new grain was doing its work. Around it, the roots continued to grow, and the wind continued to carry grit through the opening.

The power went on its way.
