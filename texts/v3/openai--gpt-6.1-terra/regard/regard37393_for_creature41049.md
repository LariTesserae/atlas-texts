The power came down the stair without drying it. At the bottom it stood for a long time beside the pump house, listening through the noise.

The pump was sound for the moment. One bearing was wearing unevenly; a coupling on the return pipe had a hairline crack. The drains were another matter. Water still passed through them, but not by much, and the next loose mat of roots might lodge where it could not easily be reached.

The power followed the channels to the sump. It did not pull the growth from the grates. It found an old overflow instead, sealed behind a panel of brick and forgotten when the pump was installed. Beyond the panel, the pipe ran clear for most of its length, then narrowed around a deposit of lime.

It opened the pipe. The work took an afternoon, and when it was finished only a small additional trickle entered the sump. If the water rose, the opening would take more.

At the far wall, the power parted the ferns and examined the concrete behind them. Water had been running down a crack there for years. It might one day widen enough to admit soil from above. The power strengthened the wall from the other side, leaving the wet threads undisturbed.

It looked into the houses. In one, a lamp had failed, and leaves had browned beneath it. The power replaced the lamp, then left another bulb and a pair of dry gloves on the nearest table. It made no beds, laid no fires, and opened no doors that had been closed from within.

By the pump it left a tool for the worn coupling and a drawing, clear enough to follow in the weak light. It could have repaired the coupling itself. Instead, it made certain the crack would hold for several more weeks, and set a small light above it to change color if it widened sooner.

It did not know who tended the machinery, or whether that one wished to be seen. The rubbed edges of the doors and the cleared places around the brackets were evidence enough that someone came this way. The power waited at the stair until the lamps changed to their dimmer setting, but nobody appeared.

Before leaving, it cleared one grate by hand. It laid the lifted roots and sediment in a shallow tray beside the wall, where water reached them and some of the green growth might continue. The drain ran faster for a little while, carrying a brown cloud into the channel.

Then the power went back up the steps.

Below, the pump continued its roar. Water fell from the pipes and leaves. At the far wall, the youngest ferns uncurled into the space the older ones had left them.
