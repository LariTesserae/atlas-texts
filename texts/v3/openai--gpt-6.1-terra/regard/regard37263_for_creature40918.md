The power came to the chamber at a time when the water had vanished.

It did not arrive through an entrance. For a while it attended to the chamber without changing it: to the seams closing, to the eggs hatching on dry stone, to a walker standing where its damp line ended.

The walker waited. Its forelegs touched the end of the line, then the dry floor beyond it. It turned a fraction and touched again.

Farther away, beneath the moss, a large larva made its slow way toward the depression. The floor there was already dry. The larva pressed its mouth down nevertheless.

The power felt the movement of water through the stone. It found that the water could remain a little longer without flooding the eggs, or softening the walls, or carrying the smallest animals away. It found, too, that a shallow hollow could be made beneath the moss, where a little water might stay after the rest had gone.

It made the hollow first. A few of the larvae rolled into it as the floor shifted. They lay still, then began to move again.

When the next film gathered, some of it reached the hollow. The large larva drank there. A smaller one pressed beside it; another climbed partly over its back. Above them, a drop rose from the moss into the stone.

At the edge of the larger film, the hunter folded around a larva. The power did not pull it away. Later, when the hunter moved off, the small dry shape remained. A walker found it and carried it to a seam.

The walker whose line had ended was still waiting. The water had appeared and vanished while it stood there. The power laid another length of dampness on the floor, beginning at the end of the first.

The walker touched it and followed. The line led around a rising ridge and stopped at the hollow. The walker found the water with its forelegs and lowered itself to drink.

After that, other lines appeared where lines had run out. Some led toward water; some led away from a seam that was about to close. Once, when a walker carrying young came to the end of its line, the power began another in two directions. The walker felt along both, then followed the one that passed nearest the wall.

The power left the shelter alone. It watched through the gap as a round animal passed in the distance, and watched the walkers feeling along the outside of the chips. When a grain of stone fell from the ceiling and struck the floor, the animals paused, their shadows still moving.

The hollow beneath the moss was small. Sometimes it dried before all the larvae reached it. When that happened, the power drew a little more moisture through the stone, taking care not to fill it too deeply.

It stayed long enough to learn where the water went. Then it made a fine channel within the floor, too narrow for any of the animals to enter, through which some of the gathered moisture would find its way back to the hollow.

The next time the water vanished, a little remained beneath the moss. A late larva arrived and drank. The winged animals settled above it, facing upward.

The power withdrew. The stone continued forming around the channel, closing on either side of it without closing it.
