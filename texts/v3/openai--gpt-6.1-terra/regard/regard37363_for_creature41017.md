The power arrived without opening the roof.

It had been prepared to quiet the booming. Standing in the hollow, it heard how one sound traveled the length of the street, returned from the far wall, and met the next. It waited to learn whether the noise was an injury.

The roof rose and fell. Sap moved through the fibers. A thread parted in a room whose doorway had nearly closed.

The power waited longer. It could wait as the city waited: not by making an instant last forever, but by allowing years to pass and remaining attentive through them.

In that time it learned that the booming did no harm. It learned, too, that the plant could distinguish pressure at the seam from pressure farther along the roof, and that when a change persisted, it sometimes grew in response.

The power made a light touch beside the entrance and withdrew it. After a while, it touched the same place again.

The plant did not answer at once. Over many seasons, fibers gathered beneath that place. The power varied its touch and watched what happened. It made mistakes. Once, holding the roof too long, it caused a crease that took the plant years to mend. After that it was more careful.

Eventually it could ask a question, though not in words. It could indicate a notch at the end of the street, then hold open the possibility of keeping that notch from closing. It could also leave the notch to narrow, and let the plant’s growth show which way it tended.

The plant thickened the notch’s sides.

The power let it be.

At the entrance, though, the seam had grown so fine that it was difficult even for air to pass through. The power eased the adjoining fibers apart, just enough to make a small passage, and waited. The plant grew around the opening without closing it. Later, the power widened it a little more. Again the plant kept it.

This became their work. When the plant’s own movement made a place difficult for it to mend, the power helped; when the plant grew across an old cut, the power left the threads undisturbed. It learned to recognize some responses and remained uncertain of others.

The handprints slowly softened. The oldest notches closed.

Before each was gone, the power attended to it: the depth of the cut, the shape of its edges, the way the plant had folded its fibers around it. It kept these things in its own memory. It did not carve them again.

A long time later, someone found the seam in the plain. They crouched through it and stood in the dark, listening to the sound of a roof moving overhead. When they put a hand against the wall, they felt the faint pulse beneath it.

The power was still there, though the visitor did not know it. It watched the visitor leave, and watched the plant begin, very slowly, to grow around the warmth their hand had left.
