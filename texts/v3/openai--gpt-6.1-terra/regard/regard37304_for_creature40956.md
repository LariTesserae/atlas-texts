The power did not first appear above the plateau. There was little to be learned from there that the light had not already shown.

It attended instead to a place beneath a fallen frond, where a film of amber organisms was softening the tissue and a young grazer was feeding. The grazer worked its snout slowly into the powder, withdrew it, and left a small fan of clean grains. Farther along the passage, an older animal rested with its plates lifted a fraction for breath.

The power followed the grains out into the light, across the flats and up the low side of a ridge. It felt the weight of the powder moving there, the way each grain pressed upon the next, and the hollows beneath where others had passed. It attended to the larger animal waiting at the colony’s edge, and to the empty passages in its belly, and to the tiny travellers sheltered in the folds of its skin.

It remained through the long lifting of a buried colony and through the laying down of another veil. It learned how much the plants could bear, and how a grazer turned in a narrowing passage, and how long the larger animal could lie beneath the dust before it needed to rise.

Then, in a high basin beyond the nearest ridge, a current of powder changed its course.

The change was small at first. A bank gave way, and grains spilled into a channel whose floor had been raised by an older deposit. The current climbed out of the channel and spread. By the time it reached the colony, it was deep and dense, and it continued to flow after the first veil had settled.

The outer fronds bowed. Powder gathered in their joints and poured through the spaces between them. In one passage, a fallen rib shifted across the packed floor. Three young grazers were on the far side of it. The older animal had begun to dig from within, pushing powder backward with its feet, but more came through the roof each time it cleared a little space.

The power held the rib up.

It did not lift it high. It kept one end just clear of the floor, and the older grazer squeezed beneath. The young followed, brushing pale tracks through the powder. Only when they had reached a deeper chamber did the rib settle again.

Outside, the flow had filled the low places around the colony. A larger animal lay half buried against the slope of a ridge. It had chosen a good place, but the current had come from a direction it could not have anticipated, and its four feet were caught beneath a heavy bank.

The power loosened the bank grain by grain, beginning near the feet. When the animal pushed, the powder gave way. It rested, then pushed again, and at last stood with its narrow body above the drift. Grains ran down its sides for a long time. The small creatures in its skin creases remained where they were.

The current continued to come down from the high basin. The power went there and found the raised channel. Across its lower end lay a broad shelf of packed mineral. With time, the current would wear through it and return to its old course. The power opened a shallow gap in the shelf.

Powder began to move through. The gap widened slowly, without a rush, and the weight spreading toward the colony diminished. By the time the sky cleared, the flow had settled back between the old banks.

The grazers dug outward. The plants lifted over several days, their fronds parting and shedding streams of grit. Where the current had reached deepest, some of the outer growth stayed flattened, but farther in the chambers opened and the young grazers came out to feed.

The larger animal waited near a passage after the colony rose. It had gone a long time without food. Eventually a grazer emerged, stopped in the bright air, and was taken before it could turn. The larger animal fed. Afterward it crossed the flat, carrying some of the colony’s minute lives in the dust on its flanks.

The power stayed.

It watched the high channels and the colonies growing at their edges. When a frond fell across a nursery hollow, it sometimes eased the weight until a passage could be dug. When a travelling animal lay down with a burden of tiny lives in its skin, the power took note of where it rose again. It learned the old, slow courses of the powder, and the newer courses forming among them.

At times it made a space for something to live: a narrow shelter between two stones, a small break in a bank, a fold in a frond held open through a difficult settling. The spaces did not remain exactly as they had been made. Grains filled them, roots entered them, feet wore them wider. The power attended to what became of them and learned from that, too.

Long afterward, a grazer came out from a passage into moving dust. It paused, braced on six pale legs. For a while the grains gathered along its plates.

Then it went on, its small shadow travelling beside it over the bright ground.
