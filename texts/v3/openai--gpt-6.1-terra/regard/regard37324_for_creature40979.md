The power came to the basin in a dry interval, when the loudest sound was a jaw working at the wall.

It could have made the pavement level. It could have sealed every crack, righted every bowl and held it steady. It waited beside a bowl instead and watched a small creature enter through the crack in its bottom. Beneath the pavement, water occupied the hollows of broken pots. Eggs clung to their undersides. The cracks that opened after each rain were also the ways in and out.

On the roof, a patch of clay had loosened around a bundle of reeds. The next hard shower would wash some of it away. If enough went, a heavier stream would fall in one place, and the tile below might shift against the lip of an egg chamber.

The power pressed the patch back into the weave. It added a little clay around the edges, leaving the reeds beside it open to the air. Then it waited for rain.

When rain came, drops gathered as before. They hung from the reeds, traveled along them, and fell. The smallest creatures appeared at the wall; the larger ones came up between the tiles. They passed one another, pale feet lifting and setting down, each with a fragment tucked beneath its jaws.

One bowl tipped. Water spilled across the pavement, carrying a heap of creatures with it. They found their footing and scattered. A small one paused at a seam and packed a white crumb of pottery into the dark opening.

The shower passed without the roof breaking. Under the tiles, the water clapped slowly as it drained.

The power stayed until the floor was nearly dry. Then, before going, it touched the outside of the wall, where a line of thumb marks had become shallow with age. A little moisture gathered in them and ran down to the moss.

There was still work for the creatures when the next rain came.
