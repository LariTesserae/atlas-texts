After rain, a young pod-breaker comes down from the ledge with the others. It has a limp, acquired two days earlier when a stone rolled against its hind foot. The foot is swollen. It can still grasp a branch, but it cannot put its full weight on the ground, and the band is moving ahead.

The benevolent power notices the foot. It notices, too, the hollow beneath the ledge, where water has been entering a long crack. Roots hold the surface together, but the stone below them has begun to separate.

The pod-breaker finds a pebble and carries it to a fallen pod. It strikes twice. On the third attempt the pebble slips from its mouth and rolls downslope. It follows, keeping its injured foot raised, and reaches into the crack after the stone.

The ledge gives a small movement. Through their tails and feet, the resting animals feel it. They begin to stir.

The power holds the ledge until they have gone.

It keeps the crack open around the young one’s hand while the animal pulls free, then steadies the ground beneath it. The pod-breaker scrambles after the others, dropping the pebble. Once it is clear, the ridge settles with a long tearing sound. Leaves and loose stone slide into the basin. The herd feeding below has time to move away.

The power attends to the injured foot. It draws out the trapped blood and sets the small bones where they belong. It eases the pain enough for the animal to travel, though for a little while the foot will still be tender. The pod-breaker pauses on a root, touches the foot to the ground, and then moves on more quickly.

On the ridge above, the band has gathered around a split pod. The young one approaches at the edge of the crowd and finds a piece that has fallen into the leaves. It sits with the fragment between its hands and eats.

The power remains while the animals feed. It follows the water entering the fractured ridge and gives it another course, down through the stone to a basin where the ground is firm. There it leaves a shallow pool, shaded from the brief gray light and fed by the fine threads of condensation that gather toward morning. Ridgebacks will find it, and the smaller animals will come in their tracks.

When the power withdraws, the pod-breaker is still holding its piece of shell. Another approaches and reaches for it. The young one pulls it close, shifts onto its mending foot, and climbs into the ferns.
