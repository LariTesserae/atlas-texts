One of the bright points had begun to fail.

At first it was difficult to tell. The points shifted continually, and this one still answered the keepers’ plates. But it drifted back from the storms sooner than the others, and afterward the air in the eastern chambers was slow to warm.

The keepers marked its place on a strip of polished ceramic. Each dusk they added a scratch showing where it had settled. Over the years the scratches gathered farther apart.

When they compared the strip with older records, they found that a point had failed once before. Its light had dwindled over three generations. The town had closed several chambers and grown others nearer the center. No record said what had become of the point.

The keepers began to measure the air in the eastern chambers more often. They asked the families there to keep their breathing bladders close and to let them know if the walls grew cold.

A child found the failing point one evening at the edge of town. It had settled near the ground behind a white cup, where a drift of charged dust lay under the rim.

The child put a limb down beside it. The point rose a little, then sank again.

She fetched a keeper, who came with a signaling plate. He angled it toward the point. The point brightened, but did not rise.

Through the plant, beneath the keeper’s feet, came a short vibration.

He waited. The sound came again: three pulses, a pause, and three more.

He touched his forelimb to the ground and sent a greeting. The answer took a long time. By then two other keepers had arrived, and they all felt it: a sustained note, low enough that the dust shifted around their feet.

They knew many things that could send vibrations through the plant. This was not like any of them. One keeper stayed beside the point while the others went to call the town’s listeners.

By the time the listeners arrived, the failing point was aloft again. It had risen as far as the rim of the cup and hung there, unsteady. The ground remained quiet.

The listeners waited through the night. Near morning, one of them pressed a forelimb down and repeated the three pulses.

The answer came at once.

It took the better part of a season to establish how to speak. The presence beyond the town could send vibrations into the plant, but at first its signals traveled too strongly or too faintly. Sometimes they arrived from the wrong direction. Sometimes a whole ridge trembled when a single street groove would have carried the sound.

The listeners taught it the limits of their hearing. They used the patterns with which travelers gave their names and destinations across the flats, then added signs for the things they needed to ask. The presence learned slowly enough that the town could interrupt and correct it.

It told them that it had noticed the town because of the bright points. It had come closer and found the plant, the grazers, the chambers, and the people who listened through their limbs. It had found the failing point too.

Could it help?

The keepers had questions. How did the points work? Why had one failed before? Could the others fail? The presence could answer some things, though its first explanations were difficult to follow. The points had been made to draw energy from the charged dust and to hold the town’s air. They had lasted a long time. The failing point had sustained damage that was spreading within it.

The presence said it could mend the point. It could also change the way the points drew energy, so that the damage was less likely to happen again.

The keepers asked what else would change.

The presence did not answer immediately. When it did, it asked for time to examine the storms.

For many days it sent questions into the ground. The keepers brought out their ceramic strips, their pressure records, and the old accounts of winters when storms had passed too close. Collectors showed where the charged dust gathered and which deposits were left for the plant. People who had traveled described the air beyond town and the caches along the routes.

At last the presence said it could mend the point without taking much dust from the plateau. Changing all the points would be harder. They would draw less energy for a time, and the town would need to close its outer chambers during the work.

The households in those chambers were consulted. Some could move in with neighbors. One family had a wall that was still growing around a new room; they feared the cold would stop its growth. The keepers and builders studied it together and found a way to shelter the young fibers under layers of stored dust and ceramic.

Then the town agreed.

The presence asked the keepers to signal the failing point down. It came to rest in a public hollow. People gathered around at a distance while the keepers watched their instruments.

The light contracted until it was no larger than a grain of pale dust. The keeper nearest it felt a pressure through the ground, so faint that he nearly missed it. The grain remained still for a long while. Then it lifted, widened, and returned to its former brightness.

The point rose above the hollow. It held its place beside the others without drifting.

The work on the remaining points took longer. While it was underway, several households crowded into the central chambers. Children slept among vessels and folded bladders. The oldest residents remembered such closeness from a season of severe storms, and told stories that kept the children awake longer than their elders intended.

At dusk the keepers met with the presence on the ridge. It showed them how to test a point for the spreading damage and how to direct it to a sheltered place. Some of the instruments required careful growing and shaping; the town’s makers began them at once, though the finest would take years to finish.

The family with the young room visited it each day. Its wall grew more slowly in the cold, but it continued to grow. When the points resumed their usual formation, the family carried their possessions home and uncovered the pale new arch.

The presence stayed through another storm season. It watched the points meet the fronts and asked the collectors what they found afterward. It listened to the pressure in the chambers and to the reports of travelers returning from the flats.

Then it told the town it would go farther across the plateau. It had detected other points, very distant, and wanted to learn whether they were tended.

Before it left, the child who had found the failing light went with the keepers to the ridge. She pressed her limb to the ground and asked whether it would hear them from that far away.

The answer came through the soles of everyone standing there: it did not know, but it would listen.

The next storm arrived after the presence had gone. The points spread out to meet it, the walls began to sing, and the keepers listened for any rise in the note. When the dust passed, the town came out with vessels and polished plates. The repaired point hung above them, steady in the dim light, while a fresh drift crackled in the lee of the white cup.
