The power came to the plateau without casting a light. The ring-makers had no use for light, and there was nothing else there that needed to see.

It stayed through the lifting and sinking of a mound, through the opening of three fissures and the slow cooling of their edges. Beneath a slab it found a cluster of young, and farther down, in warmth too deep for any ring-maker to reach, it found the forces that would in time break the slab apart.

It could have stilled those forces. The rings would have stood where they were built, and the young would have hardened in their shelter. But the warmth came from the same depths as the movement, and new stone came with it. The power watched a ring-maker travel during a tremor, slipping into a seam that closed behind it, and left the depths alone.

There was an adult at the ring above the slab. It had placed two broad stones on the side toward the nearest fissure. In the ring’s lee, crusts grew on a sloping face, sheltered by a pebble that the adult had turned on its side. The power watched it feed there, taking little pale flakes into its mouth, then carry another flake away and press it into a place where the warmth was steady.

When the heave came, it was stronger than the others.

A long break opened beneath the ring. One broad stone tipped inward; the other went down into the gap. The slab shifted, and the crack under it narrowed at one end while opening at the other. The young were carried apart.

The adult emerged when the movement stopped. It felt along the broken edge and found three of them. One at a time it carried them beneath its body to a crack beyond the break. Then it returned. It searched the edge, withdrew, and came back to search again.

A fourth lay under a fallen plate of stone. The adult passed near it twice. The plate was warm across its whole surface, and the small warmth beneath made no difference that the adult could feel.

The power lifted one corner of the plate, just enough to let air pass underneath. It did not lift the plate away. The adult was close, and a sudden movement might send it back beneath the ground.

After a long time, the adult crossed the plate again. It stopped, turned, and pressed its underside to the narrow opening. Then it worked its way beneath the stone. When it came out, the young one was sheltered under its plates.

The adult carried it to the others. On its way back, it paused beside a stone that had rolled free of the ring. It tested the stone, shifted it a short distance, and left it near the new crack.

The power made one other change. Beneath the place where the young now lay, it eased a seam in the rock so that a little more warmth could rise. It shaped the passage carefully: if the deeper stone grew hotter, the seam would narrow. If it cooled, the seam would remain open. The adjustment would last for some time, though not forever.

The adult began to build around the new shelter. It brought the stone it had left nearby and turned it twice before placing it. Later it returned to the old ring and gathered what it could from the scattered heap.

Some of the crusts had survived on the sloping face. The adult visited them once more before the power left, and carried away a small pale flake. It held the flake beneath its body while it crossed the broken ground, then pressed it against a sheltered stone beside the new ring.
