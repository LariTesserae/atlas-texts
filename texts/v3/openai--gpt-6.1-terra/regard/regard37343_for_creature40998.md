The power listened first.

It listened through the turning of the rotors, through the deep pulse that traveled along the concrete and out into the saturated ground. It listened to water moving between the moss stems, to the small mammal working its way beneath the willow, to the workers speaking close to one another’s ears.

It followed the cables down out of the basin. The current went to places with lights and pumps and rooms that needed warming. There were people there who had never seen the wet green ground or the pale rock around it. Some depended on what the rotors made.

So the power did not stop them.

At the western gap it laid an influence so slight that the wind passed through unchanged. The blades continued to turn, and the current continued down the cables. But beyond the foundations, the long waves of sound began to lose their force. The throb that had crossed the basin grew faint; the higher roar fell away into the ordinary rushing of air.

Near the machinery there was still enough sound for the workers to hear its changes. A bearing beginning to fail would not go unnoticed. A shouted warning would carry.

The first afternoon, a fly settled on a sedge above one of the northern pools. Water spread from the fractures in the rock, over grit and young moss, and made a sound too small to have been heard there before. The small mammal came to the edge of the walkway and remained for a moment among its own tracks. Then it went on beneath the willow.

The moss held water. Nothing in it needed to know what had happened.

When the workers returned, they stood for a while outside the power house, looking up at the turning blades. They checked the gauges and walked the cables. One of them found that she could hear her companion calling from the farther shed. They made a note of the change and came back with instruments, and for several seasons they measured what they could.

The power remained long enough to see that the foundations held, that the intakes stayed clear, that the quiet did not conceal a fault. It watched the water find new connections between the pools and the lichen spread over the sharp faces of the fallen slabs.

Then it left the basin to its work, keeping only the small influence at the gap.

The peat went on gathering. The rotors turned. In the brief warm season, flowers opened above the moss, and insects crossed from one pale bloom to another over the bright, shallow water.
