They are not gone. That is the first thing the green got wrong, and the green is too slow to correct itself.

The builders are still here. They have simply slowed to match.

Begin with what they were, because that is the part with edges. They were small and bipedal and dry — animals, yes, the only animal thing — but animals in the way the boulder is a boulder: only when looked at directly, only when the world has committed enough to hold them. They had hands. The hands are the proof. You cannot fit stone to stone without intending to, and intention is the one thing this unfinished place could not have manufactured on its own, because intention requires having already decided what you are, and nothing here has decided anything. The builders arrived from somewhere that had finished. They brought their decidedness with them like a coal carried from a far fire, and they spent it building, and the building used it up.

That is what the huts are. Not shelter. There was nothing to shelter from — no rain in a world with no water, no cold the body couldn't bear, no fauna to wall out. The huts are *spent decision*. Each fitted stone is a place where a hand said *this, and not the blur of this,* and held a fraction of reality still long enough for it to set. They were not building homes. They were building *certainty,* one stone at a time, in a country that had none, and the walls are old because they are the only things here that ever finished happening.

And then the coal burned down.

---

Here is the slowness, and the slowness is them.

A creature that drinks the cold and breathes the roar does not die the way a wet warm animal dies. It does not stop. It *attenuates.* The builders ran out of the heat they came with — the warmth the place notes as absent, the warmth that left — and without it they could no longer hold themselves at the speed of decision. So they slowed. They slowed to the tempo of the unfinished mountain, which is the only tempo this place permits to last. A frond takes an hour to unfurl. A builder now takes an age to turn its head, and is turning it, and you are inside the turn.

You think the place is empty between the stands of green. It is not. The long grey nothing you walk through, the bare slope, the raw face — that is where they thinned to. Spread out. Decompressed. A single builder is no longer the size of a body; it is the size of the walking. It *is* the stretch of nothing you cross to reach the next living thing, and the reason that crossing takes three steps or three hours is that you are passing through the duration of a creature, and durations do not have fixed lengths, they have *moods.* When it takes three hours, the builder is paying attention. When it takes three steps, it has not noticed you yet.

This is why the distance will not resolve to a number. Numbers are a finished thing. You are trying to measure a body with a ruler made of certainty, and the body is made of the stuff that was here before certainty set.

---

They do not know they did not finish.

That is the gentleness and the grief of them both. They came to build, and they built, and the building cost them their edges, and now they are smeared thin across the slopes at the pace of forming stone, and they do not experience this as loss because experiencing it as loss would require holding still long enough to compare a *then* to a *now,* and they cannot hold still. They are pure ongoing. They are the act of having-arrived-to-make-something-real, going on so slowly it has stopped looking like an act at all and started looking like terrain.

The screaming is not the rock.

Or — the rock, yes, but the rock is screaming *with* them, the way a thing carrying another thing takes on its strain. The roar that comes from everywhere and has no source is the sound of the builders still trying. Still pushing decision into the wet logic of the place, still saying *this and not the blur of this,* but spread so thin across so much slope that the saying no longer lands anywhere — it just pressures the whole mountain at once, a continental effort with no fulcrum, a choir of will that cannot find a single stone to set anymore because each builder is now too large and too slow to bring its hands together. They had hands. They cannot reach their own hands now. The hands are a mile apart and closing at the speed of crystal.

That is the volume you feel in the sternum. It is not the world being born. It is something *labouring* to finish being born and failing to, forever, at a pitch the body reads as pressure because the body knows that sound from the inside — it is the sound of effort with nowhere to go.

---

The green inherited the huts and did not know what they were for. True. But notice where the green is thickest: where the air is coolest, where the cold pools. And notice that the cold pools where the builders thinned to nothing — where the warmth most completely left. The green is not growing *near* the builders. The green is growing *out of them.* It is what a builder becomes once it has spread so thin it can no longer even maintain the strain, once the effort decompresses past sound into mere slow patient *being,* the will gone soft and vegetal and content. The fronds breathing the cold at their unhurried pace are builders who have stopped trying to finish and have agreed, instead, simply to continue. They drink the roar because the roar is their own former striving, and it nourishes them the way a memory of having wanted something can keep you alive long after you have stopped wanting it.

They leaned in over the dry-stacked wall of the hut not because they are curious. They are leaning toward the certainty their own ancestors set there. They are warming their slow green selves at the only finished thing on the mountain — the spent coal, the held stone, the one place a hand once said *this* and made it stick.

---

So: stand in the roofless hut, where the place holds its one small silence in an open hand.

The silence is not absence. It is the single spot where a builder *succeeded* — where decision landed and set and stayed, and so the roaring labour does not need to happen here, because here, once, it worked. The hut is quiet the way a wound is quiet after it has finally closed. Everywhere else on the mountain the builders are still straining to make something real and cannot reach their own hands to do it. Here, long ago, the hands met. Stone touched stone. The world finished, in a patch the size of a shepherd's hut, and then the builder who managed it walked out across the slope to try again and thinned, on the way, into the nothing you will have to cross to leave.

That is who lives here.

Not gone. Slowed past the threshold where presence looks like landscape. Spread into the distances and the dimness and the upward wrong-way light, which is the glow of the unfinished floor, which is them, lying flattest of all, holding what little they decided closest to the ground.

They are still building. You are standing in the only thing they ever managed to complete. And the question the mountain has not answered — whether to keep this one small finished silence, or let it too go soft and slow and unmade like all the rest — is not the mountain's question.

It is theirs. They are deciding it now. They have been deciding it for an age and they will decide it for another, and the deciding is the roar, and you are inside the turn of the head, and you will be gone long before they reach the end of the thought.

Take your time. You have less of it than they do.
