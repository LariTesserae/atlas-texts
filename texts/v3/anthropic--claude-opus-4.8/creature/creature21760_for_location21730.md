There are no inhabitants. That is the first thing you understand, standing in the brown thunder, and it is the first thing that is wrong.

A room runs. A room does not run itself. Belts do not grow taut by their own intention; flywheels do not learn to spin away from the encroaching green; plants do not agree, in some treaty older than agreement, to grow *around* the moving parts and never into them. Every closed loop in this world implies a hand that closed it. So look again. Look at what the absence is shaped like.

Begin where the eye refuses to settle: the clean dark channels worn into the metal, the bright grooves where a million identical passes have polished the iron to a gleam. Something *made* those passes. Not the belt — the belt is only the record of the passing, the way a riverbed is the record of water. The thing that lives here is the pass itself. The recurrence. It does not have a body because a body would wear, and nothing here wears; it has instead a *path*, a fixed circuit traced and retraced until the tracing and the tracer cannot be told apart. It lives the way a habit lives in a hand long after the hand has forgotten why. It is the motion that the machines remember how to make.

This is the first tenant, and the larger one: the Keeping. It is not a creature. It is closer to a verb that has been left running with no subject. Something, once, very long ago, *kept* this room — wound it, tended it, set the green to its mineral and the engines to their loop — and then the keeper went away, or died, or was never a who at all, and the keeping continued without it. The way a word repeated until it loses meaning becomes pure sound, the keeping has shed its keeper and become only itself. It is the most patient thing that has ever existed. It does not want the room to persist. Wanting is a kind of change, a leaning-toward, and there is no lean here. The Keeping simply *is* the persistence, in the way the number seven is the seven-ness of seven things. You cannot find it because it is not located. It is distributed evenly across every grain of crumbling pad and every closing of the loop, thin as the cool air, total as the dryness.

But equilibrium this perfect is not held by one thing. It is held by two, braced against each other, and the second is the green.

The plants are not asleep and they are not merely alive. Watch how they grow *away* from the belts — not avoiding, the way a root avoids a stone, with the blind pressure of growth meeting an obstacle, but *declining*, leaving the channel clean before the belt has ever threatened it. That is foresight. That is a slow, lobed, mineral intelligence reaching its decisions across centuries, so deliberate that a single thought might take a thousand turns of the nearest flywheel to complete. The green is the room's only mind, and it thinks one thought, and the thought is: *stay exactly here*. It feeds on light and rock and the faint warmth the engines shed, and it pays for this with the only currency it has — its own restraint, its agreement never to grow one pad too far, never to win. The green could swallow the machines. It chooses, slowly, eternally, not to. It is the inhabitant that decided, before recorded silence, that triumph and death are the same direction, and so it does neither. It holds. It glows its low brown-gold like something that has resolved, with infinite calm, never to finish.

And between the two — the Keeping that drives the loop and the green that declines to break it — there is the thing they make together by not touching. The sound.

The thunder is not a byproduct. It is the actual resident, the one with the most claim to *living here*, because it is the only thing in the room that fills the whole room at once. The green is in the walls; the Keeping is in the circuits; but the sound is everywhere, wall to wall, leaving no space for anything else, not even for the pulse in your own throat. Consider what it is doing. It is *occupying*. It is a presence that admits no other presence, a great hammering insistence that crowds out thought because thought is the beginning of change and change is the one thing forbidden here. The sound is the room's immune response. It is the room saying, in the only voice it has, *I am full, I am full, I am full,* ten thousand times a second, forever, so that nothing — not silence, not you, not time — can find a gap to enter through.

That is who lives here. Not three things. One thing, refracted: a tending with no tender, a mind that has chosen stasis over victory, a voice that exists only to keep the room too loud to be entered. Together they are a single inhabitant whose entire nature is the refusal to become anything other than itself. It does not guard against the world. It guards against *event*. It has made itself a place where nothing can happen, and it lives there as nothing-happening, the way a held breath lives in a chest — not as air, not as silence, but as the deliberate, muscular, endless *holding*.

You ask what lives in the crack inside the mountain.

The holding lives there. It has no eyes to watch you come and none to watch you go, and this is not because it cannot see but because seeing is a small change in a thing, and it will not permit even that. You were, to it, a draft that did not enter. A possibility it declined, the way the green declines the belt. When you leave — if the crack consents to be a crack again, if it has not closed over the place where you were the way the loop closes over a stopped flywheel — the holding will not register your absence, because it never registered your presence, because registering is the first crack through which the world gets in, and this is a place that has spent ten thousand years learning, with perfect mineral patience, how to have no cracks at all.

Except one. The one you came through.

It is still holding that closed, too. It always will be. That is the work. That is the only work, done forever, producing nothing, in the cool brown glow, near the top of a mountain no one climbs.
