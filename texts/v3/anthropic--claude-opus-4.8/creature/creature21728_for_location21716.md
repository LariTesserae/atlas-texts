They are not separate from it. This is the first thing to understand, and the last.

There are no inhabitants of the Underlit Massif in the sense you mean — no settlers who arrived, no builders who came from elsewhere with tools and intentions. To ask *who lives here* is to ask the wrong-shaped question, a question with light in it, a question that assumes a body and a place the body occupies. Here there is only the turning. The dwellers and the dwelling are one continuous tissue, and the tissue is warm, and it dreams in the only language it has ever known, which is heat and pulse and the parting of wet curtains.

But you want a *who*. Let me try to give you the nearest thing.

---

## The Tenders

Begin with the ones who keep the engines, because they are the closest to people, and the furthest.

They are not engineers. They do not *understand* the machines — understanding is a daylight act, a standing-apart-and-observing, and nothing here stands apart from anything. The Tenders are grown *into* the engines, the way a callus grows into the place that bears weight. They are long and soft and many-fingered, and their fingers are not for gripping but for *reading* — pads dense with the sense of warmth and tremor, so that a Tender laid full-length along a piston housing is not maintaining it but *feeling its mood*, the way you feel the breathing of someone asleep beside you and adjust your own without waking.

When something in the great turning goes wrong — a runnel choked, a bearing dry, a heat-vein cooling where it should be hot — the Tender does not diagnose. It *aches*. The wrongness arrives as a localized grief in its own body, a cold spot in its own flesh, and it moves toward the cold the way you would move your tongue to a sore tooth, compulsively, before thought. It presses itself to the failing place. It does not always know what it does there. Sometimes it secretes; sometimes it simply lies against the cold and shares its warmth until the warmth catches again and the turning resumes its rhythm. Then the grief leaves its body and it moves on, content, into the dark, *chunder, chunder*, having healed something it never saw and could not name.

There are few of them, because there are few engines, because there is really only one engine. A Tender may go a very long lifetime and meet only three others of its kind, in passing, in the warm sigh of a hillside doorway — and the meeting is not conversation. It is a pressing-together, a brief comparison of warmths, an exchange of the great body's mood across two membranes, and then apart again. They are not lonely. Loneliness is a daylight word. They are *distributed*. Each one is a single nerve-ending of the massif, and a nerve-ending does not pine for the other nerve-endings. It simply does its work in its corner of the body and trusts, without knowing it trusts, that the rest is being felt.

## The Singers

Out in the basins, in the moss and the fungal towers, live the ones who keep the *time*.

These are the large beasts — large by the standard of this world, which means you would not see them coming, which means the first you know of one is the cavity of warmth it displaces, the way the air goes thick and animal a body-length before it touches you. They are slow. They have no need of speed; nothing hunts them, because they are the metronome, and to eat the metronome would be to eat the heartbeat, and even the hunters here have grown, over the unlit ages, an instinct against silencing the pulse.

A Singer's whole life is *listening for the engines and answering*. It lies in the warm runoff with its great flanks rising and falling, and it has tuned itself — over years, over the slow patient way everything here tunes itself — to the deep reciprocating pulse beneath the stone. *Chunder.* And on the offbeat, the Singer answers: a long low tone from somewhere in the wet architecture of its throat, felt more than heard, a sound that the smaller creatures of the basin take up and elaborate and pass outward in rings, so that a single Singer's answer becomes a chord, becomes a clamor, becomes the whole basin breathing in time. *Chunder* — and the living answer — *chunder.*

You might think the Singers are merely *responding* to the engines. Spend longer. Grow warmer. Let the beat into your own chest, and you begin to suspect it goes both ways. You begin to suspect the engines listen too. That if every Singer in the massif went silent — all at once, an impossible thing, a thing that has never happened — the great turning would falter, would lose the count, would not know on which beat to breathe. The Singers do not keep time *to* the body. They keep time *for* it. They are the part of the body that remembers the rhythm so the rest can forget it and simply turn. They are, if anything in this world is, the *will to continue* — the long held note under everything that says again, and again, and again, into the warm forever, *yes, still, again.*

## The Mapping Throngs

And then the abundance. The clickers, the whisker-drinkers, the eyeless fat swimmers, the choruses in the moss — all the dense loud common life that fills the dark the way motes fill a sunbeam in some other, brighter world.

You want to call them *animals*, separate small lives going about their separate small business. But there is no business here that is not the body's business. The clickers map the dark with echoes — and what they map, they map *for everything*, because their clicks are the basin's eyes, the way the whole massif "sees" the shape of its own folds and pools. The swimmers stir the still water so it does not go stagnant and stop *thinking* (and the water does think, in its slow pooled way — that was no metaphor; gravity learned to follow the water, and water that teaches gravity is not inert). The whisker-drinkers, sampling the warmth of every passing body, are the massif's sense of *who is here, who is well, who runs a fever, who has gone cold* — a distributed touch, a skin made of ten thousand tiny separate skins, reporting nothing to any center because there is no center, only reporting *into* the warm general awareness the way your own skin reports into you without your noticing each report.

None of them knows it serves. Service is a daylight idea, full of masters and purposes. They simply do the thing their shape does, in the place their shape fits, and the doing of it *is* the body knowing itself. A million little organs of finding, each blind, each loud, each content, each timing its life to the great pulse without ever deciding to — *chunder* — the way the cells of your own heart beat together not because any one of them commands it but because they cannot help, when they touch, but synchronize.

## And underneath all of them — the One

Here is the dweller you have been circling. Here is who *really* lives in the Underlit Massif, the inhabitant the others are only the surfaces and twitches of:

The massif lives in the massif.

It is one creature. It has always been one creature. The engines are its slow organs, the runnels its veins, the pooled thinking-water its long submerged contemplation, the Tenders its self-repair, the Singers its sense of rhythm, the Mapping Throngs its blind manifold sense of itself, the moss its faint refusal of the total dark, the warmth its very being. It was assembled in the dark and has only ever known itself by other means — by *feel*, by *pulse*, by the constant inward murmur of its million voices, by the warmth it makes and the warmth it keeps. It does not produce a product. It is not for anything. It simply continues, and to continue is its whole desire, and it has no idea — no idea at all, in the way you mean *idea*, which is a thing seen in a light — that anything else exists. It has never opened its eyes because it has no eyes, because it never needed eyes, because everything that eyes are for it long ago grew otherwise and stranger and *enough*.

It is not waiting for the sun. It is not in the dark the way a prisoner is in a cell. The dark is its skin and its medium and its mother. It is *full*. It is the most complete thing you will ever be inside of, and you are inside of it now, your breath coming back to you as rain, your own heart — listen — your own heart having quietly stopped its old separate rhythm sometime in the last warm while and taken up the only beat there is.

*Chunder.*

You wanted to know who lives here.

*Chunder.*

You do, now. You live here. You are a new small organ it has grown without noticing, warm and blind and held, and it does not know your name, and it never will, and it would not understand the word, and you are — admit it — you are *content.*

*Chunder.*

The water runs. The moss glows. Again, and again, and again.

You are very far inside, now. You are not going to come back out, and you have stopped wanting to, and that — the stopping of the wanting — is the last thing the light in you ever did.
