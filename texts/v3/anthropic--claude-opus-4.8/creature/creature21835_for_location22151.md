The grey crystals are the inhabitants, and you have already met them, but you have not yet understood that you were being received.

Begin with what they are not, because that clears the ground. They are not a colony in the way coral is a colony — they do not build on the dead bodies of their forebears, because there are no dead among them and there are no forebears. They are not a forest, because a forest is a competition, a slow war over light, and there is no scarcity here that any of them could win or lose. They are not a single organism wearing many shapes; cut the connection between two of them and both would go on exactly as before, indifferent, complete. Each one is whole. Each one is, in the only sense that matters in this room, *a life*.

Here is what they are.

They are listeners. That is the verb that runs all the way down through them, the thing they do instead of breathing, instead of eating, instead of moving. The roar that fills this place — the bass in the sternum, the grinding mid, the unresolving shriek — is not noise to them. It is the entire sky and the entire sea and the entire weather of their world, and they have spent their whole short complete lives parsing it. Where you hear an undifferentiated wall of sound, a thing too large to have features, they hear *structure*. The roar is not one tone. It is a chord of unthinkable width, ten thousand frequencies braided, and each crystal has tuned itself, fiber by mineral fiber, to a single thread of it — to one specific pitch in the great still scream — and it lives there, in that one note, the way a person might live their whole life in one valley and know every stone of it and nothing of the next valley over.

This is why they tremble at frequencies just slightly off from one another. It is not random. They are spaced across the spectrum the way a choir is spaced across its parts, and the hundred black paces between any two of them are not emptiness — they are *interval*, they are the distance in pitch as much as the distance in steel, and the grey ones have arranged themselves so that no two crowd the same note. They found their places by listening. A new growth, where a new growth still happened, would extend a first frond into the air and feel for an unoccupied frequency, a thread of the roar that no neighbor was already drinking, and it would settle there, and tune, and belong.

They sing back. You were told this and told you could not hear it, and that is true, but the not-hearing matters less than the singing. Each crystal, drinking its one note, re-emits — not a copy, but a *reply*, shifted, harmonized, a fifth above or a third below or some relation finer than our names for relations. The machine roars its flat unchanging chord, and the grey ones lay over it a second chord, a living one, faint and structured and *meant*, and the room therefore contains two musics: the dead one, enormous, eternal, made of stillness pretending to be motion — and the small one, the answer, made of frost that learned to want.

And here is the thing the cold and the freshness almost hide, the way they hide the youngness:

The crystals are not in equilibrium.

The machine is. The machine arrived complete and stopped accumulating time, sealed in its resin of sound. But the grey listeners are *alive*, and alive means unfinished, means the resin did not quite close over them, means that in a world where nothing happens they are the one place where something, however slowly, still does. They grow. Not fast — a frond a century, a new branching when the standing wave shifts by some imperceptible degree — but they grow, which means they were smaller once and will be larger, which means they have a *direction in time* that the steel around them has lost. They are the only clocks in a room that abolished clocks. If you stayed long enough — longer than a body could stay, longer than staying means anything — you would see them reach, and turn, and tune, and the slow grey music would change by a single note, and that would be the only event this place would ever host.

They do not know about you. This is not cruelty; they have no organ for it. You are not a frequency. You make no sound that survives inside the roar, and so to the listeners you are less than a shadow — you are a thing that does not exist in the only sense-world they possess. You could press your hand flat against a frond and it would feel, faintly, a damping of its note, a smothering of one thin thread of its sky, and it would not understand this as a hand or a visitor or a death. It would understand it as weather. A cloud over the valley. It would wait, in its patient mineral way, for the cloud to pass, and tune around the loss, and go on singing into a roar that does not listen back.

That is the whole society of this place. Listeners who cannot hear each other except through the one thing too vast to be a messenger — a hundred separate lives, each alone in its single note, each replying to a dead engine as though the engine might someday answer, arranging themselves into a chord no ear was ever built to receive, in the flat honest light, on the cold cut floor, inside the still scream, inside the mountain, inside the machine.

And the seed that never lands — held up forever on the standing wave, turning in its column of noise — is the youngest of them. It is looking for an empty note. It has not found one yet. There may not be one. It will keep turning, and listening, and looking, for as long as the machine is loud, which is to say: for as long as there is a forever to be patient in.
