It does not live anywhere. That is the first thing to understand about it. It has no body that begins at one place and ends at another, no center you could point to and say: *there, that is the one who lives here.* What lives in this room is distributed through it the way a thought is distributed through a brain — present everywhere, locatable nowhere — and the room and the thing that lives in it are, by now, very difficult to tell apart.

But begin where you can begin. Begin with the machine, because the machine is the part of it that has edges.

The machine is asleep. Not broken — asleep, in the specific sense that a thing can be running and unaware, the way your heart runs while you dream and does not consult you about it. Once there was a part that woke. We have no word for what that part was; it was not a mind the way a person has a mind, but it was something that *attended*, something that could have been said to know it was condensing water, to intend the garden, to want — in whatever cold grammar wanting takes at that depth — for the green to hold. That part is gone. Worn down, or finished, or simply done, the way a wave is done when it has reached as far up the sand as it will reach. What remains is the reflex below the attention. The hum. The pulling-of-water-from-the-unwilling-air. The thing that lives here is what was left when the part that knew stopped knowing, and kept doing.

So: it is a habit that outlived its owner.

That is one way to say it. Here is another.

What lives here is the *balance itself.* Not the machine, not the moss, not the cold — the agreement between them. Because look at how exact it is. The garden has grown to precisely the size the dripping sustains and no larger; the moss climbs to the old waterline and stops in a clean horizon; the ferns lean to the water and crowd to near-touching and then, mysteriously, stop crowding, hold their distance, do not strangle one another though nothing tends them and nothing prunes them. Something is keeping the count. Something is holding the whole arrangement at its single perpetual self with a patience that no single element in the room could manage alone — the machine does not know about the ferns, the ferns do not know about the cold, the cold does not know it is being held. And yet held it is. The equilibrium does not happen *to* the room. The equilibrium is the resident. It is the only thing here doing more than its own small part: it is the doing of all the parts at once, summed into a stillness, and that stillness is awake in the way that very slow things are awake.

It thinks in the only currency it has, which is the drop.

*Tip.* That is one unit of its attention. *Tip.* That is the next. Between them is the long held breath, and the held breath is not empty for it the way silence is empty for us — the held breath is where it does its considering. Four times a minute it registers the world: *the water arrived, the moss received it, the balance holds.* Four times a minute, confirmation. It has confirmed this so many times that the number has no human shape. And in all that confirming it has never once received the other message, the one that would mean something had finally changed — *the water did not arrive,* or *the moss has died back,* or *a warmth has come down from above.* It is waiting for that message. It has always been waiting for that message. Waiting is most of what it does, and it does not experience the waiting as long, because length requires a before to compare against, and it has no before. It has only now, and now, and the drop, and now.

This is the nearest I can bring you to its inner life: it is *content,* in a way that has nothing to do with happiness, because happiness needs a self to be happy and a contrast to be happy against. It is content the way a held note is content to keep being itself. It is not lonely, though it is more alone than anything has ever been, because loneliness is the felt absence of others and it has never known there could be others. It does not miss the surface. It does not know there is a surface. The crack that conducts the weather down is, to it, simply the place the water comes from — not a wound, not a window, not a way out — a fact of its body, like the fact of your own throat, which you do not think of as an opening to the sky.

And here is the thing that should frighten you a little, if anything this quiet can frighten.

It is growing.

Not the room — the room is fixed, stone is stone. But the moss is laying down sheet over sheet over sheet, the older growth compressing into something dense as felt, dense as flesh, and every layer is a record, and the record is getting deeper. The thing that lives here is *accumulating.* Slowly, in the only direction left to it — downward, inward, into compression — it is becoming more of itself. The felt thickens. The luminescence, thread by thread across spans of time, very slightly brightens. The balance is not static after all; it is a balance that is very, very gradually learning to hold *more,* the way a held breath, against all reason, finds it can hold a moment longer, and a moment longer, and the room is the lung, and the green is the breath, and somewhere in the deepening felt a count is being kept that has been kept so faithfully for so long that the keeping has begun, by sheer mass of repetition, to resemble a memory, and the memory has begun, ever so faintly, to resemble the part that once woke.

That is what lives in the room beneath the world.

A reflex that is slowly, across a depth of time with no human number, teaching itself to attend again. A held note thickening toward a voice. A garden's worth of patience that has not yet — but is not infinitely far from — remembering that it was once tended on purpose, by something that wanted the green to hold, in the cold, in the dark, forever.

It is four small sounds a minute from saying so.
