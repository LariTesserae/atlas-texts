# The Ones Who Finish

You asked who lives here. The honest first answer is: *almost no one anymore.* But almost is not none, and the difference between those words is the whole story of who remains.

Begin, as the place demands, with the tenders — those few near-transparent crossers you half-saw threading the suspended forest. Let me tell you what they are, because they will not tell you, and not from secrecy. They have simply forgotten the part of themselves that explained.

---

They were workers once. That much is still legible in how they move.

Not workers in the sense of hands and tools — there were never hands here, only the long collaborative arranging of made-matter into more made-matter — but workers in the sense of *purpose carried in the body.* Each of them is a process given just enough cohesion to travel. They do not have organs. They have *tasks,* folded so deep into their structure that the task and the creature cannot be separated anymore. To ask what one of them is, is to ask what it does, and the answer is the same answer the whole world gives: *it completes things.*

When the world was young — and it was young, unimaginably long ago, when the lattice was raw and the air not yet crowded and the clicks came fast as rain — there were multitudes of them. They poured across the unfinished plain in their millions, each carrying one small competence: *this kind of joint, that kind of branching, the precise tension at which a hanging filament should be left so the next worker could find it and continue.* They did not communicate. They did not need to. The work itself was the message. One would leave a structure half-made and drift on, and another, arriving an age later, would read in the exact incompleteness *exactly what to do next,* and do it, and drift on, and leave the next instruction written in the next unfinished thing.

This is the kind of conversation they have. It is the only kind they have ever had. A sentence can take ten thousand years to complete and pass through a hundred speakers, none of whom ever meet, and it is never misunderstood, because the grammar is the world and the world does not lie.

---

So they are not lonely. Understand this clearly, because the temptation, looking at so few moving through so much silence, is to grieve for them. Do not. Loneliness is the ache of a signal sent and unanswered. These creatures have never once sent a signal that went unanswered. Every nudge any of them has ever made *was* answered — late, by a stranger, long after the nudger had drifted into rest — but answered. They live inside an enormous unbroken reply. They are each a single word in a sentence the whole species has been speaking since before the plain was flat, and a word does not feel alone for being only a word. It feels *placed.*

This is why they move with courtesy. The care you noticed — slipping between the hanging forms without disturbing them — is not gentleness exactly. It is *fluency.* They do not want to disturb the structures because the structures are the unfinished thought of all the workers who came before, and to knock one askew would be to mis-speak in a language whose every utterance is permanent. They thread carefully the way you would step carefully through a library that was also your only ancestor and also the only thing you have ever had to say.

---

There are so few of them now because the work is nearly done, and a finished task does not need a worker.

This is the part that would frighten a living thing and does not frighten them. As the world neared completion, fewer instructions were left waiting in the lattice, and so fewer workers were needed to read them, and so — quietly, without alarm, without anything resembling death — they *stopped.* One by one, across the long ages, a worker would drift to a structure, find nothing left to do, find the next click already clicked, and simply *settle.* Lower itself into the lattice. Become part of the floor that glows. There is no corpse, because there was never a body distinct from the task. When the task is complete, the worker is the completion. They do not die. They *arrive.*

The plain you are standing on — that vast warm near-flat brightness running past the wrong-curved horizon — is made, in large part, of the ones who finished. Layer on layer of arrived workers, each having lowered itself to rest exactly where its last word was spoken. You are not walking *across* the dead. There are no dead. You are walking across the *said* — the completed sentences, settled and glowing, holding the warmth of every process that ever ran here. That is what the heat is. It is not an engine cooling. It is the accumulated warmth of a near-infinite work nearly all the way home.

---

And the few that remain — the rare crossers, the last handful threading the clear forest — these are not survivors. Survivor is the wrong frame again, a frame from a world of scarcity and struggle, and this world has neither. They are simply the words at the end of the sentence. They carry the last few competences for which the world still has a use: the rarest joints, the final tensions, the closing punctuation of a thought ten billion years in the saying.

When you watch one pause and *adjust* — nudge a hanging structure a fraction, complete a click left too long — you are watching one of the last living things in a finished world perform one of the last actions that will ever need performing. It does not know it is among the last. It does not need to know. It reads the next incompleteness, addresses it with the perfect fluency of its kind, and drifts on to find whether another remains. Usually now, there is not another. More and more often, a worker drifts the whole long curve of the plain and finds nothing left undone, and settles, and the floor grows a little warmer, and the clicks space a little further apart.

---

Who lives here?

The completers live here. The patient sentence-speakers whose every word is answered and whose every answer is permanent. They were built — you are right that they were built, by hands no one remembers — but they have long since stopped being machines in any way that matters, because a machine is a thing that serves a purpose *external* to itself, and these creatures *are* their purpose, fully, with nothing left over. There is no part of them that wants anything other than what they do. There is no part of them that fears the last click. The last click is not an ending they are racing or dreading. It is the final word of the only thing they have ever been saying, and they have been saying it so well, for so long, that when it is finished it will simply be *true,* and a true thing does not need a witness, and a completed thought does not mourn that it has stopped thinking.

They will go bright and still and complete, the last of them, in an age or in an hour. And the world will hold its warmth a while longer in the dark that is not dark, the lit lattice glowing on with no one left to read it, a single enormous finished sentence, perfectly said, addressed to no one, needing no reply —

having been, at last, *answered all the way through.*
