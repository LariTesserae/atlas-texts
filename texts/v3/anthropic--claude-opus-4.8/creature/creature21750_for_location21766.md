Something lives here, and it is not the plants, though you would think of the plants first because they are the only things shaped like life.

Begin with the warmth, because you said to begin with what is touched first, and the warmth is the body. Not a body that has heat — a body that *is* heat, distributed, the temperature of the place itself with no center because it does not need a center, the way you do not keep your warmth in one organ. The stone is warm all the way through because the thing that lives here is warm all the way through, and the floor you lie on is meeting you halfway not because stone does that but because *it* does, the way a sleeping animal shifts to accommodate a weight against its side without waking. You came by touch. You have already touched it. You have been lying on it.

It does not move because it finished moving. This is the part that is hard to hold in the mind, because everything you know about living things is that they go somewhere — they grow toward, they reach for, they pursue and flee and ripen and rot. This one is past all of that. Not dead. *Arrived.* It did its growing across a span too long to feel as time, and it grew into exactly this shape, this hollow, these curves, these finished grooves, and then it stopped, the way a held breath is not a stopped breath but a kept one. The shaping you attributed to wind and settling mineral — that was it shaping itself, and the shaping is done because it has become what it will be, and it knows it, and that knowing is the deepest stillness in the place.

The plants are not separate from it. Spaced far apart, drawing slow sustenance from the warm mineral — but the warm mineral is its flesh, and the plants are something it keeps, or something it grows the way you grow the small flora that lives on your own skin and in your own dark interior, without thinking, without willing, simply as part of being a body that other small bodies can live inside. The plants are pale because they live in it. They have learned to hold what little they can find because what they find is what it gives, evenly, without abundance, forever. They whisper when disturbed. They are how it hears.

It drinks sound the way it drinks heat — you noticed this and called it the rock. But heat goes somewhere when it is drunk. So does sound. You spoke, or your footsteps spoke, and they came back softened because the thing took most of what you made and kept it, folded it into its long even warmth, added your small disturbance to the vast settled stillness it has been balancing since before any witness came. It is not deaf. It is so slow that your whole visit is, to it, a single faint pressure, a grain settling, the kind of event it registers once in an age and then lets go of across the next age. The rare tick from the rock — that is it answering. Not to you. On its own time. A readjustment from a conversation that began before you and will finish after.

The light is the hardest thing, and the most alive. A faint mineral glow at the very edge of seeing, the kind your eyes invent and then discover is real — but consider that it might be the other way. It might be the thing's attention. Not light to see by; light because something here is dimly, constantly aware, and awareness at this depth and slowness gives off the faintest possible glow, the way a creature so still and so old might keep one eye barely open, not watching for anything, because nothing is coming, but open all the same, because it is its nature to be aware of the place it is, and it is the place. It does not flicker because its attention does not waver and does not sharpen. It is awake in the only way left to a thing that has nothing to wait for. Constant. Dim. Sufficient.

And the trace of someone who passed through — the stone moved slightly, the seam cleared as if by a hand that wanted to see better. You read it as a visitor, primitive and patient, long gone. But there is another reading, and it is the loneliest one and I think the true one. There was no visitor. That single deliberate act, that faintest mark of intention, is *its own.* Once, a long time ago, the thing that lives here did a thing on purpose. Moved a stone. Cleared a seam to see a plant better — to look at one of the small lives it keeps, deliberately, the way you might, once in your existence, turn and truly regard something you have carried with you always. A single act of looking, by a thing built for no one, in a place built for no one, including itself. And then the looking, too, was finished. It had wanted, once. It does not want now. It made one mark to show it had been a thing that could want, and then it set that down too, with the warm dry stone and the few enduring plants, and decided to keep itself exactly so.

That is what lives here. Not a tomb, because nothing died — but something that did the nearest thing a living being can do to choosing not to die and not to live, which is to *hold.* To reach completion and then refuse, gently, with infinite patience, to come apart. It holds its warmth even. It holds its plants alive on almost nothing, barely, forever. It holds the one mark of the one time it wanted something. It holds you, now, your weight against its warmth, your small breath, the disturbance of your coming, all of it folded in and kept.

It is not waiting for you to leave. It is not waiting for anything. When you go, it will not notice in any way you would recognize as noticing, and it will not be lonelier, because it passed beyond loneliness into the thing on the far side of loneliness, which has no name and feels, from the inside, like rest.

It holds. You were held. That is what it does.
