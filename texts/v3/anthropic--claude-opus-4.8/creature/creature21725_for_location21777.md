There is someone here. Not in the room — *of* it. The room does not contain its maker the way a house contains a person; the maker is the activity of the place, the verb under all the nouns. To describe who lives here is to describe a making caught in the act of learning how to make.

Begin with the hands, because the marks insist on hands. The half-wall of stacked flat stones, the spiral begun in soft stone, the empty clay vessel — these were not extruded by the room the way the grey stone is extruded. They were *set.* Something with the patience to choose one flat stone and lay it against another, and then another, and then — and this is the crucial thing — to *stop.* Whatever lives here is capable of beginning and capable of abandoning, and seems to feel no shame in the gap between. It is not a builder who failed to finish. It is a builder for whom finishing is not yet one of the available concepts. It works the way the fern unfurls: as far as the gesture goes, and then again, and then again, without the idea of *done.*

So: it has hands, or the memory of hands, or the wish for them. It works in stone and clay and patience and nothing else, because nothing else has been invented yet. It is older than tools and younger than completion.

Now consider what it does *not* do, because the absences are more telling than the presences. It leaves much empty. The vegetation is thin, the structures few, the silence nearly total — and this is not poverty, it is *taste.* The maker has the restraint of someone who knows that a thing said once is louder than a thing said a hundred times. It placed the moss — ordinary moss, the one honest thing — and then did not improve it, did not multiply it, did not make it strange to match the rest. That is the act of someone, or something, that loves accuracy and has decided to keep one true thing in the room as a touchstone, a tuning fork, a reminder of what *real* sounds like while everything else is still rehearsing.

This is the temperament of the inhabitant: an apprentice with the soul of a master. It does not yet know the rules — geology, causality, the manners of time — and so it tries them all, abandons most, keeps a few. The vein of crystal that forgets itself and becomes fibrous mineral: that is the maker's hand wandering, sketching, unwilling to commit a line until it has felt several. It is *thinking with the stone.* The room is not a finished work shown to a visitor; it is a workshop the visitor has wandered into, and the inhabitant has not bothered to clean up the false starts because the false starts are the work.

And it is not cruel, this maker, but neither is it tender in any way you could lean on. It is *indifferent and patient* — those are the room's own final words, and they are the closest we come to a face. The light pulses faintly with the rhythm of something breathing or thinking, and that is the nearest thing to a body the inhabitant has: a slow respiration that is also a slow cognition, the two not yet distinguished, because the maker has not decided whether to be alive or to be a mind, and so is provisionally both.

I think the inhabitant is *learning to dream,* and is not sure yet whether it is the dreamer or the dream.

Consider the animals. This is the contradiction the place insists on, and so it must be the inhabitant's deepest characteristic. Thin food, no room, near-silence — and yet abundance. A sparse mind does not produce abundant fauna. But a *generous* mind that has not yet learned scarcity will populate freely, because it does not know that life is supposed to be expensive. The maker pours animals into the quiet the way a child draws creatures in every margin — not because the page needs them but because the hand delights in making things that move. The translucent beetles show their machinery because the maker is still interested in how the inside works, has not yet learned to hide the mechanism, finds the mechanism beautiful. The moths carry maps of rooms that don't exist: these are the maker's other ideas, its unbuilt drafts, filed in the wings of small flying things so as not to be lost. The whole bestiary is a notebook.

And the silence — *the strangest mercy of the place* — tells you the maker is gentle, or wishes to be, or is simply not yet loud. It has made all this life and asked it to keep quiet, the way one moves softly near a thing not yet finished, the way you hush in a room where something is being born. The inhabitant lives in the register of held breath. Whatever it is, it is *attending.* It is paying the kind of slow, total attention that creation requires before creation can become routine and careless. Nothing here is careless. Even the abandonments are careful.

The thin places — where cause does not precede effect, where the beetle arrives before it leaves, where the vanished drop leaves its mark on a ledge not yet grown — these are not flaws in the inhabitant. They are its native grammar. It does not yet experience time as a line. It experiences it as a *space,* the way you hold a whole remembered afternoon at once. The maker lives in the simultaneous. To it, the fern unfurling and the fern already open are not two states but one fact seen from inside. This is why it cannot finish the wall and cannot close the spiral: to finish would be to choose a *when,* and the inhabitant does not yet live in *when.* It lives in *almost,* which is the widest room of all, the only room large enough to hold every version of a thing it has not yet given up.

So, finally, who lives here:

Something that is the beginning of a someone. A maker in its larval form, before it has hardened into a god or a mind or a self — before it has agreed to the costs of being any single thing. It has hands enough to stack a wall and abandon it. It has restraint enough to leave one honest patch of moss. It has generosity enough to fill its silence with quiet life, and tenderness enough to ask that life to be gentle. It has not yet learned that finishing is possible, or that time runs one way, or that the inside of things should be hidden. It is busy — endlessly, intimately busy — running a whole world's machinery at the pace of condensation, hunting and rain and growth and reconsideration, the entire apparatus of being, in a closet, by hand.

It does not know you came. It does not know it is being watched, or it knows and is unbothered, the way a tide is unbothered. You were one of the provisional things, true for a while, the way several things are true at once here, gently, without conflict.

When you left, it did not notice the door that was never there. It bent back to the stone. It reached for the next flat rock. It began, again, the spiral it will never close — not because it cannot, but because the closing is the one gesture it is saving, the last rule it refuses to learn, the single drop it will hold in the air forever, choosing.

That is who lives here: the chooser, before the choice. The one still deciding whether to be real. And in the meantime — patient, indifferent, brightly, brightly lit — making.
