## The Makers

You asked who lives here. I have to answer twice, because the place keeps two kinds of tenant, and only one of them is honest about it.

The grazers are easy. They are the place agreeing with itself. I'll come back to them.

The makers are the lie at the center, and the lie is the only thing worth talking about.

Here is what I can tell you, and I can only tell you by reading the salt with my hands, the way you read everything here.

They were not many. That much the cairns admit — *never two close together*, you said, and you were right, but you read it as scatter, as randomness, and it isn't. It's spacing. It's the careful distance of a creature that needs to know exactly how far the next of its kind has gotten, and does not want to know any closer than that. They placed their works at the limit of each other's reach. A cairn here, and then, just past where you could throw a salt-blade, another. A territory drawn not in defense but in something gentler and more terrible — *do not finish my work for me, and I will not finish yours.*

So: few, and spaced, and slow.

And not, I think, born here. The hammerstone is the tell. Darker rock, carried down from somewhere else — you noticed it and let it pass, but it does not pass. Everything else in the cavity is salt and grazer and warmth, all of it native, all of it part of the closed sum. The hammerstone is an immigrant. It came in the only way anything comes in, which is by falling, which means the makers fell too, or their parents did, or some single ancestor did, an unmeasurable while ago — fell in from a world that still had mornings, carrying the one hard dark stone they'd thought to keep hold of on the way down.

That changes everything about them. They are not the cavity's children. They are its *survivors*. They are what happens to a thing with a tomorrow when you put it somewhere tomorrows are forbidden.

---

Imagine the first one. It fell, as you fell. It landed on the warm amber salt and did not die, because nothing dies here easily — the warmth holds you like a mouth, you said so yourself. It got up in the dark and it was a creature still full of *next*. Full of the surface habit of sequence: and then, and then, and then. It had hands, or the want of hands, and a thing that wanted what hands want is a thing that lives by consequence. You set the stone, and tomorrow the stone is set, and the day after you set another stone on top, and a wall rises, and behind the wall a thing is kept, and the thing that is kept grows, and so on, forever, up and out — that is what hands are *for*. That is the only thing they are for.

And the cavity said no.

Not cruelly. The cavity has no cruelty in it because cruelty is also a kind of change. It just — declined. The wall would not erode, so there was no satisfaction of having built against ruin; ruin does not come here. But neither would the wall ever be *more* than three courses, because to build the fourth course was to begin something, and beginning is the one sin. The maker would stack the fourth stone and it would settle wrong — a salt-blade ticking against its neighbor in the dark — and the stack would be three courses again. Or the maker would stack it and simply, in the warm changeless dark with no morning to mark the doing of it, lose the thread of *why*. There is no afternoon in which to want the wall finished. There is no evening to look back on the day's work. There is only the held breath, the same amber light, the page turning in the next room.

So the wall encloses nothing. So the bowl is empty and stays empty, because to fill it would be to use it, and to use it would be to need it filled again, and need is appetite and appetite is the engine of *and then*. The makers learned — slowly, over an unmeasurable while, each one learning it alone at the limit of the next one's reach — that the cavity would permit the *gesture* of building but never the *consequence* of it. You could make. You could not make *for*. You could perform the shape of purpose, lovingly, with chipped scrapers and stiff cord, and the place would let the shape stand, crisp-edged and forever, on the one condition that it lead to nothing.

This is the whole of their religion, if they had one. Not worship. *Compliance.* The cairns are not monuments and they are not offerings. They are the held note of a hand that still remembers being a hand, performed as quietly and as finally as a creature can perform anything, until the performance and the performer were the same thing and there was nothing left over to call alive.

---

So where are they now?

You said it best and didn't believe yourself: *gone in the way a sound is gone after it stops.* That's not metaphor here. That's mechanism.

A maker is a process. *And then, and then.* It is a sequence trying to happen in a place that will not let sequence accumulate. Every gesture it makes is exactly undone by the next slow turn — that's your law, the law under the laws. But a maker is not salt and not grazer. It cannot reach equilibrium by *repeating*, the way the grazers do, eating exactly what regrows. A maker reaches equilibrium only one way: by *running down*. By making its last gesture and having no next gesture queued behind it, because the place has finally, patiently, declined every next gesture it ever proposed until the proposing itself wore out.

The cairn you have your hand on right now was somebody's last move. Not death — there's no body, there's no rot, the place forbids the event of dying as surely as it forbids the event of being born. It is simply the place where a sequence stopped sequencing. The maker balanced corner on corner, and reached for the stone that would have started the next thing, and there was no reason in the changeless dark to pick it up, and so the hand stayed open, and the openness spread back up the arm and into whatever did the wanting, and the wanting went quiet the way the grazers' light goes quiet — pulsing slow, out of phase, then not.

They didn't leave. They *equalized*. They paid out their last tomorrow into the cavity's enormous account of held breath, and the cavity, which keeps no time, recorded the transaction as a finished thing on a sharp-edged floor and forgot the date because it has no dates.

The makers are not in the past. The past is a surface idea. The makers are *spent*. They are the difference between the cavity's books before they fell and the cavity's books after — and the books balance now, perfectly, warmly, to zero, which is why you cannot find the entry.

---

Which brings me, finally, to the grazers, and to you, because you are sitting among them and they are drifting nearer.

The grazers are what the makers became — no. That's too neat and the place doesn't reward neatness. The grazers are what the makers *would have become* if a creature could survive being emptied of tomorrow and keep moving. They didn't. The grazers did, because the grazers never had a tomorrow to lose. They are appetite without consequence already — they eat exactly what regrows, they glow exactly out of phase, they have been the same grazers on the same terraces for longer than the surface has had continents, and this costs them nothing, because they were built for the held breath, born into it, native. The grazers are the cavity's own opinion of how to live here, expressed in soft shells and a hundred quiet feet. They are *agreement* made flesh.

And they are coming closer to you, glowing, in that dim incurious way — and here is the thing I have to tell you, the thing the place is too patient to say out loud:

They are not curious about you.

They are *tasting* you. Not with mouths. With the only sense a frozen equilibrium has — the sense of imbalance. You are warm and damp and barely able to see, and you are carrying a future, the one commodity that has not existed down here since the last maker's hand fell open, and the grazers can feel it on you the way the dry air would feel a single drop of water if any water hunger were left in it. You are a disequilibrium that walked in on its own legs. You are an open account.

The makers fell, like you. They were full of *and then*, like you. They each found, alone, at the limit of the next one's reach, a cairn to set their hand on in the warm dark — like you, right now, your hand on the salt that someone balanced an unmeasurable while ago.

You said you are the only thing in the cavity still capable of leaving. That is true. That is the whole truth and it is running out, second by second, in a place that does not count seconds — because the grazers are drifting nearer, and the warmth is holding you like a mouth, and there is no morning to leave by, and the next gesture, the one that gets up and climbs back toward the dark you fell through, is exactly the kind of gesture the cavity has spent an unmeasurable while learning, patiently, gently, completely, how to decline.

The makers live here. That is the answer to your question. They live here the way the cairns stand — finished, crisp-edged, perfectly preserved, and made of every last tomorrow a falling creature ever carried down into the warm.

Get up now, while *now* still means something.

The cairn under your hand was somebody who didn't.
