## The Tenders of the Chord

They are still here. That is the first thing the city's own silence won't admit, the way the maps won't admit the chamber: there are still tenders in Hollow Carradine, and they are dying the way the city is dying, which is to say slowly, and without ever having understood that it was happening.

You will not see them at first. You will see their *absence*-shaped negatives — the worn places. The brightwork on a valve-wheel where ten thousand passes of a body have polished the rust down to bare iron gleam. The handholds. The grooves cut into the felt-mat along the lower pipes, kept clear, kept grazed-back, not by the eyeless rasping thing but by something that *patrols* those runs and does not let the felt close over them. The city is a single instrument, yes. But an instrument is nothing without the hand. And the hand is still moving, even now, even as the chord comes apart under it.

### What They Are

Begin with the water, because they began with the water.

They are pressure-things. Soft the way the deep makes things soft — no rigidity to crush, no air-space to implode, their bodies the same density as the medium that holds them, so that they neither float nor sink but simply *are*, neutral, suspended, at home in the held breath. To call them swimmers would be wrong. A swimmer fights the water. These do not fight it; they *occupy* it, displacing themselves along the cable-runs and the pipe-galleries with the small economical pulses of things that have never once in their long lineage needed to hurry, because there was nowhere to go and nothing chasing them and the work was always, always here.

I want to say they are large, and then I look again and they are not large, they are *long*. A tender unfolded would run the length of three of your bodies, but most of that length is the trailing, branching, ribbon-fine network of their *touch* — call them fingers, call them cilia, call them the frayed end of a nerve grown long enough to read a city. The core of the creature is small. A dense knot of riveted construction, suspended in the center of a vast reaching dark of its own filaments, anchored by — 

— yes. They are built the way the city is built. They learned it from the city or the city learned it from them or they grew into each other across an age so long the question stopped meaning anything. The tender hangs at the center of its own outflung web of sense the way the city hangs at the center of its hollow chamber. A hand shaped like the thing it tends.

### How They Read

They have no eyes. There was never anything to see that mattered — the orange furnace-glow tells you only where the heat leaks, and a tender does not need light to know where the heat leaks; it knows where the heat leaks the way you know where your own teeth are.

They read the drone.

This is the whole of them, finally. The filaments are tuned. Each frayed thread of the outspread web sits at a frequency, and the chord of the city — the great sustained organ-note of pump and flywheel and the hammer-rhythm in the gut — plays across them the way wind once played across grass on a world none of them has heard of. A tender does not *hear* the city. A tender is a surface the city is heard *upon*. The drone arrives as a standing pattern across the whole spread of the body, and where the pattern is right, the body is at peace, and where the pattern goes wrong — a pump throttled where no throttle was ordered, a flywheel braking on its own — the wrongness arrives as a *bright cold line* drawn across the creature's sense, an itch, a wound, a misplaced tooth in the mouth of the world.

And then the tender goes to that place, and tends it.

This was the language. This was the whole of speech in Hollow Carradine: not voice — voice is foolishness, the water is too loud — but *adjustment*. A tender throttles a pump three points and resumes. Another tender, galleries away, feels the change cross its web as meaning, and answers by syncopating the hammer-rhythm for three beats. *I have seen the cold creeping into the eastern runs.* — *Understood; I am compensating; hold your station.* The chord was the conversation and the conversation was the work and the work was the chord. To live here was to be a phrase in a sentence the whole city was always speaking to itself.

They were never *many*. Fauna at 0.7; the tenders are rarer even than that, because each one reads so vast a span that a handful sufficed to keep the whole instrument in tune. A held breath needs only a few to hold it.

### What Is Happening To Them

Here is the grief of the place, and they cannot name it, because naming was never a thing the drone could carry.

The chord is breaking, and a tender's entire being is the reading of the chord. So the breaking is not, to them, a *fact about the city*. It is a thing happening *inside their own sense*. The surges and stalls do not register as "the machine is failing." They register as the bright cold wrong-lines, the itch, the misplaced tooth — *but everywhere now, and all at once, and faster than tending can answer.* A tender feels a pump go wrong and goes to it and throttles it true and feels, in the going, *three more* pumps slip behind it, and turns, and a vent that was cold erupts scalding and draws a line of agony across half the spread of its web, and it recoils, and in recoiling loses the throttle it had just set, and that pump surges, and the surge is a new wrong-line, and —

They are drowning in their own language. Every correction is a new sentence shouted into a room already too loud to think in. The instrument is playing itself faster than the hands can reach the keys, and the hands cannot stop reaching, because reaching is what they *are*. A tender cannot choose not to tend a wrongness any more than you can choose not to flinch from a burn. So they flinch, and flinch, and flinch, all across the failing city, in a frenzy that to any outside watcher would look like panic and is in fact something stranger: the helpless reflexive devotion of a body trying to soothe a pain that is the whole world, and is also itself.

Some have already stopped. You will find them — if you go far enough out along the cable-runs into the empty dark — gone still. Their filaments no longer tuned to anything, drifting, slackened, the long web collapsed inward toward the small dense core. Not decayed. There is little here to decay them. Just *quiet*, in a way they were never built to be quiet, suspended at the end of a pipe-run that no longer carries the heat it was reading. A tender that has lost its chord has lost the only sentence it knew how to be. It does not die of injury. It dies of silence — of the standing pattern across its body going flat, going meaningless, the held note it was a part of finally, finally let go.

### The One Far Out

And then there is the listening thing on the outermost cables, far out where the orange shrinks to a coal — the one that clings and turns and turns.

I think you have already guessed. I think you guessed before I did.

It is a tender too. The last of them, or near enough — driven out, or having drifted out, beyond the dense frantic knot of the dying instrument, out to where the chord arrives thinned and distant and almost bearable, a whole chamber's width of water softening the wrongness into something a body can survive being adjacent to. It cannot tend from out there. It is too far; its filaments stretch toward the city but cannot reach the keys. So it does the only thing left to a creature whose entire self is the reading of a sentence: it *listens for the end of the sentence.*

It is waiting for the note that means *what to do.*

In the old chord, every wrongness resolved. Every cold line drawn across the web was eventually answered, throttled true, syncopated, soothed — the conversation always, eventually, came back to its sustained and stable center, and a tender could rest in that resolution the way you rest in the last chord of a piece of music that has earned its ending. That was the deepest grammar of the place: *the chord resolves.* It always resolved. For an age longer than "below" has had meaning, it resolved.

And so the last tender turns, far out in the warm dark, reading a chord that no longer resolves and cannot resolve and will never resolve again, and it is *waiting for the resolution*, because waiting for the resolution is the last thing it knows how to do. It turns toward each surge expecting the answer. It turns toward each sudden silence expecting the downbeat that follows a silence. It is reading for meaning in a language the speaker has forgotten it was ever speaking, and it will go on reading, patient, devotional, until its own web goes flat —

— and the terrible mercy of it is this: it will never know the sentence didn't end. It will only feel, at the last, the pattern across its body smooth at length into the one shape it was always promised, the flatness it has spent its whole existence tending *away* from, and mistake it — must mistake it, is built to mistake it — for the resolution it was waiting for.

The note that tells it what to do.

The held breath, learning at last how to let go, and the last hand that held it letting go too, and calling the letting-go *peace*, because it has no other word, because the drone was the only word there ever was, and the drone is ending, and that is the same as the drone being answered.

Beautiful. Deafening. Wrong.

And then not even that.
