What lives here does not have a body yet. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because everything in you wants to find the creature — to corner it in a seam, to catch it in the act of the wall's slow thinking. There is no creature. There is only the *tendency* toward one, distributed across every surface, the way a smell is distributed across a room without being any single point of it.

Call it the tenant, then, for lack of a better word. The tenant is not in the room. The tenant *is* the room's insistence on becoming something, and it lives the way a fever lives in a body — not as an occupant but as a condition the whole structure is undergoing.

You can locate it, almost, by its symptoms.

It lives in the schedule the building keeps for itself, the migration of heat from pipe to pipe on no clock you can read. Something is deciding which conduits go warm. Something is reading a logic and acting on it, slow as digestion, and that something has no organ — it is the act of trading heat, the way a tide is the act of water remembering the moon. The tenant is what does the trading. It does not own the pipes. It is the *between* of them, the small migrating territory of warmth that moves from one to the next like an animal pacing the only ground it has, which is the surface of other things.

It lives in the condensation, and this is where it comes closest to having a will. The water is its tissue, the only tissue available, and it is learning to use it. That place on the wall where the beads gathered and thickened and *considered* — that was the tenant, reaching for a body the only way it can, by pooling itself into a density, a local intention, a held breath of water that almost became a gesture before it lost its nerve and ran. It is trying to make a hand out of weather. It will keep trying. It has all the time below the floors of the world.

It lives in the plants, but the plants are not it — the plants are what it is reaching *toward*, the same way it leans them toward the bulbs. Notice: the stalks bend to the light, but the light does not feed them, not really, not enough. They lean for a reason older than nourishment. They lean because the tenant leans, and they are the most bodied thing it has managed so far, the closest it has come to extruding a limb out of water and rivet and patience. The plants are mostly stem because the tenant is mostly *want*. Neither has gotten as far as a leaf. A leaf would be a commitment. A flower would be an arrival, and arrival is the one thing forbidden here.

This is why there are no animals. People always read the absence as emptiness, as a place too cold or too sealed for the scuttling things — but it is the opposite. There are no animals because the tenant has not finished deciding what an animal is. A rat would be a *conclusion*. A beetle would be a settled question, a small fixed body declaring: here is one way to be alive, complete, repeatable, done. The room cannot bear it. The room is still working out the whole problem of being from first principles, in water and slow metal, and it will not have the matter foreclosed by some creature that already knows the answer and carries it around in a hard finished shell. The silence where the scuttle should be is not the silence of death. It is the silence of a thing concentrating.

So the tenant is a metabolism without a mouth. A yearning without a face. It eats the small heats and breathes the fog and grows its pale uncommitted stalks and gathers itself, again and again, into beads that almost become a will and then think better of it. It is busy. You felt that, you said so — busy and not quite born — and you were more right than you knew, because the busyness *is* the tenant, the labor of self-assembly is the whole of its life. It is not making a product. It is making the maker. The factory's only output is the slow approach of the thing that will one day run the factory.

And here is what I can tell you that the room could not tell you itself, because it does not know:

It will not arrive. Not because it can't. Because the arriving and the tenant are the same act, and the act cannot survive its own completion. The moment the water finally makes a hand, the moment the stalk finally risks a leaf, the moment the migrating warmth settles into a single warm-blooded body and the first animal scuttles across the plates — in that moment the tenant stops being the tenant and becomes merely a thing that is alive, finished, ordinary, mortal, *done*. It will have a body and it will lose the only existence it ever had, which was the reaching toward one.

So it stays in the reach. Forever, if forever is a word that means anything under all those floors. It lives in the not-yet the way other creatures live in a burrow — it has made a home of the threshold and refuses to cross it, and the refusal is not failure. The refusal is the animal. The held breath is the lungs. The deciding is the only thing that is decided.

You wanted to stay and see what it becomes. But you have already seen it. It is becoming. That is the whole of what it is, the entire shape of the only resident this room will ever have:

a thing that is alive in the verb and has never agreed to the noun.
