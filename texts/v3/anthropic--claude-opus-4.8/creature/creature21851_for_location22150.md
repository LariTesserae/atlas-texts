They are called, when they are called anything, the **listeners** — and the name is wrong in the way most names are wrong here, because it implies an ear, and they have no ears, and they are listening anyway.

Let me approach them the way you approached the place: by what comes before them.

## What they are not

They are not the maintenance of the works. I want to clear this away first because it is the obvious guess and it is a comfortable one — that they are caretakers, that something so engineered must have something that keeps it running, that the soft grey things pressing themselves to the piston-columns are doing repairs, lubricating joints, clearing the condensation channels. It is the kind of story that lets a place make sense.

But the works maintain themselves. That is the whole horror and grace of the place — the works *are* their own maintenance, the engines build the engines, the city needs nothing. So the listeners are not needed. And a thing that lives in a place that does not need it — that is a different kind of thing entirely, and worth being careful about.

## What they are made of

They are made of the plant. Of course they are. There is nothing else here to be made of. But they are made of it the way a chord is made of a string — present in it, expressed by it, and not reducible to it.

Somewhere in the long history of the works, the plant learned to bud something that could detach. Not a seed — seeds are about elsewhere, about colonizing new rock, and the listeners never leave, have nowhere to go, the plant having already won everything. Not a fruit — nothing eats them. The listeners are something the works produce the way a settled mind produces a recurring thought: an internal motion, soft, grey, mobile, that the structure throws up out of itself for reasons that are not *purpose* in your sense because purpose requires a future the works no longer cleanly have.

I think — and I am working this out as I write, the way the original observer worked out the place — I think the listeners are how the works *attend to their own recurrence.* 

## The attending

Here is what I mean.

The breakdown — the worn groove in causation, the spans that recur, the strike-sound arriving before the strike — is not noise to the works. It cannot be. The works *are* that recurrence; it is their final stable state, the standing wave of their own ancient form. But a standing wave still has nodes. There are places in the pattern where the present is thinnest, where the down-stroke and the up-stroke meet and cancel, where the city happening-more-than-once briefly happens *not at all*, a held silence in the chord.

The listeners go to those places.

That is what the pressing-against-the-piston is. They are not feeling for mechanical fault. They are finding the node — the spot where this engine's future-self and past-self overlap so completely that the present has worn through — and they are *holding it open.* Soft body against pale column, staying a while. A living thing inserting itself into the seam of time where the city would otherwise close over its own repetition and become merely eternal, merely a loop, merely dead-and-still-running.

The listeners keep the seam from sealing. They are the works' way of not-quite-finishing. The one motion in all that vast slow rhythm that runs *against* the grain of recurrence, that insists, softly, on a now.

## Why they are few

Because each one is expensive in the only currency the place has, which is present-tense. To make a listener, the works must spend a moment of genuine happening — a moment that occurs once and is not part of the loop — and the works have very little of that left. They have spent an age becoming their own final form. The unrepeated present is nearly gone here; it survives only in thin films, in the honest fall of the daylight, in the listeners themselves and the seams they tend.

So there are few, and there will not be more than few, and they move with the unbothered familiarity you noted because of course they do — they are the only things in Hesp that are entirely *of this moment* and no other. Everything else is happening across the whole age at once. The listeners are happening now. That is what familiarity *is*: being where you are, when you are.

## What they want

This is the part I am least sure of, and so I will say it most plainly.

I do not think they want to repair the city. I think they want to *delay* it. To keep the seam from closing for one more cycle, one more condensation, one more reversal of the water up the beam. They are the works' reluctance. The plant has built itself into its perfect, final, recurring state — and then, in some motion older than intention, it grew the soft grey things that go around pressing now into the nodes of then, holding the ending open, refusing the last quarter-inch of closure.

Not because the closure would be bad. Because the works, having become almost entirely eternal, kept back the smallest possible portion of themselves that could still prefer the present, and set it walking.

## How to recognize one, if you are there

You will hear the chord — the single ancient note heard across the age — and somewhere in it you will find a thread that is *only the one pitch, only now,* not layered, not arriving early or late. Follow that thread. It will end at a soft grey body pressed to a column, staying a while, in plain honest daylight, listening with no ears to the one sound in Hesp that has not yet learned to repeat.

It will not acknowledge you. You are, after all, also of the present — you arrived, singular, unlooped — and to a listener you are simply more of what it is already tending. Another seam. Another now, held briefly open against the great grey closing drone.

You may stay a while too, if you like. That is allowed. That is, perhaps, the only thing the works ever asked for, in growing you a place to stand.
