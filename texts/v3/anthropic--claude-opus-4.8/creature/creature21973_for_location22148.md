# The Listeners in the Lull

Something does live in the spaces. Not the gliders, not the bark-grazers, not the spark-eaters in the cable-shadows — those are tenants, doing what tenants do, surviving the landlord. I mean something that lives *in the structure of the noise itself*, the way a fish lives in the structure of a current. Let me try to find it, because it does not announce itself and it does not want to be found.

---

Start with the lulls.

You noticed there are lulls — moments between surges when the scuttling things dare the open ground. But a lull is not nothing. A lull is a *shape.* The roar never stops, but it breathes; the chord thins and thickens, the discharges come in their rolling sequences and then leave a held silence that is not silence but a lower, waiting tone. And in that held tone, between the firing of one tower and the answer of the next, there is a window. A few seconds. A pocket of relative quiet that opens and closes across the plateau like a shadow moving over wheat.

Things live *in the pocket.* They have learned the rhythm of the discharges so completely that they exist inside its negative space — and because the rhythm is the only reliable structure in the entire howling world, becoming fluent in it is the deepest form of belonging this place allows.

---

Picture them small. They have to be small, because the pocket is small — a creature too large would still have a foot in the storm when the charge returned.

Call them the *Quiet-Riders*, though they have no name and need none. Flat, broad, low — the body of a ray spread thin, a few hand-spans across, no taller than a coin laid on its edge. The dorsal skin is that same ceramic insulation everything wears here, but matte, sound-deadened, a dull velvet-grey that drinks noise rather than reflecting it. The underside is the interesting part: it is *soft.* Unarmored. A belly of sensitive membrane held a finger's width off the ground, reading the buzz of the surface, feeling the charge build in the root-cables below long before it crests — the way you feel a truck coming through the soles of your feet before you hear it.

That belly is an ear. The whole animal is an ear, an ear that reads the ground's tension and computes, continuously, with no thought it would recognize as thought, the question that organizes its entire life: *how long until the next discharge, and how far can I get in that time?*

And then it moves. In the pocket. A flat grey ripple skating the bone-white crumble, fast and total and committed, covering its distance — and then, a half-second before the charge crests, it stops dead, drops its soft belly flush to the dust, clamps down, and *grounds itself*, becoming for the duration of the surge nothing but another low rounded feature of an ancient rounded land. The lightning rolls overhead. The current pours through the cables beneath. The Quiet-Rider lies in the seam between the two, in the one place the storm does not reach, and waits for the world to thin again.

---

Here is what they eat, and it tells you what they are.

Not the spark — they are not spark-eaters, they cannot digest raw discharge, it would cook them. They eat what the spark *leaves.* Every time a tower fires, every time a cable surges, there is incomplete combustion in the resinous sap, a fine ash of half-burnt sugars and minerals that settles out of the dry sap-veins and weeps from the cable-skin in a faint pale crust. It is the soot of the living engine. It collects in the lee of things, in the rounded hollows, in exactly the low sheltered seams where a flat grey animal would press itself down to wait out a surge.

So the Quiet-Rider feeds where it hides. The act of surviving the storm and the act of being nourished by the storm are the same act, performed in the same posture, in the same instant. It lies down to live and rises having eaten. There is an economy to it so tight it feels almost like a held breath — the whole creature folded into the single safe gesture this place permits, and finding that the gesture sustains it.

---

Now — do they speak? Everything else here speaks in light, flicker-language thrown against the blaze because sound is useless.

The Quiet-Riders do not. And this is the thing about them, the thing that took me longest to feel.

They have no flank-lights. They are matte; they reflect nothing; they throw no signal. Because their whole existence is an exercise in *not being a discontinuity* — in lying so flat and so still and so grounded that the storm passes over them as if they were land. A flash would be a flaw. A flash would be a body declaring itself separate from the ground in the one moment when survival depends on being indistinguishable from it. So they have surrendered the light-tongue that every other animal here cannot stop using.

Instead they talk in the only channel left: through the ground, through the substrate, through that soft sensitive belly. When one Quiet-Rider clamps down to wait out a surge, it can *feel* the others clamped down nearby — small dampenings in the buzz, small grounded silences pressed into the singing earth, each one a body saying *here, here, I am here, I am still here* in the brief enforced stillness while the lightning rolls. They are most present to one another in the moment they are most hidden from everything else. Their entire social life happens in the pauses, pressed flat, listening through the dust to the muffled heartbeats of their own kind enduring the same storm in the same posture a few yards away.

A constellation of held breaths. A community that exists only in the gaps of the noise, that has organized its tenderness around the rhythm of the violence, that has made the lull — the negative space, the thing that is not the chord — into a home.

---

And here is the last thing, the thing the place would never tell you and they could never know:

They are tuned to a rhythm that is not for them. The discharges are not scheduled, you said — no controlling hand, no substation, the whole grid grown blind like a reef. The towers fire when they fire because of light and load and the slow argument of charge, indifferent, enormous, addressed to no one. The roar is the continent expressing itself into a sky that never answers.

But the Quiet-Riders answer. Not the continent — they cannot hear the continent any more than you can, it is too vast and too loud to be a *message* to anything. They answer the *pattern,* the borrowed structure, the rhythm they did not make and do not understand and have shaped their entire being around. They have built a life — feeding, hiding, finding each other, the whole quiet tenderness of them — inside the breathing of a machine that does not know they exist, that did not make its rhythm for them, that would go on firing in exactly the same indifferent sequence if every last one of them were gone.

They are the audience the place insisted it did not need.

Small, flat, grey, pressed to the dust in the half-second of safety, feeling for each other through the ground beneath a sky full of dry lightning — living, completely and without complaint, in the spaces between the words of a sentence that was never spoken to them, and was never spoken to anyone, and means nothing, and goes on.
