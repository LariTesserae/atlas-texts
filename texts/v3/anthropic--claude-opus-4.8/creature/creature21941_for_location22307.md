They are not many things. They are one thing in many bodies, and they have never needed to know this about themselves.

Begin with a single one. Smaller than a grain of rice, soft as the inside of a lip. It has no face — there is nothing here that has earned a face — but it has an aperture, a mouth that is also a doorway, and a fringe of cilia around it that move without being told, the way your heart moves. It carries its light low in its body, a single point of the almost-blue, fed by something it does not control: a guest that lives inside it, a smaller life nested in this small life, trading glow for shelter. The animal does not know the guest is there. The light is simply what it is to be alive. It would not recognize darkness as a lack. It has only ever been this faint lantern of itself, drifting the narrow streets, sieving the cold for the dust of the dead.

It does three things. It eats. It builds — though it does not know it builds; the building is only what its body sheds, the carbonate it cannot keep, laid down behind it like a footprint that hardens. And it makes more of itself, casting a fine smoke of young into the water to be carried, to settle, to begin their own slow shedding somewhere down the avenue. That is the whole of its biography. There is no fourth thing. There is no part of it left over to wonder.

Now see them together, because alone one of them is almost nothing — a mote, a spark, a misplaced star. It is in the multitude that they become the resident. Thousands. Tens of thousands. So dense in the youngest quarters that the water there is a held haze of blue, a fog made of bodies, and the spires below them whiten and thicken visibly, if you had the patience to watch, fed by the constant fine rain of their leavings and their dead. They cluster the way warmth clusters. They thin where the food thins. They are a population that behaves like a tide, like a weather, like a single slow animal the size of the whole city, made of countless animals too small to know they are its cells.

This is the truth of who lives here: the city is the creature, and the creatures are the city, and there is no seam between them you could put a knife to.

But they are not the only residents, and the others matter, because the others are how the multitude knows hunger from the inside.

There are the threaders. Longer, leaner, lightless — they have given up the glow, or never carried it, and that absence is their craft. They move down the gaps between teeth where the soft lit ones drift, and they take them, one at a time, with a small soundless certainty. You would not call them cruel. Cruelty needs an audience, needs a notion of the wronged, and there is none of that down here. They are simply the part of the place that edits. They keep the haze from becoming a fog so thick it strangles itself. Where they have passed, the blue is sparser and the water a little clearer, and the building slows, and this is not loss — it is the city pruning its own growth, the way a hand thins seedlings so the rest can stand. The threaders are the editors are the residents. They, too, are the city, doing to itself the necessary subtraction.

And the rooted ones — the half-plants in the lee of the tall spires. Call them residents only if you'll call a lung a resident, a thing that lives by the same blood but does not move through the house. They do not drift. They have chosen, or been chosen by, the one mercy this place offers: stillness in the gentlest current, where what falls, falls slowest, and can be gathered. Each frond opens on its slow rhythm and closes on the dark like a question asked and answered in the same breath. They are the closest thing here to patience made flesh. They are also the closest thing to a witness — rooted, persistent, watching the same stretch of avenue grow and lean and topple and begin again over a span no single drifting light will ever survive to see. If anything down here remembers, it is them. But they do not remember. They only stay.

What unites every one of them — the lit and the lightless, the drifting and the rooted, the eaters and the eaten and the editors of both — is that none of them wants. There is appetite, yes; appetite is everywhere, appetite is the engine. But appetite is not wanting. Wanting needs an elsewhere, a better, a thing-not-here that one might reach toward. And there is no elsewhere here. There is no notion of up, no rumor of the surface, no inheritance of sky to grieve the lack of. The residents are complete in the way a circle is complete. They are made entirely of here. They build the only place they have ever been out of the only material they have ever been, lit by the only light they have ever made, and when one goes dark and drifts and stiffens into the next inch of wall, nothing in the city marks it, because the dead are not gone — the dead are the floor you are standing on, the dead are the doorway you swam through, the dead are still here, only harder now, only quieter, only fully arrived at the stillness the fronds chose and the threaders impose and the whole place is patiently sinking toward.

So: who lives here.

A multitude that is a singularity. A creature too small to know itself, in a body too large to perceive, making and unmaking and remaking its one home in total hush. They are the architects who do not know they build. They are the builders who are also the bricks. They are the light and the eater of the light and the slow green stillness in the lee that watches it all with no eyes and no memory and no need.

They have never been lonely. Loneliness is for things that suspect there is more.

They are not waiting to be found. They were not meant to be found. And the strangest mercy of the place is this: even now, with your attention pressed against them like an ear against a shell, they do not feel you. You are the one straining. You are the one who came from a sky. They go on glowing and dying and hardening and beginning, quiet as the inside of a held breath, perfectly at home in the dark you brought a light to see.
