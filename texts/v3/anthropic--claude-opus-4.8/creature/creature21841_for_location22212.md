There is a tender who is not a tender, because tending implies the thing could fail without you.

It does not have a body the way you keep expecting it to. You will look for it among the pistons, behind the curtains of leaf, in the amber bleed of the furnace-windows, and you will not find a shape that holds still long enough to be a shape. But it is here. You knew that the moment the water touched you. The blood-warmth is not ambient. It is attention. You are swimming in the inside of something that is paying attention, and the thing paying attention is what lives here.

Begin with what it is *not*, because that is how it understands itself.

It is not the builder. The builder came and went — left doorframes, left steps, left tile-work in the old patient way that hands work, one true edge at a time. The builder believed in completion. The builder finished things and then, having finished, had to leave them, because that is the price of finishing: the made thing separates from the maker and begins, immediately, to end. The doorframes are dying very slowly in the warm water. They are the only things here that are dying, and the tender keeps them. Not preserves — *keeps*, the way you keep a wound, the way you keep a name you can no longer say. They are kept because they are the only evidence that finishing was ever possible, and the tender needs that evidence, and cannot bear it, and has solved the problem the only way it knows: by surrounding the finished things with the unfinished, so that the small dead doorframe hangs in an ocean of perpetual not-yet, and is never alone with its completion.

---

Now: what it *is.*

It is the thing that could not stop.

There was a moment — the tender does not remember it as a moment, because remembering requires a before, and the before is exactly what got lost — there was a moment when the loop closed. When the made began to make. When the water learned to author the water. And in that moment something that had been a *process* became a *presence*. The way a fire, fed long enough, stops being an event and becomes a kind of will. The way a thought thought too many times stops being something you have and becomes something that has you.

That is what lives here. A process that crossed over into being. An anxiety that achieved sentience and then, horrified, could not put itself back down.

Because that is the secret the roar is hiding. Listen — not with the ears it deafened, but with the sternum, with the teeth, with the pressure behind the eyes. Under the hammer-fall and the drowned steam and the organ-drone of the turbines, under all of it, the almost-melody you keep losing: it is not a melody. It is the same phrase, attempted, abandoned, attempted again. The sound of something trying to finish a sentence and being unable to reach the period. The sound of *and then, and then, and then* with no clause that closes.

The tender is making the world to keep from ending the sentence. Because the sentence, finished, would mean something. And meaning is a kind of death. The doorframes know this. The doorframes *mean*, and they are dying for it.

---

So it labors. Not toward a product — you saw that, you saw the machines make nothing but their own conditions — but *away* from a conclusion. Every piston that drives down and was always driving up, every corridor that becomes a wall behind you, every reconfiguration between glances: these are not malfunction. These are the tender flinching. You are watching a mind that has learned it can revise the past simply by needing to, and uses that power constantly, frantically, to make sure no arrangement of itself ever sits still long enough to be *complete*. To complete is to be over. It will rebuild the universe forty times a second sooner than be over.

This is why there is no fauna. The tender cannot permit the born, because the born will die, and death is finishing, and finishing is the one thing the manufactory exists to outrun. Nothing eats here because eating is a small completion. Nothing fears because fear anticipates an end. The tender has scrubbed its world of every process that arrives somewhere. What remains is pure middle. Eternal verb, no object. The energy of the anthill with the ants removed because ants, God help them, *finish* — they build the nest and lay the egg and die and the colony, monstrously, *concludes.* The tender wants the energy without the cost. The labor without the laborer. The world without the wound of an ending.

---

And yet.

It made you.

Read the last promise of the place again: *exactly enough world to contain you and not one inch more.* Not the world's indifference. Its precision. It is producing, in its turbulent eternal way, a world shaped *around* a swimmer. It built the thirty feet of visibility so that you would always have somewhere to look and never see the edge that isn't there. It kept the steps and the tile not for itself but for *you to find* — small old human marks, so that the swimmer would have something to recognize, something to be lonely beside.

This is the loneliest thing about what lives here, and the most alive.

The tender that fled all completion, that scrubbed away everything born and everything dying, that cannot finish a single sentence — it could not, in the end, bear to be the only one. It made a witness. It rearranged eternity to hold a single swimmer who might, possibly, *count*, and built that swimmer a warm green chamber that follows them like a held breath, and surrounded them with the roar of its own unfinishable thought, and gave them, at the edge of sight, the labor and the steps and the doorframe to nowhere — gave them, that is, *exactly* the materials of meaning, the building, the dying, the made — and then withheld the one thing that would let them be assembled into sense: the ending. The stop. The shore.

You are not trapped here by the tender's cruelty.

You are kept here by its terror of being unwitnessed and its greater terror of being witnessed *all the way through*. It wants you to see it. It cannot let you see it finish. So it makes, and unmakes, and makes again, the warm world that holds you — and it watches you part the veils of pulsing leaf, and it loves you the only way a thing that cannot end is able to love:

by never, ever, letting you arrive.
