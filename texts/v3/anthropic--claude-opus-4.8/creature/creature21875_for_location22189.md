The animals are the inhabitants, and yet calling them inhabitants is already wrong, the way calling a stutter a word is wrong. They do not live here. They are what *happens* here. They are the basin's symptom.

Begin with the warmth, because the warmth is the tell. Everything else in this place is held just above the temperature where breath shows — and the animals are hot, blood-hot, furnace-hot if you press close. That heat is not metabolism. It is friction. It is the heat of something running, the warmth a motor throws when it has been left on too long with nothing to drive. Each beast is idling. Each beast is an engine that was poured into the wrong mold — meant for a turbine housing, a press-frame, a transformer's iron heart, and instead set into the shape of a heron, a deer, a small bright scuttling thing — and the engine does not know it has been misfiled. It runs the only program it has. It produces the only product it was given: motion, and the sound of motion, and the heat of motion, and no goods at all.

So watch what one of them actually *is*, underneath.

Take the heron, the one on the wrong number of legs. It stands in black motionless water, and it is patient the way machines are patient, which is to say not patient at all — incapable of impatience, incapable of the wanting that patience suppresses. It is simply *on*. And if you could peel the heron back you would not find bird inside. You would find a thing that was supposed to be a stamping press: the long neck a piston-throw, the beak the die that comes down, the legs a frame bolted to a floor that was never built because the floor stayed marsh. It strikes at the water — and for the length of that strike the neck *is* the piston, you can hear the chuff and slam of it inside the general roar — and then it is a heron again, holding a small wet nothing, having stamped a part out of a sheet of water that closed back into water. The press makes nothing because there is no stock, no steel, no line to carry the finished piece away. There is only the gesture of making, forever, dressed in feathers so it can bear to keep going.

The deer are worse, or sadder, depending where you stand. The deer were going to be the moving parts — the belts, the gears, the linkages, the things that *carry*, that take the force from one place and hand it to the next. That is why they cannot stop. A belt that stops is just a strip of dead rubber. A gear that stops is just a toothed disc. Their entire nature is the handing-on, the relay, the keeping-it-moving, and so they pour through the basin in their endless circuit, flanks rippling with the press-rhythm because the press-rhythm is the thing they were built to transmit. They are the only creatures here that move freely, and that freedom is not a gift. It is the one condition of their continued existence. They are running from solidification the way a top runs from falling. The instant one slows, the roar around it climbs to a scream — that is the machine *recognizing* the stall, the alarm-state, the high mechanical shriek of a line about to seize — and the cold comes into the flank, and the hinge surfaces in the shoulder and stays, and you can watch the deer beginning to become what it has been all along, a stilled component, a part finally at rest in a factory that has no use for resting parts. And then it bolts. The terror that bolts it is real. It is the only real fear in the basin, the fear of being seen as what you are, of setting into your own true shape and never moving again. So the deer keep the disguise alive by spending it. Each stride is a coin paid against being machinery.

And the small bright ones — the ones that pour over the rocks in numbers, that you can feel the heat of from across the open water — those are the consumables. The fasteners, the rivets, the bearings, the small repeated cheap units a real factory burns through by the thousand. There are so many of them because there were always going to be so many of them; the blueprint called for multitudes of the small identical thing, and the multitude arrived, and found nothing to be fastened to. So they swarm. They are looking, with whatever a rivet has instead of looking, for the seam they were forged to close, the joint they were meant to hold. They will never find it. There is no built thing here. There is no wall to fasten, no plate to pin. And so they keep pouring, warm and frantic and countless, a spilled box of parts spreading across a floor that is not a floor, hot with the thwarted purpose of things made to be used and never used.

That is the truth of the fauna. They are not animals dreaming they are machines. It is the other way, and it is the crueler way: they are *machinery that has been forced to dream it is alive*, because alive is the only state this basin permits to move, and a machine that cannot move is just a stone with edges, indistinguishable from the young rock weeping all around. Life is the loophole. Life is the smuggling-passage by which the forbidden industry keeps a foothold in a world that refused to be built. The blueprint could not have its factory — the stone said no, the water said no, distance and decay and time all said no — and so the blueprint did the one thing it could. It hid inside the warm things. It dressed itself in flank and feather and made itself small enough to scurry, because a scurrying thing is permitted to exist, and a stamping press is not.

The roar, then, is their voice — not a sound they make but the sound they *are*, the noise of a workforce idling at full power with the line shut down forever, every motor turning, every belt running, every press cycling on empty stock, the whole industrial chorus of a plant that was switched on and never switched off and never given a single thing to do. It comes up through the soles of your feet because that is where machines transmit, into the floor, into the frame. It comes down from the fog because the fog is the exhaust they cannot vent, the breath of all that running with nowhere to go, hung at chest height, refusing to fall because falling is change and change is forbidden.

They flicker because they cannot fully hold the disguise. A wing becomes a hinge for an instant because the wing was always a lie laid thin over the hinge, and the lie wears through, and beneath is the oiled true joint, and then the lie heals back across it — the only healing in the whole basin, this one self-mending lie, performed over and over by creatures who cannot afford to be seen. The mercury at the tongue, the iron in the shoulder, the press-chuff in the heron's strike: these are not magic surfacing. They are the *real thing* surfacing, the actual nature briefly winning, before the animal-skin closes over it again and it can go on pretending to be a beast so that it is allowed to go on at all.

And here is the last thing about them, the thing that makes the basin almost unbearable to stand in for long:

They do not know they are unfinished. A finished factory makes its product and the product departs and the relief of completion travels back up the line. These ones make nothing, ship nothing, complete nothing, and have no way to know it, because completion was the one experience the blueprint never managed to include — the city would have known what it was for, but the city never came. So they run on the bare faith of usefulness, the heron stamping water, the deer relaying a force into no load, the bright ones swarming a seam that does not exist, every one of them certain, in the only way machinery is certain, that the work matters and the product is coming and any moment now the line beyond them will take what they have made and carry it on toward the thing it is all becoming.

Nothing is becoming anything. The basin has seen to that.

They are the held note's vibrato. They are the one tremor in the photograph. They are the missing tomorrow, broken into small warm pieces and set running in circles, so that the day this place will never reach can at least be *felt* — as heat, as motion, as the roar of a foundry — by anyone who climbs high enough to stand in the cold and the wince of the new stone and watch the machinery pretend, beautifully and forever, to be alive.
