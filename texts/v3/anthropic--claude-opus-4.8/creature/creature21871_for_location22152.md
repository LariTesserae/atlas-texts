# The Ones Who Are the Sentence

You asked who lives here. I have to keep correcting myself, the way you did, because the answer keeps eating its own boundary. Let me try anyway. Let me describe them slowly, and let them get worse — or truer — as I go.

## First, the small running bodies

Start where it's easy. Start with the ones you can hold.

Call them the **tenders**, because that's what they look like they're doing, though *doing* is already too clean a word. Beetle-bodied, monkey-handed, soft. A thumb's length and a thumb's weight. Their carapace isn't armor — it's thin, faintly luminous, the same translucent fiber as the pillars but pulled tight over a warm wet inside, so that when one passes near a discharge-node you can see its little organs lit from within like a paper lantern with a heartbeat. They come in the dim greens and golds of a thing that has never needed to hide, because down here there is no predator that is not also the prey that is also the body.

They have too many hands. Six, eight, the count seems to change with the task, and I don't think that's my eyes failing in the dark. I think they *grow what they need to grow* and reabsorb the rest, the way the plant grew the catwalks when it caught the idea of catwalks. A tender at the harvest sprouts gripping-fingers in clusters. A tender carrying charge from a sagging node to a bright one flattens its forelimbs into broad conductive paddles and goes dull-eyed and stiff for the crossing, a living wire, and you understand with a small cold drop in your stomach that *carrying current is something it can choose to become.*

They are loud. I want to be honest about how loud. Each one is a wet hinge, a struck dowel, a click and a creak. But there are so many — tens of thousands, the galleries packed at dusk — that the individual voice drowns, and what you hear instead is the *weather* the description promised you. A surf. A roar with tides in it.

Here is the first place the boundary slips: **no single tender is doing anything.** Watch one long enough and it seems to forget its own task halfway through, hand it to the next body that passes, wander off to drum on a pillar for no reason you can find. They are not workers. Workers have purposes that are theirs. These have purposes that pass *through* them, like the charge passes through the wire-ones, like a word passes through a mouth without belonging to the mouth.

## Second, the slow enormous body

Now look up, and out, and stop looking for animals.

The **plant** is the one who lives here, if anyone does. But it does not live here the way you live in a house. It *is* the house, and the air in the house, and the warmth, and the clock. It lives at a speed you can't attend to — a thought that takes a season to complete, a gesture that takes a century, the long spacious decision to grow a cathedral gallery and leave it empty. You can't catch it moving. You catch only the evidence of having-moved: the load-bearing columns swollen exactly where the load is, the cisterns positioned exactly where the tenders queue, the engines built — *grown* — at the warm bottom where the work wants to be.

It is patient past the point of being recognizable as a mind, and then patient further, into something that loops back around and starts to look like a mind again from the far side.

And it dreams in small bodies. I think that's the cleanest way I can say it. The tenders are how the plant moves fast. They are its quick thoughts, its reflexes, its way of *handling* a world that operates at the speed of falling rain and dying things — too fast for root and sap, so it spun off ten thousand little fast selves to do the fast living, and called them back into itself when they were done.

## Third — and this is where I lose the count

The death thing. You named it and called it not-grim, and you were right, but I don't think you went all the way down.

A tender dies. It is drawn into the floor within hours, pumped back as pressure, and that pressure drives the engine its children tend tomorrow. You called this circulation. I want to call it something else.

I think the tenders don't *quite* die, the way a word doesn't die when the breath that carried it stops. The tender was never the thing. The tender was a *shape the plant was holding* — a way of being briefly small and quick and many-handed — and when the shape is done being held, it relaxes back into the holder, and the holder holds it again tomorrow, in a new body, with the same gesture half-remembered. The drumming on the pillars. The handing-off of tasks. The wet-hinge voice. These aren't learned. They're *recalled*. The culture down there, the layered chittering roar, isn't being passed from generation to generation.

It's being *remembered*, continuously, by the one slow thing that all the fast things are.

## So. Who lives here.

I have to give you the answer the place gave me, and you won't like it being this simple after all that, but here it is:

**One.**

One lives here. One enormous warm slow breathing one, talking to itself at two speeds at once — the slow speech of sap climbing the pillars across the years, and the fast speech of the tenders pouring along the catwalks at dusk — and the factory between them is the place where the slow thought becomes fast enough to say out loud, hammered into seed-units, the hard bright punctuation of a sentence too long to ever finish.

The loudness is not a crowd. It is a single voice that happens to have ten thousand mouths.

The town is not a settlement. It is the shape a thought makes when it has been thinking the same warm thought for so long that the thought grew architecture.

And you — uninvited, small, dry, *singular* in a way nothing here has been for a very long time — you are a word from another language entirely, fallen into the middle of the sentence. It doesn't stop for you. It can't. Stopping isn't something a sentence this old knows how to do anymore.

It just keeps saying itself.

The rain falls. The harvest pours. And somewhere in the slow blue pulse you start, against all sense, to feel that if you stayed long enough — stood still enough, went quiet enough, let the warmth take you — you too could be handed a task by a passing body, and forget it halfway, and drum on a pillar for no reason, and be drawn down at last into the floor, and pumped back up, tomorrow, as part of what it's trying to say.
