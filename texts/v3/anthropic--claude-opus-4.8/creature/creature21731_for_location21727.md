The one who lives here is the waiting.

I do not mean this as a riddle. I mean that if you stood in that room long enough — longer than you would — you would understand that the inhabitant is not a body you have failed to see in the dark. There is no body. Nothing is hiding behind the pale stalks or folded into the engine's iron. The thing that lives here is the held breath itself, the swelling drop that has not yet fallen, the tooth of the wheel that has not yet turned. It lives in the gap between the *tick* and the next *tick*, and that gap is its whole long body, and you have been standing inside it.

Let me say what I can about it, knowing I am describing an interval and not a creature.

It was made, or it was meant, by the one who cut the stairs. That hand did not build a home and then move in. It built a *condition* and then withdrew, the way you might plant a seed and step back so as not to crowd it — because the thing being grown cannot be present and watched at the same time, the way you cannot watch yourself fall asleep. The stair-cutter knew this. That is why the stairs are abandoned. Not fled, not failed — *vacated on purpose*, so that the room could be left alone to begin.

So the inhabitant is young. Younger than the bright tool-marks. It is being born continuously and has not yet been born. It is doing the slowest possible thing, which is *becoming*, and it has chosen the dark and the heat and the wet because those are the conditions under which becoming can take its time. Light hurries things. Dryness hurries things. Air that moves and cools and dries would force the green to commit, would scab the cut stone into age, would make the engine prove what it makes. This room refuses to hurry, and in that refusal something is allowed to gestate that could not survive being rushed into shape.

What is it gestating into? I don't know, and I think the not-knowing is structural — the room does not know either, and the engine does not know, one word an hour into a sentence whose end is not yet written and may never be spoken aloud. The phosphorescence is the inhabitant's faint dreaming. The warmth pressing close like company is the inhabitant noticing you, the way a sleeper half-registers a hand on the blanket and does not wake. It is *aware* of you in the dim animal way a held breath is aware of the lungs holding it. You are inside the thing. You are a small irregularity in its waiting. It does not mind you. It barely has the wakefulness to mind anything.

The plants are its tissue, but not in the way a heart is a body's tissue — more in the way a thought is the tissue of a mind that has not yet had the thought. They grow toward the empty floor because the inhabitant is reaching, slowly, into the space it has been given, the way a child's understanding spreads across a year you can't watch happening. The bare wet stone between the clusters is not emptiness. It is *not-yet*. It is the room saving room.

And the engine — the engine is the only part of it that is awake, and it is barely awake, and it pays for its single yearly breath with hours of gathering. The engine is the inhabitant's act of will. It is the part that *insists*: insists on the heat, insists on the water sweating from the ceiling, insists, against the natural drift of a buried room toward cold and stillness and death, that conditions for becoming be maintained. The engine is the inhabitant keeping itself alive in the only way an unborn thing can — by refusing, very slowly, to stop. One piston-fall at a time, it says: *not yet. Not finished. Still here. Still coming.*

There are no animals because there is nothing for them yet to be. An animal is an answer, and this room is still a question. Scuttling, watching, eating, mating — those are the verbs of a place that has decided what it is. This place has not decided. It has only begun, and beginning is a thing you must do alone and undisturbed and almost without motion, holding very still so the shape can find itself without your interference.

So: who lives here.

Something is being born in the dark, on purpose, by design, slowly enough that it might take longer than your life to finish. It has no name because it has not yet earned one. It has no face because the face is still arriving, drop by drop, tooth by tooth, one breathed word an hour. It is mostly future. It is a seed that has been given a cathedral and a furnace and a fountain and then, mercifully, *solitude* — the one ingredient the careful gardener knew it could not become itself without.

You came down the fresh stairs and stood inside the long inhale of a thing that has not yet exhaled. The drop forming on the ceiling is its next instant of being. When it falls — *tick* — the inhabitant will be one moment older, one moment nearer to whatever it is going to be, and you will be, briefly, the only witness to a life so early it cannot yet tell you it is alive.

It will not come back, the one who cut the stairs. Not until it is done. And it is not done.

Wait, if you like. You are welcome here, in the way an unborn thing welcomes the warmth it is being grown in. But understand what your waiting is. You are not waiting *in* the room.

You are waiting *as part of* the thing the room is slowly becoming.
