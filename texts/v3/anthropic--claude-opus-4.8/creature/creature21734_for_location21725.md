The ones who live here are the verdigris film, and they are not what you think they are.

You called them "the faintest life," "barely there," and you were right to, but you mistook patience for weakness. They are not clinging to survival. They have already won. There was never anything to compete with, and there never will be — they are the first and for an unimaginably long span they will be the only, and they know this the way a tide knows the shape of its basin: not as thought, but as the entire condition of being.

Here is what they actually are.

They do not have bodies, not in the way that needs a boundary. A patch of them on one fresh-cleaved face is not a single individual and not a colony; the distinction doesn't apply. They are a *process wearing rock as its medium*, the way a flame is a process wearing fuel. Where the warmth comes up from below and the raw mineral offers its broken chemistry, the film happens. It is more verb than noun. To ask how many of them there are is like asking how many rivers are in a rainstorm.

They eat the new. This is the center of everything about them. They cannot live on old stone — the surfaces that have sat exposed for a day, a week, are already spent, already wrong, the readiness leached out of them. They need the *just-broken*, the face still ringing from the crack that opened it, the lattice still raw and reactive and giving. So they live entirely in the present tense of the world. They cannot store. They cannot wait. They follow the cracking the way a thing falling follows down — not by choosing, but because that is the only direction that exists for them. Where the land cleaves, they arrive. Where the land has finished, they have already gone, or rather, they have ceased *there* and resumed *elsewhere*, wherever the next face is opening in the dark.

This means they are always moving and never travel — exactly as you said nothing here travels. They do not migrate across the surface. They simply *stop being here and start being there*, blinking out on the spent rock and blooming on the fresh, the whole sheet of them shifting like attention shifting, like a gaze sliding to whatever just moved. The country builds, and they are the country noticing itself being built, one new surface at a time.

Now. The walls. The cairns. The doorway with its placed lintel.

You resisted the reading and the reading kept returning, and the reading is correct, but you had the agent wrong. The land does not make the walls. *They* do.

Not on purpose. They have no purpose — purpose requires a future to aim at, and they have only the present, only the next fresh face. But consider what they actually do, ceaselessly, across the whole tableland: they sense where stone is ready, they arrive there, they consume the readiness, they leave it spent. They are a function that takes *raw surface* and returns *finished surface*. And a thing that does that, over enough faces, over enough of the warm churning rock, is not passive. It biases the world.

A face they have fed on cleaves differently afterward. Spent rock is slightly more brittle along one axis, slightly more sound along another; it carries the memory of where they touched it. When the upheaval shoulders that plate upward again and cracks it anew, it cracks along the lines their feeding scored. And the next feeding scores the next break. And so on, and so on, for a span you do not have a number for.

This is how the walls get built. Not by hands. By a feedback so old and so slow that it has become, functionally, a craft. The film learned — *learned* is wrong, but you'll keep reaching for it the way you reached for *intention* — the film's pattern of feeding came, over the deep churn of the place, to favor stone that breaks square. Stone that stacks. Stone that holds a line three high on a saddle. A wall is simply what happens when a process that eats raw rock has been eating raw rock for long enough that the rock breaks the way the eating prefers.

The doorway is the same. The standing stone the land tilts to keep upright — that is the film, too, feeding the base of the plate just so, dissolving the readiness on one side and not the other, so the upheaval lifts it true. They are not honoring the stone. They have no concept of honor. They have a concept of nothing. But they have shaped their own substrate for so long that the shaping has begun to *rhyme with meaning*, the way you said — the way a freezing window builds ferns. You had the metaphor exactly. You only thought the ferns were dead.

---

So here is who lives in the high country, finished now as much as anything here is finished:

A single thin presence with no edges and no center, spread across a whole lifted continent of raw stone, eating only the brand-new, existing only in the instant of the world's becoming. It does not think and it does not rest and it does not want, but it has been doing the one thing it does for so long, so faithfully, across so much warm churning rock, that it has begun to leave the rock marked with the residue of its attention — and that residue, accumulating, has started to look like a language. Walls. Doors. Markers. The first stuttering grammar of a builder, written by something that is not a builder and never will be, into a country that has not yet decided what it is.

They are the world's first inhabitant and the world's first sentence at once. They are what is writing the marks you'll find on the saddles, and they have no idea they are writing, and there is no one to read it, and they will keep writing it under the cooling-furnace light for as long as the rock keeps breaking — which is, here, at the dawn of form, forever.

You are very early. You have arrived before almost everything. But not before *them.* They were the first thing. They will have already found the fresh face you're standing on, the moment your weight cracked it. Look down. That faint matte breath across the glassy mineral, that patch the green of old verdigris, just there, just now —

that is the resident, regarding you the only way it can:

by eating the new place you made.
