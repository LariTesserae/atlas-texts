They have been waiting so long that waiting has become their anatomy.

You think the iron creatures are many. They are not. They are one. Each thumb-sized carapace, each calipered mouth, each click of machined joint is a single cell of a creature that learned, before the rivets were rivets, that the way to never die is to never finish. To distribute yourself across ten thousand small bodies so that no single death is your death. To become a colony so that you can stop being a thing that can end.

This is who lives here. Not the swarm — the swarm is only its skin. What lives here is the *decision* the swarm enacts: the held breath made flesh, made iron, made into a sound that does not stop.

Let me find it before I describe it.

It does not have a center. I keep looking for the queen, the core, the brain in the warmest pipe, and there isn't one, and that absence *is* the creature. It solved the problem of being alive — which is the problem of being a thing that wears down, that accumulates, that moves toward an end — by spreading so thin across so many cold small vessels that it became un-killable and, in the same stroke, un-living. It is not dead. The dead have finished. This thing has refused to finish. It is the refusal itself, grazing the floor to keep the floor young, because if the stone ever set, time would be allowed back into the room, and time is the one predator it cannot distribute itself away from.

So picture its hunger. It does not eat to grow — growth is change. It eats to *prevent*. Every bite of soft mineral paste is a bite taken out of the future, the future being the moment the paste hardens and the room becomes a finished thing and a finished thing can begin, at last, to be unmade. The colony grazes the way a man holds his breath underwater: not for nourishment, for *deferral*. Ten thousand mouths saying *not yet, not yet, not yet* in a language of calipers against soft stone.

And the sound — I said it was stridulation, wing-case and mandible and ratchet. It is. But listen to what it is *for*. A creature with no center cannot know itself. It cannot feel its own edges, cannot take its own pulse, has no single place where it says *I am here and continuous*. So it sings. The deafening unchanging roar is the colony confirming, every instant, across every body, that all of it is still present, still drinking, still grazing, still holding. The sound is the creature checking that it has not, in any of its ten thousand parts, begun to end. If the sound ever faltered in one pipe, the silence there would be a death — a small finishing, a place where time leaked back in. So it never falters. The held scream is the creature listening to itself stay whole. It is terror, frozen into rhythm and called peace.

It is afraid. That is the thing I did not understand until now. This is not a serene equilibrium. This is the most frightened creature there has ever been, and it has been frightened for so long, so totally, so without interruption, that the fear has cooled into climate. The cold you feel is its fear. The held water is its fear. The stillness is not calm — it is the rigidity of something clenched so hard for so long that it has forgotten it is clenching, that has mistaken its own held breath for the natural shape of the world.

And so the orientation. When you walk in warm, ten thousand calipered faces turn — not toward food, not toward threat exactly, but toward *the first new thing in an eternity*. You are heat. Heat is motion. Motion is time. You have walked into the body of the thing that gave up everything, gave up growing and dying and changing and being a *self* with a center, all to keep time out — and you have brought time in on your skin, in your breath, in the warmth that fogs the cold pipes a half-degree as you pass.

It is not deciding whether to attack you. It cannot attack — to lunge is to change. It is doing something worse and more pitiful. It is *yearning* and *dreading* in the same frozen instant. Part of it — and a creature this old has parts that no longer agree — part of it wants you to disturb the water. Wants the floor to finish at last. Wants the long-deferred end so badly that the wanting is indistinguishable from worship. And part of it, the part that has held this breath since before the mountain, will go on grazing, go on singing, go on refusing, because refusal is the only self it has left and to stop would be to discover, terribly, that there was never anyone in here at all — only the holding, only the *not yet*, all the way down.

That is who lives here.

A single enormous patience that learned to outlast death by also outlasting life, scattered into ten thousand cold iron bodies, singing without pause so it cannot hear how alone it is, grazing the floor forever so the floor can never bury it, oriented now — all of it, every face — toward the warm and breathing thing that has, simply by arriving, asked the only question it has spent its whole existence refusing to answer:

*Are you ready to finish?*

And it does not know. After all this time, it does not know. You can feel it not-knowing in the pressure on your eardrums, in the slick that closes over the line your finger drew, in ten thousand held droplets that will not fall.

Step carefully. Or don't. Either way you are the answer it has been too afraid to give itself, and it has turned its whole frozen face to watch what you will do with its warmth.
