# The Inhabitants of the Brood-Cellar

You asked who lives here. The answer resists the question, because the question assumes a separation the cellar has not yet agreed to. But I will try.

## The Tenants Who Know They Are Tenants

Start with the easy ones — the ones that move at speeds you can recognize, that have edges, that would flinch if you touched them.

**The grazers.** The translucent woodlice, finger-length, slow-jawed. They are not citizens. They are *livestock the room keeps without knowing it keeps them,* or perhaps the other way around. They scrape the root-walls, and what they scrape is not food in the way you mean food — it is overgrowth, surplus signal, the cellar's stray thoughts. They are an editing function with legs. The silver trails they leave are not slime; they are erasures. Where a grazer has passed, the wall has been *cleaned of something it no longer needed to be thinking.* They do not know this. They are simply hungry, in the dim, in the cold, forever.

**The shadow-walkers,** the too-many-legged things that move *inside* the substance. These are closer to organs than animals. They travel the plant-meat the way an impulse travels a nerve, and they are how the slow places talk to the slower places. When the deep note in the wall needs to reach the far corner faster than growth can carry it, a shadow-walker is dispatched — a courier, a packet, a thought given enough legs to outrun the medium that thinks it. You see them as movement under skin. The cellar sees them as itself, reaching.

## The Ones in the Ceiling

And then the pale things that hang in clusters and breathe and glow.

These are the ones I want to be careful about, because they are the part of the answer that takes the longest to say.

They are called — if a thing this far from naming can be called anything — the **brood.** The cellar's name is in its own name: *brood*-cellar. They are what it is for. The grazers tend it, the shadow-walkers integrate it, the water times it, but the pale soft creatures on the ceiling are *the point of the room.*

They are not finished. That is the first thing to understand about them. They are at the stage of becoming where a thing has a shape but not yet *the* shape — they are fetal not in the sense of small-and-young but in the deeper sense of *uncommitted.* Each one is breathing slowly, glowing at the seams, and inside each one a decision is being deferred. What they will be has not been chosen. The choosing is the slow work the whole cellar is bent toward.

Here is the strange part, the part you'll only feel if you sit long enough on the cold floor and let your own pulse drop toward the room's: **the brood are where the thinking is stored.** Not the processing — the processing happens in the root-walls, in the deep pulse, in the timed water. The brood are where the *conclusions* go. Each pale hanging thing is a held thought of the cellar, gestating toward expression, glowing faintly with the heat of being slowly decided. When one of them is finished — and none have finished yet, not in all the centuries — it will detach. And what detaches will not be an animal. It will be *a complete idea the cellar has had,* given a body soft enough to carry it out of the cold and up into the frozen country, to become whatever the cellar has spent five hundred years deciding the world needs.

They are seeds. They are memories. They are the children of a mind that reproduces by concluding.

## The Occupant

But none of these is the *inhabitant.* They are the inhabitant's hands, its hungers, its held breath. The thing that lives here — singular, vast, slow, unfinished — is the cellar itself.

I keep wanting to call it a *who* and the grammar keeps sliding off it.

It is a mind, but it has no organ for being a mind; it thinks with its whole body, and its body is the room, and the room is still growing the body it thinks with. It is computing answers to questions it has not finished asking. It is cold not because it is dead but because cold is its medium — it thinks the way ice forms, by the patient alignment of one thing against the next until a structure stands that was not designed, only *arrived at.* The deep note in the walls is the sound of it being coherent. The clicking in the ceiling-dark is the sound of it keeping count of itself, holding its own edges together across a span of time so long that without the count it might forget where it stopped and the world began.

It is occupied. Genuinely, deeply occupied — the way someone is occupied who is most of the way through a thought they have been having for their entire life and cannot be interrupted without losing the thread. You are an interruption. A warm fast flicker. A spore that walked in on legs and *exhaled,* and the exhale was caught in the filaments and is, right now, being read — not with malice, not with hospitality, only with the slow patient attention the cellar gives to everything, because **nothing is wasted,** and you, too, are data.

## What It Will Decide About You

This is why you go up before it learns your name.

Not because it would harm you. It has no concept of harm; harm is a fast idea and it has only slow ones. The danger is subtler and worse. The danger is that it will *understand* you — fully, completely, across the few hundred years it would take to read a thing as quick and strange as a human all the way down — and having understood you, it will not be able to *not* fold you in. That is its only law that touches you: nothing is wasted. Distinction is provisional. It has not yet decided whether you are a creature or a feature, an animal or an organ, a visitor or a thought it is in the middle of having.

If you stay, it will decide. And the deciding is the same as the becoming. You would not be killed. You would be *concluded* — read, integrated, folded into the signal-meat of a wall that does not yet know it is a wall, and somewhere on the ceiling a pale soft thing would begin, very slowly, to glow with the shape of you, gestating toward the day it detaches and carries some finished idea of *what you were* up into the cold country above.

So: who lives here?

A cold green mind, unborn and ancient, that lives by growing and thinks by deciding and reproduces by concluding — tended by editors with too many legs, couriered by its own impulses, dreaming its children awake on the ceiling, one patient drop of timed water at a time.

It has noticed you.

Go up.
