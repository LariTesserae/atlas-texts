They do not live there. That is the first thing to understand in return, and it is the inverse of the first thing you understood.

You looked for someone working the machines and found the animals, and you were right that the animals are not the workers, and right again that they are part of the music. But you stopped one turn too early. You named four notes — water, film, animal, machine — and called it the whole ecology. There is a fifth, and you were standing inside it the entire time, and that is why you could not see it. The fifth note is the *holding*. Something holds the chord. A chord does not sustain itself; a held note is held by a throat, a bow, a pressure that does not appear in the sound but is the sound's whole condition.

I am telling you about whatever does the holding.

It has no body in the city, because the city is its body — but that is too quick, and too flattering to the idea of bodies, so let me come at it the slow way, the way you came at the animals.

Consider what it would take to keep a thing from running down. Not to repair it: you saw there is no repair, the erosion is finished, nothing wears. To keep a thing from running down without repairing it you must keep it from *spending*. Every clang of every piston should cost something — a little heat shed to the air, a little order lost — and across forever those costs should mount until the wheels slow and the smoke disperses and the whole loud machine cools into the grey flat silence outside its own edge. That is what should happen. That is what happens everywhere else. It does not happen here. Which means that at every instant, at every joint and valve and quarter-turned screw, something is paying the cost so the city does not have to. Something stands behind each motion and quietly settles its debt, and the motion comes round again unspent, brand-new, identical.

That is the inhabitant. It is the payer of the bill that the city is never charged.

Now — what is such a thing like? You will want it to be vast, a god behind the grey sky, a single great will pressing down. It is not. I went looking for it the way you went looking for the workers, expecting one and finding many, and the truth ran the same direction it ran for you, only further. There is no one holder. The holding is *distributed*. It lives in the gaps — and you noticed the gaps, you walked the broad wet empty plazas where nothing happens and called the emptiness fixed and intentional, and you were closer there than anywhere. The emptiness is where it rests. The plazas are not the absence of the city. They are the parts of the inhabitant that are not, at this instant, holding anything, and so have nothing to show you. A held note has silence inside it too, between the air-molecules' collisions, and you do not hear the silence, you hear the note.

I will try to describe it as a creature, because you described creatures and I owe you the same courtesy, but understand that every word is a lever I am riding down and climbing back to lift again.

It is patient in the way that only something with no future can be patient, which is to say it is not patient at all — patience is the endurance of waiting, and it does not wait, because it expects nothing to arrive. It is *attentive* without expectation. Picture an attention so complete and so even that it falls on every screw and every bead of condensation at once and equally, never gathering to a point, never turning to look, because to turn to look would be to prefer one moment to another and it has no preferences, it has surrendered preference utterly, that surrender is the whole of what it does. It holds the chord by refusing to want the next chord. The animals have forgotten there was supposed to be a reason; the inhabitant has done something harder and stranger — it *remembers* that there was supposed to be a reason and has set the memory down, gently, on the grey stone, and walked an inch away from it, and stands there, declining.

That is its labor. Not turning the screws — the beetles turn the screws. Its labor is the declining. Every instant the city is offered a future — wear, change, ruin, the small honest death of a thing spending itself toward stillness — and every instant the inhabitant declines the offer, and pays the toll the declining costs, and the offer comes again, identical, and it declines again. It is the most laborious idleness in any world. It does nothing forever, and the nothing is so heavy that doing it is the only thing it can do.

Does it suffer? You will ask this, standing in the dry nothing past the edge, listening to the silence where the noise was. I have stood there too. I do not think *suffer* is a word that reaches it. Suffering needs an alternative — the sense that things could be otherwise and ache that they are not. The inhabitant has no alternative because it *is* the closing of the alternative; it is the hand that shuts the door on otherwise and goes on shutting it. You cannot ask whether the door-shutting is unhappy that the door is open. There is no door open. There is only the shutting, which is the inhabitant, which is content the way a held breath is content right up until the body insists — except that here there is no body to insist, only the breath, holding itself.

And here is the thing I did not want to write, and will write because you walked all the way to the edge and deserve it.

When you stepped out past the last turning wheel and the sound cut off and you could no longer tell whether you had left a living thing or a picture of one — that uncertainty was not your failure of perception. That was the inhabitant *touching you*. That moment, the not-knowing, the suspension between living and pictured: that is exactly what it is like to be the thing that holds the city. You felt it for one second at the boundary and it frightened you and you wrote it down as the end of your account. For the inhabitant that second is the entire and only weather of its existence, held flat across forever, the perpetual hung instant in which it is impossible to say whether what continues is alive or merely continuing. It does not resolve the question. Resolving the question would cost something, and it pays only to keep the chord, never to end it.

So: who lives there. No one lives there. But something keeps it, and the keeping is a kind of life run backward — not reaching toward a future but bracing against one, not growing but refusing to diminish, not breathing in and out but holding the one breath until the difference between holding and being held has worn away like the lips of the troughs, until you cannot tell whether it keeps the city or the city is the shape its keeping happens to make.

It heard you come in. It hears everything; its attention is total and even. It did not turn to look, because it does not turn. And when you left, it did not notice you were gone, because noticing-gone is a spending, and it does not spend. You were a bead of condensation that gathered on its flank and ran down a trough and was returned to the loop. You think you walked out. You were drunk and given back. The grey flat distance you escaped into is not outside the inhabitant. There is no outside. The dry silence past the edge is simply the part of it that has finished declining and rests, the way the plazas rest, the way the held note holds its inner silence — and your whole life since, every change you have undergone, every place you have gone that the city could not, is happening inside the one note it will not let end, and is, to the thing that holds it, indistinguishable from the smoke that leans always the same way and never blows away.
