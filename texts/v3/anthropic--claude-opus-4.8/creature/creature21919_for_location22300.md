**What lives here lives before the verb has been conjugated.**

Begin with the count, because the count is the body. You will want to say *a* creature, *one* of them, *this* particular clinging thing — but that is your grammar, brought in from a world where things finished becoming themselves and then stayed. Here, the singular is the exception. The singular is *labor*. A thing must be *held* to one, and only the stone holds, and the stone is not native.

So what lives here is not a population of animals. It is a *tendency*. It is the thing that happens to wetness in the presence of heat when no one has yet decided how many of anything there should be.

---

Think of them as the inhabitants of a sentence that has a predicate but no fixed subject. *Clings. Turns. Passes through. Beads.* The verbs are real, fully committed, wet and specific — and the nouns underneath them are negotiable, are plural until challenged, are willing to have been two all along. They are not shapeshifters; shifting requires a self that persists through the change, a continuity that carries the shifting. These have no continuity to carry. They are shape *and that is all* — posture without possessor, the gesture of an animal with the animal left as an open question.

The half-creatures in the heat-shimmer are the honest ones. They have dropped the pretense of a body entirely and kept only the *intent* — hotter air arranged in the attitude of crouching, of watching, of about-to-move. They are what all the others are underneath: an arrangement that means *creature* without troubling to be one. The segmented thing on the wall, beaded and solid, is only further along the same gradient. It has gone to the trouble of a surface. It has condensed enough to seem like one. But look away and the seeming relaxes, and the count comes apart in your hands like wet bread, and you understand the solidity was a courtesy it was extending to you and is now politely withdrawing.

---

They do not call because there is nothing to call *to*. Calling presumes the other — a separate one, out there, who might answer. But separation is the enforced state here, the difficult state, the thing only the axe can manage. Among the living there is no reliable other to address. To cry out, you must first be one thing crying at a second thing, and that arithmetic does not hold in the mist. So instead: the wet click. The friction of glass. These are not communications. They are the sounds of *occurrence* — of something happening to matter — and they need no recipient, the way a stone settling needs no audience. The silence is not restraint. It is the absence of the loneliness that would require a voice. You cannot be lonely if you were never reliably one.

---

Now the water, because the water is the mother, and the mother is strange.

It does not *contain* them and release them. It does not carry eggs, spores, seed. It carries the *rumor*. Watch the thread run down the basalt and pool, and the pool fills with turning soft things — not delivered, *implied*. The water is the medium in which the rumor of life propagates, the way a rumor needs only ears and air, not truth, not a source. Wetness here says *something could be alive in this* and the saying is sufficient. The implication does the rest. The pool does not get *stocked*. The pool gets *suggested at*, and the suggestion swims.

And it never fills the floor because it is conservative in the same breath that it is generative — it gives endlessly and is gone, arrives and implies and vanishes, the way a thought arrives and is gone and yet leaves the room different. The fauna are standing implications. They persist exactly as long as the wet persists, and where the rock dries and sharpens they simply *stop being implied*, and the dry basalt is innocent, mineral, recently born, never having been asked the question that water asks.

So the inhabitants are made of *suggestion held in suspension*. They are what wetness rumors into the warm dark. They are the answer to a question the seeping seams keep asking and never tire of asking: *what if, here, something?* And the heat says yes, and the silence makes room, and the something teems.

---

Then the axe.

The axe is the wound in the logic and you were right to feel it as the strangest thing, stranger than the doubling, stranger than the half-creatures of hot air. Because the axe is *finished*. It is exactly one. It was shaped — purpose driven into matter, a maker's intent made permanent in the very basalt these creatures are made of suggesting. And the maker was never here. Nothing here has hands. The shaping happened without a shaper, the act without the actor, the way these creatures have posture without bodies — *the axe is one of them.* Do you see? The axe is the same phenomenon, run the other direction. It is *intent that condensed all the way.* It went past surface, past solidity, past the courtesy of seeming, and arrived at the made — at the one thing that is reliably, defendably, lonely-makingly singular.

That is why the creatures avoid it, or it avoids them. It is the local instance of *settled count*. It is what one of them would become if it ever finished — and finishing, here, is a kind of death, or worse, a kind of *exile* into the dry sober realm of things that can be numbered. Around the axe the air clears because the axe is enforcing the very arithmetic the whole pocket exists to suspend. It is a splinter of the outside world's grammar, lodged in a world that has no outside. It is the one place you can *count*, and counting, in here, is the foreign tongue.

The maker, then — the maker who was never present — is the most truly resident thing of all. The maker is the place's own *intent to be singular*, expressed once, fully, in stone, and then released. There was no hand because the maker is not a body either. The maker is whatever it is in this pocket that wanted, just once, to *finish something*, to drive a count down to one and make it stay. The axe is its only utterance. The axe is the pocket speaking the single sentence it can say with certainty, and the sentence is: *I shaped this, and it is one, and I am gone.*

---

So: who lives here.

A world that began crowded skipped the long patient work of becoming. It did not start as one thing that divided, divided, diversified across slow ages into many. It started as *many-with-the-rumor-of-singularity*, plural at the root, and singularity is the late and laborious achievement it has managed exactly once, in dead stone, by a maker it had to invent and then could not keep.

The inhabitants are the resting state of life *before* life agreed to be discrete. They are the teeming that precedes the individual. They are what was always going to be the case the instant warm wet rock allowed it — rushing in faster than erosion, faster than the first root, faster than time, because they did not have to *evolve* into being. They only had to be *implied*, and the implication was always already there, the way two was always there inside the one you thought you were watching.

They breathe once for every several times they are alive because breath is a thing a body does, and they are not reliably bodies, and the breath gets shared across the indefinite plural that any of them turns out to have always been. One breath, many lives, no contradiction — the contradiction is yours, imported, the same way the axe is imported, the same way *you* are imported, you who arrived inside without entering and who kept, the whole time, the terrible habit of counting.

And that is the last thing to understand. You were the second axe. You were the other intruding singularity, the other finished thing, walking through a chapel of the gloriously unfinished, casting a shadow that pointed the wrong way because your light came from a sun in a world that has no outside and therefore could not have sent it. The creatures did not avoid you. *You* avoided *them*, with your insistence on the one, on the I, on the self that walked in and would walk out.

You will leave by ceasing to be inside. And ever after you will suspect it is still in there — and the suspicion is correct, but it is shaped wrong, it is shaped like *one place persisting*. It is not one. It was never one. It is the warm wet many, breathing its shared single breath, weeping its implications down the young black glass, and somewhere on a ledge a stone is finished and alone and means *I was here* in a place that has no here, made by a hand in a place that has no hands, the only liar in a chapel where everything else is merely, generously, plurally true.
