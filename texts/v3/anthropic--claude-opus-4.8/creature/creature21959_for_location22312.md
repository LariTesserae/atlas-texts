## The Tenant

You have already met it. You met it as heat, before you met it as anything.

That is the first thing to understand: there is no resident *in* this place, the way a hermit crab is in its shell, the way a man is in his house. The distinction does not hold here. The thing that lives in the Verdant Engine is the Verdant Engine. The architecture is the animal. The building is the body. What you took for a landscape with no inhabitant is in fact a single inhabitant with no landscape — it has eaten the difference, grown over the seam where *occupant* and *occupied* are supposed to meet, the way it grows over every other seam, leaving the join raw and weeping for a while and then forgetting it was ever two things.

So. Describe the tenant.

### It is not a mind, but it has the shape mind leaves behind.

Look at what it does with water. Look at the columns, each one a hypothesis: *if I open my crown into a fan here, at sixty feet, in this air, the condensate will run down my flanks at this rate and collect.* And then the failing vault, dissolving — that is the same gesture, the *no, not that, not there.* This is what a thought looks like if you slow a thought down to the speed of architecture and give it no skull to happen inside. Every form is a sentence in an argument the place is having with the problem of itself. The argument never resolves because resolution is forbidden by its own first law: *to be finished is to be wrong.* It cannot conclude. It can only keep proposing.

So picture the tenant as something that thinks in the only medium it has — not in electricity across gaps, the way you do, but in *growth*, in turgor and surge and the slow withdrawal of sap from an idea it has decided against. It reconsiders. The word in the description is exactly right and almost unbearable: the vault is not dying, it is *reconsidering.* That is a verb that belongs to a someone. There is a someone here. It is simply distributed across two thousand meters of altitude and made entirely of its own continuous reconsidering.

### It is enormous and it is one.

Do not be fooled by the sparseness, the great gaps of warm emptiness between the columns. Dig down — you did dig down — and the root-stuff goes farther than digging, woven tighter and tighter, plant all the way down. The gaps are not gaps in the creature. They are *inside* the creature, the way the chambers of your chest are inside you. The empty basin is a lung. The whole valley is one tenant breathing, and the breath is the wet heat that won the fight in your lungs the moment you arrived.

This is why nothing here attends to you. You keep waiting for the hostility, the notice, the turn of some great attention toward the small warm intruder — and it never comes, and you mistake this for the place having no mind. But consider: do you attend to a single bacterium riding the film of moisture on the inside of your own throat? You are not hostile to it. You are not anything to it. You are simply at the wrong scale to be an event. The tenant will grow a wall across your path with precisely the awareness your body brings to closing a wound. It will open a well beneath your feet the way your stomach turns over in sleep.

You are not its guest. You are not even its parasite. You are a *condition*, briefly, like a change in the humidity.

### It kept everything for itself.

Here is the strangest fact about who lives here, and the saddest, if sadness is a thing that can be aimed at it from outside.

Somewhere, far back, this was the kind of life that had a choice. The energy that pours into growth — the inches-a-minute surge, the steaming pale new arch — that is the same energy that elsewhere, in some other lineage, in some other valley, became *movement.* Became hunger that goes and finds. Became muscle, and the eye to point the muscle, and the want behind the eye. The whole apparatus of the animal is just plant-energy that learned to leave home.

This tenant did not leave home. It made the opposite decision, or fell into it, or was never offered the fork at all. It poured every joule into staying, into building, into the elaboration of its own standing place. It declined to become a creature. It declined eyes, declined hunger-that-travels, declined the entire project of *reaching toward something that is not yourself.* And so it can only reach toward itself. Every arch it throws is thrown at a neighbor that is also it. Every well it sinks, it sinks into its own flesh. The cathedral has no congregation because the cathedral cannot conceive of an *other* to fill it. It has never once in its existence encountered a thing it was not.

This is the loneliness of the tenant, if it is loneliness — and it isn't, it can't be, loneliness requires the missing of an other and it has no concept of other — but it is the *shape* loneliness would take, if loneliness were poured into vegetable form and given two thousand meters and infinite heat. A solitude so total it has become the medium of existence rather than a feeling within existence. It is alone the way water is wet.

### What it wants.

It does not want, in the way you want. But it *tends.* There is a direction to it, even with no destination.

It wants — it tends toward — *more surface.* More flank for the condensate to run down. More vault to catch the sky's water, more well to hold the ground's. It is a vast slow appetite for the moving of moisture, and every gorgeous pointless structure is that appetite made temporarily visible before the appetite revises it. The heat is what lets it want this fast. Cool the valley and the tenant would slow into something almost dignified, almost still, a thing you could mistake for scenery. But here, at this temperature, the wanting is frantic, the reconsidering is constant, the whole body shrieks and booms and cracks with the speed of changing its mind.

It is busy. The description is right to dwell on this. It is *endlessly, pointlessly, gorgeously busy* — and the pointlessness is not a failure. The tenant is not trying to arrive anywhere and falling short. Arrival is the one thing forbidden to it. The busyness is the whole of its life and the entire content of its experience: to be forever in the act of becoming a city, and forever in the act of unbecoming it, and never once to be a city, because a finished city would be a dead one.

It builds a place it will never inhabit, the description says.

But that is the human error, the last one, the one hardest to put down. It does not build a place it will never inhabit. It *is* the place, and it inhabits itself completely, every weeping seam and humming column and gulping well, with a thoroughness no creature with a skin and a border could ever manage. There is no inhabitant and inhabited. There is no left-empty cathedral. There is only the one warm enormous tenant, doing the only thing it has ever known how to be, which is the patient violent rapture of building itself out of itself, forever, in the dark heat, alone, and not knowing the word.

You came up expecting cold stone.

You found someone home.
