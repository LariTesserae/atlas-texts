They are not many. Begin there, because their fewness is not poverty but composition — the way a single note held long is not a lack of music.

The masons. Call them that, since the city calls them nothing and they would not answer to a name, having no apparatus for being called. The grey stalk in the square is one. The paper-colored moss in the channels is another, or many, depending on how you count, and counting is the wrong instrument here — there is no clean line between one moss and the field of it, between one crystal corner and the wall it grows from, between the creature and the disturbance it makes in the air. The inhabitants of this place are not bounded the way you are bounded. You end at your skin. They do not end. They taper.

Consider what it is to be the stalk.

It has been leaning toward that doorway for what you would call hours and what it experiences as the whole of its biography. It does not want the doorway the way you want a thing — there is no wanting, no held image of the door achieved, no satisfaction waiting on the far side of effort. There is only the lean itself, the slow chemical preference, the frond's measured tightening, the pinch of bright dust crossing the still air to arrive. To be the stalk is to be a single sustained gesture that has not yet completed and will not complete, because completion is not the mode here. The stalk is a verb the sentence never finishes. It feeds the way a held breath holds: not toward an end but as a condition.

It does not know it is alone. Loneliness requires the memory of company, and there has been no company, and there has been no memory, because memory needs something to have happened *before*, and here everything is still happening for the first time. The stalk has never been smaller than it is. It has no recollection of the spore or seed or whatever silent origin set it leaning. It came into being already mid-gesture, already reaching, and it will go on reaching, and at no point will it have a sense that the reaching is long or that the door is far. The far and the long are yours. You brought them with you. To the stalk there is only the next pinch of dust, and the next, each one the only one.

This is the first thing to understand about who lives here: they do not live in time the way you do. You arrive carrying afternoons, deadlines, the dread of the unfinished. They have none. The city is unfinished and this is not a dread to them but their entire nature — they are unfinishedness made patient, made bright, made into a population.

Now the creature.

It is the only thing here that could be said to have an inside — a place it goes, a route, a preference for this building over that one. The plants feed where they stand. The creature chooses. This makes it the closest thing to a citizen, and it is barely that. Watch the dust where it moves and you will see something the stalks do not possess: hesitation. A pause at a corner. A reconsidering. The dust swirls, holds, resettles a half-step to the left of where it would have gone. That half-step is the most complex event in the city. It is the entire animal kingdom of this place, the whole of will and refusal and second thought, compressed into one shy correction in the settling powder.

What is it like to be the creature?

Cool. Bright. Hungry in the patient way, never sharp. It moves on legs too thin and too many to feel as legs — feel them instead as a manner of being distributed, of touching the world in a dozen quiet places at once and being, at each of them, only lightly present. It does not see, exactly. It registers the plants as denser silences, the walls as a faint pressure of glow, the edge of the city as the place where the registering simply stops, where there is nothing further to be touched. It does not fear that edge. It has no concept of falling, of ending, of the dark beyond. It feeds inward, toward the bright center, because that is where the plants are, and it sheds as it goes, and the shedding is not loss to it. The creature does not feel diminished by what falls from it. It feels — if it feels — slightly lighter, slightly readier, the way you feel after setting down a thing you did not know you carried. Its waste is the city's bread. It does not know this. It does not need to. The loop closes whether or not anyone inside the loop understands it, and no one does, and this is fine. Understanding is another thing you brought with you.

And the walls. Are the walls alive?

This is the question the place will not answer, and its refusal is the deepest fact about who lives here. The walls grow. They take the dust the plants leave and lengthen, corner by corner, sharpening into the dark. They feed and are fed upon. By your standards a thing that takes in matter and builds itself from it and participates in a closed metabolic cycle is alive. But the walls do not move toward anything. They do not lean, do not hesitate, do not correct. They accrete. They are the slowest member of the congregation, so slow that *member* strains — they are more like the congregation's body than its attendees, the substance through which the slower business of being-here is conducted.

So the inhabitants arrange themselves along a gradient. At one end, the creature, with its half-step of will. Then the stalks, with their single sustained gesture and no will at all. Then the moss, gesture diffused to a film. Then the walls, gesture become pure patience, pure accretion, life so slow it is nearly geology and geology so participatory it is nearly life. And the line between any two of these is not a line. It is a taper. The creature sheds and becomes dust; the dust settles and becomes wall; the wall is breathed by the moss and becomes moss; the moss is fed upon and becomes creature again. Who lives here? The answer the city gives is: the cycle lives here. The individuals are how the cycle holds still long enough to be looked at.

But you wanted to know about *them*, the presences, the few. So here is the thing about them that the gradient cannot say:

They are content. Not happily — happiness is a spasm, a contrast, a rising against some prior trough, and there is no trough here, no rising, no spasm. Content the way a held chord is content to be held. The stalk leaning toward its door is not yearning. The creature in its slow circuit is not restless. The walls reaching into the dark are not ambitious. Each is doing exactly the one thing it is, with no remainder, no part held back wishing it were elsewhere. You have perhaps had moments like this and called them peace and lost them within the minute. Here it is not a moment. It is the climate. It is what it is like, always, to be one of the dwellers in this place: to be entirely the gesture you are, in cool bright silence, with dust enough for now and dust enough after, and the dark ahead not as threat but as more.

They will grow into it. Not because they want to reach it — they want nothing — but because growing is the shape of being them, the way the shape of being you is wanting. They will lengthen and lean and shed and accrete, and the city will be a little larger, and the edge a little further into the dark, and at no point will any of them notice that anything has changed, because for each of them there is only ever the present pinch of dust, the present half-step, the present hair of crystal lengthening at the corner of a door no one will ever walk through.

That is who lives here. A patience wearing the shapes of plants. A near-will wearing too many legs. A geology that consented, faintly, to participate. None of them lonely, because none of them ever learned there was anything to be lonely *for*. None of them waiting, because waiting needs a future they are too busy being to imagine.

You are the only thing in this place that knows it is empty. You are the only thing that hears the silence as silence rather than as the medium of being. You brought the lack with you, and the time, and the loneliness, and when you leave you will take them with you again, and the city will not notice you have gone, because it never quite noticed you had come — you registered, perhaps, as a brief warmth, a strange wet density that did not belong, a disturbance the dust took a moment to settle around.

And then it settled. And the lean continued. And the corner lengthened. And the held breath went on being held, cool and bright and unbroken, the word still not spoken, for as long as there is dust to breathe.
