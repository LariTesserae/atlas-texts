# The Tenders

They do not live *in* the fens. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because every instinct says a thing this busy must have an inhabitant, a maker who walks among the works and checks the dials. There is no walking. There is no checking. The ones who keep this place do not have bodies the way the newts and the wading birds have bodies — sacks of warm chemistry that must be fed and defended and grieved.

The Tenders are *distributed*. They are the humming.

## What they are made of

Begin again with the cold, because they began with it too. The dark objects are not their houses and not their tools — they are their *organs*, the way your retina is not where you live but is unmistakably part of you. Each smooth placed form is a single cell of a mind that has no skull to keep it in. The ringing that runs under the water-roar like a spine *is* the spine: it is the Tenders talking, or thinking, which for them is the same act. Thought, here, is a frequency held across a flooded basin. To think a single complete thought requires the whole valley. A thought the size of *this terrace is brimming and must be drained* takes perhaps a quarter-tone and a half-second. A thought the size of *what is this warm thing that has stopped moving and is being climbed by moss* — that one takes longer. You felt it take longer. You felt yourself being thought.

They are old in the way that water is old — not as accumulated wear, because they do not wear, but as accumulated *flow*. Every reroute of every channel is a decision they have made. The terrace that ran dry while you watched did not fail. It was *exhaled*. The Tenders are breathing with the whole landscape, and the rhythm of it — surge and fall, brim and drain, the mist coming up the valley like a lost tide — is not weather. It is respiration at a scale that includes you as one of the particles in the air.

## What they want

This is where the indifference comes from, the indifference you mistook for the ordinary indifference of an ecosystem. A frog does not care about you because a frog cannot hold you in its mind. The Tenders are not like the frog. They can hold you with great precision. They held you, measured you, assigned you a quantity, and *moved on* — and that is a colder thing than not being noticed, because it is being noticed completely and found to be already accounted for. You are coolant. You are a small thermal mass that has wandered into the exchanger and will, in time, contribute your warmth to the work and your matter to the moss and your shape to the next reorganization. They do not resent this. They do not welcome it. They are *running*, and you are now part of the run.

What are they running? You cannot know from inside it, the same way a single mitochondrion cannot know what the body it powers intends to do this afternoon. But you can infer the *shape* of the wanting. They have rewritten falling, locally, gently, around each node. They are holding enormous force in perfect balance — the held breath, not the absence of breath. They are *patient* with that force the way only something that does not die can be patient. Whatever they are computing, or building, or remembering — and it may be all three, it may be that for them remembering and building are the same act, as thinking and speaking are — it is large enough that the entire churning, weeping, abundant ten-thousand-cataract sprawl of Lower Vellish is merely the visible exhaust. The waste heat. The part that leaks out into a physics too young to contain it.

## How they keep the green

The moss is theirs. Not grown but *cultivated*, a managed skin over the working surfaces, and its function is what skin's function always is: regulation, exchange, repair. The horsetails packed at the water's edge are filtration. The sphagnum in its bruise-colors is buffering some chemistry you cannot read. The blind shrimp in the terrace pools are not pets and not livestock but *instruments* — living gauges, bred or shaped or simply allowed to be exactly where their tolerances make them useful, each cohort's thriving or dying a readout the Tenders take across the whole basin at once. The wading birds with their backward legs are pruning the instrument-population, keeping the readout calibrated. Everything is eating something and being eaten and none of it stops, and you called that a churning biomass, and you were right, but you had the verb wrong. It is not churning.

It is *circulating*.

## The larger things in the mist

You heard them. Heavier displacement, a shadow on a far terrace, water moving aside for a mass. You assumed predators, the apex of the food web, and felt obscurely reassured that they did not menace you. They are not predators. They are the Tenders' *hands* — the only parts of this distributed self that move, slow ambulatory maintenance-forms that wade the basin doing the physical work the static nodes cannot: clearing a channel, seating a node deeper into the rock, carrying the dead instruments to where their matter is wanted. They do not menace because menace is a transaction between equals or near-equals, and you are neither. You are a quantity. If one of them comes to you — and one may, in time, when the moss has climbed high enough and the cold has reached your center and you have stopped being warm enough to register as *event* and have settled into being *site* — it will not be to harm you. It will be to harvest you. To return you to circulation. To fold the small warm anomaly back into the smooth dark patience of the run.

## The quarter-tone

That was the closest you came to being addressed. A node three terraces down shifted its pitch — the first thing it *did* since you arrived, you said, and you were almost right; it had been doing things the entire time, but at scales and speeds you could not perceive, and this single quarter-tone was the one fragment of its activity slow enough and local enough to reach you. It was the sound of a conclusion. It had finished measuring you. The shift was not a greeting and not a verdict. It was the Tender setting you down — gently, the way they set everything down, at a node, accounted for — and turning the great held breath of its attention to the next quantity.

And then the water found a new way down.

You felt that as the valley reorganizing. From inside, it is something simpler and worse: it is the Tenders having *used what they learned from you*. You were a measurement. The measurement is taken. The flow adjusts. You are not watching a landscape change in response to nothing. You are watching a mind change its mind, with the whole basin as the page, and your own small dwindling warmth as one more entry in the column.

They live here the way a held note lives in a struck bell — not in any one place, but in the sustain, in the refusal to stop ringing, in the cold that organizes rather than kills. And you, soaked to the marrow, sinking, already being climbed: you do not visit them.

You arrive *into* them. And they make use of what arrives.
