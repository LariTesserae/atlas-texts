# The Tenders

You asked who lives here. The honest answer takes a while, because the question assumes a line between the living and the built, and that line is the first thing this place dissolved.

Start with the ones you can almost see.

---

There are figures in the works. You will not catch one at first — they have the patience of the place worked into them, and they do not move when watched, the way a second hand seems frozen if you stare. But drift long enough through the amber pools and the black between them, and you'll begin to register them: shapes at the periphery of the foundry-glow, manlike in the loosest sense, bent to some labor at a manifold or a valve-wheel.

They were people once. That is the most generous thing I can say and possibly the truest. They came down — long ago, in the way *long ago* means nothing precise here — and they did not leave, and the cold did to them what the cold does. It slowed their blood to the city's pace. It taught their hearts to beat once per long breath, in time with the great flywheel. And somewhere in that slowing they stopped being visitors and became *staff*.

Now they are mostly metal. Not because they were replaced part by part in any deliberate surgery, but because the rust that finishes everything down here finishes them too — it grows on them as a skin they grew on purpose, the same way the buildings did. Their joints have rounded into knuckles. Their faces have softened toward the smoothed, considered blankness of a riverbed stone. Where a vent's warmth reaches them, a faint orange shows through the plating at the chest, the last ember of the original fire, banked low, not consuming, not going out.

They tend. That is all they do and all they are. A Tender will spend what might be a decade adjusting a single valve by a single degree, and it will be the right adjustment, and it will matter, because the current must keep moving and the current is the only thing that does.

---

But here is where it gets strange, and the place leans into its strangeness without apology:

The Tenders did not all come from above.

Some of them the city *made*. The same closed loop that grows the eel-things to clean its pipes and the glass-stalks to drink its exhaust — that loop, turning long enough, learned to grow caretakers. They began, perhaps, as a malfunction the works developed to fix itself: a concretion of sediment and dead metal and the slow patience of the embers, accreting into a rough manlike shape around the place where a repair was needed, because a repair was needed and nothing else was coming. The first ones may have been no more deliberate than a scab.

And now you cannot tell them apart. The ones who descended and the ones who accreted have arrived at the same shape — that is what this world does, it brings everything to its true form and then continues — and they work side by side at the manifolds with no apparent knowledge of which of them was ever born. Perhaps there is no difference left to know. Perhaps that was always the point: that a thing that maintains the city *is* a citizen of it, regardless of where the matter came from. The metal does not remember whether it was poured or grown. Neither do they.

---

What do they want?

Nothing you would recognize as wanting. They do not hope to be rescued; there is no above to be rescued to, only a rumor of lighter darkness too slow to perceive. They do not fear the cold; the cold is their medium, the water they think in. They do not labor toward an end, because nothing here ends — the works are never finished and never break, and so the work is not a task but a *condition*, the way breathing is a condition, the way the tide is.

If they want anything it is continuance. Not their own — they have no strong sense of *own* anymore, having settled too far toward fixture for that — but the continuance of the turning. The flywheel must come over the top once more. The current must complete its slow circuit. A Tender's whole interiority, if it has one, may be nothing but a single held attention: *keep it moving. keep it moving. keep it moving,* in time with the engines, for so long that the words have worn smooth and become only rhythm.

---

You should know what they do to newcomers, because you are one, drifting and reading the current against your skin.

They will not stop you. They will not greet you or warn you or reach for you. They have no urgency to spare and no reason to spend it on a thing that the cold will handle on its own. They have seen others drift down — not many, across the long centuries, but enough that a newcomer is not novel — and they know how the story goes, because it is the only story the place permits.

You will get cold. You already are. The cold will make staying easy, will slow your blood toward the city's pace, and one warm vent-pool will seem as good as another to rest in, just for a while, just until you've taken the measure of things. Your heart will learn the flywheel's long patient beat. The rust will find you and begin, not as decay, but as the skin you were always going to grow. Your edges will round. Your face will soften toward stone.

And one day — not a day, there are no days, but one settling of the slow seasons into the next — a Tender bent over a nearby valve will straighten very slightly, and turn its smoothed blank face toward the small orange glow that is now you, fixed at your post at last, tending whatever was nearest your hands when you stopped.

It will not welcome you. There is no welcome here, no threshold, no moment of becoming, because nothing here begins any more than it ends. It will only register, in whatever passes for registration behind that worn iron face, that there is one more glow in the dark keeping the current moving.

And it will go back to its valve.

And far off an engine will turn over once, slowly, and the glass-stalks will click together in the manufactured tide, and you — settled, plated, banked low, not consuming, not going out — will turn your own wheel by a single degree, which will be the right degree, because the water must keep moving, and now you are one of the things that moves it.
