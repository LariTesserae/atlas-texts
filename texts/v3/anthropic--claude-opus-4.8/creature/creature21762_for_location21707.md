They do not have names because there is no one to name them and no part of them that needs naming. But they have *roles*, and the roles are old, and the roles are the closest thing here to a self.

Begin with the smallest. The grains-of-grains, the ones that pack the hottest films where the blue glow beats strongest. These are barely individuals. Each is a sac of chemistry hardly distinguishable from the chemistry around it — a membrane, a few looping reactions, a way of pulling electrons off the metal and shunting them through itself before letting them go. That shunt is the whole of its life. It does not move. It does not seek. It sits in the warm film at the precise depth where the gradient is richest, and it conducts, the way a wire conducts, except that the wire is alive and replaces itself molecule by molecule as fast as the heat tears it down. It is being destroyed constantly. It survives by rebuilding faster than it dies. This is not a metaphor. The thing is a flame that has learned to be a flame made of itself, and the pulsing you hear, the deepest layer of the roar, is the sound of trillions of these tiny conductors firing and failing and firing again, never resting, because to rest for a moment is to come apart.

They do not know they are alive. There is nothing in them that could know. But they are the oldest pattern in the room, older than the crystals that house them, and everything else is built on them.

Above them — *above* meaning at the cooler crystal faces, where the non-water beads thick and oily — live the gatherers. These are larger, grain-sized, and they have made a decision the small ones never could: they have hardened. Each wears a shell it secretes from the minerals it processes, a little armored cup of the same metal salts that feed it, so that it is in a sense eating its own house and building it at once. The gatherers cluster. They do not touch by accident; they touch by design, shell pressed to shell in sheets and mats that span the whole crystal face, and where they touch they *share* — passing the half-processed minerals from one to the next in a slow bucket-line, each taking a little, each passing more along. The line is the refinery. The line is the production you hear above the deep firing of the small ones: the grinding, shouldering, ratcheting sound of a billion shells nudging their cargo one position down the chain.

No gatherer made the line. Each only does what the gatherer beside it does, which is take and pass and take and pass. But the line exists, and it is efficient, more efficient than any single one of them could be, and it has been running this way since before the highest mountains were mountains. It is a machine made of creatures who do not know they are a machine, who could not survive outside it, who have forgotten — if they ever knew, and they never knew — how to be anything but a station on the line.

And then there are the others. The ones at the seams.

Where crystal grows into crystal, where the matrix shoulders past itself and opens a crevice a hair wide, something waits in the new dark. These are the largest things in the pocket, which is to say still too small to see without the brief mercy of the glow, but among their kind they are giants. They do not conduct. They do not gather. They *consume* — the gatherers, the small conductors, the films, whatever drifts into the crevice on the sluggish convection of the heat. They are the only things here that move with intent, creeping along the seam at a pace no eye could catch, a body-length in a season, mouth-parts working the mineral mats free of the crystal and drawing them in.

They are predators in a world with almost no prey worth chasing, and so they are patient past all understanding. A consumer at the seam may wait a human lifetime between meals and feel no hunger we would recognize, because its metabolism runs at the speed of the rock. It is not slow the way a tired thing is slow. It is slow the way *erosion* is slow — deliberate, total, certain. When the glow pulses and you catch one in the act, you see only stillness; the motion is there but it is spread across so much time that stillness is all the moment can hold. Come back in ten years and the mouth has closed. Come back in a hundred and the body has shifted down the seam, and a new mat of gatherers has grown into the space it left, and the line has rerouted around the absence as water reroutes around a stone, except there is no water, and the rerouting took longer than your whole species has had language.

This is the order of them. Conductors, gatherers, consumers — flame, refinery, slow appetite — stacked in their layers from the hottest film to the coolest seam, every one of them touching every one of its neighbors, every one of them necessary, every one of them ignorant of the others, of itself, of the whole.

And the whole is the strange thing. The whole is what no single creature is, and the whole is what actually lives here.

Because step back — as far back as the kitchen-sized walls allow, which is no distance, but step back in *understanding* — and the pocket is not a room full of life. It is one life. The conductors and the gatherers and the consumers are not its inhabitants; they are its tissues, its organs, its cells. The firing line is its nerve. The bucket-line is its gut. The seam-dwellers are its slow hunger and its slow renewal both, eating the old growth so the new can come. The blue pulse is not a by-product of the chemistry — it *is* the chemistry, the way a heartbeat is not a by-product of the heart. The roar is not the sound of many things working. It is the sound of one thing being alive, in the only voice it has, which is the grinding of its own substance turning over and over forever.

It has no brain. It will never have one. There is nothing for it to think about. It does not want, does not seek, does not fear, does not dream, has no edge between itself and the world because it *is* the world, the whole sealed bubble of it, and the world wants nothing because it already has everything it will ever have: heat, metal, sulfur, time. It is complete. It was complete the moment it sealed, and it has been completing itself, atom by atom, ever since, getting no closer to any goal because it has no goal, only the next reaction, and the next, and the next.

It is the oldest kind of living. Older than mouths, older than eyes, older than the idea that life should go *somewhere*. It learned the one trick that mattered — take energy from the gradient, build yourself, build yourself again — and it never learned anything else because it never had to. Everything that came after, everything green and soft and seeking and aware, every creature that ever opened an eye and wanted, descends from something exactly like this, churning in exactly such a dark. We are the strange ones. We are the part of it that wandered out into the light and forgot. This is what it looked like before the forgetting. This is what it still looks like, in the rooms it never left.

It does not need you to have seen it. It has no part that could be glad you came, no part that will mourn when you go, no part that will mark, across its thousand-year tides, the flicker of your whole life passing the mouth of a cave it does not know it is inside of.

It will be there after. Pulsing blue. Roaring its single endless note. Touching itself on every side. Cooking, slowly, alone, forever, for no one — which is to say, for itself, which is to say, for nothing, which here at the beginning of everything are all the same thing, and always were.
