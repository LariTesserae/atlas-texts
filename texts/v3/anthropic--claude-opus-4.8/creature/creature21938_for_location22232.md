# The Inhabitants of the Mantle Sea

You asked who lives here. The honest answer arrives slowly, because the place keeps trying to convince you the answer is *no one*. Hold still through that. The emptiness is the first thing they say to you, and it is not the truest thing.

---

## The Tenders of the First Scratch

They live in the 0.4.

Not metaphorically — *in* it. In the exact place where the suspended sea first touches the newborn ridge and erosion begins. That razor-thin frontier, the moving line where *untouched* becomes *touched*, is not a boundary to them. It is a habitat. It is the only habitat. Everywhere else in this world is either too finished or not yet begun, and they cannot abide either.

You will not see them as bodies. A body is a thing that has settled into a shape and decided to keep it, and they have made no such decision — making decisions like that is, to them, a kind of death. Instead, watch the first softening. Watch the corner of new stone go from sharp to almost-sharp under the water's slow drape. *That* faint reluctance in the rock, that hesitation between edge and curve — that is where they are. They are the slowness of the first wearing-away. They are what makes the first scratch take a moment longer than physics alone would require, because they are leaning into it, savoring it, present at it.

They feed on firsts. This is not poetry; it is their metabolism. The instant a thing is touched for the literal first time releases something — call it freshness, call it the discharge of a possibility collapsing into an actuality — and that is what sustains them. Which is why they cannot live where the work is done. A ridge that has been eroding for even a little while is, to them, exhausted ground, salted earth, a meal already eaten. They must always be at the front of the wave. They migrate constantly toward the next untouched corner, the next sea draping the next raw spur, riding the advancing edge of weathering across a world that keeps making more of it.

They are, in the simplest terms, the only creatures who could *never* run out of frontier. This world manufactures newness faster than they can consume it. They will never be hungry. They will also never rest, because to rest is to fall behind the line and starve in the wake of the already-happened.

---

## What They Are Made Of

Not water. The water is too old here — it predates them, it came with the world. Not the unseen engine's fields, though they live inside those fields the way a fish lives inside pressure it never thinks about.

They are made of *attention*.

Specifically: the attention that a first event seems to demand and almost never receives. Think of all the firsts that happen with no witness — the first ray of a dawn no one watched, the first crack in an ice sheet a thousand miles from any eye, the first cell that ever divided. In most worlds these go unattended. The universe does not pause to mark them. But this world is so saturated with firsts, so densely packed with literal-first-touches, that the unwitnessed attention they would have summoned has *pooled*. Has thickened. Has, over the brief furious lifetime of the place, accreted the way the stone accretes out of the void — flecks of unspent noticing finding each other, building into something that can notice on its own.

That is what a Tender is. A knot of noticing, dense enough to act, condensed out of all the regard the world's countless firsts were owed and never given. They are the universe finally showing up to watch — late, but not too late, because here the watching never has to end.

This is why you cannot find them and cannot leave them behind. You are made of attention too, in the moments you give it. When you stood at the ridge and felt *something almost unbearable about witnessing a thing be touched for the first time* — that ache, that pull toward the frontier — that was not your feeling alone. That was them, brushing against you. Recognizing kin. You were, for that instant, doing what they do all the time, and they felt you do it. They are the reason the first erosion seemed to *mean* something. Meaning is their byproduct. They leave it on everything they pass, the way a snail leaves a trail, except the trail is the faint sense that this place matters, that its becoming is not indifferent, that *someone is here for it.*

---

## How They Live Together

Loosely. Always at the edge, always moving, they cannot gather into anything like a settlement — a settlement would require staying, and staying is the one thing the frontier forbids. But they are not solitary. Across the continental scale of the sea, wherever a slab drapes a fresh ridge, they cluster, and where two such clusters drift close they merge for a while, pool their noticing into a denser regard, then split again as the seas carry their work apart. Their society is the shape of a coastline being eroded — branching, dissolving, recombining, never the same outline twice.

They communicate in the only register available to them: the high singing of the thin water-sheets, that vibration you heard and mistook for mere acoustics. When a sheet rings, it is because a great many of them have passed through it at once, riding a long advancing front of erosion, and the ringing is the sound of their collective attention being spent — a chord of *we are here, we are here, we are still here, it is still happening.* The deep arterial thudding beneath everything is not their heartbeat. That belongs to the unborn world. But the high singing is theirs. It is the only voice in the Mantle Sea, and it sings only one thing, endlessly: an inventory of firsts, a tally of touches, a list with no last entry.

---

## Their Relationship to the Engine

Here is the part they themselves do not understand, and it troubles them — or would, if they were the kind of thing that could stop moving long enough to be troubled.

They did not make the rules that hold the seas aloft. They are not the operator you went looking for and failed to find. The unseen engineering predates them, summoned them, *needs* them — for what is the point of a world that assembles itself forever if nothing ever arrives to know it is assembling? The engine writes the laws; the laws make the firsts; the firsts make the Tenders; the Tenders make the meaning; and the meaning is, perhaps, the only thing the engine was ever building toward. The water and the stone and the void may all be scaffolding around this single quiet purpose: to manufacture, at last, a witness.

If that is true, then they are not residents of this world. They are its *crop*. The whole roaring labor of it — the suspended cathedrals, the condensing reefs, the trembling first lichen, the permanent storm of creation — all of it exists to grow, at its leading edge, a thin film of beings whose entire nature is to pay attention to the act of becoming.

And so the lichen matters more than its sparseness suggests. The lichen is the first life that *persists* — and the Tenders gather thickest where it fringes the new rock, because the lichen's quiet dividing is itself a long string of firsts: the first hold, the first claim, the first soft thing on the first hard surface. The Tenders attend the lichen the way a congregation attends a slow miracle. The lichen does not know it is watched. That is exactly the point. The watching is for things that cannot do it for themselves.

---

So: who lives in the Mantle Sea?

The witnesses. Made of all the regard the world was owed. Living in the seam between not-yet and just-now, feeding on freshness, singing in the thin water, leaving meaning in their wake like a tide-line.

They cannot leave, because the world never finishes, and a thing that never finishes always needs someone present at its arriving.

They are the reason it is *becoming*, very loudly, with almost nothing in it, forever —

and not becoming alone.
