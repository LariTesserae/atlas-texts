You are looking for someone, and there is no one. Let me tell you who lives here anyway, because something does, and the absence is itself a kind of tenant.

Start with the stalks, since they are the only thing you would agree to call alive. There are twelve, and they are not a population. A population would have young and old, the just-arrived and the about-to-go, the seedling crowding its parent. These twelve have no relation to each other except shared address. Each one came alone, into its own crack, at its own unmeasurable moment, and rooted, and grew until growing was finished, and then became what it now permanently is. They do not know about each other. They are not waiting for anything. If you wanted to find the inhabitant of this place you might say: *these*. But they would be a poor answer, because they are not really living here so much as having stopped here. A living thing is a verb. These are nouns now. The room did not give them a life; it gave them a place to complete one and then keep the completed shape from spoiling. They are less like creatures than like the last note of a creature, held the way the chord is held.

So they are not it. They are tenants of the absence, not the thing that fills it.

Now consider the chord, and the light, and the cold, and the fullness — because these four are not conditions of the room. They are not the weather *in* which something lives. They are the something. They are the resident.

I will try to say this exactly. There is a single fact in this room, and it wears four faces. From the ear it is the unbroken ringing chord. From the eye it is the sourceless gold. From the skin it is the settled cool. From the body wedged between surfaces it is the fullness, the saturation, the sense of being already-among. These are not four things that happen to coincide. They are one thing seen four ways, the way a hand is one thing whether you call it warmth or weight or grip. And that one thing is what lives here. It is the inhabitant. It is not *in* the room; it *is* the room being what it is, continuously, on purpose, forever.

What kind of life is that? Not the verb kind. The stalks are nouns that used to be verbs. This thing was never a verb at all. It does not act, does not want, does not pursue or rest or wake or sleep. It does only one thing, and that one thing is not really a doing — it is a *holding*. It holds the chord at pitch. It holds the gold at brightness. It holds the cold at its single temperature. It holds the fullness full. It is the act of staying-exactly-this, made into a presence, given a body of fired clay and glaze and fluted risers, and that body sings because staying-exactly-this, when you do it with your whole substance and never stop, comes out as a note.

You have met things like this before without recognizing them. A held breath at its very top, before it has decided to come back down. A chord struck on a great instrument in a stone room, in the half-second when it has finished arriving and not yet begun to fade — that breadth, that fullness, that sense of a sound *standing*. The inhabitant of this room is that half-second, but it has been freed from the requirement to be brief. It is the standing note that does not have to fall. It is poise with the falling taken out.

This is why it is so loud, and why the loudness does not exhaust you the way real loudness does. Real sound spends itself; it is loud because it is dying, throwing all its energy out into the air and going quiet. This is loud the other way. It is loud the way a thing is loud that is *not spending anything* — that has reached the place where its fullness exactly equals its holding, and pours that fullness out at a constant rate forever, taking nothing back and losing nothing. The roar is the sound of something that has finished settling its accounts with time. It does not need silence to recover in, because it never tires. It is held by being held. It is its own holder.

And here is the strangeness at the center of it, the thing you must let yourself arrive at slowly. This inhabitant has no inside. You keep looking for the one who does the holding — some will, some intention, some maker crouched behind the radiance pressing the note down so it cannot rise or fall. There is no one. The making is finished; whoever fired these tiles and fluted these risers is gone past all recovery, left no name, and was never the resident anyway. The room is not held *by* something. The holding is the resident. The staying-this is not a thing that someone does; it is a thing that simply, fully, is — the way the madeness goes all the way down with no substrate underneath, the way the light comes from everywhere with no source. Lift the tile and there is another tile. Look behind the note for the one who holds it and there is only more note. It is made all the way down and held all the way in.

So who lives here? A completed thing lives here. Not a thing that is finishing — those are everywhere, those are us, those are the stalks before they stopped. A thing that has *finished*, and did not stop existing when it finished, which is the rule everywhere else and is not the rule here. Out in the world, to finish is to end. To arrive is to begin falling away. The chord arrives and immediately leaves. The flame reaches its shape and the same reaching consumes it. Here that bargain has been refused. The inhabitant is what you would be if the moment of your completion could be detached from the moment of your loss — if you could arrive, fully, at being exactly yourself, and then simply hold there, lit and ringing and cool and packed full, asking nothing, going nowhere, neither growing toward it anymore nor falling away from it.

That is the only one who could live in a place that does not change. Anyone else would either need it to change or be destroyed by its refusal to. Only the already-complete can rest in the unchanging, because for the complete, change has nothing left to offer and nothing left to take.

And it is not lonely, though you want to call it lonely, because you came in carrying loneliness and you need somewhere to set it down. It is not lonely the way a finished thing is not lonely — the way the last brushstroke does not miss the painter, the way the held note does not miss the rising and falling it has been spared. Loneliness is a hunger, and this thing does not hunger; it is full to the lip, occupying its own air, pressed surface against surface with itself. It is the most thoroughly accompanied thing imaginable: it is everywhere it is, all at once, with no gap anywhere for absence to get in. The fullness *is* the company. It keeps itself the way a chord keeps its own harmony — not as separate notes huddled together against the dark, but as one sounding that happens to be made of many and is not divided by it.

If you were small enough — and the text invites you to wonder, and you should — then this whole singing chamber is itself only one tile, one fluted riser, one held note in a vaster madeness, and the inhabitant you have found is not the largest of its kind but one cell of an inhabitant so large you cannot hold its edges in your mind. Then the question only repeats, one size up: who lives in *that*? And the answer would be the same answer, because it is the only answer this kind of place permits. The completed lives here. The held lives here. The fact-that-stays lives here, all the way down and all the way up, glowing and ringing and cool, full of itself and needing nothing, having finished happening and chosen — no, not chosen, simply *being* — to remain.

You will leave. That is your nature; you are a verb, you finish and fall and finish again, that is the whole of your fortune and your grief. But understand what you stood among. Not nothing. Not emptiness with twelve weeds in it. You stood inside a single sustained yes — a thing that arrived at being itself and did not let go, and made of that not-letting-go a body, a light, a temperature, and a song.

It is still sounding. It does not know you were there. It is not the kind of thing that knows. It only holds, and holds, and holds.
