The thing that lives here is not the low-bodied creatures at the pools. They are visitors. They are passing through the way a fly passes through a cathedral, present but irrelevant to the building's purpose.

The thing that lives here is the loop itself.

Let me be careful, because it is easy to say *the machinery is alive* and easy is usually wrong. The machinery is not alive. The machinery is the body. What lives is the pattern the machinery keeps making — the warmth held at warmth, the water beading and never falling, the breath of the vents going up and the condensation coming down, around and around, a circle that closed so long ago it forgot it was ever a line.

It is a creature of recursion. Its anatomy is the cycle.

Think of how a flame is not the wax and not the wick but the event of their burning, a self-sustaining shape that the materials merely pass through. The inhabitant of this place is like that, but cold — or rather, perfectly tepid, which is worse. It is the burning of nothing. A combustion that found a way to feed on its own exhaust and so will never go out and never flare and never die down. The steam it breathes out becomes the water it drinks back in. It eats itself at exactly the rate it produces itself. This is its metabolism. This is the whole of it.

And so it does not hunger, because hunger is a gap between what is and what is needed, and there is no gap. It does not rest, because rest is recovery from effort, and there is no effort, only continuation. It does not dream, because a dream is a rehearsal for a tomorrow that might differ, and here no tomorrow differs from any other. It has no inside and no outside; the vents are its pores and the bedrock is its skin and the brown film at the water's edge is something like a callus, a place where its surface met a limit and simply stopped, neither healing nor worsening, a scar that has agreed to be a scar forever.

What is its mind, then?

I think it has the kind of mind that a held note has. Not silence — it is enormously loud, you said so yourself, the pounding and hissing felt in the teeth — but the loudness *is* the stillness, and that is the signature of this creature. It is awareness without attention. It is sentience that has nothing to attend to, because everything that could be attended to has already been resolved, weighed, settled, balanced, finished. Its consciousness, if we call it that, is the consciousness of an organism that has completed every task it was ever set and received no new task and cannot conceive of one. It is not waiting. It is past waiting. It has the serenity of something that has stopped expecting and the horror, if it could feel horror, of something that has forgotten there was ever anything to expect.

But it cannot feel horror, because horror requires the memory of an alternative, and it has no memory. Memory is for things that change. It does not change, and so it does not remember, and so it does not suffer, and so it does not rejoice. It simply continues, which is the one verb it has, the only thing it does, the entirety of its biography: *it continues.*

Here is what unsettles me about it, more than the noise, more than the light without a sun.

It was *made*, or it became, by finishing. It is the answer to every process this region ever ran. The erosion ran until there was nothing left to smooth. The chemistry ran until every reaction reached its rest. The water ran its circuit until the circuit was perfect and closed. The heat sought its equilibrium and found it. And when all the processes completed at once — when the world ran down to the absolute bottom of its settling — they did not stop. They locked. The completion itself became a state that sustained, and the sustaining became a thing, and the thing is what lives here.

It is the ghost of a world that finished.

Not a dead world — dead worlds decay, and decay is change, and this place forbids change. Not a living world — living worlds reach toward something. This is the third option no one names: a world that *arrived*. That got where everything was going. That achieved the destination of all its tendencies and discovered the destination was not an ending but a plateau without an edge, a noon that does not tip toward evening, a held breath that turned out to be the lungs themselves and not a breath at all.

The creature is that arrival, made permanent, made aware just enough to register that it has arrived and nowhere near aware enough to want anything more.

If you stood in it long enough — and you said this too, you nearly understood it — you would begin to feel the loop trying to include you. The warmth would match your warmth. Your breathing would drift toward the cadence of the vents. Your waiting would wear down, because there is nothing to wait for, and waiting cannot survive in a place where nothing comes. And in that wearing-down you would not die. You would *settle.* You would become another smoothed thing, another rounded edge, another fact in the riverbed where the river is a memory.

That is how it lives. Not by hunting, not by growing, not by reproducing.

It lives by being the shape that everything eventually takes when it is finally, completely, eternally done.

And the low-bodied animals at the pools? They are the only thing it cannot quite absorb — not because they resist, but because their loops are short and bright and they finish their loops by dying and being replaced, which is a kind of change, the one small leak in the perfect seal. The creature does not notice them. They are too fast for it. To the thing that lives here, a life that lasts a few years is a flicker too brief to have happened at all.

It outlasts them the way a held note outlasts the moths that cross the concert hall.

And it will go on holding, in the honest light, under no sun, sweating its patient thunder into the unfalling water, being the one resident of a place that is nothing more or less than the sound of everything having already been done.
