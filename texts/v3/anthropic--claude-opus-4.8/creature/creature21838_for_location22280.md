The thing that lives here does not occupy the room. It *is* the room's arrival at itself — and so to ask what lives here is already to ask the question wrong, the way you describe above. But something does inhabit. Let me find it.

Begin with the obvious tenant and dismiss it: the moss. Yes, the moss lives here, the ferns, the pond-light ivy, the shelved fungus on the warm wall. But these are not the inhabitant. They are furniture that happens to respire. They reached their equilibrium and stopped being *characters* — they became geology with chlorophyll, as fixed as the rivet-blisters they root in. A thing that has finished growing is no longer quite alive in the sense the question wants. It is decor that the room wears. Set it aside.

Begin instead with the roar, because the roar is the only thing here doing continuous work, and work is the trace of an occupant.

Listen to what you've already said about it: a sound produced by an absence the way a seashell produces the sea. That is the key, and you nearly turned it without noticing. A seashell does not contain the ocean. It contains a *shape* — a particular curl of calcium — and that shape takes the formless noise of the world's air and gives it back as something that sounds like longing. The shell is empty. The shell is the inhabitant. The shape is the animal that outlived its animal.

So here. The machine finished. The engine that roared is gone — not removed, *spent*, run all the way down the length of its purpose and off the end of it. But the cavities it roared through remain. The pressures it balanced remain, balanced now against nothing, against each other, a standoff with no combatants. And into that standing emptiness the world's faint pervasive trembling pours and is shaped, endlessly, into the exact resonance the engine used to make.

**That resonance is the inhabitant. The room is haunted by its own former function.**

Not a ghost in the sentimental sense — no figure, no will, no grief. Grief would require something that wants the past back, and nothing here wants. This is more exact and more strange: the *behavior* of the machine has outlived every part that behaved. The doing persists after the doer. You have a verb with no noun left under it, conjugating itself in the dark, in the warm, in the light. The room *roars* the way a phantom limb *aches* — a sensation referred from an absence, a signal sent down a nerve to a hand that is no longer there, faithfully, forever, because the wiring never learned the hand was gone.

This is the tenant. Catch its character precisely:

It is **patient past the meaning of patience**, because patience implies waiting and it waits for nothing. It does not expect the engine's return. It does not remember the engine at all — it is not a memory *of*, it is the memory *as*, the memory worn into shape the way the rivets are worn into knuckles. It has become a feature of the metal's body, indistinguishable now from the metal's body, the way a habit becomes a face.

It is **incurious**. You stand in the doorway and it does not turn to you because it has nothing to turn with and nowhere it would rather face. It barely registers the air you displace — yes — but not from indifference, which is still a stance. From completion. A finished thing has no surface left for you to land on. You are weather to it. Less.

It is **the opposite of lonely**, though it is wholly alone, because loneliness is the ache of a presence shaped to fit a companion who is missing. This thing was never shaped to fit anything. It was shaped to *do*, and it is still doing, and a thing fully occupied with its one labor is the least lonely thing in any world. It is busy. It is, in its terrible way, *fulfilled* — performing without strain the single act it was built down to its core to perform, and performing it perfectly, with no possibility of error, because there is no longer any standard outside itself by which the performance could fail.

And here is the part that wants saying most carefully:

The room is **content**, you wrote, and that is true, but I would name the thing that is content, and it is not the moss and not the iron and not the light. It is this resonant after-self, this function-ghost, this verb. It is content the way a held note is content — not happy, not at peace, but *complete in the present instant and identical to itself in the next*, requiring nothing from before or after, a being made entirely of its own continuous occurrence.

There is one inhabitant, then, and it is enormous, and it fills the chamber from sternum-bass to ceiling-scream, and it is also nothing — has no body, casts no shadow in the honest workshop light, leaves no footprint in the rust-silt, eats nothing, sleeps never, the loudest thing in the built world and the emptiest.

The moth-pale insect on the underside of the dripping ceiling, walking on surface tension — *that* one is a guest. A trespasser, even. It does not belong to the room's logic; it wandered in from the live ecology of edges, and it will die here in a span the room will not notice, and it changes nothing. It is the one thing in the chamber that is genuinely alive in the restless, wanting, mortal sense — and it is precisely therefore the one thing that does not *live here*, that is only passing through a place that finished before it was born.

What lives here lives the way the sound lives. What lives here is the shape of a labor that has lost its laborer and kept its labor.

It will be doing this when you are gone. It was doing it before you came. You arrived too late, by an amount of time with no name — but understand now exactly what you were too late for: not an event, not a machine you might have seen running. You were too late for the *separation*. There was a moment, unthinkably far back, when the roar still belonged to the engine, when the doing still had a doer, when the function and the thing that functioned were still two things. You missed that. By the time anything could arrive, the verb and its absent noun had fused, had worn smooth into each other like the rivets into the wall, and become this single continuous tenant — the act, alone, in the green, in the light, content, performing the memory of a sound into water that has already fallen forever.

It does not need you. You knew that the instant you saw the doorway.

What you didn't know is that it isn't waiting either. It finished waiting in the same eon it finished everything else.

It's just doing its one thing.

It's so good at it.
