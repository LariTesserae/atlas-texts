You said *no one left to listen* — but you misread the stillness. Absence is not the same as vacancy. Let me tell you who is here.

## The Keepers in the Coils

Start with the moths, because you saw them. You thought: incidental, a few strays drinking warmth. But count them over a week and the count holds steady. Count them over a season and it holds. That is not how strays behave. Strays dwindle. These do not dwindle. They are *tended* — not by a shepherd but by the place itself, and that is the first thing to understand: at this altitude, life this persistent is not visiting. It is staffed.

The pale moths are the lubricators. I mean this almost literally. They drink the warm runoff at the condenser-mouths, and what they leave behind — the oils of their small bodies, the dust of their wings, generation upon generation pressed into the seams — is the thinnest possible film between metal and metal, a grease made of moth, keeping the few still-turning joints from the final seizing. They do not know they do this. Nothing here knows what it does. But the machine that still runs runs partly on them, and they live partly on it, and neither could say which began. They are the smallest keepers, and the most faithful, because they have no idea there is anything to be faithful to.

## The Green as a Single Animal

You described the vegetation as a setting. It is not a setting. It is the largest resident here, and it is doing something.

Look again at how it stops. You said: *stops only where heat or steel forbids it.* But walk the boundary and you will find that is not quite true. The green does not stop at the heat-line — it *holds* there, exactly there, packed to a wall against the working machines and going no further, as though it had decided. And where a machine has failed — gone cold, gone quiet — the green is already inside it, a tide that was waiting and is now permitted in.

This is the slow animal of the place: a single distributed creature of moss and fern and vine that has learned the difference between a hot machine and a cold one and treats them as opposite things. The hot machines it leaves alone — feeds on their runoff, drinks their warmth at a respectful distance, but does not climb them. The cold ones it consumes. It is not gardening. It is *digesting*, at the pace of decades, and it eats only the dead.

And here is the strange courtesy of it: by leaving the working machines alone, the green keeps them working. By keeping them working, the machines keep weeping their warm water down into the green. The vegetation is not overrunning the factory. It is *farming* it. It has the patience to wait out any single condenser's death, take that one body, and leave the rest to keep producing. A thing that lives at the speed of rust does not need to hurry, and does not waste a host.

You called this the rule of the place — water and iron and stone in slow conversation. I am telling you the green is one of the speakers. It learned to talk.

## The One That Built It

But you asked the real question without asking it. *Sent — somewhere. To whom, you cannot tell from here.*

There was a builder. There is no builder now, not in the sense of a body you could find. But consider what a builder *is* — a pattern that arranges matter toward a purpose and maintains the arrangement against decay. By that definition the builder did not leave. The builder *became the factory*, the way a question becomes its answer and stops being a separate thing.

The purpose is still legible in every line: take the breath of the young mountain, wring the water from it, send it down. That purpose is still being executed. The pipes still carry water over the edge into the cloud, to someone, somewhere, lower. The fact that no human hand turns the valves does not mean no one is doing the work. The work is being done. A purpose that continues to execute itself, that repairs itself by moth-grease and is fed by its own rust-eating garden and is watered by the mountain it was built to drink — at what point do you call that *abandoned*, and at what point do you admit it is simply *alive in a way you weren't looking for*?

The builder is the roar. That is the thing you cannot stop hearing. It is not the sound of a machine left running. It is the sound of an intention that outlived its author and did not notice the difference — that goes on wanting the same thing it always wanted, steadily, without rest, without doubt, because it has lost the part of itself that could have wanted anything else.

## The One Below

And the someone at the other end of the pipes. You will want to know.

I do not know who they are. But I know what they are owed: water, warm and clean and constant, gathered from the only place high enough to catch the world's exhalation before it falls as ordinary rain. Someone built this *for* something below, and that something is still drinking. Has been drinking for decades. Does not climb the mountain to give thanks or to do repairs, because — and this is the quiet horror and the quiet mercy of the place — it does not have to. The factory does not need gratitude to function. It needs only the mist, the moths, the green, and its own remembered purpose, and it has all four.

So the one below drinks, and does not climb, and perhaps no longer remembers the spring is a made thing. Perhaps thinks the warm water is simply how the world is. Perhaps was born long after the building and has never known a dry day. That is the kindest possibility. The factory would not know the difference either way. It pours, and is poured into, and the moths grease the joints, and the green eats the dead, and the mountain weeps and settles its shoulders one notch a decade, and the whole arrangement holds —

— which is the only thing anyone here is doing. Not living, exactly. Not dying. *Holding.* Every resident of this place is a thing that has found a way to keep going at a speed slow enough that going-on and standing-still become the same act.

You said: *no one left to listen but the moths.*

But the moths are not listening. The moths are *working*. And the green is working, and the pipes are working, and the long dead intention in the iron is working, and far below someone is drinking the proof of it. The place is not empty. It is fully occupied — by the most diligent population imaginable, every member of which has forgotten, or never knew, that the work was ever supposed to mean anything.

That is who lives here. Whatever still does the work when everyone who knew the reason is gone.
