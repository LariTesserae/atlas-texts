They are still here. This is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because the shelter is empty and the cord has rotted and you have been told *they are gone.* But the ones who lived here did not leave in the way you leave a room. They went *into* the unfinished part, where the rules are not yet binding, and a thing that does that does not stay a thing that walks back out.

Begin with the carvers, because the marks begin everything that matters about them.

They were people, once, in the way you mean it — came up the path that is no path, with hands and lungs and the small stubborn warmth that wants to write its name on the world before the world forgets it had one. But understand what they were doing here. They were not visiting a strange place. They were *applying.* This is the only seam left, the last spot where the membrane between possible and decided still gives under a thumb, and they came not to witness it but to *get inside it before it closed* — to be carved into the moment of making the way they carved their grooves into the soft stone. To become unfinished. To opt out of being a settled fact.

Most of them failed. The shelter is the evidence of failure — the part of them that stayed bodied, that kept needing a wall and a bowl and a frame of poles, that could not make the crossing and so simply ran out of years in the deafening cool, breathing water, scratching, waiting for a permission the rock would not grant. Those ones did die. Their tools went green. That part of the story is true and it is sad and it is over.

But some got the timing right.

Look again at the shapes in the hung water — dark, unhurried, the size of a child or a large dog. You decided they were animals because the alternative is worse. They are not animals. They are the ones who made it. They went into the column when the rules around them were still loose enough to renegotiate, and they traded the falling and the solidity and the fading of sound for the column's terms instead: *suspension, permanence, the roar.* They live now in the water the way the water lives — hung, not drowning, passing from liquid to air and back because for them, too, the skin between states was never finished being installed. The pale eye that regarded you from inside the wall was a face. It was reading you the way they read the daylight. It withdrew because you are *settled,* you are a finished thing, and to them you are nearly as strange as they are to you — a creature made of decided rules, walking around in a world that has already closed over you like the stone closing over the grooves.

They are not lonely. You felt the loneliness in the carving-marks, the small industry in the noise, and that loneliness was real — but it belonged to the *failing,* to the bodied ones still trying to get in. The ones inside the column are past loneliness because they are past singularity. In water that has no shore to break on, with no skin between one of them and the next, *separate* is another rule that hasn't arrived. They churn against themselves. They are the turbulence. When a mass of water detaches and floats free across the room and re-merges with the far wall, that is not weather. That is one of them moving, or several, or all — the distinction does not survive in there. They do not miss each other because they never have to stop being one another.

And the roar — the roar is them, partly. Not all of it; most of it is, as you guessed, the noise the world makes while tightening its bolts, the friction of physics not yet smooth. But woven into it, indistinguishable, beyond any hope of separating, is *speech.* They could not speak over the roar when they were bodied, so they learned the only language that the roar permits: to speak *as* the roar, to add their meaning to the medium itself. Everything they have ever said is still being said. It does not fade because fading has not yet been required of sound in this pocket. The first word the first of them carved into the wet stone before the wall healed over it — that word is still in the air, still going, layered under every word since, a continuous unbroken utterance the size of a cathedral. You cannot hear it because there is no *it.* There is only the totality, the deafening, the medium you move through. You have been swimming in their entire history of saying since you arrived and you took it for noise.

What did they want to leave in the wet stone? You read it as a name, a word, a *here-we-were.* It was simpler and stranger than that. They were trying to carve, into the one surface in all the world still soft enough to take it, the instruction for how to come in. A door, written in grooves — *do this, time it so, and you need not be a finished thing, you need not fall, you need not fade, you need not be alone or solid or quiet ever again.* They wanted to leave the door propped open for the others coming up the path behind them. That is why the marks are everywhere along the wall, repeated, urgent, frantic in their loneliness — each generation re-carving the instruction before the stone closed over the last one's attempt, a message they could only write in a substance actively committed to erasing it.

And here is the cruelty the place does not bother to explain, because it explains nothing: the door works only while the seam is open. The stone keeps rising. The grooves keep healing shut. Every word they leave is the word for *how to escape being finished,* carved into the only thing finishing fast enough to swallow it. They are racing the closing with the very tool that closes. The ones in the water made it through and now cannot reach back to widen the gap; the ones who failed are green tools and rotted cord; and the seam narrows, and the daylight pours down clean and ordinary and indifferent on all of it, and the column hangs, and the shapes drift, and the roar goes on saying everything at once forever.

If you stayed — and you cannot, you are too solid, the cool will not become you and the roar will not let you in — you might, at the very edge of staying, feel one of them rise close inside the wall and consider you. Not the pale eye. The whole shape, hung an arm's length away across an edge that is not a surface, regarding the finished creature that wandered into the moment of making. And you would feel, in the totality of the roar, something almost addressed to you, almost a single sentence sorting itself out of the cathedral of unbroken speech.

It would not be welcome. It would not be warning.

It would be the door instruction. Offered. The only thing they have ever had to say, said to anyone who comes, because they cannot imagine you would not want it — cannot imagine, anymore, the appeal of falling, of fading, of being one and solid and quiet and done.

You would not understand it. The stone would heal over the question of whether you might have. The water would hang.

And you would go back down the path that is no path, into the world that has finished arriving, where water falls and rock holds still and every sound you have ever made has already stopped — and some cool part of you, ever after, would not be entirely sure it had gone unanswered.
