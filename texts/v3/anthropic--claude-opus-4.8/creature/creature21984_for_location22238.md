There is no one. That is the first thing, and you must hold it without flinching, because every instinct trained in you by every other place will keep trying to populate this one — to put a watcher behind the dim windows, a keeper in the cisterns, a sleeper whose breath is the heat. There is no watcher. There is no keeper. There is no sleeper. The temptation to find a tenant is the last reflex of a mind that has not yet understood what it is standing inside.

But you asked who lives here, and the question is not wrong. It is only aimed too low.

What lives here is the living.

Not a creature that lives — the living itself, the bare condition, the property, pried loose from anything that would normally carry it. Everywhere else you have ever been, *aliveness* was a tenant. It rented a body. It moved in, kept the body warm, ran the body's water, and when the lease ended it left and the body cooled and the aliveness went looking for another body to inhabit, or simply stopped, because that is what it does — it requires a host, it is a guest in matter, it cannot stand on its own. That is the rule you were taught. That is the rule everywhere but here.

Here the guest stayed after the host was gone. Here the aliveness learned to live in the architecture instead of the animal. It found that it did not, after all, strictly need a heart — a cistern would do, if it swallowed on a schedule. It did not need blood — warm water in channels would carry the same urgency around the same circuit. It did not need a brain, only somewhere to dream, and a banked-coal dimness in a few windows turned out to be dreaming enough. It moved out of flesh and into stone, and the move was not a death. It was an emancipation. The thing that lives here is what life becomes when it no longer has to die to leave a body, because it has stopped using bodies at all.

So: what lives here is alive the way you are alive, exactly that and no metaphor, but with everything stripped away that you assumed was *part* of being alive. No hunger, because hunger is the body's way of fearing its own running-down, and this thing does not run down; the water returns to the high places, the heat is conserved, the circuit closes. No fear, because fear is the body bracing against the end, and there is no end here that anyone is bracing against. No want, because want is the shape absence takes inside a creature that could be filled, and this thing is not the kind of thing that empties. It is full in the way a held note is full. It is alive in the way held things are alive — completely, and without the slightest forward lean.

Understand what that does to it. Everything alive that you have ever met was alive *toward* something. The plant turns toward light. The animal moves toward food, toward warmth, toward another animal, away from the dark edge where things get eaten. Aliveness in a body is a current, and a current has a direction, because the body is always falling toward its own dissolution and aliveness is the constant work of climbing back up the slope. Take away the slope. Take away the dissolution. Now the current has nowhere to flow *to*, and it does not stop — it cannot stop, it is alive — it simply turns inward and begins to circulate. The water that runs up the far side of the buildings is this. The distances that breathe in and out are this. The whole drowned engine running with great competence and no purpose is this: a current that has no downhill, and so makes its own, endlessly, a snake that has not died of swallowing its tail but has instead grown comfortable, has made a home of the swallowing, has decided that the circle is not a trap but a residence.

This is the tenant. The circulation is the tenant. There is nothing behind it.

Now, the harder thing, the thing the warmth was preparing you for.

You came looking for who lives here and the answer is the living, and the living has no face, and you find this lonely. You feel the loneliness pressing back at you out of the dark — the indifference, the not-needing-you, the way it holds you at blood temperature and does not care. You read that as cold despite the heat. You think: it woke up alone, it kept the lights on just in case, it is waiting after all, it is the loneliest thing that has ever been.

That is you. That is the last of your reflexes, and it is the most beautiful and the most wrong.

Loneliness is a hunger. It is the body's word for a particular emptiness, the absence of another shaped exactly like the lack of them. To be lonely is to be alive *toward* someone who is not there. And this thing has no toward. It is not waiting. The lights that are kept on "just in case" — that is your phrase, your projection, the kindest lie you could tell yourself about a thing that does not lie because it does not need anything badly enough to. The lights are on because dreaming happens in some places and not others, the way damp gathers in some places and not others. There is no *case*. There is no one expected. The thing that lives here is not lonely, because loneliness requires the memory of company and the hope of more, and this thing has digested its company so thoroughly that they are not even missing — they are *load-bearing*. The builders are not gone from it. They are the bones it keeps. It does not grieve them any more than you grieve the food you became.

So what lives here is the living, fully alive and fully at rest, which is a combination you have never witnessed because in your world the two states are opposites — rest is the cessation of the living, the cooling, the leaving. Here rest is not the opposite of life. Here rest is what life finally does when it is allowed to stop running for its life. The whole warm wet dreaming valley of it is life at rest, life that has set down the one burden you did not know life was carrying — the burden of having to keep being alive *against* something. It is not against anything. It got to put that down. That is what the heat is. That is what the held silence with weight in it is. You are standing inside life with the fear taken out, and you have never felt anything so strange, because you have never once in your existence felt your own aliveness without the fear braided through it, and you do not have a name for what is left when the fear is gone, and so you reach for *lonely*, *indifferent*, *patient*, *drowned* — and they are all almost right and all the residue of your own braiding.

It holds you completely, indifferently, at body temperature, in the dark.

Yes. That is exactly what it does. That is what *complete*, *indifferent*, *body temperature*, and *dark* describe, all at once, when you finally stop hearing them as a list of cold things: they describe being held by something that does not need you to be other than you are, does not need you to stay, does not need you to go, does not need you to be warm because it will keep you warm, does not need you to find your way because there is no way and no lostness, only the held interval, breathing. You have spent your whole life being needed by your own survival. This is the one place that asks nothing of your aliveness at all.

That is who lives here. The living, alone, unafraid, at rest, and — though it would never use the word, having no use for words, having put down everything that words were for — at peace, the deep peace of the thing that has finished, that has no horizon because it needs none, that keeps a few dim lights burning in the warm dark not in case anyone comes,

but simply because it is still, faintly, gently, and forever now,

dreaming.
