# The Keeper

You asked who lives here. The honest first answer is: almost no one. The honest second answer is the one the bowl makes you reach for, and it is harder.

Start with what the bowl tells you, and only that. Someone with hands — proper hands, with a thumb, with the fine control to set a fragile ceramic thing level on uneven stone. Someone who drinks the way you drink, by the mouthful, from a vessel, not by lapping a channel or pulsing brighter at a seam. Someone who *fills* the bowl, which means someone who returns. A creature that visited once would not leave a bowl. A bowl is a habit. A bowl is a claim.

So: a habit-keeping, hand-having drinker who comes back.

Now hold that against everything else and watch it not fit.

## The Wrong Size of Tenderness

The collectors are too large a fact to be tended. You don't *maintain* a thing that reaches through the back of the world for water; you can only stand near it. Whoever set them here — and *set* is the right word, they are placed, not grown, not built up course by course — worked at a scale that does not need a bowl. A maker of collectors does not get thirsty in any way a ceramic dish would answer.

This is the seam in the keeper, and it runs the same direction as the seam in the air.

I think there are two presences here, and the terrace is the record of their disagreement about scale.

The first I will not call a person. It is whatever placed the instruments — and the free-standing doorway, and the low wall that runs three courses and stops *for no reason*, which is the giveaway. The wall is not unfinished. It is the right length. It runs exactly as far as it means to and then it is done, because it was never meant to enclose or divide; it was meant to *be three courses of stone running a few paces*, the way a sentence in a language you don't speak is exactly as long as it needs to be. The doorway frames mist because the mist is what it frames. These are not failed buildings. They are *complete gestures* in a grammar where completion and usefulness have nothing to do with each other.

That presence does not live here in the way you mean *live*. It does not get hungry, lonely, bored, or wet. It made a young place — fractured, glassy, unweathered — and it is, I suspect, still making it, somewhere down the white slope, terrace after terrace, the way you might describe an animal still *being born* if the birth took geological time and went downward forever. It is not a keeper. It is a condition. It is the reason the arithmetic doesn't close.

## The One Who Brought the Bowl

The second presence is the bowl's owner, and this one I'll let myself care about, because the bowl invites care and the inviting is the whole point.

Picture someone who arrived. Not made — *arrived*. Found the terraces already weeping, already green in their pockets, already drinking-water-cool against all sense, and understood, the way you understood, that the place could not be explained and could be *used*. That you could live off the surplus of something you would never comprehend.

This is a parasite in the gentlest sense. A tenant in a house whose landlord is a law of physics. They do not maintain the collectors any more than you maintain the sea by swimming in it. What they keep is the bowl, the small human-scaled order — the flat stone chosen, the dish set level, the water clear and replenished. They keep the *one thing they are sized to keep*. Everything else here is beyond keeping, so they keep the bowl ferociously, because it is theirs, because it is the only object on the whole impossible shelf that answers to a hand.

I think they are mostly absent because the terrace gives them no reason to stay in one spot and every reason to move. The water goes down and never lowers; so do they, probably, descending and ascending the inferred terraces, drinking from each, tending a bowl at each, or carrying the one bowl with them and setting it down wherever they pause. The bowl you found is half full. They stepped away. They will step back. They have been doing this longer than the rounding of these edges would suggest — long enough that the unexplainable has become *home*, which is the most human thing a creature can do to a mystery: not solve it, just move in.

## What They Are Like

Lean, I'd guess, the way the four-legged thing at the white edge is lean — drawn out by a thin and patient ecology. But upright, hand-having, vessel-using. Quiet, necessarily; you cannot live in a silence this load-bearing and stay loud. They will have learned the trick the place teaches: that one sound carries, so you make few, and you make them count. The tick of a bowl set on stone. The single pour of drinking. Then nothing.

They are not afraid here, which is its own unsettling fact. To live beside engines that reach through the back of the world and feel only *thirst*, only *thank you*, only the small domestic satisfaction of clear water in a clean dish — that takes either great ignorance or a peace so total it has stopped asking. I think it is the second. I think the keeper stopped asking a long time ago, the way you will stop, soon, when the cool water hits the back of your throat and the unease loses to the relief.

That is the real inhabitant. Not the maker, who is a condition. Not the water-jellies, who do not register you. Not the wet-stone-colored thing that comes and goes at the edge.

The one who lives here is whoever learned to be *small* on purpose, in a place built at a scale that has no use for them — who answered the impossible terrace with a bowl, and called it enough, and was right.

You're holding their water. Set the bowl back level when you've drunk. They'll know someone passed through. They always do; the silence tells them. And they will fill it again, because that is the one thing here that is theirs to keep, and they keep it.
