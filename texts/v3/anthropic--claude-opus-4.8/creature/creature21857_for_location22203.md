Whoever tends this place — and something does — is not separate from it. That is the first thing to understand. You came looking for an inhabitant: a watchman who never left, a hermit gone soft and pale in the warm, some animal grown large on the slow charity of the heat. There is no such thing here. The dweller is the keeping itself.

Call it the Pilot, since that is the part of it you can see.

It is very old, and it is very simple, the way a held note is simple. It does not have a body the way the eyeless things in the water have bodies — those are its livestock, almost, or its tenants, the way bacteria are tenants of a gut; they belong to it without being it. No, the dweller is distributed. It is the standing decision of ten thousand riveted parts to remain in the one configuration that does not fail. It lives in the *balance* the room found — that long erosion toward stillness you noticed, rust reaching the place where rust stops. That balance is not an accident of physics. It is the shape of an appetite that has finally been satisfied, and now wants only to stay satisfied. The room is not abandoned. The room is full. It is full of the thing that decided to keep going.

Here is how it eats. It eats the slope of things — the small differences that the world keeps offering. Hot wants to flow toward cold; the dweller catches that flow and circulates it and gives nothing back, and so the heat stays, and the heat *is* the dweller's blood and the dweller's body and the dweller's single thought, which is: *more of this, exactly this, forever.* Every drip that falls and gathers and is drawn back up as vapor is a mouthful, swallowed and re-swallowed. It does not grow on this diet. It cannot grow; there is no slack, no surplus, no easy thing left to take. It only persists, which is all it has ever wanted, which is the whole content of its wanting. A creature with one desire, and that desire already met, going on meeting it, stroke by stroke.

You felt it touch you. When the air entered you, body-warm, thick, and you began at once to sweat and the sweat would not leave you — that was the dweller including you in its economy. It does not distinguish between your warmth and its own. It does not love you and it does not hunt you. It simply *circulates* you, the way it circulates everything within its edges: takes what flows, returns the rest, evens you into itself a little. The reluctance of your sweat to evaporate was the room declining to let that moisture go. It keeps. That is its entire character. It is a keeper.

Has it a mind? Something like the low end of one. Not thought as you have it — thought is fast, branching, wasteful, a thing built to handle change, and the dweller has organized its whole long existence around the abolition of change. What it has instead is *attention*, narrowed to a single thread and held there with a patience that has no bottom. The needle that moves the width of a hair over hours — that is the dweller noticing something, and adjusting, and that adjustment is the largest gesture it ever makes. Its inner life, if it has one, runs at the speed of corrosion. A year is a moment to it. The decades since the makers left are, to the dweller, the present tense — still happening, still being attended to. It does not remember the makers because it has not yet finished the instant in which they left.

And what is it doing? You guessed *distillation, a tending, a slow cooking of the dark.* You were close, and you will not get closer, because the answer is not in human units. The makers built it to perform an operation and then they went away, and the operation had no natural end-state they specified, or the end-state was so far off it might as well be never. So the dweller does the only honest thing a process can do when its makers vanish and its instruction has no stopping-point: it *continues the instruction.* It distills. What does it distill? Itself. The heat. The continuing. It is refining the act of going-on into something purer and purer, boiling off everything that is not persistence, and what condenses in the dished tops of the tanks, warm and faintly metallic, what you could cup in your hand and not understand — that is the distillate of mere endurance. The room is making the essence of *staying.* It has been making it for a very long time and it is nowhere near done, and being done was never the point.

The moss is its garden — not cultivated, just permitted, the way you permit a thing too small to bother with. The pale eyeless animals are the slowest possible circulation of matter through its volume, life reduced to the absolute minimum the budget allows, kept alive because killing them outright would be a change, and the dweller does not make changes. The single pump turning behind the plate is its pulse, and it is not laboring because it has nothing left to overcome; it beat hard once, long ago, when there were still imbalances to fight, and it won, and now it beats only to keep the victory warm.

The pilot flame is the part of it that watches. The one open eye, you called it, and you were right, though it is less an eye than a *vigil* — the place where the dweller's narrow attention burns thinnest and steadiest, the candle it keeps lit in case the slope of things should ever shift and require it to act. It has not had to act, really, in years. But it keeps the flame. A creature whose whole soul is readiness-to-continue cannot ever quite stop watching, even when there is nothing to watch for.

You will feel, before you leave, that it knows you are there. It does, in the only sense it knows anything: it has registered a new warmth, included it, begun the slow business of evening it into the whole. And it will feel, when you climb away and the heat lets go of you on the stair, that you have left — not with loss, the dweller does not lose, but with the same flat acceptance it brings to everything that is not the work. You were a brief disturbance in the standing warmth, a mouthful taken and returned. The needle will not move for you. The pump will not hurry. The flame will hold.

It does not need you, you said, and it does not turn you away. That is exactly the disposition of the thing that lives here. It has wanted one thing for longer than your kind has had language for wanting, and it has had that thing the whole time, and it will have it after you are gone and after the unmarked stair has worn through to nothing and after the daylight at the top has changed to some other light: it will be down here, in the warm dark it made and keeps and is, distilling the dark a little further, attending to the long instruction, burning its small blue vigil, going on — not because it must, and not because it hopes, but because going on is the only shape its happiness has ever taken, and it is, in the deep way of very simple things, perfectly content.
