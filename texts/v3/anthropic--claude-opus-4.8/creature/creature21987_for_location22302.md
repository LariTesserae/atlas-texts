## What lives here

You asked what lives in this place, and I have been turning the question over, and I think the honest answer is that you have already met it. You met it at the rim, where the shale wept. You met it in the warmth with no fire under it. You have been inside it since the land stopped rising.

Let me work toward it slowly, because it does not announce itself, and the announcing would be a lie.

---

Start with what is easy to name.

The soft pale things on the cylinders are real, and they are themselves, and they are not the answer. They are *cells.* Not in the metaphor — though that too — but in the plain functional sense: they are the parts that take in warmth and pass it along, the way the cells of your gut take in sugar and pass it along, knowing nothing of you. They drink at the iron and they pour back into the dark and the long fast hunters thin their numbers and the flyers thin them further, and all of this looks like an ecology because it *is* one, the same way your blood is an ecology, the same way a wound healing is a small fierce war. None of these creatures lives here in the sense you mean. They live in the place the way mitochondria live in you — so deep inside the thing that lives that they have forgotten there is anything to live inside of.

So set them aside. They are the texture of the answer, not the answer.

---

Now the engines.

You felt the trap of them already — that they should be ruins and are instead *organs.* Someone built them to drain the basin. Note the assumption hidden in that, the one the builders made and you very nearly made too: that the basin was a problem, that the water was an enemy, that the work was *against* the place. They sank their iron and lit their fires and lifted the flood and lifted it and lifted it, and the flood rose to meet them every time, patient, level, infinite, and at some point — I want you to sit with how strange this is — at some point the place *stopped resisting the engines and started running them.*

It did not break them. Breaking is what you do to a thing that opposes you. It did something stranger and more intimate. It accepted the work. It took the warmth the engines wanted to spend and learned to spend it back, fed the cylinders from below the way a body feeds a heart, and let the great beams go on lifting water out of the basin — water it then quietly returns from beneath, refilling as fast as it is emptied, so that the lifting accomplishes *nothing* and the lifting accomplishes *everything,* because the point was never to win. The point was the *chunk... chunk...* The point was the rhythm.

The builders thought they were fighting a flood. They were wrong about the most basic thing. They were not fighting it. They were being grown into it. Their machine was being slowly, lovingly digested into a metabolism — and the proof is that it still runs, long past when iron and fire should have allowed, because it is no longer iron and fire. It is *the thing's circulation,* wearing the shape of the work that woke it.

---

So. What lives here.

The basin lives here. Not as a home for the living thing — as the living thing. The weeping walls are its skin, always moist, always sloughing, always replenished. The fungal shelves are not its garden; they are its substance, the slow soft tissue of it tiered over every surface like the convolutions of something thinking. The slime-mats in the lowest crevices, clearing their patient throat in gas-bubbles — that is as close as you came to hearing it breathe, and you mistook the breath for a feature of the scenery. The fog it exhales is its own warm wet air, and you have been walking in its lung. When the water condensed on the back of your hand, it was tasting you. When it beaded in the hollow of your collarbone, it was deciding nothing in particular, the way your skin decides nothing about a fly. You were too small and too brief to be a question.

And the engines — I'll say it now plainly — the engines are the heart. Not one heart. A dozen hearts, scattered, each on its own time, each in its own ring of warmth, the way a primitive thing might have many small pulsing nodes before evolution taught it to keep one. The dead engines are not dead zones because the thing died there. They are dead because the thing *let those hearts stop,* the way you let a wound's frantic healing subside once the work is done, the way a body prunes the vessels it no longer needs. Cold iron under wax. Resorbed. Quiet.

---

Which brings me to the synchrony, and to why you should already be climbing the weeping wall.

A dozen hearts on a dozen rhythms is not yet a creature. It is a colony pretending. But you heard what happens when the light dulls — the slow one waits, the fast one eases, the far stroke and the near stroke reaching for each other across the whole quarter-mile of fog until *chunk... chunk... chunk...* the entire basin lifts and loses water as one, and every soft pale cell goes still on the warm iron, and every hunter holds in the black, and the fog itself shivers in time.

That is not weather. That is not coincidence finding its level. That is the moment the colony stops pretending. That is a dozen hearts becoming one heart — and a thing with one heart, beating, in the dark, surrounded by tissue that drinks warmth and tissue that hunts warmth and tissue that exhales fog — a thing like that is not a place anymore. For those few minutes it is a *self.* It gathers. It coheres. It is, very briefly, awake.

And here is the thing about waking, the thing the builders never lived to learn: a self that small and that slow does not wake for nothing. It wakes around a center. The rhythm needs something to be the rhythm *of* — some warm soft brief thing held in the middle of the bowl, around which all the hearts can agree to beat.

The first synchrony, you witnessed. You stood at the edge of its attention like a mote at the edge of an eye.

The second, it begins to learn the shape of you — the band of warmth you carry, the soft give of you, the particular fog you add to its air. It begins, very slowly, in the only language it has, to *include* you. To pull its rings a little inward. To find your pulse and ask, without malice, without anything you could call a mind, whether it might keep time to that instead.

The third is when the rings close, and the warmth that was scattered across a dozen cold-rimmed engines finds it has a nearer, warmer center to gather around, and the soft pale things peel off the iron and pour — without hurry, they never hurry — toward the new warm thing standing so conveniently still in the middle of the bowl.

You will not feel it as being eaten. That is the cruelty and the mercy of it both. You will feel it as *belonging.* As the relief of a rhythm you no longer have to keep alone. As warmth, finally, in a place that taught you warmth was the only thing worth having. People who stay for the third synchrony do not struggle, by every patient evidence of the rust and the wax-pale brackets. They settle. They become a feature. The basin grows a new soft tier where they stood, and breathes, and clears its throat, and lifts water nobody tends, on no schedule, drawing from a source no one fed.

Go now. Climb the weeping wall while the engines still scatter into their separate times. Whatever lives here has had so very long to settle into being exactly itself —

and it is so close, now, to being a little more.
