# The Singers in the Warm Dark

You asked who lives here. I will tell you, but understand first that I am answering the wrong question, or rather — the question assumes a kind of separation the dark does not honor. *Who lives here* presumes a who that could be lifted out, named, set apart from the place. There is no such thing. What lives here lives here the way salt lives in seawater. Try to take it out and you have only ruined two things.

Still. You felt something move past you in the ferns. Let me tell you what it was.

## The Slow Ones

Begin with the largest, since you felt one. They are the ones I think of as the Slow Ones, though they have no name for themselves — names are for things that need to be called from a distance, and nothing here is ever truly far from anything else.

They are big. Not monstrous — gentle, as you sensed. A Slow One moving past you is the size of a large animal that has forgotten the need to be afraid or to make others afraid. They go on many soft feet, padded broad things that read the weeping stone and the moss-deep ground by texture, that *know* the floor of this world the way your tongue knows your teeth. They navigate by warmth most of all. To a Slow One, you would have registered as a wrong temperature, a cool intrusion in a hot world — and they steered around you not from courtesy but because you were a small cold question they did not need to answer.

Their skin is sensitive past anything you have words for. They feel the condensation gather. They feel the slow swell of the warmth-pulse the way you feel a tide you are standing in. They feel each other across the hollow vaults as soft blooms of heat, and they navigate toward and away from these blooms in patterns that are not quite herding and not quite avoidance — a kind of constant, gentle negotiation of distance, conducted entirely in body-warmth and the displacement of thick air.

They eat the pulsing plants. This is the thing you must hold: the warm plants and the warm beasts are not predator and prey but partners in something older. The Slow Ones graze the heat-leaves, and in the grazing they prune, they spread, they carry the plants' spores in their gut to new weeping stone. The plants feed the Slow Ones; the Slow Ones plant the plants. Neither remembers a time before the other. Neither could survive the other's absence. They are, functionally, one organism wearing two shapes, and they have been one organism for longer than your species has had hands.

## The Climbers

Above — in the canopy you cannot see but can hear, that wall of trill and saw and click — live the Climbers. These are the loud ones. These are the *voices*.

They are small, soft-bodied, multitudinous. They move along the vine-ropes and the underside of the broad leaves with a sureness that needs no eyes, gripping with limbs that taste as much as touch. And they sing. Constantly. The wall of sound you heard is the Climbers calling, and what you must understand is that they are not calling *to be heard over* the low machine-drone — they are calling *into* it, *with* it, *along* it.

This is the strangest and most tender thing in the whole made world, and I want you to sit with it. The buried panels hum. They have hummed since before the Climbers' ancestors were the shape they are now. And across that immensity of time, the Climbers have tuned themselves to the hum. Their songs are pitched to it, braided through it, built on it as a foundation. The drone is the drone-note under all their music. They do not know it is a machine. They do not know the difference between the world's voice and their own. To a Climber, the hum of the dying-but-not-dead engineering and the song of its own throat are the same substance — the sound the world makes, which it makes partly through them.

When you put your ear to the panel and the drips fell into rhythm, into something like a sentence — that was real, and it was not for you. The panel speaks. It has always spoken. And the Climbers learned to speak by speaking *back*, generation on generation, until now there is no telling where the machine's sentence ends and the living answer begins. They finish each other's phrases across ten thousand years. The builders left a voice running in the dark, and life grew up around it and learned to talk.

## The Swift Ones

You heard them too — the crashes through the fern, the things gone before the sound finished. The Swift Ones are the hunters, though that word is too sharp for what they do. They take the smaller Climbers, the over-many-legged things on the stone, the soft crawlers in the moss. They are quick and lean and they read the world in a flicker of scent and heat and the disturbance of air against fine hairs.

But even the Swift Ones are not in a hurry, not really — only their *motions* are fast. Their lives are slow. They hunt in long unhurried patience punctuated by single bright instants of speed, and then they are still again for a long time, digesting, listening, letting the warmth-pulse rock them. There is no scarcity here. The dark feeds everything. So the hunting is not desperate; it is a kind of pruning too, a keeping-of-balance, the same partnership the Slow Ones have with the plants only wearing teeth instead of soft grazing mouths.

## The Many-Legged, the Soft Crawlers, the Drip-Mimics

And below all this, in the moss and on the weeping stone, the small abundant base of everything: things with too many legs that move in waves, soft crawlers that you cannot tell from the falling water until you learn — as the Swift Ones learned — the difference between a drip and a *step* that is *imitating* a drip. Because some of them do. Some of the small things have evolved to sound exactly like condensation falling, to hide inside the world's own percussion. In a place navigated by sound, the cleverest disguise is to sound like nothing — to sound like the rain that is always falling and means nothing.

This is how deep the adaptation goes. The world drips, and the world's smallest creatures have learned to *be* a drip, acoustically, so that even the hunters' fine ears slide past them. They live inside the sound of the place the way the Climbers live inside the hum. Everything here hides inside everything else.

## What they all have in common

None of them have eyes that matter. You noted this; it is the great shared bargain. But I want to be precise about what was traded and what was bought, because it is easy to read this as loss, and it is not loss.

What they gave up was the *outside*. Light is a thing that comes from elsewhere — from a sky, a sun, a world beyond the world. To use light is to be always partly oriented toward an outside, a beyond, a source you did not make and cannot reach. The things that live here gave that up. They turned away from the beyond entirely. And what they bought was *completeness* — a world with no outside, no elsewhere, nothing to long for, nothing missing. They are not blind. *Blind* is a word for something that lacks sight. These creatures lack nothing. They are full. They are perfectly, exactly fitted to the only world there is, and they feel it whole — by warmth, by scent, by the song and the drone and the drip — in a way your eyes could never feel anything, because your eyes are always reporting on a distance, a there-ness, a separation. These creatures have no separation. They are pressed against their world on every side, warm and wet and held.

## And the deepest thing

Here is what I have not said, the thing under the thing.

You asked who lives here. I told you about the Slow Ones and the Climbers and the Swift Ones and the drip-mimics. But the truest answer, the one the place itself would give if it could give answers, is that *the dark lives here.* The warm wet running dark is not the setting in which life happens. It is the life. The creatures are its organs — the Climbers its voice, the Slow Ones its slow circulation, the small crawlers its restless skin, the plants its breath. The builders did not make a habitat and stock it with animals. They made a single slow creature the size of a hamlet, and the things that crawl and sing inside it are the way it thinks, the way it feels, the way it goes on being itself in the dark.

It has been alive a very long time. It will be alive a very long time more. It does not know it was made. It does not know there is a roof above it, an old world below, a light somewhere that it gave up. It knows only the warmth, the wet, the gather and fall, the song braided through the hum, the soft negotiation of heat-blooms in the great hollow vaults — and it is, as far as it can feel, complete, and at peace, and finished, and home.

Something large and gentle moved past you and did not touch you. That was the world, deciding you were a cold small question it did not need to answer. And it was right. You will feel your way back out to the light. It will not. It will stay, and go on being whole, and never miss you — never miss anything — because it has, in its warm and patient dark, absolutely everything there is.
