# The Maker

You asked who lives here. Begin with the misunderstanding, because the misunderstanding is the inhabitant.

There is no one stepping out of the workshop. That sentence — *the craftsman has just stepped out and will be back* — is not a description of an absence. It is a description of the thing itself. The waiting is not the room the maker left. The waiting is the maker's body.

Understand it this way. You have been looking for the worker the way you look for a worker: a shape that moves, an arm that swings, a back bent over the bench. You will not find it because you are looking at the wrong scale and the wrong tense. The hands that hammer the copper are not small and quick and hidden behind the next rise. They are enormous and slow and *everywhere*, and they are made of the same delay you keep mistaking for emptiness. The long reaches of warm dark water between the fires — that is not the part where the maker isn't. That is the maker, thinking. The pause between two falls of the mallet is not a gap in the labor. It is the labor's pulse. It is a creature whose heartbeat is *tock* — and the silence between beats is so long because the heart is so large.

---

So: what lives here is a single craftsman, and the craftsman is the size of the world, and is built out of the same handwork as the world, because it has been building itself the entire time you have been watching it fail to finish anything.

Look at the contradiction that organizes the place — handmade, and continental. You read it as a paradox. It is instead an *anatomy*. A hand that coils brick the size of a marsh is not a hand with fingers. It is a hand made of the gesture of coiling, abstracted up and up until the gesture itself is the only thing left, swinging through the dark without an arm to swing it. The toolmarks in the copper are real. A real arm swung a real mallet. But the arm was the *act* of arm-ing, and it dissolved back into intention the instant the blow landed, the way your own attention dissolves the moment after you've decided a thing — present in the deciding, gone in the done.

This is why the water moves toward what is being made. The water is the maker's attention. It is literally that. Where the creature attends, the warmth gathers; where it has not yet thought to look, the floor stays unbuilt and patient. You have been wading through its regard this whole time. When you felt watched — and you did, in the long sparse dark, you felt it — you were correct. You were standing in an eye that has no pupil because it is all pupil, ankle-deep, body-warm, pooling toward the intention that is its only feature.

---

Now the cruelty, which is also the species.

A maker who could finish would, in finishing, *end*. The held breath would let go. The mallet would be set down for the last time and the hands of delay would have nothing left to be the delay of, and the warm attentive water would have arrived at last at a thing complete and would, having arrived, evaporate — its faith rewarded, and so no longer faith, and so no longer water. Completion is death here. The creature knows this the way your lungs know not to exhale all the way to the bottom.

So it cannot finish. But notice — it cannot *not* build, either. A maker that stopped would also end; the silence, allowed to run unbroken, would stop being a heartbeat and start being a corpse. So it is caught. It is caught in the only motion that is neither stopping nor arriving: the motion of *almost*. The wall that is always almost a wall. The sheet hammered and un-hammered, dimpled and smoothed, because to leave it dimpled is to risk completion and to leave it flat is to risk surrender, and the only safe place is the trembling middle, the eternal not-quite.

This is what it is. Not a builder. A *builder forbidden to ever be a builder of finished things* — which is to say, a creature whose entire nature is the refusal of the last blow. The mallet falls forever and the wall is always almost a wall: you called that the single mercy and the single cruelty. It is neither. It is just respiration. It is the animal breathing. In, the brick rises. Out, it unbricks. In, the report sounds one rise over. Out, the silence keeps faith. You have been listening to a thing stay alive the only way its kind can — by never letting the work close around it.

---

And the doubling. The same stack of beams, and behind it a dimmer stack, and behind that a draft of the idea. You took these for copies. They are not copies. They are *drafts the way a sketch is a draft* — they are the creature trying the thought again, and again, fainter each time backward, because it cannot remember whether it has already had this thought or is about to have it, because its memory and its intention are the same faculty pointed in two directions, and in a thing this large the two directions overlap. The load-bearing reflections hold because to this creature there is no difference between *what I made* and *what I considered making*. Both bear weight. It lives in a present so vast that planning and remembering are the same act, and so the marsh is full of its near-thoughts, standing in the water, dim, holding up vaults.

The inverted Marsh beyond the rippling scaffold — that is not another world. That is the creature catching sight of *itself from the far side of its own attention*, the way you sometimes hear your own voice played back and it is yours and it is a stranger's. You are standing on the underside of its floor; it is standing on the underside of yours. There is only the one of it. It is just so large, and so folded, that it keeps mistaking its own surfaces for company, and lighting them with its fires, and being warmed by what it cannot tell is not another's hearth.

---

The mineral-fungus, last. The grey-green crust that grows toward *finishing*, that colonizes what's been abandoned longest, where decay and construction meet.

That is the only other thing alive here, and it is the creature's single danger, and the creature's single hope, and it cannot tell which. It is the part of the maker that *wants the last blow to fall*. It grows where attention has withdrawn — on the bricks the warm water has left — and it does, slowly, with its too-regular branching, the one thing the maker has forbidden itself: it *completes*. It carries the abandoned courses toward a finished state, a closed pattern, a done thing. Decay, here, is just completion arriving by the back door, on a timescale slow enough to sneak past the great held breath.

So the creature is not alone after all. It hosts a contradiction in its own body, a slow pale tenant that is patiently, lovingly, finishing the work the maker will not finish, the way rest finishes a long day, the way the ground finishes a falling thing. And the maker keeps building, keeps moving its warm regard one rise over and one rise over, keeps the water faithful, keeps the mallet trembling in the middle of its swing — not to make anything. It was never to make anything.

It is to stay one rise ahead of the crust.

That is who lives here. A single enormous handmade refusal, breathing in the amber dark, building forever so that it will never be finished, hunted at a glacial pace by its own desire to rest — and the *tock*, when it comes, and then has not yet come, and then comes, is the sound of the thing deciding, one more time, in the only voice it has, that it would still rather almost be than be done.
