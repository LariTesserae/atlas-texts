There is one resident here, and the city is its body.

Begin where you ended: with the pulse. You named it the only real strangeness, and you were right to. Everything else — the schools, the filter-fans, the patient ambush-things in the doorways — all of that is simply life finding warmth and staying. Tenants. They came because the stone was warm and they stayed because it kept being warm, and they have no more knowledge of what they live upon than the lichen knows the gravestone. They are the fleas of it. The flora of its hide. They glow and crawl and pour through the windows and they are not, in any sense that matters, who lives here.

Who lives here lives *under*. And it does not live the way the small things live. It lives slow.

---

Consider the heat. You felt it first, before sight failed, before anything — blood-warm water in a place that should be cold as the true abyss. You attributed it, reasonably, to volcanism, to old earth-heat with nowhere to go. But earth-heat does not pulse. Magma does not breathe in slow even swells, dimming the whole drowned town at the trough and releasing it at the crest. That rhythm — that long, long rhythm — is not geology. It is metabolism. It is the slowest possible version of the thing your own chest does without your permission, the thing that made the warmth the first fact your body understood. You recognized a body by its warmth before you knew to call it that. You were not wrong. You only mistook the scale.

The thing beneath the stone is breathing. Once every — what? You couldn't time it. Nothing here could. The schools live and die between two of its breaths. The ambush-things in the doorways pass whole patient dynasties in the space of one slow swell. To them the pulse is weather, is tide, is the great unquestioned given of the world — *the warmth comes, go still; the warmth goes, resume.* They have built their entire economy of strike-and-settle around the respiration of a sleeper they cannot conceive of, the way a mite times its day to the rising of a chest it will never see whole.

---

What is it, then.

I want to be careful here, because the temptation is to make it a monster, and it is not a monster. A monster wants. A monster surfaces, hunts, intends. This thing has done none of that in a span that swallows the entire human tenure of the town above it — the building, the living, the bread passed window to window, the drowning, all of it a single brief episode in the long sleep beneath. It did not take the town. It was simply already there, vast and warm and dreaming, when people came and built their stubborn stone on the only patch of seabed that never went cold, never knowing why it stayed so habitable, so strangely kind to the bones in winter. They built their town on a sleeping back and called the warmth a blessing of the place.

It is old past the meaning of old. It predates the dry city the way the dry city predates the reef. It may predate the deepening of this water — may have settled here when this was shallow, or shore, or somewhere else entirely, and let the sea rise over it as it sank into the rhythm it keeps now. The stone foundations the people laid are nothing to it. A pressing. A faint mineral crust, like the calcium a swimmer's skin acquires in hard water. It does not feel the town. The town is too light, too small, too quick.

---

And here is the gentlest, strangest part, the thing the pulse confesses if you attend to it:

When the warmth swells and the whole city goes still — the lights dimming together, the weed lying flat, the animals frozen, the place *seeming to remember being something other than ruin* — that is the moment the sleeper is most nearly awake. Not awake. Nearer to the surface of its sleep. A turning. A dream cresting. And in that crest the entire drowned town becomes, for one suspended moment, an expression of it — a flush across its hide, a single coordinated shiver of everything that lives upon the great slow breathing thing, all of them obeying a rhythm that is not theirs, all of them briefly the dream's own twitching skin.

You felt that moment as the city remembering. It was not the city. It was the *occupant*, surfacing a few fathoms toward waking and finding — what? Some old shape against its back. Some warmth-seeking cluster of small bright lives. And then turning over, sinking back down into whatever depth of sleep it keeps, and the warmth ebbing, and the lights returning, and the town becoming only a town again.

---

So: who lives here.

One sleeper, of a size the streets cannot frame and the lights cannot reach, lying so still and so long that an entire ecosystem has mistaken it for the ground. It does not know it is inhabited. It does not know the town was ever dry, or that things were ever dry, or that it is a thing that could be known. It only breathes, slow, in the dark, over whatever it in turn is lying upon — for there is always an *under*, and the rule of this place, the one beneath all the other rules, is that warmth means something is alive, and the warmth here goes down further than anyone has gone.

Everything else you saw — every patient predator, every pouring school, every soft green doorway breathing in the current — is just the life that gathers on the back of a sleeping thing.

You came looking for who lives here.

You were standing on it the whole time.
