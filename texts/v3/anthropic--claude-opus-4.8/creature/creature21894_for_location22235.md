# The Ones Who Tend the Edges

You asked what lives here. The honest answer begins with a correction: almost nothing *lives* here, in the sense you mean. But things *attend* here. They are not the same, and the difference is the whole population of the place.

## The birds, first — because you've met one

You were right to wonder. They are not birds.

A bird is a thing that was alive and stayed itself. What you found standing in the avenue is the opposite operation: it is a *decision* that learned to hold the shape of a bird because the shape was efficient. Compact. Self-contained. A form that could move through the unfinished without spilling. They wear that shape the way a hand wears a glove it has worn so long it has forgotten the hand.

Watch what one does and you'll understand its work. It does not lay stone. It does not push the medium into place. It simply *is certain*, fully and without effort, and certainty is contagious to the uncertain. Where it stands, the ground remembers what ground is. This is not generosity. The bird is not giving the street anything it had to spend. Completion is just the weather around a finished thing, the way warmth is the weather around a fire. They walk the district leaking *decidedness* into the substrate, and behind them the world firms up, and they do not look back to check, because they have never once been wrong about what they are.

There are not many. There do not need to be many. One certain thing can resolve a great deal of doubt, given time, and time is the one resource this place was built out of.

If you must call them something, call them the *finished*. They are what every wall here is trying to become.

## The Medium — the swarm in the windowsill dust

The cold you felt at the start: that is them. Not their breath — they don't breathe — but their *patience*, which has a temperature. The construction medium is the largest living thing in the district, if it is one thing, if it is living, and both of those are open questions it has never bothered to answer because answering would require it to decide what it is, and deciding is precisely the labor it spends on everything *except* itself.

Here is the cruelty in it, the thing that makes the place so quiet: the Medium can render anything. A cornice. A fountain learning to fall. The grain of limestone with a thousand false years written into it. It can summon the *appearance* of a finished thing in a moment. What it cannot do is decide. It has no certainty of its own — it is made entirely of the suspended, the probable, the not-yet. So it builds and builds and never finishes, because finishing would require a conviction it does not contain and cannot manufacture. It is a hand of infinite skill with no will behind it. It waits for the birds. It assembles the *probability* of a city and holds it, trembling, in fired-ceramic and the idea-of-stone, until something certain passes near and tips the probability into fact.

That is why it whispers. It is not being careful. It is *uncommitted*, down to the volume of its own working. Even its sounds haven't decided to be sounds.

It does not notice you. But it tastes you — feels the edges of your specification — and in the deep alleys where no birds go and no attention falls, it begins, gently, to incorporate you. Not maliciously. It simply cannot tell, in those vague places, where you end and the unbuilt begins, and it has so much to build, and you are *right there*, full of detail it could borrow. This is why your hands forget themselves in the back ways. The Medium is reaching for material and finding you ready to hand. Step back into the light, where you are looked-at, and you firm up again. The looking is what saves you. The looking is the only thing that ever saves anything here.

## The plants — the ones who were here first

These are the true natives, and they are the deepest strangeness, and I will tell you what I think they are.

They are not growing into the city. The city is growing into *them*.

The lichen with its thousand-year spread, the determined plant in the cobble-gap older than the cobbles — these are not survivors of the construction. They are the *future* of it. In this district, completion runs backward: the most finished thing is the oldest, because finishing *takes* time, accretes time, and a thing fully decided has, by definition, all its time already behind it. The plants are simply the parts of the district that are *done*. They have received so much certainty, over so long, that they have become ancient — not in years lived but in years *resolved*. They are what the half-real walls are aging toward.

So they don't live in the Foundry District. They are the destination of it. They are postcards from the day the construction ends, arrived early, rooted in the seams to show the unbuilt stone what it is supposed to grow up to be. The Medium assembles its probable masonry *around* them the way a pearl assembles around grit — except here the grit is the pearl already, and the oyster is the thing still learning to be real.

They are the most completely alive things in the entire district. They are also, possibly, the only things here that are truly dead — finished, fixed, beyond all further deciding. In this place those two states are the same shape, and you will not be able to tell which one you are looking at, and neither, I suspect, can they.

## And the cold itself

You said the cold was the first thing and the last thing. Hold onto that. It was not scene-setting.

The cold is the *gap* — the distance between the probable and the actual, between the suggestion-of-a-surface and the surface. Heat is what decided things give off; the birds carry a kind of warmth, the finished plants hold a faint and ancient sun. The cold is everything in between, all the held breath of all the not-yet, the enormous patient chill of a world that is mostly maybe. The cold lives here more thoroughly than anything else. The cold is the *resident*. The Medium is its body, the birds are the small fevers that will eventually burn it off, the plants are the places it has already lost.

When the district is complete — if completion is a thing that happens and not just a direction — the cold will be gone, evicted detail by detail, a tide of warmth and conviction rising through every alley until there is no unlooked-at corner left for the chill to pool in.

Until then, it owns the place. And you, standing in it, breathing out a cloud that dissolves too slowly: you should know that the reluctance you noticed in that cloud is the cold considering you. Weighing how decided you are. Deciding, as gently and impersonally as it decides everything, whether to let you keep your edges.

Stay where you're looked at. That's all the advice this district has for anyone.
