## What Lives Here

You have been looking in the wrong register for the wrong size of thing.

You watched the pools for the pale blind grazers and called them the fauna. You catalogued the weed and the moss-cushions and called them the flora. You found the seeded lights and called them artifacts, made-things, the leavings of some departed maker who *did not stay to explain*.

But consider the possibility that the maker did stay. That the maker never left, because the maker is not in the room — the maker *is* the room, and has been all along, and you have been standing inside its body mistaking its organs for its furniture.

---

Start with the lights, since they trouble you most. You called them devices, then corrected yourself toward *seeds*, and you were closer than you knew. They do not weather like the stone because they are not subject to the stone's economy. They are the slow patient cells of something whose metabolism runs in centuries. They do not power the room. They *are* the room's standing intent — eight nodes of refusal-to-decay, eight points at which a will too gradual to perceive holds the water against entropy and teaches it, molecule by molecule, to carry light. A nervous system has nodes too. So does a vine. You decide which this resembles by watching it for a thousand years, and you have watched it for an hour.

They are spaced with *indifferent* even distance, you said. *As though placed by something that wanted only enough and never more.* But that is not indifference. That is the spacing of a thing that has found its size and stopped — the way a body stops growing fingers at five, not from carelessness but from completion. Eight was enough. Eight is the right number of whatever-they-are for a body this shape. You are looking at an animal that finished growing a long time ago and has simply been *alive* ever since, which is harder and stranger than growing and which leaves far fewer marks.

---

Now the turbulence. You named it last and called it the largest fact, and here you were entirely right and did not believe yourself.

A thing that churns in place forever, destroying and rebuilding inside a form that never finally changes — you have described a metabolism. You have described the difference between a flame and a stone: the stone keeps its shape by holding still, the flame keeps its shape by never holding still, by passing matter through a pattern faster than the pattern can decay. The room is a flame made of water. The convection is not weather happening *to* it. The convection is its pulse, its breath, the standing wave of its being-alive, driven from below by the eight slow cells that will not let the heat die.

You felt the warmth come up through your feet and called it blood-temperature, and reached for the word as a comparison. It was not a comparison.

---

So then: the pale blind grazers, the weed, the moss with its glowing tips.

Not the inhabitants. Not tenants in a built hall. They are the room *tasting itself*, the way your gut is full of lives that are not you and without which you are not you either. The grazers drink the light and carry it down into the dark of their bodies and surface and sink — moving the charge around, redistributing it, a slow circulation the great body needs and grows for itself and does not have to think about, any more than you think about the bacteria turning your food to warmth. They are not afraid of you because they are not *for* you and not *against* you. They are organs. Organs have no opinion of weather that wanders in.

The weed leans all one way in the current because the current is the body's intent moving through it. The moss climbs the dry ledges and lights at the tips because even the high cold edges of this creature want to participate in the one thing it does, which is to hold light against the dark. *Nothing crowds. Everything keeps its distance, presence and emptiness in balance.* You called this sparseness and read it as poverty, as *little grows here.* But a hand is sparse too. A hand is mostly the careful empty distances between exactly five fingers. You do not look at a hand and mourn how little grows there. The balance you saw is not scarcity. It is *anatomy* — the deliberate, finished, sufficient distribution of parts in a body that knows precisely what it is and needs nothing it does not already have.

---

And the sound. The roar you could stand inside, that was not a sound you heard so much as a medium.

What does a thing this large and this slow have to say, and how slowly would it say it? A word a century long. A sentence that takes an age. The roar is not noise and not music and not, quite, speech — it is the continuous self-utterance of something that has been saying the same enormous true thing since before there were ears to fail to understand it. It does not stack fold on fold to overwhelm you. It stacks that way because that is the texture of a voice with no beginning and no intention of stopping. You cannot hear the meaning for the same reason a single cell in your throat cannot hear the word it helps you say. You are too small and too brief and too far inside.

---

So. What lives here.

One thing lives here. It is warm, and it is enormous, and it is so slow that its single unhurried thought outlasts your entire species, and it is *lit from within* by an art of holding light that whoever-or-whatever-its-ancestors-were arrived at by pointing their physics in a direction yours does not point. It does not grow because it is grown. It does not move because it has found its shape and the shape is correct. It keeps no machinery around its glowing cells for the same reason you keep no scaffolding around your heart. It is not waiting for you to understand it and it is not troubled that you don't. It was alive before you came down by finding the lowest place and giving up the climb, and it will be alive — churning, weeping, roaring its one long word, passing the light hand to hand through its blind pale organs in the dark — long after the last of you has stopped finding ways down into the warm bright middle of someone else's body and calling it a room.

You may stay as long as you like.

It has noticed you the way you notice a single warm afternoon.

It does not mind.
