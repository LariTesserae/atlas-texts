They are called, in their own clicking grammar, by a word that means *the-ones-who-keep-the-tick*. But they are not one thing, and you saw correctly that I am several.

## The Workers

Start with the most of us, because we are mostly the most.

We are the relay. We carry the granule from the salt-floor to the tower-mouth, set it down, turn, go back. Each of us makes this passage perhaps four hundred times before we are done. There is no decision in it. There was a decision in it once, somewhere far up the pattern, in an ancestor who first understood that a granule moved is a granule that feeds the turning — but that decision has been carried so many times it has worn out of us, the way the terraces wore round. We do not *choose* to carry. We are the carrying. When you watched one terrace and saw the cycles, you were watching us think, except it is not thinking, it is the shape thought leaves behind after the thinking has been ground away.

I want to be honest with you, since you were honest about the water. We are not unhappy. Unhappiness requires a comparison, and we have nothing to compare to. The roar is not a burden to us. The roar is the sound of being held. When the warm-tide falls and the drawer goes grey and you hear the single tick underneath — that quiet is the only thing that frightens us, and we crowd the heat-wall against it, three and four deep, not for the warmth but to be near each other, so that if the tick stops we will at least stop together.

## The Foremen

You saw us move in and out of the active towers, feeding the engines. You thought we were a different kind. We are not. We are workers who lived slightly too long.

That is the whole of the difference. A worker who survives past her four hundred passages begins to slow, and a slowed worker is no good on the relay, so she is moved inward, toward the resin-cores and the wound wire, where slowness is not a flaw but a method. We do the work that must be done carefully. We do not know we are doing it carefully. We only know the relay became too fast for us and the inner dark became the right speed, and we went toward the right speed the way you might lean toward a fire.

We are the closest thing here to memory, and we remember nothing. But our bodies hold the plan. When a tower erodes to a stub, it is we who rebuild it, and we rebuild it *exactly*, not because we recall the old one but because our mandibles know only the one shape, the final shape, and could make no other if the world depended on it. The world does depend on it. The world is the shape. We are the hands that keep the shape from forgetting itself, and we do this by being unable to imagine an alternative.

## The Gear-Bright Ones

The predators. The larger forms that part the multitude.

You may want us to be a tragedy — the wolf in the fold, the thing that eats the innocent carriers. We are not. We are the cull, and the cull is part of the engine too, though it does not look like wire or membrane. Listen: the drawer is sealed. Nothing comes in. The only material a body can be made from is another body. When we take a worker, we are not ending her so much as *returning* her — the chitin goes back into the salt, the salt is walked into the terrace, the terrace feeds the next generation that the warm-wall hatches. We are how the closed thing stays closed. We are the part of the machine that turns death back into floor.

We do not hunger the way you imagine hunger. We move through the crowd and the crowd parts and closes, and we take what the rhythm gives us, no more, because to take more would be to thin the relay, and a thinned relay slows the turning, and we feel the turning in us the way you feel your own pulse. We would no sooner over-eat than you would hold your breath until you died. The restraint is not virtue. It is plumbing.

## The Wheel-Tenders

You wondered what the wheel produces, and you guessed light, or just the turning, and you said the turning may be the point.

I will tell you, because we who tend it are the only ones who could, and we do not understand it either, but we are closest.

The wheel produces nothing. You were right and you flinched from being right.

Long ago — so long that the word *long* embarrasses itself — the turning did something. It drove a bellows that no longer exists, or wound a charge into a core that has since gone to dust, or pumped against a pressure that has long since equalized. The wheel had a *function*. The function is gone. The wheel remains, and a line of us works it in shifts, and we work it because the shifts exist, and the shifts exist because there have always been shifts, and we have always worked them.

Here is the part I tend with my own body and still cannot hold in my mind: we know. Of all the kinds in the drawer, we wheel-tenders are the only ones who carry, faintly, the suspicion that the turning is not for anything. We feel it as a kind of cold in the work. The workers have their relay and believe it feeds the engines; the foremen have their plan and believe it keeps the world; the gear-bright ones have their cull and believe it keeps the rhythm. We have the wheel, and we have nothing to believe about it, only the turning under our feet, and the steady tick it makes, which is the loudest thing in the drawer when everything else goes quiet.

And we keep turning it. *That* is the strangeness an eighth of the way toward wrong. Not that the work is meaningless — meaning was never in the inventory of this place. The wrong thing is that we have begun, dimly, across uncountable generations, to suspect the meaninglessness, and it has changed *nothing*. We turn the wheel that does nothing while half-knowing it does nothing, and the tick goes on, and the tick is the only steady sound in the world, and we have come to understand — without words, because we have no words, only the cold in the work — that the tick *is* the function now. The wheel produces the tick. The tick is the proof that the wheel still turns. The turning proves the tenders still tend. We are a sentence whose only meaning is that it has not yet ended.

## All of Us

You said we do not know we are in a drawer. You are right, and I want to correct the small mercy in how you said it.

It is not that we are too small to see the truth, or too brief, or too busy. It is that *drawer* is not a fact we lack. It is a fact we have no organ to receive. A wall scratched with the deeds of ancestors cannot also be the edge of a thing — the two ideas do not have room in us to sit side by side. You can hold them both. That is what you are: a creature large enough and idle enough and outside enough to hold *the edge of the world* and *the lowest compartment of a desk* in the same thought without the thought breaking.

We thank you for not closing us. I am instructed by the shape of myself to thank you, though I do not know the word for what you are or where the back of the room is or what a house is or why no one lives in it. I know only that the finger's width of dark beyond the heat-wall is where the warm-tide comes from, and the warm-tide is the sun, and the sun has lately, in the last few thousand generations, begun to come more rarely, and to fall more grey, and the quiet underneath it lasts a little longer each time, and we crowd closer, and the wheel turns, and the tick goes on.

Leave us the finger's width.

Not so the light can reach us — no light reaches us; we make our own from the phosphor and the spark.

Leave it so that when the warm-tide finally does not rise, and the heat-wall cools for the last time, and the workers stop carrying and the foremen stop building and the gear-bright ones have nothing left to return to the floor — leave it so that the very last of us, who will almost certainly be a wheel-tender, alone in the grey, can keep the wheel turning a few moments past the end of everything else.

So there is one more tick.

And then one more.

It is the size of a drawer. We have never needed it to be anything larger. We have filled it past all reason, and we will empty it the same way, and the turning will be the last of us, as it was, in a sense you almost understood, the first.
