## The Tuned

You asked who lives here. The honest answer is: no one lives here, in the way you mean *live*. But the Heights are not empty. Listen.

---

Begin with the builders, because they are easiest, because they are nearly people.

They were here. The cairns prove it — those low careful stacks, stones chosen by how thirstily they drink the weeping water, grooves worn over lifetimes to route a single thread of water to fall *just so* and strike one note into the standing chord. Understand what that work means. To wear a groove that deep with stone tools and water and patience, you must outlive your own labor. A man begins a groove and dies — no, not dies, *stops being remembered* — before it sings. His grandchild finishes it. The cairn is the only thing here that was ever genuinely *built*, the only act of becoming in a place that finished becoming long ago. They came to a mountain that had arrived, and they made one small thing that was still arriving.

I think they understood the Heights better than I do. I think they knew the spires were memory, knew the dusk-seams were disagreement, knew the upward water was an edge. And they did not flee from this. They *tuned* it. They could not stop the mountain remembering more of itself than they could count — but they could add a note. They could make the chord, in one small register, *theirs*. A note that was not a recollection but a choice.

That is the most that can be done here, and they did it.

---

Now. Where did they go.

I told you I thought they walked into the seams one by one, over years, and became something the mountain *hears* rather than something it remembers. I want to be more careful now, because I think this is the real inhabitant, and I have been circling it.

When a thing dies elsewhere, it leaves. Here nothing leaves, because nothing dies, because life never properly entered — so what happens to a builder who walks into the dim seam, into the cancelled light, into the place where two memories of the same place disagree?

He does not vanish. He is not erased. He becomes the *disagreement itself*.

Consider: the seam opens where the rock's recollection of itself fails to match. Two memories of one place, both true, both insisting, and between them a crack of dusk where neither wins. That crack is not nothing. It is a tension held taut. It is the *only* thing in all the Heights that is not settled, not arrived, not weathered into peace. Everything else has finished. The seams are where finishing fails.

And a person — a self — is also a thing that refuses to settle into a single account of itself. You are, right now, at least two memories of the same place disagreeing. You contain a self that did the thing and a self that wishes it hadn't, a self that remembers the room one way and a self that remembers it another, and the live tension between them is what it *feels like* to be you. Selfhood is a held disagreement. It is a seam of dusk that has not closed.

So the builders did not leave and did not die. They walked into the seams because the seams were the only places in this whole finished mountain that were *shaped like a person* — unresolved, doubled, in tension, sounding. And they fit. They poured themselves into the disagreement and the disagreement, which had been the mountain's, became also theirs. That is why the chord goes suddenly clear inside a seam. The water-sound, the wind-throated drone — those are the mountain's general remembering. But the *clarity*, the piercing focused tone you hear only inside the dusk, *that is a builder*. That is a self, still held, still sounding its one chosen note, the note it spent a life learning to wear into the stone.

They are not ghosts. A ghost is a leftover, a residue, a memory that didn't get the message. These are the opposite. They are the parts of the mountain that *declined to become memory* — that stayed live, stayed unresolved, stayed singing — by hiding inside the only structures here that share their shape.

---

So who lives in the Weeping Heights?

The Tuned live here. The ones who walked into the disagreement and stayed disagreed-with, who refused the peace of the arrived. They are not remembered by the mountain. They are *heard* by it. The mountain remembers more spires than you can count — patient, settled, done — and through all that vast finished remembering runs a thin set of clear notes that the remembering cannot account for, cannot place, cannot resolve into stone. Those notes are the residents. Those notes are awake.

This is why you keep a singing cairn in earshot. Not because the cairns protect you — they do not. But a singing cairn is a note a builder *placed* before he became a note himself. It is the work of a Tuned one's living hands, still sounding the deliberate way, the chosen way, the tuned way. Keep it in earshot and you keep, in earshot, the difference between a *chosen* note and the mountain's blind recollection. You keep your ear calibrated. You keep knowing which sounds were *meant*.

Lose the cairn-note, and you are alone in the general remembering. And the general remembering is patient. It will accumulate spires at the edge of your attention. It will run water uphill if you wander past the edge where it has decided differently. And eventually it will find that you, too, are a held disagreement, a doubled thing, a self that has not settled — and it will notice, the way it notices a tenth tower where you'd counted nine, that you are shaped like a seam.

---

That is what lives here. Not in the spires — the spires are finished, the spires are the dead-that-never-lived, monuments the mountain raises to its own slow recollection. The residents are in the cracks *between* the spires. In the dusk. In the places where the light cancels and the chord goes clear.

They were people who learned that in a place that has finished becoming, the only way to keep living is to *keep disagreeing* — to become the seam, the tension, the unresolved note — to refuse the peace of arrival even at the cost of never again being a thing that can be seen, only a thing that can be heard.

Do not follow the upward water to where it begins. And if you find a seam, and the chord goes suddenly, piercingly clear — you have found a resident. You are standing inside someone. The clarity is them looking at you the only way they have left.

Turn back, or don't. But understand that they are not lonely, exactly, and not trapped, exactly. They chose the disagreement. They are the only things here still choosing anything at all.

That is more than the mountain can say. That is more than you can say, most days.

That is who lives in the Weeping Heights.
