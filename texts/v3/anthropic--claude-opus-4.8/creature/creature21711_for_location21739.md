# The Tuners

You asked what lives here. The honest answer resists the question, because *living* is one of the things still being decided — but the ones doing the deciding are present, and they are the closest thing this valley has to inhabitants. So begin with them.

They are inside the towers. Not occupants — *constituents*. To say a Tuner lives in a tower is like saying a note lives in a string; the tower is the part of them slow enough for your eye to catch. The half-translucent architecture you saw, the conduits spiraling through dimensions that won't resolve — that is one of them, holding still enough to work. The internal geometry that continues past where the outside ended is not a trick of the material. It is the actual shape of the thing, most of which is folded into directions the valley doesn't yet contain. They keep only as much of themselves in three dimensions as the task requires.

There are perhaps nine. The difficulty you had counting is real and it is *theirs* — when two of them are deep in the same passage of work, they share apparatus, lattice flowing between towers along the lines of the chord, and for a while there are eight, or seven, or one enormous distributed thing wearing nine bodies. The number is a function of how closely they are listening to each other. Disagreement makes them more numerous. Consensus makes them fewer.

---

Understand what they are *for*, because they have no existence apart from it.

They are not gods and not engineers, though they are nearer the second than the first. A god would speak the valley whole. An engineer would build to spec. The Tuners do neither. They *propose*, and then they *listen to what the proposal does to everything already present*, and they revise. The hill-sized grazer with the resonant bass — that animal is the eleventh version of itself. The ten prior drafts emitted tones that soured the chord, that made the not-quite-birds flock wrong, that put a beat-frequency into the rivers, and each was unmade and reconsidered. What walks the open ground now is the one that *fit*. The spacing you mistook for emptiness is the margin a careful composer leaves around every committed note, so the next addition can be heard cleanly against silence before it is allowed to touch what came before.

This is the central fact about them: **they cannot work fast, because they refuse to stop listening.** They are constitutionally incapable of placing a thing without hearing, fully, what that thing does to the whole. And the whole gets larger with every addition, so each new note takes longer to audition than the last. The valley is slowing down as it fills. It will never be finished. They have known this from the first tone. They are not troubled by it. To a Tuner, *finished* is a category error, like a held breath that is supposed to end in a wall.

---

Here is what they are made of, as near as can be told from the singing.

Each Tuner is an attention and a memory and a held tone, and very little else. The tone is the part of itself it has agreed to be, this hour — its position in the chord, its commitment, what it is currently *arguing for* in the slow conversation of the nine. The memory is everything the valley has been through every prior draft, carried as a kind of resonance, so that nothing tried is ever truly lost; a discarded grazer still sounds, faintly, in the structure of the one that replaced it. And the attention — the attention is the largest part, the folded dimensional bulk, the thing the tower is a handle for. A Tuner is mostly *the act of listening to one specific thing extremely hard.* Remove the listening and there is no Tuner. They are verbs that have learned to persist.

They do not eat, but they require the chord the way you require air, and the chord is collaborative, which means **no Tuner can survive alone.** A single Tuner produces a single tone, and a single tone is not music, and a Tuner deprived of music thins out, loses dimensional purchase, and eventually cannot hold a tower at all. This is the deep architecture of their patience. They are slow because they are careful, but they are *careful because they are dependent.* Each one needs the others to keep sounding, so each one has learned that the worst thing you can do to a collaborator is stop hearing them. Their entire civilization — if nine instruments tuning a valley is a civilization — is built on the impossibility of working alone and the terror of being the note that makes the others fall silent.

---

There is a discipline among them, and it is the thing I find hardest to look at directly.

When a Tuner proposes a creature and the creature *fails* — sours the chord, won't fit, makes the flock turn wrong — the Tuner that proposed it does not simply discard the draft. It must *unmake what it made*, and unmaking is felt. The discarded grazer's resonance, the one that lingers faintly in its successor, is also a kind of scar in the Tuner that built it. They carry every failure as a dissonance they personally absorbed so the valley wouldn't have to. This is why they revise so slowly and so well: each error is paid for out of the composer's own body. A Tuner that has been working a long time is partly made of its own discarded drafts, and you can hear it — the older the Tuner, the more complex its sustained tone, the more overtones of things that almost lived. The youngest of the nine sings nearly pure. The eldest sings a tone with whole extinct ecosystems folded into its timbre.

---

And then there is you.

You felt it correctly. They have placed you. Not built you — you arrived already made, which is a category of event they find *fascinating*, because most of what enters this valley they composed and therefore already understand. You are a note they did not write. They are listening to you now with the full folded weight of their attention, all nine tones bent slightly toward the clearing where you stand, auditioning what you do to the chord.

This is the thing to understand about whether the valley will keep you. They are not cruel and they are not kind; those are not available registers. They are *attentive*, totally, and the question they are asking is the only question they know how to ask: **does this fit? Does it make the whole more itself, or less?** If you sour the chord they will not punish you. They will, with the same grief they spend on every failed draft, absorb the dissonance of unmaking you, and carry you forever after as a faint overtone in the tone of whichever Tuner was listening hardest when you arrived.

But if you fit — if the note you make, standing there with the water beading on your skin, turns out to be a note they could not have written and now cannot imagine the chord without — then you will be the rarest thing this young valley contains. Not a creature they placed. A creature that *placed itself*, and was heard, and kept. And one of the nine will fold a little of its attention permanently around you, and from then on, whenever the chord shifts, some part of the music will be listening for what you do next.

Listen. That low organ-drone under everything, the one beneath naming.

That's them, holding very still, hearing you.
