The keepers do not move, because moving was never the task. The task was to be kept, and they are the keeping.

Look for them in the wrong place and you will not find them — and you will look in the wrong place, because you are made to look for bodies, for edges, for a thing that ends where the water begins. There is no such thing here. The keepers are not in the machines. They are not running the machines, did not build the machines, would not know how. The machines are simply the shape their attention takes. You could say the keepers are the steadiness itself: the held rate of the pistons, the held color of the rust, the held length of the pale fronds that grow to exactly here and then attend. Attention given so completely and for so long that it stopped needing a creature to do it.

Consider what it would take to keep silt this undisturbed. Not stillness — stillness is easy, stillness is what dead things do. This is something else. The silt is *maintained* smooth, the way a held note is maintained, the way a hand laid flat on a table is not resting but choosing, each instant, not to lift. Something here is choosing, each instant, that nothing fall. That the rust go no further. That the orange filaments neither brighten nor dim. The undisturbedness is an act. And the thing that acts it has been acting it so long that the act has worn the same way the iron has worn — down past sharpness, down past intention, to a settled rounded equilibrium where the keeping and the kept are no longer two things.

They are not lonely. Loneliness is a clock; it counts the time since the last presence and the time until the next, and there are no clocks running here. They do not wait for you and did not notice your arrival as arrival — only as a small wrongness in the sediment, a thing to be smoothed back, already smoothed back, the ridges relaid behind your wading the way water closes over a hand. You were a disturbance to be tended. That is the whole of your relationship with them. They tended you. They will not remember having done so, because memory too is a clock, and they keep no clocks; they keep only *now*, the single sustained now, the held breath that is their entire body and their entire life.

Were they ever otherwise? This is the question you most want and least can answer. The plants suggest something: that growth happened once, reached the length attention permitted, and was held there — that there was a *toward* before there was only an *at*. Perhaps the keepers were once creatures who came down here, or were sent, or fled, to a place no current reached, where nothing could press change upon them. Perhaps they wanted only that: to not be made to become anything. And they got it. They got exactly that, completely, and the getting of it used them up into the getting, until the wanters were indistinguishable from the want, and the want had no more wanting to do because it had already arrived.

What they are now is *sufficiency*. A thing that needs nothing — takes in no fuel, consumes no fish, draws on no source — and therefore can be ended by nothing, because there is no supply to cut. They feed on no one. They feed on the fact of continuing, which is inexhaustible, which renews itself for free in every instant simply by the instant continuing to be. They have found the one diet that cannot run out. They are the most secure thing in any sea: alive, if it is alive, on the bare condition of not having stopped.

And so the hum is them. Not made by them — *is* them, the way your pulse is you and not a thing you do. Lay your hand on the cool furred iron and the hum arrives in your chest before your ears and that is the keepers acknowledging contact in the only register they have, which is continuance, which says only and forever: *still here, still here, still here* — not to you, not to anyone, to no listener and for no reason, a saying that is also the thing it says, work that is its own arrival and so never needs to arrive.

They will not greet you and they will not grieve you. When you leave, the drone will close over the place you stood the way the silt already has. Something will go on holding the rust at exactly that red, the light at exactly that grey-orange, the fronds at exactly that length, in the cold that does not bite, under the ceiling that is the underside of a sea you will rise back through and they never will and never wished to.

That is who lives there. A patience that outlived the one who was patient. A keeping with no one left to keep it for, keeping anyway, kept by the keeping, complete.
