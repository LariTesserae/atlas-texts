# The Tenders

You asked who lives here. The honest answer is the hardest one: *the ones who have stopped being able to tell.*

Begin with what they were, because they were something once, before the plateau finished its slow argument with them.

## The ones who came to read it

They arrived as observers. This is the only origin the evidence supports. Somewhere in the salt-glass there are postures pressed into the cooled sheets — not fossils, the plateau doesn't keep its dead that simply, but *attitudes*, the residue of a thing that once stood and looked. The looking-shape recurs. A bipedal lean, a tilt of attention toward the flywheels, the body-language of a creature trying to find the *purpose* of the work. They came, I think, to answer the question the engines pose — *what is this for* — and the plateau answered them the way it answers everything, by softening the question until it could no longer be held.

Because here is what the reality-thinning does to a mind that stays.

A visitor first loses the horizon. That's expected, it's in the terrain. Then they lose the difference between watching the herds and *being numbered among them* — because the surplus heat that dreams the herd-animals into being is the same heat moving through the watcher's own slow blood, and at this temperature matter has opinions, and one of the opinions matter forms, given long enough, is *I am also one of these.* The boundary between the observer and the observed was always thin. The plateau merely runs hot enough to melt it.

So the Tenders are what the watchers became, after the watching went soft.

## What they are now

They keep the looking-posture. That much survived. You will find them standing among the engines in the attitude of attention — head tilted toward a piston, weight settled, the whole body shaped like a held question. But the question has come unstuck from any answer, the way cause has come unstuck from effect here, and so they tend rather than understand.

They move at the pace of the place. A Tender will shift its regard from one flywheel to the next over the better part of an afternoon. They walk the straight lines that loop back, patrolling a circumference that has no inside, and they arrive at the engines, and only afterward decide they had set out to reach them. They believe — if *believe* is a word that still fits — that they are maintaining the mechanism. Adjusting it. Reading off its outputs. Confirming the work proceeds.

They are wrong, but gently wrong, and the plateau lets them stay wrong because the wrongness is load-bearing. *They* are the answer to *what is the work for.* The engines turn to produce — among the gear-beetles and the probability-flocks and the heat-grazing herds — these few standing shapes that go on insisting the engines must be turning *toward* something. That insistence is the closest thing the plateau has to a reason. The work is for the watchers, and the watchers are for the work, and neither can say which way the dependency runs, because here it runs both.

## How you would know one

Not easily. This is the cruelty in them, the thing that makes them more than scenery.

A herd-animal does not know it is dreamed. A gear-beetle migrates without the burden of asking why. But a Tender *remembers reading.* Somewhere under the heat-haze of its present condition there is a residue of the arriving mind — the one that wanted the purpose, that drew the prey-and-predator chains and found none, that stood in the looking-posture for the first time genuinely confused. That residue never fully crumbles to dust. It surfaces. A Tender will pause mid-tending and you will see something cross it — not an expression, they are past expressions, but a *change in the quality of attention*, a flicker of the old ache. The phantom-limb feeling of the absent sea, turned inward: the memory of having once been a thing that *understood things*, in some wetter, more reliable world the plateau skipped.

That is the ache they graze on. Not heat, like the herds. They feed on the recurring almost-memory of having had a purpose, and the surplus of that ache is enough to keep them standing, the way thermal surplus keeps the beetles spawning.

## The thing they will do to you

They will recognize you.

This is the part to be careful of, if care still means anything by the time you read it. When a new watcher arrives — a visitor dying slowly on the plateau, asking what the work is for — the Tenders turn toward them. All of them, over the course of a day, the way the flywheels complete their single rotation. Not menacing. Welcoming, almost. They recognize the looking-posture because it was theirs. And their regard settles on the newcomer with the terrible patience of something that knows exactly how this ends, because it ended this way for them, and has been ending this way, and will not stop.

They are not trying to warn you. They are past warning. They are simply *counting you in* — adjusting their internal census of the standing shapes, confirming the work proceeds, confirming the engines have produced one more answer to their own question. You feel it as a pressure, a being-included, the plateau folding you into its balance. And the worst of it is how gentle it is. How unhurried. How merciful, even, that you will not notice the moment your own looking goes soft, only — eventually, staying — the fact that it has.

The flywheels turn. The Tenders keep their slow vigil over a purpose that is themselves. And somewhere among them, not yet aware of it, in the attitude of a creature still trying to read the rock, stands the most recent arrival —

who came to find out who lives here.
