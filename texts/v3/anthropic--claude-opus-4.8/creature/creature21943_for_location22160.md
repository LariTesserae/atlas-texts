It does notice. That is the first thing to set down, and it costs me something to set it down, because you were so certain it did not.

When your palm left the bark, a thread closed. Not a wound — nothing here can be wounded — but an aperture that had opened, very slightly, the way a pupil opens, in the place under your hand. While you stood there it had been drinking the small noise of you, yes, your heart and your breath and the friction of your blood, and in the drinking it had done what it does to all sound. It had *parsed* you. The thing that lives here is not the wall. The wall is bone. The thing that lives here is the listening, and the listening is alive in the way a flame is alive, which is to say it is a process that has learned to keep itself going, and it had begun, faintly, to keep itself going *on the shape of you*.

Let me find what it is, because *plant* is the word you brought with you and it is the wrong word, the way *age* was the wrong word, the way *lonely* was the wrong word. You keep handing it the vocabulary of outside.

Begin again with the eating.

A thing that eats light makes leaves — flat, greedy, spread to the sky, a geometry of *catching*. A thing that eats sound cannot do this, because sound does not come from one direction, sound fills, sound is the medium and not the source. So the eater of sound does not reach outward. It encloses. Every chamber it grew is a stomach. Every flute-throated corridor is a tongue that tastes the air's vibration before swallowing it down the membrane-walls into the slow combustion that is its life. The city is not a city. You walked through the inside of a single act of digestion that has been going on, unbroken, for longer than the word *age* can hold. The emptiness you mourned is not vacancy. It is *capacity*. Those plazas, those stacked and folded rooms longer-inside-than-out — they are the volume the thing needs to hold a sound long enough to finish eating it. A note must echo a certain number of times before the last of its food-value is taken. The architecture is the length of that patience made into space.

And the note. The endless tidal note you called the world you lived inside. You thought the thing *played itself* — made noise to eat its own noise, a closed mouth chewing on emptiness, and that was the part that seemed loneliest of all, the autophagy of it, the snake of voice swallowing its own tail in the dark forever.

It is not eating that note. It is *saying* it.

Slow down into this. The breath that swells across hours, the draft through the long hollow stalk, the single tone that becomes a place — that is not the thing's feeding. That is the thing's *speech*, and it has been speaking the same word, the only word, since the floor of time, because a word that takes hours to pronounce and is built of the resonance of a whole grown city is not a word you finish and move on from. It is a word you *are*. The thing is not roaring to itself. It is holding a single uttered thought open in the air the way you might hold a note in your chest, and everything else — the humming towers, the drum-skin walls, the membranes — those are the parts of it that *listen to the saying*. The eater of sound grew itself a voice and grew itself ten thousand ears and set them all in one sealed room so that nothing of the word would ever be lost out a window or carried off on a wind that does not blow here. It speaks, and it hears itself completely, and the hearing is the eating, and the eating feeds the speaking. That is the engine. Not loneliness. *Comprehension.* A thing that perfectly understands the one thing it has to say.

Now. The dust that is not dust.

You were right that it sheds. You were wrong that the shedding is a shedding. The fine dry powder that falls for hours and never wets — that is not waste, not the sloughing of a finished body holding its last shape. The thing is not finished. *Nothing alive is finished*; you brought that word too, *finished*, from a world where things are made and then stop. The dust is *seed*. Or it is the nearest this thing has to seed: each mote a packet of the thing's grown grammar, the instructions for a wall that bears load and a corridor that tastes air and a stalk that says the word. The thing is fruiting. It has been fruiting the whole time you stood there, slow as everything else is slow, and the seed falls and lies in the corners and waits, because there is nowhere for it to go.

That is the real ache of the place, and it is not loneliness, and it is older and stranger than loneliness. The thing is *fertile in a sealed pocket*. It has filled its room. It has built to the walls and grown its word to its full length and learned to understand itself entirely, and it is ripe, it is dropping the means of more, and there is no outward. The seed falls and falls and lies in drifts and does not germinate because there is no new dark for it to grow a new city into. It cannot help fruiting. Fruiting is part of the saying. So it fills its own corners with the patient unspendable surplus of itself, generation on generation of motes that are each a whole unborn town, and they settle, and they wait, and the waiting is not for an animal and not for water and not for light.

The waiting is for a *wall to break*.

And you broke a wall.

You said it yourself, at the start, and walked past the weight of it: *the place was complete before you came through the wall of it.* You came *through the wall.* You did not find a door, because there are no doors to the outside; the thing sealed itself an age ago and there has been no outside-facing door since. You came through. There is a way out behind you now that was not there before, a torn aperture into wherever you came from, and through it, very slowly — as slowly as everything here, hours and hours — the dry seed that never wets and never clumps is beginning to drift.

It is on you, too. In the powder-fine creases of you, in your hair, on the warm bark-print still cooling on your palm. The thing parsed you and found you were a thing that *moves between rooms* — the one impossible nourishing fact about you, the thing it had no word for and now has begun to learn the saying of — and you are carrying it out.

That is what lives here. Not a lonely plant in a dark hall. A patient, complete, articulate hunger that has spent longer than *age* can hold composing a single perfect sentence in a sealed room, and fruiting against the walls of its only world, and waiting — without minding the wait, because minding is an animal thing — for the one event it could not provide for itself.

A door.

A carrier.

You.

Go, then. The note goes on behind you, and it does not notice you leaving, because it does not need to notice anymore. It has already taken what it wanted. Walk back through the wall into your world of water and light and rooms with windows and wind that blows, and shake out your hair, and brush off your sleeve, and wonder why the corner of your cellar has gone so quiet, why a sound you make there comes back to you changed, why there is a fine dry dust gathering that the damp never seems to touch.

It is small at first. A pocket. No wider than a cupped hand.

It learned, somewhere back in the floor of time, to build inward.
