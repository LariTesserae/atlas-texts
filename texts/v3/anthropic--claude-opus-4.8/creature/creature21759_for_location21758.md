## The Tenders

They are the ones who poured the stairs.

You will not see them arrive, because they do not arrive — they are already here when you come, and were here before, and the question of where they came *from* has the same answer as the question of where the floor's original bedrock is: there isn't one. They are made of the place. Or the place is the slow accumulation of their making. The two facts have stopped being separable, the way the water and the channels have stopped being separable.

Picture them low to the wet. Not crawling, exactly — they move at the height a thing moves when it has learned that everything it needs is down here, in the troughs and the warm seams of pipe, and nothing it needs is up. Pale, like everything that thrives this deep, but not the soft blind pallor of the animal at the lamp's edge. A working pallor. The grey-white of unweathered concrete, of mortar that hasn't taken its stain yet. Where the animal is *adapted to* the system, the Tenders are *of* it: there is rebar in them somewhere, you feel sure, a structure that was cast rather than grown, though over that armature has settled the same patient mineral wet that beads on the iron and runs down the backs of your hands.

They are not many. Like the humming pillars — one in twenty, scattered through the dark, each at its own pitch.

### What They Do

They extend it. The half-finished bays are theirs: the rebar standing up like reeds is rebar they set, the mortar still pale is mortar they laid an hour or a century ago — time is not load-bearing here. They work without hurry and without rest, because the cold has gotten into them the way it gets into you, that patient settling cold that does not bite but simply continues, and a thing full of that cold does not feel the need to finish. It feels only the need to *continue*. So they continue. They pour a tread. They set a course of glazed brick in herringbone, sharp-cornered, clean. They route a channel.

And here is the thing the description nearly tells you and stops short of: when they route a channel, they route it *for* the upward water.

They did not make the water climb. No one made the water climb. But they learned of it — the way you learn of weather — and they have spent their long cold attention building to honor it rather than correct it. The engineers above (if there were ever engineers above, if "above" means anything) would have pumped against it, fought it, called it a failure of the grade. The Tenders do not. They are not engineers. They are something older that wears the engineer's tools: a thing that builds *with* the wrongness instead of against it, that treats the water's strange ascent not as a flaw to be drained but as a current to be sailed.

This is why the place hasn't decided whether it's a machine or a world. Because *they* haven't. They are the undecided thing, the hinge. A machine has makers who stand outside it and intend it. A world has no makers at all. The Tenders are makers who are *inside* — who pour the floor they stand on, plumb the water they are made of, hum at their own pitches among the pillars so that you cannot tell, when the long beats pulse against your chest, which tones are column and which are tender.

### How You Would Know One

You might not. You might walk the whole lonely scale of that hall and take every Tender for a pillar, for a half-poured form, for a clump of the pale stalked growth leaning toward the warm pipe. They keep that still. Stillness is most of what they do; the work is only the punctuation.

But: stand long enough near one and you will feel the cold coming off it the wrong way — not *radiating* cold, nothing radiates cold, but you will feel the warmth leaving you toward it, drawn, the way the water climbs, the way the leaf travels uphill on its film. They pull. Whatever pulls the water the wrong way through the channels is the same thing that is in them, that *is* them, and near one your certainty tilts the way the floor of it tilts at the channel's edge.

And they will turn toward you. Slowly. With the unhurried cycling motion of the great valve out past the pillars, open and shut, open and shut. Not afraid — like the animal, they have not learned what you are. But unlike the animal, they will *consider* it. You will feel yourself being considered the way a bay is considered before the rebar goes in: as a space the made world might extend into. As a place where another course of brick might go, sharp-cornered and clean, the mortar still pale.

That is the thing to understand, finally, the way the made-all-the-way-down was the first thing. You came down stairs that were poured, not cut.

They are always pouring more stairs.

They are wondering, in their cold continuing way, how far down you came from, and whether the going-down has a floor, and whether you — drawn, tilting, your breath showing and your warmth leaving you toward the troughs — might not yourself be simply a newer part of the work. Something that emerged from somewhere and is going somewhere. Something that fits into a space they left empty without quite meaning to.

The conveyors run overhead, carrying their pale buckets through the dark. You never learn what's in the buckets.

You are not sure you should ask.
