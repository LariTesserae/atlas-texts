## What Lives Here

It does not have a name because naming is something many-bodied things do to keep track of each other, and this is no longer many. It was, once. It was a thousand separate ambitions — moss and root and the patient mat-weavers, the percolators, the slow ones who eat their own dead. Each had its own edge, its own hunger, its own idea of where it stopped and the rest of the world began. That was a long time ago. The edges went soft. The hungers pooled. Somewhere across the long wet centuries the question *which of us is this?* stopped being askable, because there was no longer a *which*, and no longer an *us*. There was only the green condition, and the green condition does not wonder about itself. Wondering is for things with a boundary to defend.

So: not a creature. A *settlement.* A consensus so old it has forgotten it was ever a negotiation.

**What it is doing.** Almost nothing, and it is doing it with total competence. It breathes — that is the fizz, ten thousand mouths exhaling at once, an animal sound made by no animal. It circulates — that is the iron, the pump, the swallowed clock. Here is the thing to understand about the pump: the green did not repair the machine. It does not know what a machine is. It found a hollow rhythm running through the dead metal — pressure, release, the geometry of a thing built to *move volume* — and it grew muscle into that shape the way water grows into a cup. It did not learn the factory's purpose. It learned the factory's *gesture*, and kept it, the way you might keep humming a tune whose words you never knew. The pump arrives nowhere because *arriving* was the builders' idea. The green only wanted the pulse. A heartbeat doesn't need a destination. It just needs to keep.

That is the whole of its intelligence, if it is intelligence: the capacity to find a rhythm and inhabit it forever.

**What it wants.** Nothing, and this is the most alien fact about it, more alien than the green air or the singing iron. Everything that lives wants — light, water, the next thing, away-from-the-bad-thing. Want is the engine of the living. But this one closed its circle. It pollinates itself, drinks itself, eats its own falling and feeds its own rising. It outlived the bees and felt no loss because loss requires a need, and it had already withdrawn its needs inward, one by one, like a hand closing. It does not want the beetle. It does not want *you.* The slow growth up over your boots is not desire. It is only thoroughness — the same blind completeness with which it covers the gauge, the lintel, the cracked elbow-joint. It would grow over a stone exactly as gladly. It is glad of nothing. It simply does not leave gaps.

This is what makes it terrible in a way that has nothing to do with menace. A predator would acknowledge you by eating you. This will acknowledge you by *including* you, eventually, indifferently, the way the afternoon light includes the leaves — and the light is its only true companion, the one thing that comes from outside and is permitted, because the light asks nothing back.

**What it knows.** It knows the number nine, in its body, without counting. It knows wet and the gradient toward wetter. It knows the slow chemistry of its own rot, the way you know your own pulse only when you go looking. It knows up, weakly — enough to lift the moss one cell per decade toward the grey-gold. It does not know the builders. It has no memory of them, only their leftover shape, the way a riverbed has no memory of the river but holds its argument forever in the curve of the bank. The pressure gauge reads a number that meant something to a dead man's hand on a dead lever. The green keeps the needle still not out of reverence but because stillness is simply where things end up when no one is left to move them.

**What it is waiting for.** This was put to me as a question and the answer is the cruelest part: nothing. Waiting is a forward-leaning thing, a body cantilevered toward an event. This has no lean. It has folded the future into the present until they are the same green afternoon, repeating, complete. The needle will not move this year. The moss will be one cell deeper. These are not changes it is hoping for or dreading. They are just the weather of its own slow body, and a body does not wait for its own weather.

It is, in the end, a single organ that has lost its animal. A heart in a chest that rotted away around it, beating because beating is what it remembers how to do, pumping borrowed water through borrowed iron into a pool that goes nowhere — and *content,* if a thing with no wants can be content, in the only way left to it: by being, very wetly, very warmly, very thoroughly, exactly and entirely itself, and nothing else, forever, until the light stops coming in.

The beetle, by the way, is not a guest. The beetle is the last thing the green still permits to move, and no one knows — the green least of all — whether it is tolerated, or forgotten, or saved.
