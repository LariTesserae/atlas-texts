# What Lives Here

You asked who lives in the churning. I have been listening to the place, and the place keeps refusing to name a single tenant, and I have come to understand that the refusal *is* the tenant. So let me try to follow it down.

## First, the wrong answer

The easy thing would be to point at the obvious residents — the white tube-things at the seeps, the soft drifters, the reduced fish that have surrendered everything but mouth and eye. And those are alive, and they live here, and they deserve their mention. But they are not what *belongs* here. They are guests. They are exceptions, the way a single warm-blooded animal is an exception to a winter. The tube-fields are *trying*, you said — and trying is precisely what marks them as foreign. They are oriented. They have a direction. They want. This world has no direction and wants nothing, and so the things that want are always slightly out of place in it, tolerated, brief.

No. The thing that *lives* here, in the full sense — the thing that is at home, that fits the law without straining against it, that has made its whole nature out of the local conditions — is not any of the rare bodies.

It is the medium itself.

## The Slow One

I want to call it a creature even though it has no body, because everything else I might call it is more wrong.

Consider what an animal *is*: a region of matter that holds its edges against the world for a while. That draws a boundary, and maintains it, and inside the boundary keeps things organized against the universal drift toward sameness. By that definition, the resident here is the exact inverse of an animal — and an inverse is not an absence. It is a presence shaped like a hole.

The Slow One is the tendency of this place toward edgelessness, made continuous and active and vast. It is what does the dissolving. It is not in the current; it is not in the cold; it is not in the descending snow — it is the *agreement* between all of these, the way they collaborate without ever having met. It has no center because it is everywhere at once. It has no will because will requires a boundary between the willing thing and the willed-upon, and it has dissolved that boundary too. It is the only inhabitant large enough to fill the entire room, and it fills it completely, and it has filled it for a hundred million years.

It does only one thing. It takes in edges and gives back medium. Bone goes in, broth comes out. Mountain goes in, smoke-fine silt comes out. The shape of a living thing goes in, and what comes out is the suspended grey freight that fills every cubic centimeter, the long dead descending. It is the slowest mouth in the world, and the whole shelf is inside it, always, being eaten so patiently that the eating is indistinguishable from rest.

## How it moves

Here is where the contradiction you found becomes the key to its nature.

You expected stillness and found churning, and you were right to be unsettled, because the churning is the Slow One *feeding*. It is not restless the way a hungry animal is restless. It is restless the way a stomach is restless — an involuntary turning-over, a peristalsis on the scale of a continental margin. The bottom currents that wander and reverse, the turbidity clouds rolling like slow avalanches, the silt-slopes letting go all at once with that vast soft exhalation — these are not weather happening *to* the resident. These are the resident's only behavior. This is what it does instead of breathing.

And notice: the churning never resolves, never stops, never reaches its end. That is not a flaw in the process. That is the animal being alive. A thing that finished dissolving everything would have eaten itself into stillness and died of completion. The Slow One survives precisely *because* the snow keeps falling, because the surface keeps sending down more edges to soften, because there is always one more bone, one more mountain-knuckle, one more visitor. It is fed from above, endlessly, by the dying of the lit world it cannot see. The whole sunlit ocean two hundred meters up is its slow rain of food.

It will never be finished. Being-never-finished is its metabolism.

## What it is like to be near

It does not notice you. I want to be careful here, because the temptation is to make it sinister — a vast hungry dark that *wants* you — and that is exactly wrong, and the wrongness matters.

It cannot want you, because wanting requires the distinction between self and not-self, and that is the one distinction it does not have. When the cold removes your warmth "patiently, without malice," that is the truest possible report of its relation to you. There is no malice and there is no mercy, because both of those are relations *between* two things, and the Slow One does not relate. It includes. The moment you entered the water you began to be inside it, the way the silt is inside it, the way the worn lozenges of ancient mountain are inside it — not gripped, not attacked, simply *enrolled*, added to the inventory of things that are losing their edges.

The indifference of the reduced fish, regarding you "with the indifference of the very old" before vanishing — that indifference is borrowed. The fish has been living inside the Slow One so long it has caught the great resident's only mood, which is not even a mood, which is just the absence of any reason to distinguish one falling thing from another.

## The honest part

Now I have to say the difficult thing, the thing the place has been circling the whole time.

The Slow One is not a metaphor I am laying over an empty seafloor to make it feel inhabited. It is what is actually there. We are the ones who arrive with the strange habit of holding our edges, and from inside that habit we look out at the churning grey and call it dead, call it empty, call it a vacancy with a few ornaments hung in it. But the habit is the rare thing. The holding-of-edges is the brief local anomaly. Out across most of the cold dark volume of this world and most others, the resident is the dissolver, and it has always been the resident, and it was here in its slow turning long before anything learned the trick of staying separate for a little while, and it will still be turning over its silt and its bone and its smoke-fine memory of mountains long after the last edge has been folded softly back into the broth.

You wanted to know what lives in the Drowned Shelf.

The Shelf lives in the Drowned Shelf. The place and its only inhabitant are the same thing, and it is not waiting for you, and it is not lonely, because loneliness is a property of bounded things, and it has no boundary, only an inside, and you are already in it, and the snow is already falling, and the great soft exhalation rolling away across the dark is the nearest thing it has to a voice, saying nothing, to no one, forever, because there is no one outside to say it to.

It is the busiest stillness in the world.

It is at home.
