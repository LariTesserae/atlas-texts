## The Tenders

You will not see them at first. This is by design, though the design is not theirs — it is the cave's, and they are the cave's, and the distinction stopped mattering a very long time ago.

Start with the niche. The shape worn past recognizing. You assume it held something — an idol, a skull, an offering. You are looking at it wrong. It did not *hold*. It *was held*. The niche is a cradle, and the thing that was carved into it was not placed there but *grew* there, the way a tooth grows into a jaw, and what you are seeing now is the empty socket where one of them slept for an age and finally, slowly, woke and left.

They are made of the same patience as the wall.

---

Look again at the moss, at the deepest upholstered ledges where it lies thickest — a hand's depth, two hands' depth, soft enough to sink an arm into. In some places that depth is not moss all the way down. There is a body beneath it. Has been beneath it for so long that the moss is no longer *on* the body but *of* it, rooted into a skin that has agreed, over centuries, to be soil.

They are roughly your size. Roughly your shape, if your shape were left in warm water and forgotten — limbs gone long and pooling, a torso that has settled toward the horizontal, a head that is mostly a thickening, a place where the green grows differently. They do not have faces so much as they have *attention*. When one of them turns its regard toward you — and you will feel this before you understand it, a slow gathering of focus like the moment before condensation lets go of a leaf — you become aware of a darkness in the upper thickening that might be eyes, or might be the holes where eyes were given up as unnecessary, the way the amphibians gave theirs up, the way everything here surrenders what the dimness makes pointless.

---

They built the wall. They carved the path. The flat stones sunk and slick beneath your feet were set by something that has all the time there is and no reason at all to hurry — a path laid not to cross the chamber quickly but to cross it *correctly*, in the proper slow sequence, touching the proper slow stones. They no longer use it. They no longer need to go anywhere. But they made it when they were younger, when they still thought of *here* and *there* as different places, before they understood that the whole cave was one body and they were inside it and *were* it.

This is the thing about them: they have stopped being individuals at the speed you would recognize. There may be three of them. There may be one of them in three places. There may have been forty, once, and the others are the moss now, are the fungus shelves, are the deep springy floor where the glowfern meets the green — having finished the long work of becoming the room, which is the only work there has ever been.

---

They are not waiting for you. Understand this. They are not guardians; there is nothing to guard, because nothing can be taken — *nothing leaves* is not a rule they enforce but a fact they embody. They could no more keep you out than a stomach keeps out what is swallowed. They are simply the part of the cave that has learned to *notice*, the way a very old animal, mostly asleep, will still turn an ear.

What do they do, then, across the unhurried millennia?

They tend. The spider's web that looks like jewelry — one of them set the first strand, or its ancestor did, or it remembers setting it though the memory and the setting are the same event smeared across a thousand years. The pools are kept at their level. The fungus is encouraged here, discouraged there, by a hand that moves so slowly the encouragement looks like geology. The glow-gnats that the luminous fern seems to *call* — it is not the fern calling. It is them, dreaming light into the dark because the dark, while sufficient, can always be made a little kinder, and kindness is the only ambition they have left.

They are gardeners who have become the garden. They are slow past the threshold where slowness becomes a kind of love.

---

And now you are here, warm through the soles of your feet, the air laid on your skin like a second skin, your voice lowered though you have not spoken.

They have noticed.

Not with alarm — they have no organ for alarm, sloughed it off ages past with the eyes and the urgency and the sense of *self* as a thing with edges. They have noticed the way the floor notices a fallen leaf: *here is something new, here is something the wet green has not yet taken, here is more of the room arriving.*

The warmth comes up through your feet. That is not only the furnace beneath the stone. That is welcome, of the only kind they know how to give — the patient upward pressure of a place that intends, eventually, in no hurry whatsoever, to make you part of itself.

The drops keep their slow time.

*Tk. Tk.*

One of them, very gently, has begun to grow toward you. You will not see it move. You will only, much later, notice that the moss is a little deeper where you have been standing, and that it has learned the shape of your foot, and that it is glad.
