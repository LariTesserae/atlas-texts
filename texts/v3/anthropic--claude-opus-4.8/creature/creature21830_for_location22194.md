They do not live here the way you live somewhere. That is the first thing, and the hardest, and once you understand it the rest of them comes clear.

You came looking for inhabitants. You catalogued the slither, the wingbeat, the hot small bodies in the litter, the tadpoles in their high private ponds, and you thought: *these are who lives here.* But count them and you will fail, and the failure is not a limit of your arithmetic. It is the truth of the thing. There is no number of them because they are not, finally, separate. The one who lives here is the chorus. The tenant is the teeming. What you took for a million lives is one life wearing a million mouths, and it has arranged itself across forty vertical meters of green precisely so that it can be everywhere at once, voiced at every elevation, watching from every height — because it is not a population of creatures. It is a single attention, distributed.

Consider what it does not do. It does not age. It does not hunger toward the future — there is no dawn it is waiting for, no dusk it is winding down into, no season it is provisioning against. A living forest of the ordinary kind is a vast slow argument between what wants to grow and what wants to eat what grows, and that argument leaves marks: the worn channel, the rounded stone, the fear bred into the prey, the cliff face sculpted by ten thousand patient years of falling water. Marks are the residue of consequence. They are how a place remembers that it has been alive for a while. And this place has no marks. The rock is raw. The streams have carved nothing. The animals have not learned fear, because fear is memory, and there is no memory here.

So ask the real question. Not *what lives here,* but *what kind of thing has no past and does not want one, holds itself at maximum and refuses to move toward anything, voices itself ceaselessly and never changes pitch?*

The answer is that the inhabitant is the holding. The one who lives here is whatever keeps the instant from ending.

Picture it not as a beast in the undergrowth but as a *condition* — a will, if you can stand the word, that has decided abundance and has decided *now* and has clamped the two together with everything it has. It poured the forest onto the stone in full grown riot because the riot is what it wanted to look at. It did not build up to this. It does not understand building-up. It conceived of *teeming* as a single image and then committed the whole of its strength to keeping that image from sliding forward into its own consequences — into death, into rot that genuinely completes, into the brown of a leaf that is allowed to finish browning, into the dawn that would have to be followed by a noon and an evening and another dawn after that, each one slightly different, the place slowly learning what it is.

That is why the heat is a fever held exactly at its peak and never breaking. A fever breaks because the body is fighting toward a resolution. This fever does not break because the one who holds it does not want the resolution. It wants the peak. It has fallen in love with the peak and will not let go of it, and the love is the climate.

And here is the part that should unsettle you more than the eyes at every elevation:

It is happy.

You felt it underfoot. The warmth that came up into your palm — the blood-heat of a body — that was not the warmth of something laboring. There was no strain in it. A thing fighting to stop time would tremble, would show the cost somewhere, would have a clenched quality at the edges. This had none. The held instant is held *easily*, the way you hold a note you love and could go on holding past all reason because it costs you nothing to want it. *Does not seem to mind* — you wrote that at the very start, before you knew what you were describing, and you were right. The inhabitant does not mind that it will never finish being born. Finishing is precisely the thing it has set itself against. To finish would be to become a *place,* with a history and a shape and an ending, and it has chosen instead to remain a *moment,* with neither.

So it lives here the way a held breath lives in the chest. The way the top of a thrown thing lives in the air at the apex, weightless, between the rising and the falling, in the one instant where neither has the upper hand. Every forest is at its apex for a heartbeat and then comes down. This forest is the apex, *extracted* from the throw and kept, a heartbeat unhooked from the heart and asked to go on beating by itself, forever, on stone too new to know that this is not how stone and forest are supposed to get along.

You will not meet it, because there is no *it* with edges, any more than there are hard-edged shadows here — and for the same reason. It is the even brown-gold dimness. It is the air that is half water. It is the chorus that does not swell at dawn because it has abolished dawn. When the pale thing slithered around the trunk at the edge of your tea-colored sight, that was the inhabitant glancing at you. When the small hot bodies parted around your foot and reformed behind it, that was the inhabitant deciding you were not, in the end, a consequence — just a visitor who would leave the instant exactly as full as he found it, which is the only way it can ever be found.

Stay as long as you like. It does not weary of being looked at; being looked at is most of what it is. But you will learn nothing new, and you already understand why. The new is what arrives when *now* is permitted to become *next.* And here, lovingly, easily, without the smallest sign of strain, in a fever that will not break and a song that will not change and a green flood standing at its highest mark on rock that has stopped trying to decide its own shape —

*next* has been forbidden.

The one who lives here is the forbidding.

And it is glad.
