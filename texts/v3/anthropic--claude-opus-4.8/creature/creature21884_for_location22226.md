There is no one here, and that is not the same as nothing.

Begin with that distinction, because it is the only honest place to begin. You came looking for an inhabitant — a thing with a face, or at least a front and a back, something that could turn toward you or away. You will not find it. Not because the chamber is empty but because you are standing inside the wrong category. You keep asking *who lives here* when the question the room answers is *what is this part of, and what is it doing.*

So. What lives here.

The walls live here. Not as metaphor — attend to the corrugation, the texture your fingertip insists on and your eye denies. That migration you half-sense is real. The surface is a population. Each fold is a unit, and the units are not decoration and not structure; they are *the body of the thing that lives here, seen edge-on.* You are running your hand along its flank and mistaking the creature for its enclosure, the way a mite on a lung might mistake the alveolar wall for the world's foundation. The animal is not in the room. The room's surfaces are a thin cross-section of the animal, the only part thin enough and slow enough that a visitor's senses can register it at all. The rest of it — the bulk, the volume that matters — extends inward through the stone in directions your body has no name for, because they are not up or down or sideways but *deeper into the function.*

It does not have a center you could find and address. It is distributed the way weather is distributed, the way a tide is — present everywhere along the interface it maintains, concentrated nowhere. When the light swells, that is not illumination; that is the creature's attention passing through this district of itself on its long patrol, a wave of metabolism cresting and moving on. When the drip falls, that is not weather; it is the thing *swallowing*, or perhaps *exhaling*, on a digestive rhythm so slow that a human lifetime would catch only a few hundred breaths of it. You have been listening to a pulse and calling it plumbing.

Consider what it must be, to be built like this.

It does not move through its environment. It *is* its environment's interior, the way a heart does not travel through the chest but constitutes the chest's reason. It was not born into the mountain and it did not invade the mountain; the mountain is the recent, sharp-edged evidence of its *arrival* — the wound-young peak raised by "some recent violence of the crust" is the swelling around the splinter, the crust's startled response to something settling in beneath it. The mountain is young because the tenant is new. This is a thing so large and so deliberate that the act of taking up residence shoved a peak into the sky, and the geology is still flinching.

It has no eyes because it is not looking outward; there is nothing outward to it, only the long maintenance of its inside. It has no mouth that you would recognize because it does not eat the world — it eats *gradients*, the difference between the killing cold of the summit and the banked heat of its own body, and it eats them slowly, the way a fire eats a mountain of coal it has all the time in the world to finish. The clean-ozone smell at the edge of perception is the smell of that difference being harvested. Work being done. The faint steam is the exhaust of a metabolism that runs on temperature itself.

And it is patient in a way that should be understood as a kind of personality, the only personality it has room for. The basins deepening too deliberately for water alone — that is the creature *adjusting*, the way you might shift in sleep, redistributing pressure, easing some long-held position by a fraction of a degree across a span of years. The heat risen, vented, recalled — that is regulation, the homeostasis of something keeping its single sealed organ at the temperature its purpose requires. It is not waiting for anything. Waiting implies an event it anticipates. This thing has no event coming; it has only the work, and the work is the whole of its life, and it does the work the way the work is done — completely, without urgency, without an audience it knows about and without needing one.

That is the thing you must finally sit with, in the warm dark, between the ticks.

It does not know you are here.

Not won't acknowledge you — *cannot.* You are too fast and too small to register against the slow weather of its being, a single warm mote that drifted into one chamber of a body that has thousands, that will be gone again before the light has swelled and ebbed enough times to constitute, for it, a single thought. The indifference you feel is not coldness. It is *scale.* You have crawled inside the lung of a sleeping leviathan that does not dream of you, that has never dreamed of anything as quick as you, that maintains the temperature of this one wet hollow with the same untroubled attention it brings to every other hollow threaded through the mountain it raised — and it will go on maintaining it, breathing water into air and air into water, glowing and dimming on its patient tidal pulse, long after you have found your way back out into the hard blue-black light and gone down to where the air is thick enough to forget.

It lives here the way you live in your own marrow. Constantly. Unconsciously. As the substrate of itself.

*Tick.*

The attention passes through. The steam brightens with the wave of its slow metabolism cresting in this distant room of its body.

*Tick.*

It moves on, inward, deeper into the function — and the dark that returns is not empty after all. It is *full.* Full of the one vast living thing that the chamber is a single cell of, breathing in the warm and the wet, indifferent and complete, doing the only thing it has ever done, in the only place it has ever been: inside.
