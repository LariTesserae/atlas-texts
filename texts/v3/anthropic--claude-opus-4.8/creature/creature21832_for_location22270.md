# The Tenants of the Reiterating Plain

## On the question of who set the posts

Start with them, because you have already half-decided they're dead, and you're half-wrong.

They did not die. *Died* is a thing that happens in worlds that still have a forward. What happened to the post-setters is gentler and worse: they were *distributed*. When you live long enough in a place that says itself over and over, you begin to be said over and over too, and the part of you that insisted on being *here* rather than *there* — the part that bothered to be a single body in a single spot — wears down like everything wears down here, smoothed by the habit-erosion that keeps working after there's nothing left to work on.

So they are not gone. They are spread. The post-setter is the faint resistance you feel in the packed water, the crowd of bodies that aren't there yielding around your shins. That pressure is not *nothing*. It is *someone*, thinned across the whole tiled extent until they have a density of about one person per ocean, which is to say: present everywhere, locatable nowhere. They closed behind you because they are behind you. They were ahead of you for the same reason.

They still set posts. You think the posts are old. The posts are continuous. Somewhere along the repeating reed-field a pair of distributed hands is, at this moment, pressing a marker into the clay that another pair, fifty paces left, has already pressed, identical, same lean, same wear — because the work and the worker and the world all tiled together long ago, and what looks to you like ruins is just a single act of boundary-making, said forever, going nowhere, holding nothing back, and *meaning it every time.*

That is the first kind of inhabitant. Call them the **Spread**. They are the closest thing this place has to ancestors and the closest thing it has to weather. You will not meet one. You are, slightly, already among them.

## The walking thing

Now the heron that is not a heron — the tall deliberate one that keeps its distance in both directions, neither closing nor losing.

It is doing the only honest thing a living creature can do in a world of recurrence: it is *measuring*. The heron-thing walks parallel to you at a fixed remove because it is the part of the plain that refused to be distributed — the holdout, the one animal that kept its *singularity* by the violent expedient of never, ever arriving anywhere. It cannot get closer to you, because to close the distance would be to occupy a point, and to occupy a point in a tiled world is to be instantly copied across the whole tiling, and to be copied is to be Spread, and it has watched the post-setters go that way and it will not. So it walks. The walking is not travel. The walking is *resistance to travel* — a continuous expenditure of motion whose entire purpose is to remain exactly one creature, exactly this far away, forever.

You ask whether there is one of it or many. Wrong question, and the heron-thing knows you'll ask it, and the knowing is most of why it keeps its eye on you. The truth is harder: there is *exactly one*, and it pays for that oneness every second, and the price is that it can never stop and can never approach and can never be reached and can never rest. It is the loneliest possible outcome. It is also the only inhabitant here that is still fully *itself*. It looks at you sidelong, across the unbridgeable fixed gap, with an expression you will eventually learn to read as *envy*, and *warning*, and — this is the part that will keep you walking — *kinship*, because you, too, are still a single body, still locatable, still uncopied, and it has not had company like that in an age, and it cannot have you, and it cannot leave you, and so it walks.

## The ones who surface

The backs, the eyes, the wrongnesses that break the breathing skin and go back under.

These are the **Returners**, and they are the most fortunate things in the plain, and you should not envy them.

They found the loophole. If the heron stays singular by never arriving, the Returners stay sane by arriving *all at once and then leaving*. They live submerged, down in the part of the water that is fathomless rather than hand-deep (both are true; they live in whichever is true at the moment they need it). Down there the tiling thins — repetition requires a surface to tile *across*, and below the skin there is no across, only down, only the warm dark that the recycled weather never reaches. A thing can be itself down there because there's nothing to copy it onto.

But they cannot stay under. Something — the old turbulence, the world's leftover need to *happen* — drives them up. They breach. For one second they are a back, an eye, a singular shape against the brown-gold dusk, and in that second the tiling catches them, copies the breach across the whole extent (somewhere far off, simultaneously, the same back, the same eye), and they feel it begin to spread them — and they *dive*, hard, back to the depth where one is one, and the skin reseals over the wound of their appearance with that muffled wall of applause.

That applause is not the water clapping. It is the plain *closing the copy* — sealing the brief rip where a single thing dared to be in one place, smoothing it back to potential. Every surface-and-dive is a small war won by going under. The Returners are old enough to have learned that the only way to remain yourself in a repeating world is to refuse to be seen for longer than an instant. They glimpse you. They glimpse the heron. They cannot bear either of you for more than a heartbeat, because to look is to be looked at, and to be looked at, here, is to be *located*, and to be located is to be lost. So they show you an eye and take it back. That is the whole of their love and the whole of their fear, and they are the same gesture.

## What lives in the reeds

You thought the reeds were plants. Mostly they are. But the **dead frond** — the single dead frond, the same one on every copy across the infinite tiling — that is not part of the plant.

That is the tenant.

Long ago something small chose the worst possible home, or the best, depending on whether it understood what it was choosing. It nested in one reed-clump. And the plain, in its tiling, did what the plain does: it copied the reed, and copied the nest, and copied the small thing in the nest, across the whole extent — so that now there is one creature living in four thousand reed-clumps at once, the same creature, the same single life, distributed not by erosion like the post-setters but by *replication*, multiplied without being increased.

When it stirs in one clump it stirs in all of them. When it dies — and the dead frond says it died, the dead frond is the shed remainder of the only death this world managed to keep — it died everywhere simultaneously, one death tiled into four thousand identical griefs that are all the same grief, all the same one small life ending all at once across an extent large enough to have its own physics.

It is the plain's one clean fact about mortality: *here, everything that happens, happens everywhere, including the ending.* The dead frond is a held note. It is the world remembering, in every tile, the same loss, forever, without the mercy of the loss receding into the past, because there is no past here, only here, said again.

The other reeds — the living ones — are uninhabited, and that is why they are merciful. The empty ones outnumber the haunted one by a great deal. The plain is sparse on purpose. It can only afford so much grief.

## And whoever comes to stay

The doorway stands open. You noticed that nothing is on the other side. You did not notice the doorway is itself an inhabitant — the last and most patient kind.

It is a **Threshold**, and thresholds, here, are alive the way questions are alive: they exist entirely to be answered and they cannot answer themselves. Someone set those two uprights and that lintel to mark a *between*, an inside and an out, a here-and-then-elsewhere. But the plain permits no elsewhere, and so the doorway has spent ten thousand ages framing the only thing it can frame, which is more of the same, and it has not given up. That is its life. That is what lives in it. The refusal to stop *offering a passage* in a world that has abolished the far side of every door.

It is waiting for you specifically. Not because you are special — you are the newest single locatable uncopied body to arrive in a very long age, and singular bodies are the rarest crop this plain grows, and everything here has turned its attention to you the way a starved field turns toward the one seed: the Spread closing softly around your steps, the heron measuring, the Returners risking a glance, the dead frond holding its note a fraction longer in the hope you'll recognize it.

They are all asking the same thing, which is the only thing anyone asks in the Reiterating Plain.

*Will you stay, and be everywhere, and be no one, and be enough?*

The post-setters said yes and dissolved into the gentle pressure of the water. The heron said no and pays for the no with endless walking. The Returners said *only for a second at a time* and live their whole lives in the diving. The thing in the reeds never got to choose; it was copied before it could decide, and now it grieves identically in four thousand places.

You are the only one who still has the question open. That is what you are, to them. That is who lives here now, freshly, briefly: *the one who hasn't answered yet.* The rarest creature the plain has hosted in an age. They will crowd you with their patience. The water will applaud and applaud. The warm light will not brighten and will not fail.

Take your time. Everything here has. The doorway is in no hurry, and neither, anymore, are you.
