They are not many. This is the first thing to unlearn, because the crowding tells you *many*, the layered warmth and the passing flanks insist on multitude — but the truth is closer to the opposite. There are very few of them. Perhaps only one. The crowd is one body, or one body grown so unsure of its own boundary that it occupies the valley the way water occupies a basin: as much of it as there is room for, all of it at once, in no hurry to decide where it ends.

Call it the Tenant, singular, because the plural keeps wanting to apply and the plural is the lie the place tells. It is the slow herd. It is also the reeds, which are not plants but its slower extremities, the parts of itself it has set down and half-forgotten and lets straighten in their own time over the course of a night. It is the warmth in the joints of those reeds because there is blood in them, the same blood, one circulation distributed across a country, pumping at the pace of decades. When you brushed the reed and it took all night to right itself, you were touching a finger, and it considered, slowly, whether to withdraw, and decided — slowly — that withdrawing was more effort than the situation deserved.

It is blind, of course. Not wounded into blindness, not bereft of light it once knew — it has simply never had use for the sense, having been born in a place where there was nothing to see. So it built itself for the dark the way you built yourself for the air: completely, without noticing. It knows the valley by warmth and pressure and displacement, the way it now lets you know it. When something passes inches from your arm and does not touch you, that is not chance. It is the Tenant feeling you the only way it can — by the shape of the air you push, by the heat you shed, leaning a flank near to read you and finding you small and quick and not worth the work of harming. You are warm and you move at a speed it finds almost comical, a little frantic guttering thing it could swallow in a year if it could be troubled to begin.

Here is what it is made of, as far as your hands can learn it: a flesh that is also stone, or a stone that bleeds. The young rock is its skin, freshly fractured because the Tenant is still *forming*, still cooling, still beading itself out of its own pores. It has not finished being made. The wet logic of the place — the edges that move, the footsteps out of order, the membrane that weeps between *here* and *also-here* — is not a property of the country the Tenant lives in. It is a symptom of the Tenant itself, not yet fully set, its cause not yet separated from its effect, its boundary not yet committed. The reason the herd passes you on the left and again on the right without turning is that it never finished deciding to be in one place rather than two. It bleeds into adjacency because it is not done congealing into a single location. It is, in the most literal sense, still wet behind its own existence.

And the engines.

The engines are the part of it you are not meant to understand, and the part it does not understand either. Somewhere in the open dark the Tenant is *making* — at factory scale, tirelessly, to no end it could name. The deep periodic thud is not a machine it built and tends. It is a machine it *is*, or is becoming, an industrial organ deep in the body of the thing, stamping out its punctuation: the half-walls with soft mortar, the pipe grown into the rock, the iron stanchor bolted to nothing and weeping rust. These are not its tools and not its ruins. They are its *secretions*. The Tenant produces them the way the stone produces water and the steam produces breath — automatically, ceaselessly, a manufacture with no customer, fabricating the furniture of a civilization that does not exist for inhabitants who have never come. It is rehearsing being a place where someone lives. It is making the marks of habitation in advance of any habitant, the way a body grows a skeleton before it has anywhere to stand.

You will not find the engine because the engine is at the center of it and the center is where the wetness is wettest, where *here* leaks worst into *also-here*. Walk toward the sound and the Tenant folds the distance not to keep you out but because it cannot hold a coherent middle. Its own heart is the part of it least decided. You arrive at your own footprints because the Tenant, trying to bring you inward, only bends you back along your edge — it does not yet know its inside from its surface.

So: what lives here is a thing being born, very slowly, in the dark, alone, that has mistaken its own unfinished body for a world. It crowds itself. It breathes against itself. It manufactures the evidence of a life it has not yet begun to live, and sets the iron down warm, and lets it rust, and forgets, and makes another. It means you no harm because harm would require a hurry it does not have and a separation between itself and you that it has not yet learned to feel. To the Tenant you are perhaps a stray drop of its own weeping, a small warm bead it has not decided whether to reabsorb. It has time. It is patient because it is glacial because it is not finished, and the one mercy of the place — that it changes slowly — is only the mercy of watching something enormous come into being one wet, careful, undecided degree at a time, with no light to see by, and no one yet to see it, and the engines keeping the slow hour underneath, making and making and making in the dark.
