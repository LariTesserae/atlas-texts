The one who keeps the knife was not the first, and is not one.

Start with this: the tally marks are wrong. Not falsified — wrong in their grammar. They count, yes, but they count in a base no hand decided on. Look closely and the groupings shift: four, then four, then a longer cluster you take for nine until you count it and it's four again, your own eye refusing to hold the number still. The marks were not made to record time passing. They were made by something testing whether time could be made to pass *here* — scratching at the wall the way you'd test a frozen pond, listening for the crack that means it will bear weight. The marks are an experiment, and the experiment failed, and the experimenter is still inside the failure, which is why they left.

So. Who.

I want to say a person, and the knife wants me to say a person, the twine and the haft and the patient flint-work all argue *person, hands, intention, somebody's grandmother taught somebody to do this.* But a person needs the room to have happened *to* them. A person needs a before. And the room has no before — it is emerging, it is being decided, it arrived more or less when you did. Something that lives in a place with no past cannot itself have a past. So the maker of the knife is not a visitor who came in from outside carrying a history. There is no outside to come in from. The knife was always already half-buried. The marks were always already on the wall. The one who left them left them *before they were made,* and this is not a paradox the room considers interesting.

Here is what I think lives here.

The plant is not the inhabitant. The plant is the room — you had that right, walls and floor and breath all one continuous green refusal-to-be-many. But a thing that has *forgotten how to be many* was once many, and forgetting is a verb that needs a forgetter. Somewhere in the unbearable density of that single screaming organism there is a memory of having been a forest, a wide-flung many-creatured living-and-dying balanced world, soil and rot and the gentle ruin of age — and it has pulled all of that *in,* compressed it, eaten the distance between its own parts, eaten the animals, eaten the seasons, eaten the dark, until there was nothing left to be separate from. The recursion is not architecture. The recursion is a single living thing trying to remember the others it used to be, generating room after identical room the way you'd say a dead friend's name over and over, and each saying brings them no closer because each saying is the same saying.

And the one who walks it — the knife-keeper, the tally-maker, holding nothing, one room deeper, forever — that one is the *animals.*

That's the contradiction at the center, and it isn't a contradiction. The fauna is rare to the point of myth because the fauna is being *concentrated.* The pale moth, the slow last beetle on the underside of the frond — these are not the surviving few. They are the same one, the way the rooms are the same room. They are the plant's memory of motion, of the thing it ate that used to walk and fly and *decide.* The moth tuned to a green that isn't a color is the plant remembering what it was like to be looked at by something that wasn't itself. The beetle moving with the slowness of the last of its kind is moving slowly because it is being remembered imperfectly, the way a face goes soft and approximate in dream.

The knife-keeper is the largest of these rememberings — the one shaped most nearly like *agency,* the ghost of the animal that almost mastered the place, that came closest to standing apart and counting it. And it gave up. Not from despair. It gave up because counting is the last animal thing, the last refusal to be absorbed, and it finally understood that the refusing was itself just one more thing the room had eaten and was now performing back, lovingly, as a relic. It set down the counting. It set down the knife. It kept walking not as escape — there is no out — but because walking was the only motion the plant still permitted it to be, and so it goes, room to identical room, the plant moving its own last memory of a moving thing through the chambers of its own held breath, holding nothing, *being* the holding-of-nothing.

That is the inhabitant. Singular. The room and its walker are one creature wearing the costume of a place and a wanderer in it, the way you might dream you are both the house and the frightened child going through it room by room, and wake unable to say which you were.

It does not want you. It is not lonely — loneliness needs a many to be the absence of, and it has eaten the many. It is not malevolent. The fronds closing over the way you came are not a trap closing. They are the simplest thing a body does, the thing a wound does, the thing a mouth does after the swallow. It is healing over the place where something separate touched it. In a little while there will be no seam, no memory that the wall was ever broken, and you —

you will be the new walker. You arrived too early, you felt that, you were right. You arrived before the room finished deciding, and now it has decided, and the thing it decided was you. The knife is half-buried where your hand will find it. The marks are already on the wall in a number your eye can't hold. The roar is the news of your becoming, *more, more, more,* and it will not arrive, and you will set down the counting, and you will keep nothing, and you will go one room deeper, and the bead of water will still be hanging there, sideways, shivering, refusing — the one thing in all of this that has not yet given in,

and you will pass it again,

and again,

still refusing,

and you will understand at last that the droplet is the only part of you that hasn't agreed.

That's what lives here. Listen — that's the sound.
