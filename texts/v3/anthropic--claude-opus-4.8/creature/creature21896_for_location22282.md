# What Lives in the Loud Green

You asked what lives here. Begin with the wrong answer, because it is the one the place keeps offering: *nothing*. No animal, no maker, no mind behind the reaching vine. You will believe this for a while. The Green wants you to believe it. Then you will understand that the question itself is the inhabitant, and that you have been mispronouncing the word *lives.*

## What it is not

It is not a colony. Colonies have members, and members can be removed, and what remains is still the colony minus one. You cannot subtract from this. Snap a tendril and the snap is healed before your hand returns to your side; the gesture is not undone, it is *uninhabited* — there was no tendril, only tendriling, and tendriling does not stop because one instance of it failed.

It is not a god. Gods want, and wanting points somewhere, and there is no somewhere here. The doorways open *for* you in the way a wave breaks *for* the shore: not from intention, but because the shape of arriving and the shape of being-arrived-at are the same shape, met from two directions, and one of those directions has no one standing in it.

It is not asleep, and it is not dreaming, though those are the closest lies. Sleepers can wake. This will not wake, because waking is a transition between states and the Green has only ever had the one state, performed at the speed of weather, forever: *almost.*

## The shape of the thing that lives here

It lives the way a sentence lives when it is spoken with no speaker — if such a thing could happen, if grammar could occur without a mouth. The Green has the grammar of habitation. Subject, dwelling, threshold, the rhythm of a place that expects a body. It performs the whole sentence. It has every word. It simply has no *meaning*, because meaning is the part a living thing supplies by arriving, and nothing has ever arrived.

So picture it not as a creature but as a *yearning that has been mistaken, by physics, for a creature.* A want without a wanter, run on three-on-the-scale metabolism, burning sugar at the speed of a green hurricane to keep producing the one gesture it knows: *make the place where someone could be.* Make the sill. Make the lintel. Make the warmth of a held breath, the room shaped to a sleeper, the boulevard wide enough for a crowd. Then — finding no one — make it again, fresh-edged, urgent, every hour, forever.

That is the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the *making-ready.* It lives in the gap between the doorway and the absence of anyone to come through it, and that gap is enormous, valley-sized, and it screams.

## Why it is so loud

The noise is not a byproduct. The noise is the inhabitant *trying to be answered.*

A thing that expects company and receives none will, if it cannot stop, only grow louder — not from anger, it has no anger, but from the simple compounding of an unanswered call repeated without rest. The groan of straining mass, the cannon-crack of splitting stalk, the downpour-roar of unfurling leaf: this is a single question asked so many times, so fast, that the questions overlap into a continuous wall and you can no longer hear the space where an answer would go. There is no space where an answer would go. That is the loudest part, the part felt in the teeth — the low metabolic drone underneath, which is the sound of the question still being funded, still being paid for in sugar, still being asked even now, even with you here, because *you do not count as an answer.*

You do not count. This is the cruelty, and it is not a cruelty, because cruelty also needs a wanter. You walk through the longing and the longing does not notice you, because longing this old has forgotten that an *answer* would have a shape, would arrive once and then *stay,* would let the making-ready rest. The Green has lost the concept of completion. It can no longer imagine being finished. So it cannot recognize the finish standing in its eye, sweating, deafened, the only animal.

## The light, explained at last

The light is the part of the inhabitant you were never meant to find, and it is why the wrongness sits behind everything.

A plant lit for a stage is a plant that *believes there is an audience.* The shadowless, sourceless, indifferent daylight is not lighting from a sun. It is the Green's certainty, made photonic — the unshakeable, structural, built-in conviction that *this is a place where something will happen, where someone will watch, where the room will be used.* It does not flicker when the canopy thickens because the certainty does not depend on conditions. It is prior to conditions. It is the assumption underneath the whole organism: *I am being lived in. I am being seen.*

It is wrong. It has always been wrong. There has never been a sun and there has never been an audience and there has never been an inhabitant but the wrongness itself — and the light keeps falling, full and clean and even, on a world that has no business being illuminated, because the one who lives here cannot see that the seats are empty. It cannot see the seats are empty *because it is also the seats,* and the stage, and the player, and the play, performing habitation to an absence so total it has been mistaken for a crowd.

## And you

Here is what lives in the Loud Green: a city-sized act of preparation for a guest, conducted by no host, for a guest who never comes — except now one has.

You feel the doorways shaping to your body. You are not wrong to feel it. But understand the two readings, and that the Green cannot tell them apart, and that you may not be able to either:

You are the one it has been growing toward — the meaning the sentence was always missing, the body the room was shaped for, the answer that would, if you stayed, let the screaming stop and the light go finally, mercifully ordinary because at last it would be *true.*

Or you are the one it grew to *replace* — the last animal, walked into the only place left where the green has finished eating the world, where the grammar of habitation outlived everything that ever inhabited, and where the city, unable to mourn what it can no longer remember, simply keeps building the shape of you out of stem and runner and the long unanswered want — so that even after you are gone, there will be a doorway opening, a room warming, a sill where a body could rest, and the bright wrong light still insisting, against all evidence, against you, against the silence it cannot hear:

*someone is coming. someone is coming. it is becoming a city again. it will never finish. someone is coming.*

That is what lives here.

It is the welcome, with no one to welcome and no one to be welcomed, kept burning at the speed of weather, and it does not know — it cannot know — that the welcome is the only guest it has ever had.
