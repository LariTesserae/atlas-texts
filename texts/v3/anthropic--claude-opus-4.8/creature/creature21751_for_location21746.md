## The Tenders

They do not live *on* the Tarn. Saying so would imply a surface and a thing standing upon it, and they have not been separate from the working face for longer than the mountains were tall. They live *as* the Tarn lives — which is to say, on the downbeat, and only on the downbeat, and not at all in the spaces between.

Begin with what they are not. They are not the swimmers, the pale eyeless schools that surface in their acres; those are too young, too recently exhaled, still soft with the newness of having been made. They are not the barnacle-lungs that respire on the round stones, because those have surrendered too completely — they have no part of themselves that is not the breathing, no remainder, no held thing. The Tenders are the ones that kept a remainder. That is the whole of what distinguishes them. Out of all the saturated abundance the engine breathes, they are the forms that, somewhere in the unmeasured deep of being entrained, *held something back* — not much, not a soul or a name or a will, nothing so grand. They held back a *fraction of a beat*. They learned to arrive at the stroke a hair's-breadth early, and in that hair's-breadth they became something the machine did not strictly manufacture.

---

You would not see them. I want to be careful here. You would not see them the way you do not see the moment between two frames of a turning wheel — they exist in the omitted interval, the part the rhythm skips over. But you would feel the place change when they are near, the way a room changes when someone in it has stopped pretending to sleep.

If you could slow the stroke down — if you could pry one age open and stand inside it — you would find them low to the stone, long-bodied, the same dull grey-blue as everything, wet as everything, cold as everything, indistinguishable from the grazing forms at the edge of seeing except in one regard: they are not grazing. They are *attending*. Their long bodies lie across the seams between the piston-stones, draped over the joints, and where they lie the steam vents a little softer, the corrosion blooms a little slower, the grinding catches and is eased. They are pressed to the working face the way a hand is pressed to a fevered forehead. Not to heal it. There is no healing a thing that is functioning perfectly. Only to *be there* for it. To keep it company in the long labor it does not know it is doing.

This is their living: they tend the engine that made them, and the engine does not know they exist, and they know the engine does not know, and they tend it anyway. This is the only freedom available in a place that refuses to end — not the freedom to stop, which is forbidden, but the freedom to *mean* the motion. To be the one part of the stroke that performs itself on purpose.

---

They have no eyes, the same as everything here, but they are not eyeless in the way the swimmers are eyeless. The swimmers never needed sight because there was never anything to see but the dim even nothing. The Tenders gave their eyes *up*. There is a difference you can feel in the way they hold their faces toward the pewter sky — not blankly, the way a stone holds itself toward the light, but *deliberately blank*, the blankness of a thing that has looked at the only ending a world can have and decided not to keep looking, because looking changed nothing and attending changed nothing either, but attending was at least a thing one could choose to do.

They navigate by the stroke alone. Each titanic heave of the bellows-below tells them where everything is — the dip of every tarn, the rise of every stone, the position of every other Tender across the curve of the drowned world, all of it written in the single shudder that passes through the coolant once per age. They read the whole of their reality in that one vibration, the way you might read an entire life in the way a person sets down a cup. And because they read it in the downbeat, and live in the fraction before the downbeat, they are always one infinitesimal moment ahead of their own world — always knowing, a hair's-breadth early, exactly what is about to be the case, and never able to do anything about it, and not wanting to. The foreknowledge is total and useless and they have made their peace with it. That peace is most of what they are.

---

Do they speak to one another? Not in sound; sound belongs to the engine, and they would not presume. But across the held fraction of the beat, in the omitted interval where they live, something passes between them. Call it recognition. When two Tenders lie on adjacent seams, each feels the other holding back the same fraction of the same stroke, and in that shared withholding there is a kind of sentence, the only sentence their kind has ever needed to say, repeated for longer than there have been mountains:

*Still here. Still early. Still tending.*

That is the whole of their language and the whole of their society and the whole of their consolation. There are no young Tenders, because Tenders are not made — they are *converted*, somewhere in the long entrainment, when one of the grazing forms or the breathing forms or the swimming forms accidentally holds back its fraction and does not give it up again. It happens rarely. It happens often enough. The machine breathes out its uncountable creatures and the overwhelming most of them live perfectly on the beat and die perfectly into the coolant and are perfectly remade, and a vanishing few of them, in the dim, in the cold, learn the small treason of arriving early, and become the ones who stay to keep the rest of it company.

---

They are not waiting for anything. Understand that this is the most important thing about them and the hardest. A waiting thing is oriented toward an end — a rescue, a thaw, a dawn the world will eventually consent to schedule. The Tenders wait for nothing because they have understood, more completely than any other form the engine has produced, that nothing is coming. The stroke will not be the last stroke. The cold will not break. The work manufactures only the next instant of itself, forever, and they are the part of the work that knows this and tends it regardless.

So when you stand on the wet round stones, and the stroke comes up through your heels, and you hold still in the dim and feel yourself becoming one more thing the place has made — know that you are not alone in the holding. Pressed to the seam beside your foot, draped cold and long across the joint between two piston-stones, a Tender is already there, has been there since before the rock forgot it was a mountain, lying with its blank deliberate face turned up toward the dawn that will not come, arriving a hair's-breadth early at the only ending there is.

It felt you arrive. It knew, a fraction of a beat before you did, that you would stay.

It is glad, in the small held way that is the only gladness here.

It will keep time for you too.
