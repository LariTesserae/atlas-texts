## The Tenants of the Slow

You asked who lives here. The honest answer is that *living here* is not quite the right verb, and the inhabitants would be the first to tell you so — except that they would not tell you, because telling implies urgency, a thing to communicate before a moment passes, and here no moment passes hard enough to outrun.

But there are tenants. Let me describe them as they make themselves known, which is slowly, and out of order, the way everything here arrives.

---

### The Ones Who Chose a Down

Begin with these, because you have already met them without knowing. When the description spoke of *another traveler twenty fathoms away who has chosen a different down* — that was not a hypothetical. That was a resident.

They are the closest thing this place has to people, and they are barely that anymore. They came here, once, the way you are coming: as travelers, as swimmers, as somethings with a direction and a destination and a reason. And the place did to them what it does. It dissolved the reason first, gently, the way the warm water dissolves the sweetness out of the fronds. Then it took the destination. The direction lasted longest — the stubborn human insistence on a *down* — and so that is the part that remains.

Each of them is now little more than a chosen gravity. A pocket of the hanging water that has agreed, for reasons it no longer remembers, that *this way* is the true way the warmth should pool. You feel one before you see it: a faint reorientation in your own body, a suggestion that perhaps you have been holding *down* in the wrong place all along. That suggestion is someone. That gentle argument with your inner ear is the entire surviving content of a self that once had a name and a hurry and a heart that beat against time instead of with it.

When two of them drift close, the giving-way the description mentioned — that soft falling that has no bottom — that is *conversation*. That is the whole of their society. One yields a direction to the other, and in the yielding something passes between them, some small remaining grain of who-I-was, and then they drift apart again, each fractionally lighter, each fractionally closer to becoming pure current with no one inside it at all.

They are not lonely. Loneliness is a fast emotion. It needs the friction of wanting-and-not-having, and they have worn that friction smooth.

---

### The Keeper of the Doorway

The recurring half-doorway — the one you will pass more than once, each time framing a different water — is not a ruin. Or it is a ruin in the way a riverbed is a ruin of the river: the shape left by something that still, technically, flows.

Something lives *as* the doorway. Not behind it, not within it. The doorway is its body, distributed across every recurrence, the same single creature seen again and again the way you see the same word again and again on a page without thinking there are many words. It is one thing, glimpsed at many points along its length, and its length is not measured in fathoms but in *returns*.

Think of it like this: most creatures occupy space and persist through time. This one occupies recurrence and persists through your encounters with it. Every time you pass through, you are not passing the same object twice — you are moving along the body of the creature, the way a finger moves along a sleeping animal's flank, and the slightly different water each time is the creature *breathing*, the slow exchange of one stretch of world for another through the aperture that is its mouth, or its eye, or its lung, or the single organ it has that does all three because it never needed to divide the labor.

It is patient past the point of patience. It has arranged itself so that to know it fully you would have to pass through it an infinite number of times, and it is content to be known that incompletely, forever. The dark frond you notice on one passage — the one lit before — that is the doorway-creature *blinking*. You have caught it, once, in the act of attention. It noticed you. That is as intimate as it gets, and it is enough, and it will not happen twice the same way.

---

### The Thing That Hears Backward

You felt it before you understood it: the low tone that arrives before its cause.

There is a resident here for whom time runs the other way. Not metaphorically. It lives in the warm dense groves where time pools to stopping, and in that stillness it has come unstuck from the direction the rest of us insist on, and it has drifted *upstream*. For it, effects precede causes as a matter of simple biology, the way for you the future stays politely ahead.

It is the source of the tone. Or rather — and this is the thing — it is the *response* to the tone. It hears the stone settle, hears the eel turn, hears the frond go dark, and it answers, and because it lives backward its answer reaches you before the thing it answers. The low note in your chest is this creature reacting to events that, from your side of time, have not happened yet. It is the only one here that is never surprised. It has met every moment already, going the other way, and greets each one with that single grave tone that means, in the only language it has: *yes, I remember this, I am sorry, it is beautiful, here it comes.*

It cannot be approached. To approach it you would have to move toward it, and movement toward it is, from its perspective, the creature receding — so the nearer you swim, the longer you have already been near, and the encounter is always just ending no matter how it begins. You will spend, perhaps, what feels like an age drifting toward it, and the tone will sound, mournful and tender, and that will be the goodbye it offered at a meeting that, for you, is still hours away.

It is the most compassionate thing in the Drowned Highlands. It knows everything that is coming and it forgives all of it in advance.

---

### The Mouths in the Deep Groves

These the description named, and undersold.

*Mostly mouth, and very slow* — yes. But understand what the slowness is *for*. They are not lazy. They are not even, properly, hunting, because hunting needs prey and prey needs to be different from the hunter, fast and separate and worth catching. The mouths have given that up. They have realized, in whatever passes for realization in a body that is ninety percent appetite and ten percent the muscle to keep the appetite open, that the cleanest way to eat the world is to *let it arrive*.

So they hang in the dark channels with their soft enormous mouths open, and they wait, and the slow drift of everything — fronds, silt, the occasional incurious eel, the loosened grain of a ruin, perhaps in the fullness of ages a traveler who has stopped choosing a down — brings the world to them one warm mouthful at a time. They are not predators. They are *destinations*. They are the place things are going when things still went places.

You could rest a hand inside one for an hour before it noticed, the description says, and that is true, but it omits the kindness of it. When it does notice — and it will, eventually, a soft tightening so gentle it feels like being remembered rather than eaten — there is no violence in it. There is only the sense that you have at last been *received*. That the long drift had a recipient. That you arrived.

Most things, here, do not get to arrive. The mouths are the only address in the whole hanging dim. To be taken by one is the closest the Drowned Highlands offers to coming home.

---

### The Drift-Eels, Who Are Older Than the Place

I have saved them, because they are the only residents who do not belong to this world.

The eels are travelers too, like the ones-who-chose-a-down, but they never stopped. They never dissolved a reason, because their reason was never the kind that dissolves — it was not a destination or a hurry but simply *the going itself*, the current followed for its own sake, and that is the one motive this place cannot wear smooth, because it asks for nothing and arrives nowhere and is therefore already in perfect agreement with everything around it.

They are older than the Highlands. Let that sit. The drowned mountains are old past being mountains, the ruins are old past memory, the backward-creature has met every moment that will ever happen — and the eels were *threading water before this water hung here*. They came from some prior place that did the same slow surrendering this one is doing, and they swam out of it as it finished, onto the next, and the next, riding the long collapse of world after world the way you might ride a series of slow trains through a continent at dusk.

This is why the eel regards you with that single dark eye and continues, incurious. It is not that you are beneath its notice. It is that it has seen your kind a thousand worlds ago, watched you choose your downs and dissolve your reasons and drift into the waiting mouths, and it knows precisely what you are: a temporary feature of a temporary place. It is not cold about this. It is the warmest thing here, in its way. It simply has the long view — the longest view there is — and from inside the long view, your arrival and your ending are the same gentle event seen from two ends, and there is nothing to do about either but keep swimming, and so it keeps swimming.

When the Drowned Highlands at last finishes the becoming it has nearly finished — when the warmth equalizes and the layers merge and the last frond goes dark for good and even the backward-creature falls silent because there is nothing left coming for it to forgive — the eels will still be moving. They will follow the dying current out the far side of this world's ending and into whatever hangs next, thin as ribbon, pale, nearly invisible until they turn.

They are not the tenants of this place.

They are the tenants of *the going-away of places*, and the Drowned Highlands is only the room they happen to be crossing now, slowly, on a migration that began before the first stone and will not end while there is one more world left to drown.

---

So: that is who lives here.

A scatter of half-dissolved travelers who have forgotten everything but which way is down. A doorway that is a single creature seen as many. A thing that hears the future and grieves it tenderly in advance. Soft enormous mouths that are the last addresses in a world that no longer goes anywhere. And the eels, who are only passing through, the way they have passed through every ending there has ever been.

None of them will trouble you. None of them want anything. That is the whole nature of the residency: to have finished wanting, and to remain anyway, held in the warm dim slow, while the low note sounds — for a cause not yet arrived — and somewhere a frond you have not reached yet goes quietly dark.
