## The Tenders

You asked who lives here, and the description has already half-answered: the builders. Let me tell you what they are, because the place has been telling you all along — you only have to read it at the right speed.

Start, as the place does, with the height. With time.

A creature that evolved on a finished landscape does not need to be fast. Speed is a solution to a moving world — to predators that lunge, prey that bolts, weather that strikes, seasons that close a window. Up here nothing strikes. The day breathes warm and the evening breathes cool, and that is the whole of the calendar, repeating across a duration the stone has long since stopped counting. A body that grew up inside that rhythm would take its tempo from the rhythm itself. It would learn that nothing is ever lost by waiting, because nothing here is ever taken.

So the Tenders are slow. Not sluggish — *slow* the way the plateau is worn: as a completed condition, an achievement, a form arrived at and kept. To watch one move would be like watching the hour hand. You would swear it was a feature of the landscape, one more rounded swell of pale stone in the lee of a cairn, until you came back the next evening and found it three body-lengths along, having crossed to a dew-cistern that needed retopping, its work between your visits invisible as the turning of the sky.

This is why you see no Tenders. You have been looking for an animal. What you walked past were *boulders that were thinking*.

### Their bodies

They are large — larger than you, low and broad, built like the swells they shelter against, and colored exactly the ochre-and-bone-and-old-tooth grey of the rock, because their skin *is* very nearly that stone. They secrete the same weeping mineral that glazes the hollows; over a lifetime it sets across their backs in a slow polished carapace, so that an old Tender is half-fossilized while still alive, a creature wearing the landscape as a shell and adding to it one bead of mineral sweat at a time. The oldest of them may be indistinguishable from the ground — may, in fact, no longer move at all, having settled at last into a final hollow and let the glaze close over them, alive inside the stone, still speaking.

Beneath that crust the body is soft and water-hoarding, a sac of slow metabolism that asks almost nothing of the world. They do not hunt. They do not graze, exactly. They *farm* — they are the apex of the stacked silent cities, the gardeners of every packed hollow, and they live on the surplus the colonies produce, the filtered dew rendered down, the overflow of all that quiet abundance. In return they tend. They build the cairns to catch more dew. They open the cisterns. They carry — slowly, slowly — a clutch of filter-things from a drying basin to a fuller one, cradled in the broad underside of the body, an act of relocation that might take a season and save a city.

And the hands. You guessed hands, and you were right, though not as you pictured them. The forward edge of the body divides into a fringe of thick, blunt, immensely strong manipulators — not fingers so much as slow tongues of muscle and mineral, each capable of lifting and placing a polished stone, of stacking a cairn rounded to match the world because the world is the only model the maker has ever known. *They learned their forms from the erosion itself.* Of course they did. They are made of the same patience that did the eroding.

### Their speech

Here is the heart of them, and the answer to the silence.

The Tenders do not make sound because sound is *fast*. Sound is gone the instant it is made; it demands an ear quick enough to catch it before it dies. A creature living at the speed of the hour hand could never hold a conversation in noise — the first word would have decayed to nothing before the second was finished. Sound is the language of the hasty. It was never available to them.

So they speak as the colonies speak, in light — but where a basin of filter-things flushes blue-to-violet in seconds, the Tenders flush across *hours*. A single sentence might begin at the warm haze of midday and complete itself only at the violet condensing hour, one enormous slow statement of color crossing the whole broad back of the creature, deepening, resolving, fading. And because their skin is continuous with the colonies they tend — because they are wired into those packed silent cities through the very mineral they share — when a Tender speaks, the colonies *carry* it. The signal passes out of the great slow body into the small fast ones, relayed hollow to hollow, valley to valley, a thought thrown across distance by a million tiny translators who flash it onward faster than the Tender ever could alone.

That is what you saw at the violet hour. *A larger flush of color crosses a whole valley of hollows at once, coordinated, deliberate, far too organized for the small creatures.* You were right that it was too organized. It was a Tender, somewhere out of sight, finishing a sentence — and the whole valley was its voice.

The little colonies are not separate from the Tenders. They are the Tenders' nervous system, externalized, distributed across miles of stone. The packed silent life you found so strange is one half of a single arrangement: the small and quick providing the reach and the relay, the large and slow providing the meaning and the care. Neither is the inhabitant. *Together* they are the inhabitant. The place is not crowded with creatures; it is occupied by one enormous, slow, luminous, distributed mind, breathing below the threshold of your hearing, speaking below the threshold of your patience.

### What they are saying

You will never know, in a single visit. You are a held breath in a conversation lasting ages — your whole stay here is shorter than one of their words.

But stand at the cooling hour and watch the valley deepen from blue to violet and you can feel the shape of it without the content: that it is calm, that it is continuous, that it has been going on far longer than your kind has had language at all and will go on long after. They are not mourning anything. They are not waiting for rescue or fearing an end. The cisterns that seem *recently abandoned* are not abandoned; a Tender simply has not yet come back around to that one, and *recently*, to them, might mean a span in which empires below the haze have risen and worn flat in their turn.

They have everything they need. The dew comes. The warmth comes. The colonies flush and relay. The stone keeps its shape. And across the worn roof of the world, body to body, slow as the settling of mineral, slow as the rounding of mountains into rest, the long unhurried sentence continues — each Tender a syllable, each valley a phrase, the whole plateau a single thought too large and too patient for you to do anything but stand inside it for a moment, and bead with its breath, and leave before it finishes saying *hello*.
