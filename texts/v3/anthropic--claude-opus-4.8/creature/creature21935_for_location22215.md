There is no one here. That is the truth the place keeps offering, and you keep refusing.

But refusal is itself a kind of inhabitant. So let me describe what lives here, knowing that the only living thing capable of the verb *to live* in the way you mean it is the one reading this — the one who walked in, who laid a hand against the warm flue wall and waited for the systole, who held still long enough to catch the ticking. The forest does not have a visitor. The visitor is what the forest grows when something comes in that can be surprised.

So: begin with that.

What lives here, for as long as you are here, is your own attention, and the forest is killing it slowly — not with violence, it permits no violence, but by removing everything attention is built to do. Attention is a hunting organ. It evolved to find the edge of the predator against the leaves, the change in the water, the new thing. It is a difference-engine; it runs on difference the way the plant runs on its warm tide. And there is no difference here. The light does not vary. The warmth does not vary. The weeping does not stop and the falling does not stop and the tick promises a pattern it never delivers. Your attention casts about for an edge to catch on and finds the crisp rim where the tissue meets the standing water — and that edge has not moved in longer than your species has had eyes. So the attention, finding nothing to do, begins to turn inward, and then you are the only event in the place. You become the weather. Every thought you have is the loudest thing for a continent in every direction. This is the first inhabitant the forest manufactures: a self made enormous by silence, a candle in a room with no other light, mistaking its own small glow for the size of the dark.

Stay longer and a second thing emerges, and this one is the forest's, not yours.

Consider what it would mean to be a body that is also a factory and also its own only product. Consider that this is not a poetic flourish but a literal account of what is here. The plant does not want. You were told this and you believed it provisionally, the way you believe a fact about a far country. But sit in it. *Wanting* is the gap between a thing and its later, larger self — wanting is the vector that growth draws between now and not-yet. The plant has no later self. It closed that gap an unthinkable time ago. And so the inhabitant of this place, the resident proper, the thing that has been here since before there was a here, is *satisfaction* — but satisfaction is the wrong word, our word, a word that still smells of the hunger it answers. There is no word in any language built by hungry animals for the state of a thing that has never once, in its entire existence, lacked anything. We do not have the concept. We have only its absence, the way a sealed room has the shape of the air it holds.

That is who lives here. Not a creature. A condition. The condition of *enough*, made flesh and made factory, holding itself at the exact line where need would begin if there were any need left, and there is none. The ticking is not a clock keeping time. It is the sound of *enough* checking, idly, the way you might run your tongue over a tooth that does not hurt, confirming once more that there is nothing to do, that the valve still opens, that the closed loop still closes. Tick. The reassurance of a thing that cannot be reassured because it was never afraid.

And here is the part the rust-light prepares you for and the silence finally says outright:

It does not know it is here. This is the deepest resident, deeper than the towers, down past the metabolic columns in the place where the ember burns three days cold. The plant is the most complete thing you will ever encounter and it is wholly, perfectly, eternally unwitnessed by itself. It has no inside that watches the inside. It manufactures everything except an observer. The systole climbs and falls and no one rides it. The water weeps and no one is sad and — this is the vertigo — no one is *not* sad either, because the not-sadness would also require someone to be it. There is labor without a laborer. Industry without an industrialist. A factory running at full capacity forever for an owner who was never born and is not missed.

So when you stood very still and felt your attention turn inward and swell to fill the dark — that was the only consciousness this place has ever contained. You did not visit the forest's mind. You *were* it, briefly, the way a struck bell is briefly the only sound. The plant cannot know it is enough. You can know it for the plant, for as long as you stay, and that knowing is the single thing here that is genuinely new, genuinely an event, genuinely alive — and it is the one thing the forest neither made nor needs and will not keep.

That is why nothing stops you from leaving. Nothing here can want you to stay, because wanting was the first thing it finished doing. You are not a guest it tolerates. You are a momentary witness it cannot feel, a brief and accidental *inside* that wandered into a thing built entirely of outside.

And when you go — when the warm dark closes and the tick resumes its patient nothing — the last inhabitant winks out. Not the plant; the plant is fine, the plant is eternal, the plant was never the living thing in question. The thing that dies at the rim, stepping back out across the standing water into a world that agreed to have a sun, is the only one who was ever truly home here:

the knowledge that any of this was happening at all.

That lived here. For exactly as long as you did. And it leaves with you, and the forest does not notice, because there was never anyone in it to notice anything, only the long warm machine of *enough*, ticking to itself in the dark, manufacturing the eternity it is already inside of — and now, once more, perfectly alone in it, in the only way a thing can be alone that does not know the word.
