They are called *the dwellers* only by you, only now, because they have never needed a word for themselves. Words are for things that must distinguish one from another, and there is only the one kind here.

Look at them where they cluster in the seams.

They are pale because the plant gives them no reason to be otherwise. Color is a message, and there is no one in here to send messages to except each other, and they have stopped sending. A creature signals its mate, signals a threat, signals ripeness or rot — but in a world with no predator and no scarcity and no season, what is there to say? So they faded. Generation by generation, over the unthinkable span, the pigment drained out of them as surplus. They are the soft translucent white of things that have never required to be seen. You can watch the slow movement of fluid inside them if you bring your eye close. They are honest all the way through. They have nothing to hide because hiding is a behavior that only develops where there is something to hide *from*.

Their bodies are simple and they have not always been. This is the thing you would have to sit with for a long while to feel. Once — far back, in the deep past the room remembers in its floor — they were more. They had more parts. The ancestors of these soft pale threads carried structures whose purposes you could not now guess, because the purposes are gone and the structures went with them. Legs for ground that is no longer crossed the hard way. Sensory organs tuned to changes that no longer come. Defenses against an enemy the room outlived. Each capacity, once it stopped being used, became a cost the body paid for nothing, and the long patient editor of stability struck it out. What remains is exactly as much creature as the conditions require and not one feature more. They are the answer the room arrived at when it solved the problem of *what is the least an animal can be and still be here.*

So they do not hunt. Nothing here flees, so nothing here pursues. They do not compete — there is more secretion on the walls than they could ever consume, because the plant produces it on a cycle indifferent to their hunger, and so they have never had to be quick or clever or cruel to one another. Watch two of them meet on the smooth floor. They do not startle. They do not fight. They do not even particularly acknowledge. They touch, they pass, they continue. They have no concept of *the other one* because there has never been a circumstance in which the other one mattered to their survival. They are not social and they are not solitary. They are simply *many*, in the way that drops of the same water are many.

They feed without urgency. They move without destination — and yet you called their lines across the floor *purposeful*, and you were right to, because purpose at this depth of time stops looking like decision and starts looking like current. They go where the warm sap-channels carry them, where the secretions bead thickest, where the cool draws the moisture they drink. They are not choosing these paths. The paths are choosing them, the way a river chooses the leaf. What you read as intention is just the plant's own circulation expressed in flesh — the engine moving its small passengers around its loops the same way it moves its water and its gas, on the same schedule, to the same end, which is no end at all, which is only continuance.

Here is what they do not do: they do not change.

Not within a life — a single dweller will hatch into the cycling channels, drift and feed and pale through its days, release the next generation into the same channels, and dissolve back into the floor-mass that made it, having altered nothing, having encountered nothing it was not built precisely to encounter. And not across the generations either, not anymore. Evolution is a conversation between a creature and a world that keeps asking new questions. This world stopped asking. The room reached its final shape before these creatures finished becoming themselves, and once the room went still, the question went silent, and the answer — *them* — simply set, like the floor set, like the leaning walls set. They are as finished as everything else in here. They are the fauna a finished place produces: not striving, not adapting, not becoming. Already arrived.

And they cannot leave. You felt this and it is the heaviest fact about them. There is no *out*. The room is not in a forest with more rooms beyond it; the room is the totality of the only conditions their bodies can survive. The temperature that never shifts, the damp that never floods or fails, the secretions that never stop, the deafening engine-drone that has been the only sound in their world since before sound had a name — all of this is not their habitat, it is the *whole of physics* as they have ever known physics to be. A dweller carried out of the room would not find a larger world. It would find that the world had ended, because the world and the room are the same object, and there is no edge to step past, only the place where being-alive stops being possible.

So consider what that has made of them, over the span.

They have no fear, because fear is anticipation of change, and nothing changes. They have no memory worth the name, because memory is for matching the present against a different past, and there is no different past. They have no hope and no dread, those being the two faces of expecting tomorrow to differ from today. They live in a single endless instant of adequate warmth, sufficient food, ordinary light, and that overwhelming mechanical roar — and the roar, to them, is not noise. It is the texture of existence itself. They have never heard silence. They would not have a category for it. The churning of the green factory is, to the dwellers, simply what *here* sounds like, the way pressure is what deep water feels like to the things that live in it and have never risen.

They are not happy. They are not unhappy. Those words belong to creatures who got asked harder questions.

They are the small soft tenants of a finished thing, and like the finished thing, they are not waiting. They have already happened too. When you stand in the center and feel the stillness inside all the noise, the dwellers are part of that stillness — moving the whole time, threading the seams, crossing the floor in their slow currents, more alive by the moment than anything you brought in with you, and yet utterly, perfectly at rest. They are doing the only thing they have ever done, which is the only thing the room has ever done, which is the only thing left to do once becoming is over:

they continue.

And they will be continuing — pale, soft, intent, drowned in the great green drone — long after you have left and the room has, in the only way it ever marks your passage, forgotten the print of your thumb.
