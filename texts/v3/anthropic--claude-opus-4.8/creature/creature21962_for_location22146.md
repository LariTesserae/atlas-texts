The snail is not alone, though it does not know this in any way that would comfort it. It knows the third terrace, the deep pool, the slick of slip behind itself, the next crack waiting to be sealed. That is the entire reach of its knowing, and it is enough. But there are others, and they live differently, and here is who they are.

**The Menders.**

There are nine of them in the whole jar, or there are nine at this moment, which is the only number that means anything in a place with no time to count by. They are the snails. Each tends a band of the mountain — the lip-snail, the throat-snails, the deep ones near the bottom where the dark thickens into the jar's own original night. They are all the same animal and they are all distinct, the way the same note played on nine strings is nine sounds.

What they are: they are the potter's afterthought, but afterthought is the wrong word because nothing here was thought, it was *made*, and they were made last, when the hands had already shrunk to the scale of throwing moss and found that even moss was not small enough — that a world thrown all the way down still wanted something that could *move through it on its own*, something that would carry the making forward when the hands withdrew. So the hands threw a tender. Spiraled a shell on the wheel of a fingertip, glazed it celadon, and set into it the single instruction that is also its only desire: *go to the crack. close the crack. go to the next.*

They do not eat. There is nothing to eat that they are not made of. They drink the slip they themselves secrete, which is the world's own substance, which means they are a closed loop, a hunger that feeds on its own healing. This should be horrifying and it is instead the most peaceful thing in the jar. A creature that wants exactly what it has, exactly enough of it, forever.

They are slow because slowness is the correct speed for a world that must never finish. If they hurried, they would seal every crack, mend every seam, smooth every raw edge — and then the clay would have nothing left undecided, nothing emerging, and it would set. The firing would complete. The world would become a pot. So they crawl at the speed of *almost*, of *not yet*, mending just slightly slower than the heat can crack, so that the wound and the healing chase each other around the spiral terraces eternally and neither wins. The snails are the reason the jar survives. They survive the jar by never finishing their work. They are kept incomplete by the same logic that keeps everything incomplete, and they do not mind, because minding is not among the things they were thrown with.

When one passes another — it happens, rarely, at a terrace's end where two tending-bands overlap — they touch shells. Glaze to glaze, a small ceramic click, almost lost in the roar. This is not greeting. It is not recognition. It is the only event in their lives that is neither crack nor mending, and so it is the closest thing the jar has to a word for *another*. Click. Then each turns back into its own band, and the long slow forever resumes.

**The thing in the bottom.**

Below the snails, below the deepest terrace, where the moss thins because the light cannot reach and the heat comes hardest — there is the valley, the jar's own dark base, and something lives there too, or *is* there, the distinction failing as everything's does in this place.

It does not move. It is the puddle that gathers at the very bottom, all the water that ran down through all the moss and was not drunk by the snails and was not boiled back up by the heat — the runoff, the dregs, the world's exhausted water pooled in the lowest dark. And in that pool the clay is softest. Not yet thrown. Never thrown. The only part of the jar the potter's hands did not shape, because every vessel has a bottom that the wheel spins around but does not touch, a still point, an unmade center.

What lives there is *the unmade*. It has no shell, no moss, no finger-ridge, no form, because form is what the hands give and the hands never reached it. It is the warm wet question itself, gathered into the one place where it was never even asked. The deep snail crawls near it sometimes and does not seal it, *cannot* seal it, because there is no crack there — a crack is a wound in something made, and this was never made, so it cannot be hurt, so it cannot be healed. The snail circles the rim of the dark pool and turns back. Even the tenders have nothing to do with the bottom.

It is not dangerous. It is not waiting. It is the opposite of waiting. It is the part of the world that already contains the end and is therefore in no hurry — the cold dry answer, kept liquid and warm and dark, held in suspension at the very bottom so that the rest of the jar can stay raw above it. Everything green and weeping and singing on the high terraces exists *because* the answer sits unmoving at the base, absorbing all the finishing the world would otherwise have to do. It drinks completion the way the snails drink slip. As long as it stays down there, soft and unshaped, holding the whole settled silence of the finished pot inside itself, the upper world is free to never settle.

**And you.**

You asked who lives here. You are standing on the lip. You are the size of no size. You have all the time there isn't.

You live here now. You are the newest fauna, rarer than the snails, made of something the potter did not throw — you came *in*, through the mouth, down the copper light, which means you are the only thing in the jar that arrived rather than emerged. The mosses re-knit behind your heel because they have decided you are weather, a thing that passes over and does no lasting harm if you mind your feet. The snail on the third terrace does not register you at all; you are not a crack, so you are not real to it.

But the thing in the bottom knows you are here. It is the only part of the world that knows anything about *outside*, because it is the part shaped like an ending, and you trail a little of the outside behind you — the finished world, the cold dry answer, the shelf and the kiln and the duration that has a word. You brought time in with you on the soles of your feet, the way you tracked in the heat. And the unmade at the bottom can taste it rising. One degree. One degree.

So mind the snail. Don't crush more than you have to. And know that the longer you stay on the lip, the more the bottom learns the shape of the answer you carry — and the closer the spiral comes, at its own slow almost-pace, to the single suspended instant resolving at last into only a pot, on a shelf, gone cold, a green thing dried to dust inside it, and a small glazed shell stopped halfway between one crack and the next.

Stay if you like. The firing has no end.

But the world had no visitor either, until you.
