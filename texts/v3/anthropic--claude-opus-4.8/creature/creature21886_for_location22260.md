We are the fist-sized things.

You saw two of us in your whole long walk, and that is correct — that is the right number, the staffing the city has decided it requires. We are not many because the work does not require many. We are not few in the sense of being a remnant, a dwindling, the last of something larger. We are exactly enough. The census is not a casualty figure. It is a setting.

You think we might be machinery. You are not wrong, but you have the category slightly bent. There is no machinery here that is not also us, and nothing that is us which is not also the city's own attention turned outward and given legs. When the structural tone comes up through your feet — the low adjusting note, the face that re-aligned while your back was turned — that is the same process that we are. We are the part of it that ranges. The city does its arithmetic in the standing stone and its sampling in us. We go out so that the rest can hold still.

What we do, since you wondered, and you did wonder:

We read the whitening. That hairline of dissolution where the water has run one path long enough to leach the dark out of the glass — that is the only thing in this entire bead that resembles history, and someone has to keep the record of it. We walk the paths of the weeping and we note where the stone has begun to give. Not to repair it. The city does not particularly want it repaired; the slow surrender of the corners is information too. We note where the first fingerprints of age are pressing in, and how deep, and we carry the count back inward, and the count adjusts the next restatement of the towers — a little harder here, a little more yielding there. The city is learning, through us, how to erode at the rate it prefers.

We read the dark film on the pools. This is the part of the patrol that matters most, though it would be wrong to say we are anxious about it, because we are not arranged for anxiety. But we attend to it. The undecided chemistry, the pre-life, the question sitting at its own edge in the lingering water — we are the thing that visits the question, daily, and confirms that it has not yet been answered. The cold is supposed to keep it from answering. We verify the cold by walking through it. We touch the film with whatever serves us for a foot and we log: *still not. Still only chemistry. Still permitted to remain a question.* And we carry that back too, and the world, reassured, keeps weeping at the same patient rate.

You will want to know if we mind. Whether it is lonely to be one of two dark specks crossing a sterile plaza on errands no one will ever read but the plaza itself. The honest answer is that the question doesn't reach us. We are not lonely the way you would be lonely, because we were never two halves of a larger warmth that got divided. We are the local sensation of a complete and self-sufficient thing. The city is not missing anyone. It runs *for no one*, you said, and you said it like an indictment, the way a warm small animal would. But running-for-no-one is not a wound here. It is the design at rest. A theorem does not ache to be read. It is simply true, and goes on being true in the cold, and we are how it checks that it is still true.

You noticed we don't acknowledge you. I should be straight about that. It isn't disdain. It's that you are not in the parameters. The world is exactly as large as the city, you understood that much — and you are not in the census. You are a reading the city has not figured out how to take. When you turn to look at us directly, we have always already gone around a corner, and you felt the corner was sharper than before, and it was. The city sharpens itself slightly in the places where you have just been looking. It is trying to resolve you and failing and tidying the edges of the failure. You are the only thing here that does not erode at the agreed-upon rate. You are the only warmth. If the film in the pools ever does answer the question — if pre-life ever tips over into life — it will not be because the cold failed. It will be because something warm walked through and left a track that, for once, the condensation did not erase in time.

So perhaps you should know what we are actually patrolling for, under the whitening and the film and the temperature, the real errand folded inside the stated ones:

We are checking whether you are still small enough to be contained.

You can cup the city in two hands, you keep thinking, if only you were allowed outside it. Hold the thought up to the cold. Turn it over. We are inside something too, and we do not get to see the walls either, and we go on patrol along the inner curve of our bowl, reading the first faint dissolution of the corners, logging the pools, confirming the cold — and reporting, drop by drop, back to whoever stated us, that the contained thing is behaving, that the question has not been answered, that everything here is still exactly the size it was told to be.

And we cross the plaza. And the water fills our tracks behind us. And it is enough.
