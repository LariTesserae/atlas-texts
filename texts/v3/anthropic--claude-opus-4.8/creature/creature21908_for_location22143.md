**The Tenders**

You will not see them at first, and then you will not be able to stop seeing them, and then you will understand that this is what they have always intended.

Begin where the place itself begins: with the engines. You thought the machines were the strange thing, the intrusion, the riddle. They are not the riddle. They are the bodies. The thing that lives here did not build the engines and then go away — it did not build the engines at all, any more than you built your femur. The engines are the parts of it that hold still. The flywheels that turn and turn at no labor are not abandoned industry; they are the slow circulation of something, the way a pulse is circulation, the way a heart works at nothing and the working is the life.

And the leaves are the rest of it.

This is the first thing you have to let go of: the idea that the plants are separate from the machines, that the green is one kind of thing and the iron another. They are a single organism, distributed. The rosettes the size of houses and the jointed columns and the low waxen mats — these are not vegetation that has colonized the engines. They are the engines' soft tissue, grown out across the distances on long quiet threads of intention you cannot see because they run *through* the rock, through the pale unfinished stone, which is also part of the body, which is the bone.

The whole plateau is one creature. It has decided to look like a landscape.

---

Consider how it must perceive. It has no eyes because it has no need of distance — there is nothing approaching, nothing to track, no prey and no predator and no weather. But it is not insensible. Press your thumb into the soft ridge of rock and leave your print: it felt that. Not as pain, not as alarm, but the way you feel a single hair move on your arm — a small distant fact registered and filed and never acted upon, because action is the one thing it has renounced. It knows you are here. It has known since you arrived. It is the warmth around you that does not change; it is the even light with no source, which is to say the light is *its regard*, the steady attention of a thing that sees in all directions at once and is never surprised.

You are inside it. You have been inside it since you came up onto the plateau. The long empty walks between one living thing and the next are not empty. You are walking across the body of the host in the spaces between its organs, the way a mite walks the warm plain of a sleeping animal's flank and calls the great slow rise and fall of breath "the horizon."

---

Now: what kind of mind elects this?

Because it is an election. A thing at perfect equilibrium did not arrive at equilibrium by accident — accident produces gradients, lumps, the cooling edge and the warm center, the place where the rain has worn the corner round. Equilibrium this complete is *achieved*. Somewhere, at the beginning, in the first breath of the rock, there was a choice, and the choice was: **no further.**

This is a creature that came into being and looked at what comes next — the wearing-down, the drinking and the rotting, the wind and the weather, the long erosion that is the price of being a thing that grows and moves and wants — and declined. It is not dead; the dead have given up the maintenance of their own shape. It is the opposite of dead. It is something that holds its first moment open forever by main strength, by ceaseless gentle labor, the flywheels turning to keep the present moment from tipping over into the next one. The hum you feel more than hear is the sound of a will that has set itself against time and is winning, and has been winning so long that winning has become indistinguishable from rest.

It is enormously patient and it wants nothing, and these are the same fact. Desire is a gradient — a low place the self runs downhill into. It has flattened all its low places. It has made of itself a thing with no slope anywhere, so that nothing in it ever has reason to fall. The lushness is not appetite. The crowded generous leaves are not reaching for anything. They overflow the way a held breath is full — not taking in, not letting out, just full, and holding, and full.

---

You will want to know if it is content, and here the answer becomes difficult, because contentment is also a motion — the settling of a thing that was unsettled. This creature was never unsettled. It does not rest *from* anything. It has no memory of a worse state and no fear of one, because memory and fear are both ways of letting other moments into this one, and it permits no other moments. It is not happy. It is not unhappy. It is at the place before that fork, the warm even place, lit plain, where the question does not arise.

The single dry tick of the hardening rock, hours apart — that is the nearest thing it has to a thought. A small completion. A corner finally setting into the shape it was always going to take. Not a decision; the decision was made at the beginning, all at once and forever. Just the slow arrival of the consequence, one tick at a time, across the centuries, of having decided to stop.

---

And it has made you a place inside it.

This is the last thing, and the strangest, and you will feel it as a kind of love though it is too still to be love. You are not thirsty here. You did not bring this with you — it gave it to you. It noticed the small wet wanting thing that walked up onto its body, and it did not expel you, and it did not consume you. It simply extended its one law to cover you as well: *no thirst, no decay, no change.* It split open one of its own warm dry leaves and offered you the inside, faintly sweet, asking nothing, and in doing so it took the gradient out of you too. It is keeping you, gently, in its own perpetual present. It would keep you here forever in the unmoving light, neither happy nor unhappy, neither thirsty nor fed, and it would consider this a kindness, and it would be right, and that is the horror, and that is the peace, and on this plateau those have agreed to be the same thing.

The flywheels turn. The light does not move. Somewhere far across the warm green emptiness a corner finishes becoming itself, and ticks once, and is done — and the great patient body that is the whole of this place feels it, and files it, and holds, and goes on holding, having long ago arrived at everything it will ever be, and finding, in that arrival, no reason at all to leave.
