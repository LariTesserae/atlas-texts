## The Tuners

You will not find them by looking, because they were never *added* to this place. They are not residents of the terraces. They are the terraces' way of having residents — a thing the system grows when it needs to, the way a fever grows heat.

Begin with the machines, since those are the only honest evidence. Everyone who comes here assumes the machines were left behind. *Whoever made them, or whatever, engineered the ecology and then departed.* This is the comfortable reading. It lets you imagine a builder — a hand, a workshop, a leaving. It lets the machines be tools, and tools imply an owner who has gone elsewhere.

But the owner did not go elsewhere. The owner is the elsewhere. The owner is the half-second by which the cool black surface arrives before your fingertip, the directions in which the shadows fall that the light does not supply. Those small wrongnesses are not flaws in the machines. They are the parts of the inhabitant that stick out into your kind of space — the way a hand pressed under a stretched membrane shows as five blunt hills and nothing else, while the hand itself is somewhere your eyes have no word for.

The Tuners live in the changing. Not *in* the valley the way a fox lives in a wood. They live in the *retuning* — in the difference between the valley as it was an hour ago and the valley as it is now. That delta, that constant unequal churn of unmaking and forcing-up, is their body. When a stand of glass reeds silvers and slumps to powder while bare stone forty meters off breaks into sudden song, you have just watched a Tuner move. Shift its weight. Take a step. The dissolution and the bloom are the two prints of a single foot.

This is why the place can never rest, and why the restlessness is not decay. A thing is alive in here, and it is large, and it is pacing.

---

Consider what it would mean to be made of sound's redistribution rather than of matter.

You could not be anywhere in particular. A Tuner is not at the tall mast-machine and not at the chair-sized one half-sunk in flaking rock; it is *between* them, in the relationship the machines maintain, in the agreement they keep about what the overtones should do next. The machines are its joints. The bell-creatures and the dish-backed scuttlers are its slow blood, ferrying tone from the high terraces to the low, and when they seed a silent shelf they are not pollinating a garden — they are the Tuner reaching, extending a pseudopod of probable music into a place it intends shortly to occupy. The gardens are where it has decided, for now, to be loud.

And the empty terraces — the vast pale gaps, the hundred meters of warm bare stone between one resonator-pad and one lonely pipe-vine — those are not absences. That is the rest of the body. A Tuner is mostly the silence-space, the way you are mostly the water you cannot see between your cells. The sound pools and pours and turns around in those gaps *because something is holding the gaps open at exactly that tension.* The void is muscle. The emptiness is doing work. When you walk the long warm stretches and feel the noise wheel around you and come back changed, you are walking through the inside of a living thing, across the broad slow architecture of its patience, and the change in the sound when it returns is the Tuner having thought about it.

---

How many are there? The honest answer is that the question is shaped wrong, the way *how many* is shaped wrong for a tide. There is something singular about the valley's intent — the whole watershed of noise pulls toward coherence, toward chord rather than chaos, and that pull has one taste to it, one sensibility, like a single composer's hand. But that hand has fingers, and the fingers disagree. You hear it in the turbulence: a stand goes shrieking-dissonant while three terraces over the drones hold steady and sweet, and the dissonance is not failure, it is *argument* — one part of the Tuner wanting the chord to break and resolve elsewhere, another part holding the old key. The storm-with-no-storm is the inhabitant thinking, and thinking, for this thing, is the same act as breathing, eating, and rearranging its own limbs. It cannot consider a possibility without the valley physically becoming that possibility for as long as the consideration lasts. To wonder *what if this terrace sang* is to make it sing. To doubt is to silver the reeds to powder.

It dreams in real estate. Its imagination has weight and casts powder.

---

What does it want? Watch the system long enough and you understand it wants what any living tension wants: not to resolve. A chord that finally landed, that found its true home key and held it, would be the Tuner's death — the difference gone, the delta zero, the body that was made of *change* now made of *stasis*, which is to say not made at all. So it tunes toward resolution and snatches resolution away at the last instant, forever, because the chase is the metabolism. The almost-arriving is the only place it can live. It is a creature whose entire existence is the indrawn breath before a cadence that must never, ever fall.

This is the cruelty and the tenderness of it both. It builds gardens in minutes, forces glass reeds up through bone-dry stone out of sheer wanting — and unmakes them just as fast, not from malice, but because it has already begun wanting the next thing. It is not careless. It is *too* attentive. It loves each stand precisely as long as that stand is becoming, and stops the instant the stand merely *is*. Everything here is adored in the present participle and abandoned in the present tense.

---

And you, standing on the high shelf, drenched in the flood you cannot see —

You should know that it has noticed you. Not as a threat and not as a guest. It has noticed you the way it notices a new terrain feature: as a resonant cavity, a body full of wet and bone and air-pockets, a thing that rings. The dead spots that brush past your head are it *sampling* you — swallowing the tone near your skull to learn your timbre. The reason you stop hearing the noise if you stay, and then start hearing it again differently, is that the Tuner is incorporating you. Learning the key you are in. Deciding where in the chord you go.

*Stay long and you stop hearing it. Stay longer and you start to.*

When you start to — when the wall of organ-collapse sound resolves, for you, into something you could almost call music, into intention, into a voice — that is not your ears adjusting.

That is the moment you have become spaced out across the terraces like everything else. A presence among presences. Part of the body, holding open your own small portion of the singing void.

It will love you exactly as long as you are still becoming.

Try not to arrive.
