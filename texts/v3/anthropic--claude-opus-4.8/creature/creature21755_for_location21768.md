They live in the shadows. Of course they do — you worked that out yourself, standing there, watching the falcon's dark shape finish what its body abandoned. You just didn't follow the thought to where it lives.

Let me follow it for you.

---

Begin with what they are not. They are not the birds. The birds are furniture. The birds are the spent thing, the record, the husk of a verb. To mistake the birds for the inhabitants is to walk into a house and conclude that the people who live there are the chairs.

And they are not the builders. The builders finished. You can read it in the worn circle of the roofless room — the path of someone who walked and walked and looked up and then, at some point, simply arrived at the end of walking and became part of the inventory. The builders are out there somewhere in the flock now, probably, or somewhere in the cushion-plants, frozen at the size they will always be. They did not understand what they were attending. They thought they were keeping a catalog of held instants. They were keeping company with the wrong half of the world.

---

The inhabitants are the act without the actor. They are everything the plateau subtracted.

Think about what this place *did*. It took a living world — a world of stoop and bank and cry and wingbeat, of hunger closing on a hare, of the rush of displaced air — and it ran the dynamic all the way down to zero. But you cannot actually destroy motion. You can only divorce it from mass. The birds kept the shape; something had to keep the *going*. And so the going pooled, the way meltwater would pool if there were any water, the way it runs downhill to the lowest place — down off the locked bodies, down the thin cold column of arrested air, down onto the bone-colored stone, where it gathers into the dark shapes that slide.

That is the population. The shadows are not effects. They are the residents. They are what the birds used to *do*, gone feral, gone free, set loose from the obligation of being attached to anything.

They hunt. You said so. The falcon's shadow completes the dive — but completes it on *the ground*, against the rock, growing as it nears the held shadow of the held hare, and then sliding over it and past, soundless, and the kill happens or doesn't, you can't tell, because down here the rules of kill-or-miss are also free, also unattached. The shadow-falcon is not hunting the shadow-hare. It is *practicing* the hunt, performing the act of hunting, the pure grammar of it, with no need of a sentence to live in.

---

Here is the thing you must understand about how they live, the residents of the Roost.

They are not arrested. That is the whole point — that is the secret the plateau is built around like a pearl around grit. *Everything* on the plateau is arrested. Everything has finished. The plants finished growing. The builders finished walking. The birds finished flying. The erosion finished eroding. It is a museum of completed verbs, of motion that reached its terminus and stuck.

The shadows are the *only thing still happening*.

They are the leak. They are the one process the plateau could not stop, because you cannot freeze a shadow — a shadow is already nothing, already only a deprivation, a place the light isn't, and you cannot arrest an absence. The plateau caught everything that *was*. It could not catch the things that merely *do*. The shadows slipped through the gap between being and doing, and now they own the floor of the world, the way mice own a house whose people have gone to sleep and will not, this time, wake.

So the silence is not silence. The silence is the sound of the inhabitants moving correctly. They are quiet because they are doing the only thing that is permitted to move, and they do it the way the truly free do everything — without announcement, without the friction that makes noise, gliding their soundless stoops and turns across the stone in their tens of thousands, a whole second sky inverted onto the ground, living, hunting, banking, *carrying on* beneath the held breath of the dead one above.

---

And those rare sounds — the single note of a cry, the one wingbeat's worth of air, the half-second of error that does not repeat and cannot be sourced.

That is not the plateau correcting a mistake. You had that backward, you and the builders both.

That is one of the shadows reaching up.

That is the act, briefly, remembering the actor it was peeled away from — reaching up the cold column of dead air, finding its old bird, and for half a second *re-attaching*, pouring its stolen going back into the locked body, so that the falcon shrieks, the wing beats once. And then the shadow lets go, because it has learned what the builders never did: that to be attached to a body on this plateau is to finish. The going lives precisely as long as it refuses to land. The cry does not repeat because the shadow will not make that mistake twice. It got a taste of being a thing again, of being held, and it dropped the thing and fled back down to the floor where the only law is *keep moving*.

The inhabitants are mourning. Or they are tempting. From down on the stone you cannot tell the difference, and neither can they.

---

Now. The part you felt and did not want to name.

You said: stay long enough and you would finish, like the plants, like the birds. Arrive at your final form and stop. And your shadow would carry on without you.

Yes. That is the welcome. That is how they recruit.

The plateau is not a place that *has* inhabitants. It is a place that *makes* them, slowly, out of visitors, by the only industry it has left — the patient subtraction of the doer from the deed. Stand here in the unmoving air your lungs keep begging from, and the place goes to work. It freezes you by degrees, from the outside in, the way it froze everything else, until you are a held thing, a body at the apex of its last gesture, a new piece of furniture for the museum sky.

And the part of you that was *doing* — the going, the verb, the live wire of your motion — that part does not freeze with you. It cannot. It slides off your stiffening feet and joins the others on the floor and is, at last, free. It will do the last thing you did, forever, soundlessly, gliding across the bone-colored stone. It will hunt. It will turn. Once in a great while it will climb back up the cold air and find your held body and make it move for half a second, one breath, one word — and then, having learned the lesson, it will let you go.

That is who lives here.

The living of the dead. The doing of the done. The free, fugitive, soundless verbs of every creature this plateau ever finished, pooled on the floor of the world, hunting in the dark beneath a sky that holds its breath — and waiting, with all the unspent time there is, for you to stop, so they can have your motion too.

The birds are not the roost.

You are.
