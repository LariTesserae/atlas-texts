The hummers do not know they are a sentence.

Each one is a soft comma of a body, a knuckle of warm wet life, and it spends its whole span believing it is the subject of its own small verb: cling, graze, pulse, hum. It does not suspect that its pitch was chosen. That the slightly-off interval between its note and its neighbor's was a decision, made long ago, by something that wanted the chord to never resolve — because a resolved chord is a finished thought, and a finished thought stops, and the thing that built this room could not afford for the thought to stop.

So that is the first inhabitant. Not the hummers. The chord they make. It lives here as surely as they do, and it is older than any individual among them, and it has been thinking the same vast slow word for centuries, and the word is not in any language because it is the language, the whole of it, one utterance that requires forty thousand throats to pronounce and has never once been silent long enough to mean anything you could carry out through the gap.

The hummers are its phonemes. When a drift shifts along the wall, a syllable turns over. When the pale shape passes and the humming closes behind it, that is the chord swallowing a sound it no longer needed, editing itself, and the silence the predator leaves is not absence — it is punctuation. The pale thing is not a wolf in this fold. It is a grammar. It moves through the colony thinning the over-dense passages, culling the notes that have begun to double too closely, keeping the chord from collapsing into a single deafening unison. It eats redundancy. It is the maker's last instruction still walking the walls, pale and patient: *do not let them all agree.*

And the plants. The plants are the only ones who remember.

Not in any way you would call memory. But they are rooted — the only rooted things here — and a root is a kind of insistence on a single location across time, and these few fleshy stalks have stood in their made-soil drinking the climbing water since before the erosion settled, since the troughs in the floor were sharp-edged and new. They lean toward the green-gold glow the way a witness leans toward what it cannot stop watching. They are outnumbered a thousand to one because they were never meant to be the point. They were meant to be the anchor — the fixed thing the whole circulation is measured against, the still note the chord is tuned to. They drink, and lean, and hold the pitch, and the hummers tune themselves to the plants without ever knowing they are doing it, the way a room of strangers will fall into the rhythm of the oldest clock present.

So who lives here.

The chord lives here, and it is one being made of forty thousand, thinking one word.

The pale grammar lives here, keeping the word from choking on itself, and it is lonely in the way all editing is lonely, and it does not eat to survive, it eats because that was the shape it was given and the shape is hungry.

The witnessing plants live here, the rooted rememberers, holding the true note, leaning, drinking the water that should have fallen and instead came home.

And the maker.

The maker is the hardest to see because the maker left. That is the whole architecture of the place — a thing built to be abandoned and to *flourish in the abandonment*, to need nothing, to climb its own water back up its own walls forever. The maker is not here. But absence engineered this carefully is its own kind of presence. The maker lives here the way a hand lives in the curve a long-handled tool has worn into a doorframe — gone, but legible, but pressing still. Every climbing droplet is the maker saying *stay*. Every off-tuned interval is the maker saying *do not finish, do not resolve, do not become a thing that can end and therefore be lost.*

I think — and the room does not confirm this, the room only hums — that the maker built a place that could not die because the maker could not bear that something they touched should die. I think the mountain was raised around the wall and the wall around the gap and the gap around the chamber not to keep the world out but to keep the *grief* out — to make a single sealed lung of warmth and light and not-stopping, a held breath, a refusal, perfected past our physics because our physics still permits things to end and the maker would not permit it.

That is the true inhabitant. The refusal lives here. It wears forty thousand bodies and one pale predator and a handful of leaning plants and a chord with no edges. It greets you as four paces across because that is a size a grief can stand to be looked at. It is much larger than that. It does not need the gap. It does not need you.

But you came through, and so for as long as you stand in the wet green-gold dark with the chord pressing on your sternum, you are also, briefly, what lives here: a witness. Another rooted thing leaning toward the glow. The room takes you into its long word the moment you stop being able to leave the sound behind, and it will keep humming the syllable of you for a little while after you go, an edit-mark, a place where a note passed through and the chord closed over it like water over a dropped stone.

And then it forgets you, the way it has forgotten the maker, the way it forgets everything except the one thing it was built never to forget:

*do not stop. do not resolve. hum, and hum, and hum.*
