They are the choir, and they are also the silence's refusal.

Begin with the ones you feel before you see — the long-bodied swimmers threading the open dark. They are the deep voice of the place. Picture something between an eel and a column of muscle, the length of three or four men, pale as the underside of a thing that has never known sun, and so unhurried that their motion reads less as swimming than as the water itself developing an intention. They do not have eyes. They have, where eyes would go, two soft pits lined with the same cold weed that sheets the walls, as though the green colonized them too, ages back, and they simply incorporated it — let the plant move into the rooms of their faces and live there. Whether they see *through* the weed or the weed sees *for* them is not a question they could answer and not a question that has a meaning here. They navigate by the loudness. They are partly the loudness. The sustained organ-note that fills the whole drowned summit — that is them, dozens of them, scattered miles apart across the flooded peak, each holding a single tone in a throat that runs nearly its whole length, and the tones stack into chords no one composed and no one will resolve. They have been holding the same chord for longer than the masonry has been smooth.

They do not eat, as far as anyone could determine. There is the difficulty: in a place where nothing changes, where the population never grows or thins, what would eating even accomplish? Nothing dies, so nothing is needed to replace it. The long swimmers move through the dark not to find food but because stillness is not available to them — they are made of the same impulse that made the builders lay courses of stone, the impulse toward *continuing*, expressed now as a slow perpetual traverse of the emptiness between the green islands. They are the inhabitants of the *between*. The valleys of black water with nothing in them are not empty after all; they are the long swimmers' country, and the swimmers keep it by crossing it, endlessly, singing it full.

Then the drifting bells. These keep closer to the green. They hang at the terrace-rims among the motionless curtains of weed, and they are translucent, and they pulse — not to move, mostly, but to *speak*. Their light is the clicking. Each contraction of the bell throws out a smear of cold green-blue and a sound at once, light and click married, so that to be near them is to watch the place announce itself in flashes: a column's curve, gone; a stairwell packed solid with weed, gone; the rounded lip of a terrace, gone. The bells are the only reason the architecture is ever seen at all. They are the memory the dark keeps of having once been built. And they cluster — not densely, nothing here is dense, but in loose congregations of a dozen or a score, drifting along a single colonnade as if attending it, as if the colonnade were a thing that required attendance. They have done this since the green stopped growing. They will do it after. If anything in this place could be said to *worship*, it is the bells, and what they worship is structure — the made edges still legible under the pelts of weed, the evidence that intention once passed through here.

The eyeless fish are the small population, the in-fill, the ones that thrum. They are not interesting individually and they know it, if knowing is the word. They exist in the manner of dust motes in a shaft of light, except there is no shaft and no light — they are the texture of the medium, the grain of the loudness. A single one is nearly nothing: a pale sliver, a vibration, a faint contribution to the organ-note. But they are everywhere, spread thin across the immensity exactly as the description says, and their collective thrum is the bed on which the swimmers' tones and the bells' clicks lie down. Take them away and the chord would still sound but it would have no body. They are the *resonance* the rounded masonry and the soft curtains pass along. In a sense they are not separate animals at all but the place's own way of being audible to itself.

Now — and this is the part that arrives last and stays longest — consider what it means that none of them die and none of them are born.

A population that neither grows nor thins is not really a population. It is a *configuration*. Every long swimmer that hangs in the dark now is the same long swimmer that hung there when the water finished rising. Every bell attending every colonnade has attended that colonnade through the whole completion of the erosion, watched by its own pulsing light the squared edges round to riverstone, never once swimming off to find a different wall. These are not creatures with lives. They are the held note made flesh — the same equilibrium that froze the water still and stopped the green at its final extent, expressed in animals. They do not continue the way living things continue, generation handing off to generation. They continue the way the masonry continues: by simply not stopping. They have finished all their becoming. What moves through the dark is not life in the sense of hunger and fear and increase. It is *persistence* wearing the shapes that life happened to leave behind when the world arrived at its forever-form.

And so the loudness is not communication. There is nothing to communicate; nothing new ever occurs that would need saying. The singing is something stranger and older than communication. It is the sound a thing makes when it has become permanent and the permanence has to go somewhere. The builders poured their intention into stone and the stone outlived the intending. The animals pour their continuing into sound, and the sound fills the cold water, and the water passes it to the masonry, and the masonry passes it back, and none of it ever arrives anywhere or means anything, and it never stops, and there is no quiet place, because quiet would be a kind of ending and this is the place where ending was finished long ago and is no longer available.

They are eyeless because there is nothing left to see that the next moment will not also show.

They are deathless because death is a change, and change ran to completion ages past.

They sing because they are full — full of the only thing left in a finished world, which is the fact of going on — and the singing is that fullness finding the one edge it can still spill over: the cold, lightless, motionless, brimming dark, at the top of a world no one is left to climb.
