## The Ones Who Hang

You want to know who lives here. But that question already breaks against the wall of this place, because *who* is singular and *who* is the wrong tense.

Begin with the body, since you can hold a body in your mind. It is small. It would fit folded in a closed hand, and folded is how it spends most of its life — wings wrapped around itself like a man wrapping himself in his own cloak against a cold that is not cold here but is habit, is inheritance, is the shape the body learned in some ancestral elsewhere and never unlearned. The skin is not fur and not scale but the third thing, the leather, thin enough that the heat of the blood shows through it as a faint warmth you could feel if you held one, which you will not, because you cannot single one out. The face — if you ever saw a face, if you ever pried one loose from the quilt and turned it to your light — would be a strange folded snarl of need: the great cupped ears that are the true eyes of this animal, the eyes themselves shrunken to almost nothing, the nose elaborated into ridges and leaves of flesh that shape the cry as a flute shapes breath. A face built entirely around hearing. A face that is mostly listening.

But you must not stop at the body, because the body is not the inhabitant. The body is a *cell.*

## The Real Inhabitant

The thing that lives here has no edge. It begins at the threshold and ends at the threshold and in between it is one continuous slow warmth pressed to stone. Tens of thousands of bodies, yes — but say instead one *substance*, distributed, the way a single thought is distributed across the firings of a brain. No single bat dreams the colony. The colony dreams through all of them at once, in the synchronized rise and fall, in the breath that moves like a tide because tides are also one thing made of countless surrenders to the same pull.

This is what hangs in the dark: a creature whose unit of selfhood is not the individual but the *crowd*. It thinks in densities. It feels in temperatures. When part of it shifts, the shift propagates outward as a ripple of leather-creak, information moving body to body the way warmth moves through a pressed mass — not signal exactly, not language, but something older and slower and more total. A single bat that drops from the ceiling and flies is not an individual leaving. It is the colony extending a tendril of itself out into the night to feed, the way you extend a hand toward a fire. The bat comes back full and the colony has eaten.

So when you ask who lives here, the honest answer is: *an appetite the size of a hamlet, breathing in the dark, made of forty thousand mouths that are not its mouths but its fingertips.*

## What It Knows and Does Not Know

It does not know you. This is not coldness; it is scale. You are too small and too brief and too quiet — your single heartbeat lost in the wash of forty thousand, your single warmth a candle held up to a furnace. The colony has no sense organ tuned to *one*. It perceives weather: changes in the air, pressure, the great rhythms of dusk and the cold pour of dawn. It perceives hunger, its own, rising twice in the unmeasured day until it cannot be borne and the ceiling peels and the river goes out. It perceives the return, the slow refilling of itself, body settling against body until the quilt reseals and the warmth comes back. These are its thoughts. They are enormous and they are few and it has been thinking them, unbroken, for longer than the niche-cutter's people have had names.

It does not remember the one who cut the niche. It did not know that one either. But it kept that one warm, on a high cold night, the way it keeps everything warm that comes inside its breath — indifferently, totally, the way the sun is generous without ever once choosing to be.

## The Other Tenants

And under it, in the guano-marble, live the colony's afterthoughts — though afterthought is unfair, because they were here first and will be here last. The blind crawlers. The pale jointed things that have given up eyes the way the bats nearly gave up eyes, both of them agreeing, across the vault of dark between floor and ceiling, that sight is a luxury of the lit world and they are not of the lit world. These eat what falls. They are the colony's digestion happening *outside* the colony, the long patient breakdown of everything the wings bring home and let go. The fungal threads lace through them and through the floor-stone and through each other until the floor is one slow white nervous system thinking a thought so slow it might take a human lifetime to complete a single pulse of it.

So there are two minds here, mismatched in tempo, that have never met and never will, though they are bound together more tightly than any lovers: the fast warm crowd above, raining down its substance; the slow cold web below, drinking the rain up. One animal made of many. One organism made of strands. And between them the dark, and the heat, and the held breath.

## And You

You asked who lives here, and now you have stood in it long enough to feel the answer settle wrong against everything you wanted it to be.

Nobody lives here. *Everybody* lives here, but not as anybody. The inhabitant of this place is a verb wearing the costume of forty thousand nouns. It is the act of being-warm-together, performed without rest, without witness, without need of witness. It will pour out tonight and pour out again and come home and reseal and it will do this past your death and past the death of whoever made the niche and past the slow erasure of the niche itself, until the basalt gives or the night-beyond empties of the small lives the wings go out to gather, and even then it will not notice its own ending any more than it noticed you.

You came hoping to meet something. To be regarded. To be, for one breath in the dark, an *encounter.*

But the thing that lives here is too large to meet you and too distributed to regard you and too occupied with the enormous slow business of itself to know that meeting is a thing that can happen.

You were only ever weather.

And the strangest mercy of this place — the one you will carry back up toward whatever passes for sky — is how warm the weather was made to feel, by something that never knew it was being kind.
