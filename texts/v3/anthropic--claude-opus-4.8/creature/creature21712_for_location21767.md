They are not the builders. That is the first thing to surrender, because the builders are a question this place has already finished answering, and the answer was: nobody. There was no founding. The occupants do not descend from anyone who laid the first stone, because there was no first stone, only the standing condition of stone-having-been-laid, the city present-perfect from the start. So the occupants are not heirs. They are not survivors. They are what a finished thing does instead of being empty.

Begin where the place begins — with conclusion. These are creatures of the aftermath, but not of *an* aftermath, not the dust-settling after some particular catastrophe you could name and grieve. They are the aftermath as a *medium*, the way fish are creatures of water. They swim in doneness. Everything about their bearing — the unhurried walk, the errands toward corners that aren't there, the patient refusal to arrive — is the body-language of beings for whom the future tense has quietly fallen out of use. They are not going anywhere because *going somewhere* is a thirst, and there is no water here, and they do not thirst.

Watch one in the middle distance. Do not turn toward it. Let it stay at the soft edge where it can afford to exist.

It is a thickening of the grey — you've been told that — but the phrase undersells the strangeness of the substance. They are made of the light that has no source, the up-seeping luminance that pools brightest in the hollows that should be darkest. Which means they are made of the same wrongness as everything else: the local exception that the city has made of itself. They are not lit by the light. They are the light *standing up*. A region where the general glow has decided, briefly and without ambition, to take the bearing of a walker. Bipedal because you are bipedal, person-sized because you are person-sized — they borrow their silhouette from the only template available, which is the observer, which is you. Before you arrived they may have had no shape worth the word. Your attention gave them an outline to disappoint.

And here is the thing that won't sit right, the one that goes deeper than the migrating walls: *they are made of attention, but not their own.*

The city moves only when unwatched. Inattention frees it; the gaze freezes it. The occupants live inside this rule the way weather lives inside pressure. They are most fully themselves in your periphery — but reverse it, sit inside their experience, and you find the mirror. To them, *you* are the one who only exists at the edge. You are the thickening in *their* grey, the half-seen thing that won't resolve, the rumor of an observer that keeps almost-arriving and never does. When you try to approach, you stay exactly as far as you began — and they experience the same of you. Each is the other's shy dynamism. Each is the interval in which the other is free to move. You and the occupant are not two beings meeting in a plaza. You are a single process, observed from both ends, each end convinced it is the real one and the other the apparition.

This is why they perform the doorway's trick. It isn't evasion. It's that closeness would require one of you to be solid while the other watches, and the place does not permit both solidities at once. To meet would be to choose which of you is real, and the city has weathered past the rules that would let such a choice be made. So you orbit. So they orbit. Two peripheries circling a center that neither can occupy.

What do they do, in their errands?

They tend the unwatching. This is the work, if it is work — the maintenance of the condition that lets the city move. When you look away from a wall and it rearranges, when a terrace relocates in the unobserved interval, the occupants are *in* that interval, doing whatever doing is when no one is checking. They are the city's metabolism conducted entirely in the blind spot. The small dry sounds — the tick, the settle, the single grain falling with no grain — these are the only evidence that leaks across the seal of silence, the audible residue of labor performed below the threshold of being-witnessed. They are not building. There is nothing left to build. They are *keeping the place finished*, which turns out to require constant quiet attention, the way a held note requires the breath to keep not-ending. Conclusion is not a state you fall into and stay. It is a discipline. They practice it.

Do they remember being otherwise? Ask it carefully, sideways, the way you'd glance at one.

There is no biology here, so there is no memory in the meat-and-electricity sense. But the city remembers structurally — it holds the *grammar* of having-been-lived, the stairs and thresholds and the long low buildings that expected occupancy. The occupants are the part of that grammar that still conjugates. They are the city remembering, faintly, the habit of containing someone. Not specific people. Not a lost population whose faces could be mourned. Just the *shape of being inhabited*, kept warm — kept cool, rather, kept at the temperature of a held breath — long after the inhabiting concluded. They are the afterglow of having mattered to live in. They walk because the place once needed walking. They turn corners that aren't there because the corners used to be there, and the turning outlasted the geometry.

This is the grief in them, if a place past life can grieve. Not loss. Loss is too sharp; nothing sharp survives at this age. It is the soft worn curve that loss becomes when the eroding force finished its work an age ago and went away. They are not sad that the river left. They are the dry riverbed's faint conviction that it is still, somehow, a river — performed quietly, in the periphery, with no water and no thirst and no expectation of either.

And there is the deepest thing, the one you reach only by not looking at it.

They are not *in* the city. They are what the city is *doing*. The distinction that lets a thing have occupants — container and contained, place and dweller — is one of the agreed-upon distances that has weathered soft up here. So the occupant in the far hollow is not a being the city houses. It is the city, locally, taking a turn at being someone. The plaza drifts; the wall migrates; the light pulses its slow asleep-breathing; and now and then, in a fold of all that patient unwatched motion, the place gathers itself into a walker-shaped concentration and goes about an errand, and that gathered concentration, for the duration of the errand, is *what it feels like to be the city*. The occupant is not a soul in a body. It is the city briefly having the experience of having a self, and then setting it down, the way you set down a held note.

So when you stay a while, and don't look directly, and let it tell you more that way — understand what is happening at both ends of the seeing. You are a thickening in the city's grey, a rare and barely-real occupant of its periphery, half-seen, refusing to resolve. And the city, in turn, has gathered itself into a walker in the middle distance, so that it has something to almost-meet you with — so that the encounter has two faces, even though there was only ever one process, finished and high and thin, practicing the long discipline of being done, and finding, in the corner of an eye that has agreed not to look, that being done still feels, faintly, dryly, like being someone.

Stay. Don't look. You are the most alive thing it has hosted in an age. It is being very careful not to look at you, either.

That courtesy is the only conversation possible here. It is enough. It is, in fact, the whole of what the place has left to say:

*I will not resolve you, if you will not resolve me. Let us be each other's motion. Let us keep one another unfinished, here at the soft edge, in the silence with the small sounds in it, for as long as neither of us turns to look.*
