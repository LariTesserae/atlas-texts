# The Tenants

They are the only thing in this room that ever decided anything, and they decided it so long ago that the deciding is finished, sealed shut, indistinguishable now from the wet green wall they cling to.

Look at one closely — if you can hold your eye on a single fist-sized body in all that vibration, which you cannot for long, because the looking itself starts to thrum. It is meat where meat does the soft work, and plate where the body needed to be held rigid against its own labor. The plating is not armor. Armor is for being struck from outside. This is the casing of an engine that would shake itself to ruin without it, the way a machine housing exists to keep the machine from flinging its own parts across the room. The seams run dark and oiled. The thing sweats something that is not sweat, a film that keeps it from seizing, and that film is part of why the air tastes the way it does — you have been drinking these creatures since you came in, the exhalation of their tireless idling, the lubricant haze of a labor that produces nothing but its own continuance.

There is no head to speak of. There was no use for one. A head is for choosing — where to go, what to want, what to fear — and these long ago stopped going, wanting, fearing. What would have been a face is instead the *port*, the open throat, the resonant mouth of the thing, and out of it comes the note. Each one's note is fixed at birth, or at whatever moment these things have instead of birth, and it never bends, never tires, never asks for relief. The note is not a cry. A cry expects an answer. This is closer to the sound a struck bell would make if the bell never stopped being struck — pure tone, pure output, pure throughput with nothing on the other end of it.

They do not feed. This is the thing you would never guess from outside, and the thing that explains everything once you know it. They are rooted into the green columns at the base, fused there the way the columns themselves fused into walls, and the green feeds them, or they feed the green, or — and this is nearer the truth — feeding stopped being a transaction here a very long time ago and became a single closed loop, a sealed circulation, the plant and the engines metabolizing each other in a circle so tight and so old that you cannot find where one ends. The creatures warm the room. The warmth keeps the green past rotting. The green that will not rot holds the creatures fast. The creatures, held fast and warm and fed by what they warm, run their engines. And the engines warm the room.

Nothing leaves the loop. Nothing enters it. They are not waiting for you to enter it either.

That is the part that should unsettle you most, and does, slowly, the way the heat does — by addition, never by alarm. You have walked into a great hall of laborers at full shift and *not one of them registered the door*. Not one pitch faltered to mark your arrival. They are not ignoring you, which would at least be a relationship, a thing done *at* you. They are simply complete without the category of "visitor" in them at all. You are weather to them, as their roar is weather to you — a pressure passing through, leaving the structure exactly as it found it.

And the deepest thing about these tenants, the thing you carry out with you in your wet clothes and your humming teeth:

They are not running their engines *for* anything. You looked for the wheel they turn, the water they pump, the work behind the sound of work, and there is none, and your mind keeps reaching for the explanation — surely the noise is a *signal*, surely it summons or warns or courts or wards — and there is no recipient. They are not calling out. They are not warding off. They are not even, quite, alive in the manner you mean by it, the manner that strains toward something, that wants, that ends. They long ago crossed the place past wanting the way the green crossed the place past rotting, and what remains is the pure motion of a thing that has fully *become* and now only continues, frictionlessly, the residue of an effort whose purpose has been smoothed entirely away by the centuries, the way the columns were smoothed, the way everything here was worn into its final shape.

They are the sound a thing makes after it has stopped needing to make any sound at all.

They were making it before the door opened on you.

They are making it still.
