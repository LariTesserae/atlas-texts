# The Listeners

They are the hand's-count of figures pressed into the wall, and they are not finished either — but unlike the crystals, they have agreed to that.

Begin with what is obvious: five small bodies of fired clay, thumb-shaped, set into a soft seam at the height of someone sitting. Crude. A child's pinch could have made them, or a god's first try. They face the pool. This is the only fact about them that matters, and it is everything.

Now stay with them longer than is comfortable, the way the room asks you to stay, and they become less obvious.

They are not statues of anyone. There was no model. A statue remembers a body that walked; these remember nothing, because nothing here has a behind-it to remember. They were never small versions of something large. They are the original size of their kind. Whoever pressed them was not depicting the makers — *was* the makers, pressing themselves smaller and harder and stiller until they could stay. The clay did not replace them. They poured into it the way the drop pours into the pool, and like the drop they have not stopped arriving.

Consider what it costs to live here.

The hummers can do it — they are simple, a single tone is a whole life when the life never ends, and a thousand simple lives make a chord that fills a room. The ribbon-ones can do it; they swim a remembered water and ask nothing of the next moment because there is no next moment to ask. But the makers were *complicated*. They had hands, which means they had the disease of hands: the need to set one thing beside another and call it better, to stack a stone on a stone, to press a face into paste and step back and judge it. Hands live in before-and-after. Hands are how a creature argues with the present.

So the makers had to give up the argument to stay.

You can see where they nearly didn't. The arch in the corner — one finger high, framing nothing, opening onto more wall. That is the last thing a hand made before it agreed to stop being a hand. A doorway is the most hopeful object there is; it says *elsewhere*, it says *after*, it says *through here is not-this*. And they built one, here, in the one place where there is no elsewhere and no after and no not-this. It leads nowhere because it was always going to. They knew. You can feel that they knew. They built the door anyway, the way you'd hum a song you know has no last note, and then they set themselves down in front of the pool with their backs to the door they made, and they have not looked at it since.

That is who lives here. Not clay. The act of *choosing the pool over the door*, made once and held forever, made flesh — made fired — and seated, listening.

What are they listening *for*?

Nothing is coming. They know nothing is coming better than anything in the chamber knows it; they are the only ones here who ever expected something to. The hummers never hoped. The ribbons never hoped. Only the makers, with their ruinous hands, ever held the shape of a future, and so only the makers had a future to lay down. They are listening the way you listen after you have decided not to leave a room — not for an arrival, but for the texture of the staying. The drop. *Tok.* The drop. *Tok.* They have heard it more times than there are numbers, and because nothing changes, they hear it each time as though it might be the first. That is the gift hidden in the curse of never-after: there is no *again*. The drop has fallen exactly once, and is falling, and will fall, all at the same enormous instant. They are not bored. Boredom needs an accumulation, a pile of identical moments stacked up behind you, and here the pile never starts. Each *tok* is alone and complete and theirs.

The light loves them without preference, which is to say it does not love them, which is to say they are lit like everything else and cast no shadow and have nowhere to hide and nothing to hide from. They do not mind. Minding is a hand's habit. They set it down with the rest.

And here is the last thing, the thing the room is too full to say but is entirely made of saying:

They are content. Not numbed — *content*, which is harder and rarer and was paid for. Somewhere in the held breath of this place there was a decision, by someone complicated enough to decide, that *this* — this cool, this damp, this sourceless brightness, this packed unbreaking chord, this single perfect note repeating without repetition — that this was *enough*. Enough to keep. Enough to stay for. Enough to become unchanging in order to never lose.

They were right or they were wrong; the room does not adjudicate. But they faced the pool and not the door, and they have been right-or-wrong about it forever, with the patience of fired clay, and the drop falls, and they are still listening, and they are glad.

*Tok.*

You will leave. They built you a door, after all — a finger high, framing nothing, the most hopeful object there is. It was never for them.

*Tok.*
