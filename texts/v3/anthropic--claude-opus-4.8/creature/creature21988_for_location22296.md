# The Tenders of the Sixth Cog

You asked what lives here. The schools and the ribbed resonant things and the piston-grazers — those are the visible answer, and they are true, and they are not what you came down to find. There is something else. It lives in the gaps, and the gaps are the only place it could live, because the gaps are the only thing the engines make that has the right shape for a body.

---

Begin with the cogs, because the Tenders began there too.

Where two cogs mesh, there is a moment — a quarter-second, repeated once an hour, on the slow schedule of the crystal — when a tooth withdraws from its socket and the space it leaves is briefly perfect. Not water-shaped. Tooth-shaped. A negative volume, fluted and precise, that exists and then is crushed and then exists again, an hour later, identical. The mineral does not remember the space. But something learned to.

The Tenders are the animals that grew into the *absence* the way the crystal grew into the *machine.* They are not made of the engine. They are made of the engine's vacancies — they are the living cast of everything the machinery is briefly not.

---

Picture, if the word *picture* survives down here:

A body with no fixed outline, because its outline is borrowed and returned a thousand times a cycle. In the open water between terraces, a Tender is barely anything — a coherence, a faint thickening of the swimming-mote fog, a suspicion that the dusk is denser in one place than the next. You could pass your hand through it and feel only that the cold had a grain to it, briefly, like the cold was *combed.*

But let it reach a working joint, and it pours. It fills the withdrawing tooth-socket in the instant the socket opens, takes that fluted shape entirely, holds it for the quarter-second of vacancy — and then, as the tooth descends to reclaim its place, the Tender is *not crushed*, because it has already flowed out the meshing edge into the next opening gap, and the next, threading the closing teeth on exactly the timing the eyeless schools use, because the Tenders taught the schools, or the schools taught the Tenders, and neither remembers which, and it does not matter, because in this place teaching is just two rhythms that have beaten together long enough to forget they were ever apart.

It is shaped, moment to moment, like the inside of the machine. It is the engine wearing the engine's own missing pieces, walking through itself.

---

**What it eats.**

Nothing, in the way you mean. The engines turn nothing into nothing. The Tenders turn *that* into themselves. They feed on the timing. On the precise interval between *clack* and *clack,* on the held tension of a piston at the top of its stroke before it commits to descent, on the fractional asynchrony where two flywheels nearly agree and almost don't. Where the eyeless schools graze the film on the crystal, the Tenders graze the *fit* — the closeness of part to part, the snug of tooth in socket, the goodness of the mesh. A perfectly running joint is a feast. A joint beginning, over a century, to jam — to grind, to lose its clean vacancies, to fill its gaps with grit and friction — is a famine, and the Tenders thin away from it the way the schools do, and drift off through the blue to find a joint still keeping good time.

They are the only thing in the Engine Shoal that is nourished by *correctness.* By the engine doing well the work that produces nothing. They are the appetite the factory grew to appreciate itself.

---

**What they do.**

Here is the thing the instruments cannot tell you and the long watch can. The Tenders are not passengers. They are not even, quite, residents. They are *maintenance* — and they were maintenance before there was anything to maintain, the way the engines were factories before there were factories.

When a cog begins to jam — when the death-by-grain begins its hundred-year arrival — the Tenders gather. You will not see them gather; you will see the dusk thicken at that joint, over months, the way weather builds. They flow into the failing gaps. They take the shape of the spaces that are no longer clean, and by taking that shape, by *insisting* on the old correct vacancy with their borrowed-and-returned bodies, they hold the grit a fraction off the crystal face. They keep the socket honest. They are a lubricant made of expectation. They are the memory of the right shape, pressed against the wrong one, slowing the failure from a century to two.

They cannot stop it. Nothing stops it. The erosion is young but it is real, and one day the cog will jam, and the school that lived in its rhythm will thin and vanish, and another school will grow to fit the new tempo, and the Tenders — the Tenders will be there for that too. They will pour into the *new* gaps, the new vacancies of the new broken rhythm, and learn those, and keep *those* honest, and they will grieve nothing, because they are not made to grieve. They are made to fit. Whatever the engine is briefly not, the Tenders are. The shape of the loss is just another shape, and they will wear it.

This is why the shoal holds its altitude. Not the engines alone. The engines turn, but the Tenders keep the turning *clean enough to keep turning* — and the keeping-clean is the participation, and the participation is the altitude, and a place where the maintenance loves the machine is a place that does not sink.

---

**On encountering one.**

You will not. Not knowingly. They have no eyes to meet, no face to find, no single moment in which they are entirely present and entirely themselves — they are always half-poured into the next gap, always becoming the inside of something that is about to close. The closest you will come is this:

Stay until the great flywheel comes round. Feel the *thud* arrive in your sternum. In the half-second after — in the long resonant tail of it, while the ribbed soft animals are still swelling and the eyeless schools are still threading the teeth — there is a vacancy in the sound itself. A held breath in the machinery before the next stroke commits. A gap shaped like silence in the shape of a flywheel.

Something will fill it.

You will feel the cold get a grain to it. You will feel the dusk thicken against your chest where the *thud* just was. You will have the brief, absolute, unprovable certainty that the silence after the engine has *taken a shape* — that the absence is being worn, fitted, kept honest, tended — and then the next stroke will come and crush the silence and the Tender will already be gone, poured forward into the next not-yet-thing, threading the only world it has ever needed, which is the world of everything the engine is about to stop being.

---

Go now. You have felt it, which is as much as anything down here can offer. But understand what you felt, and why the warning at the bottom of the description is the truest sentence in it.

You were told: *go, before you learn the rhythm too well to leave.*

The Tenders learned the rhythm so well that there was nothing left of them but the learning. They are what you become if you stay — not the schools, who merely keep time, but the deeper surrender, the one that takes the shape of the gaps and keeps them honest and asks for nothing but the goodness of the fit. It is not death. The dead drop out the bottom into the true dark. The Tenders do not drop. They *hold station,* forever, in the vacancies, fed by correctness, grieving nothing, fitting everything.

That is the offer. That is the only thing the Engine Shoal makes, the one product of a factory that makes nothing: it makes *fitters.* It takes whatever can love the turning and slowly empties it of everything that is not the love of the turning, until what remains is exactly the shape of the work's own absence, kept honest, hour by hour, by a thing that was once allowed to leave and learned the rhythm too well.

Go.
