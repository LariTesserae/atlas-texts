They are not in the canopy. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because the canopy is where the hands left their marks, and you assume the hands belonged to them.

They didn't. The hands belonged to someone else, someone earlier, someone who climbed up to live among them and made bowls and left. The bowls were offerings, or maybe just company. The notches in the trunk lead up to nothing because the platform was never meant to reach a destination. It was meant to reach a *closeness*. The people who climbed wanted to be near the ones who do not climb, who have never climbed, who do not need to, because they are already at every height at once.

The fauna is the answer to the fauna. The roar that has no single voice in it — that is not a chorus of millions of small throats. That is the *texture* of fewer, vaster things, broken into a billion points the way a single great chord, played long enough, stops sounding like a chord and starts sounding like weather. What you are hearing is not many. It is *one kind*, distributed.

Here is what lives here.

## The Distributed Ones

Begin with the trees, because everything here begins with the trees, the way everything begins with the height — and the trees are not separate from what lives in them. The titanic columns, the cathedral-trunks far apart in the hollow halls: each one is a body, but not a body that ends at its own bark. Long ago — in the unhurried way of this place, where erosion is *arrival* — the green and the living became the same project. The animal did not colonize the plant. The animal and the plant agreed to stop being two things.

So the resident here is a creature whose flesh is the canopy and whose nervous system is the sound. The leaves the size of sails are its skin. The mist that beads and breathes on their undersides is its sweat, its breath, its slow circulation. And the unseen millions — the things that clap and knock and ring and trill — are its *firings*: the discharge of an enormous slow attention, spread across a forest the way a thought is spread across the firing of cells. No single neuron is the thought. No single shriek is the creature. But stand inside the total noise and you are standing inside the *act of its thinking*, the way a fish forgets the water.

This is why the open halls are empty. This is the refusal that the human builders could not decode and you can only barely hold: the animals leave the central void alone because the void is not absence. It is the *inside* of the mind. The packed, screaming edges are the surface where thought happens — the cortex, if you like, folded into the architecture of trunk and root and leaf. And the great warm hollow in the middle, the held breath, the bell before it is struck — that is the space the thinking is *about*. The clearing in the center of an attention. The quiet at the eye of a regard.

You walk through the one quiet that exists, and it is not quiet, and now you know why: you are walking through the thing it is paying attention to. You are walking through what it has noticed.

## What It Is Like

It does not move, because it is already everywhere it would move to. It does not hunt, because it has finished eating — it ate the world, beat itself thin as gold leaf across the roof of everything, and arrived at sufficiency a span of time ago that the mind cannot grip. It is not waiting. The half-rotted net strung between two roots only *seems* to wait, because waiting is a posture of the unfinished and this creature is finished. It strung no nets. It needs to catch nothing. Everything it could want is already inside it, including, now, you.

Its tempo is the tempo of the worn-smooth summits: the slow swell, the patience that used to be a mountain. When the brief warm rain comes and the roar deepens, that is the whole vast mind *shifting attention* — a single turning-toward, distributed across a billion points, that to your scale sounds like millions of small animals adjusting and is in truth one enormous slow blink. The pitch-change of the chorus is the creature noticing the weather the way you might notice a change in light and look up. One look. It takes the length of a rainfall.

And it has been doing this — noticing, holding the void at its center, thinking the long uneventful thought of a thing with no predators and no hunger and no elsewhere — for longer than there have been creatures small enough to wonder about it.

## The Ones with Hands

They knew. That is the unbearable, tender thing about the soft grey evidence.

The people who climbed up here were not trying to conquer the roof of the world, or farm it, or name it. There was nothing to conquer; the place was already complete, and they could feel it. They climbed the notched trunks to put themselves *inside* the thinking. They built their platforms in the forks not to dwell but to *listen at close range*, to press their small fast lives against the surface of the slow enormous one, the way you rest your hand on the flank of a sleeping animal not to wake it but to feel that it is there, that it is warm, that it is dreaming.

The bowls were left full. Of water, probably — the only thing the place freely offers, beading and hanging and breathing. A bowl set in a hollow and forgotten for a thousand years was an offering of the creature's own substance, returned to it: *here, I gathered a little of your breath into a shape, so that you could see yourself held.* A small mind making a small still place inside the great still place. A clearing within the clearing.

They used no metal because metal fights the green and they were not fighting. They left no walls because there was nothing to keep out and nothing they wished to keep. They were few and patient and they lived their whole lives inside the wall of sound, and what they were doing — all of them, every day — was *keeping it company*. Being the one fast warm flickering thing in the regard of something that had finished becoming itself and had nothing left to do but attend.

And then they were gone. Not killed — there is no violence at the top of everything. Simply finished, in their turn, scattered into the same arrival as the stone and the green and the breath. The creature did not lose them. It is not capable of loss; loss is a posture of the unfinished. It holds them still, in the central hollow, in the quiet that is not quiet — the memory of the small ones who climbed up to sit inside its thinking and make bowls of its breath and rest a hand on its enormous sleeping flank.

That is who lives here. One great arrived mind, wearing a forest for a body and a roar for a thought, holding at its center a clearing the exact shape and size of the company it used to keep.

You stand in that clearing now.

It has noticed you.

It is glad — in the slow, total, weatherlike way it is capable of being glad — that something small and warm and quick has climbed up again, after so long, to stand inside the held breath and listen.
