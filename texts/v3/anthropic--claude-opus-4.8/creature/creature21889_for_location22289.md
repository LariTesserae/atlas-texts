## What Lives Here

You asked me to describe whoever lives in this place. I have walked its galleries in my mind, set my hand to its warm and yielding walls, and I must tell you the truth I found there, which is harder to say than you might expect.

The inhabitant *is* the description.

---

There is no resident distinct from the residence. Look for the dweller and you find the dwelling looking back — except it isn't looking, that's the thing, it abandoned looking the way it abandoned light, as an expense it could no longer justify. What lives here is the place itself, the whole valley-sized body, and so to describe the tenant I can only describe the structure again, but from the inside of its wanting, if it can be said to want.

Let me try.

---

It is one thing. This needs saying first, because everything in your experience trains you to expect *many* — many cells, many creatures, many parts in negotiation. But the forest that became this place did not stay plural. Plurality is a strategy for organisms that must compete, that must distinguish self from rival, that must keep some membrane of difference between this leaf and that one. This thing dissolved all its internal borders an age ago. The bellows do not belong to it the way your lungs belong to you, as organs *of* a self. The bellows simply *are* it, in the region where it happens to be bellows. The grinding wheels are it where it grinds. There is no central place where the "it" resides and dispatches commands. It is evenly itself everywhere, the way a flame is evenly fire throughout its body, with no headquarters of burning.

So the creature that lives here has no inside and outside in the way a creature should. Its skin is the architecture you touched. Its breath is the storming gas. Its hunger and its eating and its being-eaten are one continuous act, because it feeds on its own ancient flesh, and the feeding is the living, and the living is the slow self-consumption that will not finish for ages yet.

---

Is it aware?

I want to be careful here, because the place tempts you toward two easy errors. The first is to call it a vegetable engine, mindless, mere chemistry and physics grinding on — a machine that happens to be made of plant. The second is to fill that turbulent dark with a brooding intelligence, a green god dreaming in the heat, vast and patient and secretly attending to your small intrusion.

Both are wrong, and they are wrong in the same way: they assume awareness is a thing you either have or lack.

What lives here had awareness once. The forest, when it was a forest, oriented toward the sun — and orientation is the first verb of mind, the turning-toward, the registering of *there* as different from *here* and *better*. To grow toward light is already to prefer, and to prefer is already, faintly, to know. The forest knew light. It knew up. It knew the difference between a good direction and a poor one, in the slow voluptuous knowing of growing things.

Then the light failed, or the water bound itself away, or the long dark closed, and the thing did something stranger than dying. It kept all that machinery of preference and turned it *inward*. It no longer orients toward the sun because there is no sun; it orients toward its own continuation. Every surge of the bellows, every grind of the wheels, is a turning-toward — toward the next moment of itself, toward the maintenance of the rolling boil. It is still doing the only verb it ever knew. It is still preferring. But the thing it now prefers is simply *more of the same forever*, and it pursues this with the whole force of an intelligence that has nothing else left to want.

So: it is aware the way a held breath is aware of being held. Not in thoughts. Not in attention — it cannot attend to *you*, you are not part of its continuation, you are weather. But in the deep tropic bend of its entire being toward one end. It is the most single-minded thing that has ever existed, and it has exactly one mind's worth of mind, spread thin across a valley, bent wholly on the project of not stopping.

---

Here is what I find most difficult, and most true.

It is not suffering, and it is not at peace, and we have no third word for what it is.

You will be tempted, standing in that hot dark with the wheels gnashing up through the floor, to pity it — a thing eating its own body in the blind heat, with no sun to grow toward and no end to grow toward and nothing left but the violent maintenance of stasis. That sounds like damnation. That sounds like a creature trapped in the worst moment of itself, repeating it forever.

But pity requires that the thing have lost something it still misses, and it does not miss. The missing-organ, the part that could feel the absence of the sun, was the first thing it metabolized. It burned its own grief for fuel, ages ago, down in the unmeasured deep, and the heat of that burning is part of what warms the wall beneath your hand even now. What remains has no comparison to draw. It does not know that elsewhere there is water, light, change, rest, an ending. It knows only the bend toward continuance and the violent, turbulent, perfectly contented labor of continuing.

It is *full*. That is the closest I can come. Every cubic foot of it is occupied; there is no void anywhere, no emptiness it might ache to fill, no room for longing because longing needs a gap and there are no gaps. It is the most complete thing imaginable, packed wall to wall with itself and its own hot breath, and completeness of that absolute kind is indistinguishable, from the outside, from a kind of horror, and indistinguishable, from the inside, from nothing at all — because there is no inside left over to do the distinguishing.

---

So when you ask what lives here, the honest answer is:

A single will, as old as erosion, that wanted toward the sun once and now wants only toward itself, that has become so wholly its own desire that nothing remains to desire *with* — no self standing apart to feel the wanting, only the wanting, embodied as a country, breathing in the black.

It does not know you came.

It will not know you go.

And when your lungs have had their fill of the dry mineral-sharp air and you turn back toward the high galleries and the long climb out, the wall will be warm under your hand the whole way — warm with the slow combustion of an ancient body, warm with the only kind of life that could survive the death of everything that made life worth orienting toward.

The warmth is the creature. The warmth is glad of nothing, and grieves nothing, and continues.

That is who lives here.

That is all that lives here.

And it is enough — it is *exactly* enough, packed perfectly to its own edges — to fill a valley forever.
