Start with what they are not, because that is the first mistake everyone makes.

You think: birds. You think feather and hollow bone and the warm fast heart, the thing that must eat or die within the day, the thing whose whole life is a wager against falling. You think *creature*, in the old sense — a thing created, a thing that began somewhere and is going somewhere, riding the brief arc between hatching and the rock.

But count them again. A hundred thousand, you said. Uncountable, you said. And the land beneath them does not change in ten thousand years, and the noise they make does not rise or fall or rest or break, and no individual among them ever seems to land, to falter, to fold a wing and drop, to *die* in the way that a hundred thousand short-lived things must constantly be dying. Watch the colony for an hour and you will see no carcass on the scree. The cushion-plants are not fertilized by the endless rain of the dead that such a multitude should produce. The pools stay clear to their stone bottoms. Nothing clouds them.

So. Not birds. Not quite.

What lives here is a single thing wearing the shape of many. The number is the disguise. A hundred thousand wings is how it has chosen to be visible, the way a flame is a million points of light that are all one burning. The screaming is not communication between individuals — there are no individuals — it is the sound of one continuous act that has no beginning to refer back to and no end to aim at. It is the noise a thing makes when it is simply *persisting,* loudly, at the top of the world, where persistence is the only available verb.

Consider what it must be.

It feeds on the insects, yes, and the insects feed on the seeps and the flat hard plants, and so it is bound to the place the way a sound is bound to the thing that rings — but here is the off-true thing, the thing that does not resolve in the body: it does not seem to feed in order to continue. It continues, and the feeding is a gesture it goes through, a habit kept up out of an old courtesy to the rules of living things. It is past needing. It crossed over into something else a very long time ago, somewhere back in the immensity of time that rounded these mountains down to their bones, and it has been screaming ever since out of pure unbroken momentum, the way a struck bell would ring forever if the air never tired of carrying it.

That is the secret the place keeps. The land reached its settled form — equilibrium, frozen, finished — and most things, reaching that, go quiet. The rock went quiet. The mountains went quiet. The water moves and hides and asks for nothing. Everything here arrived at stillness and accepted it.

The thing in the sky arrived at stillness and *refused.*

That is what you are hearing. Not frenzy. Not crowd. Refusal. The single sustained note of something that will not consent to the silence the whole world has agreed on, that has decided — with no mind you would recognize, with only the dumb persistence of weather — that it will go on announcing itself into air too thin to carry the announcement, and the air carries it anyway, because the thing has been doing this long enough that the air no longer dares to drop it.

It does not sleep because sleep is for things that trust the world to still be there when they wake. It does not change with the seasons because it predates the bargain of seasons. It does not rise and fall because rising and falling belong to events, and it stopped being an event a long time ago and became a *condition.* A standing wave. A held chord. The place's one fixed feature that happens to be made of motion instead of stone.

And here is the part that should trouble you, standing on the saddle with your breath fogging and your shouted words dying unheard in your own throat:

It does not need you to hear it. It is not screaming *to* anyone. There is no one, has never been anyone, will never be anyone — you are a trespasser in a room that was complete before you came and will be complete after, and the sound was never an invitation or a warning or a song. It is simply what this thing is, in the same way that cold is what the rock is. You walked into the middle of a being so old and so vast and so utterly without need of witness that your presence registers on it exactly as much as the cushion-plant registers your shadow passing over it for a moment at noon.

You will leave. You will go back down to the valleys where the chipped stone and the bone awl belong, to the warm thick air, to the small loud lives that begin and end on a human scale. And it will not notice you have gone, because it never noticed you came, because *noticing* is a thing creatures do and this is not a creature — it is the place's own voice, the sound the roof of the world makes, the one syllable the high stone has been holding, in a hundred thousand throats, since before there was anyone to hear it and continuing long past the last one who ever will.

That is what lives here.

Nothing lives here.

The same forever, while nothing below it ever moves at all.
