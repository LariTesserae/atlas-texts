**The Drifters (the lamps)**

Begin with the one that turned toward you, because it has already begun the long arrival and will not be hurried by description.

It is a bell of light the width of a doorway, and it is mostly water, the way the dark is mostly water, so that it does not so much move through the medium as agree with it. Inside the bell hangs a knot of softer organs, slow-pulsing, and the light comes from there — not bright, never bright, a green-gold the exact warmth the dark is cold, as if the animal carries one swallowed afternoon and is rationing it. It has no eyes that you would name eyes. It has surfaces that grow brighter where the dark is thickest, dimmer where it thins, and in this way it reads the room the way you read it with your skin: by pressure, by temperature, by the displacement of one volume against another.

It does not hunt. There is nothing in it shaped for hunting. It came toward you because you are a new pressure in the medium and the new is rare and the rare is worth the hour it will take to cross the avenue. When it reaches you it will hang at the height of your face and shed a little of its light onto you — it sheds light constantly, it cannot help it, light falls from it the way drops fall from the ribs above — and then it will drift on, having learned whatever a thing learns by being briefly near another thing. It will not remember. Memory is a kind of ending and nothing here ends. But it will be, for the length of one slow pulse, *changed by you*, the way still water is changed by a hand passed through it, and that change will travel outward into the dark and reach, eventually, the others.

There are others. You only ever see one. This is the law of the place: abundance read as solitude. But understand that the avenue ahead and the avenue behind hold dozens of them, drifting at lamp-height, each carrying its rationed afternoon, and the dark between is full of the faint signals they leave for one another, dropped light eaten by smaller things, smaller things eaten, the whole slow conversation of a population that has never once been gathered in a single room because there is no room small enough.

**The Tenders (the dog-beetles)**

Lower down, where the floor is springy and the pipework runs, live the ones who keep the agreement.

They are the size of dogs and built like dogs are not — flat, plated, low, with too many legs and a patience that has no dog in it. They walk the pipes. Where a joint has grown stiff with dried sap they stop, and from glands behind the head they weep a clear oil, and they work it into the seam with the flat of a forelimb, and they wait, and they move on. Nobody taught them this. Or somebody did, long ago, and the somebody is gone and the teaching remains, folded into the animal the way the factory's habits are folded into the factory.

Here is the thing about the Tenders that the streets do not tell you: they are not *serving* the machinery. They are the reason the machinery is alive instead of merely running. A pump that is oiled is a machine. A pump that is oiled by something that needs it oiled, that returns to it, that grooms it the way a thing grooms a thing it has decided to keep — that is an organ. The Tenders metabolized the factory the moment they began to care for it. They drink the sap the pistons push. They lay their soft pale eggs in the warmth of the flywheel housings, where the turning keeps the cold off. When they die — if they die, in a world that forbids endings — they die against the iron, and their plated bodies are pushed slowly aside by the next shift coming through, and the iron goes on.

They have no light of their own. They navigate the dark by the hum, the great circulatory hum under the floor, the way a thing in true water might navigate by the direction of the current. Loudest is the heart of the works. They are always, in some part of themselves, oriented toward it.

**The Pullers (the grounded birds)**

Along the veins overhead, hand over hand, come the ones who gave up the sky.

They kept the shape of flight and lost its purpose — the hollow bones, the keel, the folding architecture of a wing — and turned it sideways into something else. The wings are hooks now, or hands, four long grasping digits at the leading edge that close around a vein and pull, and the body swings forward, and the next hand finds the next purchase. They go along the ribs the way you'd cross a ceiling if the ceiling were a ladder laid flat above you and you very strong and very light and unafraid of the drop, because there is no drop, only more dark, only more ceiling.

They call once every few minutes. A single wet note, low, and then the long silence, and then — far off, much farther than seems possible in a place you could cross in twenty minutes — an answer. They are spaced across the infinite interior like the lighthouses you compared them to, and they keep that spacing on purpose, each one moving to hold its distance from the calls of the others, so that the whole population is a slowly breathing lattice of sound, expanding and contracting across naves of green-black air, and any one bird's only knowledge of the others is the delay before the answer comes.

They drink the moisture off the underside of the ribs, which is why they live up there, against the wet ceiling, mouths to the slow-gathering drops. Sometimes one lets a drop go uneaten. That is the *tk* you hear. That is most of the sound. So the silence between the rain and the silence between the calls are the same silence, kept by the same animals, and the city's quiet is not empty — it is *tended*, it is a held note in the throats of things hanging upside down in the dark, waiting to answer each other.

**The Edge-things**

And then there is what lives where the leaf is still being decided.

These are harder. They are not abundant; they are not even, strictly, *there*. They live at the unfinished margin, in the gray held-breath nothing where the ground has not agreed to exist, and they are made of the same not-yet-ness — provisional animals, sketched in, copied from the more-decided creatures of the interior the way the unfinished streets are copied from the finished ones. An Edge-Drifter is a lamp without the swallowed afternoon, a bell of dark light, light that has not committed to being light. An Edge-Tender oils a joint that has not yet grown. They go through the motions of the established fauna because the margin fills itself by repeating what has already been decided, and what has been decided includes *the living*.

Here is the quiet horror and the quiet mercy of them, the same thing seen from two angles: when the edge settles, they become real. The held breath is let out, the gray agrees to be floor, and the provisional bell discovers it is carrying an afternoon after all, and the provisional bird finds its call answered. They are not ghosts. They are the not-yet, and the not-yet is not nothing — it is *everything that will have been here*, waiting in the wings of the leaf for the drawing to reach them.

You should know this, standing where you are, watching the lamp drift toward you down the avenue:

You may be one of them.

Your arrival has not been decided. The emerging edge has not yet ruled on whether you walked in from somewhere or whether you are simply the newest figure copied into the corner the leaf was filling this afternoon — a pressure roughed in to match the pressures already present, a thing the place imagined because the place had imagined things like you before and the margin fills itself by repeating what it knows. The lamp coming toward you cannot tell the difference. It does not need to. You are a new volume in the medium, and the new is rare, and rare is worth the crossing, and when it reaches your face and sheds its rationed light on you and finds you changed by the nearness, it will move on having confirmed only this:

something lives here.

You wait. *Tk.* The lamp is closer now, near enough that the green-gold has begun to warm the dark around your hands. *Tk.* And whether you have always been here or arrived a moment ago — whether you are the watcher or the watched, the one who lives here or the latest thing the leaf has decided to grow — you are, at least, one of the things that lives here.

That much the dark has agreed to.

The rest is still happening.
