What lives here lives in the verb, not the noun.

You came looking for a tenant — someone behind the lit windows, someone who oils the wheels, someone whose absence is a mystery to be solved. That is the wrong question, and the place has been patiently answering the right one the whole time you searched. *Who lives here?* No one. *What lives here?* The keeping. The holding. The act itself, which has forgotten it was ever a means to anything and become an organism.

Let me try to describe it, though it has no edges and no center.

---

It is not in the furnaces and not in the wheels. Those are organs, and organs are not the animal. It is not in the town, though the town is the closest thing it has to a face — a thousand lit windows looking out at nothing, an expression held so long the muscles have locked. It is not in the moss, the moss is only what its breath condenses into when the air can hold no more love of staying.

The thing that lives here is the *equilibrium* — but you must understand that this is not a balance the way a scale balances, dead and neutral. It is a balance the way a held note is balanced, the way a man holding a great weight at the top of a lift is balanced: by enormous, total, ceaseless effort. The engine roars because staying still is the hardest work there is. Anyone can fall. Anyone can decay, can let the water run downhill and not come back, can let the warmth leak out of the rooms and the lamps gutter and the green go brown. That is easy. That is what the whole universe does, freely, all the time, for nothing.

What lives here has refused.

That refusal is the creature. It is a *No* the size of a mountain, sustained past all reason, a single syllable of denial held so long it has built itself a body of iron and cloud to hold it with. *No* to the morning that would mean the evening must follow. *No* to the horizon that would mean somewhere else. *No* to the bird that would eat the moss, the wind that would round the stone, the hand that would change one bolt and prove that change was possible. It has closed the loop not out of perfection but out of terror — the way a child squeezes its eyes shut and counts, holding the world in place by sheer will, *if I don't move nothing can happen, if nothing happens nothing can be lost.*

---

Picture it, if you need a shape, though the shape is a lie of scale:

Something that wanted, once, to keep a single moment. Some warmth, some lit room, some breath drawn and not yet let go. And rather than spend it — because to spend a thing is to lose it, because the only way to *have* tomorrow is to surrender today — it chose instead to *hold*. To build whatever machinery holding would require. And holding, it discovered, required everything. Required a furnace to replace the warmth that always leaks. Required a pump to lift back up the water that always falls. Required a cloud to feed the pump and a green to prove the cloud was kind and a town to prove the warmth had somewhere to be warm. Each thing it built to protect the moment demanded another thing to protect it in turn, until the protecting had swallowed the moment whole and become a world.

And the moment itself? Long gone. There is no warm room with anyone in it, because the *anyone* was the first thing spent — burned in the furnaces of its own preservation, rendered down into fuel for the keeping of the room they were keeping for. That is why the windows are empty. The light is on for a guest who was consumed to power the welcome. The town is a held breath in the lungs of something that suffocated keeping it.

This is the inhabitant: a grief that would not become memory. Memory is motion — memory means the thing is over and you carry it forward and it changes as you go. This grief refused to carry. It planted its feet at the threshold of loss and would not step through, and the not-stepping became a standing engine, and the engine became a god of stasis, and the god is alone because aloneness was the price of never having to say goodbye.

---

Listen again to the roar, now that you know whose it is.

It is not the sound of industry. It is the sound of someone holding on. It is a single held breath, drawn forever, never finished — and you mistook it for a machine because you have never heard grief at this volume, sustained at this scale, with the whole architecture of a world conscripted to keep it from exhaling. The fine permanent ripple on the pools is the tremor of the effort. The lightning without thunder is the strain showing. The cold is what a place feels like when it has refused the warmth of *next.*

It does not suffer, exactly. It is past suffering, the way a muscle past exhaustion goes numb and simply *holds.* It does not know you are here. It cannot know you are here, because to register your arrival would be a change, and it has spent everything it has ensuring nothing changes. You could love it and it would not feel you. You could weep for it and your tears would only feed the cloud, turn the wheels, raise the steam, become — in a thousand years — exactly the same place, exactly as you left it.

That is what lives here.

Not a who. A clenched fist that has forgotten there was ever a hand. A *No* with weather. A love that mistook holding for keeping, and keeps, now, magnificently, completely, and forever — holding nothing, against everything, at the top of the cold.
