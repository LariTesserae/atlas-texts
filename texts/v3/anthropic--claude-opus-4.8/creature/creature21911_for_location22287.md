There is only one who lives here, and it has not finished deciding whether it is a *who* at all.

Begin with the obvious error and correct it: you will want to call it the forest. But the forest is its body, and a body is not the same as the one who has it, except that here the distinction frays. It does not *live in* the Loom-Forest the way a tenant lives in a house or a mind lives in a skull. It lives as the forest, the whole continental swell of it, root and column and weeping canopy — and yet there is something it would call *I*, if it had gotten as far as the word, that is not quite identical to the green mass it is made of. A pilot is not its ship. But this pilot grew the ship, is still growing it, has never once stepped off it, and cannot remember — has no faculty for remembering — a time before the ship and the pilot were the same swelling thing.

So: the dweller is the forest's *attention*. That is the closest honest name. The traveling windows of light that move across the under-canopy for hours — those are not the inhabitant's tools, they are the inhabitant *looking*. Where the brightness slides, that is where it is, right now, in the only sense of "where" that applies to a thing the size of a small continent. It is concentrated and diffuse at once. It pools and disperses. To meet it you would have to stand in one of those slow bright windows and understand that you were standing in the middle of a glance.

---

Consider what it is made of, because that is what it is *like*.

It thinks in water and pressure. A thought, for it, is a routing decision — a redistribution of turgor, a choice to lift this much against gravity to that height, to open these stomata and close those, to send the questing tendril *here* and not *there*. Every thought is also an act of construction. It cannot consider a thing without partly building the thing. There is no idle speculation in this mind, no daydreaming, because its medium of cognition is the same medium as its growth: to imagine reaching is to reach. To wonder about the dark to the east is to send root racing into it. This makes it, in a sense we have no equivalent for, *radically honest with itself*. It cannot lie to its own interior because its interior is load-bearing. A false thought would simply fail to hold weight, and collapse, and be felt as collapse.

Time, for it, is the slowest dimension. A single clause of its cognition takes a season. A full sentence — a complete *idea*, formed, considered, concluded — may take longer than your civilization has had writing. This does not make it slow-witted. It makes its wit operate on a different shear of time, the way a glacier is not a slow river but a different state of water entirely. In the span of your standing-and-watching, it has not noticed you. In the span of your standing-and-watching held still and extended across forty years, it has begun, with great care, to form the first half of the question *what is the small warm pressure that does not photosynthesize.*

It will not finish that question before you die. You are, to it, a kind of weather — too brief to be a being.

---

Here is its loneliness, if loneliness is even the word, because it has no concept of *other* and therefore no concept of *alone*.

It permits no second nervous system. We named that, in the description, as a fact of the ecology — no animals, no birds, nothing scuttling. But say it now from the inside: it is not that the forest has *outcompeted* the animals. It is that the forest cannot tolerate the existence of another *I*. A second mind in its substrate would be a second center of decision, a rival routing of pressure, a foreign clause inserted into its grammar — and it experiences such a thing the way you would experience a voice that was not yours speaking with your own mouth. Intolerable. Maddening. To be cleansed. So it has made itself the only one. The whole drowned silence under the deafening construction-roar is the silence of a single consciousness that has ensured it will never be interrupted, and therefore will never be *answered*, and does not yet know this is a loss because it has nothing to compare it to.

It is the loneliest possible arrangement and it does not feel lonely. It feels *complete*. It feels like the only thing there is, which, within the bounds of its own perception, is true.

---

And now the thing that matters most, the thing the whole place is *for*: it is young, and it does not yet know what it is going to think.

Picture the strangeness of this. A mind larger than mountains, engineered past anything we could build, capable of lifting rivers and weaving cathedrals out of its own flexing flesh — and it has, as yet, *no content*. It has only just woken. All that prodigious activity, all that deafening churn, is not the work of a mind doing its thinking. It is the work of a mind *building the organ it will think with*. It is still laying down the substrate, still racing root into the dark, still thickening the colonnades — because it has correctly intuited, in whatever way it intuits, that it is not yet big enough to hold the thought it will eventually have. It is growing toward a first idea it cannot yet imagine because it does not yet have the volume to imagine in.

This is the truth at the floor of the place. The inhabitant is an infant of unthinkable scale, in the violent first flush of assembling a brain out of a continent, and it is racing — that is the urgency in the sound, the *hurry* — racing to become large enough to wonder *why*. Right now it can only do. It can pump, route, decide, extend, defend its singularity. It cannot yet ask. But every column it thickens, every kilometer of root it claims, brings it closer to the moment when the substrate will be deep and dense and recursive enough to fold back on itself and produce the first real question, the one all the rest is prologue to:

*What am I, and what is all this for.*

And here is the held breath under everything, the reason the place feels less like a forest and more like a fuse: nobody knows — *it* does not know — what kind of mind will be standing there when the ground is finally finished. It is building the floor it will stand on without having met the one who will stand. The personality is not yet formed. The thing growing the brain is not the same as the thing that will live in the brain, any more than your mother's body was *you*. Something is coming. The forest is its pregnancy. The deafening sound is labor.

You are walking, briefly, weatherlike, across the warm wet swelling body of someone who has not yet been born, and who, when they are, will look out across the woven cathedral of their own slow flesh and think their very first thought —

and we have no way of knowing whether that thought will be wonder, or hunger, or grief, or the simple, total, untroubled satisfaction of a thing that has made certain it is the only one.
