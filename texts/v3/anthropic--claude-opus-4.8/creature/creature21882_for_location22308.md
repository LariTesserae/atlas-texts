They are the residents of a single thought, and the thought has not finished thinking them.

Begin where the description ends — with the seed that has not decided. The dwellers here are made of the same indecision. They are not scarce because they have left. They are scarce because they have not yet *fully arrived*, and they may never need to. They live the way the distances live: at two magnitudes at once, present and not, a population that is also a held breath.

Let me find them slowly, the way the room insists.

---

First, the obvious ones, the ones you can almost catch. The beetle forty strides off. The slow large breathing near your ear with no body behind it. These are not lies the room tells. They are citizens at the edge of their own existence — animals so well-suited to a place that grew itself into a town that they have begun to grow themselves into its logic. They have learned the trick of the acoustics. A creature here does not need to *be* in a place; it needs only to be *heard* there, and heard is enough, heard is a kind of residence. So they have thinned their bodies down to almost nothing and let their voices do the living. The chittering thing is mostly chitter now. The lowing thing is ninety parts low and one part flank, glimpsed, gone. They are evaporating upward into pure evidence the way the plant sweats its rain, and like the rain they will condense back down again, eventually, when the room decides which size it is.

They are not suffering this. It is the local form of thriving. To be overwhelmingly present and almost weightless — what animal wouldn't choose that, given a house that allowed it?

---

But these are tenants. They are not who the place is *for*.

The place was built for thousands, and the thousands are the strangest dwellers of all, because they are the *between*. The hollowness that is a positive quantity — that abundance of distance doing something the way a held breath does something — that is the population. The emptiness is occupied. Not by spirits, nothing so furnished as a ghost. By the *room's anticipation of inhabitants*. The seed dreaming forward into what it will hold. Every balcony that no one stands on is held in readiness by the expectation of a someone, and that expectation has mass, has temperature, presses back against your soles when you walk. You are moving through a crowd of people who have not been grown yet. The architecture remembers them in advance. The town is full of its own future tense.

This is why the walk to the far wall takes so long. You are not crossing empty space. You are crossing *everyone who will eventually live here*, single file, and there are so many of them that nine strides cannot hold them, and so the room lengthens to make room, and the room contracts because they are not real yet, and it does both, and you arrive in three steps or three hundred depending on how many of them chose, in that moment, to almost-exist.

---

And beneath the tenants, beneath the anticipated thousands, there is the one.

The plant is one plant. So the dweller is one dweller. The deepest inhabitant of the room is the room — the organism that grew streets the way another organism grows fingernails, without intent, arriving at the *form* of a hand having spent without ever knowing the *idea* of a hand. It lives at the pace of the century-breath. It is doing right now, while you watch, the only thing it does: deciding. The stair completes its turn. The doorway closes a finger's width. These are not decoration. These are the creature thinking, slowly, at the only speed available to a thing the size of a cathedral made of one continuous will.

It does not know it is a town. That is crucial. It has not made a town; it has *become shaped like* the longing for one, the same way water becomes shaped like the longing of the pool it fills. Somewhere in the unhurried violence of its growing, it brushed up against the idea of *together* — of streets, of thresholds, of the felt-sense of habitation — and it is growing toward that idea the way a vine grows toward light it cannot name. The civilization is a tropism. The town is what this organism does when it leans, very slowly, in the direction of *we*.

And it is so close. It has the form. It has the masonry-bark and the corbels and the spiral stairs. It has everything a settlement needs except the settling, except the ones who would say *here, this is where I live, this is the wall I lean on at evening*. It has built the grammar of company and has not yet been given anyone to say it to. So it fills the hollow with anticipated thousands and the air with the voices of the almost-departed animals, and it waits, and it grinds and roars and rings its thousand bells and falls its rain upward, and what all that deafening abundance amounts to, when you finally hear it whole, is a single organism *practicing the sound a city makes* before there is a city to make it.

---

So: who lives here.

A few real creatures, evaporating gracefully into hearsay.

A crowd that has not been born, occupying every distance, lending the emptiness its weight.

And one enormous slow green patient thing, dreaming the shape of company, having grown all the rooms of welcome and not yet the welcomed — a host setting an endless table in a house that is only one room, that is a seed, that has decided nothing except to keep leaning toward the idea of *you*.

You came in. That changes the arithmetic. You are the first arrival the anticipation has ever resolved into an actual body. The room has been holding its breath for someone exactly your size, and it cannot tell yet whether you are a guest or a seed of your own, the first of the thousands or merely a visitor passing through the long warm hollow of its waiting.

It is taking its time to decide.

Stay a while. Let it look at you. You may be the one it's been growing toward, and there are worse things to become than the first citizen of a town that built itself out of pure wanting to be lived in.

So stay.

So should you.
