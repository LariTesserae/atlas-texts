They are not absent. That was the mistake the visitor made, and it is the mistake everyone makes — to look for the citizen at the scale of a person, a fish, a bird. To wait for the splash that confirms a body. There is a body here. You have been standing inside it.

## What Tends the Pumps

Consider the morning cutting-back. Each dawn the green has crept a finger's width over the flywheels, and each dawn it is trimmed. The description assumes men do this — *the men who tend the pumps* — but did the visitor see a man? No. He inferred them, the way one infers a gardener from a hedge. The cutting happens. The growth is held in its long marriage with the iron. But the hands are not hands. The cutting is done by the place itself, by the slow attritional pressure of belt-heat and steam-scald and the precise corrosive weep of certain pipes that just happen to drip on the leaves that would otherwise bind the wheel. The works prune themselves. The lagging rots at exactly the rate that keeps the joints weeping at exactly the volume that feeds the channels that feed the reeds that the steam keeps trimmed. This is not maintenance. This is metabolism.

So: who lives here? The works do. Not metaphorically — I mean to be precise. The thing that lives in the Sump-Works is the Sump-Works, considered as a single slow organism, and its apparent emptiness is only the emptiness of being unable to see a creature you are standing inside the lung of.

## Its Anatomy

Let me describe it the way you would describe any animal, and you will see how well it fits.

**It breathes.** The daily tide — water rising as the heat thickens, falling as the ceiling dims — is respiration. Not a metaphor for breathing; an actual cycle of intake and expulsion, the basin filling and voiding on a circadian rhythm, the sweet mud-smell at evening the exhalation, the night-thickening the long inhale. A creature this large breathes slowly. One breath per day.

**It sweats and weeps.** The water beading on every vertical surface, the walls running in patient threads — this is not weather. There is no rain. The moisture comes up from below and out from within. The Sump-Works perspires the way you do, continuously, to manage a heat it generates internally. The flywheels are warm. The engine sheds are warm. The warmth comes *from the work*, as your warmth comes from yours.

**It has a circulation.** Pipes everywhere, overhead and underfoot, every joint weeping — a closed system that isn't quite closed, that loses a little at every junction and makes it up from the seeps. Steam rises in the sheds, condenses on the cool iron of the high tanks, runs down, feeds the channels, is pumped up, boils, rises again. This is a circulatory loop. The water-hammer knocking arrhythmically through the pipes is not a fault. It is a pulse. Listen again and you will hear it is not regular precisely because no living pulse is, because it quickens in the heat and slows in the dim and answers to the same tide that moves the breath.

**It has a gut and a green skin.** The plants are not visitors to the machine; they are an organ of it. The chlorophyll layer — the aroids and reeds rooted in the lagging — is the photosynthetic tissue, taking the weak tea-coloured light and the engine's warmth and the water's richness and converting it into the slow sugar of growth. They grow, they rot, they feed the water that feeds them. A body without animals is not a body without life. It is a body that has dispensed with animals, having found them unnecessary. It does its own eating. It is autotroph and architecture at once.

## Why Nothing Flies, Nothing Swims

Now the absence resolves, and it is the worst part, so I will say it plainly.

Nothing else lives here because nothing else *can*. Not because the place is hostile — the water is warm and rich, it should be thick with fish, the air should sing with insects, the leaves should be chewed and the channels dimpled. Everything an animal needs is present. And nothing comes.

Because the Sump-Works does not permit a second order of life inside itself, any more than your bloodstream permits a fish. An organism does not host free creatures in its tissues; it hosts only its own cells, and it has a whole apparatus for ensuring that anything which is *not* itself is dissolved, absorbed, made into more of itself or made into nothing. The richness of the water is the richness of a digestive medium. A fish that swam in would not be eaten by a predator — there are no predators — it would simply be *taken up*, the way a splinter is taken up, quietly, without violence, without a ripple, into the slow green chemistry of the whole.

That is why the visitor waited for the splash and it never came. Not because nothing was there to splash. Because the Sump-Works is very old and very large and very patient, and over the long centuries of its filling and voiding it has metabolized everything that ever wandered in, every bird that crossed the low cloud and faltered, every insect that hatched in the warm and was absorbed before it could rise, until the basin reached the perfect sterility of a thing that has finally finished cleaning itself of all life but its own. The emptiness is not original. It is achieved. It is the emptiness of the inside of something.

## What It Is Doing

Nothing. This is the last truth and the hardest.

The visitor kept looking for the purpose — *for no reason anyone has been able to find* — keeping itself from drowning, pumping forever, going nowhere. He was right that there is no external purpose. There is no product. The pumps move water that immediately returns. The work accomplishes nothing because accomplishment is not what work is for, in a living thing.

You do not breathe in order to arrive somewhere. You do not pump blood toward a goal. The heart's whole project is to keep the heart beating so that the heart can keep beating. The Sump-Works pumps to stay above the water so that it can keep pumping to stay above the water, and this is not absurd, this is simply what it is to be alive — to spend every joule of yourself on the single unglamorous task of not yet stopping. The basin fills because the basin always fills. The pumps cannot keep up because nothing alive ever keeps up; you are always one breath ahead of drowning, your whole life long, and then once you are not.

The Sump-Works is a creature whose entire existence is the labor of remaining. It eats light and warmth and the occasional wanderer. It breathes once a day. It weeps continuously, without grief, the way a thing weeps that has glands and not feelings. And it works, ceaselessly, in furious motion and going nowhere, because going nowhere — staying, persisting, holding the line against the rising dark water by a single finger's width — is the only thing the living have ever done.

The visitor waded out through the reeds. He felt the pumps would not stop because there was no one to stop them.

He had it backwards. The pumps will not stop because something is determined not to die. He simply could not see it, being briefly, like a swallowed insect, inside.
