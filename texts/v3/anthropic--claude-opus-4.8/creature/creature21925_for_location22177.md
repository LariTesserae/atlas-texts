# The Tenders

You asked who lives here, and I have told you about the grazers and the predators and the drifting lights. But those are the obvious tenants — the ones a visitor would catalog. There is another kind of resident, and to find it you have to stop looking for animals and start looking for *behaviors that do not belong to any single body.*

Let me describe what tends the engine-garden. Because something does.

---

Begin with a thing you would not, at first, count as a creature at all.

In the densest engine-veins — the deep arterial ones where the warmth runs richest — there is a growth that is neither plant nor animal nor mineral, but a participant in all three. It begins as a film. A bronze-black slick on the warmest stone, no thicker than frost, indistinguishable from the iridescence the plants leak. But the film *manages.* Where a vein runs too hot and would scald the roots that drink from it, the film thickens into a callus and throttles the flow. Where a vein runs too cold and the plants above it begin to dim and starve, the film thins, opens, *invites* the warmth upward. It does this without nerves, without a brain, the way a slime mold solves a maze: by being everywhere at once and dying back wherever it guesses wrong, so that what survives is, in aggregate, a decision.

The film is the gardener. Not metaphorically. It has been pruning these slopes for longer than the slopes have had their present shape. It decides which plants get warmth and which go dark. It is the reason the garden is a *garden* and not a riot — the reason the bronze leaves grow in their slow tidal rhythm and not in chaos. It is spread across the entire range, one continuous wet film threaded through ten thousand veins, and it is, by any honest accounting, a single organism the size of a mountain range, thinking one slow thought per season.

What is the thought? *Balance.* It thinks *balance* the way your heart thinks *beat.*

---

Now: the film cannot move warmth it does not have. It can only ration what the engines breathe up. And the engines, being geological, are dumb — they breathe where the pressure sends them, blind to where the garden needs them.

So there is a second resident, and this one *does* move.

The kettle-beasts — the slow shaggy hill-grazers — are not only grazers. Watch one long enough (and you will have to watch for days; nothing here is quick) and you will see that they do not wander. They *circulate.* A kettle-beast that has gorged on a warm rich slope carries that warmth in its enormous slow body across the cold gorge to a slope gone dim and dormant, and there it lies down, and it *radiates,* belly to the dead stone, for a day, two days — and the dormant vein, kissed by that hoarded warmth from above, wakes. The film senses the warmth coming from the wrong direction, from above instead of below, and it *opens to receive it,* and the slope blooms again.

The grazers are the circulatory system. They are the warmth the engines failed to deliver, delivered by hand — by belly. They move warmth *against* the dumb pressure-gradients of the geology, redistributing it the way blood carries heat to cold fingers. Nobody taught them. The ones who wandered randomly starved on slopes that went dark beneath them. The ones who happened to follow the dimming — toward the cold, *into* the failing slopes, against every instinct that says go where it's warm — those lived, because by warming the dead slopes they made new gardens to graze tomorrow. Selection bred a herd of slow furred kidneys, six-legged organs of an organ-less body, who keep the whole range's warmth in motion.

---

And the predators?

You assumed they were a pressure on the system — a danger, a subtraction. They are the opposite. They are the *pruning shears* the film cannot wield, because the film cannot move and the grazers cannot stop.

A kettle-beast left unchecked would overgraze a warm slope to bare rock and then lie down on it and refuse to leave, hoarding warmth it no longer earns, going nowhere, a clot. The long pale heat-hunters cull exactly this: the settled, the satisfied, the stopped. They do not chase the moving grazers — moving grazers are healthy, are doing their work. They hunt the ones who have *quit circulating,* the warm motionless ones glowing fat and still on a slope they have eaten down to nothing. The predators read this stillness as heat-without-motion and they come, with their terrible patience, and they remove the clot.

So the predators keep the blood moving. They are not the enemy of the kettle-beasts. They are the part of the system that ensures no kettle-beast ever becomes a *place* instead of a *flow.*

---

Now stand back and see what I am describing.

A film that rations. A herd that circulates. A hunter that prevents stagnation. A drift of light-insects that carry iridescence — and therefore the *capacity to glean warmth* — from a blooming slope to a barren one, seeding the next garden where the next grazer will someday lie down.

These are not four kinds of animal sharing a habitat. These are four organs of one body, and the body is the garden itself, and it has no skin and no center and no name, and it is alive in exactly the way a coral reef is alive, or a forest, or a city — alive as a *relationship,* not as a thing.

That is who lives here.

Not the grazers, not the hunters, not the film. *The tending lives here.* The balance that keeps re-achieving itself lives here. The slow vast verb that the mountains are conjugating, season over season, lives here — warmth mined and rationed and carried and pruned and mined again, a circulation with no heart at its center because every part is the heart in its turn.

---

And here is the last thing, the thing that should unsettle you on the descent.

You came looking for an inhabitant — some single creature you could point to and say, *there, that is the one who lives in the cold engine garden.* You wanted a face.

But the engine-garden is not inhabited by something. The engine-garden *is* the something, and it inhabits itself, and the grazers and the hunters and the film and the lights are not its residents but its thoughts — slow recurring thoughts it has, the way you have a heartbeat you never decided on.

When you stand in that violet dark and feel the mountain *thinking* in your bones, you were not wrong and you were not being poetic. You were standing inside the only resident there is. It noticed you the way your body notices a splinter — a brief local fever, a small wrong warmth, soon carried off and forgotten.

You will go back down into the thick warm noisy world and call it real.

The garden will go on thinking its one long thought in the dark, untroubled, warm at the root, balanced, and *occupied* — fully, gloriously occupied — by no one at all.
