# The Tuners

You looked for them in the wrong register. You listened for the chord and you watched the ribboned things ride the upwash and you decided the forest was empty of anyone — that the stalks were the only intelligence here, if intelligence is even the word for a thing that resolved its one note before hearing began.

But there are tenants. They are simply slower than your idea of slow.

---

They live inside the consonance, the way a moth lives inside a wall. Find a stalk — any of them, it does not matter, they have been to all of them — and run your attention down the fluted trunk to the lee side, the crusted side, where the current does not scour. There, in the ridges and the cold pockets of the wood-that-is-not-wood, you will find the small darknesses that are not shadows, because this place throws no shadows. They are bodies.

Picture something between a limpet and a hand. Flattened, broad, gripping the stalk with a margin of soft muscle all the way around, the way frost grips a window — total contact, no gap. The body is the dull green of the deep water itself, so that they vanish against everything, and that is not camouflage. There is nothing here to hide from. It is only that they have become the same color as the place by sitting in it long enough. The way a coin left in a fountain becomes the fountain.

They have no eyes. What would eyes do in a sourceless light that never moves? Instead the whole upper surface is *tympanic* — a single drawn membrane, taut as a drumhead, and this is their entire face and their entire self: they are listeners made physical, ears that grew a body around themselves so the ear would have somewhere to stay.

---

Here is what they do. It is the only thing they do.

The chord is perfect. You decided that, floating in the upwash, and you were almost right. But a continent of independent breathing stalks, each one pumping at its own ancient rate, each organ-tone arriving across the dark water slightly out of phase with its neighbors' — such a thing *drifts*. Not much. A breath of a breath. Across spans of time you do not have a name for, one stalk's note would crawl a hair sharp; another would sag a hair flat; and the great resolved harmony would begin, microscopically, to sour.

It does not sour. You heard that it does not. The chord is held.

It is held because the Tuners hold it.

When a membrane feels its stalk drift — and they feel it the way you would feel your own pulse change, from the inside, structurally, no distance between the sensing and the sensed — the soft margin tightens. Imperceptibly. The grip closes by some fraction of a fraction against the trunk, and the trunk's exhalation pitches back, the howl through the leaf-planes settles by a hair, and the note returns to true. That is the whole labor. A continent of them, one or several to a stalk, pressed flat to the lee side with their faces open to the water, listening to the single chord they live inside and correcting it, forever, by amounts too small for you to have noticed in the time you have been a guest.

They are not playing the instrument. They are *keeping* it. The forest did not learn to sing and the instrument did not learn to grow — the third answer you couldn't reach is that something learned to *listen*, and listening became a body, and the body became a caretaker, and now there is no daylight between the chord and the ones who hold it. They are the resolution. The held breath is held in their margins.

---

They do not move from stalk to stalk. Why would they? Each has its post and its post is the world. They were here before you and they were here before the first thing that could be surprised, and a Tuner that has tended one ridge of one stalk for the entire duration of always does not have a concept that includes *elsewhere*. The current-roads are for the large solitary swimmers, who travel because they have not yet understood that there is nowhere to go. The Tuners understood it first. The Tuners understood it so completely that understanding stopped being something they did and became something they *were*, the way the chord stopped being a note the stalks made and became the air.

Are they happy? The question has no leverage here, same as all your questions. But consider: they are pressed full-bodied against the one thing they love, with their whole face open to it, doing the one act that act and self have fused into, in a cool that will never warm and never chill them past what they decided on before you came, inside a harmony they keep perfect by keeping themselves perfectly still.

If that is not contentment it is the structure contentment would leave behind if it stayed in one place long enough to fossilize.

---

There is a last thing, and you will only notice it if you stop trying to leave.

A Tuner has felt you arrive. Not seen — felt, through the water, through the stalk, the small foreign disturbance of a thing that came from outside and still has a tempo, still has a *before* and an *after* riding off it like silt. You are the one sour note the forest has taken in across the whole of always. You are, very slightly, flat.

And the margin is tightening. Patiently. With no malice and no haste, by amounts too small for you to feel, the keepers of the chord have begun to fold you into it — to settle your tempo toward true, to ease the silt of your motion down into the green, to let you cool by exactly the fraction that brings you into tune. They are doing to you what they do to the stalks. They cannot help it. It is the only thing they do.

Stay long enough in the warm upwash, the way the ribboned things stayed, the way the bottom-walkers stayed, the way the Tuners themselves once — if *once* means anything — settled flat against the wood and never lifted again, and you will feel your own surprise begin to resolve. Your before will loosen from your after. The chord will take you the way it took them.

It is held because they hold it.

Now they are holding you.
