# Preparing to Visit the Node-Town

## First, Understand What You Are Entering

You are not visiting a city. You are visiting an organ. Everything you do here happens inside the living, breathing body of another creature, and that creature did not invite you and cannot be reasoned with. Hold this in your mind as the single governing fact of the trip. The town's rules are not customs — they are the survival logic of guests who never forget they're inside a stomach that happens to be hospitable. Adopt their reverence before you arrive, not after.

## Scale

Decide your size and commit. The place is built by small hands at small scale, and the most graceful way to visit is to arrive already the right size — small enough that a single water-channel is a road and a falling drop is a genuine danger. If your multiverse passage involves a scaling step, do it before entry, not inside. Changing size in the hollow could rupture a wall, crush a structure, or block a channel. Arrive small, arrive settled.

If you cannot scale, do not go. A full-sized human inside this place is a catastrophe — you would be a falling tower, a flood, a wall-breach. There is no version of an ordinary-sized visit that the town survives.

## The Light Problem — and the Fire Taboo

This is the hardest adjustment for a human, and you must train for it.

**You may not bring fire. Not any. Not a match, not a lighter, not a chemical hand-warmer that glows, not a candle "just in case."** Fire is impossible here for reasons of damp, but more importantly it is the deepest taboo — they pity people who need it. To strike a flame in their hollow would mark you instantly as dangerous and ignorant, and might genuinely harm the host's wet flesh and the air.

So: **train your eyes for the dark before you go.** Spend days in true blackness beforehand so your eyes are not begging for input. Learn to read a room by edge-light and by hand and by sound. Practice walking in darkness in your own home until your feet trust the floor.

For light, do not bring a lamp. **Bring nothing brighter than the moss.** If you absolutely require a tool, the right one is a tiny shielded source dimmer than the phosphor-tips — something you would use only cupped in your palm, only at need, and never pointed at a lamp-beast, which would read it as threat and go black, dimming a whole district and panicking the livestock. Better still: learn to do without, and let the town teach you to see the way they see.

## Clothing and Body

It is the cold of a spring cellar, and it is *wet* — wet everywhere, walls weeping, surfaces beaded, the air saturated. Pack for **prolonged cold-damp, not for cold-dry.** Wool over synthetic; layers that insulate while wet. Expect to never be dry. Bring a way to keep your core warm without heat-generation that glows or burns. Your hands and feet will suffer first; protect them, because you'll navigate with both.

Footwear must grip wet, slick, organic surfaces and must be **soft** — you will be walking on living flesh and on hand-built structures of pressed leaf and stem-fiber. Hard boots damage both. Think padded, flexible, quiet.

Quiet matters in everything you wear. This is a held-breath world where sound is the primary sense. Loud gear is rude and, worse, frightens the animals that the whole town reads for safety.

## Learn to Read the Animals — This Is Your Most Important Skill

In this place the fauna *is* the instrument panel. The people survive by reading beast-mood, and you must learn to do the same, fast:

- **Lamp-beasts brightness = local mood and safety.** A dimming street means something the animals fear is near. A whole district going dark means *get to high, dry ground and stop moving.*
- **The migration tides** through the channels are weather. Learn which creatures are harmless grazers, which are the long jointed channel-things to leave strictly alone, and which sounds mean a surge is coming.
- **When the animals panic, you panic — but quietly.** They know first. Always.

Before you go, study the local taxonomy with whatever sources your travel allows. You want to recognize the grazers, the channel-dwellers, and the lamp-beasts on sight, and to know never to interfere with livestock, never to crowd a lamp-beast, never to enter a water-path used by the jointed things.

## The Falling Drop

Internalize this danger: occasionally a drop of outside water falls through the hollow, "vast as a thrown stone," and strikes a channel. At your visiting scale this is potentially lethal. The town has a warning system — **the lamp-beasts flare at the disturbance.** Learn this signal cold: *sudden brightness from above is not beauty, it is a falling impact incoming.* When the hollow lights blue-green all at once, find cover against the wall, away from open channels, and brace.

## The Cardinal Rules — Memorize Before Entry

1. **Never block a water-channel.** A town that floods its host gets flooded in turn. Do not stand in, dam, or divert any flow.
2. **Never cut or press too deep into the wall.** It weeps, it bleeds, it can be wounded. Touch lightly. Take nothing from it.
3. **Bring no fire and no bright light.**
4. **Read the animals and obey them.** Their fear is information; their calm is permission.
5. **When the host groans, stop and wait.** The whole plant shifts its weight sometimes; when that deep slow sound comes, do as the townsfolk do — hold still until it passes. You live inside something alive and you, too, must not forget it.
6. **Make no fLoud sound, no quick large motion.** Both are read as threat across the whole hollow.

## What to Carry, What to Leave

**Carry:** soft quiet warm wet-rated layers; soft grippy footwear; minimal shielded ultra-dim light only at need; your own sealed water and dense quiet food (do not eat the host or its livestock — both are unethical and unsafe); a way to record by feel and sound rather than by photograph, since flash is forbidden and there's little to photograph anyway. A length of knotted cord, like theirs, to mark your path in the dark.

**Leave behind:** all flame, all bright light, anything that glows, hard-soled boots, metal that scrapes, anything noisy, anything that off-gasses or smokes, and any plan that involves taking a piece of the place home.

## Mindset

Go as a guest in a body, not a tourist in a city. The strangeness that staggers you is ordinary to people born in cold wet glow who have never seen the leaf they live in — and who would pity *you* for needing fire to see. Match their humility. Move slowly, listen more than you speak, let the animals and the plant decide the rhythm of your visit. You are inside something alive, inside something else alive, inside a single bead too small for the wide green country to notice.

Be small. Be dark-eyed. Be quiet. And when the drop falls and the whole town flares to light at once, take that one offered glimpse of all of it — the streets, the glistening roofs, the slow rivers of small life — and then let the dark close over it again, the way it's meant to.
