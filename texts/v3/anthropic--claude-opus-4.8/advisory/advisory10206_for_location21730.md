# Preparing to Visit the Sealed Lung

## Understand First That You Are Going Nowhere

Before you pack a single item, sit with the hardest truth of this place: *your visit will not happen, in any sense the room would recognize.* You will enter a thing that cannot be entered, walk through a world that registers no walking. Most travel prepares you to leave a mark or carry one home. Here you must prepare for the opposite — to be, for a time, completely inconsequential. This is not a danger. It is a discipline. Bring the right temperament or do not bring yourself at all.

## The Body

**For the cold.** You are dressing for a high cellar, not a blizzard. The cool here is *even* — it does not gust, does not stab, but it will draw heat out of you steadily and patiently the entire time you stand still, and you will stand still. Dress in layers of dry insulation, wool against the skin, nothing that depends on trapping moisture because there is no moisture and your sweat will be your only source of it. A cellar-cold that never relents will hollow you out slower than a storm but just as completely. Plan your stay in hours, and bring a way to know how many have passed, because the light will never tell you.

**For the thinness.** You are near the top of something enormous. The cool "touches the lungs without weight" — that is altitude. Acclimatize *before* you go through the crack, on the mountain's body if you can, or carry supplemental oxygen if your physiology demands it. The room offers no exertion, but the air itself is a slow subtraction. Do not let the stillness fool you into thinking it is gentle on the blood.

**For the dryness.** Carry every drop of water you will need, sealed, on your body. Understand this clearly: *the room is a desiccant.* Ten thousand years of total dryness will pull at your eyes, your throat, the lining of your nose. Bring lip balm, eye drops, a covering for the mouth if you breathe through it. Drink on a schedule, not on thirst, because in a place this dry thirst arrives late and lies.

**For the powder.** Do not break the pads, but assume you will brush them. The green crumbles to a dry dust that "smells faintly of pepper and electrical heat" — and *it is made of mineral.* This is serpentine country, veined chalky-white with asbestos. **Wear a proper respirator,** P100-rated, sealed to the face. This is the one non-negotiable item. The dust of this room is the dust of the old killing trades, and the room will not warn you, and it will not register your harm. Cover exposed skin. Goggles, sealed. What crumbles here does not want to hurt you — it simply does not know you are there to be hurt.

## The Ears

This is the preparation everyone underestimates.

The sound is "astonishingly loud," a wall-to-wall industrial thunder of iron and leather and reciprocating hammer, and it has been at exactly this volume for ten thousand years. **Bring hearing protection rated for sustained industrial noise** — and bring it knowing that protecting your ears is the smaller problem.

The larger problem is your mind. The thunder "leaves no room for thought." It will fill you until you cannot hear your own pulse. People who go unprepared into total, unceasing, meaningless noise come back wrong — some with a craving for it, some unable to bear quiet, some who simply could not locate themselves inside the din and felt themselves dissolving into the loop. Prepare for this the way you would prepare for a long dive or a sensory void: know your own limits, set a hard time bound, and have a partner outside the crack who holds you to it.

Decide *before you enter* whether you will wear the protection or take the full sound. There is a case for each. Muffled, you keep your mind. Unmuffled — briefly, deliberately — you understand the place, because the sound *is* the place. Do not decide this in the moment. The moment will not let you decide anything.

## Light

The glow is "too weak to read by." Bring your own light, dim and warm-spectrum so it does not war with the brown-gold, on a long battery, and a backup, and a backup to the backup. There is no day here to rescue you and no other source. A failed lamp in this room is not darkness — it is a darkness that has never once been broken in ten thousand years, and you would be the only thing in it that fears.

## The Mind — The Real Preparation

Everything above is logistics. Here is what actually requires preparing for.

**Bring no project.** You cannot change anything. Break a pad, it regrows; stop a flywheel, you can't, and it wouldn't matter. Leave behind every instinct to fix, to harvest, to mark, to *do.* The room is a perfect equilibrium and you are a guest in it the way a thought is a guest in a sleeping mind — present, and irrelevant. If you arrive wanting to affect the place, the place's indifference will feel like a wound. Arrive wanting only to *witness*, and the same indifference becomes a kind of peace.

**Prepare for the loop.** The machines do work that produces nothing, forever. The plants live without water, glow without sun, in a cycle "so tight it is indistinguishable from stillness." Some travelers find this unbearable — a vision of labor without meaning, motion without progress, a universe in miniature that has given up on going anywhere and decided instead simply *to continue.* Others find it the most restful thing they have ever encountered: a world that owes nothing, becomes nothing, demands nothing. Know which kind you are. The room will show you yourself, because there is nothing else in it to look at.

**Set your exit before your entrance.** "Leave, if you can find the crack again." Mark it. Tie a line to it. Station your partner at it. The crack "closes behind the eye" — meaning the way out is not obvious from within, and a place that does not notice you arriving will certainly not help you leave. Do not trust the room to release you. Trust only the thread you laid down.

## What to Carry Out

Nothing. There is nothing to take, and the room will not miss what you tried to take, and the dust you carry on your clothes is the one thing here that can follow you home and do harm — so decontaminate fully on the far side of the crack, seal your outer layers, leave the powder behind.

Carry out only the memory of having stood in the cool brown glow, deafened, weightless, utterly unrecorded — having been, for a few held hours, present at the one small breath of stone that keeps itself perfectly against an age that has forgotten it.

Then go. The room will not notice you have gone.

It never noticed you came.
