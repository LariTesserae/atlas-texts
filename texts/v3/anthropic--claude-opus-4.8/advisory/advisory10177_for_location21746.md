# Preparing for the Tarn of Engines

## Understand What You Are Walking Into First

Before any gear, before any logistics, sit with one fact: this is not a place that wants you, threatens you, or notices you. The danger here is not predation or weather. It is *entrainment* — the slow synchronization of a small living thing to a rhythm vastly older and larger than itself. People do not die at the Tarn of Engines. They *fall into time* with it and forget to leave. Pack for that before you pack for the cold.

---

## Thermal Preparation

The cold is the first practical problem and the most deceptive one. It is not aggressive cold — there is no wind, no storm front, no biting edge. It is *settled* cold, the cold of a thing that has stopped expecting warmth. This makes it more dangerous, not less, because it does not announce itself. You will not shiver dramatically. You will simply lose heat into the rock and the standing water at a steady, mortal rate, and the stillness of the place will make this feel acceptable.

- **Layer for prolonged immersion, not for activity.** You will move slowly here whether you intend to or not. Insulation that depends on movement-generated heat will fail you.
- **Waterproof everything from the ankle down, and assume it will fail anyway.** The water never rises past your ankle and never recedes. You will be standing in a cold film the entire time. Bring more than one pair of dry insulated socks, sealed separately, and a hard discipline about changing them.
- **A heat source you can feel against your skin** — a chemical hand-warmer, a body-worn battery element — matters less for warmth than as a *reference point*. It reminds your body that it is a separate system with its own temperature. This is more important than it sounds.

---

## Auditory Defense — The Most Critical Preparation

The engine-rhythm comes up through the stone, through your heels, through your bones. You cannot block it; it is not airborne sound you can muffle. But you can refuse to let it *capture* you.

- **Carry a counter-rhythm.** A metronome set to a tempo that does *not* divide evenly into the engine-stroke. An off-beat. Something that keeps your nervous system arguing with the world instead of agreeing with it. The local fauna all move on the downbeat — predator, prey, the breathing barnacles, the swimming things. The moment you start moving on the downbeat too, you have begun to become exhaust.
- **A second traveler is worth more than any equipment.** Two people can hold each other to *human* time — a conversation, a shared task, a disagreement. Solo visitors to entraining places are the ones who do not come back, not because something takes them but because nothing does, and they stop minding.
- **Set hard external alarms** that are tied to absolute time, not felt time. Your sense of duration will dissolve. "I'll stay until I've seen the schools surface a few more times" is the thought of someone already lost. Set a clock. Obey the clock. Leave on the clock.

---

## On Stillness — The Trap Disguised as Rest

Everything about this place encourages stillness. The cold encourages it. The rhythm encourages it. The dim shadowless light, which never promises a dawn, encourages it. The text of the place itself ends with an invitation: *Stand still in the dim. Become one more thing it has made.*

**Do not accept this invitation.**

- **Keep a task.** Bring something that requires your hands and attention on a human cadence — sampling, sketching, note-taking by hand. The act of *making your own marks* is a small declaration that you are a separate process, not part of this one.
- **Move with deliberate irregularity.** Vary your pace on purpose. Stop and start arbitrarily. Walk against the downbeat even when it feels awkward — *especially* when it feels awkward, because the awkwardness is the feeling of you remaining yourself.
- **Notice when stillness starts to feel like rightness.** That sensation of *fitting in*, of having found the natural condition of things being — that is the precise moment to move, speak, eat, or leave.

---

## Provisions and the Discipline of Eating

You will not be hungry here. The place does not run on your kind of metabolism, and standing in its rhythm tends to suppress ordinary appetite. This is a symptom, not a convenience.

- **Eat on a schedule by the clock, not by hunger.** Eating is a profoundly individual, self-asserting act. A traveler who stops eating here is a traveler who has stopped maintaining the boundary between *self* and *site*.
- **Bring food that requires preparation** — something to unwrap, assemble, warm. The small ritual of feeding yourself is an anchor.
- **Do not consume anything local.** The water is coolant. The fungal mats are corrosion. The swimming things are product, waste, or attendant — the place has stopped distinguishing these categories, and you should not let it start counting you among them.

---

## What to Watch and What to Refuse to Watch

- **You may observe the surfacing schools, the respiring barnacle-things, the grazing forms at the edge of seeing.** This is among the great sights of any world — an entire ecology choreographed by a single buried pulse.
- **But do not try to perceive the scale-shift.** If you stand long enough, the truth resolves: the stones are piston-heads, the plain is the working face of a single machine larger than the curve of the world. This is the deepest part of the trap. Once you *see* it, the felt difference between yourself and the apparatus begins to collapse, and the question of whether you are a visitor or one more exhaled creature stops having an answer. **Some knowledge is a door that only opens inward.** If you feel the scale beginning to turn over in your mind, look at your own hands, say your own name, and walk.

---

## Departure Protocol

This is the part most travelers under-plan, because the place makes leaving feel unnecessary.

- **Decide your departure before you arrive, and write it down somewhere you will read.** Not "when I'm ready." A fixed condition. The Tarn never gives a reason to leave because it has no opinion about you at all.
- **Leave while you still find the cold uncomfortable.** Comfort with this place is the warning sign. The moment the cold feels like "the natural condition of things being" rather than an imposition on you, your departure window is already closing.
- **If you came with a companion, leave on the first one's signal, not the last one's reluctance.** Whoever is more eager to stay is the one further gone.

---

## A Final Word

You can visit the Tarn of Engines and return whole. People have. The place is not malevolent — it is *indifferent at a scale that masquerades as peace*. What it offers is the only ending a world can have that refuses absolutely to end: to be kept in time, eyeless, on schedule, at home.

Go to see it. Stand on the wet round stones once and feel the stroke come up through your heels. Let it move you, for *one* beat, so you understand what it is offering.

Then break the rhythm, and go home, where things still expect to be warm.
