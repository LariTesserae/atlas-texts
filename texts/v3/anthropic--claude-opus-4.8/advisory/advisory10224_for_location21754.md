# Preparing to Visit the High Screaming Place

## Understand What You Are Walking Into

This is not a hostile world in the way a furnace or an acid sea is hostile. It will not kill you quickly. It will kill you slowly, by subtraction — taking your warmth, your breath, your water, your sense of orientation, one thin layer at a time, while presenting itself as merely beautiful and strange. The danger here is that the place *looks* survivable and feels almost holy. Prepare for the slow theft, not the sudden blow.

## The Body: Altitude First

Everything begins with the thinness of the air, because that single fact governs all the others.

- **Acclimatize before you arrive, if your crossing allows it.** Spend days at progressive heights. If your multiverse transit drops you straight onto the spine, you will arrive with a body that thinks it is at sea level and a world that disagrees violently. Expect headache, nausea, the heart laboring, sleep that won't come or won't hold.
- **Carry supplemental oxygen** — not for normal walking, but for the night, for emergencies, for the moment your judgment starts to soften without your noticing. Altitude makes you stupid before it makes you sick, and stupidity is what gets you on a place this featureless.
- **Bring acetazolamide or its analog** and know the symptoms of pulmonary and cerebral edema. If your companion stops making sense, or you do, you descend. There is no negotiating with the altitude.
- **Move at the pace the air permits.** The text is right: the world wants you slow. Fight that and it will drop you. Plan distances at a third of what your legs could do at home.

## The Light: Treat It as Radiation, Not Illumination

The sun here does not warm — it *exposes*. That scoured white-blue sky has thin atmosphere and probably little ozone screen between you and whatever burns down.

- **Glacier-grade eye protection**, wraparound, with side shields. The clear pools, the pale stone, the high sun — this is a snow-blindness environment even without snow. Bring a spare pair. Losing your eyes here is losing everything.
- **Cover all skin.** Not for warmth alone but against the burn. Long sleeves, gloves, a brimmed and draped hat, a buff over the face. The shadows are "the only relief the eye gets" — assume the same is true for your skin.
- **High-SPF, physical-block sunscreen** on anything exposed, reapplied. The undersides of your nose and chin will burn from light bouncing off the rock.

## The Cold That Hides Inside the Light

The stone keeps the night in its body all day. The sun gives light and not heat. This is the trap: it looks bright and feels deadly cold, and the two sensations confuse the body's instincts.

- **Layer for a cold that does not announce itself.** Wicking base, insulating mid, wind shell. The wind has been carving these mountains since before witnesses — assume it cuts.
- **Insulate yourself from the ground and the rock.** Do not sit on stone, do not sleep on it bare. A closed-cell pad, always. The rock will pull your warmth out through any contact.
- **Plan for the failing of the bright** — the night here, in thin dry air, will be brutally cold and the stars will be merciless and magnificent. A four-season sleeping system, minimum.

## Water: It Is There, but It Hides

- **Know the seep-lines and the meltwater threads.** Water exists — morning condensation, thin braids through the scree, the sky-colored pools — but it is "rationed" like the plants. Carry more than you think you need and refill at every clear source.
- **The pools are clear to their stone bottoms, but clear is not clean.** Filter and treat everything. A multiverse traveler has no immunity to this world's microbes, and there will be some, fed by the same insects that feed the birds.
- **Insulate your water against the cold** so it does not freeze and so drinking it does not chill you to the core. The meltwater is "cold enough to ache."
- **Dehydration is invisible at altitude** — the dry air pulls water from your breath and skin without your feeling thirsty. Drink on a schedule, not on thirst.

## The Sound: Prepare Your Mind for the Wall

This is the part no ordinary checklist covers, and it is the part most likely to unmake you.

The din is constant, total, and *unchanging*. A hundred thousand birds screaming into a single held chord that never crests and never breaks. Your brain will, after an hour, file it as a kind of silence — but it is not silence, and the dissonance between what your eyes report (emptiness, stillness) and what your ears report (frenzy, multitude) is a quiet psychological assault.

- **Bring ear protection** — not to seal it out entirely, which is dangerous, but to take the edge off. Custom musician's plugs that lower volume without muffling are ideal. You may need to remove them to hear a companion shout, then replace them. Carry several.
- **Establish nonverbal communication with anyone you travel with.** Hand signals, a written agreement of meanings, a slate. You will read lips out of necessity. Decide your signals for *stop, danger, descend, I am unwell* before you ever step onto the stone.
- **Accept that the sound is permanent.** Do not wait for it to stop, do not listen for a change that signals something. There will be none. The colony does not sleep in shifts you can hear. Make peace with the wall before you arrive, or it will work on you from underneath.
- **Watch for the slow dissociation.** When the eye says nothing-moves and the ear says everything-screams, the mind looks for a third reference point to resolve the contradiction. Be that reference point for yourself: a watch, a checklist, a planned route, a journal entry every hour. Routine is your anchor against a place that offers none.

## Navigation: There Is Nothing to Navigate By

No walls, no paths, no smoke, no made thing in any direction. The land reads as flat until your legs disagree. Distance lies — forty miles of visibility flattens scale and erases waypoints.

- **Do not trust your eyes for distance or for flat.** What looks like a level field is shattered rock that will turn an ankle.
- **Carry redundant navigation** that does not depend on this world's stars or fields: inertial backup, a recorded trail of your own track, marked turnaround points. You may not be able to read this sky.
- **Mark your transit point obsessively.** In a world with not one built thing, your way home is a spot of unremarkable stone among ten thousand identical ones. Record its location by every means you have. Losing it is fatal.
- **Set a turnaround time and obey it without argument.** The altitude that softens your judgment will also whisper that you have more time and strength than you do.

## A Quiet Word on Why You Came

Bring something to write with, or to make a mark. This is a place that has reached its settled form — it will look almost exactly like this for ten thousand years. You are a moment passing through a near-permanence. The honest preparation is partly logistical and partly this: arrive knowing you are a brief, warm, breathing thing crossing the roof of a world that does not need you and will not remember you, under a sky that screams its single chord over a silence so wide it has its own weather.

Pack light. Move slow. Protect the eyes, the lungs, the warmth, the mind. And do not stay longer than the place allows — for it allows very little, and gives its beauty freely only because it has nothing to fear from you.
