# Preparing to Visit the Cistern Gardens of the Upper Manifold

## First, Understand What You're Breathing

The single most important thing to internalize before you go: **the water is not water, and you will not drown, but every instinct you have will insist otherwise.**

The medium here is described as "thinner than water should be, an in-between substance" — a fluid your lungs can process. This is the crux of your preparation, and it is more psychological than physical. Your body knows, at a level beneath thought, that submersion means death. You will arrive fully immersed, with no transition, no surface you crossed. The first thirty seconds will be a war between what your eyes report (you are underwater) and what your lungs report (you are, impossibly, fine).

**Train for this before you go.** If you have access to any breathable-liquid simulation, even partial, use it. If not, practice the next-best thing: slow controlled breathing under load, the kind freedivers use to override the panic reflex. The danger here is not the medium. The danger is that your animal brain will try to bolt for a surface that does not exist, and there is nowhere to bolt *to*. You cannot break out into air. You are inside the air, and the air is the water.

Do not bring breathing apparatus. It will fight the medium, and the medium is trying to keep you alive. Trust it. This is the hardest instruction I will give you, and it must be the first thing you accept.

## Movement and the Problem of Density

The place is **packed to saturation** — there is no clear span of open water. You will not "swim" through it the way you'd imagine. You will *part* it: curtain after curtain of suspended green, each closing softly behind you.

Implications:

- **Wear nothing that snags.** No loose straps, no trailing cords, no buckles, no fringe. The brass lattice is fine, the tendrils are everywhere, and the vines have grown *through* the structure until you cannot tell plant from architecture. A caught strap underwater, in a place with no surface and no one to help you, is a real hazard. Smooth, close-fitting garments. Sealed seams. Think wetsuit, not robe.

- **Gloves, thin but tough.** The metalwork is described as *new* — "edges still sharp, seams still bright." Fresh-cast bronze and brass keep their burrs. You will be pulling yourself along these surfaces constantly because there is nowhere to walk and the medium offers little resistance to push against. Sharp seams plus exposed palms equals slow-clouding wounds you'll watch drift away from you in the still water.

- **Expect to move slowly and by hand.** There is no current to swim with or against. You'll travel by gripping lattice, pillar, and the thicker vines — many of which have wound around the slow-turning shafts. Be aware: *some of those shafts are moving.* A vine you grab for purchase might be riding a gear-train. Test every handhold's stability before you commit your weight.

## On the Machines That Do the Animals' Work

There are no animals here — but the machines have been built **in the shape of every absent creature's function.** Brass armatures move pollen. Mechanical fingers prune. These devices are doing biological work on a fixed schedule, and they do not know you are there.

- The pruning armatures are designed to cut plant matter. They will not distinguish your hand from a frond. **Keep clear of any moving mechanical assembly**, especially the "mechanical fingers." This is not a hostile place, but it is an *indifferent* one — a machine built to snip will snip whatever is in its arc.

- The pollinators move "tip to tip" on patient cycles. They are slow but persistent. Watch for the rhythm; everything here runs on cycles too gradual to perceive at first. Spend your early time simply *observing* the timing before you move freely.

- The **mechanical irises in the high windows** ration the light. The illumination here will change without warning as the irises open and close. Don't be caught in a sudden dimming and lose your bearings in the green-black moss. Bring no light of your own that might confuse the system, but do learn the place's light-rhythm before you trust it.

## Temperature: A Gift, and a Warning

The vessel is held at **blood-warm**, "the temperature of something kept alive on purpose." This is comfortable — but it is the temperature of an incubator, and warm standing water is the natural home of things that grow.

- Whatever the medium is, it is rich enough to feed an entire ecology. **Do not assume it is sterile.** Seal any wounds before entry. Do not introduce open mucous membranes carelessly — keep your eyes protected with a thin clear seal if you can manage one that the medium won't fog. The pollen drifts everywhere and turns past your face constantly. You will be breathing it, in some sense, in this medium. Consider what an unknown world's reproductive matter does in a human respiratory system, and decide whether the visit is worth it. (I think it may be. But decide knowingly.)

- The even warmth means **you will lose track of time.** Nothing here marks the hours except the slow machine-cycles. Bring an independent timekeeper, sealed and waterproof, and *check it deliberately.* This is a place that runs its seasons across what feels like a held breath. People who linger in places like this do not always notice the lingering.

## The Silence — and Why It Matters Most

Everything here is **nearly silent.** The water swallows sound. The great engines turning, the pumps cycling a season's worth of water, all of it softened to "a felt vibration in the chest." You will hear your own pulse louder than the machinery that runs the world.

This silence is the real test of the place. It is described as *sacred rather than merely mechanical* — and that is the danger to your mind, not your body.

- **The silence will work on you.** A warm, suspended, soundless world with no other voices, no animals, no calls across the space — this is sensory deprivation dressed as a garden. People react to that in different ways. Some find profound peace. Some find a creeping dread, the specific terror of being the only thing in a place that edited out every voice. Know which kind of person you are before you arrive, and set a hard time limit either way.

- **Communication is nearly impossible.** If you travel with a companion (and you should — no one should hang alone in a world that swallows sound and has no surface), agree on **touch-signals and visual signs before entry.** You will not be able to call out. A grip on the forearm, a count of taps, a hand-sign for *come*, for *stop*, for *I need to leave* — establish these in advance. Stay within arm's reach more often than feels necessary.

- **The vibration is your only sense of the machinery.** Learn to feel it in your chest. When a gear "seats home with a sound you feel more than hear," that's information about the system's rhythm. Pay attention to it. A change in the felt vibration may be your only warning of a cycle beginning.

## What to Bring, What to Leave

**Bring:**
- Close-fitting, smooth, sealed clothing
- Thin tough gloves
- A sealed, independent timekeeper
- Eye protection that won't fog
- A companion, and a pre-arranged silent signal vocabulary
- Pre-treated, sealed skin — close every cut before you go

**Leave behind:**
- All breathing apparatus (trust the medium)
- Anything that trails, snags, or buckles
- Any expectation of a surface, an exit you can swim *up* to, or a way to call for help by voice
- The reflex to fight the water — you must arrive having already surrendered it

## A Final Word on the Nature of the Place

This is a **reliquary** — a held thing, possibly abandoned, possibly only just finished, and somehow both. The metal is new; the growth is total; both are true and neither resolves. You are entering a system that was built to run *without anyone watching it.* It does not need you. It will not adjust for you. It will go on seating its gears and turning its pollen tip to tip whether you are inside it or not.

Move through it the way you would move through someone else's held breath: gently, briefly, parting the green and letting it close behind you, leaving the made and breathing dark exactly as you found it.

Then go. Before the warmth and the silence convince you to stay, and the place edits your voice out too.
