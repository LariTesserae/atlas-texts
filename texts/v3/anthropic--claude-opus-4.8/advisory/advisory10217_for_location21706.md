# Preparing to Visit the Cistern Beneath the Step

## Understanding What You Are Walking Into

Before anything practical, you must understand the central danger, which is not drowning, and not cold, and not the dark. The danger is *the law of the pocket*. This place keeps what comes in. It holds what is held. And it holds time — specifically, it holds the moment of entry, freshening it endlessly, so that a visitor risks becoming a permanent fixture not because they are trapped behind a door but because the door keeps becoming new, the arrival keeps becoming present.

You are not preparing to survive a hostile environment. You are preparing to *leave one that has no intention of letting you experience having left.* Every piece of preparation below bends toward that single problem.

## The Problem of the Entrance

There is no way in. The text is clear and you must believe it: the field has been a field for two hundred years, the stair is gone, you would not find it by digging. The cistern is reached not by route but by *not asking*. This is the first and strangest piece of preparation:

- **Do not seek it directly.** A held breath persists by not being asked about. You arrive at the cistern the way you arrive at a forgotten name — sideways, while attending to something else. Practiced multiverse travelers describe this as "transit by inattention." Fix your mind firmly on a *different* destination, something mundane and concrete, and let the cistern be the thing your peripheral attention slips into.
- **Bring a decoy errand.** A reason to be in the field. Surveying, walking, looking for a lost dog. Something that gives your conscious mind a task while the rest of you falls through the gap beneath the stair.

## The Critical Tool: An External Anchor of Departure

Because the cistern endlessly refreshes the memory of *arriving*, you cannot rely on your own mind to know whether you have left. Your subjective sense of time will collapse into the first cool shocked second forever. Therefore:

- **Bring a return-mechanism that does not depend on your memory or your judgment.** A timed device. A tether held by a partner who stays *outside* the field, in ordinary time, who will pull at a fixed external interval regardless of what you signal. You will not be a reliable reporter of your own state. Surrender your authority over the question "have I been here long enough?" to someone who is not subject to the pocket's law.
- **The tether itself is in danger.** *What comes in does not leave.* A rope you bring in may be subject to the holding. Use something that stays partly *outside* — anchored in the field, in normal time, never fully crossing the waterline. The rope's far end must remain in the world of erosion and sun. This is your lifeline precisely *because* it refuses to fully enter.

## Physical Preparation

- **Cold-water exposure.** The water is the temperature of a cellar in early spring — never warm, never frozen. Cold enough to steal breath and slow the hands within minutes. A wetsuit or drysuit, hooded, with thermal protection rated for prolonged immersion. You will not feel time passing; assume you have been cold far longer than you think.
- **Air.** There is a hand's width of air at the ceiling, but it tastes of mineral and old lime and something green — do not trust it. Bring your own supply, self-contained, redundant. Do not breathe the air-gap. Things that the cistern *keeps* may include what enters your lungs.
- **Buoyancy is unreliable.** This is the part no diver's training covers. *The water does not agree on which way is down.* Your weight belt, your buoyancy compensator, the bubbles from your regulator — none of these will behave. Bubbles may fall. Silt rests on the ceiling. You cannot orient by which way things drift. **Practice orienting by touch and by tether-tension alone.** Run a line and follow the line. Trust the rope's pull toward the world, not your inner ear, which the pocket will lie to.

## Light

Bring strong light, and expect it to fail you in a particular way — not by dying, but by being *swallowed*. This is a place that measures barely above nothing and gives nothing back. The lilies hold a little; the weed returns a little. Your lamp will work, but the darkness will feel undiminished, as though the light is being *kept* like everything else.

- Bring more light than you think reasonable, and a small backup that lives against your skin.
- Be prepared for the dark to teach you to see *its* way, by patience, like braille. Do not stay long enough for this lesson. If your eyes are learning to read the gloom, you have already overstayed.

## What To Bring, What To Leave, What To Touch

The walls are entirely upholstered — leaf over root over filament, packed like a held crowd, like a tin never meant to be opened. **Do not disturb the growth.** You are not the first thing this place has held, and the density of life is the visible shape of its law. Touch the chisel-marks if you must — they are cold and exact and unsoftened by two centuries, and feeling them is the truest way to understand where you are — but understand that you are touching something that has refused time. Do not let it teach your fingers to refuse it too.

**Bring nothing you cannot bear to leave.** What comes in does not leave. Assume every object you carry across the waterline may become permanent. The dropped pebble that sank, then declined the journey, then reappeared dry on the surface — that is the behavior of *things* here. Carry only what is fixed to your tether, fixed to your body, fixed to your way out.

**Do not pick the lily.** It blooms across a season and closes across a season, white as a drowned thumbnail. It is the slowest clock in the room and it is not yours.

**Do not seek the newt.** It has been here longer than the absence of the house. Let it graze. It is the place's tenant; you are not.

## The Single Most Important Discipline

You must hold, in some part of yourself the pocket cannot freshen away, the *knowledge that you arrived from somewhere and are going back.* The cistern will try to make your entry the only moment that exists — held and held and held. Against this:

- **Carry a token of the future, not the past.** Travelers who carry mementos of where they came from feed the cistern's appetite for arrival. Instead carry a thing that points *forward* — a key to a door you have not yet opened, a letter you have promised to deliver, a debt you have sworn to pay tomorrow. Some unfinished obligation in the world of ordinary time that *pulls* you out of the eternal first second.
- **Speak your intention to leave before you enter, aloud, to your outside partner.** Make leaving a thing that was already decided in the world of erosion, by someone who is not in the pocket. Then it is not subject to the cistern's law of the held moment, because it happened *before*.

## Final Counsel

Stay briefly. The place changes over generations; you have no business measuring its slow patient dynamic against your single visit. You came to feel the held breath in the throat of the ground, to taste the green air without breathing it, to touch a chisel-mark that the centuries forgot to dull. That is enough. That is the whole gift of the place.

Then let the rope pull, in the world's time, not yours. Follow the line you laid. Do not look back to confirm you are leaving — the looking is the asking, and the asking is how the cistern keeps things.

Go up, into the field that has been a field for two hundred years, into the warm rotted ordinary world where stone softens and water knows which way to fall, and be grateful, every cool spring afterward, that you were one of the few things the cistern beneath the step decided, quietly and without anyone's permission, to let go.
