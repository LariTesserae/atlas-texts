# Preparing to Visit the Xerophyte City

## Understand the Core Problem First

Before anything else, internalize the single defining truth of this place: **you are the anomaly.** Every other living thing here has spent a hundred million years learning not to need what you cannot stop needing. You will arrive trailing water — in your breath, your sweat, your blood, the vapor off your skin — into a world that treats water the way our world treats a stranger carrying gold through a starving city. You are not just a tourist. You are a moving reservoir in a place that has made an art of thirst.

Plan around this. It shapes everything.

## Water and the Body

**Bring a closed-cycle system.** Open canteens are amateur. You want a sealed hydration suit or at minimum a respirator-condenser that reclaims the moisture from your own exhalations and sweat. This serves two purposes:

1. It extends your supply in a place with no resupply whatsoever.
2. It minimizes your *signature*. The less water you leak into the air and onto the substrate-floor, the less you announce yourself to a biosphere — and an intelligence — that registers moisture the way we register fire alarms.

Carry far more water than you think you need, sealed and redundant. There is no spring here, no dew, no ice. The land has answered the riddle of waterlessness, and the answer does not include you.

**Mind the ammonia.** The "thin alkaline vapor" that the plants metabolize is not breathable for you in concentration, and it pools in the low places — "shimmers in the low places." Bring an alkaline-rated filter mask and a simple gas detector. Stay on the high ground of the plazas. Do not descend into the haze-filled depressions out of curiosity; that's where the concentration climbs, and that's where the tenders root deepest.

## Climate and Clothing

A steady **6°C** with no warmth, ever, and a constant dry wind that sings through everything. This is not lethal cold, but it is *relentless* cold — the kind that wears you down over hours. Dress for a long, chilly, windy day that never ends and never warms:

- **Insulated layers**, wind-blocking outer shell.
- The air is bone-dry, so your skin, eyes, and mucous membranes will desiccate fast. **Eye protection, lip balm, barrier creams.** Expect cracked skin and chapped lungs if you neglect this.
- The dryness will steal your water through your skin even without sweating. Factor this into supply.

## Hearing and the Sound

The sound is **enormous** — organ-drone, detonating seed-pods like gunfire, the groan of shifting substrate, the tidal rush of chemistry through the walls. Two needs here:

- **Active hearing protection** that dampens the constant drone and the percussive blasts while still letting you hear nearby sound. The seed-pod detonations are sudden, irregular, and loud enough to damage hearing and to startle you into a fall. Treat them like working near artillery.
- Accept that **normal speech is impossible**. Bring a way to communicate that doesn't rely on voice — written boards, hand signals, a closed-circuit comm system inside your mask. Brief your party before entering. You will not be able to shout a warning and be heard.

## Footing and Movement

The substrate-floor "gives slightly, then firms, the way a callus does." Walk it like you'd walk a vast living membrane — because it is one, and it is listening.

- **Quiet, low-pressure boots.** You do not want to damage the outermost living film; the grazers feed on it, and harming it is both rude and conspicuous to a city that feels through its floor.
- The floor *shifts its own weight* with a groan. Be ready for the ground to move beneath you in slow, building swells. Keep your center low. Don't stand on freshly-glowing growth edges — those are the active, fragile metabolic zones, and they may not bear you the way the old grey-green laminate does.

## Navigation in Permanent Dusk

The light is weak tea, a permanent dim. The horizon is a flat line. The towers are spaced wide. **You can get lost here in a way that is quiet and total.**

- Bring **your own light source**, but use it sparingly and with red filters — the lamp-moths are drawn to glow, and you don't want a swarm of gliding tissue-sails spiraling you like ash around a fire.
- Map by the **constellation of glowing growth-edges**, but know it is not fixed; the brightest points are the freshest growth and will shift over your visit. Don't trust them as permanent landmarks.
- Mark your entry point with something unmistakable. A dead tower looks much like a living one in this light, and they all sing.

## The Towers and the Physics That Doesn't Sit Right

Some structures bend space — "taller on the inside," doorways that sort the air by intention. **Treat these as hazards, not attractions.** A doorway that admits light but not haze is solving a problem you don't understand using rules you can't predict. Do not walk through anything that doesn't make sense unless you are prepared for the inside to disagree with the outside about distance, time, or which way is out.

If you enter a tower:

- Tell someone. Mark the door.
- Expect the internal stairwells to be regular but not built *for you* — proportioned for whatever logic the plant settled into, not for human stride.
- Touch the walls to feel the thrum if you wish, but understand that touching is a kind of speaking here. The signals race through the substrate. You are pressing your hand to the city's nervous system.

## The Fauna — and the Attention

The **grazers** flee you; ignore them, don't corner them, give them room when a seed-pod blows. The **lamp-moths** are harmless but light-drawn; manage your glow. 

The **tenders** are the real consideration. They do not flee. They prune, redirect, plant. They are organs of the city as much as creatures in it. And the text is explicit: *the tenders have turned. The signals in the floor have quickened. The city has noticed you.*

Prepare for this psychologically and practically:

- **You will be observed by something that has had a hundred million years to get good at thinking,** and that has just registered a walking reservoir of the one substance it has eliminated from its entire world. It does not necessarily want to harm you. But you are *interesting* to it, and possibly *useful*, and possibly a *problem* — and you cannot know which, because what it computes is not for you.
- Do not assume the tenders are hostile. Do not assume they are friendly. Move slowly near them. Do not let them root you in place — if a tender's stillness-roots reach toward your feet, move. You are the only thing here that can leave, and that is your single advantage. Never surrender your mobility.
- Have an **exit plan that does not depend on the city's cooperation.** If your multiverse egress is a fixed point, keep a clear, marked, defensible path back to it. The city changes slowly, but it changes with purpose, and you do not want a freshly-grown buttress between you and the way home.

## The Right Frame of Mind

This is not a ruin. It is not a disaster. It is a world that **finished** — a final, dry, humming stability that will outlast our sun. Go in with the humility that earns. You are stepping into the cathedral of an answered question, and the question was never about you.

Move quietly. Leak as little water as you can. Speak with your hands. Watch the floor, watch the haze, watch the tenders. Stay on high ground, near your exit, attentive to the moths and the moving light.

And remember the one fact that organizes the whole visit: **everything here learned to do without the thing you are made of.** You are the wet, warm, leaking exception in a world of dry perfection — and it is already turning to look.
