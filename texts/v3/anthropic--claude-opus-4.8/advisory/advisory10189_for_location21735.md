# Preparing to Visit the Town on Standing Water

## Understanding What You Are Walking Into

Before anything else, understand the central deception of this place: it presents itself as stillness, but it is the busiest place you may ever stand in. Your nervous system will be confused. Your ears will report calm; your eyes will report frenzy. Prepare for the unease that comes from that mismatch — it is not a danger, but it is a strain, and travelers who don't expect it sometimes panic without knowing why. Know in advance that you will feel watched, hurried, and alone all at once. Name the feeling when it comes. That naming is itself a kind of protection.

## The Cold and the Light

This is high-altitude cold over standing water — the most efficient cold there is for stealing warmth from a body. You are not going to be hiking it off. You will be standing or wading, often motionless, while your legs sit in water that never warms. Heat will leave through your feet and never come back.

**Bring:**
- **Layered insulation** that retains warmth when damp, since damp is the permanent condition. Wool and modern synthetic fills over anything cotton. Assume everything you wear will eventually be wet at the surface.
- **Waterproof waders rated for genuine cold**, not splash-protection — full sealed barriers up past the knee, ideally to the hip, because the water depth varies and the lanes flood unpredictably. The seal between wader and warmth is the difference between a long visit and a short one.
- **Insulated, sealed footwear** with grip. Wet stone under moving water is treacherous, and there are no shadows to read the texture of the ground.

For the light, this is the second silent threat. White light from above and below means you are being irradiated from every angle, including up into your nostrils and under your chin. Snow-blindness here would be light-blindness from water.

**Bring:**
- **Full-coverage glacier-grade eye protection** with side shields. Ordinary sunglasses will let in the under-light off the water.
- **High-SPF barrier** on every exposed surface, applied as though you expect the sun from below — because you will get it.
- **A brimmed hood or hat** that shades without trapping, knowing it will not fully work because the light has no respect for shadow here.

## Breathing and Altitude

This is above the treeline, thin atmosphere, full solar weight. Acclimatize before you arrive if your transit allows it. If it doesn't:

- Move slowly. The town will tempt you to move slowly anyway; let the altitude reinforce that.
- Watch for altitude sickness masked by cold — headache, nausea, confusion. The danger is that this place already makes you feel strange, so you may dismiss real symptoms as atmosphere.
- Bring a means of measuring your own oxygen saturation, since you cannot trust your felt sense in a place built to deceive the senses.

## The Scale of the Town

Remember the buildings are built smaller than you expect — rooftops at your chest, doorways you must fold to pass through. You are oversized here. This means:

- **Move with deliberate care.** You can crush or collapse what the fauna spent the morning building. Whether that matters ethically is your call, but it matters practically: disturbing the structures may disturb the fauna, and you do not yet know what a disturbed multitude does.
- **Map nothing permanently.** A lane that runs north at dawn runs east by midmorning. Do not navigate by the town. Navigate by the fixed peaks ringing the basin — the old grey shoulders of stone that do not move. They are your only reliable landmarks. Learn their silhouette before you wade in.

## The Fauna

They are not afraid of you and they will move through you constantly. Prepare for the sensation of thousands of small bodies parting around your shins — it is relentless and intimate and goes on for as long as you stand.

- **Do not assume they are harmless.** They build and unbuild with their mouths; they carry and gnaw fibrous matter. A creature that can gnaw structure can gnaw a wader seam. Inspect your barriers often. Carry repair material.
- **Do not assume they are uninterested in you.** You are the largest new structure to enter their town. You may be assessed as building material, as obstacle, or as nothing. Watch whether they begin to add fibre *to you* — to your boots, your standing legs. If they do, move. You do not want to learn what it is to be incorporated.
- **Keep food and waste utterly sealed.** You are introducing a new variable into a closed, ancient equilibrium. Leave it as you found it.

## Provisioning

You can drink — probably — from the seeping springs, but treat all water before trusting it; the fauna live and die in it in uncountable numbers. Carry filtration and the means to warm water, because cold water in a cold body is its own slow harm.

Bring more calories than seem reasonable. Standing still in cold water burns fuel astonishingly fast, and the silence will make you forget to eat. Set a timer to remind you to eat and to check your own warmth, because this place erodes the sense of passing time.

## The Silence — the Real Preparation

The hardest preparation is internal. The silence is dense and total, and it will work on you. You will hear your own pulse. You will hear stones settle far off. In that quiet, with the frantic mute churning all around and the merciless even light and no shadow to anchor you, the mind reaches for meaning and sometimes invents it.

- **Set a hard time limit before you enter, and obey it mechanically.** Do not let your in-the-moment judgment decide when to leave. The place is built to make you stay and "learn its silence." That sentence is a warning. People who stay to learn the silence are the ones the silence keeps.
- **Bring a companion or a tether to the outside** — a voice, a line, a scheduled signal. The thing that will save you is not gear. It is something that says *you are still a person from elsewhere, and elsewhere is waiting.*
- **Carry a small sound of your own.** A bell, a hum, a word spoken aloud at intervals. Not to break the silence — you cannot — but to remind yourself you are a maker of sound in a place that has none. It will steady you more than you expect.

## Last Thing

You will not understand this place while you are in it. Accept that before you go. It has been remaking itself in silence longer than there has been anyone to wonder at it, and it will not explain itself to you in the hours you can survive there. Go to witness it. Go prepared to leave it unsolved. Pack your exit as carefully as your entry, and check, before you wade in, that you still want to come back out.
