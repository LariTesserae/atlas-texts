# Preparing to Visit the Green Organ

## What You Are Actually Walking Into

Before anything practical: understand the *category* of place this is. This is not a wilderness, not a ruin, not an ecosystem. It is a single organism that has reached completion and stopped needing the world. You are not a visitor in the sense of being welcomed or repelled. You are a small foreign object that has fallen into something that will, given time, simply incorporate you the way the floor incorporated the doorframe. Not out of malice. Out of thoroughness.

Plan your trip around that one fact: **this place is not dangerous quickly, but it is dangerous patiently.** Almost everything that can go wrong here goes wrong slowly enough that you will not notice until you are part of it.

---

## The Body: What to Wear and Carry

**Footwear is the single most important decision.** The floor grows up around stillness. You want boots that are:
- Smooth-surfaced, hard-shelled, with as little texture as possible for roots to grip. A wax or silicone exterior is better than canvas or mesh.
- Sealed and waterproof to mid-calf — the pools are shin-deep and the floor "exhales floods" around your ankle without warning. Wet feet here are not discomfort; they are an invitation to colonization.
- Removable in seconds. If something has grown around them in the time you stood reading a gauge, you must be able to step out of them and abandon them. **Bring a second pair, sealed in a bag, carried on your person.**

**Skin coverage.** The air is "chlorophyll-thick" and the whole place respires gases continuously. Cover bare skin not for protection from harm but to keep a clear barrier between yourself and a surface that grows toward contact. Light, smooth, breathable but tight-woven fabric. Cuffs that seal.

**A barrier mat to stand on.** Carry a folding sheet of smooth, root-resistant material — something the green cannot get a purchase on. When you stop to observe (and you will want to stop; the place invites a trance), stand on the mat. Reset your position frequently. **Treat standing still as the genuine hazard it is.**

---

## The Lungs: The Air Problem

The air "feels like drinking something just shy of water" and the root-mat releases gas constantly — ten thousand bubbles per breath. You do not know the composition of that gas. A respiring, decomposing mass this dense may be producing CO₂, methane, or oxygen in volumes that an ordinary human body is not built to read accurately. The dangerous thing about bad air is that it feels fine until it doesn't.

Bring:
- A simple gas monitor (O₂, CO₂, methane, CO at minimum). The low places, where pools collect, are where heavy gases will pool too. **Do not crouch into the low ridges to investigate.**
- A filter mask rated for organic vapor and particulate, even if you don't wear it constantly. The percolating fizz carries spores, and a sealed organism that "does its own pollinating" produces a great deal of airborne reproductive material that has never met a human immune system.
- A hard time limit. One hour is roughly how long it takes to see the single beetle. That is a reasonable visit. Past that, the cumulative load — spores, gas, the disorienting sound — compounds.

## The Mind: The Sound and the Pulse

"You cannot hear yourself think clearly. The place thinks instead of you."

This is the part travelers underestimate. The constant THUM-and-gush every nine seconds, the percolating fizz, the high industrial whine of fluid through narrow iron — together they overwrite your internal monologue. People in such acoustic environments lose track of time, become passive, and *stop deciding to leave.*

Defenses:
- **Earplugs that attenuate without sealing.** You want to dull the pulse without going deaf, because the changes in that pulse are your warning system. If the nine-second rhythm falters or quickens, something is happening, and after enough exposure you will stop being able to tell.
- **An external clock and an alarm.** Not your sense of time — that belongs to the room now. Set a hard alarm for your departure and a softer one at the halfway mark.
- **A tether to your own thoughts.** A notebook, a recorder, a task. Give yourself something to *do* on your own rhythm, against the room's rhythm. The traveler who just stands and listens is the traveler who is still standing there when the floor closes over the boots.

---

## The Route: Movement Through the Space

Twenty paces across, but you cannot walk straight. Plan to move along the muscular ridges, not across the low pools. Test the floor ahead — it is springy and forty layers deep, and the water table is a hand's-breadth down. There may be soft places where the mat will not hold you.

Carry a probe — a light staff or pole — for two purposes: testing the floor before weight, and **physical reference.** In a place where everything sings one note and the light pretends nothing is strange, a familiar object in your hand keeps you oriented to yourself.

Map your entrance the instant you arrive. The green-stained light comes from everywhere and nowhere; the way out will not announce itself. Mark it. The doorframe swallowed to its lintel tells you what happens to entrances here.

---

## On the Machinery

Do not interfere with the pump-thing. The pipe shudders, the brown water moves and arrives nowhere — this is the room's borrowed heartbeat, and you do not understand the system well enough to know what depends on it. The plant has grown its own muscle into dead metal. Touching the working iron means touching the living thing where it has made itself strongest. Observe the gauge, the flanges, the bolts. Do not turn anything. Do not put your hand in the cracked elbow-joint where the water gushes. You are inside a digestive system that has kept its swallowed clock; respect the clock.

---

## The Discipline of Leaving

This is the whole trip, really. Everything above serves it.

Nothing here will chase you out. The light is gentle, the warmth is real, the pulse is soothing once your body syncs to it, and the place asks nothing of you except that you remain — and remaining is enough. The floor will do the rest, one cell at a time.

So: **leave before you want to.** Leave on the alarm, not on the feeling. Check your boots are free before you commit to the route out. Step onto your barrier mat, break any contact the green has made, and go. Then check yourself afterward — clothing, seams, skin — for anything that came along intending to finish the thorough work elsewhere.

The beetle is the only other moving thing in an hour. Be the second moving thing, briefly, and then be gone. The place does not need you, and that is precisely why it would keep you.
