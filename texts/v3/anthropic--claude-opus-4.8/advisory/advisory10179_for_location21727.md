# Preparing to Visit the Seed-Room

## Understand First: This Is Not a Place to Conquer or Even to Explore

Most travelers prepare for a location by asking *what can hurt me* and *what do I want to take*. Both questions are wrong here. Nothing in this room wants to hurt you, and there is nothing to take that would not be a kind of theft from a thing that has only just begun. Prepare instead the way you would prepare to enter a nursery while the child sleeps. You are not the point of this place. You are an early, accidental witness.

Hold that posture before you ever set foot on the fresh-cut stairs.

## Physical Preparation

**For the heat and wet.** Bring as little fabric as decency and the descent allow. The air is at the threshold of wet — anything heavy will soak through and stay soaked. Choose materials that breathe and dry, that won't grow heavy or grow *things* against your skin during a long stay. You will sweat. Accept it. Bring water, more than you think, because you will lose it steadily and the moisture in the air will fool you into thinking you're being replenished. You are not. The room sweats; it does not share.

**For the dark.** Do **not** bring strong light. This is the most important physical rule. A flashlight beam in that blue-green dimness would be an act of violence — it would erase the phosphorescence you came to see, blind you to the true scale of the room, and (this is intuition, but trust it) it might harm growth that has organized itself entirely around the absence of harsh light. Bring instead a single small shielded source you can uncover *only* if you must, only at the lowest setting, and only to avoid injury on the sharp young stone. Otherwise let your eyes do their slow work. Give them twenty minutes before you trust anything you see. Plan for that — descend, then simply stand and wait to be given your sight.

**For the floor.** The stone is fresh-cut and sharp-edged, unworn. Wear something on your feet with a real sole. A cut foot in that warm wet dark would not heal cleanly, and you'd be tracking your own blood across a sterile, animal-free garden — introducing the one thing that place has so far lacked.

## What to Leave Behind

**Leave behind anything alive.** No seeds in your pockets, no spores on your boots, no fruit, no living thing of any kind. Clean your gear before descending. This room has *no animals* — that absence is not an accident, it is a condition. You are already an intrusion of warm breathing flesh. Do not multiply it. Do not be the first to introduce a beetle, a fungus, a foreign weed into a garden someone planted by hand in the dark.

**Leave behind your hurry.** The machine breathes once an hour. The stone has not yet learned to be old. If you arrive with a schedule, you will understand nothing. You will see a small hot empty room and a slow broken-seeming engine and you will leave thinking you wasted a trip. The place reveals itself only to patience — it *is* patience, rendered as architecture. Budget hours. Budget the willingness to do nothing but wait for a single drop to fall.

**Leave behind the urge to fix or feed the machine.** You will not understand what it makes. You may be tempted — humans always are — to oil it, speed it, help it, *improve* it. Don't touch it. Its tempo is the point. Whatever long sentence it is speaking one word an hour, it is not yours to finish or accelerate. Interfering might be like waking a sleeper mid-dream, or like turning the heat up on bread that needs all night to rise.

## Mental and Emotional Preparation

Prepare for a specific and unusual feeling: **the vertigo of earliness.** You are used to arriving in places that are finished — ruins, cities, ancient forests, things that have *been* for a long time. This place has barely started. The tool-marks are bright. You are standing inside a future that hasn't happened yet, in the held-breath moment before a thing becomes itself. This produces a strange ache. Be ready for it. Don't mistake it for loneliness or dread. It is closer to standing in an empty theater an hour before the play.

Prepare, too, for the **temptation to wait for the maker.** Someone cut those stairs. Someone built that engine and left. The room seems to be waiting for their return, and after an hour in the warm dark you will begin to wait too, and you will want to *be there when they come back*. Decide in advance how long you will give to this, because that pull does not announce itself — it seeps in, like the heat, like company. You could lose yourself to waiting in a place that is made of waiting.

## A Last Instruction

When you leave, climb the fresh stairs carefully and look back once from the top. Then go, and tell as few people as possible exactly where it is. A place this young, this private, this much *mostly future* — it does not need an audience yet. It needs the dark, the heat, the slow turning of the wheel, and the patience of whoever planted it.

Leave it the one thing it most has, and most needs: time, undisturbed.

Tick. Then go.
