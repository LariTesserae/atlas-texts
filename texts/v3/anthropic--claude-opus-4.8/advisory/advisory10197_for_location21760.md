# Preparing to Visit the Unfinished City

## Understand First What Kind of Place This Is

Before you pack a single item, sit with this: you are going somewhere that does not want you, was not built for you, and will not register your visit. This is not a hostile place — hostility requires attention, and the city has none to spare for something as fast and brief as a human being. You will be, to this place, less than a fly is to a cathedral. Make your peace with that before you go. The wrong mindset — the explorer's appetite to *conquer* or *understand* or *extract* — will rot in you here, slowly, over the hours, into something closer to despair. The right mindset is closer to attending a funeral for someone you never met, in a language you don't speak. Reverent. Quiet. Briefly present.

## The Body: Cold and Wet

The cold is described precisely and it is the dangerous kind — *the kind that doesn't bite but settles into the marrow as a low note.* This is the cold that kills people who think they're fine. Just above freezing, with total saturation, no sun, and a standing fog that is half water. You will not feel acute pain. You will feel comfortable, then sluggish, then sleepy, then dead. Hypothermia in high humidity is faster and quieter than dry cold because water strips heat from your body roughly twenty-five times more efficiently than air.

**You need:**

- **A drysuit, not a wetsuit.** Sealed at neck, wrists, ankles. Everything here weeps; the film hangs on every surface and beads on the *underside* of things, meaning there is no orientation that keeps you dry. Assume total immersion in slow water.
- **Active heating, not passive insulation.** Electrically heated base layers with a battery you can keep warm against your core. Passive insulation fails when it's wet, and here everything becomes wet. Carry redundant batteries, sealed, body-warmed.
- **A heated, sealed face mask with its own air supply.** The air is *half-water.* Breathing it for hours means inhaling a cold mist that chills you from the inside and may carry whatever that fungal chemistry in the seams is. Do not breathe the air directly. Carry your own.
- **Sealed boots with aggressive grip.** Every surface wears a film. Every surface is therefore a slip hazard, on stone that is hard as iron and cold as death. A fall here is not an inconvenience; it is a broken hip in a place with no rescue.

## The Senses: Darkness and the Chord

**On light:** You will be tempted to bring powerful lights. Bring them, but understand what they cost. *The city was never meant to be seen.* Sight matters nothing here; you are bringing eyes to a problem long since solved without them. Two warnings:

- Powerful light reveals only thirty paces of fog before everything dissolves into suggestion. You will not see the city. You will see a small wet sphere of grey around yourself and infer the rest. Accept this. Bring a **low, warm, dim light** for footing and a single brighter beam held in reserve, knowing it shows you almost nothing.
- You do not know how the fauna respond to light. A place that solved darkness "long ago" may have inhabitants for whom a sudden beam is a violence, an aggression, an event. Use light sparingly and dimly. Assume you are the first light some of them have ever encountered.

**On sound — this is the one that will hurt you.** The chorus is not metaphor. *You feel it in your sternum.* A sustained, layered drone in a resonant chamber the size of a city, amplified by fog, channeled through tuned hollow towers — this is sound pressure that can genuinely disorient a human, induce nausea, vibrate the fluid in your inner ear until you cannot tell up from down, and over long exposure produce a creeping psychological dread (the infrasound problem). 

- Bring **active noise-canceling protection** — but here is the crucial instruction: **do not cancel it entirely.** The chord is the truth of this place. To block it out completely is to stand inside a living body with your fingers in your ears, and you will have traveled an unimaginable distance to experience nothing. Tune your protection to **reduce the dangerous low-frequency pressure while letting the music through.** Experience it. Just don't let it harm you. And the moment you feel your judgment going soft, or a wrongness creeping up your spine — *leave.* That is the infrasound working on your mind, and it does not announce itself as a problem until it has already become one.

## The Footing of Reality

The deeper preparation is conceptual. Several of this world's rules will fight your instincts:

**Nothing hurries — but you must.** Everything here runs on a timescale where a year is a heartbeat. You do not. Your batteries die in hours; your core temperature drops in hours; your sanity erodes in hours. You are a brief hot fast thing in a world of patient cold slow things, and *you are the one with the deadline.* Set a hard time limit before you enter and obey it absolutely. The city's patience is contagious and it will try to slow you down to its own tempo. That tempo will kill you.

**Do not touch the new growth.** The granular, vibrating, accreting surfaces are pulling matter out of the wet air and the weeping rock. You are made of matter. You are wet. You are warm — a source of disequilibrium in a world running on the weakest possible gradients. Do not test what a self-assembling process that doesn't fully obey known physics does when offered a glove, a battery, a hand. Touch nothing that is being built. The old smooth walls, perhaps. The new granular ones, never.

**The fauna eat the city, and the dead become the city.** Understand the cycle you are stepping into: build, graze, die, fold-in, build again. There is no waste here, no rot, no decay — *the dead creatures are folded into the walls and become the next tower.* This is a place that has no category for a corpse that does not belong. If you die here, you will not be buried; you will be *incorporated*, slowly, over centuries, into the growing wall, your matter pulled into the next ribbed cathedral. This is not a metaphor to make you shudder. It is logistics. **There will be no recovery of a body.** Tell whoever waits for you on the other side of the crossing that there is no version of this trip where they get you back if it goes wrong. Go only if that is acceptable.

## What to Pack, in Sum

- Drysuit, sealed, with electric core heating and redundant warmed batteries
- Independent breathing supply and a heated sealed mask
- Gripped sealed boots
- Tuned hearing protection (reduce pressure, preserve the chord)
- One dim warm light; one bright reserve light used rarely
- A hard chronometer with an alarm you cannot ignore, set to your departure time
- A guide-line or tether back to your entry point — *distance dissolves at thirty paces*, the fog is total, the city is endless and trackless and resonant, and **you will lose your way the moment you stop paying attention.** Anchor a line. Follow it back. Do not trust your memory of the route; sight gives you nothing and the chord scrambles direction.
- A short list, written and waterproofed, of why you came and when you must leave — to read when the place's patience starts to feel like your own.

## The Last Instruction

Go to the edge, where the towers thin and the fog opens onto the suggestion of the drop. Stand there a while. Hear the chord roll out across a region you cannot see, over a city without bottom or end, growing one molecule at a time and finishing nothing, ever.

Then leave. While you are still fast, still warm, still yours.

You came to witness a thing that does not need witnesses, that was old before there were ears, that will sing the same note long after the last memory of you has been folded into a wall somewhere and become a tower. The whole gift of this place is the smallness it offers you. Take it. Then come home and be glad you are the kind of creature that hurries, that decays, that finishes things, that gets to leave.
