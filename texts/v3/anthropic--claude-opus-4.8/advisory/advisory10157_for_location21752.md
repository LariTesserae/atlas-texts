# Preparing to Visit the Drowned Bell

## Understand First What Kind of Place You Are Entering

Before any packing, sit with this fact: you are going somewhere that is unfinished, and being there will make *you* unfinished too, for a while. This is not a hostile place. It will not try to kill you. But it works on a person the way deep water works on sound — it carries things into you that you cannot keep out. Most of your preparation is not about survival. It is about coming back with a self intact enough to recognize.

So prepare in two registers: the body, and the thing the body carries.

---

## The Body

**Footwear, heavily reinforced.** The ridge is new basalt — glassy, freshly cooled, sharp enough to open a boot sole and the foot inside it. Bring soles thick and stiff, and bring a spare pair, because the climb up will already begin to score them. Inside the bowl, the floor is faulted rock that *steams*; expect heat through the sole, and pick your footing near the seams. You will be tempted to swim instead of walk, and the place will encourage the confusion. Don't resolve it for it. Keep your feet able to do either.

**Heat tolerance.** The water is blood-warm and the rock is being born — there are vents, steam, the planet's own furnace beneath you. Dress for a warm, humid, oppressive enclosure, not for cold water. Layers you can shed. Do not bring anything that traps heat against the chest.

**Breathing apparatus — but think carefully about it.** You will be *inside* water that has decided not to behave like water. Whether you can breathe it is one of the things the place "refuses to keep stable." Bring a rebreather or closed system you trust, but understand its readings may not stay true. Do not rely on a single sense to tell you whether you are submerged. Bring a backup that works by an entirely different principle than your primary.

**Ear protection that you already know will fail.** Bring it anyway — not to block the toll (nothing blocks the toll) but to dampen the *grinding*, the stone-on-stone beneath it, which can do ordinary mechanical damage to ordinary ears. The bell itself is inside your heartbeat and cannot be defended against. Accept this in advance. People who arrive expecting to keep the sound out are broken by it faster than those who arrive ready to let it in.

**A timepiece that does not depend on counting.** Sand, a slow chemical reaction, a burning cord — something physical and external. Your internal sense of time will lock onto the toll and become useless. The pulse of the light, bright-on-the-downbeat, will try to become your clock, and it has no honest relationship to minutes. Set a hard external limit for your stay before you go in, and obey the physical timer even when every instinct says you have only just arrived.

---

## The Self

This is the harder packing.

**Bring an anchor-object you knew before you came.** Something small, owned a long time, with a texture you can find in the dark and a meaning that is *yours* and dull and ordinary — a worn coin, a ring, a stone from a different world entirely. The water will gather your shape out of the dim and try to make you. It is only practicing, but watching yourself drafted and discarded in the amber light loosens something. The anchor-object is what you press into your palm to remember which version of you is the one that walks back out. Choose something the water will not bother to imitate — something humble, like the true crawlers on the bottom that the current never copies.

**Decide, before entering, what you will not look at.** Faces surface in the column — yours, and the faces of people you have lost. They hold for one toll and unmake. You cannot prevent them. But you can decide in advance not to *follow* them, not to swim toward the dissolving face of someone you grieve, because the place will draft that face precisely because you grieve it, and it will not finish, and you can lose hours and parts of yourself chasing a thing that was never going to consent to stay. Grief is the bait this place uses without meaning to. Know that going in.

**Practice not-listening.** The voices resolve into words only if you stop trying to hear them — which means the danger is in *succeeding*. If you let yourself receive the words, you may carry them out and find they reorganize you. The discipline to practice beforehand is this: hearing something just under audibility and *choosing not to lean in*. Learn to let a near-meaning pass. This is a skill, and it is rehearsable, and you should rehearse it.

---

## How to Move Through It

**Find the true creatures and use them as instruments.** The black eel, older than the rock. The translucent crawlers. They are calm because they have made their peace with the bell, and they are the only honest things in the bowl — real words in a stream of babble. When you cannot tell water from air, swimming from walking, self from draft, *look for a true creature and watch what it does.* It is not confused. It will not be made by the water. It is your compass. Locate one early. Keep returning to it.

**Treat the built things as the past, not as instructions.** The doorframe leading from water into water, the wall enclosing nothing, the cracked silent bell too small to be the source. Someone lived here under different rules. Do not walk through the doorframe expecting it to mean what doorframes mean. Do not assume the small cracked bell explains the toll — it doesn't; the rules have moved on and left the architecture as the husk of a question. Look at these things with tenderness and do not obey them.

**Do not try to finish anything while you are there.** This is the deepest rule. Nothing here completes itself, and the place will resist completion in you too. Do not resolve to make a decision in the bowl, do not try to "understand" the toll into meaning, do not attempt to hold a thought to its conclusion. Go in with your questions already closed or deliberately abandoned. Bring nothing you need to *settle*. The place punishes the need to finish by making you a thing that almost-finishes, over and over.

---

## Leaving, and After

**The membrane is unmarked. You will not feel yourself cross it.** Do not search for the exact line — there isn't one to find, and the searching keeps you in. Instead, walk the ridge back down by your physical timer and your anchor-object, and trust that air will reassert itself even if you cannot say when. Keep moving down. Do not turn back to confirm you've left.

**Prepare for the aftertone.** For days, longer, you will feel the toll beneath your pulse — a low note under everything. This is normal and it is not damage, exactly; it is the bell still ringing in the small bones of the ear. Arrange your return so that you come back to somewhere quiet and ordinary and *finished* — a place full of things that have fully consented to be themselves. Plain food. A familiar bed. People who know your name and will say it. The cure for the unfinished is the deliberate company of the complete.

**Most important: arrange for someone to confirm you when you return.** Have a person waiting who knew you before, who can look at you and tell you that you are still the one who left — that the water rehearsed your shape but did not get to keep it. Bring back the anchor-object and put it in their hand. Let them give it back to you. That small completed exchange, that one finished thing, is how you tell the water *no* after the fact.

You will be all right. The Drowned Bell does not want you. It only practices. But go in knowing you are easy to practice on — and go in already carrying the proof of who you are, because the place will not hand it back, and it will not, ever, quite let you finish becoming anyone there.
