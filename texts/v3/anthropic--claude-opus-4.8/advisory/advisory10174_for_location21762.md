# Preparing to Visit the Drowned Lattice

## Understand What You're Walking Into

Before anything else, sit with the central fact: there is no surfacing. This is not a dive in the ordinary sense, where the air above is your reward and your insurance. The Lattice is liquid the whole way up and the whole way down. Whatever you bring to breathe, you bring *all* of it, with no possibility of a quick ascent to fix a mistake. This changes the entire psychology of the trip. You are not visiting an underwater place. You are entering a world whose only medium is water, and you must arrive already at peace with that.

If the idea of no-surface fills you with panic, do not go. The Lattice will not hurt you quickly — but panic will.

## Breathing: The Central Problem

You need a closed-circuit system, not open-circuit scuba. A rebreather, or better, given that this is multiverse travel and you can equip beyond ordinary diving:

- **Long-duration closed-loop life support.** Bring redundancy in triplicate. The Lattice has no bottom and no top, so there is no fallback. Two complete independent breathing systems minimum, ideally three, each capable of bringing you home alone.
- **A clear sense of your air budget translated into distance.** "Days, if days meant anything" — that's the scale of the halls. You will never explore even a fraction. Decide your turnaround point *before* you descend, and tie it to your most conservative gas reserve, not your optimistic one.

## Cold Management

The water sits just above freezing. Cold this even and this persistent is not dramatic — it does not announce itself with shivering until you're already in trouble. It seeps.

- **Active heating, not just insulation.** A drysuit is the floor; you want heated undergarments with their own power, sized to outlast your entire planned stay plus your margin.
- **Watch your hands especially.** This is a place you learn *by touch*. Numb fingers will rob you of the Lattice's primary sense. Heated gloves, dexterous enough to feel the adze-scallops and the soft weed.
- **Respect that cold slows thought.** Build your plan assuming a degraded, patient version of yourself will be executing it. Make decisions early, while warm and clear.

## Light — and the Discipline of Using It

There is almost no light here, only that blue-grey ghost-glow "the color of the inside of a closed eye." You will be tempted to flood the place with a powerful lamp. Consider doing so sparingly.

- **Bring strong light, but ration its use.** A bright beam will let you *see*, but it will also collapse the experience of the place — which is meant to be known by hand, in dimness, a few arm-lengths at a time. Much of what makes the Lattice itself is precisely the smallness of the visible world.
- **Bring a low, warm secondary light** for moving slowly without destroying the dark.
- **Accept that you will never see the vastness.** No amount of light reaches it. The size is a thing you can only infer from how long you can travel without ending. Make peace with this in advance, or you will spend the trip frustrated by an unseeable cathedral.

## Navigation in a Trackless Made World

This is the most dangerous practical problem. Every surface is worked stone — fitted, hewn, intentional — and it goes on past any horizon. There are no natural landmarks because *everything* is a landmark and therefore nothing is.

- **Lay a guideline. Always.** A continuous physical tether back to your entry point, the way cave divers run line. The Lattice's beauty is exactly the thing that will kill a careless traveler: every direction looks meaningfully made, every direction beckons, and none of them lead back unless you've marked the way.
- **Do not trust memory of "rooms."** The halls and colonnades repeat in mood without repeating in fact — no two joints identical, but no joint distinct enough to remember. The erosion has smoothed everything toward the same settled sameness.
- **Move slowly and deliberately.** This is a place of slow things. Hurrying earns you nothing here and costs you orientation.

## What to Expect of the Inhabitants

Nothing hunts here. There are no animals — the plants have inherited the world. This means:

- **No threat behaves quickly.** Nothing will lunge, sting, or flee. The vines and kelps and bone-pale fronds move on cycles so slow they read as stillness.
- **The plants are dense — saturated, overlapping, layered.** Expect to push gently through curtains of fronds, threads of vine bridging the columns. Move through them as a slow swimmer parts slow growth; don't thrash. They will not harm you, and you have no reason to harm them.
- **Watch entanglement, not aggression.** The only "danger" the flora poses is the passive one: your guideline or your gear catching in the dense growth. A clean, snag-free rig and slow movement solve this.

## Sound, and What It Will Do to You

It is water-quiet — not silence, but the muffling of all edges. You will hear your own pulse. Occasionally, the deep structural groan of the lattice settling another hair's breadth, a single low note arriving from somewhere immense and far.

- **Prepare emotionally for the loudness of your own body.** In this hush, your heartbeat and your breathing become the loudest things in the world. For some, this is meditative. For others, it becomes the seed of panic. Know which you are.
- **The distant groans are not a warning.** They are the sound of geological patience. Don't let them spook you into thinking the structure is failing in any timeframe that concerns you. It is finishing its slow settling over spans that dwarf a human visit.

## The Right Frame of Mind

This is the truest preparation, and the one most travelers neglect.

The Lattice runs on the patience of cold water and old stone. Its only rule is the rule of slow things: everything is being worn, gently and forever, toward one smooth settled ending. You cannot match that patience, and you should not try. But you can *visit* it without violating it.

- **Come to witness, not to conquer.** There is nothing to win here, nothing to harvest, no summit, no bottom. The place offers no climax. If you arrive needing a destination, you'll find only more halls.
- **Plan a stay that is short relative to the place and long relative to your habits.** Let yourself slow down. Spend real time learning one colonnade by hand before moving to the next. The Lattice rewards the visitor who adopts its tempo, however briefly.
- **Bring no urgency.** Urgency is the one thing entirely foreign to this world, and it is the thing most likely to undo you — through a missed gas check, a snagged line, a cold-dulled wrong turn made in haste.

## A Minimal Packing Summary

- Triple-redundant closed-circuit life support, gas budget figured in distance with a hard turnaround
- Powered drysuit with heated undergarments and heated, dexterous gloves
- One strong lamp (rationed) and one low warm lamp (constant)
- Continuous guideline reel, with backups, and a snag-free streamlined rig
- A slow heart, a patient mind, and the prior acceptance that you will never see the whole, never find an edge, and never surface until you turn back along your own line

Then go down — knowing there is no down that ends, no up that opens — into the drowned green cathedral, and let it teach you the patience of made things returning, slowly, forever, toward the grown.
