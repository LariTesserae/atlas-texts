# Preparing to Visit the Sealed Pocket

## Understand First: You Are Not Welcome, But Not Refused Either

There is no hostility here, but there is no accommodation. This place was not built for visitors — it was not built at all. Every preparation you make is a preparation against indifference, which is harder to survive than malice, because indifference never lets up. Begin by accepting that you will be a brief contaminant in a system that has run undisturbed for ages and will keep running after you go. Make peace with being irrelevant. It will help you stay calm.

## The Body Cannot Go As It Is

**Heat.** "Hot enough to cook in" is the simplest and most lethal fact. You need a full active-cooling hardsuit — not insulation alone, because insulation only delays, and there is no cool reservoir to delay toward. You need a closed-loop heat pump that dumps warmth somewhere. Here is the cruel part: *there is nowhere to dump it.* The walls are hot. The droplets are hot. The crystals are hot. You will be carrying your own coldness on your back, a finite battery of chill, and the moment it fails, you begin to cook from the outside in. Budget your time in the pocket against your coolant reserve, not against your air, not against your courage. When the cooling margin reaches half, you leave. No exceptions, no "just a little longer." The room does not care, and it will take you slowly enough that you'll think you have time.

**Air.** Bring all of it. The pocket is sealed — there is no air, only mineral films and beaded non-liquids and the saturated dark. A fully self-contained rebreather or tank system, redundant, with reserves sized for the round trip plus a wide failure margin. Assume the atmosphere outside the suit is unbreathable and probably reactive — sulfur, metal salts, acidic vapors. A single suit breach is not an inconvenience here; it is the end.

**Skin and seals.** The chemistry runs on sulfur and acid and dissolved metal. Whatever touches the outside of your suit will try to eat it. Choose materials rated against hot acidic sulfurous environments, and inspect every seal twice. The droplets are not water — they are dense, oily, mineral-thick, and they will not run off cleanly. They will cling. Plan to be smeared.

## The Smallness Is a Trap

It is the size of a kitchen, and that sounds manageable, almost cozy. It is not. The pocket is *nearly solid with itself* — crystal grown into crystal, pooled mineral filling the gaps, everything pressed against everything. There is almost no void to occupy.

Understand what this means: **you may not fit.** There may be no room for a human body inside this place. Bring this expectation with you. You may have to observe from the threshold of an entry bore, reaching in with instruments rather than stepping inside. If you do enter, you will displace the place itself — crushing crystal fingers that took centuries to grow, smearing the laboring colonies, breaking the saturated fullness that is the whole point of what this room *is*. There is no way to enter gently. Decide in advance whether you can bear to do that damage, because you cannot undo it.

## Protect Your Ears Before Anything Else You Forget

It *roars.* Prepare for sound where you expect silence. The grinding of growth, the deep rhythmic industrial pulse of millions of small engines — this will not be a background hum. In a sealed stone cavity, sound has nowhere to go; it will fill you. Bring active hearing protection integrated into the suit, and dampers, and the knowledge that the sound itself may disorient you. People panic in loud darkness. The roar has no source you can locate and no rhythm you set, and it will work on your nerves. Train yourself beforehand in loud, dark, enclosed spaces so the sensation is familiar when it arrives. Do not let the sound make your decisions.

## Bring Your Own Light, and Expect It to Feel Like Trespass

The faint blue-green pulse is not for seeing by. It only confirms there is something there. Bring strong, reliable lighting — but understand that to light this place is to *intrude* on it in a way nothing ever has. No light has touched these crystal faces. When your beam falls on them, you will be the first event of that kind in the history of the pocket. Use it sparingly. Use it with something like respect. And carry redundant light, because if your light fails in here, the place will not give you any of its own.

## What to Carry for the Mind

This is, finally, the hardest preparation, and you cannot pack it in a case.

This place was never meant to be seen. It runs *for no one.* You are going somewhere that has no need of you, no record of visitors, no story that includes a witness. Most travelers go to places that, however dangerous, are in some sense *about* the encounter — vistas, ruins, the dwellings of others. This is not that. You will stand in a roaring hot dark room watching an ancient factory turn the same reactions over and over for the millionth-millionth time, and it will continue exactly as before whether you are there or not.

Some travelers find this unbearable — the sheer *pointlessness of being present.* Prepare for it. Decide before you go why you are going, because the place will offer you no reason of its own. The only meaning here is the meaning you bring in your own head, and you must bring enough of it to last the visit, because the pocket will lend you none.

## Practical Itinerary

- **Approach** through the deep stone of the impossibly high mountain. The thin air above does not matter; no air gets in. You are descending or boring into sealed rock.
- **Breach** the pocket only if you must, knowing it has been sealed since its making and your opening it is itself an irreversible act.
- **Limit your stay** by coolant, not curiosity.
- **Touch nothing you do not have to.** Every surface is hot enough to ruin a hand, and every surface is alive or growing.
- **Bring samples out only if you can do so without collapsing the system** — and accept that you probably cannot. The thing's whole nature is fullness, saturation, contact on all sides. Take a piece and you have changed what it was.
- **Leave before the place tells you to,** because it never will.

## Last Thing

Pack the understanding that you are visiting *time* more than place — a room that adds a hair's width of crystal per century, where the tide of life turns over a thousand years. Whatever you do in your handful of minutes there is, to the pocket, instantaneous and gone. You will be the fastest thing that ever happened in that room, and it will not notice you happened at all.

Go quietly. Come back whole. Let it keep churning in the dark, alone, slowly, forever, for no one.
