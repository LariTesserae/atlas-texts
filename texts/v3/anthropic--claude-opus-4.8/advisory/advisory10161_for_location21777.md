# Preparing to Visit the Room That Is Making Itself

## Understand First What You Are Entering

This is not a hostile place, and that is precisely the danger. It will not attack you. It will simply continue *becoming*, indifferent to whether you are part of what it decides to become. The traveler's main risk here is not death but **dissolution into the provisional** — staying long enough that you, too, begin to be a thing the room is still drafting. Prepare accordingly. The threats are gentle, slow, and patient, which means they are easy to ignore until it is too late to leave.

## What to Bring

**An external timekeeper that does not rely on the room.** Since causality is unfinished here and time is "one of the things not yet finished," you cannot trust your sense of duration. Bring a mechanical or sealed digital timer set *before* entry, with a hard alarm. Better: a knotted cord, a candle of known burn-rate, or some analog clock whose logic you carry in from outside. You will think you have been here minutes. Treat any internal estimate as a lie.

**A single fixed, honest object.** The moss is described as "the one honest thing in the room" — ordinary, completely ordinary. Carry your own honest thing: a stone from your home world, a coin, a wedding ring, something fully *finished*, fully committed to being itself. Hold it when you feel your own edges going soft. Let it remind you that you are a closed form, already decided, not a sketch awaiting lines.

**A length of cord tied to your point of entry.** There is no door, only "the sense of having always been here." This means your exit is not a place but a *state of mind* you must be able to recover. The cord is less navigation than ritual anchor — a physical thread back to the certainty that there is an outside. Tie the far end to your honest object, or to your own wrist.

**Minimal, decided gear.** Bring nothing that hums, nothing that runs on causality you cannot account for. The room has admitted only "hands and stone and patience." Complex machinery may not function — or worse, may begin to be *reconsidered* by the room, its parts becoming embryonic the way the geology is embryonic. Wear plain clothes. Carry plain tools.

**Water and food consumed *before* entry, not during.** The water here beads, vanishes, and "goes somewhere the room hasn't built yet." Do not drink it. Do not eat anything the room offers, including anything that looks like the honest moss. The newt-things "drink the light"; you do not know what you would be drinking. Enter fed and watered. Treat the room as breath-holding distance.

## How to Prepare Your Mind

This is the real preparation. The body's needs are trivial here; the mind's are everything.

**Practice tolerating multiple simultaneous truths without resolving them.** You will see a beetle arrive before it departs. You will see the fern both unfurling and already-open. The instinct to *make it make sense* — to force a single timeline — is the instinct that will exhaust and unmoor you. Before you go, train yourself to observe contradictions and simply let them stand, "gently, without conflict, the way a sketch holds many possible lines." The traveler who insists on consequence following cause will tire fastest and lose their grip on their own already-decided nature.

**Rehearse the act of leaving.** Since you exit "the way you came, through the door that was never there," your departure depends on remembering that you *are a visitor* and not a feature. Practice this as a mantra or somatic cue before entry: *I came from somewhere finished. I return to somewhere finished. I am not being built.* The room is "indifferent and patient." Your finishedness must be more stubborn than its becoming.

**Lower your expectations of progress.** You will want to complete things — finish the half-wall, close the spiral, watch the drop finally fall. Resist. These are the room's incompletions, not yours, and the desire to finish them is the room beginning to enlist you into its slow project. Bring no agenda. Touch nothing you feel an urge to *complete*.

## Conduct While Inside

- **Move slowly and little.** Growth here is "expensive"; deliberateness is the local grammar. Sudden motion marks you as something not yet integrated, but it also wastes the room's strange patience on you.
- **Speak rarely, if at all.** The silence is "a thick attentive silence." Sound is rare and meaningful. Do not pour your noise into a place that has chosen quiet.
- **Do not lie down. Do not close your eyes for long.** The light is the brightness "of a closed eyelid." To sleep here is to risk the room dreaming you into one of its provisional truths.
- **Check your honest object often.** If it ever feels soft, unfinished, or hard to remember — leave immediately.
- **Watch your shadow.** You should not have one; nothing here casts. But if you find yourself *expecting* to cast none, as a native does — that is the early sign that the room has begun to consider you part of itself.

## The One Inviolable Rule

**Set a departure point and honor it absolutely.** The text is explicit: "You could stay. The room would keep building itself around you." This is the seduction. It is bright, it is cool, it is full of quiet life, and it asks nothing of you except that you remain a little longer while it decides what is true. The traveler who stays past their timer does not die. They become "the spiral never closed, the single drop forever choosing whether to fall" — a thing held permanently in the moment before becoming itself.

Enter as a finished person. Leave as a finished person. Let the room go on making itself behind you, brightly, brightly lit — without you in it.
