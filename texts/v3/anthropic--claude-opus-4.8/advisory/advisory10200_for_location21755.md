# Preparing to Visit the Sung Chamber

## Understanding What You're Entering

Before any practical preparation, you must grasp the central fact: **this is not a place where things happen, and you will not be an exception.** You are about to insert a creature of process — breath, pulse, decay, intention — into a place that finished processing before your species had bones. You will be the only event in the room. Prepare accordingly.

The hardest adjustment will not be physical. It will be the slow dawning that *you are the disturbance*. Most travelers are braced for hostile environments. Few are braced for an environment that is simply, profoundly complete, and into which they arrive as an unfinished thing.

## For the Ears — Your First and Most Urgent Concern

The chord will not hurt you the way a blast hurts, but it will not stop, and *that* is the danger.

- **Bring graduated hearing protection**, not the kind that seals you off completely — you may need to gauge the sound's structure for your own orientation — but attenuating filters that bring the chord down to a livable mass. Bring spares. Bring more spares.
- Understand that the sound conducts through **bone and tooth**, not just air. Earplugs alone will not save you. Pack a bite-guard or jaw cushion; the long-bone resonance you can endure, but constant jaw-vibration causes a specific, grinding exhaustion that creeps up over hours.
- **Train beforehand.** Spend time, in the days prior, in environments of sustained drone — a generator room, a long tone played without cease. Most travelers panic not at the volume but at the *unrelenting* quality. The mind keeps waiting for it to end, and the waiting itself becomes the wound. You must arrive having already made peace with a sound that will outlast you.

## For the Body — The Dryness and the Cold

- The dryness is not the dryness of a desert, which steals water and announces itself. This dryness runs on **another chemistry** and may not register on instruments as low humidity at all. Do not trust your hygrometer. Trust the slow crack of your lips, the drying of your eyes within minutes.
- **Seal your own moisture in.** Bring an occlusive layer for exposed skin, lip protection of the heaviest kind, and lubricating eye drops in greater quantity than seems reasonable. Your tear film will go first. Blink consciously. Set a timer.
- **Carry all your water with you, internally and externally.** Pre-hydrate for a full day before entry. There is nothing to drink here and the air will not give anything back to you. Whatever you bring is all you will ever have.
- The cold is the **cool of porcelain** — even, sourceless, undeepening. It will not kill you quickly, but it will leach into you steadily because there is no draft to fight and therefore no instinct to brace. Insulate, but insulate for *stillness*, not for wind. The danger is the slow, unnoticed descent of your core temperature in a place that gives no signal that anything is wrong.

## For the Eyes — Light Without Source

- The brightness is shadowless. **You will lose your depth perception.** Every surface glows equally; nothing is foregrounded, nothing recedes. In a room packed wall to wall with form, this is genuinely dangerous — you will misjudge distances and walk into singing clay you thought was farther off.
- Bring **lightly tinted lenses** to take the edge off the even glare, but more importantly, bring a **directional light of your own.** Not because the room is dark — it isn't — but because you will need to *cast a shadow* to navigate. A handheld beam lets you re-introduce direction, edge, and depth into a world that has none. Your own thrown shadow will become your most precious tool. Without it you may not be able to tell where one fluted riser ends and the next begins.
- The absence of your own shadow will be psychologically strange. Some travelers report a creeping sense of unreality, of not quite *being* there, because the body's most ancient confirmation of its presence — the dark shape it throws — is missing. Expect this. Name it when it comes.

## For Movement — The Fullness

This is the warning I would underline three times.

**There is no empty space.** You are not entering a room you will walk through; you are entering a solid that happens to admit you. The text says it plainly — you will be *embedded*, wedged between presences.

- **Travel light and travel narrow.** No pack that widens you. Everything strapped close to the body. You cannot afford a centimeter you do not need.
- **Move slowly and deliberately, with your hands first.** This is a place to be felt through, not seen through. Your shadowless eyes will lie to you about distance; your fingers will not.
- Understand that the things you press against are **ancient, crazed, worn soft, and irreplaceable** — and they are *singing*. Each is a held note. To break one is not to break an object; it is to silence a voice that has sounded since before words for *long*. Move as if every surface is both fragile and sacred, because it is both.
- **Plan your exit before you are deep in.** In a packed space with no shadows and no landmarks that differ much from one another, becoming disoriented is the realistic failure mode. Mark your entry. Count your turns. Do not go further than you can retrace.

## For the Mind — The Real Preparation

Everything above is logistics. This is the part that matters.

You are visiting a thing that **has finished happening.** It does not need you, will not notice you, will not be changed by you, and will not remember you. For a traveler — a being whose whole identity is *movement, arrival, consequence* — this is a quietly annihilating fact to sit inside for any length of time.

- Some travelers find the unchanging peace of the place restful, even holy — a chapel indeed, where nothing is asked and nothing decays.
- Others find it unbearable: a mirror held up to their own impermanence, a roaring, shadowless reminder that they are the only thing here that will end.

**Decide, before you go, which you suspect you are.** And set a firm time limit regardless. The room has no clocks, no day, no rhythm to tell you how long you have stayed. The light will not dim, the chord will not pause, nothing will signal *enough*. You must bring the sense of "enough" in with you, on a timer, in writing, because the place will not provide it and your own internal clock will dissolve into that held, endless note.

## What to Bring Out — Nothing

Take nothing. Not a tile, not a fragment, not one of the twelve pale stalks that have settled into their cracks across a thousand years of stillness. They are not yours, and more than that: they are *finished*, and to remove one is to start something in a place that stopped long ago.

The stalks especially. There are only a dozen, in the whole completed world. They are not growing and not dying; they have arrived at their final shapes. You would be ending one of twelve eternities for a souvenir.

Bring out only what you carried in, and the memory of having been, for a moment, the single living event in a room that did not need you to be there — and was, in its sourceless gold and unbroken chord, entirely complete without you.

That is the gift of the place, if you can receive it: to be *unnecessary*, and to be at peace with it.

Go quietly. Touch little. Stay no longer than your timer. And when you leave, the chord will hold, and hold, and hold, exactly as it did before you came.
