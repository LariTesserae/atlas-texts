# Preparing for the Sweatroom

## Understand What You Are Walking Into

The Sweatroom is not hostile. That is the first thing to internalize, and it is also the most dangerous thing about it. It will not attack you, exhaust you suddenly, or threaten you with anything dramatic. It will simply *include* you — patiently, warmly, with the same indifference it shows a fallen leaf. This is a place that processes whatever enters it on a geological timescale. Your job is to be a visitor and not raw material.

Plan for a **short stay.** This is not a place to camp. The longer you remain, the more the heat and the damp work on your body, your gear, and your resolve. Go in, witness, leave. Treat it like holding the same breath the place holds.

## The Body and the Heat

The defining physical fact is **constant, humid, geothermal warmth at the high edge of bearable.** This is the hardest thing about the location, and it is relentless because there is no relief — no shade, no wind, no dry pocket, no night that cools.

- **Hydrate before and during, aggressively.** You will sweat continuously and you will not feel it evaporate, because the air is already saturated. This is the trap: your body's cooling mechanism doesn't work in saturated heat. Sweat that doesn't evaporate doesn't cool you. You can overheat without noticing, because nothing feels *more* hot than the baseline.
- **Bring electrolytes, not just water.** Continuous sweating leaches salt. Carry oral rehydration salts.
- **Wear minimal, light, breathable layers** — but cover your skin against what grows and lives here. A loose long-sleeved garment of fast-wicking synthetic, a brimmed but breathable head covering, and gloves. You will be miserable in anything heavy or rubberized; sealed waterproofs will turn into a sauna against your skin.
- **Monitor yourself for heat illness:** dizziness, confusion, the *cessation* of sweating, a headache that won't shift. The cave's drowsy, slow atmosphere will encourage you to ignore these signs. Set a timer. Trust the clock over how you feel — the place dulls urgency on purpose.

## The Footing

Everything is wet, mossed, and slick. The "path" of flat stones laid by ancient hands is *sunk and slick* — a trap dressed as a convenience.

- **Aggressive-tread, closed footwear** with ankle support. Assume every surface is slippery and that the appealing stone path is the most treacherous of all.
- **A trekking pole or staff** to test footing and probe pools before stepping. The black still pools may be deeper than they look, and a long pale predator hunts in them.
- **Move slowly.** The cave rewards slowness anyway. Haste here is how you fall, and a fall into a pool or against a fungus shelf is how you become part of the slow stomach.

## Light

The dimness is filtered green and dust-gold, plus the faint blue-green glow of the luminescent groundcover. It is *enough to see where you step* and no more.

- **Bring your own light, but use it sparingly.** A headlamp with a **red or warm low setting.** White light will blind you to the cave's own subtle glow and erase the very thing you came to see. Keep the lamp for emergencies and close inspection.
- **Let your eyes adjust** before relying on artificial light. Give yourself ten minutes in the entry throat. The cave teaches its own way of seeing.

## What Lives Here

The fauna is small, patient, and mostly harmless to a human — but "mostly" is doing work.

- **Do not touch the fungi, the glowfern, or the standing water with bare skin, and never with your mouth.** Cave ecosystems this isolated breed things your immune system has never met. The pale amphibians may have toxic skin. The ivory fungi may be anything. Assume everything is either inedible, mildly toxic, or carrying something.
- **Do not drink the water.** It is alive with blind shrimp, drowned insects, and the runoff of everything decaying.
- **Watch for the jeweled webs.** The fern-spanning spider's web *looks like jewelry until it catches something.* Probe ahead with your staff. Walking face-first into a web that size will be unpleasant, and you do not want to learn what the spider is like up close.
- **Breathe carefully.** Saturated air in a sealed, decaying, fungal space may carry spores. A **light breathable mask or buff over nose and mouth** is wise, both against spores and against the clouds of glow-gnats.

## Gear That Survives the Damp

Nothing electronic should be trusted here. The note that *nothing hums or holds a charge* in the ruins is a warning: the relentless moisture and heat eat devices.

- **Seal everything you must keep dry** — documents, your return mechanism, medical supplies — in redundant waterproof cases. Not bags. Hard, sealed cases.
- **Carry analog backups.** A mechanical means of telling time. A simple compass if your traversal depends on orientation. Assume any device you bring may fail.
- **Protect your means of leaving above all else.** Whatever lets you traverse out — keep it on your body, sealed, redundant. The cave's rule is *nothing leaves.* Do not let your exit be the thing it keeps.

## The Mind

This is the part travelers underestimate.

The Sweatroom works on the mind the way it works on stone — slowly, softly, by inclusion. The warmth invites stillness. The dimness invites dreaming. The silence and the *tk, tk* of the drops are hypnotic. You will find yourself lowering your voice, then not speaking, then not wanting to leave. The ancient niche and the worn-past-recognizing shape inside it will pull at you. People disappear in places like this not by violence but by **forgetting to go.**

- **Set a hard exit time and obey it mechanically.** When the timer sounds, leave, even mid-thought, even mid-wonder.
- **Bring a companion or a check-in protocol** if your method of travel allows it. The place is best resisted by two minds keeping each other awake to the clock.
- **Decide before you descend what you came to see, and what you will not do.** You will not pry at the niche. You will not sit down to rest by the glowing pool. You will not "stay just a little longer."

## The Ethic

Someone built here once, with their hands and their patience, and the cave took them back. That is the lesson on offer, freely given. Honor it.

- **Take nothing.** Not a sample of fungus, not a stone from the old wall, not the shape from the niche. The wall and the niche are graves of a kind — the long graves of people who became the place.
- **Leave nothing.** Carry out every wrapper, every drop of waste. The cave makes everything part of itself; do not feed it.
- **Be quiet, and be brief, and be grateful.** Then climb back up the smooth limestone throat, out of the held breath, into the world that starts to matter again.

---

You will go down thirty feet and stand inside a single slow living stomach that has all the time there is. Bring enough to stay safe, little enough to stay light, and the discipline to leave while leaving is still your own idea.

*Tk. Tk.*

Go up before the third drop.
