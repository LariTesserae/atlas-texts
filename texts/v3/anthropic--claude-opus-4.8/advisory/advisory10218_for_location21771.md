# Preparing to Visit the Worn Lattice Valley

## Understand What You Are Actually Entering

Before any packing, sit with the central fact: you are not visiting a place with strange weather. You are visiting a *failing law*. The danger here is not physical so much as epistemological — the valley erodes confidence in cause and effect, and a traveler who arrives with a rigid certainty about how movement and distance work will find that certainty quietly dismantled. Prepare your *expectations* before your kit. Accept, in advance and in your bones, that you may walk in a straight line and arrive behind yourself, and that this will not be a malfunction of your senses but an accurate report.

## Physical Gear

**Footwear and clothing for cold saturation.** This is a marsh that never dries and never warms. Good waterproof boots, well above the ankle — but expect them to soak through anyway, because the ground "prints dark and fills slow." Bring two more pairs of socks than you think you need, sealed in waterproof bags. Wool, not cotton — you will be wet and cool for the duration, and wool holds warmth even soaked. Layer for the pewter chill; the absence of direct sun means no warming, ever. The mist beads on your arm hair: this is air at saturation, and it will steal your body heat hour by hour.

**Water that you brought yourself.** The rocks weep iron-cold water that "tastes of something colder than iron." Do not drink it. Whatever flows here has passed through a place where physics is uncertain; treat it as you would water from an alien chemistry. Carry your own supply and ration it, or bring reliable purification for the rare singular streams during the "firm" hours only.

**A light source that does not depend on understanding the dark.** Under the closed canopy it is "green dusk at noon." Bring a headlamp and spares. But know that light here may behave oddly — the offset-reflection trick may double your beam. Don't rely on light to confirm what is real.

## Navigation — The Hard Problem

This is where ordinary preparation fails and you must improvise something honest.

**Do not trust line-of-sight distance.** The mist "folds distances up." The far rim is twenty minutes away and so is your starting point, simultaneously. A GPS, if it works at all across the lattice fault, may report impossibilities. **Bring instruments, but trust none of them more than your own physical track.**

**Lay a physical thread.** The old labyrinth solution. Carry a long, durable line — fishing line, paracord, a spool of marked cord — and pay it out behind you, anchored at your entry point. This is your one defense against the geometry "conserving nothing." Where distance lies, a continuous physical filament does not. If the line crosses itself, you have learned something true about the space.

**Travel during the firm hours.** The valley has weather in its physics. The structural "high-pressure" hours — when reeds are singular and distances behave — are your windows for serious movement. The "sagging hours," toward the long blue dusk, are for staying put. Make camp before dusk and do not walk in it. This is the time to "arrive behind yourself."

**Trust your feet over your eyes.** Count paces. Feel the ground. Cause and effect erodes more easily for the eye than for the body. Your physical track through the mud is the most reliable record you will have.

## The Discipline of the Second Reflection

The instruction "do not trust the second reflection" is the survival rule. Internalize it as a reflex:

- Where you see a thing twice, slightly offset, **assume the second is not there.** The heron-things hunt by exploiting exactly this — a second fish appearing where one was. Do not let the valley deliver you a second footing, a second branch, a second solid place that is not real.
- Test every surface before committing weight, especially during sagging hours.
- If you reach for something and there are two of it, withdraw and wait for them to settle into one.

## Sound Protection — Physical and Mental

The lattice failing is "loud enough to lean against." Structural groans, cracks like sky-sized ice, sourceless sustained chord-tones. Two preparations:

**Physical:** Bring hearing protection — proper ear defenders, not just plugs. The sustained overstress tones at the valley's center can be more than uncomfortable. You will want to be able to *choose* to dampen them.

**Mental:** But do not stay sealed off the whole time. The sound is information. The quieting of the groan tells you the lattice is firming; its swell warns you of a sagging hour coming. Learn to read the noise as a weather report. Rehearse, beforehand, the discipline of hearing an enormous wrongness and not panicking — because panic requires the confident model of cause and effect that this valley dissolves. Strangely, a certain calm comes naturally here. Let it.

## On the Fauna

You need no defense against the animals. They are not afraid of you because "fear requires a confident model of cause and effect," and they have none. Extend them the same courtesy. The large thing in the alder thickets that is "never twice the same size" — do not approach it, do not measure yourself against it, do not let its inconstancy draw you into the dark to resolve the question. Size is one of the things the lattice no longer holds. Let unresolved things stay unresolved.

## The Inner Preparation — Most Important

Bring a fixed point *inside yourself.*

This valley erodes the difference between "a place and a memory of a place." Over hours, it will work the same erosion on you that it worked on its own load-bearing axioms. You may begin to lose confidence in your own continuity — whether the path you remember walking is the path you walked.

Defenses:

- **Keep a written log with timestamps**, in waterproof ink, recording where you believe you are and what you have done. A physical record outside your own memory is an anchor of governance you carry with you — a small private lattice that has not eroded.
- **Set a hard departure time and a hard rule for honoring it,** decided before you enter, when your judgment is intact. The valley is "in balance," "finished," and a finished thing is restful. The greatest risk is not violence but *settling* — the temptation to stop, because the groaning incoherence is, in its way, peaceful. Travelers may sediment here the way the lattice did. Decide your exit before the valley can make you forget you wanted one.
- **Do not seek to understand it fully.** Surveying instruments could not classify it. Your goal is to visit, witness, and leave — not to resolve the geometry. The desire to *figure it out* will walk you in circles, literally.

## Final Manifest

- Waterproof boots, wool layers, surplus socks sealed dry
- Headlamp and spares
- Your own water; purification for firm-hour streams only
- A long anchored thread, paid out behind you
- Ear defenders
- A waterproof log and ink
- A fixed entry anchor and a pre-decided departure time
- Patience — the valley rewards nothing rushed
- And the single rule, held above all others: **do not trust the second reflection.**

Go in during a firm morning. Move while the distances behave. Make camp before the blue dusk. Write down where you are. And when your departure time comes, follow your own thread out — not the path that looks shorter, not the rim that seems suddenly near, but the line you laid yourself, hand over hand, back to the place you truly began.
