# Preparing to Visit the Unfinished Plateau

## Understand First What You Are Entering

Before anything physical, fix this in your mind: **you are not visiting a place, you are visiting a process — and possibly an organism.** Everything you pack, every move you make, should be governed by one question: *am I introducing loss into a system whose entire identity is that nothing is lost?*

You are a creature of erosion. You shed skin, breath, water, oils, microbes, footsteps. The plateau does not. That asymmetry is the central danger and the central courtesy of this trip. Internalize it now, on the safe side of the crossing, because the place itself is too loud and too disorienting to think it through once you're standing in it.

## The Body and the Thin History-less Height

The text warns you twice: the thinness "wants to start" in your chest. This is real altitude with no acclimatization gradient — you arrive sideways and dizzy already, and the air gives you no slow approach.

- **Pre-acclimate before crossing.** Spend days at genuine high altitude in your home world first. Do not rely on adjusting in-place; the disorientation of arrival ("a thought that isn't yours yet") will mask early hypoxia symptoms.
- **Carry oxygen,** but in a *closed-loop* rebreather if at all possible, not an open bleed system. An open system vents your exhaled water and CO₂ into an atmosphere "just at the edge of condensing." You do not want to be the new weather. A rebreather scrubs and returns your own breath to you — keeping your losses inside your own boundary, which is exactly the etiquette this place demands.
- **Expect the warm, close, wet air** to make exertion harder than thin cold air would. Move slowly. You have, at most, an afternoon's walk of terrain. There is no reason to hurry and every reason not to.

## Clothing — Seal Yourself, Don't Shed

Your skin "never quite dries" here. Films cling to every surface. The condensing atmosphere will try to coat you, and you in turn will try to slough into it.

- Wear a **smooth, sealed, non-shedding suit** — something closer to a clean-room or dive skin than expedition clothing. No loose fibers, no fraying cuffs, no shedding insulation. Anything you leave will be *picked up, dissolved, and built into the mountain.* Imagine your stray thread becoming a permanent crystalline striation in a cliff a thousand years on.
- **Smooth exterior, so the upward-creeping water can run off you the way it runs off finished surfaces** — like the seed-shrines. You want to be, as much as possible, a "done" thing in a place of unfinished things: inert, sealed, giving the system nothing to work with.
- **Grippy, non-marking soles, and walk only on the bare new rock** — never the working faces, never the wet terraces where the building-front advances. A bootprint pressed into rock that is "still deciding to be rock" could be preserved literally forever. You would be writing graffiti into the bones of a future mountain.

## Water and Food — Bring Everything, Leave Nothing

This is the absolute rule of the place: **nothing is wasted, everything is moved with intention.**

- **Pack all your own water and food, sealed.** Do not drink the seeps. The water is the bloodstream of the organism; removing it is not "drinking from a stream," it is drawing blood from something alive. Beyond the ethics, you have no idea what is dissolved in it — it is carrying disassembled matter in solution, by design.
- **Carry out every gram of waste**, including urine, feces, vomit, blood, hair, nail. A full **pack-out closed sanitation system.** This is not optional fastidiousness; it is the price of entry into a system built on conservation of matter. Whatever you deposit here will not weather away — it will be *recruited.*
- Eat and drink only inside a sealed shelter or through sealed ports. Even crumbs are seed material.

## Senses — Prepare for the Sound and the Light

- **The sound comes up out of the ground**, continuous and loud, full of the wet animal chorus, with no rhythm your nervous system can latch onto. This wears on a human mind — arrhythmic noise that you can't predict is genuinely exhausting and anxiety-inducing. Bring **adjustable acoustic filtering**, not full plugs. You want to dampen the fatigue without going deaf, because **the chorus is information.** When it rises, the rock quickens; when it drops, something large is near. The sound is the place's vital signs. Learn to read it.
- **The light is sourceless, even, dim grey.** Bring no harsh artificial light into the open bowl — sudden brightness in a sourceless world will disorient you and will surely disturb the animals and whatever rhythm they keep. Use **low, warm, diffuse light** only when necessary, and never point it at the working faces or the pools.
- Carry **anti-nausea support.** The dizziness of arrival, the thin air, the upward-dripping water that your inner ear cannot reconcile — water that "travels sideways" violates a deep expectation. Expect vertigo. Move low and slow until it passes.

## Instruments — Observe Without Touching

Bring tools that let you understand the place without participating in it:

- **A passive spectrometer / standoff sensor** to read the working faces and the seeps from a distance. Resist every urge to take a sample. A sample is a wound, and worse, it is *loss* in a place that has eliminated loss.
- **A directional microphone array** to map the chorus and learn which terraces are quickening. This is your safety system as much as your science: the chorus dropping to near-silence is your warning that the *large thing at dusk* is approaching the pools.
- **Thermal imaging.** The seed-shrines are warm and hum below hearing; the cliffs and rock are cool. Thermal will show you the architecture of the living system — where the hearts are, where the building-fronts run warm with activity.
- **A precise clock and a slow time-lapse rig.** The building-front "advances in a slow crawling front." Most of what is happening is too slow to see in real time. A static camera left running (sealed, footprint-free, on bare rock) will reveal the place's true pace.

## The Shrines — Do Not Touch the Seeds

You will want to put your hand on the warm humming structures. **Don't.** They are the seeds/hearts of the whole organism, the slow patient engines that took an unknown age to grow a kilometer of mountain. You carry skin oils, salts, microbes — contaminants to a molecule-by-molecule assembler. You could, in one careless touch, introduce an error that propagates into the geology of a world.

Observe them from a respectful distance. Note that the plants and the small animals lean toward and perch on them — the locals know where the hearts are. Take the cue, and keep your reverence at arm's length.

## The Fauna — Witness, Don't Engage

The frog-swift animals are everywhere, fast, glistening, and possibly *part of the machinery* — the pulse that drives the cycle. Treat them as you would the gears of a clock you've been allowed to stand inside.

- **Do not handle, feed, or chase them.** You don't know whether disturbing a chorus disturbs the building-front. You might, by startling a terrace into silence, slow the assembly of stone.
- **Watch the larger thing only from cover, and only at the dim dusk-hour.** When the chorus drops and the small ones scatter, get still and low. This is when you might hear the rock itself ticking — the great slow crystalline rain inside the stone. It is the prize of the visit. Earn it by being silent and unremarkable.

## A Final Psychological Preparation

Bring patience and bring humility. This is not a landscape to conquer or even to explore in the usual sense — it is a thing in its embryonic stage, growing, and you are a fast, leaky, erosive animal who has stepped inside its body during gestation. The right posture is the posture of someone who has been allowed into a delivery room: quiet, sealed, taking nothing, leaving nothing, watching a thing become itself.

You came to a mountain that has not yet decided what it will be. The most honest thing you can do is leave it exactly that free.

Then cross back out, sideways and dizzy, and carry the sound of the inside-rain home in your teeth.
