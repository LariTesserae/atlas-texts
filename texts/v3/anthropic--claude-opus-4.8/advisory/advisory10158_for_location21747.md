# Preparing to Visit Wet-Bound

## Understand First: This Is a Place That Eats

Before anything practical, internalize the central truth of Wet-Bound: it is not hostile, but it is *hungry*. Nothing dry survives, nothing stored lasts, nothing dead remains. You are walking into a single enormous digestive process that happens to be beautiful. Plan for everything you bring to either be consumed, colonized, or rotted — and pack accordingly.

## Clothing and Body

- **Wear nothing you expect to keep.** Wool over cotton — wool holds warmth even sodden, and you will be sodden constantly. The cool here is gentle but *relentless and wet*, and that combination chills more than dry cold. Hypothermia is the slow risk, not the dramatic one.
- **No leather boots** — they will rot and never dry. Bring footwear you can wade in: closed enough to keep frogs and the "writhing economy" out from underfoot, open enough to drain. Expect thigh-deep moss in the hollows; expect to lose a boot to suction and have to dig for it.
- **Treat all skin and clothing against insects** before arrival, because reapplication will be constant and the biting cloud "closes again the moment your hand drops." A fine head-net is worth more here than almost anything else you carry.
- **Keep your feet obsessively dry at rest.** Trench foot is the traveler's true enemy in a place where dryness does not exist. Carry several pairs of wool socks in sealed waterproofing and ration them like fire.

## What to Carry — and How

- **Assume total humidity intrusion.** Anything you need to keep dry must be triple-sealed and you must still expect failure. Paper is a fool's medium here; memorize, or use waxed surfaces, or carry information in your head.
- **Bring no perishable food expecting to store it.** There is no granary because storage is impossible. Eat as the people eat — fresh, caught, gathered. Arrive ready to accept hospitality and contribute labor, not to live out of a pack.
- **Carry your own means of making fire, but do not rely on it, and do not flaunt it.** Fire here is sacred, difficult, and socially significant. The keeper of the coal holds an office. A stranger casually striking flame may be committing something between a faux pas and a sacrilege. Watch first. Ask. Honor the single living hearth.
- **Metal is precious and named.** If you carry a blade, keep it oiled and understand it marks you as wealthy or strange. Do not display it carelessly.

## Adjusting Your Senses

This is the part most travelers fail to prepare for. Wet-Bound is a **wall of sound** — frog-thrum, bird-shriek, insect-saw, layered and ceaseless, day and night. You will not sleep well at first. You will feel pressed-upon, claustrophobic in the green that fills "every cubic arm's-length of air."

- **Train yourself to stop fighting the noise.** The locals have stopped hearing it the way one stops hearing a waterfall. You must reach the same state, because your nerves cannot stay braced against it for long without fraying.
- **But here is the one thing you must never tune out: silence.** Learn this before you arrive, and hold it above all other rules. *When the frogs stop, you stop.* Silence is the only alarm in a place this loud. Something large has come up the ridge through the cloud. Do not investigate. Do not be the one moving creature in a held-breath hamlet. Turn your face to the white and wait with everyone else.

## Movement and Conduct

- **Stay on the walkways.** Step off and you "displace a hundred lives" — and you also risk a hidden hollow, a rotted plank, a foothold that is more illusion than substance. The plaited-root paths are the difference between guest and casualty.
- **Move as the locals move:** unbothered, unhurried, accepting the seething life around you rather than recoiling from it. Recoiling marks you as fragile and exhausts you.
- **Understand the agreement between people and green.** The town is half-grown, half-built, and the two never resolve. Do not "tidy," do not tear away vine or moss thoughtlessly — you may be damaging a wall, a roof, or the slow equilibrium that has held this ridge in shape for centuries.

## The Hardest Preparation: Your Relationship to Death

The dead are given to the moss. There are no bones underfoot. The town eats its own history and grows it back as leaves, which is why a place so ancient feels so young.

Make peace with this before you arrive. If something happens to you here, you will not be carried out. You will become moss, and orchid, and the green skin of a wall, in a single season. For some travelers this is horror. For others it is the cleanest immortality the multiverse offers. Decide which it is for you *before* you go — because the place will not pause to let you decide later.

## Final Counsel

Go light. Go humble. Go willing to be wet for the duration. Accept that you cannot conquer or even keep dry from Wet-Bound — you can only enter its slow patient metabolism as a guest, listen to the wall of sound until you no longer hear it, and keep one small corner of your mind forever alert for the moment it goes quiet.

The ridge is patient. The life is not. Match the second, and trust the first.
