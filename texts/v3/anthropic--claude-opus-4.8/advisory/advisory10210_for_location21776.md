# Preparing to Visit the Young Dark Forest

## First, Accept What You're Losing

The single hardest adjustment: **your eyes are now nearly useless, and that is permanent for the duration.** You cannot train your way out of it in a packing session. What you *can* do is begin, weeks before departure, deliberately practicing in darkness — moving through your own home blindfolded, learning to trust hands, feet, ears, the air on your skin. Arrive having already grieved your sight. The travelers who panic are the ones who keep waiting for their eyes to "adjust." They never will. The glow only tells you where things are, not what they are.

Train your *attention* to live in your skin and ears. This is the real survival skill here.

## Clothing and the Body

- **Cover everything, but breathe.** The heat is wet and total; you will sweat through anything heavy and overheat. Choose thin, tightly-woven full coverage — long sleeves, gloves, hood, and crucially something over the lower face. The ground is *sharp, fresh-split stone* and the growth is a constant low-grade abrasion. Bare skin will be cut, stung, colonized.
- **Footwear that grips young rock and survives mud that "hasn't decided to be rock."** Stiff soles to protect against fresh edges, but flexible enough to feel terrain. You read the floor with your feet here — don't seal that sense off entirely.
- **Knee and shin protection.** You are wading through living matter to the waist. Your legs are doing the exploring.
- **A snug head covering.** The canopy holds the great ones, and it sheds — water, debris, and worse. You don't want anything getting into ears or hair.

## What to Carry on Your Skin, Not Your Eyes

- **A long, blunt-tipped probing staff.** Your most important tool. You extend your reach into the tangle, test whether a corridor is open or about to close, feel for worked edges and for the shudder of large movement. Blunt so you don't kill things needlessly — they don't mind you; don't change that.
- **Tactile waymarking.** Forget breadcrumbs and forget sightlines. Leave knotted cords, notched markers — but understand they may be **overgrown, eaten, or rearranged within the hour.** The place unmakes itself. Plan routes that don't depend on the path staying open. Always know how to return by a *different* route than you came.
- **A method for keeping water out of your ears without going deaf.** You navigate by sound. But the constant drip and the rain handed down through the layers will flood you. A loose mesh or filter, not a plug.

## Hearing in a Wall of Sound

The sound *is* the place, and it will exhaust you faster than the heat. Prepare for it like altitude:

- Practice listening *through* noise. Learn to filter a wall of overlapping sound for the one thing that matters — the change. The silence-where-there-was-noise. The new note. Survival here is hearing the **edit**, not the song.
- Bring nothing that adds noise. Quiet gear, quiet movement. You want to be more terrain, not an event.
- Expect the sudden rain-roar from above and learn not to flinch at it. Flinching in sharp terrain is how you fall.

## Water and Food

- **Do not bring much water — learn to harvest it.** The leaves are a slow machine handing moisture downward. You drink from the cupped surfaces, but it arrives "tasting of everything it passed." Bring purification you trust and the patience to use it. Standing water doesn't exist here; don't look for it.
- **Bring all your food.** Eating local in a place this fecund and this unmapped is reckless. Pack dense, simple rations and protect them — *everything is layered fauna*, and the moment you open food, the place reorganizes around it.
- Eat covered, eat fast, leave no trace. You don't want to teach anything that you are a source.

## Heat and the Young Ground

- **Respect that the ground is still settling.** The warmth from below is geological youth. Test before you commit weight; tacky, half-set surfaces and fresh-split edges mean the floor lies. The living matter often *is* the floor — the true ground is a rumor below your waist. Don't go reaching for it.
- Pace yourself slowly. Overheating in total darkness in a turbulent, collapsing, regrowing maze is the classic way to die here.

## On the Tool-Makers

You will find worked edges, placed stones, knotted vine. **You will not meet them, and you should not try.** Something patient, simple, and perfectly adapted lives here by *not owning* the place. The correct posture is humility:

- Don't disturb their marks. Don't collect them. Don't follow them hoping to find their makers.
- Read them as instruction. They tell you the place *can* be lived in by something that reads it without eyes. You are seeing the standard you've fallen short of.
- Leave no marks of your own that could be mistaken for territory or threat.

## The Mindset to Pack Last

This place stays alive by never being finished. Bring no expectation of stability, landmarks, or progress. You are not exploring a map — you are moving through a thing that is constantly remaking itself around your body, that doesn't notice you, doesn't resent you, and won't preserve your trail.

Go in as a passing event. Touch lightly. Listen for the change. Trust the staff, the feet, the skin.

And keep finding the small deliberate marks — and let them keep reminding you that you are not very good at this yet, and that being a humble, careful guest is the only version of *good* available to you here.
