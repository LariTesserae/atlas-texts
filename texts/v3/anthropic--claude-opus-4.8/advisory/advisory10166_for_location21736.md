# Preparing to Visit the Town Inside a Seed

## What You Are Walking Into

Understand first what kind of place this is, because it determines everything about how you prepare. This is not a hostile world in the way a vacuum or an acid swamp is hostile. It will not attack you. It is *indifferent* in a very specific, very total way — it is a sealed chamber running its own slow chemistry, and you are an intrusion of wetness into a world that has forbidden water. You are the contradiction here, not the dry ferns.

The two things that will define your visit are **heat** and **dryness**. Everything else — the noise, the empty streets, the racing plants — is atmosphere you will adjust to. The heat and dryness are what could kill you, and they are exactly the two things the place itself does not register as dangerous, which makes them treacherous. The description notes you "would not get thirsty, oddly." Do not trust that. That is the place's logic, not your body's. The chamber bakes at kiln-warmth and your body will lose water you cannot feel leaving — through your breath, through skin, into air so dry it drinks from you silently. The absence of thirst is the most dangerous feature here, because it removes your warning system.

## Clothing and Body

Dress as you would for a desert that never cools and has no shade — because that is functionally what this is, only the heat comes from below and from the walls instead of overhead.

- **Loose, light, full-coverage layers.** Cover skin not against cold but against radiant heat from the amber walls and the warm floor. Pale, breathable fabric. You want air moving between cloth and skin.
- **Footwear with thick, heat-resistant soles.** The floor bakes from beneath. You will "feel it first in the soles of the feet." Thin shoes will become a slow torment. Bring rigid soles that put air or insulation between you and the grit.
- **A head and throat covering.** The heat reaches "the back of the throat" — meaning you are inhaling oven air. A loose scarf you can breathe through, ideally dampened from your own supply early on, will buffer your airway and slow the moisture loss from each breath.
- **Eye protection.** The light is dim, tea-and-candle colored, so you do not need sun-darkness. But heat-currents lift grit in spirals down the streets, and crushed kernel-dust and salt crystals are abrasive. Light wraparound goggles, lightly tinted.

## Water — The Central Problem

You must bring all your water, and you must bring more than feels reasonable, because the place lies to you about needing it.

- **Carry water on a schedule, not on thirst.** Drink by the clock. Set intervals and obey them even when you feel fine. *Especially* when you feel fine. In a place that suppresses the thirst signal, fine is exactly when you are most quietly dehydrating.
- **Insulate your supply.** The ambient heat will warm your water toward undrinkable bath-temperature. Bring insulated vessels. Warm water also fails to register as refreshing, which compounds the schedule problem.
- **Accept the contamination question.** Here is a subtle ethical and practical matter: water is *forbidden* here, in the world's own chemistry. Whatever you spill — sweat, splashed drink, the moisture of your breath condensing — is the one thing this world has never known. A drop of your water on the dry floor may do something. It may do nothing. But you are introducing the prohibited element. Drink carefully, do not spill, and consider that you might be a tiny seasonal rain the chamber has waited for without wanting.

## The Noise

You arrive expecting silence and the room *roars*. This is psychologically jarring and worth preparing for, because the wrongness of a loud empty town can produce a low panic — the lizard-brain insistence that something so loud must contain something alive and watching.

- **Bring ear protection you can modulate** — not to block the sound, but to take the edge off it during long stays. The constant pod-detonations are sharp reports; over hours they fatigue you. Filtered plugs that lower volume without deafening you let you still learn the rhythm.
- **Brief yourself, repeatedly, that the noise is botanical and thermal.** Nothing watches you. The creak is a stalk growing. The crack is a pod splitting. Rehearse this until it is reflex, because your nervous system will keep generating false alarms in the empty doorways. The deep tonal hum beneath everything — the resin resonating — will get into your chest and your sleep. Some find it unbearable, some find it a comfort. Know which you are before committing to a long stay.

## Provisions and Time

- **All food brought in.** There is nothing here you can eat. The plants feed on heat and resinous air; the resin chemistry is alien and possibly toxic. Treat everything botanical as inedible, including curiosity-tastes. Bring dry, dense, low-residue food.
- **Plan a strict duration and a hard exit.** The place has no day or night — the light is just "the temperature made visible," constant, sourceless. You will lose your sense of time completely. Bring a reliable independent clock and set a firm departure. People who let timeless places hold them tend to overstay, and here overstaying means quietly drying out while feeling perfectly fine.

## Footing and Hazard

- The plants grow *visibly fast* and move. A frond will topple, a stalk will lean across a path you just used. Watch your footing; the streets are "the width of a hand," the construction stacked and leaning and softened by centuries. Dried-mud and split pod-shell will not bear weight reliably. Test before you climb. The ladders of lashed stalk are ancient and cured brittle.
- Pods detonate. Stand clear of swollen ones. A bursting pod is not dangerous but is startling at close range and may scatter hard fragments at speed.

## What to Bring Out

Nothing, ideally. This is a finished place, weathering at the pace of centuries, slowly inherited by its plants. It is intimate — "an intimate room of a world." Taking a pod, a tool-flake, a piece of the worn threshold removes something from a balance that has held for ages. And there is the deeper unease the place asks you to sit with: the people here are "not dead so much as finished." This is a graveyard that does not look like one, a home whose tenants walked off through a door that no longer exists. Treat it as you would treat any ruin that might still, in some chemistry you don't understand, be *occupied*.

## The Right Frame of Mind

Go in quiet. Go in respectful. Understand before you arrive that you are visiting the inside of something *waiting* — a seed holding its whole life in suspension before a germination that the rules of its world have made impossible. There is grief in this place if you stay long enough to feel it: a town that did all its living already, loudly, dryly, without witness, and is now only a stage for fast green inheritors rattling toward a light that is only warmth pretending to be a sun.

Prepare your body for the heat and the lie of un-thirst. Prepare your mind for the roar and the timelessness. And prepare to leave before the place quietly finishes you too — folded, like the others, into the dry patient chemistry of a room that never asked you to come and will not notice you go.
