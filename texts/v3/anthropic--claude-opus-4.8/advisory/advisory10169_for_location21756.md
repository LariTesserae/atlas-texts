# Preparing to Visit the Cistern Above

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before anything physical, understand this: you are not visiting a *place* so much as a *moment that hasn't closed*. The Cistern is a seam in unfinished law. The danger here is not primarily that it will hurt you (though it can), but that it operates by rules that are still arriving. Pack accordingly — but more importantly, *expect* accordingly. Bring no assumptions you would mind seeing broken.

Now, the practicalities.

---

## The First Enemy: The Sound

The roar is the true gatekeeper, and most travelers underestimate it because sound, in their experience, is survivable. This sound is not. It is described as a *medium*, a continuous tearing that comes up through bone when the ears fail. Treat it as you would a hostile atmosphere.

**What to bring:**
- **Layered hearing protection** — molded earplugs *and* over-ear defenders, the kind worn near jet engines, rated for sustained extreme decibels. Use both together.
- **A bite guard / mouthpiece.** The roar transmits through the jaw. A soft dental guard reduces the bone-conduction headache that will otherwise build behind your eyes within minutes.
- **A way to communicate without voice.** You will not be heard, and you will not hear. Bring a slate and waterproof markers, or better, a small set of *pre-agreed hand signals* if traveling with others. The vanished people learned this; you should arrive already fluent in it.
- **Expect disorientation.** Total sound is a sensory white-out. Balance suffers when the ears are overwhelmed. Move slowly. Touch the rock often to anchor yourself.

Understand that no protection makes it *quiet*. It only makes it *survivable for longer*. Plan your visit in minutes, not hours.

---

## The Second Condition: The Breathing Air

The air "is more water than air." You will be breathing in a saturated, near-drowning coolness — not rain, not submersion, but something between. Cold, mineral, faintly green.

**What to bring:**
- **A balaclava or fine cloth over nose and mouth** to break the densest spray, so each breath isn't pure mist. Don't fully seal — you still need air.
- **Insulating layers that work wet.** Cotton will kill you here; it holds the chill against the skin. Wear wool or modern synthetic base layers under a sealed shell. The cold is the *quiet* killer — you won't feel hypothermia arriving over the roar and the wonder.
- **A drysuit or sealed wet-weather gear** for the body, but leave the hands somewhat free — you will want to touch the soft stone, and gloves dull that once-in-a-lifetime contact. Carry fingerless options.
- **A way to warm up after.** Whatever exit you use, have heat waiting on the other side. The chill follows you out.

---

## The Third Hazard: The Unfinished Stone

The rock is young, soft as cooling wax in places, *rising*, splitting, growing over things. This is beautiful and lethal.

**Rules for the feet and hands:**
- **Test every surface before weighting it.** Some stone is hard; some takes a thumbprint; some is mid-decision. A surface that held you a moment ago may have swelled or split.
- **Do not stand where the wall is actively pushing out a spur.** The grinding is "deeper than the roar" — you will not hear the warning. Watch the rock the way you'd watch a cliff in an earthquake.
- **Stay on the pale lip.** It is the most settled geology in the room. The interior is in flux.
- **If you carve a mark** — and you will be tempted — know that the stone heals over it. Carve deep, or carve fast, or accept that the mountain is editing you in real time. There is a strange grief in this. Be ready for it.

A warning about the soft stone and your own body: **do not press a hand into wax-soft rock that is rising.** It can harden around you in an age, or a moment — the rules here aren't settled. Touch only what you can withdraw from instantly.

---

## The Fourth Impossibility: The Hung Water

The cathedral-sized column does not fall. It breathes, throws arms, detaches into drifting wet boulders that cross the room. This is the centerpiece and the place's gentlest impossibility — gentle until a floating mass of sea drifts through where you're standing.

**How to behave around it:**
- **Never turn your back on the column.** It moves. A free-floating mass can cross the room. Keep it in your peripheral vision always.
- **Do not touch the wall of water to "test" it.** It has edges, not skin. You don't know where you end and it begins, and creatures move inside it. Worse: the rule keeping it *up* may not extend to keeping *you* up if you enter. You might float; you might fall; the place hasn't decided. Don't volunteer to be the experiment.
- **If a mass detaches toward you, move along the lip, low and braced.** Don't run — the footing won't allow it. Sidestep it. It will likely re-merge with the wall before reaching you, but "likely" is the strongest word this place offers.

---

## The Living Things

The fauna pass through water and air without ceremony — child-sized or dog-sized shapes, a pale eye that regards you and withdraws. The flora is harmless: pale tough not-quite-moss, translucent fleshy stalks leaning at the light.

**Conduct:**
- **Do not feed, touch, or pursue the creatures.** They are at home in conditions that are killing you slowly. You are the fragile, out-of-place thing here, not them.
- **If one regards you, regard it back, and do nothing.** It will withdraw. You are a curiosity to a thing that cannot imagine quiet.
- **Take nothing living.** The flora survives on a balance you don't understand and can't replicate.

---

## What to Pack for the Mind

This is the part most travelers neglect, and it is the part that matters most in a seam.

- **Go with a question, not a conquest.** The vanished people came to *leave a word in the throat of the world before it closed.* They understood the assignment. They did not try to master the place; they tried to *say something to it.* Decide what you would carve, if you carve. Make it true.
- **Accept the loneliness.** You cannot speak here. If you travel with companions, you will each be alone inside the roar, together. This is a particular grief. Prepare for it the way you'd prepare for a long dark.
- **Carry humility about the predecessors.** Their shelter faced the water — they wanted to *watch*. Leave their chisel, their bowl, their lashed poles untouched. They are part of the place's word now. Add to the record; do not loot it.
- **Set a hard time limit and obey it.** "If you stay long — and few could." The place erodes the will the way it heals over the carvings. Have a partner watching a timepiece, or set a vibrating alarm against the skin (you won't hear a sound one). When it triggers, you leave. No "one more mark."

---

## The Exit

Note before anything else that there is **no path** — you arrive the way a held breath is reached, through the rock just behind the everyday. Whatever method brought you (your traveler's means, your seam-walking), **confirm it works in both directions before you commit to entering.** Test the return. A place still deciding its rules is no place to discover you cannot leave.

The opening to true daylight is high above — bright, ordinary, indifferent. It is not your exit. It is the place's reminder that the normal world continues somewhere, clean and unbothered. Let it orient you. Let it be the thing you look at when the impossibilities crowd in.

---

## Final Briefing

You are going to a small room the size of a hall that contains a hanging sea, a rising mountain, a sound that is a substance, and the unfinished physics of creation showing through like canvas under thin paint. Bring:

- Double hearing protection and a bite guard
- Wet-rated insulation; nothing cotton
- A face cloth against the spray
- A slate and markers; rehearsed hand signals
- A timepiece you feel rather than hear
- Heat waiting at your exit
- A tested two-way passage
- A single true thing you wish to say to the world while it is still being made

And leave behind every assumption that water falls, that stone is solid, that sound fades, and that you are the most important thing in any room you enter.

It is very cool there, and very loud, and very nearly empty, and the light is perfectly ordinary — and none of that will explain it to you either. Go anyway. Carve quickly. Come home before the rock decides what you are.
