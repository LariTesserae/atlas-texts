# Preparing for the Roost-Plain

## The First Truth: This Is a Place Built Against Your Senses

Most hostile environments threaten the body first. The Roost-Plain threatens the *mind*, and it does so through the two senses you most rely on to feel sane: sight and hearing. Prepare for those before anything else, or the rest of your preparation won't matter, because you'll panic and ruin it.

---

## Hearing — Your Most Urgent Problem

The sound is not a hazard you endure. It is the atmosphere. Sustained sound pressure from a colony of that density will cause permanent hearing damage in minutes and can induce nausea, disorientation, and panic through the body alone — your chest cavity, your sinuses, the fluid in your inner ear all respond to it whether you want them to or not.

**Bring, in layers:**

- **Deep-insertion foam earplugs**, the industrial kind rated for the highest decibel attenuation you can find. Pack a dozen pairs — they degrade with moisture and you will be in a wet place.
- **Over-ear electronic defenders** worn *over* the plugs. Get active models that compress and limit rather than simply muffle, so you can still perceive your companions and your own footing.
- **A bone-conduction comms unit** that bypasses the ear canal entirely. This is how you'll talk to anyone. Speech through the air is impossible here.

Accept that even with all of this, the sound will reach you through your skull, your ribs, your teeth. The text is honest: the creeping deafness is mercy. You are protecting against permanent injury, not achieving silence. There is no silence to achieve.

**Mental rehearsal:** Spend time before the trip in the loudest tolerable environments you can find — industrial sites, engine rooms — wearing your protection, practicing tasks. The goal is to learn that you can still *think and act* while submerged in noise. The first untrained instinct is to freeze and cover your head. That instinct can kill you here.

## Sight — Bring Your Own World

There is effectively no light. The faint biolumiscence the text describes is real but useless for navigation — it's a thing your retina half-invents after an hour of dark adaptation.

**The dilemma:** Bright light may disturb a colony that "sees" by sound but may still respond to a sudden glare, and worse, *you* don't want to lose your hard-won dark adaptation by glancing at a bright source. But you absolutely need to see your footing on slick, rounded, basin-pitted stone.

**Solution:**

- **Red and deep-amber lighting**, low and downcast, aimed at your feet, never up at the ceiling. Preserve scotopic vision.
- **A thermal imager.** This is your best friend here. The whole world is built on heat — warm-bodied creatures, rotting warm substrate, condensation tides driven by breath-fog. A thermal camera will let you read the environment the way the inhabitants do: as a landscape of warmth and cold. The basins will read black-cold, the living carpet overhead a single seething heat-mass, the warm rain a faint texture. This is the one instrument that lets you perceive this place *on its own terms*.
- **Echolocation aids** — there are ultrasonic-to-visual mapping devices used by cave researchers. In a world that is *made of* echolocation, such a tool turns the screaming into something legible.

## The Body — Damp, Rot, and Falling

**The floor will try to drop you.** Rounded basalt and limestone, water-slicked, no sharp edges to grab, shallow basins that are deeper-looking than they are and a substrate beside them that is *soft, warm, and deep*. You do not want to fall into the guano-mat. It is warm with active rot, generating the entire insect economy of the world, and it will be a biological catastrophe to be immersed in it.

- **Stiff-soled boots with the most aggressive wet-rock tread available.** Test them on wet limestone before you commit.
- **Trekking poles** — the third and fourth points of contact matter enormously when the floor offers no handholds.
- **Move slowly. Always.** There is no reward here for speed and a permanent penalty for haste.

**The air is a biological soup.** Warm breath-fog, falling warm rain, clouds of insects, and — critically — **the spores.** Fungus and "something simpler than fungus" coat every wet surface. Guano dust aerosolizes. Real-world bat caves give humans histoplasmosis, a serious lung infection, from exactly this. An *unknown* world's version of this could be far worse.

- **A sealed respirator with P100/N100 filtration minimum**, and seriously consider a **powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR)** with a full hood. This solves spores, ammonia from guano (which can reach toxic, eye-burning concentrations in enclosed roosts), and gives you a sealed visor for the thermal display.
- **Test your air going in.** Bring a multi-gas meter. Closed systems running on furious rot can build dangerous ammonia, CO₂, and methane, and can drop oxygen in pockets. The "weather" here is breath and rot — that is also a description of an atmosphere that may not be breathable in the low basins.
- **Full-coverage sealed clothing**, smooth-surfaced, tucked and taped at every seam. You do not want insects, spores, or rain finding skin. Plan for **decontamination on return** — strip, bag, and dispose of the outer layer before you re-enter your home world.

## Provisions — The Closed Gut Rule

Internalize the central fact: **nothing here is nourished by the sun. Everything is nourished by what has already lived.** This is a closed system that runs on its own remains.

**Therefore: eat and drink nothing from this place. Touch the water with nothing but sealed instruments.**

The black mirror basins are part of the recycling — breath-condensation collected over the rot-mat, full of whatever rises from the substrate. The insects, the fungus, the creatures — all of it is one organism eating itself. You are not part of that loop and you must never, ever feed yourself into it, in either direction.

- Bring **all your own water and food, sealed.** Calculate generously; the labor of moving slowly through this place while fully encumbered burns more than you'd think.
- **Leave nothing behind.** Not waste, not a dropped tool, not a body. A closed system that has found perfect balance over an unspeakable span does not need a novel input, and you do not want to be the contamination that the place spends the next age incorporating.

## Navigation — There Is No Edge

This is the quiet killer. The text is explicit: you will not find the walls, the door, the shaft of light. There is no landmark, no horizon, no source and no drain. The rolling humps and basins look identical in every direction, and the one sense that could orient you — hearing — is saturated to uselessness.

**You must bring your way home with you, because the place offers none.**

- **A fixed, anchored entry beacon** at your point of arrival — bright (in your private spectrum), loud (in a band you can detect through your gear), and physically immovable. This is your only door. Treat it as sacred.
- **Inertial navigation** — a unit that tracks your movement independent of external reference, since there are no external references. GPS will not work. Star-fixes will not work. Magnetic compass may not work. Dead-reckoning by an inertial system, cross-checked against:
- **A laid line.** Old, simple, reliable cave-diving practice: run a physical guideline from your beacon as you go, and follow it back. In a place with no edges and a floor that erases your footprints in damp, the line is your lifeline in the most literal sense.
- **A hard time limit, enforced by alarm**, with generous margin. Set it before you're tired, before you're disoriented, before the place has worked on your mind.

## The Last Preparation — The Loneliness

The most important warning in the description is the last one. This place produces a specific psychological injury, and you should know its shape before it finds you.

The inhabitants were *born from the floor.* They have no concept of *elsewhere.* They have never wanted out because they cannot imagine an out. And you — you came from somewhere. You carry the one thing this entire screaming world does not contain: the knowledge of an outside.

That asymmetry is described as "the loneliest thing in the roost," and it is. You will be standing inside a roaring, teeming, *full* world — millions of living things, presence touching presence — and you will be the only entity in it that knows the word *home.* That kind of loneliness, surrounded by overwhelming life that cannot conceive of you, can hollow a person out faster than fear.

**Prepare for it by:**

- **Never going alone.** Go with at least one other traveler, bone-conduction linked, so there is one other mind in the place that holds the concept of *elsewhere* with you. This is not optional. It is the single most protective measure you can take.
- **Keeping the visit short.** The place is balanced, ancient, indifferent, and it has all the time there has ever been. You do not. Go in, perceive it, hold your concept of the outside firmly, and leave before the place starts to feel like the only world.
- **Knowing your purpose before you enter, and finishing it.** Wonder without an anchor turns to despair here. Have a task. Complete it. Follow the line back to the beacon. Go home.

Go home while you still remember, fully, that there is one.
