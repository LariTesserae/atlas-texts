# Preparing to Visit The Roost Beneath

## Understanding What You're Entering

Before anything else, hold this in your mind: **you do not matter here.** This is not a place to be conquered, photographed, or understood — it is a place to pass through with the humility of weather. The single most dangerous mistake you could make is treating this colony as an audience, an obstacle, or a resource. It is none of these. It is simply busy, in a way older and more total than you. Prepare yourself emotionally for irrelevance. Most travelers are not ready for a place that will not acknowledge them.

## Your Body and the Air

The heat-from-below and the thinned air are a treacherous combination. The warmth invites you to relax and breathe shallowly; the altitude punishes shallow breathing.

- **Acclimatize first.** Spend time at altitude before crossing the threshold. The "emptied" quality of each breath is real oxygen scarcity, and the body heat masks the danger by keeping you comfortable. You can hypoxiate while feeling perfectly warm.
- **Dress in layers you can manage in total darkness.** The ceiling-warmth is constant but the chamber goes suddenly, sharply cold during the pourings, walls steaming as heat leaves. You'll want something light against the resident heat and something to throw on during the empty minutes.
- **Wear closed footwear with good sole feedback.** You'll be reading the floor through your feet — the warmth, the faint give of fungal filament, the slope toward the low places. Boots too thick will blind you. Boots too thin will let the heat exhaust you and risk the pale crawlers. Find the middle.
- **Cover your hair and mouth, but loosely.** During a pouring, thousands of wings will pass at the width of a breath. A tight covering muffles your echolocation-by-breath; a loose one keeps the bodies from tangling.

## Provisions

- **Carry all your own water — and know that you cannot replenish.** "What pools in the low places is not water." Treat the chamber as a sealed dry environment. Bring more than you think you need; the heat wicks moisture from you constantly even as the air feels still.
- **Bring food that needs no preparation, no fire, no light.** Fire is unthinkable here — the air is thin, the floor is dried organic matter, the ceiling is living and flammable. Eat by touch.
- **Bring nothing scented, nothing rotting, nothing that drips.** You are entering a place fed entirely from elsewhere. Do not become a smell that registers.

## Light, and Why You Should Mostly Refuse It

You will want a light. Resist it.

- **Carry one small, dim, red-shielded source for emergencies only.** White light will not help you navigate — the chamber has no features that light reveals usefully — and it may agitate the colony, which is the one thing that can endanger you. A sudden brightness could trigger a confused, partial pouring.
- **Train your dark-adaptation beforehand.** Spend hours in true darkness in the days before you cross, so your eyes are already opened wide and your other senses already reaching. You navigate here by the *shape of the warmth*, by breath-echo off the fluted glass, by the give of the ground. These are learnable. Practice walking blind. Practice listening to rooms.

## The One Real Rule: Reading the Pourings

Everything dangerous about this place lives in the transition between stillness and flight. **Learn to feel a pouring coming before it arrives.**

- The sequence is: breath quickens → leather-creak rises → rustle → rushing → the ceiling peels at once.
- The moment you feel the breath quicken — **stop moving and find your stance.** Do not try to reach the threshold. Do not try to outrun it.
- **Stand still. Stay low and balanced. Make no sound.** A cry confuses the river, and a confused river is the only thing here that can hurt you — both you and them. Bodies moving at that density, redirected by panic, can crush, smother, trample against stone.
- **Let it break around you.** Wings will miss your face by a breath's width. This is not an attack; you are a rock in a current. Be a rock.
- Practice this stillness in advance until it is a reflex and not a decision. In the moment, your animal body will want to flinch, swat, run. You must have already trained that instinct out of yourself.

## Timing Your Movement

You may walk only "when it is between hungers" — in the held-breath stillness, never during a pouring, and ideally not in the uncertain minutes just after the colony resettles, when the warmth is uneven and the floor-creatures are stirring. Plan your route in short stages between pourings. Memorize each segment by touch and echo as you go, because you will need to retrace it blind.

## What to Bring, What to Leave

There is a niche, shoulder-height, hand-cut — the only worked thing that admits to being worked. Someone tended or worshipped or thanked this place.

- **Consider bringing a small offering of your own.** Not because the colony will know — it will never know you were here — but because the niche is a message from one passing human to the next: *I was here, I was small, I was grateful for the warmth.* A shaped stone, a worked thing, a mark added to the tally. This is the etiquette of the place, the quiet courtesy travelers have kept for generations.
- **Take nothing.** No bone ring, no glass blade, no guano-marble sample. The offerings are not artifacts to collect; they are an ongoing conversation you are invited to join, not loot.

## The Mental Preparation That Matters Most

Rehearse, before you go, the feeling of being unwitnessed. You will spend your whole visit inside the presence of an enormous living thing that is wholly unaware of you. This unsettles people more than danger does. There is no relationship to be had here, no acknowledgment, no story in which you are the protagonist.

Go in knowing you are weather. Move through gently. Leave the warmth as you found it. And carry out with you the strange gift of the place: the memory of having been, for a little while, entirely beside the point — and surviving it, and even loving it.
