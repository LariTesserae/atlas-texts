# Preparing for the Bobbin Hollow

## What You Are Walking Into

Understand the central fact before you pack a single item: **you are the only gradient.** The Hollow is a system at perfect equilibrium, and you are a thing that arrives, moves, warms unevenly, breathes out, and leaves. You are the disturbance. Everything that notices you — the eyeless listeners, perhaps the long-limbed tender — notices you precisely because you are change in a place that has finished changing. Prepare not as an explorer entering a wilderness, but as a draft entering a sealed room. Move accordingly.

## Hearing — The First and Most Serious Problem

The drone is not background. It is the medium. It is loud enough to feel in the long bones, sustained without rise or fall, and it will not let up for a single second of your visit.

- **Bring industrial-grade hearing protection and double it.** Foam plugs *under* over-ear muffs rated for sustained high-decibel exposure. This is non-negotiable. There are no quiet pockets to retreat into; the sound is total and constant, and unprotected exposure over hours risks permanent damage.
- **Accept that protection only attenuates.** You will still feel the drone through your skeleton. Expect a deep bodily fatigue from this — the kind that comes from standing near heavy machinery all day. Plan a short visit.
- **Know that taking the plugs out is a temptation.** After a while the brain stops parsing the roar as sound and starts treating it as *space*. People describe wanting to "hear it properly." Do not. That reframing is your perception failing, not the noise lessening.
- **Have a hand-signal vocabulary worked out in advance** with any companion. Speech is impossible. Agree on signs for *stop, leave now, I am hurt, look there, I am alright* before you descend.

## The Dryness

There is no water and there never has been. The dust does not clump; your breath will not fog. This is a place built to run bone-dry, and it will pull moisture out of you relentlessly.

- **Carry far more water than the duration seems to warrant.** The dry, warm air and the bodily exertion of the noise will dehydrate you faster than you notice, because there's no humidity cue to remind your body.
- **Saline eye drops and nasal gel.** Your mucous membranes will desiccate. Eyes especially — the dim phosphorescent light means you'll be straining and blinking less.
- **Lip balm, skin barrier cream.** Sounds trivial; isn't. The dryness is the place's pride.
- **Resist drinking carelessly near the machines.** A spilled drop of water would be the single most foreign thing ever to enter the Hollow. We do not know what it would do. Treat your water as something to consume deliberately, contained, and never to leave behind.

## The Descent

The way in is a throat of growing crystal — halite blades and gypsum needles, sharp but, by the testimony, they *do not cut*.

- **Wear robust clothing anyway.** Trust the report cautiously, not absolutely. Heavy canvas, reinforced shoulders, a snug fit so nothing snags. You turn sideways through these; loose gear catches.
- **No loose straps, no dangling tools.** The blades click when brushed. Minimize what hangs off you.
- **A helmet with a chin strap** — not for the blades, for the low ceiling below, where machines hang into the headroom.
- **Gloves** — thin but tough. You will steady yourself against crystal on the way down.

## The Light

Dim, steady, sourceless phosphorescence. No flicker, no moving shadows except your own.

- **Bring a light, but use it sparingly.** Your shadow is already an intrusion; a moving beam multiplies the disturbance and agitates the listeners. Use a low, warm, diffuse light if you must, and keep it steady.
- **Let your eyes adapt before you rely on the crystal-glow.** Sit a few minutes at the bottom of the descent. The Hollow's light is enough to move by once you adjust.

## Do Not Touch, Do Not Take

This is the ethical and practical core of the visit.

- **Do not stop a machine.** A stilled machine grows a ring of listener corpses within hours. To stop a wheel is to commit a small massacre and, possibly, to begin unbalancing a system that has held perfect equilibrium since before you were a species. You cannot know what cascade you'd start.
- **Do not pull a spool.** The wound bobbins are living arthropods that re-spool themselves. Pulling one unravels a hundred creatures who will scramble to reassemble. You'd be playing with lives for nothing.
- **Take nothing.** A spare bobbin, a frosted leaf, a seed that hangs and never falls — these are notes in a held chord. Removing one is a subtraction from a totality. Leave it exactly.
- **Touch nothing tuned.** Everything is tuned to everything. You do not know the tuning. Assume your touch detunes.

## The Tender

Something long-limbed moves at the edge of the light, checking the frames. It is not a predator — there is no hunger here, hunger being a gradient. But it is the keeper of the equilibrium, and you are the equilibrium's only enemy.

- **Do not approach it. Do not flee it. Do not interfere with its checking.** If it touches you the way it trails a hand along a fence, hold still and let it pass.
- **Assume it perceives you as the variable.** Whether it would correct you the way it corrects a detuned frame is unknown. Give it no reason to find you a problem worth solving.

## Psychological Preparation

This is the part travelers neglect, and it is what breaks them.

- **You will be in a place that has finished happening.** Nothing runs down, nothing winds up, nothing wears, nothing dies of its own accord. To a human mind shaped by entropy, by mornings and aging and the arrow of time, this is profoundly disorienting. Some report a creeping despair; others, an unbearable peace they don't want to leave. **Know which you are prone to.**
- **The Hollow "does not want you to leave and does not want you to stay."** It does not want. Do not project intention onto it, and do not wait for a sign to go. There will be no sign. Set a timer (silent — visual or vibration), and when it ends, leave, regardless of how you feel. Feelings are unreliable in a place with no time.
- **Bring a companion if at all possible**, and agree in advance that either of you can call the exit, no argument. The equilibrium is seductive. A second judgment is a safety line.

## The Exit

You climb back out through the same clicking blades. The chord holds behind you, exactly as loud, exactly as still.

- **Leave before exhaustion, not after.** The climb out is upward through sharp crystal; do it while you still have strength and focus.
- **Take a last accounting at the bottom of the throat:** every item you brought, still with you. Nothing left, nothing taken.
- **Expect the silence above to be its own shock.** After hours in the drone, true quiet will roar in the opposite direction — your ears will ring, your sense of space will feel thin and wrong. Reacclimate slowly. Keep your hearing protection in for a while after surfacing.

---

**The minimal kit:** doubled hearing protection, abundant sealed water, saline drops and skin barrier, tough close-fitting clothing, chin-strapped helmet, thin tough gloves, a low steady warm light, a silent timer, a worked-out hand-signal vocabulary, and a companion who will obey the call to leave.

**The minimal discipline:** touch nothing, take nothing, stop nothing, and remember at every moment that you are the only thing in the Hollow that is still happening — and that you must leave so that it can go on being the place that has finished, and kept on anyway.
