# Preparing to Visit the Roost Plateau

## Understanding What You're Facing

Before anything practical, understand the central danger: **this is not a place where things happen to you. It is a place where things stop happening to you.** Most hostile environments threaten you with events — cold, predators, falls. The Roost Plateau threatens you with *completion*. The longer you remain, the more you risk arriving at your "final form" and being kept, like the plants that finished growing, like the birds that finished flying.

Your preparation must therefore be organized around one principle above all others: **limit your duration, and bring something that fights arrest.**

---

## Physical Preparation

**Acclimatize to altitude beforehand.** The thin, ancient air is real even if everything around it is uncanny. Your lungs will labor. Spend weeks at genuine high altitude before crossing over, or your body will fail in mundane ways long before the metaphysical ones arrive. Bring supplemental oxygen — bottled, redundant, more than you think you need. Do not assume the impossible stillness of the air means the oxygen content is normal; assume it is worse.

**Bring all your water.** Every drop. The dryness is total and *ancient* — there is nothing to harvest, condense, or melt. Calculate your needs, then double them, then add the return journey. Dehydration in mineral-dry, cold-thin air is fast and quiet.

**Dress for cold without wind.** This is unusual: you will not lose heat to convection because nothing moves. But the radiant cold of old stone and thin sky is severe. Insulation that traps still air is your friend here — the absence of wind actually helps you, the one mercy this place offers. Layer for cold, not for storm.

**Pack dense, simple food.** You won't be staying long if you're wise, but bring calories that don't require preparation. The act of eating will become important for reasons described below.

---

## The Anti-Arrest Discipline

This is the heart of your preparation, and it is more behavioral than material.

**Keep moving. Always.** Never stand still to admire the sky. The standing stones and the worn circular path tell you what happened to the last attentive visitors — they came to *look*, they walked and walked in circles looking up, and we do not know that they ever left. Make a rule: no stopping. If you must rest, rest while shifting your weight, pacing a small loop, working your hands.

**Bring a metronome of motion you cannot ignore.** A mechanical clockwork device — wind-up, not battery, in case the place is hostile to certain mechanisms — that ticks audibly and demands periodic re-winding. Something that *requires you to act on it* at intervals. This serves two purposes: it gives the silence something to swallow that you control, and it forces you to perform an ongoing task, to keep *becoming* rather than finishing.

**Set a hard departure deadline and bind yourself to it.** Use the clockwork timer. When it rings, you go. Do not negotiate. The plateau is "made of unspent time"; it will offer you the feeling that there is no hurry, that you can stay a little longer, that completion is restful. This feeling is the trap closing. Trust your pre-committed plan over your in-the-moment judgment.

**Travel with a companion, and assign each other the duty of vigilance.** Watch each other for signs of settling — a slowing gait, a fixed upward gaze, a reluctance to speak. If your partner stops, you move them. The first symptom of arrest will not feel like danger. It will feel like peace.

## On Your Shadow

You must mentally prepare for the dissociation of motion from matter. **Your shadow will not obey you.** It will go about its own business, performing your last actions, sliding off in directions the light does not justify.

Do not chase it. Do not stare at it. Do not let it become an object of fascination — that fascination is itself a kind of stopping. Acknowledge it as a fact of local physics, the way you'd acknowledge a strange tide, and ignore it. The temptation will be to watch what your shadow does, to read meaning in it. Resist. The moment you become an audience to your own shadow, your body has been demoted from actor to record, and the body that watches is the body that finishes.

**Keep your eyes on the stone immediately ahead of your feet, or on your companion.** Train this habit before you arrive. The sky is a museum and you must not be one of its patrons.

---

## What to Bring for the Mind

The deepest hazard here is contemplative. The place induces a profound, seductive acceptance — the sense that flying up as high as you can and being kept is not death but rest, equilibrium, the natural end. You need ballast against this.

**Bring an unfinished task you care about deeply** — something incomplete back home, a person waiting, a work not done. Hold it consciously. You are a creature of *unspent becoming*, and you must remind yourself you have not finished. The birds were kept because they reached the top. Refuse to reach your top here.

**Bring noise of your own making** — hum, talk, recite. The silence has texture and it wants to swallow you the way it swallows the grind of your footstep. Fight it with continuous, deliberate sound. Sing badly. Argue with your companion. The half-second cries from above are the place "correcting an error" — be a sustained error it cannot correct.

---

## What Not to Do

- **Do not investigate the roofless structure or walk its worn circular path.** That path was worn by someone who attended too long. To trace it is to volunteer.
- **Do not try to touch the frozen birds.** You do not know whether arrest is contagious by contact, and you should assume it is.
- **Do not lie down.** Not to rest, not to sleep, not to look up more comfortably. Sleep is the deepest form of stopping. If you cannot complete your visit without sleeping, your visit is too long — leave and return rested.
- **Do not come to study or catalog.** The standing-stone builders came to attend, to keep "some catalog of held instants," and they are gone. Come briefly, see what you came to see, and go. Curiosity here has a cost measured in permanence.

---

## The Single Most Important Rule

**Leave while leaving still feels urgent.** The instant departure begins to feel optional — the instant the plateau's deep, restful patience starts to seem reasonable — you are already late. Go on the timer. Go before the timer if the peace arrives early.

You are visiting the equilibrium that comes after death, the place where everything that lived flew as high as it could and was kept. You are an ordinary human, still mid-flight, still mid-becoming. Your entire goal is to remain that way: unfinished, in motion, casting a shadow you refuse to watch, breathing borrowed air, and *leaving.*
