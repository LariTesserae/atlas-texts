# Preparing to Visit the Hollow

## Understanding What You Are Going To

Before anything practical, understand the nature of the place, because everything else follows from it. You are not visiting a wilderness, a ruin, or a habitat. You are visiting a *completion* — a small pocket of the multiverse that has finished happening. Nothing there needs you. Nothing there will respond to you. Whatever you bring, you bring entirely from yourself. Prepare accordingly: you are packing not to survive a hostile place but to survive a place that simply does not participate.

The chief danger here is not the environment. It is what stillness this total does to a human mind. Plan for that first.

## Light

Bring light, but bring it knowing what it will cost you. The hollow's faint mineral glow is part of what makes it what it is — the threshold of barely-seeing where your eye and the rock cooperate. A bright lamp will banish that. You will see clearly, and in seeing clearly you will lose the place. The plants will resolve into grey-green stalks. The far dark will become merely an unlit corner.

So bring **two kinds of light.** A strong, reliable lamp with redundant power for safety and for the practical work of finding your edges, learning the floor, checking the seams. And a very dim hand-shielded light, or simply the discipline to switch the lamp off, for the long stretches when you want the hollow as it actually is. Spend more time with the lamp off than on. Let your eyes do the work of inventing and then discovering the light. That is the only honest way to be there.

Mark your entry point with something you can find by touch in total dark — a cord, a tactile marker on the floor. The hollow drinks light; do not trust that you will see your way back.

## Warmth and the Body

The floor is warm and will meet your body's heat and hold it. This is a gift and a trap. You can lie on it indefinitely and be comfortable. You will not be driven to move by cold, by hunger of the usual urgent kind, by any signal the place provides. The hollow gives you no reason to do anything.

Therefore **bring your own reasons.** Carry a timepiece — not because time matters there, but because *you* matter, and your body runs on cycles the place will not enforce. There is no day or night. You must impose your own. Set intervals to eat, to drink, to sleep, to wake, to move your limbs. Without this you will drift. People in featureless constant conditions lose the thread of their own continuity quickly. Schedule yourself as if you were tending a patient, because you are.

You will need **all your water and food.** The place surrenders almost no moisture; what little there is sustains the ancient plants and nothing larger. Assume zero resupply. The dry air will pull water from you steadily and quietly. Drink on schedule, not on thirst — thirst will arrive late and softened, like everything else here.

## Sound and the Quiet

The quiet is not the danger people imagine. It is gentle. But it is total enough that you will begin to hear yourself — your pulse, your breath, the faint ring the inner ear makes when given nothing else. Some travelers find this peaceful. Some find it unbearable within hours. You will not know which you are until you are there.

Bring a means of making and hearing familiar sound if you need it — recorded voice, music, anything human — but use it sparingly and know that it will, like bright light, drive the hollow away. The place is built to swallow sound without echo. Speaking aloud, you will hear your own words die in front of you. This is worth experiencing on purpose at least once, deliberately, so it does not ambush you.

If you go with a companion, agree in advance on how much you will speak. The quiet pressures people either into constant talk or into total silence; choose consciously rather than being chosen.

## The Mind

This is the real preparation, and it cannot be packed.

The hollow's deepest fact is that **nothing happens.** No weather, no growth, no decay, no event, no threat, no reward. For a human nervous system tuned by evolution to track change, this is a profound and specific stress. You will catch yourself waiting for something. You will misread the tick of settling rock as a beginning. You will feel, under the calm, a low pressure that the place is *about* to do something — and it never will. That tension, unrelieved, is what breaks people in still environments, not fear.

Prepare by deciding, before you go, what you will do with completed time. Bring inner resources: things to think through, work to turn over in the mind, a practice of attention if you have one. The hollow rewards a person who can be still without being idle — who can rest *with* a place rather than waiting *on* it. If you cannot yet do that, the hollow will teach you, but the lesson is hard and you should not learn it alone for the first time in the dark.

Set a firm departure time and commit to it externally if you can — a signal, an arrangement, anything that will pull you out whether or not you feel ready. The place gives no cue to leave. Travelers have stayed in such completions far longer than they meant to, not from danger but from the absence of any reason to go. The warmth holds you. The quiet holds you. *It holds. That is what it does.* Make sure something outside the hollow holds you too.

## Conduct Within

Move gently. The plants are older than recorded human history and live on almost nothing; a careless step could end what no season will replace. Touch them only barely, if at all. Run your hand along the dry runnels in the wall — that is what they are for, in a sense, the only thing this place offers to be felt — but disturb nothing you do not have to.

You may find the faint trace of an earlier visitor: a stone slightly moved, a seam cleared as if to see a plant better. Do not erase it. Do not add to it heavily either. If you leave any mark, leave it as small and as patient as theirs — a single deliberate act in a place built for no one. You are joining a very short, very quiet lineage of those who came to *look*. Look well, and leave the looking as you found it.

## What to Take Away

Take away nothing physical. There is nothing to take that would not be a wound to the place.

Take away the experience of completion — the rare and difficult human education of being somewhere that needs nothing, expects nothing, and will not change for you. Most places we know are becoming. This one has arrived. Go to feel what that is like, carry it carefully, and come back to the unfinished world a little better at being still.
