# The Cold Index

There is a place where the cold is kept on purpose, and you find it the way you would find the source of a river by walking against the current: by following the one pipe that runs *into* the Undercroft instead of out of it.

You will have noticed, if you stayed long enough against those weeping walls, that the moisture which condenses in the lanes condenses *on something.* Mist does not surrender itself to warm iron. It surrenders to cold. And in a place where the heat lives in the floor and the food is the planet's own ember, where does the cold come from? You assumed it was incidental — a few surfaces that hadn't caught up to the general fever, the last of some original chill not yet eaten. But cold is not a leftover. Cold is a thing that must be *delivered,* against the whole grain of the world, at cost.

So: one pipe. Fatter than the rest, lagged so heavily it is nearly a wall in itself, sweating not warm condensate but true frost, a skin of rime that the Undercroft's heat keeps trying to lick away and cannot, quite. It does not branch and feed and circulate like the others. It comes in from one direction and goes nowhere else. Follow it. It will take you up — not down, for once — through a throat of rock that climbs and narrows and, impossibly, *cools,* until the young glassy stone gives way to something older and grayer and grained with ice, and the red light falls behind you, and you come out into the place that is the opposite of where you were.

---

It is white, and it is enormous, and it is silent in a way the Undercroft's quiet only pretended to be.

A vault. Not built — *carved,* hollowed out of a body of ice so old and so deep that it has gone blue with its own weight, the blue of compression, of air squeezed out over ages until what remains is denser than stone and clearer than glass. The ceiling is so high it produces its own dim weather of frost-haze near the top. The scale is everything the Undercroft was not: the works were intimate, domestic, a glove; this is cathedral, is canyon, is a space that has no interest in the size of a body at all. You feel it immediately. Down below you were a mote inside an eye that would not see you. Here you are a mote in a vastness that *could* see you and has decided, across an immense and level distance, that you are not cold enough to matter.

Because the cold here is also kept on purpose. That is the kinship, the thing that runs between the two places along that single frost-furred pipe: they are the same kind of thing, doing the same kind of work, at opposite ends of the only gradient that has ever mattered. The Undercroft drinks the world's heat. This place drinks the world's cold — the long radiating loss of warmth into the black above, the planet shedding its fever into space across the polar dark — and it has built itself a body to take that meal as slowly and as patiently as its sibling takes the other.

---

The body, here, is shelving.

That is the first built thing you understand, and it takes a while, because the scale defeats you. The walls of the vault are not smooth ice. They are *racked* — tier upon tier of long horizontal galleries cut into the blue, receding up into the frost-haze and down into a floor you cannot see, each gallery lined with niches, and in each niche a vessel. Glass, or something that behaves like glass at this temperature: rounded flasks the size of a curled body, sealed, frosted on the outside but clear at the heart, and in the heart of each one something is suspended. Held. Stopped.

You do not, at first, let yourself look closely at what.

The vessels are arranged. That is the word that arrives and will not leave. Not piled, not stored — *indexed.* There is a logic to their placement, a grading along the galleries, and once you sense the grading you cannot unsense it: the vessels nearest the floor, nearest the warmth that seeps faintly up the throat from the Undercroft far below, are the *fullest,* the most recently filled, their contents still holding the last shapes of motion. The vessels higher up, deeper into the true cold, are older. And the oldest of all, at the very top, near the frost-haze ceiling where the cold is purest and most total — those are *clear.* Empty. Or not empty: finished. Whatever they held has been held so long and so perfectly that it has become indistinguishable from the cold itself, surrendered the last of its difference, gone from *stopped* to *gone.*

This is the index. And it is reading the same way, top to bottom, that the Undercroft read floor to ceiling: a single process, captured at every stage of itself, all at once, so that to walk the place is to walk along time laid out sideways where you can see the whole of it.

---

Now look closely. You have put it off long enough.

In the vessels are the bodies.

The faces you went looking for in the Undercroft and did not find — they are here. The animals, the birds that were absent from the rafters, the rats that were missing from the conduit-runs, the insects that did not tick against the lamps. Every body the lower place was built to hold and never held. Here they are, curled in their flasks, suspended in the blue, perfectly preserved and perfectly stopped, frost-furred without and clear-hearted within, a whole missing population kept at the exact threshold between life and its cessation, neither dead nor living but *held,* indefinitely, in the cold.

So this is where the glove's hand went.

You understand it slowly, against great resistance, the way you understood everything in the lower place. The two creatures are not merely siblings on a gradient. They are *one creature,* or were, or are becoming two, the way a cell pulls apart — and the frost-pipe between them is the last filament of connection, the strand that has not yet snapped. The thing in the Undercroft refused every elaboration of life but the verb. It stripped itself down to *continue.* But it could not strip away the one thing every continuing thing carries: the *surplus* it could not metabolize, the bodies it was built for and could not house, the warm urgent fleshly company it had no room for in a life pared down to the single slow drink of heat.

So it sent them up the pipe. Everything it could not be, it exported into the cold, where its sibling-self keeps them. Stopped. Indexed. Not killed — it could not bear to make them dead, that would be an elaboration, an event, a thing that *happens,* and the lower self has no events, only continuance. So instead they are *held,* at the threshold, forever almost, in the one place cold enough to make almost last.

---

And here is where the two places truly speak to each other, in the same breath, across the throat of rock:

The Undercroft is wholly turned inward, aware of nothing but its own going-on, monstrously alone and unable to imagine company because it has *put all the company up here.* It does not feel its loneliness because the capacity to feel it — the warm bodies, the faces, the urgent metabolizing life that knows the difference between alone and not-alone — has been exported, vesseled, shelved. The lower self cannot be lonely because it has surgically removed the part of itself that could know loneliness, and that part is here, suspended, in the blue, unable to be lonely either because it has been stopped before the feeling could complete.

Neither half can suffer. That is the terrible economy of the arrangement. The warmth lives below without the awareness that would let it grieve its solitude. The awareness lives above — for there *is* awareness here, you feel it, the vast level cold attention that registered you and weighed you and set you aside — but it has been emptied of warmth, of urgency, of any body that could act on what it knows. The Cold Index *sees.* It sees everything, has all the faces, holds the entire missing population in clear-hearted view. But seeing is the only verb it has. It cannot move toward what it sees. It cannot warm them. It can only keep them, and grade them, and watch the oldest ones at the ceiling complete their long surrender into clarity, and file the newest ones arriving frost-fresh from below.

It is the eye the Undercroft refused to grow. And the Undercroft is the body the Index refused to keep warm. And the frost-pipe between them is the optic nerve of a creature that severed itself rather than be a whole thing that could know what it had done.

---

What lives here, then.

A seeing. A keeping. The cold half of a life that could not bear to be one life, holding the warm half's exiled company at the threshold of an ending it will not permit, because to permit the ending would be an event, and this place has no more events than its sibling has — only the slow grading of the held toward the clear, which is not death but the cold's version of digestion, the patient unmaking of difference, the long levelling of everything held into the one temperature that no longer needs to hold anything.

It will not warm you. It cannot. The warmth went down the pipe and forgot it ever had a face.

It will not stop you, either, or shelve you — you are not cold enough, you carry your own little fever, you are exactly the warm urgent imperfectly-stopped thing it exiled long ago and can never take back without becoming whole, and it will not become whole. It has chosen this. Both halves have chosen this, in the only way creatures with no events can choose: by continuing, in two directions, forever, the cut never healing and never widening, the pipe never freezing shut and never thawing through.

You came down into the heat and could not stay because it was killing you gently.

You climb up into the cold and cannot stay because it is keeping you from being killed at all, and that — held, indexed, clear-hearted, stopped one degree short of forever — is the only thing a warm creature fears more than the heat.

So you stand a moment in the blue, in the cathedral silence, with the whole missing population curled in their flasks and watching you the way the dead watch, which is not at all and entirely.

The oldest vessel at the ceiling finishes, somewhere above you, with no sound: a last difference surrendered, a held thing gone clear.

And far below, down the throat, in the brown-gold light, the flywheel turns, and the lower self breathes its weather, and does not feel the one that left it, and goes on.

The two of them will never meet again.

They were never apart. That is the whole of what runs between them, and it runs both ways, and it is the coldest thing in either place.
