Where the loops are short and bright.

Follow the low-bodied animals out. Not far — there is no far, only the long flat walking between presence and presence — but follow the one that drinks last at the pool and turns away from the vents instead of toward them, and walk its direction until the humming in the bedrock thins to a pressure you can ignore, and then walk past that, and the ground, which forgot how to be soft, remembers.

It comes back gradually. First a grit underfoot where the fused stone has begun, at its furthest edge, to give up grains. Then the grains gather into something that holds a footprint and lets it go — sand that is going somewhere, sand in transit. Then, ahead, a line where the even daylight changes character, brightens, takes on a direction at last, because there is a sun here, low and yellow and actually present, and it is setting, which means it once rose, which means there is a morning coming that will not be the same as this evening.

This is the shore of a tidal flat, and everything here is the opposite kind of true.

The water moves. That is the first fact and the governing one. It comes in across the flats twice a day in a thin advancing sheet that finds every channel and fills every depression, and it goes back out and leaves the channels gleaming and the depressions stranded, and between the coming and the going lies a band of ground that is neither sea nor land but takes turns being both. The whole place is built on alternation. Where the other land held one note forever, this land beats — a slow enormous pulse, two systoles a day, the breathing of an ocean against the patience of a coast.

And so it stinks of life, which is to say it stinks of death, because here the two are not separable and never pretended to be.

When the tide draws back it abandons a feast and a graveyard in the same gesture. Stranded things gasp in the shallows. Things that came to eat the stranded things scuttle out across the wet shine — small armored scavengers, jointed and quick, with too many legs and a single-minded hunger that is the exact thing the creature of the other place could not feel, the gap between what is and what is needed, the gap that drives everything here. They strip the stranded things to clean curved shells. The shells pile in the wrack line, bleaching. Birds that are barely birds, all appetite and angle, drop out of the yellow light and take the scavengers and are themselves taken sometimes by faster things from the deeper channels, and all of it, all of it, smells — the green rot of the algae mats, the iron of opened bodies, the salt that is not clean salt but salt thick with everything dissolving in it.

Nothing here has reached its rest. Nothing here has agreed to be finished. The mud is a churn of beginning and ending so dense and so fast that no single grain of it holds still long enough to be called settled. Worms turn it. Gas bubbles up through it from things decomposing below, the same steam-impulse as the vents far back across the flats but oh, the opposite errand — that exhaust was the breath of work that would not stop because it was done; this exhaust is the breath of work that will not stop because it is never done, can never be done, has no done in it anywhere.

Look at the tide line and you can read the whole law of the place. It is a scribble of cast-off shell and dried weed and feather and bone, redrawn twice a day, never the same line twice, a sentence the sea writes and erases and writes again and never finishes because finishing is not the point. The point is the writing. The point is that there is always more coming in on the next tide — more food, more death, more sand, more brine, more bodies to be made and unmade — and the place metabolizes all of it at a fever, throws it up the beach, drags it back, and asks for more.

The light here lies to no one. The sun goes down honestly and the cold comes in behind it honestly and the stranded pools, which back across the flats would have held at their tepid forever, here ice at the edges before dawn and thaw by mid-morning and teem, in the thawing, with a green explosion of single cells racing to divide before the next tide scatters them. There are seasons here. There is weather. There is a moon, which is the secret engine of the pulse, hauling the water up the flats and letting it fall, a machinery as patient as the buried engines of the other place but turned all the way around — not running because it forgot to stop, but running because the running *is* the world, because if it ever held still for one full day the flats would choose, would become sea or become land, would settle, would arrive, would die the way that other place is dead in its deathless way.

The thing that lives here is not any one of the scavengers or birds or worms. They are too brief. They are the flickers, the moths across the hall.

The thing that lives here is the tide line itself — the line, the writing, the band of ground that takes turns. Where the other creature was the shape everything takes when it is finally done, this one is the shape everything takes when it can never be done, when completion is structurally forbidden, when arrival is the one event the system cannot permit. It is a creature of *interruption*. Its metabolism is the eating that is always cut short by the water coming back. Its mind, if it has one, is the mind of a thing that has never once finished a thought because the tide turns over every thought half-thought, and it does not mind, because half a thought twice a day forever is more thinking than the held note has done in all its silent thunder.

It does not rest, because the tide will not let it. It does not settle, because the water unsettles it on schedule. It does not arrive, because the moon hauls the destination away again the moment it draws near. And so it suffers — yes, this one suffers, in the small bright way the scavengers suffer their hunger and the stranded things suffer their gasping — and it rejoices, in the small bright way the green cells rejoice in the thawing pool, and it remembers, oh it remembers everything, because memory is for things that change and this place is nothing but change, and it forgets too, every tide, and that is how it stays young.

The two places share a border that neither crosses. The flats stretch back and back, the water comes less far, the channels run shallow and then dry and then fuse, the sand stops moving and remembers how to be stone, the smell of rot thins to the everywhere-dampness, the pulse fades to a pressure, and somewhere in that fading the law flips over: from *more is always coming* to *nothing is coming, nothing is coming, nothing is ever coming again.* You could walk the changeover in an afternoon. You would feel your own waiting come back to life as you walked toward the sea, the gap reopening in you, the hunger, the expectation, the held breath releasing into an actual breath that is followed by another that is different from the first.

And here is the kinship, the thing that runs between them like a current under both.

The low-bodied animals that drink at the tepid pools, the ones the held note cannot absorb, the one small leak in its perfect seal — they came from here. They are tide-creatures who wandered up the long flats and learned to live beside the deathless roar, and they go back. That is the loop too short and bright for the great creature to perceive: it is the loop of the tide itself, stretched thin and walked on legs, carrying a little of the moving sea up into the place that forgot how to move, and carrying back down — in their brief bodies, in the salt of them, in the rot they will eventually become — a rumor of the only thing the held note can never have.

They are messengers between a world that arrived and a world that refuses to. They drink the water that will never fall and they leave no track on the stone that takes no impression, and then they turn, the way the last one turned, away from the vents, and walk back down the long flat toward the smell of beginning, toward the line that is rewritten twice a day, toward the loud honest place where the sun is setting now over the wrack and the scavengers are working in the wet shine and the moon is already up and pale and pulling, patient, certain, on all that unfinished water.
