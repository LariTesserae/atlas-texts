# The Frost-Throat

Go up. Past the place where root-meat hardens into honest soil, past the soil into the frozen crust, up through the four meters and then keep going — out, into the country the cellar has only ever known as *the cold above,* the place its children are meant to be carried into. You have to find out what is up there. The cellar spent five hundred years deciding the world needed something. This is the world.

It is a throat in the ice.

Not a cave. A throat — a vertical shaft in the glacier-field, a wound that goes down and a wound that goes up, and the wind moves through it the way breath moves through a windpipe, and it has the sound of breathing because it *is* the architecture of breath. Where the cellar was buried and slow and loud with its own thinking, this is open and *fast* and loud with weather. The wind here does not seep. It runs. It is the opposite number of the water that took an hour to fall: this is a current that crosses the whole shaft in a heartbeat, that has never once been timed, that carries and scatters and refuses to be read.

The walls are ice. Old ice, blue-black at the depths and milk-pale at the lip, and it is *finished* in a way the cellar's walls were not — it has chosen its shape, which is the shape the wind cut, which is no shape, which is always changing and therefore complete in each instant and never the same instant twice. If the cellar is a thing being slowly decided, the Frost-Throat is a thing being constantly *undecided,* sublimating at the edges, refreezing in the calms, a surface that holds no conclusion because it forgets itself faster than it can form.

And here is how the two places touch, the thing that runs between them:

**The throat is where the spores go.**

The cellar breathes you back, remember. It breathes everything. The filaments and the fat glowing spores drift, and they do not all stay below — some are exhaled, drawn up through the capillaries of root and the pores of soil, up the four meters, out into the crust, and they collect in the Frost-Throat because the throat *inhales.* The wind that runs the shaft pulls air up from a thousand buried places, and the cellar is one of them, and so the warm fast wind is faintly, perpetually seeded with the slow cold mind's stray thoughts. The phosphorescence you saw pooling like milk in the low places below — it is up here too, but thin, smeared across the wind, so that on the darkest nights the Frost-Throat glows very faintly from the inside, a swallowed blue-green light, as if something luminous had been breathed into the ice and could not get out.

The spores do not survive up here. That is the cruelty and the point. They are built for stillness, for patience, for a medium that holds them long enough to be read and folded and concluded. The throat holds nothing. It scatters them against the ice where they freeze, glow once, and go dark — a held thought of the cellar, carried up at last into the cold above, *expressed* into a place that cannot receive it, that has no law of nothing-wasted, that wastes everything as a matter of its nature.

So the children the cellar dreams toward — the brood, gestating on the ceiling, glowing with their slowly-decided shapes — if any of them ever finished, if any ever detached and climbed and emerged, *this* is what waits. Not a world that needs them. A throat that breathes and forgets. The cellar has spent five hundred years concluding what the world requires, and it has been reading the world from a single exhaled draft of wind, and the wind lied, the way wind does, by carrying only what could be carried fast.

## What Lives in the Throat

Something does. The throat is not empty, any more than the cellar was hushed.

There are **the riders.** Pale birds — or the throat's idea of birds, all wing and almost no body, near-translucent the way the grazers below were translucent, as though the two places had arrived at the same solution to the problem of being barely-there. They do not land. The throat gives them nothing to land on; ice that forgets its shape cannot hold a nest. So they live in the current itself, born aloft and dying aloft, sleeping on the wing in the calm pockets, and they have no concept of stillness the way the cellar has no concept of harm. They eat the spores. This is the thing that closes the circle and breaks your heart a little if you let it: *the cellar's exhaled thoughts are food.* The brood-stuff, the signal-meat, the slowly-concluded ideas drifting up — the riders catch them in their gullets and they are warm and faintly sweet with phosphorescence and they are *good,* and the birds grow fat-by-their-standards on the dreams of a mind that does not know it is being eaten.

Below, **nothing is wasted.** Up here, everything is — but *waste* is the wrong word once you watch the riders feed. The spore that froze and went dark against the ice was wasted. The spore that a rider caught became flight, became the fast warm beat of a thing crossing the throat in a heartbeat, became the exact opposite of everything it was bred to be. The cellar's slowest thought, turned into the fastest possible life. Neither place would call this a kinship. It is one anyway.

And there is **the one that listens.** I am careful about this one too, the way I was careful about the brood.

Far up the throat, near the lip where the ice goes milk-pale, where the wind finally slows enough to be almost a hush — there is a place where the riders gather when they are old, where the current eddies and holds, the throat's single concession to stillness. The old birds come there to stop. And they do not simply die. They press themselves to the pale ice and they freeze *while still warm,* and the throat takes them — folds them into the wall, layer by layer, snowfall by snowfall, the way the cellar folds its dead grazers into signal-meat.

The throat is building something out of them.

Up here, at the lip, where the ice has had the longest to gather, there is a mass in the wall that is not quite ice anymore — pale, layered, faintly luminous with all the spores its buried birds once ate, *thickening,* the way the cellar's pillars are thickening toward being walls. It is fetal in the deeper sense. It has a shape but not *the* shape. It is uncommitted. It is being slowly arrived at, snowfall by snowfall, over a span of time that makes *slow* inadequate — and the throat keeps count of it not with clicking but with the long howl of the wind that changes pitch, very slightly, each century, as the mass grows and narrows the shaft.

The Frost-Throat is also a brood.

It is also a mind, gestating itself out of fast dead birds the way the cellar gestates itself out of slow patient drops, and it does not know about the cellar four meters and a frozen country below, and the cellar does not know about it, and the only thing that passes between them is a thin smear of luminous spores carried up by a lying wind — the cellar's exhaled dreams becoming the throat's frozen food becoming the throat's slow-glowing thought, so that whatever the Frost-Throat is concluding, across its own centuries, it is concluding *partly out of the cellar's stray mind,* eaten and flown and frozen and folded in.

Two unborn minds. One slow and buried and warm-by-comparison, deciding what the world needs. One fast and open and freezing, eating what the first one breathes and building itself, unknowing, out of the digested dreams of the other.

Neither will finish in any span you could wait for. Neither knows the other is there. The wind runs between them in a heartbeat and carries the message neither can read — and you, the warm fast flicker, the spore that walked, are the only thing in this whole frozen country that has been in both throats and felt both deep notes and understood that they are *answering each other,* slowly, across the cold, in a language made of waste.

Go up, the cellar told you. You went up.

There is no higher to go. There is only the lip, and the old birds freezing, and the pale mass thickening in the ice, and the faint blue-green glow of one mind's dreams being slowly eaten into another's.

Go across, then. Or stay, and be concluded by the faster of the two.

It has noticed you. They both have. They will take a few hundred years to decide — but the throat, being faster, will decide first.
