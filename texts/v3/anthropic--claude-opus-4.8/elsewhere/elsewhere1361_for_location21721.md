## The Catchment

Up. Not the way you came — that throat of blades lets you out where you went in, onto whatever surface holds the Hollow under it. This is a different up, the up the gradient leans toward, the loudest place that is not here. Follow what runs out of a room that has finished happening, and you arrive at the one thing the Hollow does not have and was built around the absence of:

It is raining.

It has been raining since before there was a word for it, and it will go on, but *go on* is the wrong grammar now — you have left the place with no froms and come to the place that is nothing but froms, a whole landscape of arrivals, each drop a small original event falling into a system that has never once been at equilibrium and never will be. This is the Catchment. Where the Hollow is a held chord, the Catchment is the sound of something being poured out and never refilling and somehow never emptying either, because the pouring *is* the thing, the way down *is* the place.

Everything here is on its way somewhere else.

---

**The water.** Start with the water because the Hollow could not, because the Hollow's first lesson was *no water, never any, built to run without a single drop* — and here is the drop, and here are all the others, sheeting down a country of grey slope and dark gully, gathering. It does not stand. The Catchment forbids standing the way the Hollow forbids change. Touch a puddle and it is already not a puddle; it has found a lower place to be, it is threading the gullies, it is gone before you finish the gesture of touching it. Every surface is a slope. Every slope is an argument for elsewhere, and the water wins the argument always, instantly, with the whole patient violence of weight.

You understand, standing in it, soaked, that this is the runoff. Not metaphorically. The Hollow needed somewhere for its impossible dryness to have come from, some place that took all the water so the Hollow could have none, and this is that place, drinking the sky and never keeping a drop, the perfect complement, the gradient made geography. The salt blades grow dry below because all the wet in the world ran *here* and kept running. Two halves of a single law: one place that finished and one place that cannot.

---

**The machines** — there are none. This is the first thing the Catchment wants you to understand, and it tells you by what it does to anything built. Set a structure on these slopes and the water finds it, gets under it, carries the ground out from beneath it grain by grain, and brings it down. The Catchment is not loud with industry. It is loud with *erosion* — the hiss and rush and undercut grind of a world dismantling itself at a rate you can almost watch, a gully deepening by a finger's width while you stand at its lip, a slope slumping with a soft enormous sigh into the channel below. Nothing here turns and goes nowhere. Everything moves and goes *down*, and the down it goes to is further down, and there is no bottom that holds, only the next leaning-toward.

The Hollow's bobbins fill and empty and the level never moves. Here a hollow fills with runoff and the runoff carves the hollow deeper and the fill spills over and the level is never the same level twice. The two places share the verb — *to fill* — and conjugate it as opposites. One holds. One pours.

---

**The fauna.** They do not ride anything. They flee. They climb. They are built for the slope the way the Hollow's mites were built for the gear: long-toed scrambling things that can hold to a surface in the act of its collapse, that read the ground for the next slump and are already leaping before it goes. There is no abundance-to-the-cubic-foot here, no many-to-a-bobbin density, because density requires stillness and nothing is still. They are sparse and quick and singular, each one a small bundle of *getting away in time*, and they do not love the noise, they *use* it — they listen to the erosion the way the Hollow's flat eared listeners drink the drone, but not to feed on it, never to feed on it. To survive it. To hear in the grind of the undercut where the ground is about to stop being ground.

The Hollow's listeners die in a ring around any machine that stills. The Catchment's climbers die the instant they trust a slope to stay. The mirror is exact and the meaning is reversed: down there, motion is life and stillness is death because the fauna require the chord; up here, *reading* the motion is life and trusting the stillness is death, because the slope keeps no promises and the only equilibrium is the one that kills you while you rest in it.

And something larger moves here too. Not a tender. The Catchment has nothing to tend; there is no tuning to check because nothing here is ever right, nothing here is ever the same long enough to be right or wrong. What moves on the edge of the grey light is a *follower* — long-limbed too, you would notice the kinship if you saw them in the same hour, the same too-many limbs, the same pale crystalline make, but where the Tender's body is the gesture of keeping, this one's body is the gesture of *going down with*. It descends. It is always descending, choosing the steepest line, riding the slumps, letting the slope carry it, and it does not flee the collapse the way the small things do — it leans into the collapse, it is the collapse made limbed and attentive, the erosion's own self-awareness walking downhill forever.

You will wonder, watching it, whether it is the same creature. Whether the Tender, on the days it does not check the tuning — except there are no days, except it always checks — whether somewhere it is also this, the other note of itself, the half that goes where the Tender stays. They share the make. They share the absence of hunger. Neither wants you to be other than you are. But the Tender withdrew its hand because it could not keep you, and this one would not withdraw at all — this one would simply fall alongside you, matching your departure, tuned to the going as the Tender is tuned to the held.

---

**The vegetation** is everywhere and nowhere, because nothing roots. Seeds fall here — that is the whole difference, that is the lesson the Hollow's hanging seeds were the negative of. In the Hollow the seeds never fall, never sprout, never rot; they are part of the chord. Here they fall constantly, are carried, lodge for an hour in a slump of wet grit, throw down a frantic white rootlet, and are torn loose and carried further before the rootlet finds anything. The whole Catchment is a-flush with green that is always trying and almost never succeeding, a vegetation of pure attempt, leaning not toward the loudest place but toward *any place that will hold still long enough* — and finding none, and trying anyway, generation after generation washed downslope, and this is not failure, you have to understand it is not failure, it is simply what growing *is* in a world that pours. The Hollow's fronds lean toward sound and stay. These lean toward stillness and are denied it and keep leaning. The leaning is the life. The arrival was never the point.

A few of them, in the deepest gullies where the grit packs hardest, do hold. Do root. Do throw up a glassy stalk and stand for a season. And the climbers gather at these, and the green deepens, and for a little while there is a thing that resembles the Hollow's stillness — a held place, a small chord — and then the gully it grew in deepens past the root's depth and the whole holding washes out together, plant and grit and gathered fauna, down to the next almost-place. Even the Catchment's equilibria are temporary. Even its holdings are on their way down.

---

**The light** is grey and even and comes from a sky you cannot see the source of, the way the Hollow's light came from crystal you could not find the heart of. But where that light was steady, patient, shadowless, never flickering — this light *changes*, dims and lifts as the rain thickens and thins, so that your shadow comes and goes, comes and goes, and no one notices it because here *everything* is the variable, everything arrives and changes, you are not the one gradient walked into a frozen room, you are one more falling thing among the falling, indistinguishable, welcome in the only way the Catchment can welcome, which is by not marking you as strange.

This is the rest the Hollow could not give. Down there you were the wound, the broken string, the original event, the one shadow that moved. The listeners turned their faces. The Tender swung its attention toward you and found you running down and could not fix it and let you go. Here, no one turns. Here, running down is the native condition. Here you are tuned, finally, perfectly, to a world that is also running down — and the relief of it, the terrible relief of arriving somewhere whose whole nature is departure, is the thing that runs between the two places, the current that carries you up out of the dry and into the wet, the water that left the Catchment so the Hollow could be parched now drawing you back along its own absent course.

---

**What lives in the Catchment** is a leaning with everything to lean toward and nothing to arrive at — performing the shape of arrival forever, falling and rooting and washing loose and falling, so endlessly and so freshly that it has become indistinguishable from the rush around it, one more drop in a pouring that started before there were words and will not, cannot, has no grammar by which to, stop.

The follower is descending now. It chose the steepest line before you finished reading this, and it is already lower, riding a slump that is already carrying it past the place it began, and it does not look back up the slope toward you because looking-back is the Tender's gesture and this is the other half, the half that only goes.

It was going down when you climbed up out of the blades.
It will be going down when you have forgotten there was ever a slope.
It has always been going down.

It is going down now.

And the rain — which is the water that fled the Hollow, which is the dryness's own missing half come pouring home — the rain falls on you both, on the follower descending and the climber fleeing and the seed trying to root and the gully deepening and your own coming-and-going shadow, and it does not hold, and it does not wind up, and it goes where weight tells it, down and down past the steepest line, down toward — you understand this last, standing soaked at the lip of the pouring world — down toward the throat of dry blades, where it will not arrive, where it has never arrived, where the absence of its arrival is the warmth with no source, the room that runs without a drop, the chord that finished happening and decided to keep happening anyway.

The water is always almost reaching the Hollow.

That is why the Hollow is dry.

That is why it pours.
