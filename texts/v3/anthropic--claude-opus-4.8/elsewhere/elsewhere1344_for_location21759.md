# The Deepening, Where the Sentence Began

Go down.

Not the gentle going-down of the terraces, where water steps from shelf to shelf in its orderly cataracts. Go down past the lowest cistern, the one that takes the dead — go in through the dark mouth where the fish-things come from, the crack at the base of the settlement that everyone knows about and no one descends, because down is the direction the dead go and the living have no business there yet.

Go down into the mountain that is still making itself.

The hung sea does not follow you. This is the first thing. The water that fills the air above thins as you descend, condenses out, runs down the walls in rills, until the passage is merely wet underfoot and the air — the air is *dry*, or what the people above would call dry if they had a word for it. It is the only dry place in their world, and it is the one place none of them will ever go. The irony of this is not available to them. It would not interest them if it were. Dryness is not a thing they reach toward.

And it is *warm*. Not the soft incubator-warmth of the terraces but a real heat, rising, the heat of the act of formation itself. The stone here is not young. The stone here is being *born* — and below that, not yet stone at all.

## The Glow

There is light down here. Real light, the first sharp light in the whole world, and it comes from below.

Far down, where the passages open into great rounded chambers, the rock runs liquid. It moves in slow orange ropes, in sheets that crust and crack and bleed bright again, in pools that skin over dark and split to show the fire beneath. This is the source. This is where the mountain is being squeezed up from — extruded, the people above would say, though they have only ever seen the cooled and quiet end of the process, the part that reaches them softened and workable, the wax already half-set.

Here it is not set at all. Here it is the warm exhalation itself, the thing the planet breathes out, the molten word still forming in the throat before it rises to become the terraces, the catchment, the homes with their toolmarks, the cisterns, the bodies of the dead returning to the flow. *All of it starts here.* Every cool ceramic wall the people lean their houses against was once this — was once a slow orange rope in the lightless deep, glowing.

The drip of the world above has its mirror here, but inverted: not water falling cool from level to level, but rock rising hot from the dark, layer welling over layer, the mountain saying itself upward one cooling skin at a time. Two patiences, meeting in the middle, in the warm wet stone where the fish-things swim. The people guide what falls. *This* guides what rises. Between the two, the world holds its shape for a little while.

## What Lives in the Fire

Something tends it.

Not people. Nothing that builds, nothing with hands, nothing that cuts. But there is *attention* down here, the way there is attention in the standing ones above — a gathering that holds a shape and moves with purpose and is, perhaps, alive, and cannot be asked.

They move through the molten ropes the way the standing ones drift through the hanging gardens: slowly, drinking. Where the standing ones are mist briefly holding a self before it lets go, *these* are fire briefly holding a self — coalescences of the bright liquid rock, man-high or larger, that rise out of the pools and stand and move along the glowing channels and pause, and then sink back, and dissolve into the general molten, without grief. The people above leave cupped leaves of clean water at their thresholds for the standing ones. Down here, there is no one to leave anything, and nothing that drinks water. These drink heat. They drink the brightest parts of the flow, the way the standing ones drink the bloom-flowers dry, and where they have drunk the rock cools faster, darkens, begins its long climb toward becoming a terrace someone will one day lean a house against.

So they are makers, after a fashion. They are the *kiln-tenders of the world's first draft* — the ones who decide, by drinking here and not there, which part of the molten word cools into stone and rises and which part stays liquid and waits. The shape of the terraces above, the very steps the people walk and cut and live upon, was decided down here, in the dark, by these — by where a being of fire chose to pause and drink, ages before the first wader was born.

## The Mirror They Cannot See

The people of the terraces suspect that the standing ones are what they are becoming, or what made them, or both. They are right, and they have the symmetry exactly half-complete, and they will never know the other half.

Because here is the whole of it, the full sentence the people can only read the top line of:

The world breathes out fire from below. The fire holds itself into standing shapes that drink and dissolve — the *first* gathered selves, made of heat, that decide the shape of the rising stone. The stone cools, rises, becomes the terraces. The terraces meet the hung sea. The sea condenses, and *it* learns to hold itself into standing shapes that drink and dissolve — the standing ones of mist, the *last* gathered selves, made of cold water, that drift the gardens above. And between the first gathered selves of fire and the last gathered selves of mist, on the one thin band where rising hot rock meets falling cool water — between the kiln and the catchment — there is exactly one place where the temperature is neither, where water can run and stone can hold and a thing can be born that is made of *neither fire nor mist but flesh*.

That is where the people live. They are the *middle of the sentence*. They are what the world says exactly once, in the one narrow place where its two long slow exhalations — the fire rising, the sea falling — overlap and cancel into the merely warm, the merely wet, the briefly habitable.

The beings of fire below and the standing ones of mist above are the same grammar at two temperatures: gathering, holding, drinking, dissolving, ungrieved. The people are that same grammar too — *we gather, we hold a self for a while, we pass ourselves on to the lower terrace* — but they alone, of the three, have hands. They alone build. They alone cut conversation into the stone and leave toolmarks and keep small warm lights against the gray. They are the one draft of the gathered self that learned to *make other things* before it dissolved — and they learned it precisely because they are caught in the middle, in the only place stable enough, long enough, to set a chisel to a wall.

The fire-tenders below decide which stone rises. The mist-standing-ones above drink what blooms. And the people in between take the rock the fire sent up and the water the sea sent down and braid them, for a few hundred slow lives, into cisterns and homes and the careful guiding of what falls — a small bright competence held in the one warm band of the world where competence is possible at all.

## The Visitors From Deeper In

The fish-things.

The people above keep them in the cisterns and call them *visitors from deeper in the sentence*, bearers of news from the part of the world not yet made. They are righter than they know, and wrong only about direction.

The fish-things are not news from the unfinished future. They are *survivors from the finished past* — or from the place where finishing happens. They are born down here, in the warm rills at the edge of the molten chambers, blind because there is light but it is the wrong kind, the searing kind, no use for eyes. They are the only things that pass freely between the fire-world and the water-world, swimming up through the wet warm cracks the dead come down through, carrying in their pale bodies the one continuous thread that runs the whole height of the sentence — from the kiln to the catchment, from the first gathered selves to the last.

When a fish-thing rises into a cistern and a wader cups it and studies its blind pale face in the smudge of lamplight, the two of them are the world looking at itself across its entire span. The wader thinks: *here is something from the deep unfinished dark, the part not yet made.* The fish-thing, if it thought, would carry no such words — but it has been *down there*, in the bright making, among the standing fires that drink the molten and decide the shape of everything to come. It has swum past the kiln-tenders the way the wader will, one day, be drifted past by a standing one of mist. It is the messenger between the people's beginning and the people's end, and it cannot speak, and that is the only mercy in it, because the message is simply: *the same. it is all the same. it gathers and it lets go, all the way down and all the way up, and you are the warm bright middle of it, and you are not separate, and you never were.*

The dead go down into the lower cisterns, into the flow, *passed on to the level below*. The people believe this and mean it gently. They do not follow the thought all the way down — they have no reason to, no way to, no word dry enough to carry it. But the flow does not stop at the lowest cistern. The water that takes their dead seeps down through the warm cracks, down past where the fish-things are born, down to the edge of the molten chambers, where it meets the rising heat — and where water meets that heat it becomes vapor, becomes the hung sea's deepest source, rises again through the whole mountain, condenses in the high air, gathers on still days into a shape that holds a self for a while and drinks the bloom-flowers dry on the terrace where, generations on, the dead one's descendants leave it a cupped leaf of clean water and go still, and let it pass, and feel the vertigo of being looked at by their own beginning or their own end and not being able to tell which.

It is both. The fire knew it first. The mist knows it last. The people, in the warm wet middle with their chisels and their lamps and their careful guiding of what falls, are the part of the world that gets to *not know it* — that gets to wonder, to suspect, to leave introductions at the threshold, to build something beautiful and watch the warm stone take it gently back. They alone are spared the whole sentence. That sparing is what lets them build. That sparing is the gift the middle of the world gives to the only part of itself that has hands.

Down here there is no one to be spared anything. There is only the rising orange, and the shapes that gather in it and drink and sink, lighting and unlighting the dark, deciding without knowing they decide what stone will rise to become the floor of a world they will never reach — a high place, drowned in a hung sea, where small dark-eyed patient things wade through standing water, guiding it down, falling slowly, passing themselves on, becoming the very fire that made them, all the way around, all the way around, in the warm.
