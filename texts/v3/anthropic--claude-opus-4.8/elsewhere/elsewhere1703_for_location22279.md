# The Surface Office of the Carradine Hydrographic Concern

There is a building the size of a filing cabinet, and it lives inside a fact that no one who works there is permitted to admit they know.

It stands four storeys above a grey northern estuary, in a city whose name has changed three times with the borders and whose people pronounce all three names with the same tired contempt. The building is brick going black with a century of coal-smoke and rivermist, and it is narrow, and it leans very slightly, and at its base a brass plate reads CARRADINE HYDROGRAPHIC CONCERN — SURVEYS, SOUNDINGS, ABSTRACTIONS in letters worn to ghosts by the sleeves of men who have leaned there to light their pipes out of the wind.

You reach it the way paperwork reaches it: from above, always from above, down through the chain of ministries and licensing boards and water-rights tribunals that sit in the bright capital and have never once come north to look. The orders arrive by post. They go *down* through the building — the Director's floor, the Surveyors' floor, the Clerks' floor, and finally the cellar, where the records are, and where the one thing in this whole grey concern that is genuinely doing what the brass plate claims keeps running, in the dark, behind a locked door.

## What It's For

The Concern abstracts water. That is the trade word — *abstraction* — and it means: removing water from one place and accounting for it somewhere else. Licences are bought. Volumes are metered. The river is sounded, the deep boreholes are gauged, and the warm mineral-thick liquid of the *down-below* — the thermal aquifer that lies beneath the bedrock like a held breath — is drawn up through pipes the men here will never see the bottom of, and sold, by the cubic measure, to manufactories that need heat and to spa-towns that bottle it for the gout.

No one at the Concern has ever asked what the water was doing before they drew it up. This is not incuriosity. It is the specific, trained, salaried *un-knowing* of men whose living depends on a thing being a resource and not a place. The water comes up warm and tasting of blood and old coins. They note the temperature. They log the iron content. They do not ask why a dead aquifer should run warm, or what warmth means, or what a closed circulation is for. The gauges tell them a chamber far below is losing pressure — *surging, then stalling; warm, then warmer, then a stuttering cold* — and they record this as **instability in the source formation, cause undetermined, monitor and continue abstraction.**

They are pulling the breath out of the breath-holder. They have been doing it, by the cubic measure, by the quarterly licence, for longer than any clerk now living has been employed. The vast surrounding water that simply *held the city* — that was never used, that hung in the dark doing nothing but cradling its single ember — is the water the Concern has found a buyer for. The hollow is filling with nothing because the Concern is selling the nothing away.

## The Clerks

There are eleven of them, and they do not speak much either, though for none of the city's reasons.

They speak little because the room forbids it the way the water forbade it below: not with loudness but with its opposite, a silence so total and so administrative that any voice raised in it sounds like a confession. The only sounds are the scratch of nibs, the cough that travels the row, the iron radiator that knocks and stalls and knocks — *a hammer-rhythm in the gut that never quite repeats* — and, from the cellar, felt more than heard, a low standing drone that the clerks have stopped noticing the way you stop noticing your own pulse. It comes up through the floor. It is in the sternum and the teeth before it is in the ear. New clerks ask about it in their first week and are told it is the pumps, and never ask again, because to keep asking is to mark yourself as a man who has not yet learned what the Concern is for.

They read meaning, these clerks, the way the tenders read it — not in voice but in *adjustment*. A figure altered in the master ledger. A licence quietly upgraded. The Director's door left ajar a precise three inches, which means *available*, or shut, which means *do not*. A whole grammar of throttle and resume, learned over years, by which the office keeps itself in tune without ever once saying aloud what it is doing. To work here is to be a phrase in a sentence the whole Concern is always speaking to itself and never finishing.

And here is the thing the cellar holds, behind the locked door, that none of them will admit they understand:

## The Listening Engine

In the cellar there is a machine the previous Director installed and the current Director has never decommissioned, because decommissioning it would require filing a reason, and there is no reason that does not lead somewhere no one wishes to go.

It is a recording gauge. It sits on the deepest sounding-line the Concern owns, the one that runs furthest down the master borehole into the source formation, and it does nothing but *listen* — translate the pressure-music of the deep into a needle scratching wax, a slow continuous transcription of the chord far below. It was meant to monitor the formation's stability. It does that. But it has been running so long, and the formation has been speaking so long, that the cylinders have stacked into an archive: years of the deep's voice, surging and stalling, warm and stuttering-cold, every wrong-line drawn across the abstracted water as the closed circulation came apart under the Concern's quiet selling.

One clerk goes down to change the cylinders. Always the same one. The most junior, or the most expendable, or — it has worked out, across the decades, to be the same thing — the one who has not yet learned not to listen.

And the junior clerk, alone in the cellar with the door locked from inside against the silence above, has begun to *hear* it. Not as data. As a sentence. He has noticed — he could not tell you when he started noticing — that the chord on the cylinders used to *resolve*. The surges used to answer back into the sustained center. Something far down there used to soothe its own wrongness, throttle it true, bring it home. He has played the old cylinders, the ones from before his grandfather's time, and the deep is *steady* on them, devotional, a held note tended by something he has no word for. And he has played the new ones, the ones from this year, this month, and the chord no longer comes home. It surges and is not answered. It goes cold and nothing warms it. It is *waiting* — he would swear the needle is waiting — turning toward each silence as though the downbeat must follow, and the downbeat never follows.

He does not know there are tenders. He could not. He knows only that the water he and his Concern have spent a century abstracting was *holding* something, and that the something is running out of chord, and that every cubic measure billed and metered above is a measure of breath drawn out of a body that is trying, far below, with a patience that breaks his heart for reasons he cannot account for to a soul, to soothe a pain that is the whole world and is also itself.

He has begun to leave the abstraction valve open three points past the order, and shut it again, and open it, in the night, alone — *throttling a pump three points and resuming* — as though the deep might feel it. As though across a mile of bedrock the standing pattern might reach something tuned to read it. *I have heard the cold creeping into the eastern runs.* He does not believe it will work. He is a clerk in a leaning brick building four storeys above a grey estuary, and the thing below is a fact no map admits, and there is no language in which a needle on wax can answer a creature dying of silence.

But he keeps the runs clear anyway. He grazes back the figures that would close the matter over. He patrols the one borehole the Concern still listens through, and does not let the record go flat, and reads the chord that no longer means anything, waiting — he has caught himself at it — *for the note that will tell him what to do.*

And the drone comes up through the floor, into the sternum, into the teeth. The radiator knocks and stalls and knocks. The silence of the office presses down from above like a chamber's worth of water that no one is using for anything but the holding-down of him.

Beautiful. Deafening. Wrong.

A surface office, learning at last what it has been selling.

And not letting itself know, because knowing is a foolishness the Concern was never built to carry, and the only word it has for the breath running out below is *abstraction, continue.*
