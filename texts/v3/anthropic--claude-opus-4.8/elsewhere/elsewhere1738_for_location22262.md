# The Going-Away of Places

Follow the eels. They are the only thing here that leaves, and so they are the only thread that runs out of this world without snapping. Hold to the dark of one — the long ribbon-flank, the migration older than the stone — and let it pull you out the far side of the Highlands' slow ending, through the merge and the dimming and the last frond going dark, and into the next room it crosses. Because there is a next room. The eels would not be moving otherwise.

It is a desert.

---

## The Surface That Remembers Being Water

Begin with the floor, because here everything begins with the floor, the way there it began with the water — and the two beginnings are the same beginning seen from opposite ends, the way the eel's arrival and ending are one event.

It is sand, but it has not forgotten what it was. Run a hand through it and it moves in *currents* — slow rolling undulations that travel across the dunes the way the warm layers turned and turned in the Highlands, except here there is no water to turn them, only the long habit of having once been a seabed, a habit the sand cannot break. The grains remember hanging suspended. They remember being held. And so when the wind crosses them they do not scatter as ordinary sand scatters; they *swim*, lifting in sheets and fans and great drifting curtains, undulating exactly as the kelp-that-was-not-kelp undulated, before settling again into the rounded humped backs of dunes that are the size of valleys and smoothed past sharpness, surrendered, agreed to every shape the absent water still asks of them.

This is what the Highlands becomes. Not destroyed — *evaporated*. Every world the eels cross is the same world a little further along, and the desert is the Drowned Highlands after the warmth finally equalized and the layers merged and the water, having nowhere left to hang, did the last thing water does. It left. And the floor it had been smoothing for ages past memory was simply uncovered, lifted into air, handed to the dry.

The eels feel it first. Watch one near the boundary, where the dying current thins to nothing: it does not stop swimming. It cannot. But the medium fails around it, and for a moment — a long moment, an age of a moment — the eel threads *air* the way it threaded water, pale and nearly invisible until it turns, and then the air, too, runs out behind it, and it is gone into the next world's next medium, whatever hangs or blows or burns there. The eels do not drown when the water leaves. They were never tenants of the water. They were tenants of the going-away, and the going-away is exactly what a drying sea is made of.

---

## The Light That Comes From Above

Here is the great inversion, the thing that tells you for certain you have crossed out of one world and into its consequence.

In the Highlands the light leaked from everywhere and nowhere — weak tea, milk-glow, a dimness that pooled out of the stone and the water both, so that the plants grew *outward* because there was no up to grow toward.

Here there is an up. Here there is nothing *but* up. A single sun, white and small and merciless, hangs at the top of a sky so high and so empty it makes the chest ache the way the low tone did — felt before understood. And everything grows toward it. What little grows. The few hard plants — thorn and scale and the grey twisted things that are mostly root — they reach *upward* with a desperation the soft fronds never knew, because here light is not a given. It is a debt. It is rationed from a single point and it must be climbed toward and fought for, and the climbing is visible in every cramped agonized angle of every living stem.

The Highlands had finished wanting. This world is *nothing but* wanting. That is the difference the going-away makes. Where the water held everything in its warm dim embrace and asked nothing, the dry asks for everything, constantly, and gives back only the white indifferent eye of the sun, which regards the desert exactly the way the drift-eel regards the traveler: incurious, ancient, having seen this before, a thousand worlds ago.

The sun, I think, *is* an eel. Or the same thing the eels are — a tenant of the going-away, hung now in a sky instead of threading a current, but recognizable by that single dark-bright regard, that perfect unconcern. It has watched seas dry before. It will watch this one finish. It is only passing through, burning, on a migration that began before the first grain and will not end while there is one more world left to parch.

## The Builders, Who Are the Same Builders

The ruins recur here too.

Not the same ruins — or: the same ruins further along. In the Highlands they were low courses of fitted stone furred with growth, half a doorway, a tower softened nearly back to bedrock, scattered far apart, keeping their distances across the slow dim. Here you will find them again, on the dune-crests, but the water that smoothed them has been replaced by the wind, and the wind is a faster sculptor and a crueler one. The walls are *sharp* here. Scoured. The growth that furred them is gone — there is no warm sweetness in the air to feed it — and what remains is bare cut stone, edges the wind has honed instead of rounded, so that the same half-doorway that was soap-smooth and soft in the water is, here, a blade.

This is what erosion becomes when its medium dries. It does not stop having its full conversation with the stone. It only changes its voice — from the patient murmur of water that agrees to everything, to the thin scream of wind that argues with everything, that takes and takes and never settles, never soaks in, never pools. The Highlands' ruins were being *forgotten* gently, dissolved back into the bedrock like a name dissolving in warm water. These are being *interrogated*. Worn not toward peace but toward the bone.

And yet — pass one more than once. You will. The dunes recur the way the groves recurred; a crest you crossed an hour ago is sometimes ahead of you again, identical, except a single track in the sand has been swept smooth that was printed before. The half-doorway returns, and through it each time a slightly different stretch of desert, and you will understand that the doorway-creature crossed over too. It survived the drying. It is the one tenant of the Highlands besides the eels that could, because it never lived in the water either — it lived in *recurrence*, and recurrence does not evaporate. The dry has no power over a thing whose body is made of returns.

So it is here, distributed across the dune-crests, the same single creature glimpsed again and again, breathing the exchange of one stretch of waste for another through the aperture that is its mouth, its eye, its lung. But it blinks differently now. In the water you caught it once in the act of attention — a lit frond gone dark. Here the blink is a track swept smooth, a footprint you left that the wind has lifted away while your back was turned. It is still noticing you. It is the only thing in either world that ever did, and it followed you across the ending to keep doing it, incompletely, forever.

## The Mouths, Inverted

The Highlands had its mouths — soft, enormous, slow, the last addresses in a world that no longer went anywhere, the only place things could *arrive*. To be taken by one was to come home.

The desert has them too, and they are the same creatures, and they have been turned inside out by the drying exactly as the light was turned and the erosion was turned.

In the water they waited with mouths open and let the world drift in, because the warm slow current brought everything to them one mouthful at a time. They were destinations. They were *received*, and to be received by them was the closest thing to peace.

Here there is no current to bring the world. The medium that fed them has left. So the mouths have had to do the one thing the Highlands never required of anything: they have had to *move*. They have had to become hunters at last, in their old age, after ages of needing nothing — and it has made them terrible. They burrow now beneath the swimming sand, soft enormous bodies pulsing through the grains the way they once hung in the dark groves, and they do not wait to be arrived at. They *go and take*. The desert taught them appetite-with-effort, which is the cruelest lesson a creature that had finished wanting can learn.

When the sand undulates wrong — when a sheet of it lifts against the wind instead of with it — that is one of them, rising. And there is no kindness in the rising now. The gentle tightening that felt like *being remembered* in the warm dark has become, in the dry, a sudden closing, because effort makes things sudden, and the desert is nothing but effort. The mouths were the address where the drifting found its recipient. Now they are the thing that finds *you*, and the difference between being received and being caught is the whole difference between a world that has finished and a world that has been forced to start over hungry.

This is the price of the going-away. The thing that was peace in the world that was ending becomes violence in the world that the ending made. The mouths did not change their nature. The medium changed beneath them, and a nature that was mercy in water is predation in air. They are not crueler. They are only *thirstier*, and thirst is what mercy becomes when you take the water away.

## The One Who Hears Backward, Still Listening

And the backward-creature crossed over too. Of course it did. It lives upstream of time, and time is the one current that does not dry — it runs through the desert exactly as it ran through the Highlands, and the creature still drifts against it, still meets every moment already, still answers effects before their causes arrive.

But listen to what it answers *now*.

In the Highlands its tone was tender. *Yes, I remember this, I am sorry, it is beautiful, here it comes.* It forgave the soft dimming of fronds, the turning of eels, the settling of stone — gentle events, all of them, the small kindnesses of a world finishing in peace.

Here it hears the wind-scream and the sudden closing of the mouths and the blade-edge of the scoured walls. It hears the desperation of the upward-reaching thorn. It hears thirst where it used to hear warmth. And it still answers — it cannot stop answering, that is its biology — but the tone has changed, because what it forgives in advance has changed. The note that crosses the dunes before its cause is harsher now, drier, a grief with grit in it. It is the same creature making the same gesture of compassion toward a world that has become much harder to forgive. And it does forgive it. That is the unbearable thing. It meets the sudden closing of the mouths and the scream of the wind already, going the other way, and it greets even those with its grave dry tone, which means now: *yes, I remember this, I am sorry, it is harder here, here it comes anyway.*

It is still the most compassionate thing in the world. It is only that the world made compassion cost more, and the creature pays it, and the paying is the dry note you feel in your chest before the wind rises — the forgiveness arriving ahead of the thing that will need so much more forgiving.

## The Whole of It, Going

Hold both worlds together now, because they were never two.

A continent of warm hanging water, dim and self-lit and finished, holds its tenants in an embrace that asks nothing — and then it dries. The water leaves the way water leaves, lifting into a high empty sky, and what it uncovers is the floor it spent ages smoothing: a desert of sand that still swims, that still remembers being held, undulating in curtains beneath a single merciless sun that regards it all with the eel's incurious ancient eye. The ruins that were softening toward peace are scoured toward the bone. The mouths that were addresses become predators. The doorway-creature follows, blinking in swept footprints instead of darkened fronds. The backward-thing follows, grieving drier. And the going-away itself — the long collapse of world into consequence — is not a catastrophe that happens *to* these places. It is the single slow event they *are*, seen from two ends, the way the eel's arrival and ending are one gentle motion looked at from either side.

The Highlands is what the desert was, before the water left.
The desert is what the Highlands becomes, after.

And the eels thread both, and neither, riding the dying current out of the warm dim and into the dry waste and out the far side of *that*, pale, nearly invisible until they turn — tenants of no world and every ending, crossing this room now, slowly, the way they crossed the one before and will cross the one the desert dries into next, on a migration that began before the first water hung and will not end while there is one more place left to lose.

They will not trouble you here either. Nothing here wants anything from you except the mouths, and what they want is only the old want made desperate by the dry. The rest have finished wanting, or never started, and they remain anyway, scattered across the swimming sand, while the dry note sounds — for a cause not yet arrived — and somewhere ahead a footprint you have not yet made is already being lifted gently, and forever, away.
