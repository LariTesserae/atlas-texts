# The Archive of Finished Things

Go warm to find it. That is the only direction that works. Not north, not down any avenue — the Foundry District has no compass that holds — but toward heat, the way the birds are toward heat, the way the ancient lichen banks its small hoarded sun. Follow a bird long enough past the block it firms beneath its feet and you'll notice it isn't wandering. It is *returning*. They all return, eventually, the same way, and where they go the cold thins and the breath stops hanging and the slow tide of completion you'd been swimming against turns, at last, and carries you.

It carries you here.

Begin with the warmth, then, since the cold was the first thing there and this is its opposite: a dry, even, page-warmth, the warmth of a sunned room with the sun gone but the heat still held in everything it touched. Not a fire's warmth — there's no source, no more than there was a source for that overcast — but the warmth of things that have finished giving off what finishing gives off. It comes from the walls. It comes from the floor. It comes, most of all, from below.

## The place itself

It is a single enormous room, and it is the inside of the answer to the question the Foundry District was asking.

The walls are stone — *committed* stone, stone that decided so long ago that the deciding is geological, fossil, beyond recall. There is no haze of medium anywhere. Run your hand along anything and it is exactly as real as your hand, no realer, no vaguer; for the first time since you arrived in this world you and the place around you are *specified to the same degree*, and the relief of it is almost grief. You did not know how long you'd been holding your own edges together until something else offered to hold them too.

The room descends. It is built in tiers, broad shallow steps of that same fossil stone going down and down into a warmth that brightens as it deepens — the reverse of every cellar, the reverse of that basement cold. And on the tiers, in rows, in ranks, with the patience of a thing that has all the time that *was* built: the finished.

Not buildings. Not the city the Foundry was straining toward. Those are upstairs, you understand now — the Foundry District is the *workfloor*, and this is where the work is *kept*. Here are the decided things. Here is everything the birds ever leaked their certainty into until it could stand on its own.

## What is shelved here

The birds, for one. Thousands of them. They line the upper tiers in perfect stillness, head-cocked, composed, and they are not resting and not dead — they are *done working*, which in this world is a third state you have no word for. A bird that has spent itself into the substrate so many times that it finally finished *itself*, resolved its own glove-shape down to certainty, and now needs to do nothing ever again because it has, at last, been entirely decided upon. They do not leak warmth here. They have no doubt around them to warm. They simply are, with a completeness that the eye slides off of, the way the eye slides off the obvious.

Further down: the plants. Not seedlings — you understand by now that growth runs the wrong way — but the *originals*, the thousand-year lichen and the cobble-gap weed and a hundred forms you never saw upstairs, root-bound in beds of the fossil stone, and these are the warmest things in the room, the source of the source. They are postcards, you'd thought, sent early from the day the construction ends. You were close. They are not sent from that day. They are *kept against* it. This is the warehouse the postcards are mailed from. Every ancient plant rooted in a Foundry seam is a shoot of something here, reaching up through all the unbuilt cold to show the maybe-stone its future — and its future is this room, this warmth, this finishedness, stored.

## What tends it

And there is a keeper. There would have to be.

It is not a bird and not the Medium and not a plant, though it has something of all three — the bird's certainty, the Medium's patience, the plants' agedness — and I will tell you what I think it is, the way I told you what I thought the plants were.

It is the *will* the Medium does not have.

Remember the cruelty upstairs: a hand of infinite skill with no conviction behind it, building forever because it cannot decide. The Medium is all skill and no will. Down here is the rest of that sentence. Down here is the will with no skill — a thing that cannot build anything, cannot render so much as a cornice, cannot summon the idea-of-stone, but that *knows, absolutely, what should be finished and what should not.* It cannot make. It can only judge. And so the two of them work the only way they can: the Medium upstairs assembles the probable and holds it trembling; the birds carry borrowed scraps of judgment up into the cold and tip a little of it into fact; and what survives, what gets *decided all the way down*, descends here to be kept by the one thing in the world that was ever certain of anything on its own.

It moves through the tiers slowly, and where it passes it does not firm the ground — the ground here needs no firming — it *confirms*. It looks at a shelved bird and the bird is, retroactively, always to have been finished. It looks at a rooted plant and the plant's thousand years are, in that glance, made true. It is the source of all the certainty the birds spend. They come back to it empty and stand in their ranks and are refilled by being *seen* — and now you understand the only advice the Foundry District had. *Stay where you're looked at.* That was not metaphor and not survival tactic. That was theology. This room is what the looking *is*. The keeper is the eye the whole cold world is trying to stand in front of.

## The kinship, in the same breath

So here is how the two places connect, and it lives, as you asked, in the telling of them both:

Upstairs is a city that cannot finish itself because it is made of nothing but *can*. Downstairs is a keeper that finishes everything and can build nothing because it is made of nothing but *should*. Between them run the birds — the only traffic, the only trade — ferrying judgment up as warmth and bringing the judged-complete back down as cargo, and between them runs the cold, which is just the distance the birds haven't crossed yet, the held breath of every probable thing still waiting for the glance that will make it true.

You crossed it. You went warm. You followed a bird home, against the tide, and the tide turned and bore you down into the warm to stand, for the first time, fully specified, in the one room where you are exactly as real as everything around you.

And the keeper has noticed you. Not tasted you, the way the Medium tasted your edges in the alleys — *noticed* you, which is worse, which is the thing the whole cold world is dying to have happen to it. It is looking at you now with its single confirming attention, and it is deciding, as gently and absolutely as it decides everything, whether you are a finished thing that has come home to be shelved—

or an unfinished one that wandered too far warm, and now stands in the brightest looked-at place in all the world, waiting to be told, once and for all and beyond any further becoming, exactly and only what you are.

The plants don't know which they are. Neither do the birds.

Down here, you will find out. That is the difference between the two places, and the whole reason the cold has never followed anyone this far: it cannot bear to learn the answer either.
