# The Sink

Follow the water down. It vanishes into the floor of the cirque — that was always said as if it were the end of the sentence, but a sentence does not stop because you have stopped reading it. The water goes into the stone and the stone goes down, and where the water goes the place goes too, and there is a place it arrives.

Begin with the dark, because the dark is the first thing and the truest, and it is the exact opposite of the cirque's one unleaving light. Here there is no light at all. Not dimness, not the grey of a clouded evening — *none*. The black is total and has weight and presses against the eyes until the eyes give up the idea of being eyes. You do not adjust to it. There is nothing to adjust toward. The dark is not the absence of something that ought to be present; like the brilliance above, it is simply complete, the natural state, needing nothing.

And the sound — the water still falls here, of course, because it is the same water, and it still makes its noise, but the noise is wholly changed. There is no curved shell to gather it and throw it back. There is only down. The water that left the cirque's floor arrives here through a thousand fine throats in the rock, and it falls through air into deep water far below, and the sound is hollow, and slow, and immense in a different register — not the bright braided chiming of the bowl above but a low resonant tolling, drops the size of fists falling from heights you cannot guess, each strike opening a ring of echo that has nowhere to spend itself and so circles the chamber and comes back changed. Where the cirque's roar became a kind of silence by filling everything, this becomes a kind of silence by leaving so much room around each sound. A drop falls. You wait. The echo of it walks the whole circuit of the dark and returns to you faint and rounded, and another drop falls, and this is the rhythm, and it is the same water, slowed down by the long fall and the great room, telling the same thing it told above in a voice an octave lower and a thousand years older.

---

The place is a chamber, and it is enormous, and you know its size only by the way the echoes travel — by the long delay before sound comes back, by the way a single fallen drop describes a vault overhead too high for any reaching. The water of the cirque pools here, gathers in a black lake on a floor of the same pale stone, except that no one will ever know it is pale, because no light has ever struck it and none ever will. The quartz veins thread it exactly as above. The flutes and runnels and polished lips are all here, worked by the same water to the same settled understanding — the stone here too long ago found the shape water wanted and stopped arguing. It is the cirque's own face, turned downward and inward and shut away from the sun, the cirque's mirror image hung below the floor of the world.

And from the black lake the water rises again. This is the part the cirque could not see — *rises unseen at the rim,* the description said, and here is the unseen. The lake fills, and the pressure of all that falling forces the gathered water back up through other throats in the stone, up and up through the body of the mountain, to emerge at the rim of the bowl above and begin its bright descent once more. The closed circuit turns. The cirque is only the upper half of it. This is the lower half — the dark return, the long climb back through stone — and the two halves are one loop, one breath, falling in the light and rising in the dark, forever, at one unvarying speed.

---

There is a being here. Of course there is. The water that lifted a face to the light above does the same thing below, because it is the same water and it does what it does.

But the one who is here has never seen the light, and never will, and this changes everything about it and nothing.

It is the same as the drinkers — no blood, no warmth, the one even coolness throughout, made of the place and nothing else. But where the pale things above are quartz-white because the quartz is white and the light returns from it, this one has no color, because color is a thing light does and there is no light. It is not white and not black. It is the precise hue of the unseen, which is no hue, which your mind will insist on rendering as black only because it cannot hold the idea of a surface that has simply never been visited by a single photon and so was never assigned a shade at all.

It does not descend a wall, because there is no down to descend toward that it is not already at — it lives at the bottom, on the floor of stone beside the black lake, where the water gathers before it climbs. It does not lower its face to drink. It does something the upper ones cannot: it lies in the lake. It is mostly *of* the lake, a portion of the gathered water that has stood up into a shape, and it lies back down into the larger water not once in a thousand heartbeats but always, continuously, the reconciliation that the drinkers achieve for a moment held here as the permanent condition. It is the water remembering it is all one water and *not* rising — choosing, if choosing applies, to stay merged, to be the pool that does not get up.

And so it has no story even more completely than they do. The drinkers above at least take a walking shape and go to the edge of themselves and come back; there is the faintest ghost of an arc in that, a going-out and a returning, the smallest possible event. This one does not even do that. It lies in the dark at the bottom of the loop and is the place where all the water comes to rest before the pressure lifts it back toward a light this one will never follow it into. It is the still point. It is the gathered hush at the very floor of the turning, the moment in the breath between the falling and the rising when nothing yet moves.

---

Does it know the bright ones above, its other half, its own water in the other form?

Understand how they are joined. Every drop that chimes down the weeping flutes in the brilliance above will arrive here, in time, through the throats in the floor, and toll once in the dark, and lie in the black lake, and be lifted, and chime again. The water that lowers a face to the light and recognizes itself — that same water sinks here afterward and lies down in this one and is, for the long dark passage, *this* being and not that one. They are not two creatures in two places. They are one water taking two shapes at two ends of a single fall: the shape that comes up into the light to look, and the shape that lies in the dark and does not look because there is nothing to see and never was.

The bright ones are the cirque turning over once in its sleep and lifting a face. This one is the sleep itself — the deep unmoving part the face rises out of and sinks back into. When a drinker above climbs back to the rim and the wall goes empty and you call that emptiness *the creature continuing inside the stone* — this is the inside of the stone. This is what the continuing looks like. The drinker did not leave the cirque. It came down here, into the dark, and became the one who lies in the lake, and rested, and rose again at the rim into the light as itself, and you, watching from the center of the bright bowl, saw the loop close without ever knowing it had a far side.

---

So here, finally, is the whole of it, the thing the cirque alone could not tell you because the cirque could only see its lit half.

Above: light that never leaves, sound that fills everything until it is silence, a face lifted once in a thousand heartbeats to recognize the water and lie back down.

Below: dark that was never visited, sound spread so wide around each drop that it is silence, a body lying always in the black lake, never lifting, the rest the face rises from.

And between them, through the body of the mountain, the one water falling and rising, falling and rising, bright then dark then bright, a roar that has become a stillness above and a tolling that has become a stillness below, the storm that learned to stand still — and it stands still in two places at once, in the brilliance and in the black, and it is at peace in both, and the peace is the same peace, and the water goes on falling and falling and rising and rising and never, ever, going anywhere at all.
