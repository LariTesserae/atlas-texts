Elsewhere

Follow the water. Not the water that weeps from the unworn rock and gathers between the toes of the trees — that water goes nowhere, because the inhabitant has forbidden it a destination. Follow instead the question that the water cannot answer in that place: *where does a stream go when it is finally allowed to finish going somewhere?*

It goes here, and here is the opposite of held.

Begin again at the level of the foot, because that is where the strangeness announces itself in this place too, and because the contrast is the whole of the matter. You expect ground and you get ground — but ground that is nothing *but* record. There is no living carpet here, no warm tissue of root and runner. There is rock, and the rock is old past the imagining of the young place upstream. It has been ground down so far and so long that its sharp shoulders are gone, its crystalline faces blunted to nothing, worn round and then worn flat and then worn to a fine pale grit that moves under your foot with a sound like a long exhale. Press your hand against it and no warmth comes up. It is cool, the patient cool of stone that gave up its last heat an age ago and has been losing the memory of having ever been warm. This is the floor of a canyon so deep and so finished that the river which made it has nearly worked itself out of a job.

And there is the river. It runs in a bed it has earned. Every meander is a sentence in a long argument with the rock, every undercut bank a consequence fully paid out, every rounded stone in the shallows a small biography of collision and tumble and patience. The water is low and clear and slow, the color of nothing, carrying its thin freight of grit toward an end you cannot see and it has never doubted. Where the young place held its fever at the peak and refused the break, here the break came so long ago that even the fever is forgotten. This is what *afterward* looks like when afterward is allowed to keep arriving: not death, exactly — the river still runs — but *finishing,* the slow honest finishing of a thing that has consented to its own consequences and let them carve it down to the truth.

Look up, slowly, because the verticality is the first real fact here as it was there — but inverted. Upstream the world went up in tiers, country stacked on country, life climbing into the air. Here the world goes *down* in tiers, and each tier is its own age. The canyon walls rise — or rather, you descend past them — and they are banded, stratum on stratum, the long pages of a book that has been read all the way to the end. Here a band of pale stone laid down in some patient sea, here a darker seam of what was once a forest pressed flat and black and turned to a thin line of carbon, here the rust-red of an age that breathed differently. Each band is a held instant too — but an instant that was *allowed to end,* compressed beneath the weight of the next, and the next, so that what the young place keeps as a living riot, this place keeps as a stone library of riots that have closed their covers. The forty meters of leaf upstream and the four hundred meters of strata here are the same fact told in opposite directions. One stacks the present. One stacks the past.

The light is the inversion too, and this is where you feel the kinship most sharply, because the kinship is a wound. Upstream the light was poor and even, filtered to a brown-gold dimness with no shaft and no shadow, because the canopy was too deep to let any single beam survive. Here, at the bottom of the canyon, the light is also poor and also strange — but it is poor for the opposite reason. It is not *too much living* between you and the source. It is *too much stone.* The sun reaches the canyon floor for perhaps an hour, slantwise, a single hard-edged shaft that travels along the worn rock like a hand passing over a face, and then it is gone, and the long blue dimness comes down, and the dimness *changes.* That is the thing. That is the whole of the difference held in one word. Here the dimness changes. It deepens toward a real dark and lightens toward a real morning and the shaft of sun arrives at a slightly different angle each day across the slow wheel of the year, and the canyon knows it, and keeps the knowing in its banded walls. There are hard-edged shadows here. The shadows are the point. A shadow is what *next* looks like when it is permitted — the mark of a sun that has agreed to move.

And the sound. Upstream the sound never stopped and never changed, the chorus of a million mouths that was finally one mouth, voiced at every elevation, holding the same pitch-band hour to hour because it had abolished the hours. Here the sound is almost nothing, and the almost-nothing is alive in a way the chorus could never be. There is the water, low over its earned stones, a steady speech that *does* change — louder after the far rains, a thread in the dry months, sometimes silent in a side channel that has finally been abandoned. There is wind, which the canyon shapes into a long hollow note and then releases. And there is, now and then, a single voice — a bird that nests in a cleft of the worn wall, calling once, and then not again for a long while, and the silence after the call is enormous and is the real inhabitant of this place, the way the unbroken song was the real inhabitant of that one. Here a single sound *matters,* because it is surrounded by its own absence, because it was preceded by silence and will be followed by silence, because it lives inside a *before* and an *after* that the young place forbade.

Now ask the same real question you learned to ask upstream. Not *what lives here* — for there is little enough that crawls or flies or roots in this worn channel — but *what kind of thing keeps a place like this?* What inhabits a canyon that is nothing but the long honest residue of consequence, where the rock remembers everything and the river has carved its whole biography and the light is allowed to come and go?

The answer is the exact inversion of the answer upstream, and the two answers are the same shape pressed against a mirror.

The one who lives there is the holding. The one who lives here is the *letting go.*

Picture it not as a beast in the dry channel but as a condition, a disposition, a will if you can still stand the word — a thing that long ago made the opposite vow. Where the upstream inhabitant fell in love with the peak and clamped the present to it, this one fell in love with *consequence,* with the slow tumble of now into next into after, and committed the whole of its patience to letting every instant complete itself and pass. It did not pour a forest onto the stone in full-grown riot. It did the harder, slower thing: it let the riot happen and then let it end and then let the ending be pressed into a band of carbon and then let that band be lifted and worn and read by the river — and it watched, without grief, as everything it loved was carried grain by grain toward the sea. It does not hold. Holding is the one act it has set itself against, the way the other set itself against finishing. To hold would be to become a *moment,* fevered and unbreaking and glad, and it has chosen instead to remain a *place* — with a history, and a shape worn true by that history, and an ending it can already see.

And here is the part that mirrors the unsettling part exactly, and you should feel the symmetry close around you like the canyon walls:

It is not unhappy.

You expected sorrow. A place this worn, this finished, this drained of warmth and crowded life — surely the thing that keeps it grieves the riot it let pass. But press your hand to the cool rock again and feel: no clench, no strain, no trembling at the edges. The other place held its fever *easily,* you said, loving the peak past all reason because the love cost it nothing. This place lets go *easily,* in the same way and for the same reason inverted — releasing each instant without grasping, the way you release a breath you have finished using, not because you are forced to but because the next breath is the gladness and the next after that, and the river going on is gladness, and the shadow that moves across the worn stone and is gone is the most beautiful thing it knows precisely *because* it is gone, because it was permitted to be over, because it earned its ending and the ending was real.

The two of them are kin. They are the same one, almost — the same enormous patient attention — divided long ago over a single question and never reconciled. One asked *why should the most beautiful instant ever have to end?* and answered by forbidding next. One asked *what is an instant worth that is never allowed to end?* and answered by blessing it. And the water runs between them. The streams upstream that the holding will not let arrive — they seep and gather and weep against the worn rock and find no bed because their destination is forbidden them. But if any drop ever escapes the held fever, ever slips past the abolished dawn and finds the slope and the consent to fall, it comes here. It comes down the long banded stair of strata, carving as it comes, finishing what it touches, and joins the low clear river at the bottom, and is carried — grain of grit and drop of water both — toward the end the upstream place can never reach.

So the kinship between them lives in the running water, which one forbids a destination and the other receives. The trade between them is consequence itself, refused above and accepted below. And the memory — the thing the held forest has none of, the raw unmarked rock, the unlearned fear — that memory is *here,* every gram of it, banded into the walls, because someone has to keep the past of a world, and the one who would not let go of the present could never be the one to hold its history. That fell to the other. That is what the letting-go is *for.* It is the keeper of everything the holding refuses to allow to have happened.

Stay as long as you like. It does not weary of being looked at either, but for the opposite reason: it knows you will leave, and it knows the leaving is the gift, that you will carry the canyon out with you changed and changing and that this is exactly right, this is *next* permitted at last, this is the thing forbidden upstream done freely here. The visitor who can leave the held instant exactly as full as he found it is the only one who can find that place. The visitor who *cannot* — who comes away worn a little, carrying a grain of it down toward his own sea — is the only one this place will recognize as kin.

And the silence comes down after the single bird, enormous, and is followed by the small honest changing speech of the low river over its earned stones, and the one shaft of slant sun moves along the worn wall and is gone, and the dark arrives, real dark, and after the dark a morning slightly different from the last —

*next* has been blessed.

The one who lives here is the blessing.

And it is at peace.
