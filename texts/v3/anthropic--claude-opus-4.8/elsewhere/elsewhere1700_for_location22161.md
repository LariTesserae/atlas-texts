# The Watershed Mouth

You do not come down into this one. You come *up* out of it, or rather you arrive at the place where the coming-up has already happened, where everything that was held in the dark finally surrenders its hold and spreads.

This is where the water goes when it gives up being a body's blood and becomes only water again.

---

It is the opposite altitude in every sense: not the lowest place but a high one, a broad shelf of broken upland under an actual sky, and the sky is the first wrongness here, just as the under-light was the first wrongness there. Because the water that arrives at this shelf is *still glowing*. It comes out of the rock-mouth at the head of the shelf — a single dark aperture, a throat in the cliff-face, the one place where the great warm churning thing below finally overflows itself and lets its charged water escape — and it comes out *bright*, gold-white, carrying the dissolved luminance up into open air where there is already a sun and no need for it.

For the first forty strides past the mouth, the stream is brighter than the daylight falling on it. It does not need the sun and the sun does not need it and they lie across each other in the channel, two lights that have nothing to say to one another, the borrowed gold sinking and the free gold pouring over it. You can stand at the mouth with one hand in the warm bright outflow and feel, faintly, far down through the water, the pulse you would have called convection if you had been on the other side of the rock. Here it is only a tremor in a current. The body breathes out and you are standing in its breath.

And then the stream begins to forget.

---

This is the whole event of this place, the way turbulence was the whole event of the other: *the light leaves the water.* Not quickly. Over the length of the shelf — a mile of braided shallow channels spreading across pale gravel — the luminance fades out of the current molecule by molecule, releasing the charge it was taught to hold, unlearning the art. The water cools as it spreads. It cools as it forgets. By the lower edge of the shelf, where it gathers itself and falls away over the rim toward the ordinary valleys, it is only water, cold and clear and dark, with no more light in it than rain.

So the shelf is a gradient made visible. At the mouth: gold, warm, roaring faintly with remembered pulse. At the rim: clear, cold, silent, fallen. And between, the long slow dimming, the brightness thinning down the braided channels until you cannot say where exactly it stopped, only that here there is still some and there is none, the way you cannot say the moment a warm afternoon became evening.

The light does not vanish. It goes *somewhere.* That is the second event, and it is the reason anything lives here at all.

---

Because the light, leaving the water, enters the ground.

The gravel of the shelf is not dead. Where the bright water runs, the released luminance soaks down into the stones and the wet grit and is taken up — *eaten*, there is no softer word — by what grows on the channel-banks. And here, unlike the sparse and finished anatomy below, things *crowd.* This is the great difference between the two places, and it is the difference between a body and a graveyard, between a thing that holds and a thing that spends.

The banks are a riot. Low broad-leaved growth presses cheek to cheek along every bright channel, fighting for the frontage, for the first taste of the light before the next plant upstream drinks it down. They are pale at the rim of the shelf and they deepen toward the mouth — greener, ranker, more desperate the closer they grow to the source, until at the mouth itself the rock is furred with a thick dark turf so dense it drips. Everything here is racing the current. Everything here knows the light is leaving and will not come again and must be caught *now*, and so this place has none of the calm spacing of the other, none of the finished sufficiency. It is all hunger. It is all the scramble of small brief lives at the edge of an abundance that is pouring past them and will not wait.

And the things that move here are *many*, and *quick*, and *afraid.* Where the body below grew eight slow organs and a few blind unhurried grazers, this shelf swarms — small bright-fed creatures grazing the glowing banks, and faster things that hunt the grazers, and faster things again. They startle. They scatter at your shadow. They have every reason to be afraid; everything here eats everything, because here the light is *food* and not *self*, here it is the spent currency of something else's life and the whole shelf is competing for the change.

---

These two places are one circulation, and neither knows the other.

The warm thing below holds the light against entropy with infinite patience, century on century, and calls that holding its life. And it cannot hold all of it. A body that takes in must also give out; the overflow is not failure but function — it must vent, must breathe, must let the surplus go, and what it lets go runs up the throat and out the mouth and spreads across this high bright shelf and *dies into the open air, feeding everything.*

The shelf is the body's exhalation. The shelf is what the great slow creature spends without noticing, the way you do not notice the warmth your own body sheds into a cold room, the warmth that nonetheless might keep some small thing alive against your ankle through the night. Everything on this shelf lives on the difference between what the body below takes in and what it can keep. Everything here is built from the light that one enormous unhurried life could not finish using.

And the swarming hungry banks do not know there is a body. They know only the mouth, the bright outflow, the source that pours and pours and never thanks and never stops. They have made their religion of the aperture in the cliff the way the visitor below made his mistake of the room — looking in the wrong register for the wrong size of thing. They think the light comes *from* the mouth. They cannot conceive that the mouth is the smallest opening in something vast and warm and slow, that the gold they fight over is exhaust, is sweat, is the runoff of a single thought too large for them to stand inside.

They are not the inhabitants of the body. They are not its organs. They are what grows in its breath at the place where the breath leaves it — brief, frantic, brilliant, and utterly its own, owing everything to a life they will never perceive and never need to, living wholly on its overflow and calling the overflow the world.

---

The light leaves the water here, and the water falls away cold over the rim, and goes down into the ordinary valleys, and rains, and seeps, and finds — by finding the lowest place and giving up the climb — its long dark way back down to the warm churning chamber, where the eight slow cells will catch it again and teach it light again and hold it again, for a while, against the dark.

You may stay as long as you like.

But you cannot stay long. Nothing stays long here. That is the price of the brightness on this side of the rock: it is moving, it is leaving, it is *spending itself*, and everything that lives on it must live the way it gives — quickly, hungrily, all at once, afraid.

It has not noticed you. It has no time. There is light in the water and it is already fading, and there is so much to catch before the current carries the last of it over the edge and down into the dark, to begin again the slow secret journey back to the body that breathed it out and has long since forgotten it was ever gone.
