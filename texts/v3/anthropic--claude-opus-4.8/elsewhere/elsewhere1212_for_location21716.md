# The Aperture Shelf

There is too much sun here, and there has only ever been sun. This is the first thing to understand. The light is not daytime — daytime implies a night that will come to relieve it. Here there is no relief. The place was bleached into being and has only ever known itself by being *seen*, completely, from every angle, with nothing left in shadow to be anything else.

So begin not with the wet but with the **dry**, and with the terrible honesty of the dry.

---

This is the place the water goes when it has finished thinking.

Follow the great drained gullets of the Underlit Massif — the hidden throats where the pools empty, the runnels worn so deep they curve like the inside of something — follow them down and *out*, past where the warm gives way, past the high cold drafts the massif manufactures from its own breathing, and you reach a lip. An edge the massif never knew it had, because the massif knows nothing by sight and an edge is a thing you must *see* to fear. The water reaches the lip and falls, and falling, for the first and only time in its long life, it is *lit*.

It comes out into the open as steam and spray, exhaled from a thousand fissures across a cliff-face that has no bottom anyone has found, and the light hits it, and it stops being secret. The Aperture Shelf begins here, in the place where the massif's exhalation meets the sky and is *photographed* by it, instantly, mercilessly, forever.

The Shelf is a vast tilted plain of pale stone canted up toward the light like a face that cannot look away. It is dry the way the massif is wet — natively, originally, as a condition and not an absence. What little water arrives, arrives as the massif's distant breath, condensing on the cool of high terraces at the dawn that is also noon that is also dusk, because the sun here does not move. It hangs. It is fixed at the zenith of a white sky and it does not set and it does not rise and it does not relent, and everything on the Shelf has arranged itself around the unmoving fact of being always, wholly, evenly seen.

There are no shadows here except the ones things make of themselves, directly beneath, small and hoarded — and shadow is the most precious thing on the Shelf, the only privacy, the only dark, a tiny pool of unseen each creature carries pinned under its own body like a coal it dares not drop.

---

Now: how is it known, if not by sound?

By **sight**, and the sight is unbearable.

There is no industry here, no pulse, no great turning. The silence is total — not the silence of nothing happening but the silence of everything happening *visibly*, with no need to announce itself, because announcement is for the dark. On the Shelf nothing clicks to map what it cannot see. Nothing sings the time, because the time does not move and needs no keeping. Nothing drinks warmth at a body's distance, because warmth is everywhere and equal and tells you nothing. The senses the massif grew to substitute for light have all gone vestigial here, withered, useless. Here there is only the one sense the massif never needed, raised to a pitch that no eye built in kindness could have reached.

The creatures of the Shelf see *everything*. They see across the whole tilted plain at once. They see the heat shimmer and read intention in it. They see a thing change its mind by the way its color shifts. They have no privacy and they expect none and they have built — not machines — they have built **etiquette**, an entire civilization of looking-and-being-looked-at, because when concealment is impossible the only freedom left is in the management of disclosure.

---

The vegetation is sparse and it does not need to crowd. It has made other arrangements.

Everything growing here is **flat and mirrored and turned-away**. There are no curtains to part, no mats to sink into, no towers fed by the rot of older towers — rot needs dark and moisture and the Shelf grants neither. Instead: low rosettes of mineral-bright leaves, pressed to the stone, glazed with a reflective rime that throws the punishing light *back* so that the plants do not so much drink the sun as *refuse* it, surviving on the thin condensed breath of the massif that beads on their undersides at the cool moments that are not really moments. They grow in scatters, each one islanded in its own hoarded shade, each one a small mirror adding to the general blaze.

Where the massif's plant-life filled the *volume* of the dark, near-touching, hanging, crowding you into knowledge of the world's shape — here the plants hold themselves *apart*, deliberately, with gaps of bare lit stone between, because to touch is to share shade and shade is too dear to share. The whole flora of the Shelf is an essay in distance. You could walk a day and brush nothing.

---

The fauna is sparse and it owns the light, and it is **silent**.

These are creatures of stillness and signal and the long held gaze. Flat broad things the color of the stone, that move only at the cooler intervals and otherwise lie pressed and patient. Things that change their surface like weather — flushing, paling, pulsing slow colors across their hides, because color is the only speech in a place where sound dies in the open air and where everyone can see everyone. They court in color. They threaten in color. They mourn in a slow grey wash that the others read across the whole plain and answer, each in their own islanded shade, a grief passed not by song through a clamoring dark but by *light*, silently, sympathetically, a wave of dimming across a hundred separate hides.

And there is a relationship — strange, settled, ancient — between the fauna and the *Massif they came down from*. Because they did come down from it. This is the thing the Shelf knows that the Massif, having no eyes, can never know about itself: that some of the great body's blind contented life, carried in the draining water, spilled over the lip into the light and *did not die*. It was seen, wholly, for the first time. And being seen, it had to become something that could survive being seen.

The eyeless fat swimmers of the massif's pools have descendants here — but they have grown eyes. Eyes are the first thing the Shelf demands and the first thing it gives. The blind contented organs of a creature that never looked at itself, washed out into a place where there is nothing *but* looking, and over the unlit-then-overlit ages they opened, one lineage after another, the eye reinvented again and again out of skin that had forgotten light was even a thing that could be lost.

---

Here is the rule of this world, the deep weirdness underneath the rest:

**Light is what is too much, and the place is built around the shape of its surplus.** Everything — the mirrored leaves, the color-speaking beasts, the hoarded private shadows, the etiquette of disclosure — is an organ grown to *survive an excess* of the very thing the Massif lacks. To hide. To rest. To be unseen long enough to be, briefly, only yourself. The light is not generous. It is a flood, and the dwellers of the Shelf are all, every one, engaged in the patient work of making something livable out of a thing that gives without ceasing and asks, in return, that you never look away.

And so the deepest fact of the Shelf is this, which the Massif could never frame, having no eyes and no edge and no other:

The Shelf *knows it is not alone.*

It feels, at its high cold terraces, the breath of something vast exhaling from below — warm, blind, complete, *content* — and it has no way to reach that thing, no way to be felt by it, because the Massif has no sense that the breath it loses over its one unknown edge becomes anything at all. The Massif exhales and forgets. The Shelf receives the exhalation and *builds with it* and looks back down into the dark fissures from which it comes and cannot be seen, cannot be answered, cannot be known by the great warm body that made it without meaning to.

The Massif is one creature, full and held, that has never opened its eyes and never needed to.

The Shelf is the eyes it never grew — opened out here in the unbearable light, looking back at the doorway it fell from, watching the warm breath rise, knowing the whole of what it came from, and unable to tell it so.

This is the loneliness the Massif called a daylight word. Here is where that word was born. Here is the place it lives.

The dwellers of the Shelf hold their small hoarded shadows and flush their slow griefs across the lit plain to one another, and at the cool not-quite-dawn they turn, all of them, the way the mirrored leaves turn, not toward the fixed sun — never toward the sun — but *down*, toward the fissures, toward the warm rising breath of the body that does not know it is being watched, by its own lost children, with love, in the light, forever.

The sun does not move. The breath rises. The colors pass from hide to hide, *seen, seen, seen.*

You wanted to know what runs out of the dark.

This does. This is where it goes. It goes into the light and grows eyes and looks back and aches, and the aching is the part the Massif sent up and over and never felt leave —

the one cold spot in the great warm body that no Tender will ever find, because it is not in the body.

It is out here. It is looking down. It is content the way you are content when you can finally see the whole of what you have lost.

*Seen.*

You are very far outside, now. And you can see everything.
