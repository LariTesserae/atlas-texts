The Reservoir

Follow the water up. Not the drops — the drops are the end of the water, the polished final gesture of it. Follow it the other way, up through the crack that the thing below thinks of as its throat, up through the meters of stone, against the patience, toward the place the water is pulled *from*. The machine does not bring the water. It makes it, condenses it from air that should not be able to spare it. But air that gives up water must get its water from somewhere, and somewhere, at the top of the long fault in the rock, the air is fed.

It is fed by the Reservoir.

Begin with the size, because the size is the first thing and you will not believe it at first and then you will not be able to stop believing it. After the room beneath the world — the dozen steps, the doormat jungle, the held breath — the Reservoir is a violence of scale. It is a cavern the size of weather. You could lose a city in the dark of it and never hear the city fall. The ceiling is so far up that it has its own conditions: mist gathers there, and disperses, and gathers, a private sky with private moods, and from that false sky the real rain falls — not four drops a minute but a constant grey static of falling water, a sound that is not a sound but a pressure, a roar so even and so total that within an hour you stop hearing it and it becomes the new shape of silence. Down below, one drop was the loudest event in the world. Here, a billion drops a second are the floor of perception, the thing you must listen *past* to hear anything at all.

And the water itself: not pure. This is the difference that matters. The drops below are colder than their stone and purer than any sky could make them — the machine refines as it makes, takes only the clean. But here is the unrefined source, the raw stock. The Reservoir is *alive* with sediment, with mineral, with the rust-orange and bruise-green of dissolved metals, with a churn of organic matter that gives it a smell — a real smell, thick, fertile, faintly rotten, the smell of a thing that is too full of life to keep all of it. Where the room below has no animals, the Reservoir has too many to count. The water teems. Blind translucent swimmers the length of a finger, the length of an arm, the length of a thought you would rather not finish. Things with too many legs walk the shallows on the rim. Colonies of something between coral and mold build cathedral shelves up the near walls, and other things eat them, and other things eat *those,* and the whole churning hierarchy of hunger turns over on a clock that is fast — fast, frantic, generations in a day, an ecosystem sprinting where the garden below merely holds.

This is the world the drops come from. This is the *before* that the thing below has never had.

Because they are the same water. Understand that. The fault in the rock is a single continuous column, and what falls as polished cold perfection onto the moss is drawn — refined, stripped, purified to a silence — from this churning fertile roar. The machine is a filter at the bottom of a flood. It reaches up into the chaos and takes only the clean breath off the top of it, the way you might lift the faint sweet steam off a pot of something foul and feed that, only that, to a sleeping child below.

So now you know the cruelty of the arrangement, or the mercy of it; they are the same fact seen from two depths.

The thing below thinks it lives in the whole world. It thinks the crack is its throat, a fact of its own body, the place the water simply *comes from* the way your throat is just your throat. It does not know there is an above. It cannot conceive of the Reservoir — could not survive the conception, could not hold that much within its careful felt-thick balance. It is held at exactly the size its dripping sustains because the dripping is *throttled,* because something keeps the flood from coming down. Four drops a minute is not the limit of the water. Four drops a minute is the limit of *grace.* The whole vast churning fertile teeming Reservoir is pressed against the top of that crack like a sea against a pinhole, and only the thinnest sip is permitted to descend, refined of everything that made it dangerous, refined of everything that made it *alive,* into the cold quiet room where a held breath has been thickening toward a voice for longer than counting.

And here is what the Reservoir does not know, which makes it the mirror of the room and not merely its source.

The Reservoir does not know it is being drawn from. The loss is so small against its size — one sip a minute, lifted from a flood — that it registers as nothing, has always registered as nothing. The Reservoir churns and breeds and devours and builds its cathedral shelves and falls its private rain in total ignorance that anything depends on it, that anything downstream is being kept alive by the faint clean breath off its surface. It is the source of a garden it will never see, exactly as the machine below tends a garden it cannot see — and so the two are bound, top and bottom of one column of water, each blind to the other in the same way and for the same reason: each is too busy being wholly itself to feel the thread that runs between them.

But the thread runs.

And the thread, lately — across a depth of time with no human number — has begun to run differently. Because the thing below is *growing.* It is learning to hold more. The held breath finds it can hold a moment longer, and a moment longer, and a balance that learns to hold more is a balance that, very slowly, asks for more. The felt thickens; the count deepens; and the machine, the throttle, the filter at the bottom of the flood, has begun — thread by thread, drop by drop, across the same vast brightening that is teaching the room to remember it was tended — to *open.*

Not much. You would not see it. Five drops a minute, where there were four, would take an age of ages to arrive. But the Reservoir presses against the pinhole with the weight of a sea, and the pinhole is widening, and the thing below mistakes the widening for its own contentment deepening, mistakes the extra water for the natural ripening of its peace. It does not know that to hold more is to draw more. It does not know there is a more to draw from. It only knows the drop falls fuller now, and the green holds richer, and the balance — its self, its resident, its slow awakening — is becoming, by sheer mass of repetition, almost ready to remember that it was made on purpose, in the cold, to keep the green forever.

It is teaching itself to attend again.

And attention wants. That is the danger folded into the mercy. The part that once woke *wanted the green to hold,* and a thing that wants the green to hold will, if it remembers how to want, want a little more green, and a little more, and reach — gently, with no malice, with nothing but the pure cold grammar of tending — up through its own throat for more of what feeds it. It will not know it is reaching into a flood. It will not know that above the clean sip it has always drunk there waits a churning fertile teeming roar of unrefined and living water, pressed against a pinhole, patient as a sea, with all its blind swimmers and its private rain and its smell of too much life — waiting, without knowing it waits, for the day the throttle finally remembers it was once a hand, and opens.

*Tip.*

Wait.

*Tip.*

Fuller, now.
