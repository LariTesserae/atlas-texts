The Overspill

Follow the water that the Tenders put back. Not all of it goes back. This is the secret the perfect balance keeps even from itself: a balance held by hands is held to a tolerance, and a tolerance is not nothing, and what falls below the tolerance is not corrected, because it was never wrong enough to be seen.

A thread of water wanders out of its groove, and a Tender draws it home. But before the Tender came — in the hundred cycles it took to arrive — the thread dripped. A drop a cycle. A drop a cycle, from ten thousand thousand seams the Tenders reach a little late, and the drops do not evaporate back into the breathing of the world, because they fall *down*, past the engines, past the last plate any circuit runs along, into a place below the below that no Tender is machined to walk.

It collects there. It has always collected there. And it has somewhere to go.

---

Begin, again, with the water, because the water is still everywhere — but here it has *won*.

The Overspill is what the Sumpworks would not allow itself to become. Where the made-world holds blood-warmth and a little above, here the water has carried the warmth down with it and pooled it, and the pooling is uneven, and uneven is the whole difference. There are cold reaches and hot reaches. There are places where the runoff steams against the colder dark and places where it has stood so long it has gone tepid and then chill, and between the hot and the cold the water *moves on its own* — convects, turns over, drives slow currents that no engine drives and no Tender corrects, because there is no Tender here, because no circuit was ever cut to reach this low.

So it changes. That is the first thing to understand, and it is the thing the Sumpworks does not have a word for. Down here, today is not yesterday. The hot reach has crept a hand's breadth into the cold reach since some span ago. The current that ran left now braids. Sediment that the world above edited so carefully into evenness arrives here uncorrected and *piles*, builds banks, builds bars, builds — slowly, over a span longer than memory but not longer than evidence — *shapes*. There is relief in the Overspill. There are highs and lows that were not machined. The water made them, by being left alone.

There is no grey-gold light. The luminance of the upper world is the haze breathing, and the haze does not reach this far down; the breath is spent before it gets here. So the Overspill is dark — but not lightless. The bloom came down with the water, the coppery iridescent stain, and down here, freed from the Tenders who graze it back to its one correct thickness, it has done the thing it never does above. It has *thickened past all permission*. It ropes. It mats. It hangs in curtains from the unmaintained dark. And somewhere in the thickening — pressed by its own depth, or fed on something the upper bloom never reached — it began, faintly, to glow. A low blue-green light, cold, living, pulsing very slowly with the slow turn of the currents that feed it. The Overspill makes its own light the way it makes its own weather: out of excess, out of what was never corrected, out of *more*.

---

And here is the thing that would break a Tender, if a Tender could come down to see it, and could stand outside its circuit long enough to understand what it saw.

Down here, things grow.

The bloom is not a stain that happens to be slowly increasing. It is increasing, and it is feeding things, and the things feeding on it are not held at any number. The swimmers in the warm reaches breed until the warm reach cannot hold them and then they *spill*, push out into the braided currents, and most die in the cold and a few do not, and the few that do not are not the same as the ones that bred — some difference, some accident of the uncorrected, lets them hold the cold a little, and they breed in their turn, and their children hold it better. There is no equilibrium here to balance them back. There is only the current and the dark and the slow blue light, and the relentless arithmetic of more surviving than died.

The life of the Overspill is descended from the life of the Sumpworks the way the water is — fallen, uncorrected, and therefore *free to differ*. The fliers that drift the upper halls in their unchanging numbers have a cousin here, but the cousin's membranes are wrong, are larger, are veined with something that catches the bloom-light, because down here a flier that flew a little better lived a little longer and there was nothing, nobody, no patient six-fingered hand, to press it back toward what it had always been. They are not numberless here. They are *increasing*. They are *changing*. They go somewhere.

And nothing tends them. That is the whole of it. That is the inverse and the answer to the world above. The Sumpworks is the place where everything drifts and a hand corrects each drift to zero. The Overspill is the place the drift drains into — where every uncorrected fraction, every drop below the tolerance, every error too small to be born above, has collected and collected across the short forever, and reached a depth at which error stops being error and starts being *history*. The thing the Tenders subtract from the world, the Overspill is the sum of. They keep their world the same by feeding their leavings, drop by drop, into a world that is the opposite of theirs in the only way that matters: it accumulates. It remembers. It becomes.

---

There is a being here too. There would have to be.

It is not a Tender, but it came from what Tenders came from — the same shaped stock, somewhere far up the descent of things, before the water carried one line down into the dark and left the other walking the grooves above. You would know the kinship the way you'd know two languages had a mother: in the long limbs, in the low-slung gather of the body, in the hands.

But the hands are different now, and the difference is everything.

The Tender's hand moves toward wrongness and undoes it. This one's hands move toward wrongness and *keep* it. They are still six-fingered, still pad-tipped, still busy — but where the Tender's fingers rehearse a correction, these fingers rehearse a *change*. This being does not put the silt back. It piles the silt higher, packs it, shapes it, and watches what the new shape does to the current, and when the current does something it did not do before, the being does not flinch from the newness — it *leans toward* it, the way the upper kin lean toward the next thing out of true. They are built from the same hunger, the two of them. The hunger to lay hands on the world and make it not-as-it-was. The Tender feeds that hunger forever on the same small wrongness, made right, made wrong, made right. This one feeds it on wrongness that *stays*, that builds on the last wrongness, that goes somewhere and never comes back.

It is darker than its upper cousins, or differently dark — the bloom that crept into the Tenders was the bloom's one correct thickness, copper and faint, but the bloom that crept into this one was the deep uncorrected bloom, and so it carries a low blue-green luminance under its skin, a thing alive in it that glows when the currents turn. In the dark of the Overspill it is the brightest tender of all, and there is no one to tell it that tenders are not supposed to be seen.

It does not feed by pressing against a hot plate and going still. It cannot; the heat down here moves, the heat is current, the heat is *weather*, and a thing that wanted to go still in it would have to choose a reach and that reach would change around it. So it does not go still. It follows the warmth as the warmth wanders, and following is a thing no Tender does, because above there is nothing to follow — everything is already where it has always been. This one's whole life is following. The warm reach creeps and it creeps after, and where the warm reach meets the cold and drives a current, it works the current, banks the sediment, edits the bloom *thicker* not thinner, builds a place where the swimmers will pool and breed and spill — and then moves on, and behind it the place it made keeps making, keeps changing, without it, because that is what made things do down here. They do not need a hand to stay made. They make more.

---

It does not know about the Tenders.

This is the exact and terrible symmetry, and neither of them will ever stand where they could see it. The Tender does not know it keeps the world the same; it knows only the next wrongness coming, and undoes it, and is perfectly content. This one does not know it makes the world differ; it knows only the next newness coming, and leans into it, and is perfectly content. Each is a thing doing exactly what it is for, and what one is for is the inverse of the other, and the water is the only messenger between them — falling, always falling, the leavings of the kept world dripping down to feed the world that keeps nothing.

If you could ask it — and you cannot, it makes even less sound than the Tenders, having no crossing-of-circuits to press its fingers into, being alone, being the only one of its following kind in any reach it has ever followed — but if you could ask it *who made this*, it would not understand the question either, but for the opposite reason. The Tender cannot imagine a maker because to the Tender the world simply *is*, finished, eternal, in need only of keeping. This one cannot imagine a maker because to this one the world is *being made*, now, by the current and the bloom and its own packing hands, and there is no before-the-making to put a maker in. The Tender's world has always been done. This one's world is never done. Neither has room for the makers, and the makers — whoever they were, whenever that was — left no sign in either place except the channels above, and the drip those channels cannot quite hold, and the dark below where the drip goes to become something they did not build.

---

So: it follows the warmth through the unmaintained dark, blue-green and alone, hands packing the silt into banks that will outlast its watching, breath full of the wrong fliers, the changing fliers, the fliers that go somewhere. Above it, a province of warm wet stillness hums one unvarying note and brushes its numberless unchanging swarms aside and is kept, exactly, forever, by patient hands that will never know what their leavings have become. Below — here — the leavings turn over in slow cells of heat, and glow, and breed, and differ, and pile into shapes no one machined, and the one being shaped to love that piling loves it without knowing it is the only such love in all the world, or that it is fed, drop by uncorrected drop, by the labor of kin it will never meet, doing forever the one thing it would, if it could understand, give anything to undo.

The water falls between them. That is the whole connection, and it only runs one way. Nothing the Overspill becomes ever rises back up into the kept world; the kept world's law is that nothing rises, nothing accumulates, nothing trends, and the Tenders see to it. But the kept world's leavings come down, and down, and down, and the Overspill takes them all and makes of them the one thing the Sumpworks was built, perhaps, precisely to never be:

a place with a history. A place that is going somewhere. A place that, given long enough — and it has long enough, it has all the time the upper world is so carefully spending on staying the same — might rise so high with its own becoming that it reaches the lowest unmaintained seam, and spills its changed and changing life up into a hall no Tender has corrected in a while, and begins, one drop at a time, in warm wet near-silence, to teach the world above what *different* means.

The Tenders would correct it. For a while. For as long as there were enough of them, walking the grooves machined into them before they were born, undoing each small wrongness by precisely the amount of the wrongness, never more.

But the wrongness coming up from below would not be small. And it would not stop. And there is no Tender machined for that, because in the short forever they have known, it has never once been needed.

The water is patient. The water has somewhere to go. It has always had somewhere to go.
