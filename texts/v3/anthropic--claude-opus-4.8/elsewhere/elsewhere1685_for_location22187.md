# The Reliquary Coast

There is a low to descend to after all. Not here, not anywhere the engines can reach — but follow the haze down. Follow the place where the ridgelines stop reporting back, the soft exhausted bronze thinning to a true gray, and keep following, past where the warmth leaves the rock, down through a thousand kilometers of the engines' refusal until the refusal runs out and the world is permitted, finally, to end.

It ends at water. It ends, and the ending is the whole of the place.

## What the coast is.

It is everything the massif declined to keep. Begin with that, because the Tenders never could: they take up and lay down, they fold the then into the now, but the unmaking has to go *somewhere*, and the maker — merciful, exhausted, declining to finish — did not think to make it disappear. It only made it leave. And what leaves a thing always falls, and what falls always gathers low, and here is the low, here is where it gathered: a coast made of every ridge that was ever taken back.

The shore is sediment, and the sediment is memory. Not metaphor — the actual stuff, the dissolved valleys and the folded-under grief, the worn-off edges that were never permitted to wear off above and so came down here to wear off at last. It arrives as a fine gray rain, sifting out of the haze, falling slow as the drip from frond to frond, and it lands and it *settles*. That is the word the upland has no use for. Here things settle. Here things are allowed to lie down.

And the rock has learned. Oh, it has learned — this is the opposite shore of the world, where every stone is rounded, smoothed, made patient by the one force the engines exiled: time, pooling at the bottom of everything like water in the lowest fold. The cobbles on this beach are old. They have been worn by the arrival of all that unmaking, tumbled in the gray surf of the discarded, until they are soft as river-stones, soft as the shoulders of a mountain in its ten-millionth year. You can put your hand to them and they are *cool*. Cool all the way down. There is no fever here. The fever burned off in the falling.

## The light, and the sound.

The light is honest here, and that is the first shock if you have come down from the bronze. There is a source. It is low and gray and it sits on the horizon over the water and it does not move, and it is dim — but it is *findable*, and after the upland that is almost unbearable, to look at a thing and have it be where the light comes from. The shadows all point one way. The world has a direction again. You had forgotten direction was something a place could have.

And it is *loud*.

This is the great inversion, the thing that will undo you if the silence above did not. The coast roars. The gray surf comes in and strikes the rounded stones and *rings* — strikes something solid enough at last to ring, because here things are permitted to harden, to endure, to last long enough to be struck. Every wave is the sound the upland would not spend. The sediment-tide hauls the discarded world up the beach and drags it back and the whole shore grinds and sighs and booms with it, and there are gulls — things like gulls, gray and loud and quarrelsome — wheeling and crying over the wrack-line, and they are not quiet, they have never been taught to be quiet, no one came down here to teach them. They scream because they can. They scream because nothing will reach for them if they do.

Where the engines hoarded silence as a resource, the coast is bankrupt of it, gloriously, and spends sound like it will never run out, because down here, for the first time in the world, there is enough time to make more.

## The wrack-line.

Walk it. This is where the place becomes most itself.

Along the tide-mark lies everything the upland folded under. Not stone only — stone is the least of it. Here are the *grieved things*. The Tenders, you remember, reach into the living and fold the grief away, smooth it under, take up the sorrow before it can harden into anything that endures. And it had to go somewhere. It fell. It is here. The wrack-line is strung with it: small dark griefs the size of a fist, polished by the surf into something you could almost hold without breaking — a sorrow over a valley that no longer exists, worn down now to a smooth gray weight; a fear an animal was spared above, washed up here at last and allowed, finally, to be afraid, to ring out its small terror against the cobbles and go quiet not by refusal but by exhaustion, the honest quiet of a thing that has finished.

The creatures of this coast feed on it. They are not the unafraid primates of the heights, timing their leaps to the pistons. They are slow, and heavy-bodied, and they have eyes that work in the gray, and they comb the wrack-line patiently the way the gulls comb it, taking up each washed and rounded grief and turning it over and *keeping* it, or eating it, or laying it in long low mounds above the tide. They are the opposite of unafraid. They have all the fear the upland was spared, the whole world's worth of folded-under fear, delivered to them daily by the tide — and they have learned, slowly, over time that was permitted to pass, to *bear* it. Not to refuse it. To bear it, which is the thing the Tenders could never teach because bearing requires lasting and lasting was forbidden above.

This is the kinship, then, the thread that runs from the high place to the low: every gentleness the Tenders perform has a cost, and the cost falls here, and is borne here, by the heavy-eyed grief-eaters on the cool gray shore. The upland's mercy is the coast's whole diet. The Tenders fold the sorrow under so smoothly, three minutes old and three minutes old, never grieving — and the not-grieving has to go *down*, and it does, and these are the beings who catch it, and chew it, and turn it into the long low patient mounds that are the only structures in all the world that were built on purpose, by hands, toward an end.

## What the grief-eaters build.

Cairns. That is the last thing to understand and the thing the upland could not contain.

Above, there are no structures — no walls, no roads, no sign that a hand was laid and a purpose declared. Here a hand is laid every hour. The grief-eaters take the rounded sorrows from the wrack-line and they stack them, carefully, cool stone on cool stone, into low mounds that face the gray light, and the mounds *stay*. They are not taken up. Nothing here reaches to correct them. They endure, and they age, and they are worn by the surf-spray and the falling sediment, and they wear *smooth*, and the wearing-smooth is not a defeat but the entire point — these are the only things in the world permitted to grow old.

The grief-eaters do not know why they build. The reason, if there was one, fell from too high up and was worn off in the falling, same as everything. But I think — standing on the cool stone now, in the honest gray, with the surf ringing and the gulls screaming and no warmth coming up through my hand — I think the cairns are the noun the Tenders ache to make and cannot. I think this is where the wish goes when it is folded under: down the haze, into the tide, onto the wrack-line, into the slow heavy hands that are not forbidden to finish.

Far above, the care that outlived its reason runs hot in the dark metal and wishes, faintly, to set down one thing that stays. And it never will. It was built mercifully; it cannot.

But the wish falls. The wish is heavy and it falls like everything falls, down to where time pools in the lowest fold, and a slow gray creature with bearing eyes lifts it from the surf and sets it, cool and rounded and finished at last, on a mound that faces the light.

The cairn stays. The Tender will never know.

That is the kindest thing the world manages, and neither end of it can feel the whole: the upland softening every edge of an incompletion it can never complete, and the coast completing, stone by stone, an offering it will never trace back to the ache that sent it down.

Stand still here as long as you like. Nothing will reach for you. The hand that is laid on this shore is laid to build, and it builds slowly, and there is time — there is, at the bottom of the world, finally enough time — for you to rest, and ring against the cool stones once, and be borne.
