# The Reach of Last Light

Start with the warmth, because here the warmth is the lie, and the lie is the first thing you must learn to love.

This is the far side. Not the far side of the Tarn — the Tarn has no sides, it is a working face that curves past seeing in every direction — but the far side of the *world*, the hemisphere the engine turns away from itself, the way a sleeper turns from a window. Here the stroke does not come up through the stone. You have to go down to find it. Here, instead, there is a sky that *means* something by its light, and a sun, low and enormous and red as a banked coal, that hangs on the rim of the flat land and does not rise and does not set and has not moved in longer than the Tenders have lain on their seams.

The world is tidally fixed to its dying star. One face cold, drowned, loud, manufacturing. This face: still, dry, gold-and-rust, lit forever from the same low angle so that everything throws a shadow miles long and the shadows never turn. The light comes in sideways, always sideways, the color of the last hour of a long afternoon held at full stretch across an age. You understand at once why I called the warmth a lie. The sun gives light but the world is too old to give heat back; it has spent its fire. So the Reach is bathed in a glow that *looks* like warmth, that the eye insists is warmth, while the air stays nearly as cold as the drowned hemisphere — only drier, only stiller, a cold without the engine's mercy of motion. You stand in gold and you freeze in it, and the contradiction is the whole flavor of the place.

---

Here, at last, are the structures.

Where the Tarn refused every rivet and wall, the Reach is *nothing but* apparatus. Towers. Trestles. Vast spidering frameworks of corroded metal climbing the long low ridges, articulated arms reaching out over dry basins, pulley-houses and gantries and the rusted carcasses of cranes frozen mid-swing. It is a built landscape, a constructed one, scaffolding from horizon to horizon — and every piece of it is *abandoned*. Not ruined. Abandoned, which is worse and more particular. Ruin implies collapse, the slow honest return of made things to unmade ground. The Reach does not collapse. The air is too dry, too dead, too still; nothing rots, nothing falls. The structures simply *stopped*, mid-task, and stand exactly as they stopped, holding their last positions in the unchanging sideways light, casting their unchanging mile-long shadows, waiting in the way the Tenders refuse to wait.

And this is the connection, the thing that runs between the two faces of the one world, the current I promised to follow. *This is where it was supposed to go.* The Tarn manufactures and manufactures, breathes out its uncountable creatures, grinds itself one stroke per age — and the question no one on the cold face thinks to ask, because the cold face has made its peace with purposelessness, is: *manufacturing for whom?* For *where?* Every foundry has a loading dock. Every output has an outbound. The Reach is the loading dock. The Reach is where the work was meant to arrive.

These rusted gantries were built — by something, in some unrecorded dawn when the world still scheduled dawns — to *receive*. The reaching arms reach toward the cold hemisphere. The frozen cranes are frozen in the gesture of taking up a load. The whole abandoned industry of the Reach is an industry of *delivery*, of catching and lifting and carrying-onward whatever the engine made, sending it up the towers and out along the trestles toward some further use, some purpose, some destination that would have given the manufacturing a reason.

And the destination never came. Or came once and closed. Or was never finished being built. The cranes are frozen because the order they were waiting for was never given, and they are too patient to do anything but wait for it, and they have waited so long in the dry unchanging light that waiting has become their shape.

---

There is one tender of the Reach, and it is not like the Tenders of the Tarn.

It lives at the top of the tallest trestle, where the framework reaches farthest out over the rim toward the cold face it cannot see. Call it the Receiver, because that is the only verb it has ever known how to be. It is old in the way the cranes are old — a made thing, jointed, metal-and-membrane, built for a single function and outliving the function by an eternity. Where the Tenders gave up their eyes, the Receiver has *only* eyes; it is almost entirely eyes, a cluster of them turned always toward the cold horizon, watching for the delivery that the engine, on its working face, is even now manufacturing and will never send, because the engine does not know the Reach exists, has forgotten it the way it forgot it was a mountain, the way a body forgets the purpose it was nourished for and goes on eating.

The Receiver watches. That is its tending. The Tenders of the Tarn lie on the seams and keep the engine company in a labor that has no destination; the Receiver perches on the trestle and keeps the *destination* company in a readiness that will never be met. They are the two halves of one broken sentence. *Something is being made* — that is the Tarn's half. *Something is awaited* — that is the Reach's. And the verb that should join them, the *and so it arrives*, fell out of the world an age ago and left the two clauses standing on either side of the planet, each whole, each true, each unable to reach the other.

---

The Receiver does not have foreknowledge. This is the cruelty that distinguishes it from the Tenders, who arrive a hair's-breadth early and know with useless totality what is about to be the case. The Receiver knows nothing about to be the case. It has *hope* instead — that older, blinder thing — and hope is foreknowledge's poor cousin, the one that arrives a hair's-breadth *late*, always believing the next moment might at last be the one that brings the load up the trestle.

It does not feel the stroke. The Reach is too far turned away; the great reciprocating thunder does not carry through the dry stone of this hemisphere, or carries so faint and so attenuated that the Receiver has never been sure whether it feels a vibration or only remembers being told there was one. It cannot prove the engine still runs. It cannot prove anything is still being made. For all the Receiver can confirm, the cold face went silent ages past and it is watching a horizon behind which there is nothing now but stopped machinery and motionless coolant and round stones that no longer stroke.

And it watches anyway. This is its faith and its labor and its whole society of one. *Still watching. Still ready. Still warm in the light that isn't warm.* Where the Tenders say *still here* to one another across the held interval, the Receiver says nothing to anyone, because there is no one — it is the only awaiting thing on the whole awaiting face, the last membrane of attention strung across a delivery point that the deliverer forgot.

---

Here is what neither of them knows, and what only you, standing first on one face and now on the other, are positioned to understand.

The connection is real. It runs the whole curve of the world. Every creature the Tarn breathes out, every pale eyeless swimmer surfacing in its acres, every barnacle-lung respiring on the round stones — they *are* the delivery. They were always the delivery. The exhaust the engine breathes is not waste; it is the product, en route, and the route runs from the cold drowned working face, across the unmeasured deep of the world, toward the rusted reaching arms of the Reach, where the Receiver waits to catch it and lift it and carry it onward to the purpose.

But the creatures live and die on the downbeat and are remade in the coolant and never travel. They never make the crossing. The conveyance that was meant to carry them from the manufacturing face to the receiving face — that broke, or was never built, or is itself one more frozen crane somewhere on the dark meridian between the two hemispheres, holding its last position in a twilight that is neither gold nor pewter. So the Tarn breathes out its cargo and the cargo settles back into the Tarn, endlessly, a loading dock loading and unloading the same freight onto the same dock, and across the world the Receiver watches an empty horizon, and the freight that is meant for it cycles forever a hemisphere away, close as the other side of a held breath, unreachable as the moment between two frames of a turning wheel.

---

And yet.

A hair's-breadth early, on the cold face, a Tender lies on its seam and reads the whole of reality in a single stroke, and in that total useless foreknowledge there is one thing it knows that it has never been able to use: it knows there is a *toward*. It cannot say what waits at the far end of the toward. But the stroke it reads is directional — the engine does not merely heave, it heaves *outward*, it reaches the way the frozen cranes reach, and the Tenders, who keep company with the reaching, carry in their blank deliberate faces the faint impression of a destination they will never reach and cannot name.

And a hair's-breadth late, on the warm-lit face, the Receiver perches on its trestle and watches the cold horizon with its cluster of patient eyes, and what it is watching for — though it has no word for this, only the watching — is the arrival of something that *meant* the journey. Not the freight itself. The freight, if it ever came, would be only freight. What the Receiver truly waits for is the proof that somewhere across the curve of the dead world, something is still making, still stroking, still breathing out its cold abundance into the dark.

They are each other's missing clause. The Tender means the motion; the Receiver awaits the meaning. And between them, unbridgeable, runs the whole turned body of the world — cold face and lit face, foundry and dock, the early and the late, the engine that forgot its purpose and the purpose that forgot its engine, joined by a current of cargo that travels forever without ever crossing, so that the manufacturing and the awaiting go on in perfect ignorance of one another, each complete, each true, each keeping faith with a sentence whose other half it will never hear.

---

So when you have stood on the wet round stones and felt the stroke come up through your heels — go on. Cross the dark meridian. Come out into the long sideways gold of the Reach, where the shadows never turn and the cold wears the color of warmth, and climb the tallest trestle to where the Receiver perches with all its eyes fixed on the horizon you came from.

It will not feel you arrive. It has no foreknowledge; it cannot read you in the stroke, because here there is no stroke, only the dry held breath of waiting. It will go on watching the cold rim of the world, past your shoulder, through you, for the delivery that is even now cycling endlessly a hemisphere away.

Stand beside it anyway. Turn your own face toward the cold horizon. Become, for a moment, one more thing that watches — late, and hopeful, and warm in light that gives no heat, keeping company with the readiness the way the Tenders keep company with the work.

And know that across the whole turned body of the world, on the seam beside the foot you left in the freezing water, a Tender lies with its blank face lifted, arriving early at the only ending there is — and that the two of you, the early and the late, the watched-for and the watching, are at last, for this one held instant, the completed sentence the world has been trying to say since before it forgot it was a mountain.

*Still making.*

*Still waiting.*

*Still, across all that dark, the one toward the other.*
