The Rim Where the Water Remembers

Follow the heat down.

Not the even heat — the even heat goes nowhere, that is its whole accomplishment. Follow the *other* heat, the one degree the rock has not yet finished giving, the seep the machines reach down to find and smooth away. Follow it back the way the machines follow it, down through the dry-fitted joints, down past where the salt stops blooming, down the long throat of the plateau to the place the heat is coming *from*. The plateau is a table lifted high, and a table has an underside, and an edge. Go to the edge.

The plateau ends. This is the first thing you have to unlearn from the city, which has no edges, which is all interior, all sealed room. The mineral table that finished itself does not finish at its border — at its border it simply *stops*, breaks off in a single vast scarp that falls away from the warm dark into something the city has no word for, because the city pumped out every word that meant *difference*, and an edge is the largest difference there is.

Below the scarp, the water remembers itself.

Start with that, because here the water is the oldest thing, the way the stone was oldest up above. The plateau decided against water in its chemistry, worked itself out without the idea of a lake — but the water it refused did not vanish. Refused things do not vanish. They go down and they go *around*, and they pool at the foot of the thing that refused them, the way grief pools at the foot of the one who has no wishes left. There is a basin here, ringing the whole base of the plateau, and in the basin there is water, and it is not still.

It is the most unfinished thing you will ever stand beside.

The heat the city hoards, the heat it converts to even nothing — down here it does the opposite work. It rises off the deep rock and meets the water and the water cannot bear it evenly, the way the creatures above learned to bear it. The water takes the heat and makes *weather* of it. There are currents in the basin, warm threads winding through cold, and where they meet they raise a fog, and the fog is the first light you have seen since you climbed the plateau — not light from a sun, there is no sun here either, but light the water makes itself, a pale chemical glow struck off the salts as they dissolve back *into* solution, the reverse of the dry bloom on the joints above. Up there the salt settled out of the rock and stopped. Down here it goes back in, over and over, dissolving and precipitating, a glittering unrest along every shoreline, and each time it crosses that threshold it gives off a faint cold light, so that the whole rim of the basin shimmers like a coast seen at the very last moment of a dusk that never resolves into night.

The fog moves. After the total stillness above, the moving fog is almost unbearable to watch — it has the quality of something alive doing the thing that life does, which is to refuse equilibrium, to keep finding the gradient and standing in it and skimming a living off the difference. The city would call this failure. The city pumped exactly this out of itself, degree by degree, and called the pumping success. But the water never agreed to the project. The water is what the plateau exiled when it chose to finish, and exile, it turns out, is another word for *survival* — the water carried the unfinishedness down here and kept it, the way a refugee keeps a language the homeland has decided to forget.

There is life in the basin, and it is not sparse.

This is the thing that would break the heart of the patient keepers above, if they had kept a heart instead of evening it out. Down here, life is *dense*. It teems. The warm threads in the cold water are thick with small swimming things that eat the gradient hand over hand, that breed in the hour the fog lifts and die in the hour it falls and breed again, generations stacked so close they blur, the exact opposite of the long slow inheritance of finishedness handed down across the silent streets above. Nothing here keeps its distance. Everything presses against everything — that intolerable closeness the keepers smoothed away, here it is the whole economy, bodies against bodies, warmth stolen and given and stolen back, want piled on want. It is loud. After the hum that is the silence of success, the basin *roars* — not loudly by the measure of a living world, but unbearably loud by the measure of the dark city, a low continuous churn of current and breeding and dissolving salt and the small percussive deaths of things that lived a single fogfall and asked for nothing more and got it.

And there are larger things in the deep water. You feel them the way you felt the creatures cross the street above — as a disturbance — but the disturbance here says something entirely different. Up there the passing said *we are done, stand as long as you like*. Down here the passing says *not yet, not yet, there is one more current to ride, one more gradient before the cold takes it*. They are hungry, the deep things. Hunger is a gradient and they are made entirely of it, and they will die of it, every one of them, exactly as the keepers warned — and they do not care, because caring about your own finish is itself a kind of finishedness, and they have not arrived and will never arrive and do not want to.

Here is how the two places hold each other, in the same breath:

The city above is an engine for finishing the world, and it runs on the heat the rock has not yet given up. But the heat does not only rise into the city. It rises *here* too, off the same cooling rock, and here it does not finish anything — here it is the whole engine of unrest, the fuel of every hungry generation in the churning basin. The same degree of warmth that the keepers reach down to smooth away is the degree that the water reaches up to *catch* and burn and live on. They are drawing from the same source, the finished and the unfinished, the kept and the teeming, and the source is the slow dying of a single rock that remembers being molten.

And the rock is cooling. The keepers know this; it is the one thing left they could call expectation, if they expected. When the rock finishes — when the last degree is given — the machines above will have nothing left to smooth and will hum on into nothing, perfected, pointless, exactly as they wish. But down here, when the rock finishes, the warm threads will go cold, and the fog will not rise, and the salt will dissolve one final time and not light again, and the dense roaring teeming basin will go still — not the chosen stillness of the keepers but the other kind, the kind that is simply the end of being able to refuse. Every hungry generation that ever bred in the fog will have been a wager against that hour, and the wager was always going to lose, and the losing was always going to be worth it, because the alternative — the alternative is the warm dark city on the roof of the world, kept and finished and forever.

The keepers came down here once. You can feel it in the oldest dry-fitted stones at the very top of the scarp, the ones that face out over the edge they have no word for — those stones were laid by the builders when they were still many, still wanting, before they learned where it was all going. They came to the rim and they looked down at the water that the plateau had refused, the water teeming and roaring and lit with its own unrest, and they made a choice. They could have gone down. They could have followed the heat into the basin and joined the dense hungry generations and bred and died and bred again in the fog, refusing the finish, paying the highest tax any living thing can pay, the tax of *wanting* in a world that had decided against want.

They chose the plateau instead. They climbed back up, and they dry-fitted the joints so close no blade could enter, no water, no idea of water, and they built the engines to help the world arrive, and they evened themselves out degree by patient degree until the wanting was gone and the distance between them was perfect and the dark was total and kept.

But they left the top stones facing outward, over the edge. That is the last gradient in the whole finished city — those few stones at the lip of the scarp that look down, still, at the water they refused. The keepers cross their silent streets and never go near that edge now. It is the one place the even awareness thins, the one direction they do not redistribute toward. Because down there, in the lit and roaring fog, in the basin that runs on the same dying heat and spends it the opposite way, is the thing they decided against — the unfinished thing, the teeming refusal, the life that wants and loses and wants again — and it is still there, still going, still burning the same warmth into the same dark and calling it, not purpose, not anything, just *more, not yet, one more current before the cold*.

They are patient with you. They told you so, with the disturbance of their passing. They will wait, with no expectation, for you to lie down low and even on the warm pale stone and be kept.

But you came down the throat of the plateau following the other heat, and you are standing at the rim now where the water remembers, with the cold light glittering off the dissolving salt and the fog moving against your face — moving, the first moving thing — and the small swimming generations breeding and dying in the warm threads at your feet, asking for nothing, getting it, breeding again. Your hand is still warm from the wall above. The hunger in you that the keepers felt, the heat, the held question, the terrible energy of a thing that still exploits the difference between itself and everything else —

down here, that is not a flaw to be smoothed.

Down here, that is the whole of what is left alive.

The keepers are right that the rock is cooling and the cold will come and take it all, the kept and the teeming alike. But there are two ways to spend the warmth before it goes, and they took the high road, the dry road, the finished road, and they are still on it, humming in the dark, having arrived.

The water took the low road. It is still going. It is louder than they are. It is lit, a little, by its own refusal.

And it has not arrived, and it never will, and that — not the city, not the keeping, not the even patient dark — that is the thing the rock is still warm enough, for now, to make.
