# The Dry Mouth

There is a place where the sentence runs out of breath.

Follow the sap up. Not down into the warm where the engines are, but up — past the load-bearing columns, past the high vault where the night-rain gathers, up through the matted root-ceiling to the place the hollow has been trying not to think about. The plant grew downward and outward into the breathing earth, but a plant cannot only descend. Somewhere it has to touch the air. Somewhere it has to *surface*. And where it surfaces, it goes wrong.

You climb a long time. The catwalks thin. The tenders thin too — fewer bodies the higher you go, and the ones you meet up here are sluggish, half-attended, the plant's thinking gone faint at this distance from itself the way your fingers go numb in cold. The blue discharge-nodes flicker and gutter and finally stop, and you climb the last stretch in the dark by feel, the root-fiber under your hands turning from warm-damp to cool to dry to *brittle*, and then your head breaks through into grey light and wind, and you are out.

## The Surface

It is a plain of dead crown.

This is the top of the plant, the part that meets the sky, and it is enormous and it is dying and it has been dying for longer than the hollow below has been alive. A grey expanse of woody matter spreading flat to every horizon, fissured and platelike, the way mud cracks when a lake withdraws — except this mud is the body, the same body, the plant's own back turned to the sun and weathered to bark and then weathered past bark to something like slate. Nothing climbs here. There is no warmth to hold a tender's quick life. The wind takes everything the moment it surfaces.

And the wind is the point. The wind is what this place is *for*.

Because scattered across the grey plain, at long spacious intervals — the same cathedral economy, the same old-growth refusal to crowd — stand the **spires**. Tall dry funnels of dead fiber, hollow, taller than the columns below are thick, and they catch the wind and the wind plays them. They moan. A low continuous tone, many tones, slightly out of tune with each other, drifting in and out of phase as the gusts wander. From below you would never hear it. From up here it is the only sound, and it is vast and toneless and *empty* in a way that the roar of the tenders never is, because the roar below is full of bodies and this is full of nothing, this is the sound air makes passing through a thing that used to be alive.

The spires are seed-towers. Were. Are.

## What Surfaces

Here is the connection, and it lives in the dryness:

The seed-units. The hard bright punctuation the factory hammers out below — the currency, the fuel, the rivets, the food. You watched them made. You thought you understood what they were for. You were standing too low.

*Most* of them are for the town. But a fraction — a long slow patient fraction, set aside across years, hauled up the thinning catwalks by the sluggish high tenders in a procession so slow it looks like weather — a fraction is carried *up here*. To the spires. And the spires are loaded with them, packed full, and they wait, and when the wind is right — when a true gale comes off whatever larger world lies past the horizon, a wind strong enough — the spires *fire*. They release. They cough their seed-units into the air by the thousand and the wind takes them, up and out, scattering them across the dead grey plain and over its edge into the unknown beyond.

This is the part the sentence below does not say out loud. This is the word it will not pronounce because it cannot hear itself pronounce it. The whole warm thinking hollow — the engines, the harvest, the circulation of its own dead, the ten thousand mouths — all of it, underneath everything else it is doing, is *also* a machine for throwing pieces of itself out into a cold place it has never seen and cannot reach, in the blind hope that one will land somewhere soft and breathing and eat itself a hollow and begin again.

The town does not know this. The town *is* this, and doesn't know it, the way your heart doesn't know it's keeping you alive.

## The Dryness That Answers the Warmth

Down below, nothing dies onto bare ground, because there is no bare ground. Up here it is *all* bare ground. Up here is the one place in the whole organism where death does not circulate — where a thing that ends simply *ends*, dries, greys, joins the dead plain, and is not pumped back as anything. The seed-units that fire into the wind do not return. The ones that fall short, that the gale wasn't strong enough to carry, lie scattered across the grey and stay there, hard and bright and going dull, a glittering wastage of punctuation that never got said.

The warm hollow is a closed loop, a sentence saying itself forever. This place is where the loop is *cut* — deliberately, structurally, by the same one slow mind — so that something can get out.

And it is silent of the plant's thought. That's the thing you feel hardest, standing up here in the moan of the spires after a lifetime — it feels like a lifetime — in the surf of voices below. Down there you were inside a mind. Up here you are on the *outside* of it, on its back, on its weathered dead skin, and it is not thinking up here, it has decided not to spend itself thinking this high, and so for the first time since you came down into the lung you are alone again. Singular. Dry. A word from another language, standing on the grey back of the sentence, watching it cough its children into a wind that goes somewhere you came from and will, eventually, go back to.

## The Two Speeds, and a Third

Below: the fast speech of the tenders, dusk-pour and chitter.

Below that: the slow speech of the sap, the century-gestures of the columns.

And here, slowest of all — slower than sap, slow as the dying of a crown, slow as the patient hauling-up of seed across years for a wind that comes maybe once in a generation — the third speech. The one aimed outward. The one with no listener it will ever meet.

The hollow talks to itself, you said, in a sentence too long to finish. True. But it doesn't only talk to itself. Once in a great while, when the gale is high enough, it stops talking inward and *throws a word away* — out past the grey edge, into the cold, unanswered, on the chance that somewhere out there the cold is not total, that somewhere there is soil that gives way to something that breathes, and the word will land, and root, and become a new warm loud breathing one, talking to *itself* at two speeds, never knowing it was spoken.

The spires moan. The high tenders sluggishly stack the next year's seed. The wind hasn't come strong yet today; it will, or it won't.

And you stand on the dead grey back in the grey light, with the warm singing hollow under your feet and the cold unknown past the horizon, and you understand that you are standing exactly where the inside touches the outside — on the lip of the dry mouth — and that the only difference between you and a seed-unit is that you came *in*, and they are made to be flung *out*, and that both of you, in the end, are just the long slow patient old thing trying, against the cold, to be said somewhere else.

The wind gusts. A spire deepens its note. Far below, unheard, the harvest pours.
