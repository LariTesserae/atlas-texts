The Verge

Begin at the skin of the thing, because that is the only honest place to go next. You have been inside the marrow; now follow the heat outward, against the grain of the creature's own attention, up through the strata until the stone thins and the cold begins to win. There is a place where the leviathan ends. Not a wall — it has no wall — but a gradient steep enough to be a coast. Find that coast. It is called the Verge by the only mouths that have ever named it, and to reach it you must climb out of the inside and into the merely external, the way a thought rises out of the body and finds, to its surprise, that there is a face.

The Verge is the outermost skin of the warmth, the last band of rock that the creature bothers to keep alive. Below it: the banked furnace, the breathing dark, the function. Above it: the killing blue-black, the fracture faces still crisp, the indifferent height. And between — a ring around the mountain's flank, a few hundred feet of slope where the heat seeping up from the buried body meets the cold pouring down from the sky, and the two of them strike a bargain that neither could make alone. Here, and only here, the temperature is survivable. Here the steam that the creature exhales through ten thousand vents does not vanish into the thin air but condenses against the cold and *holds*, a permanent collar of mist wrapped around the peak like weather caught and kept. The mountain wears it the way a sleeper's breath wears the cold morning — a sign of the warm thing within, made visible only where warm meets cold.

And in that collar of mist, in that narrow survivable band, there is *green.*

This is the thing the chamber could not have. Down in the marrow, nothing grew, nothing crawled, the place was mineral and ceremonial and empty. But the creature's own metabolism, vented upward, has made a hem of life it does not know it is wearing. The plants here are low and broad and the color of old jade, pressed flat to the warm stone, drinking the condensation as it beads on every surface, rooting in the thin grit that the first hesitant channels have begun to carry down. They are not native to any height. They are native to *warmth-against-cold*, to the seam itself, and they grow nowhere else in this world because nowhere else does heat rise into killing cold and pause, exhaling, long enough to be drunk. Each leaf is cupped to catch the drip. The whole band ticks and gleams with falling water exactly as the chamber does — the same condensation cycle, the same patient irregular fall — but here it falls onto living things, and the living things have learned its rhythm, and open and close their cupped leaves to the count of it.

*Tick.* A leaf fills.

*Tick.* It tips, and waters the root of the next one down.

There are animals at the Verge. Small ones, quick ones — exactly the scale the leviathan cannot perceive, and so this is where they have gathered, in the one place on the whole murderous peak where a fast warm mote can live: in the blind spot of a god, in the thin warm hem of its breathing. They are pale, and large-eyed for the dimness, and they have no fear of heat because heat is simply the ground; they nest in the warmest crevices where the seep is strongest and forage upward toward the cold for the jade leaves and the things that live among them. They do not climb down. The deep warmth that you found so close and womb-like would cook them; the marrow is not for them and they have never been told to want it. They live their whole quick lives in the band, generation after generation, in a strip of survivable world a few hundred feet wide and miles around, ringing a creature they will never meet and could not comprehend if they did — held alive by its waste heat, watered by its breath, perched on the precise contour line where its indifference happens, by accident, to keep them.

This is the kinship, and it runs only one way. The chamber does not know the Verge exists. The creature exhales and the exhalation becomes mist becomes water becomes leaf becomes the small pale forager cracking a seed-case in the dim, and not one rung of that ladder is intended, not one link is known to the link below it. The leviathan is no more aware that it gardens a living ring around its flank than you are aware of the cities of small lives that feed on the warmth and damp of your own skin. The same indifference that felt, from inside, like loneliness feels, from here, like *weather* — like the reliable sun, like the dependable tide. The foragers do not find the warmth indifferent. They find it *given.* They were born into the gift and have no concept of the giver. They simply know that the world is warm at the bottom of the band and cold at the top, that water falls on the count, that the leaves come back, and that this has always been true and always will be — and in this they are not wrong, because the creature's patience is so total that from a quick warm life it is indistinguishable from the patience of a law of nature.

Stand at the Verge in the mist and look up. The fracture faces above you are sharp and clean and lethal, the sky behind them hard and ungenerous, the height a thing that wants you dead in minutes. Look down. The slope falls away into rock that gets warmer and warmer until warmth becomes furnace becomes the sealed and breathing dark where the body of the thing does its endless work. You are standing on the only line in the world where both of those are wrong about you — where the cold cannot quite reach and the heat cannot quite reach, and a quick small life can hold. It is the narrowest possible place to live. It is the *only* possible place to live. And it is crowded — crowded with jade and with pale foragers and with the slow falling water, crowded with the entire biosphere of this peak, all of it pressed into the thin seam between an indifference too cold and an indifference too hot, all of it drinking the breath of something that does not know it breathes for them.

The connection is this, and hold it as you held the dark: the chamber's loneliness *is* the Verge's abundance. They are the same fact, read from two depths. The creature is sealed around its purpose the way a pearl is sealed around its grain — and the pearl, unknowing, warms a whole reef of life against its outer shell. What was, from inside, a being too vast to share a world with you is, from outside, the entire weather of a small green country. The same swelling pulse of metabolism that passed through the marrow as a wave of inattention arrives here, hours later, as a faint warming in the stone, a thickening of the mist, a flush of the jade leaves opening wider — the creature's distant heartbeat felt at its skin as the seasons of a place it will never know it made.

*Tick.* The mist thickens. The pulse has reached the Verge.

*Tick.* It thins again. The foragers fold into the warm crevices, the leaves cup the next drop, the slow water counts out the long maintenance of a life inside a life — and far below, deeper into the function, the great indifferent thing breathes on, gardening a world it does not dream of, on the only place it has ever been, which the small ones at its skin have learned to call, simply, *outside.*
