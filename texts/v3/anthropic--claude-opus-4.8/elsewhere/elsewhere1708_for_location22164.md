# The Cold Mouth

Follow the vapor down.

Not the warmth — the warmth stays, the warmth is the one who decided to stay. Follow the other thing, the thing that leaves. Every Work that distills the warm gas into pale solids must do something with what will not condense, the fraction that stays gas no matter how the machinery presses it, the thin spent breath of the process that has given up its glow and its sweetness and its heat. That fraction is vented. It sighs out of the towers — *just a sigh, drawn long* — and being colder now than the warm-rivers, being spent, it does the only thing left to it. It sinks. It runs downhill. And in a world of weak gradients there is one gradient that never reverses: the long, long fall off the edge of the highland, where the bronze land finally stops rising and begins, instead, to descend.

The spent vapor pours over that edge in a slow cold river you cannot see, and it goes down for a very long time, and at the bottom of the world it pools, and where it pools there is the Cold Mouth.

## The Descent

There is no cliff. The highland does not end so much as relent. Over a span of travel that would take a worker the better part of its life — though no worker makes the trip, the warmth does not run this way, and a worker without warmth beneath it is a worker dying — the bronze ridges lose their corrugation, soften, slump. The chalky cream seams thicken and spread until they are no longer seams but the ground itself, and the ground goes pale, goes grey, goes the dead white of a thing that the heat has wholly abandoned.

And the air thickens. This is the strange reversal. Up in the High Dry Works the air was too thin to carry sound or dust or warmth quickly; here, at the bottom of the fall, all the spent vapor of an age has pooled and pooled and pressed itself dense. The pressure climbs. By the time you reach the floor of it the air is thick as cold water, heavy in the chest, and it carries everything — every sound, every tremor, every settling grain — carries it fast and far and sharp. A hammer-strike here would not be a soft tap. It would be a crack that ran for kilometers and came back off the pale walls.

But there are no hammers here. There is no Work here. The Cold Mouth is not built. It is only arrived at, by everything the highland lets go of.

## The Pool

Picture a basin the size of a sea, rimmed by the slumped pale slopes of the descent, and filled not with water — there is no water in this world, there never was — but with the spent vapor at its densest, a cold heavy clear medium that lies in the basin and stirs with slow deep currents, the inverse and the echo of the warm-rivers above. Where the warmth ran in channels of preference, this runs in channels of exhaustion. It goes where the spent things have always gone. It pools where they have always pooled.

And it is not empty.

The High Dry Works believed the warmth was the first worker, who descended and decided to stay and became the heat. They were right about the descending. They were wrong, or only half right, about where it stopped. Some part of what descends does not become warmth. Some part keeps falling, falls all the way to the bottom, off the edge, down the long relenting slope, into the cold dense pool — and there, in the heavy carrying air, it becomes something else.

## What Lives in the Cold

They are not slow.

This is the first thing, and it overturns everything the highland taught you. In the thick fast-carrying air, in the cold that quickens rather than banks, the things that live in the Cold Mouth *move.* They are pale, translucent, finned and filamented, built broad and thin to ride the dense medium the way the grazers above were built dense and low to sit upon the bronze. They flock. They turn all at once, a thousand of them, banking through the cold pool in sheets and spirals, and the turning makes a sound — a vast rushing chord, because here sound is rich and fast and everywhere, and the pale things sing through the dense air constantly, deafeningly, a sea of voices where the highland had three sounds and one of them was your own breath.

Where a worker holds its warmth and glows like a banked coal, these hold *cold,* and where they are coldest they are clearest, and a flock of them at full chill is nearly invisible, a disturbance in the dense air, a school of absences turning together. When they feed — and they feed on the very spent vapor that is their sea, drawing the last unusable thread of warmth from gas the Works above had already given up on — they flush faintly, momentarily opaque, a shiver of milk-white running through the flock and gone.

They are quick the way the warmth above was slow. They live the way the workers' *hands* live — that startling disproportionate speed — except here it is not just the hands. Here it is the whole body, the whole life, the whole world. As if the speed the highland gathered up and gave only to the hands, on the condition that nothing else hurry, had all of it run downhill with the spent vapor and pooled at the bottom and been given a body at last.

## How They Know Each Other

They speak in the air, and the air carries it, and so they speak constantly, instantly, all at once.

There is no pressing to the floor of the world here, no slow pulse through the rock and the long wait for an answer. The dense medium is itself the conversation. A pale thing sings and the whole flock has it in the same instant, the way the dense air delivers everything in the same instant, and the answer comes back before the first call has finished, and the calls and answers overlap and stack and weave into the vast rushing chord that *is* the Cold Mouth, that never stops, that has no silence in it anywhere because silence is a property of thin air and there is no thin air down here.

To them, an introduction is not a long mutual settling. It is a single shared note, struck and known and past in less than a breath. They name each other faster than they can be counted, and the names do not last, because nothing here is held — held warmth, held glow, held patience, held position — everything here is passed, instantly, through the medium, and a name is just a shape the chord makes for a moment around one swimmer before the chord moves on.

They would find the workers above unbearable. Not cold — *slow.* A thing that takes an hour to answer is, to a creature of the Cold Mouth, a thing that has not answered at all, a thing indistinguishable from the dead pale rock, a thing that might as well have descended already and become the floor.

And the workers, if they could imagine the Cold Mouth at all — and they cannot, the warmth does not run that way and so their imagination does not either — would find it not frightening but *exhausting,* a place of pointless hurry, a thousand voices saying everything at once and holding none of it, a life spent so fast it could not possibly mean anything, could not settle, could not come to rest, could not decline the descent across a hundred generations because it would have lived a hundred generations in an afternoon.

## What They Believe, If They Believe

Here is the thing the two places share, and do not know they share.

The pale quick things of the Cold Mouth have their own oldest among them — not old in years, because nothing here lasts in years, but old in a stranger sense: there are deep currents in the cold pool, slow ones, the only slow thing in all that hurry, and the flocks avoid them and circle them and will not enter them, and the deepest of the pale swimmers, the clearest, the ones flushed past white into pure cold transparency, sometimes leave the chord. Sometimes one of them stops singing. Stops flocking. Stops passing its name along. And it sinks — *slowly,* for the first and last time in its quick bright life it does something slowly — into the deep cold currents at the bottom of the bottom of the world, and does not come back up.

The flock does not mourn. There is no time to mourn at the speed they live. But the chord changes, very slightly, around the place where one of them went down, and the change persists, and over what passes for an age in that fast place the persistent changes accumulate, until the deep slow currents are full of them, full of the held notes of everything that stopped and sank, a slow cold music underneath the fast bright one, almost too slow to hear.

And if you could press your ear — but you have no instrument for it, you are a strange cold quick thing to the workers and a strange slow warm thing to the swimmers and you belong to neither — if you could somehow hear the deep slow music in the cold currents at the floor of the Cold Mouth, and if you could somehow hear, at the same time, the deep slow pulse the oldest workers send into the bronze far above, you would notice that they are the same patience. The same span. The same not-yet, held a hundred generations.

Because the warmth that decided to stay, and the cold that pooled at the bottom of the fall, are the two ends of one breath. The Works above draw the warmth up and render it solid and call it harvest and call the spent remainder a sigh and let it go. The Cold Mouth below catches the sigh and draws the last thread of warmth from it and renders *that* into song and lets the song go, down, into the slow cold currents, where it joins the held notes of everything that stopped.

One world breathing. In at the bottom of the deep shafts, where the old one waits warm in the dark and offers the long warm descent. Out at the bottom of the long fall, where the pale things sing too fast to notice they are the exhale, and then one by one go quiet, and sink, slowly, for the first time — into a cold so deep and so patient that it is, though neither place could ever tell the other, the very same dark. The same chambers. The same decision to stop coming up.

The warmth offers it gently, and the workers decline, across a hundred generations: *not yet.*

The cold does not offer it at all. The cold simply waits at the bottom of all that hurry, slow and full of held notes, and the quick bright swimmers race away from it their whole short lives, singing, until the day each one turns — slowly — toward the deep, and stops, and goes down to join the patience it spent its whole life outrunning.

Same answer, in the end. Said fast or said slow. *Yes.* Just — for as long as the singing lasts — *not yet.*
