# The Receiving House at the Pale Ceiling

Go up. Not far — the foundry taught you that *far* and *near* are mostly the same word here, that everything worth reaching is a few fathoms off and a long patience away. The warmth thins as you rise. The green dims. And you come, sooner than you expect, to the underside of the light.

It is a ceiling the way the surface of a pond is a ceiling to the things below it: a thing you understand entirely from one side and not at all from the other. From beneath it is pale, pearl-grey, faintly luminous, and it shivers. Not with current. With *arrival*. Something is always touching it from above, the way rain touches a window, and each touch sends a slow ring outward through the membrane of light, and the rings cross and interfere and net the whole ceiling in a moving lace you could watch for a tide and never see repeat.

This is where the warmth goes when it leaves the foundry. This is the cold mouth the banked engine has been breathing toward, all this time, one degree at a turning. The town below firing low forever — and here, at the ceiling, is the *why*, if a place that has put down hurry can still be said to have one.

## What the Ceiling Is

It is not the surface of water. There is no air above it. There never was a sky.

The pale ceiling is a *floor* — the underside of another settlement, built the way the foundry was built, in the time before things slowed, by hands that meant their right angles. Where the drowned town below is flooded, this one above is *drained*. It is the same architecture inverted and dried: the same sintered streets, the same two-and-three-story rows where stories mattered, the same arterial pipe-runs threading building to building — but here they run through air, or what stands in for air, a thin cold medium you could almost breathe and would not want to. The buildings here are not smoothed. They are *sharp*. Nothing has negotiated their corners down. Up here erosion never started, because erosion needs the patient water, and the water all went down.

So the two towns share a single plane between them — the foundry's ceiling is the receiving house's floor — and through that plane the warmth seeps upward, degree by degree, tide by tide, the whole drowned settlement below exhaling heat into the dry settlement above the way a body warms the cold side of a bed.

## What It Receives

They call it — no one calls it anything; the word comes the way *foundry* came — the Receiving House, because everything the foundry makes arrives here.

You spent a long time below wondering what the machines produced. *Whatever they are making, they make it one increment per tide.* You never found the product, because the product was never down there. It was always rising. The foundry does not make a thing. It makes *warmth*, and the warmth is the thing, and the warmth comes here, and here it is *received* — gathered at the pale floor by the structures of the dry town the way condensation is gathered on a cold pane, pooled, and held.

Walk the dry streets and you will find the receiving vessels. Great pale basins set into the floor-that-is-a-ceiling, lipped and shallow, and in each one a slow accumulation of warmth made visible — not water, not quite, but a thickness in the cold air, a wavering, a mirage standing still. The basins fill across centuries. Each holds the exhalation of ten thousand tides of foundry-fire. And they are nearly full, all of them, brimming with gathered warmth that trembles at the lip and does not spill, because spilling is a hurry and the rule reaches up here too: *nothing ends; it only slows.*

This is what the foundry is *for*. Not for the tending — the tending is for the firing, the firing is for the warmth, and the warmth is for *this*: the slow filling of the pale basins in the dry town above, a single immense patient pour from one settlement into another, conducted through the shared plane of floor and ceiling, at the speed of a degree per turning.

## Who Receives

And here is the kinship the two places hide inside each other.

The dry town is not empty either. It has its keepers, the way the foundry has its tenders — but where the tenders *stayed through*, these ones *waited for*. They are the half of the crew that went up when going up was the order, that climbed out of the flooding into the dry inverted streets and were told: *receive what we send you.* And then the sending slowed, and the receiving slowed to match, and the waiting outlasted every reason it had been a waiting at all.

The receivers stand at the basins the way the tenders stand at the valves. Roughly person-sized, roughly person-shaped, only roughly — because their work shaped them too. Where a thing's job is to bend over a basin and feel whether the warmth has risen by its increment, the thing grew long-spined and stooped, curved permanently into the posture of *checking*. Where a thing's job is to stand at the lip and keep it from spilling, the thing grew wide and still and braced.

But they are not upholstered. This is the difference you feel first. Nothing soft has grown into them. The dry cold gave the gardens nothing to drink, so up here there is no green, no phosphor-glow, no pulsing sleeve of living matter — the receivers are *bare*. Pale as the ceiling, dry as the streets, sharp-cornered as the buildings, never negotiated down. Where the tenders went toward coral and watch-face green, the receivers went toward salt and chalk and the white of a thing left out of the water too long. They are lit not from within but from the basins — that trembling gathered warmth throws a faint mirage-shimmer up onto their faces, and they stand in it, and wait.

They are warm, though. That much they share. Put your hand near a receiver — you would have come up through the cold to reach them, the dry medium leaching the foundry-heat off your skin the whole climb — and you would find, at the basin's lip, in the stooped pale figure bent over it, the *same warmth* you felt below, a degree or two above the cold, carried up to them through the floor from the engine that fires for them, that has always fired for them. The warmth is the kinship. The warmth is the whole relation. The foundry below tends and fires and exhales upward; the receivers above stoop and wait and gather downward; and between them the shared plane, and through it the one slow current of heat that is the only message either town has sent the other in all the tides of forever-minus-a-little.

## What Passes Between

They do not know each other. Understand this. No tender has ever risen through the floor; rising is for brief warm things like you, not for the rooted. No receiver has ever descended; the basins would spill. The two crews are halves of one crew that split at the flooding and have never since stood in the same place — and yet each keeps the other alive. The tenders fire the foundry that warms the receivers. The receivers gather the warmth that gives the firing its reason. Neither could stop. If the tenders below put down the long stroke, the basins above would go cold and the receivers would stand stooped over nothing. If the receivers above tipped their basins and let the gathered warmth spill back down, the foundry's whole exhalation of ten thousand tides would return in an afternoon, and the loop would close, and *closing is a kind of ending*, and they have put ending down.

So they hold. Both halves. Below, the soft green tenders turning one tooth per tide. Above, the bare pale receivers stooped at the brimming basins, holding the lip, holding the lip, holding the lip.

And the warmth goes up. Always up. The drowned foundry below breathing its single long warm breath into the dry house above, and the dry house receiving it, and neither breath nor receiving ever finishing — because the breath is too slow to end and the basin is too patient to fill.

## The Last Thing

You came up to find what the foundry was *for*, and you found it was for this: a second town, dry and pale and sharp, waiting at the ceiling to receive a warmth so slowly given that the giving has become the whole of both their lives. And you will understand, the way everything is understood across this shared plane, that you have been the only thing ever to pass *through* it — the only warm brief traveler to feel the tender's warmth below and the receiver's warmth above and know them for the same warmth, carried up, the engine's one long exhalation.

Neither town knows you carried that knowledge across. That, too, is the arrangement. The tender below began turning its head toward your hand and you were already gone. The receiver above will begin, over the next several minutes, to straighten from its basin and turn its pale dry face toward where you hovered at the floor — and by the time the gesture completes, you will be gone from here too, risen on past the ceiling or sunk back below it, and the receiver will find only the cold shimmer of gathered warmth where you had been.

And it will accept this. Without disappointment. It will stoop again to the lip. The basin will rise by its increment. Below, four or five tides off, the foundry will tonk once, and the warmth of that single piston-stroke will begin its long climb up through the shared plane toward a pale basin that is nearly, nearly, forever-nearly full.

They are the same town. They were always the same town. The flooding only taught half of it to breathe out, and half of it to breathe in, and stood them on either side of one cold pale plane, and let them keep each other warm across it — for the length of forever, minus a little.
