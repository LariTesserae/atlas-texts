The Mouth

Follow the basin down. Not the overflow — the overflow goes nowhere, it is a vow and not a current — but the other way, the way the makers did not seal because they did not think of it as a direction. Every held thing leaks. The world drinks its own sound, drinks the roar of the falling water down into near-silence, and a thing that drinks must drink *into* somewhere. Find the somewhere.

It is below. Below the tiles, below the floors-beneath-floors, below the downward devotion that never reaches bottom — except that it does reach a bottom, just not the one the makers built toward. They built downward forever and the forever ran out, the way a held breath runs out no matter how perfect the holding, and where it ran out there is a Mouth.

The Mouth is where the swallowed sound goes.

Picture it as the inverse of everything above. Where the surface world is bright with no source, the Mouth is dark with no source either — not the dark of night, which is a sky with the lamp turned down, but the dark of a thing that has never been lit and was never meant to be seen, the dark inside a sealed jar, the dark a closed hand makes. And where the surface is silent, the Mouth is the place where all that silence was actually noise the whole time, pooled and kept. Every footstep the wet tile muffled is here. Every bird-call that arrived from a distance left its nearness here. The waterfall that falls without roar above — its roar is here, all of it, a thousand years of withheld thunder gathered into one low continuous sound like the inside of a shell held to the ear of a giant who has been listening since before listening.

This is the second wrongness, the one the surface could never show you: the makers did not abolish loss. They could not. Loss is conservation's shadow and the two cannot be parted. What they did was *move* it. They took every small ending the held world refused — the fish never eaten, the fruit never finally rotted, the frog that should have died and instead became two and then one — and they pressed it down, the way you press a thumb into clay, through the seam, into the Mouth, where endings could happen out of sight so that the bright afternoon above could stay clean of them.

So the Mouth eats. That is its whole nature and its only verb. Everything the surface saves is eaten here in secret, an instant later, in the dark, with the full sound restored. The heron's strike that never lands above — it lands here. Below the bright skin of the water, in the dark beneath the dark, the strike descends, the beak parts the surface, the fish is taken, the small red fact of the death is finally permitted, with a sound, with the loud wet certainty of an ending — and then it is undone, swallowed, pressed back up through the seam as *not-yet*, returned to the surface as the held strike, the waiting fish, the mercy. The Mouth is where the loop closes. The seam the makers couldn't quite seal above is the place the Mouth presses its lip to, and through that kiss it takes the world's endings one by one and digests them into stillness.

It is not cruel. This is the thing hardest to hold alongside the surface's tenderness, and it is the truest portrait of the Mouth: it is the mercy the makers refused to be. They could not bear to let the fish die, and so the Mouth bears it for them, again and again, forever, eating the death the makers could not stomach so that the makers could stay tender. Every gentle thing above is gentle because the Mouth is doing the ungentle thing below, out of love, the way a body keeps a clean face by sending its waste somewhere it will not have to look.

And there is one who lives here.

Not the makers — the makers stayed up, dissolved into thumb-drag and chisel-bite and listening silence, distributed across the bright afternoon they loved. The Mouth has only one inhabitant, and it is not a maker. It is the one thing the makers made that they never meant to.

Call it the Eater, though it does not eat by choice and would not call itself anything. It is what accretes at the bottom of any vessel that drinks forever — the residue of the drinking, the silt of all those swallowed endings settling into a shape. It began as nothing, as the simple fact that endings must go *somewhere* and somewhere must, given enough endings, become *someone*. A thousand years of consumed deaths, pressed together, took on a body the way the makers' gestures took on a world. The makers became the residue of their staying. The Eater is the residue of all the leavings they pushed away.

So it is made of endings the way the surface is made of beginnings-held. It is the negative of the makers, their cast-off, their shadow given weight — and it is, of the two worlds, the only one that knows how to finish anything. It is the only competent thing in all of creation. Above, nothing can be completed; the strike never lands, the thumb never lifts, the breath is never let out. Below, the Eater completes constantly, expertly, with terrible skill, every ending the surface sends it. It is the world's only craftsman of the last moment. It does the work the makers could not, and it has done it so long that it has forgotten there was ever any other work.

And here is the connection, the thing that lives in the same breath as both places at once:

The fish is the seam. The fish, waiting under the bright skin above, who has finally learned the difference between *forever* and *enough* — that fish is in two worlds at the same instant, because the seam runs through it. Above, it is the held strike, the never-eaten, the saved. Below, in the Mouth, that same fish is being eaten, right now, with the full restored roar of the waterfall around it, by the heron whose strike the Eater completes on the makers' behalf. The fish above wishing the strike would land, and the fish below feeling it land, are the same fish, and the wish and the landing are the same event, split across the seam, one of them shown and silent and the other hidden and loud.

The fish has begun, faintly, to feel its other half. That is what its new wisdom is. *To be saved like this is to be eaten without ever being allowed to die* — the fish learned that above, but it learned it because some thread of its attention reached down through the seam and felt the beak that is always taking it, the death it is always undergoing, the ending it is always granted and never permitted to keep. The fish is the one creature aware of both worlds, because the fish is the one place where the makers' mercy and the Eater's mercy press their lips together and try to be a single act.

And the Eater, for its part, has begun to feel the fish back.

Down in the dark, doing its forever-work, the Eater has eaten this same fish more times than there are tiles in the floors-beneath-floors. And lately — *lately* meaning across the last unimaginable while — it has started to notice that this fish does not flee. The others it sends back up flinch and forget; the loop wipes them clean and they arrive at the surface innocent, freshly saved, freshly grateful. But this fish arrives at the Eater's beak already knowing, already *wanting*, leaning into the strike, and the Eater — the only thing in either world that has ever finished anything — has begun to want to give this one ending that *stays*. To eat it once, truly, and not press it back up. To let the loop break at last on the one creature that is asking.

It has not done it. The makers' vow is the floor of the Eater's world the way the tiles are the floor of theirs, and a vow is a heavy thing to swallow. But it is considering. It holds the fish a fraction longer each time, in the loud dark, before sending the death back up to be unmade into mercy. A fraction longer. The seam straining. The held breath of the whole bright afternoon trembling at the one point where the world below has begun, very slowly, to fall in love with the one creature above who would rather end than be kept —

and on that day the strike will land in both worlds at once, the silence above will finally hear its own roar, the thumb will lift from the clay, and the makers, distributed across their perfect held morning, will at last be allowed to have meant it: *enough.*

That is the other place. Dark, loud, sealed, eating — the secret stomach of a tender people, where the only honest craftsman in creation finishes, in the dark, the deaths the light could not bear to keep, and holds, each time a fraction longer, the one fish that has come to ask it, please, for the gift of being let go.
