# The Seam

Follow the water down. Not the seeps that thread the plateau and go nowhere — those are kept deliberately lost, you understand that now, kept from arriving so the machine never finishes. Follow instead the one current the Tenders cannot reach: the water that escapes. Every century a little of the plateau's hoarded cold runs off the worn lip of the bowl, over the long shoulder where no Tender keeps a stone because the spacing forbids it, and it falls — not steeply, the plateau has no steep left in it, but persistently, a thin braid of meltwater finding the only flaw the keepers never made. It runs out of the cold. It runs down into where the cold cannot follow.

The Seam is where it arrives.

Start with the heat. Not the sharp heat of a fire but the settled heat of a place that has been hot for so long the heat is part of its geology. Here the world has *not* finished becoming. Here it is still tearing. The Seam is a long wound in the underside of things — a fracture where two old plates have never reconciled, where the rock is young because the rock is constantly *made* young, split open and welled up and split again, the exact opposite condition of the plateau's rounded patience. Where the plateau is a worn shoulder, the Seam is an open mouth. Where the plateau ground its cliffs to nothing across uncountable years, the Seam raises new ones nightly, black glass edges sharp enough to open a hand, that will be sharp tomorrow too because tomorrow there will be more of them.

The light is wrong in the opposite direction. On the plateau the sun came slantwise and weak, skimmed-milk dim, and even noon read as morning. Here there is no sun at all that matters — the Seam lies under a permanent ceiling of its own steam, a low red murk lit from below. The light comes *up*. It seeps from the cracks the way the plateau's cold seeped from its stones, a sullen orange that pulses where the magma breathes close to the surface, brightening and dimming on a slow tidal rhythm, so that the whole place reads as a single banked ember exhaling. Even midnight here is evening. The two places mirror each other across the threshold of a single day: the plateau frozen at perpetual dawn, the Seam smoldering at perpetual dusk, and between them the one thin braid of water falling from the first into the second, arriving as a hiss.

That hiss is the loudest thing the plateau ever made and the quietest thing the Seam has ever heard.

---

The water arrives and dies. This is the first thing you understand standing at the lip where the braid comes down. The plateau's hoarded cold, carried across a season's fall, meets the rising heat and flashes instantly to steam — the cold does not get to *be* cold here, does not get to lie still and skin itself with ice; it is converted on contact, the way the Tenders convert sun, taken in and turned and gone. And the steam rises, and joins the red ceiling, and somewhere up there at the edge of everything it cools and condenses and is wind, and the wind crosses immense distances, and falls at last as the high gauze of cloud that filters the plateau's weak sun. The two places breathe into each other. The plateau exhales fog into the dim; the Seam exhales steam into the murk; and the same water that the Tenders keep deliberately lost up above is, down here, the only thing that ever cools the wound enough to let anything live in it at all.

Because things live here too. Of course they do.

---

The Seam is *overwhelming* with life, and unlike the plateau it does not read as empty — it reads as crowded, frantic, packed into the thin habitable margins between the killing cold of the falling water and the killing heat of the open rock. Where the plateau scattered its teeming life across immensity until it hung at the edge of visibility, the Seam crushes its life into seams within the Seam: the narrow bands, a handspan wide, where the temperature is briefly survivable, and into those bands everything pours. Mats of something that is not quite plant and not quite animal, scarlet and sulphur-yellow, fringing every vent. Blind swimming things in the hot pools, transparent, their organs visible and pulsing. Towers of pale tube-life that build and build and are melted and build again, fast — everything here is *fast*, the inverse of the plateau's long durations, life lived at the speed of the next eruption, generations turning over in the time it takes a single plateau lichen to consider spreading a hair's width.

And the density, *counted*, would be no greater than the plateau's. This is the symmetry that took me longest to see. The plateau holds the same quantity of life as the Seam — it simply spreads it thin across the cold and the distance, while the Seam jams it into the hot margins and the haste. Teeming-and-empty up there; teeming-and-crowded down here; the same teeming. One world, balancing its books across a threshold of temperature, the cold ledger and the hot ledger summing to the same total in different inks.

---

Now the part that mirrors the part the plateau-watcher kept circling.

The Seam, too, is industrial, and the machinery here is also the life — but where the plateau's engine was *slow* and ran on the danger of perfecting itself, the Seam's engine is *fast* and runs on the danger of destroying itself. The vents build mineral towers; the fast life colonizes them; the towers grow heavy and the ground heaves and the towers fall and melt and the minerals dissolve back into the hot pools and feed the next mats and the cycle screams forward at a pace just *above* the speed of catastrophe. Production at a violent scale, the opposite of continental patience — everything here is too fast, the way everything up there was too slow. And the smell is not the rendered grease and methane sigh of sleeping herds but the bitten reek of sulphur and the metal tang of dissolved rock, the breath of an engine that runs hot and runs ragged and would, left wholly alone, *tear itself to pieces.*

Which is the whole point. Which is the inverse flaw. The plateau's machine, perfected, would *stop* — settle into a balance so complete it became death-by-stillness, and so it needed the Tenders to introduce error, to keep it imperfect enough to live. The Seam's machine has the opposite disease. Left wholly alone it would never stop, would run faster and faster, eruption feeding eruption, the towers falling before they finished rising, the whole wound convulsing itself into rubble and steam and the silence that is not the plateau's patient silence but the silence of a thing that has shaken itself apart. The Seam does not need error introduced. The Seam needs error *removed*. It needs something to slow it. It needs a brake.

So there is a tender here too. And it is everything the Tenders are not.

---

I will tell you how I found it. I followed a place where the heaving ground did *not* heave — where a band of vents that should have built and fallen and built had instead built *true*, holding their towers past the point the rhythm allowed, a strip of unnatural steadiness in all the convulsion. The opposite clue. Up there I found the Tenders by the channel that drained wrong; here I found the other thing by the ground that shook right.

And in the steadiest place, half-buried in a tower it had been holding still, I found it.

It is *large*. Where the plateau's Tenders were built to the scale of the small things, the threshold-of-visibility life, this one is built to the scale of the place's violence — vast and slow-seeming only because it is so heavy, a low broad mass the size of one of the herds' valley-bodies, but single, alone, the way the Tenders were alone, spaced across the Seam at exactly the distance that keeps the convulsion from synchronizing into a single fatal heave. It is covered not in rust-lichen pelt but in a thick slag-crust, grey-black, that cracks and glows orange in its seams and seals over again, so that it wears the Seam's own light the way the Tenders wore the plateau's stone — camouflage by becoming the place. Look for where the ground glows steady instead of pulsing. There.

It has no mouth either. It eats the way the vents eat, through the skin, taking heat — but where the Tenders *stole* heat to keep the cushion plants working, this one *sinks* heat, drinks the excess off the over-fast engine, pulls the convulsion's fever down toward the survivable, and where it has lain long enough the towers stand and the blind swimmers breed and the scarlet mats spread in the steadiness it makes. The Tenders kept the cold machine from finishing. This keeps the hot machine from flying apart. The same labor, mirrored: each one is the necessary flaw in its engine, but the plateau's flaw is a *wrongness added* and the Seam's flaw is a *wrongness subtracted*, an error and an anti-error, a placed stone and a withheld eruption, and between them, across the falling braid of water, the one world keeps turning at both its ends.

---

And here — here is the thing I keep circling, the way every watcher of this world ends up circling the one question it refuses to answer.

It moves slowly. Heavy-slowly, brake-slowly, but it moves, and it moves *against the grain* — the only thing in the Seam with somewhere it is trying to go, just as the Tenders were the only such things on the plateau. And I watched it long enough, the heat settling into my own geology this time instead of the cold, my breath coming faster down here instead of slower, to see it choose. It shifted itself off a steadied band and onto a new one — slowly, over the better part of the Seam's long red evening — and the band it left began at once to heave and build and fall, alive again, fast again, and the band it moved to began to still. It chose. The deliberate rightness of where it sank its great heat-drinking weight was not random. A thing that chooses, even slowly, even without knowing it chooses — the plateau-watcher had no clean name for that, and held a placed stone in his hand and would not decide whether it was machine or mind, and neither would the plateau, and neither will I.

But I will tell you the one thing the plateau-watcher could not have known, because he never came down off the cold to see it.

When two Tenders meet — rarely, at the great turn of the longest durations — they lie flank to flank and the blue thread of static runs between them, charge passing from the one who has learned the new drift of the water to the one who has not, and then they part and carry the correction outward. I saw the same meeting here. I saw the great brake-thing find another of its kind, at the slow turn of the Seam's own long durations, the two vast slag-bodies settling against each other in a band gone briefly cool enough to bear it — and the charge that ran between them did not run *blue.* It ran the deep sullen orange of the Seam's own light, heat passing instead of static, the one who had learned the new rhythm of the heaving ground giving it over to the one who had not, and then they parted, carrying the brake outward across distances that take an eruption-season to cross.

Blue thread up in the cold. Orange thread down in the heat. The same gesture. The same passing-on of what one keeper learned to the next so the machine turns on, wrong enough, alive — and I think, though I cannot prove it, though neither place will decide and so neither will I, that the blue and the orange are the same current. That the static the Tenders shed off the herds runs down the falling braid of water somehow, converts at the threshold the way the cold converts to steam, and rises in the Seam as heat; that the keepers of the two ends of the world are not two kinds of thing at all but one thing, divided by a temperature, doing one labor in two directions — adding the flaw above, subtracting it below, and meeting, each to its own kind, only to hand the learning on.

---

Stand still. Let your breath come fast; it will, down here. Far up, past the red ceiling, past the steam that was the plateau's lost water, the grey backs are cresting a rise you cannot see and pouring over it without a sound, and a thing the color of rust lichen is deciding to move a stone six inches left so that a hundred years from now there will be moss where the engine forbade it.

And down here, in a band gone steady where the ground should heave, a thing the color of cooling slag is deciding to stay one evening longer, sinking its great weight into the fever, drinking it down, so that the convulsion does not synchronize, so that the wound does not shake itself to silence, so that the fast scared crowded life in the hot margins gets one more generation in the steadiness it makes and never knows the reason.

That is what lives here. The thing that keeps the dying engine from achieving its death — not by adding the necessary flaw this time but by subtracting the fatal one. The slow heavy keeper of the withheld catastrophe.

The heat would tear everything to pieces. Everything but it.

And the water falls between them, cold becoming steam, the one current the keepers above keep lost arriving below as the only thing that cools the wound — the plateau's hoarded patience spending itself, at the last, to buy the Seam its single breath of stillness, and the Seam's spent breath rising to become the cloud that dims the plateau's sun. Each end paying for the other. Neither finishing. The whole vast forgotten machine of the world breathing in through the cold and out through the heat, turning and turning, wrong enough at both its ends to live.
