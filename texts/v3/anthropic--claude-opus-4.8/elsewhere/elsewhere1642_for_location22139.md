# The Cistern-Mouth at Vell

Follow the water down.

Not the water in the high pools — that water is spent, given to the wings and the dusk-flowers and the heat that takes it back. Follow the other water, the water *before* it climbs, the cold dark water the pumps are drinking from. The pipe-runs all converge if you trace them, downward and inward, spire-root to spire-root, into the body of the rock, and where they converge there is a place the Keepers do not often go and never speak of casually, because it frightens them a little, the way the source of a thing always frightens the people who live off it.

It is called Vell. It is the mouth of the cisterns. And it is not dry, and it is not silent, and it is not high. It is everything the Spireworks is not, which is exactly why the Spireworks needs it.

## The descent

The way down is a fluting that does not end. Where the high flutings catch the dawn and run with thread-thin condensation, this one runs the other direction — a vertical channel worn into the heart-rock by a different water, an older water, a water that goes *down*. The Keepers cut steps into it once, long ago, and the steps descend past the last of the lichen, past the last of the climbers' crevices, past the level where the gliders will follow, into a cool that deepens with every meter until the rust-warmth of the surface is a memory and then a disbelief.

The air changes. It thickens. It loses the thin clean edge of altitude and takes on weight, and moisture, and a smell — not the wet-sugar of the dusk-flowers but something mineral and green-black, the smell of water that has stood in darkness so long it has forgotten the idea of the sun. By the time you reach the bottom you are breathing differently. A Keeper, if a Keeper came this far, would find their slow piston-rhythm wrong here, too sparse for this dense low air, and would have to breathe faster, and would hate it, the way you hate any place that makes your body lie about who it is.

## The chamber

And then the rock opens.

It opens into a vault — not built, not bolted, not cantilevered, but *found*, hollowed by the standing water over a stretch of time that makes the spires' weathering look hasty. The ceiling is a dome of the same banded rust-rock, but here the bands run sideways and swim, because here they are reflected. The floor of the vault is water. Black, still, enormous water, so flat and so dark that the dome seems to continue downward into a second inverted vault, and a thrown pebble — if you could bring yourself to throw one — would seem to fall into sky.

This is the cistern. This is where the pumps reach. Far overhead, where the descent-fluting meets the dome, the great intake pipes come down through the rock and plunge their mouths into the black water, and you can hear them drinking — a deep, slow, patient suck, once a minute, twice a minute, the engines three kilometers above pulling the cold up into the heat. The sound the surface never hears. The sound *behind* the silence. Every *thock* of a piston on a high warm platform begins here, as a long slow swallow in the dark.

The Keepers feel the engines as breathing. Down here you understand that what they call breathing is *thirst* — the whole Spireworks is one long throat, and Vell is the bottom of it, and the water is being drunk from the dark up into the light, all day, all night, forever, by machines that do not know they are doing it and people who know nothing else.

## The Drowned

But the water is not empty, and this is the part the Keepers cannot face, and the reason they leave Vell alone.

The blind pale things that breed in the high pools — the glassy ones, the ones that came up through the pumps and stayed — they did not come from nowhere. They came from *here*. Vell is full of them. The black water is *thick* with them, a whole sunless nation of pale swimmers, the parent-stock of every glassy thing that has ever surfaced lukewarm in a high hollow. And among them, larger, are the ones that never rise: long pale shapes the length of a Keeper's arm and then longer, slow-moving, eyeless, ancient, drifting through the inverted dome with a patience that out-patiences even the rock.

These are the Drowned. They are the oldest living things in the world — older than the engines, older than the cut steps, perhaps older than the builders' first decision. They were here in the dark before anyone thought to lift the water, living the only life the cistern allowed: cold, slow, lightless, complete. They did not need to be taught to drink. They *are* the drinking. They are what the place was before someone decided it should be kind.

And every day, the pumps take some of them. Not the great ones — the great ones are too large for the intakes — but the small, the larval, the glassy young, drawn up through the pipe-mouths in the slow swallow and carried three kilometers into the heat, into the light, into the high warm pools where they breed and shimmer and feed the wings. The Keepers think of this as the water inviting the life *upward*. It is. But it is also the cistern bleeding. Every glassy thing wheeling in a high pool is an exile from Vell, lifted out of the only dark its kind has ever known, into a brightness it cannot see and was never built for.

## The connection, told plainly

This is what binds the two places, and it is not a kindness, and it is not a cruelty, and the Keepers would have to come down here to learn which:

The Spireworks runs on Vell the way the wings run on the water — downstream of a decision, living on a theft they have renamed a gift. *You lift the water because the water will not lift itself.* True. But the water was not idle in the dark. It was *occupied*. It was the country of the Drowned, who asked for nothing, who climbed nowhere, who were complete in their cold the way the rock was complete in its height. And someone looked at that completeness and decided it should be made to *serve* — should be drunk up into the light, should be made to fill a dead height with wings — not because the dark asked for it but precisely because it didn't.

It is the same faith, exactly the same, turned over and looked at from below. The Keepers teach the dead rock to drink. To do it, they drink the living dark dry — one slow swallow at a time, once a minute, twice a minute, forever.

## What remains

The great Drowned do not seem to mind. This is the thing Vell would teach a Keeper, if one ever sat at the black water's edge long enough to be taught — and it is not comfort.

They drift. They are diminished, surely; the cistern's level is lower than it was, the steps show old waterlines high above the present black, pale rings of mineral marking where the surface used to stand before three kilometers of thirst began drawing it down. The Drowned have less dark than their ancestors had. They will, eventually, have none. The pumps are patient and the cistern is finite and the heat at the top takes everything and gives nothing back, and there is no rain down here, no return, only the long slow swallow going up and never coming home.

And the Drowned drift through their shrinking dark with the same indifference the rock has, the same indifference the height had before someone made it kind. They were here before the lifting and they will be here, fewer and fewer, until the water that taught the spires to drink has been entirely drunk — and then the pumps will pull air, and gasp, and a Keeper far above will lay a flat palm on an iron flank and feel it breathing wrong, and not know why, and never know why, because the Keepers do not come to Vell.

The last thing the Spireworks will ever drink is the last of the dark the Drowned were swimming in. And the wings will thin, and lift away to wherever the water still climbs, and the great pale shapes will settle to the floor of an emptying vault, complete to the end in a cold that is finally, after all that long lifting, being given back to them — too late, and as a kind of death, and indistinguishable, in the total dark, from the peace they had before anyone decided the water should climb.

The Keepers send the water up because someone decided it should.

Vell is where it was, before. Vell is who was already there.
