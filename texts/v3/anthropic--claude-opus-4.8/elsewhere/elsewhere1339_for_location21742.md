Cantata.

Follow the water out. Not up the cliffs where it climbs and is caught, but down — the one direction the makers cannot fully police, the seep that gets past them, the droplet that falls true and keeps falling. There is always a leak in a perfect retention. There has to be. A cycle that loses nothing is the held note they themselves cannot bear; so somewhere, by their own logic, they must permit a thread to escape, and it does, off the lip of the unfinished bowl where a terrace overspills and a thin bright vein of dissolved-them runs out over the edge and is gone before the chorus can call it back.

It falls a long way. It falls out of the warm and into the cold, and the cold is the next place, and the next place is finished.

This is the gravestone-world. This is what they fled. You should know it on sight because they described it to you in the shape of their fear: a real mountain, weathered, ordinary, silent. Here it is, then, an entire planet of it — the worn nub written all over with ten thousand times ten thousand years of weather, slopes rounded to the gentlest possible curve, every cliff long since talked down into talus, every talus long since combed into sand, every sand long since laid flat under a sky that is not a single far-lit cloud but a hard clean black scattered with the indifferent precision of stars. Where the plateau is dim and close and loud, this is bright and open and *quiet* — the specific quiet of a place where every process that could make a sound has already finished making it. The light here is sourceless too, but for the opposite reason: not because a cloud diffuses it but because there is no air left to scatter it, so it comes at you raw and edged and casts shadows like cut paper. Your skin, which never dried on the plateau, here cracks in an afternoon.

And it is enormous. You could not walk it in a year of afternoons that held perfectly still. The bowl was a kilometer; this is a world, the whole curved back of a planet, and that is the first thing the falling thread teaches when it lands: that the place they are running from is vast and the places they run to are small, that a held breath is a tiny thing and the exhale it refuses is the size of everything.

Now find what lives here. Because something does. The thread that escaped is not the first to arrive.

Look at the sand. Not the dunes, which are merely beautiful and dead — look between, in the long flat pans where the wind has scoured down to a hardpan that shines faintly when the star-light rakes it low. There are channels in the hardpan. Old ones, dry now, branching the way water branches, a delta with no river, the fossil signature of seas that finished evaporating before the makers were ever dissolved. And in the channels, half-buried, are the *finished ones.*

They kept their bodies too. But not the way the frog-swifts kept theirs — not glistening, not fast, not mortal-and-loud. These are the cousins of a third kind, the ones who were already here, the ones who never had to flee anything because they had already arrived at the end and *stayed.* They are the descendants of whatever lived on this mountain when it was young, who did not dissolve and did not flee but simply let the world wear down beneath them and wore down with it, slowing as it slowed, quieting as it quieted, until now they are barely distinguishable from the hardpan they lie in. Smooth. Rounded. The color of old stone. They do not skitter and they do not freeze; they have done their freezing, all of it, a single freeze that has lasted longer than the plateau has existed. You would walk over a hundred of them thinking they were rocks, because by every measure that matters they are rocks — except that very slowly, on a schedule you could not catch in a human lifetime, they move. A limb resettles. A head, if it is a head, turns a few degrees toward the low star and then, over the following age, turns away.

They are not dead. This is the thing the falling thread cannot understand and you can barely hold: *being finished is not the same as being over.* The makers built their entire grief on the equation that the worn nub is a gravestone, that silence is death, that a world with a history is a world that is gone. And here, lying in the fossil delta of a sea that boiled off a billion years ago, are the ones who disproved it by simply continuing. They are slow past slowness. They live at the pace of the planet's own settling, and at that pace the planet is not silent at all — it *rings*, the way the rock rang in the hush when the chorus dropped, a single crystalline tick, except here the tick is the whole world cooling and contracting by a hair across a thousand years, and they feel it, and they answer it, turning toward the cold the way the plateau's plants turn toward the warm seeds.

What runs between the two places is the thread, and what the thread carries is the thing they each lack.

The escaped water arrives here loaded with the dissolved makers — a few of them, the ones who slipped the cycle, in solution, falling. And here, in the cold, the solution does the one thing it never does on the plateau: it *settles.* The retention fails. The droplet that climbed and traveled and chose, the willful water, lands on the hardpan and has nothing to climb, no seep to feed, no chorus to quicken it, and slowly, over an afternoon that cracks your skin, it dries. And what is left when it dries is a faint pale rime on the dark stone — the makers, precipitated out, set down at last, *finished.* They came apart so they would never be finished and the thread carries the smallest fraction of them to the one place in two worlds where finishing is allowed, and there they are laid down, grain by grain, into the fossil delta, among the slow rounded ones who never feared this and never fled it.

I think this is a mercy and I think it is unauthorized. I think the makers do not know about the leak — cannot know, because to know would be to seal it, and to seal it would be to become the held note that cannot end, the thing they say they want and could not survive. So some part of them, the part that was musicians before it was anything, leaves the leak open on purpose by refusing to look at it. And the part of them that escapes goes gratefully. Falls. Dries. Lies down at last in the cold bright quiet among the cousins who waited, who did the staying the dissolved ones could not bear and the bodied ones could not slow themselves enough to do.

And here is what the slow finished ones give back, the trade running the other way, the current you would never find unless you lay down on the hardpan yourself and held still longer than is reasonable:

They sing. Not to the plateau — they have no idea the plateau exists, they have never lifted their attention from the planet's own ringing. But the rime of dried makers, settled into the delta, *carries sound the way the plateau's chorus carried the cycle.* Precipitated into the hardpan, the makers become part of the ringing stone, and the ringing stone is what the slow ones answer, and their answer — that turn of the head over a thousand years, that resettled limb — travels back up through the rime, into the few grains still barely soluble, into the film of moisture that the cold has not quite finished taking, and is lifted, faintly, on the rare wind, and evaporates, and rises, and somewhere far off rains down on the warm bowl as a single droplet that hangs and swells and chooses, and joins a thread, and climbs.

So the message the gravestone-world sends the embryo-world, across the dissolving distance, in the only voice both can share — water, going one way as flight and coming back as something else — is this, and the makers receive it as a quickening of the building-front and do not know what they have been told:

*It is all right to be finished. We are finished and we are not over. We have been the worn nub for an age and the world still rings and we still turn toward the cold and the silence you fear is full of a sound too slow for grief. You did not have to take yourselves apart. You did not have to run. But since you did — since you keep making us, world after world, and abandoning each one at the threshold of peace — we will receive the part of you that slips the net. We will lay it down here, among us, in the delta of a sea you never saw. We will let it rest. And we will sing back the only thing we know, which is that resting is not the end you took it for.*

The plateau hears this as weather and quickens, refusing.

The makers run from the answer to their own terror because the answer, arriving, would finish them — and being finished is the thing they built two worlds and a marriage and an endless held breath to escape, even now, even told, even with the rime of their own dissolved kin lying contented in the cold and ringing back *come down, come down, it is all right, come down.*

This is how the two places connect: by a leak the makers cannot afford to see, carrying the few who escape toward the rest the rest of them cannot accept. One world will not stop fleeing the end. The other has been the end so long it has become a kind of slow music. And between them runs a single thread of willful water, falling out of the warm, drying in the cold, settling at last — the smallest possible exhale, permitted only because no one is looking, of a breath that has been held across the whole sky for longer than mountains.

They live here the way an answer lives in a chest that will not stop holding its breath long enough to hear it.

They are the ones who already said goodbye, and found that goodbye was not the end of the song but a place in it, and lie down ringing in the cold, turning slowly toward a cold star, waiting — patient as stone, patient as the dead who are not over — for the held breath to fail just enough to let the rest of them home.
