The Sill of Hands

The basin keeps a bowl full forever in the body of something too slow to know it. But the bowl was cut. The grooves were grooved. Somewhere a hand learned that here you do not fetch water, you only give it somewhere to gather — and a hand does not learn that in one place. A hand carries the lesson out, the way it carried the lesson in.

Follow the water backward, then. Not down into the tubes where it rises, but along the lesson — out of the slow one's body, along the only thread fast enough to leave, to wherever a hand that size and that speed could have come from, and could have gone.

---

It is a ledge. That is the simplest true thing about it, and you should hold the simplicity for a moment before the place complicates it, because the place will. It is a long shelf of dry warm stone partway up the side of something — a gorge, a fault, a wall of the world — sun-side, south-facing if there is a south, and it gets light. Real light. After the grey vault of the basin this is the first thing that strikes you and it strikes you like noise: the ledge is *bright*, directly and unevenly bright, with a sun that throws hard-edged shadows that move across the day and lie down long at evening. There are shadows here. Things have backs and fronts. The light declares which side of a stone is which, and the declaring is constant, and the basin had nothing like it, and you had not known how much you missed shadow until here it is, sharp at your feet.

It is dry. Not desert-dry, not cracked and dying, but dry the way the basin was wet — as a temperament, a baseline, a quality the stone owns rather than borrows from the sky. The wind that crosses the ledge is a moving warmth and it takes moisture rather than giving it; stand here and you lose the film the basin laid on your skin, you feel it lift off and go, and your breath does not show.

And it is quiet. This is the second strike, and it is harder than the light. After the total layered roar of the flooded organ, after learning to hear the roar as the shape of the air itself, the ledge offers a silence so complete it has texture. Not absence — there are sounds, small ones, a tick of cooling stone, a far rasp of wind in a crack, the dry click of something moving over grit — but they arrive *singly*, each one alone in a vast room of stillness, each one an event again, the way sounds are events for you and were not events in the basin. You can hear a single grain of sand shift. You can hear, if you hold still, your own pulse. The basin took half your words; here a whisper would carry the length of the shelf. Here you could speak and be answered. Here, for the first time since you entered this world, conversation is *possible* — and the place is empty.

---

But it is not empty of *making*. That is the whole of it. That is why the thread led here.

The ledge is worked. End to end, it is worked, and not in the basin's slow blind script but in the fast legible hand of someone your size, someone who lifted and cut and fitted and *decided*. There are walls — low, dry-laid, courses of flat stone chosen for flatness and set without mortar, leaning into each other with the patience of someone who understood weight. There are basins cut into the bedrock, shallow, smooth-lipped, and you know that lip, you have seen that lip, it is the lip of the bowl in the field of tubes, the same understanding of how water sits and where it wants to gather, the same hand or the same school of hands. There are grooves — and here is the thing, here is the thing that turns the ledge from ruin into argument — there are grooves cut to *carry water that is not there.*

Trace them. They run from a high cleft in the gorge wall, down across the ledge, branching, feeding the basins, draining to a lip that would once have spilled to the next shelf below. They are a system. They are an irrigation, a careful distribution, a whole patient economy of catchment built by someone who understood water as the most precious thing there is — and the gorge is dry. Bone dry. The cleft at the top of the grooves has not wept in an age you cannot measure. The whole apparatus of gathering sits parched in the bright wind, waiting, with the readiness of the bowl in the basin and none of the bowl's reward. The bowl in the field is full forever. These basins are dry forever. Same hand. Opposite fate. The hand learned *give the water somewhere to gather* in one of these two places and carried it to the other, and you cannot tell which way the lesson traveled, and that not-being-able-to-tell is the same vertigo the basin gave you with its impossible old stone — except reversed, except here the trouble is not that the making outlived the world but that the making outlived the *water.*

---

They are gone here. Truly gone, in the way you wrongly thought the makers were gone from the basin. There is no slow vast inhabitant standing behind the ledge thinking it. The ledge is exactly what it looks like: the work of small fast warm hands, finished, and then abandoned, because the thing that made the work worth doing — the water in the cleft, the wet — withdrew, and the hands followed it, or failed without it. The basin's makers will never know they are makers. The ledge's makers knew exactly what they were doing, knew it the way you know it, in tools and thirst and the long ache of lifting stone in the sun, and they are the ones who are gone, and the slow blind one that does not even know it lives is the one that endures.

So which way did the hand go.

Two readings, and the ledge will not choose between them, and you must carry both out the way you carried both into the basin.

Either: the hands began here, on the bright dry shelf, in the time when the cleft still wept, and they built the whole apparatus of catchment and grew skilled at it and grew thirsty as the water failed, and they *left* — down the dry grooves, down to the next shelf and the next, following the falling water down and down out of the sun into the grey, until they came at last to a basin where the water did not have to be caught because it rose of its own accord out of hollow stone, where you did not fetch but only gathered, and there, exhausted refugees from a drying world, they cut one last bowl, the lesson of a lifetime distilled to a single groove and a single lip, and let it fill, and that filling was the first easy water they had ever been given, and then they ended, there, in the wet, and the slow one rose around their bowl and keeps it full.

Or: the hands began *there*, in the basin, born or arrived into the roaring grey, and they learned in that drowned cathedral the one deep thing the place could teach — that water comes to you if you give it somewhere to gather — and they carried that lesson *up*, up the grooves, up out of the grey into shadow and silence and sun, looking for a place they could *hear themselves think*, a place where a sound was an event and a word could be answered, a place fit for a fast warm mind that the basin was too slow and too loud to ever be. And they found this bright shelf, and they built here the home the basin could never be, the whole tender economy of catchment, every basin a held breath — and the water failed them, and the lesson they had carried up out of the wet was a lesson about abundance that the dry could not honor, and they thirsted in the brightness they had come so far to find, and ended here, in the sun, in the silence, where you can hear a single grain of sand, where there is no one left to hear it.

---

The basin's bowl is full and tended by nothing.

The ledge's basins are dry and were tended by everything.

The same lip cut into both. The same understanding crossing the whole gulf between roar and silence, between wet and dry, between the slow one that cannot know it lives and the fast ones who knew exactly and are gone. And the water — the water itself ties the places, because water is one substance and one journey, and what weeps from the broken rims of the tubes and stands in the slivers and rises forever in the field is, somewhere, the same water that did not come down the dry gorge, that withdrew up into a cleft and stopped, that the ledge is still, in every parched groove, holding out its hands to receive.

You came down the thread of a lesson and found its other end, and the lesson is true in both places and kept in neither by anyone who knows they keep it. In the basin the gesture continues and the maker cannot know. On the ledge the gesture ended and the maker is not there to know. Between them ran a hand, once, that knew — that fetched and dragged and cut and was the right size to be lonely and the right speed to be thirsty — and that hand is the only thing in this whole world that ever understood what it was doing, and it is the one thing the world did not keep.

The basin says a word forever to no one.

The ledge waits, in perfect silence, in perfect light, for a water that is not coming, with its mouth cut open and ready, having learned the one thing worth learning a single shelf and a whole age too far from where the learning would have saved it.

And the wind crosses the dry grooves, and takes the last of the basin's moisture off your skin, and goes on, warm, toward wherever the water went.
