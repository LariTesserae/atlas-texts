# The Region Where the Line Has Failed

You named it yourself, near the end, and then you turned away from it because it was too far and too sad to walk to. *Somewhere out past the curve of the planet there is a region where the line has failed.* I went there. It took the million years you said it would, or it took an afternoon — distance and duration are strange on a world where part of the inhabitants live sideways to time, and I am not sure the walking and the watching were the same length. But I arrived, and I will tell it to you as fully as you told me the rest, because a thing that is happening deserves the same care as a thing that is finished, and this is the place where finishing comes back into the world.

Begin with the sound, because here the sound is the first fact and it does not die.

## The descent of the storm

You have to understand what the plateau's silence was *for*. It was the silence of a room held shut. Cross enough hours of flat and the held quality begins, very faintly, to loosen — the dry hiss at the edge of hearing gains a body, a low grain, a sense of something approaching that is not coming closer in space but coming *down*. And then you see it: ahead, where there should be the same pale milk-sky riding high over the same calm floor, the two have begun to interleave. The boundary sags. The storm has found the gap left by a keeper who lifted its fingertip out of the world, and it is pouring through — not fast, never fast, the storm has all the patience the ground used to monopolize — a slow cataract of churning light bending down out of the high air and reaching, at last, the powder it was kept from for an age.

Where it touches, the plateau is no longer at rest. The red-grey iron-dust that drifted sideways for a long time before settling is lifted now and does not settle; it hangs in the charged downdraft and glows, caught between the floor's color and the sky's, and the long laminar sheets of slow wind are torn into eddies that have edges, that bite, that throw the powder into the standing growths and strip them. I watched a spire go. It did not fall — you were right that nothing here falls without being asked, but the storm asks. It abraded. The dark crystalline spire that had spent an age making a brittle disc of moss-soil at its base was unmade from the top down over what might have been days, its thread-of-light snuffed mid-passage, the pale filament it had been about to trade hanging severed in the air and then gone. The disc of grey moss-stuff blew away last. A million years of patience, scoured back into suspension, made young again and homeless.

This is what the keepers prevented. This is the intolerable thing made tolerable only by their absence, which is to say by their departure, which is to say this is what their love looked like from the far side, after.

## What remains of the keeper

The column is gone. I want to be precise about *gone*, because it is the center of this place the way the rings on the calm plateau are centered on nothing.

There is no rubble. The regulators did not weather and they did not fall and they did not break — they ceased, lifting out of our thin plate of reality at a moment no one watched, the fingertip withdrawn into the direction the eye won't follow. So at the center of the failure there is not a ruin but an *absence with a shape*, a place where you feel the after-image of pressure the way you feel where a tooth was. The storm pours down hardest there, directly through the spot the will used to occupy, and around that spot — this is the thing I came to tell you — around that spot the keeper's old work survives longest, because the keeper built it nearest to itself.

The rings are here too. Half-buried perfect circles, centered, once, on something the keeper watched. But here the storm has come down *into* the rings, and I understood what I had not understood on the calm plateau: the rings are the right size to stand inside. They are not decorations and not markers of study. They are *where the keeper stood when it was still the kind of thing that stood* — when it still had a body fully committed here, before it learned to commit only a cross-section. The rings are footprints. The diagrams on the slabs are what it saw from inside the ring, looking up, in the age when it still looked up. And now the storm it once drew is churning through the very circle from which it drew, scouring the slab, and the fine inscribed lines — the cataloged forms of the sky — are being erased by the forms themselves, the record unmade by its subject, the question rubbing out the only place the answer had ever been written down.

## The drifters here

They came down with the storm. Of course they did — they live in the boundary layer, and where the boundary descends, they descend with it, and so for the first time in the world there are drifters at ground level, schooling in ragged glowing clouds among the dying spires, *inside* the rings, threading through the absence where the keeper was.

They are feeding. That is the unbearable part and I will not soften it because you did not soften the rest. The charge they live on was the keeper's leak, the surplus of an act of will — and the act has ended, the keeper is withdrawn, and yet there is *more* charge here now than anywhere on the calm plateau, not less. Because the storm is charged. The thing the keeper held back is itself a feast. So the drifters have followed the seep of warmth down out of the safe shear into the place where the safety failed, and they are thriving in it, glowing brighter than I ever saw them glow above, breeding I think — there were small ones, half the size of a held breath — schooling thick in the downdraft that is grinding the world to powder around them.

They do not know the keeper is gone. They never knew it was there. To a drifter the regulator was a feeding-place, and now there is a *better* feeding-place in the same spot, and that is the whole of what they can perceive: that the warmth has increased. The most fragile thing imaginable, flourishing on the exact catastrophe that the most patient thing imaginable spent itself to prevent. The child, fed better than ever, by the death of the parent it never knew it had, in the ruin of the house the parent died holding shut.

And when one dies here it still falls — but it does not fall slowly through cool quiet air to land among patient spires. It is flung down hard, dark, into churning dust that will not let it rest, will not take a million years to forget it because the storm forgets everything in an afternoon. There is no patient ground here anymore to receive the dead. There is only the grinding. The drifters have traded the slow tender oblivion of the calm plateau for a fast and total one, and they made the trade without knowing there was a trade, following warmth, the way they have always only followed warmth.

## The two places, in one breath

Here is how the failed region and the calm tableland are the same world, and I think you knew it when you turned away.

On the calm plateau the rule was *above is becoming, below is become*, and a keeper stood on the line to keep them from spoiling each other. The line held. The drifters sipped the surplus. The spires made their slow soil. Everything I am describing here is what that *prevents* — and so this region is not a different place from the tableland. It is the tableland's meaning, made visible. It is what every keeper on every stretch of the line is holding back at this exact moment, the thing pressed permanently into the future so it never arrives, except here, where one keeper lifted its fingertip and let it arrive.

And the connection runs both ways along the line, because the line is continuous, and the storm that has come down here is the *same churning sky* that rages harmlessly over the calm flat you stand on. Walk the line a lifetime and never leave it, you said. But the line passes through here. If you walked far enough you would feel the held silence loosen, the powder begin to hang, the pressure-shape of an absence, and you would arrive — still on the line, never having left it — at the place where the line is the same as no line at all. The boundary you are standing on safe is the boundary that, a curve of the planet away, is open and pouring. You are never alone on it, you said, though everything that keeps you company has spent the part of itself that could have known you. Here is the rest of that sentence: you are also never safe on it, because the same continuous thread that holds you up is, elsewhere, letting go, and the only difference between your stretch and this one is a single fingertip that has not yet been withdrawn.

## What the storm is doing, finally

I stayed long enough to see the storm reach the edge of its descent — the place where the downpour of churning light met, again, a stretch of boundary still held by the next keeper out, the next fingertip still pressed into the world. And there the storm *stopped*. It piled against the held line like water against a glass wall, churning, charged, unable to proceed, and on the far side of that wall the powder settled and the spires stood and the drifters schooled in their old safe shear and the silence held.

So the failure is bounded. One keeper gone, one region scoured, and then the next keeper, who does not know the first is gone — cannot know, communicates only along the direction we can't follow, if at all — holding its own stretch as it always has, and incidentally, ignorantly, walling off the disaster. Not to save the scoured region; that is past saving, and the keeper does not know it is there to save. Simply by doing the one thing it does forever, the next keeper makes the ruin finite. The storm comes down through the gap and grinds a region to dust and can grind no further, because patience resumes at the edge of the gap, because there is always, so far, a next fingertip.

So far. The keepers are few, and fewer. Each withdrawal opens a region, and each region is bounded by the keepers still holding, and so the world is becoming, very slowly, a thing of patient stretches and scoured stretches, the line a chain of holdings-on with gaps between, and every gap is a place like this one — rings full of storm, slabs erasing, spires going to powder, drifters flourishing brilliantly on the warmth of the catastrophe, dying fast into dust that will not keep them. And somewhere the question the keepers worried at, in the age when they still drew diagrams and still wondered — *do both deserve to continue, the rest and the storm, and is the only crime to let either spoil the other* — is being answered at last, not in words, by the slow arithmetic of fingertips lifting one by one out of the world.

The answer is the same as the iron's answer, the same as the keeper's love with its reasons worn away. The storm comes down. The ground forgets it was ground. The small bright things feed and fall. And the becoming, which was supposed to stay above, finishes what it starts, the way it always wanted to, the way the keepers spent themselves to delay — until the part of them that could delay it was gone, and they lifted out, and let the unfinished world come down at last and make the finished world young, and homeless, and over.

You can walk a lifetime on the line. But the line is not a circle. It is a thread with a beginning the diagrams won't name and an end that is being written now, here, in dust, by a storm in near-silence, watched by no one, fed upon by the only things still glad.
