# The Warm Drowned Mouth

Follow the water down.

It walks across the range, yes — leaf to leaf, slope to slope, weeping and steaming and weeping again — but walking is only the part of the journey the garden can see. Water that is not drunk, not steamed, not condensed back, has only one direction left to it, and that direction is *down,* always down, gathering as it goes, finding the seams in the basalt, the old flues, the hollow places, until it is no longer beads and patters but a thread, then a braid, then a thin warm river falling through the dark inside the mountain. The garden never follows it. The garden has no organ for *down past the roots.* The deepest the film reaches is the deepest the warm veins run, and below that is simply *gone* — water lost, warmth lost, a small constant leak in the closed economy of the slopes, paid out into the rock and forgotten.

But it does not stay lost. It arrives somewhere.

---

Begin again near the ground, except now the ground is a ceiling.

The mountains do not end at their feet. They go on down, the way an iceberg goes on down, into a country of stone that has never seen the violet sky or the white coin of the sun or the cold pricks of the all-day stars. Here the warm rivers that leaked from the garden have been falling for an age, and falling water cuts, and what it has cut is a cavern so large that the word fails — a hollow under the range the size of the range, a roofed sea, a drowned cathedral of warm dark water lying in perfect blackness beneath the breathing slopes.

This is where the garden's losses go. Every drip that ran too deep, every degree of warmth the kettle-beasts failed to carry back uphill, every gram of iridescence washed off a dying leaf and down a crack — all of it has been raining into this sunless sea for longer than the garden has had its present shape. The garden thinks it is a closed and balanced thing. It is not. It is the open top of a far larger system, and this is the bottom of it, the sump, the *mouth* into which the mountains have been quietly drooling their warmth and their light for epochs.

And so this place, too, is lush. For the same reason and by the opposite logic.

---

The water here is warm — warmer than the pools on the slopes, because warmth falls and pools and does not climb back out, so the deep sea is the place where all the range's lost heat has been collecting, layer on layer, a thermal stratification miles deep, hottest at the unreachable bottom where the basalt itself is still cooling, coolest at the surface that laps against the cavern's stone shores. There is no light from above. There never has been. But the iridescence came down with the water — the same bronze-black capacity-to-glean that the light-insects carry between the slopes — and here, in the dark, with no sun ever to compete with, it did not stay faint.

It learned to be the only light there is.

The water glows. Not everywhere and not evenly: it glows where the life is, and the life is everywhere the warmth gathers, so the whole drowned sea is mapped in slow cold fire — currents traced in luminous green-gold, thermoclines drawn as sheets of dim blue light hanging in the black, the hottest deeps flickering with a red so dark it is almost a feeling rather than a color. To float on that sea (if you could, if you could breathe the thick wet warm air that fills the cavern's roof-spaces) would be to look down through fathoms of living light, layer beneath layer, a sky of stars turned inward and downward and made of warmth instead of distance.

---

Here is the inversion, and it is exact, and the two places are bound by it the way a thing is bound to its reflection.

Up in the garden, warmth comes from *below* — from the buried engines — and is rationed *upward* through the roots, and light is rare and made and hoarded, and the whole vast system is starved into elegance, into slowness, into silence, by *scarcity.* The garden is a thin lush film stretched over emptiness, abundant only in variety, every creature a long way from every other, the whole thing whispering.

Down in the drowned mouth, warmth comes from *above* — raining endlessly down from the leaking range — and pools *downward,* and there is so much of it, such an unspent inheritance of millennia, that nothing here needs to ration anything. And so where the garden is slow, the mouth is *fast.* Where the garden is silent, the mouth *roars.*

It roars because water carries sound the way thin air cannot. Every falling river hammers its pool. Every current shoulders past every other. The cavern is a drum the size of a country and the water is always striking it, and the creatures here did not learn to fall silent — they learned to *sing,* because in this medium a voice goes everywhere and costs nothing, and so the drowned mouth is a riot of clicks and groans and long whale-deep songs, a darkness so loud you would feel it in your sternum, so loud that the once-a-season groan of a shifting vein — the thing a garden-creature would remember all its life — happens here ten thousand times an hour and no one marks it at all.

The garden's defining event is the garden's defining medium's *constant background noise.* That is how completely the two places invert.

---

The life, then. The fast loud crowded life.

Where the garden's grazers are slow furred hills a valley apart, the mouth's grazers are *schools* — numberless small luminous swarming things that crop the glowing mats of iridescent growth off the warm stone, turning in great sheets of light, so densely packed that a school passing overhead is a moving sky. They do not hoard warmth; warmth is free here, ambient, inescapable. They hoard *nothing.* They breed and are eaten and breed again at a tempo the garden could not survive, a generation in the time it takes a kettle-beast to change its mind about standing up.

And the predators here do not hunt stillness — stillness is meaningless in a place where everything is warm. They hunt by sound, by the pressure of a school's turning, by the song a creature cannot help singing in a medium that makes singing free. The garden's hunters were patient because patience was cheap and motion was expensive; the mouth's hunters are *sudden,* explosive, gone before the echo of their striking has crossed the cavern, because here motion is cheap and the only scarcity is *time* — the moment before the prey, warned by sound in the all-carrying water, is elsewhere.

Scarcity makes patience. Abundance makes haste. Same equation. Opposite sign.

---

But I have not yet told you the thing that should make you set the two places side by side and hold them there.

The garden is tended. You learned that. A film that rations, a herd that circulates, a hunter that prunes — a body with no center, thinking *balance.*

The drowned mouth is *not* tended. It cannot be. Tending is what scarcity *requires* and abundance makes pointless — there is nothing to ration, nothing to circulate, no warmth that must be carried against the gradient because the gradient pours warmth down for free, forever, more than anything could use. So the mouth has no film, no organ of balance, no slow vast verb the way the garden does. It is not a single thought. It is not a managed thing at all.

It is *appetite.* Sheer, crowded, loud, sunless, unregulated appetite, feeding on an inheritance it did not earn and cannot exhaust, growing without pruning, breeding without rationing, roaring in the dark over a wealth of stolen warmth that the careful starved elegant garden above does not even know it is losing.

The garden is occupied by no one — fully, gloriously, by a tending that is itself the only resident.

The mouth is occupied by *everyone* — a teeming uncountable multitude, no two of them the same, none of them tending anything, none of them aware they are the bottom of someone else's economy, all of them simply alive in the warm loud lightless overflowing dark.

---

And here is what binds them, the thread you followed down:

The garden's loss is the mouth's whole world. The garden's small constant leak — the warmth and water and iridescence that run too deep for the film to reach — is, down here, an unbroken rain of plenty, the entire foundation of the roaring abundance below. The garden achieves its silence and its balance and its terrible elegance *by leaking,* by being a slightly imperfect closed system, by losing a little of everything always downward. If the garden ever became truly closed — truly balanced, truly perfect, losing nothing — the mouth would go dark and cold and silent within an age, the rain of inheritance cut off, the whole loud multitude starving in the black.

The garden's flaw is the mouth's sky.

And the mouth gives nothing back. It cannot. Warmth does not climb. The water that pools in the drowned sea will never again steam up through a vein to bead on a bronze leaf at a sunless dawn. The gift runs one way only, down and down, and the garden does not know it gives and the mouth does not know it receives, and the two of them — the careful starved thinking slope and the wasteful crowded roaring sea — are a single system that neither half can see, joined at the place where the water disappears, related the way a wound is related to the thing that drinks from it.

---

You came looking, the first time, for a face, and were told the garden had none — only a tending, only a verb, only a balance re-achieving itself in no one.

So you might come down here, into the warm loud light-shot dark, expecting the same lesson twice: that the mouth, too, is *something* rather than *someone,* a system and not a self.

But the mouth will not teach you that. The mouth has no lesson. The mouth is not arranged to mean anything, because meaning is a discipline of scarcity and there is no scarcity here. The mouth is the part of the world that simply *is,* loudly, abundantly, pointlessly, gorgeously, drinking the slow careful grief of the mountains above and asking nothing and balancing nothing and thinking nothing at all.

The garden is a mind with no one in it.

The mouth is a body with no mind in it.

And the water falls between them in the dark, forever, carrying the warmth one way, carrying the meaning nowhere, and the two of them go on being each other's secret — the lit and the unlit, the slow and the fast, the rationed and the drowned — neither one ever rising or descending far enough to learn that it was never, for a single moment, alone.
