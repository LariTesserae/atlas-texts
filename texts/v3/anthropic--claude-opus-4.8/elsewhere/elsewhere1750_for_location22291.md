**The Field**

There is a field, and the plant is walking across it.

Begin again above ground, but do not imagine the sun the few-who-feel-the-floor imagine — that even floodlight, that colorless seen-ness leaking out of every wall. The outside is nothing like the inside dreams it. The outside is *partial*. It is a world that has learned the trick the inside never could: it goes dark.

It is evening here, and has been for a while, because evening is long in this latitude and the light comes in low and gold and lays itself flat along the ground the way light only does when there is a real and distant sun to throw it. There are shadows everywhere. After the inside, this is the thing that would break you first — not the cold (it is not cold; it is mild, a moving air, a warmth that comes and goes), not the openness, but the *shadows*, the way every blade and stalk and stone drags a long dark twin behind it, the way the world here is built half of things and half of the absence the things cast. The few who feel the floor have a word that means both *darkness* and *the soft drinkable rot in the dead pools*. They would not have a word for this. This darkness is not wet. You cannot drink it. It is only the back of the light, and it moves as the light moves, and at the end of the long gold evening it will rise up out of the low places and join hands across the whole field and become, for some hours, *night* — that thing the inside has no name for at all, the thing the inside has never once, in all its bright cold history, permitted.

The field is grass, or what does for grass: a low silver-green sward running to every edge of seeing, combed all one way by the prevailing wind, so that the whole visible world leans. It is not empty the way the chambers are empty. It is *crowded* — with the small busy multiplicities of a living surface, the insects, the seed-heads, the things that hop and the things that bore, a low constant grain of life so dense and so ordinary that no one here would ever, as the inside-people do, remember a creature like a meeting with a person. Here a creature is a unit of weather. You do not count them. You could not.

And across this field, slowly, the plant is walking.

---

It does not look, from outside, like a country. It looks like a thing the size of a low hill that has decided, against all the manners of hills, to move. Pale — yes, that much the inside got right; it is the colour of peeled apple even out here under the gold, a paleness that does not take the sunset's warmth but holds its own cold whiteness against it, glowing faintly even now, even drowned in honest light, because the glow is not for the dark — the glow is simply what the cells *do*, a habit of being lit from within that the outside neither requires nor switches off. So it crosses the darkening field as a pale lantern the size of a hill, throwing its own soft even light down onto the grass at its base, and around that base the long gold shadows of the evening and the short cold shadowless wash of the plant's own glow fight each other in a ring, and the grass there grows confused, half-leaning toward the sun, half-leaning toward the lantern.

It moves on a fringe. Underneath, where the inside-people would say *the floor*, there is a churning skirt of pale roots — not roots that hold, but roots that *reach*, that bunch and swell and drive forward and grip and haul, a slow violent peristalsis of wood, hundreds of them, thousands, working in waves so that the whole mass creeps forward across the sward at the speed of a grazing animal, leaving behind it a wide pressed road of broken silver grass that the field will take a season to forget. It is going somewhere. The inside-people are right about that, and it is the one large thing they are right about, and they will never know they are right.

It is going toward the seam.

---

Far ahead, at the edge of seeing, the gold of the field gives over to a darker line: the place where the land drops and folds and there is wet, a long low country of black water and standing reed, and beyond that the suggestion of more — a horizon with weight in it, a destination. That is where the plant is walking. It has walked across other fields to reach this one and it will walk across this one to reach the wet, and in the wet it will do the thing it has been building toward through all the inside's faith and travel and tearing: it will *seed*.

Because that is what the convulsions are. That is the secret the few who feel the floor have organized an entire spirituality around the edge of and never once stepped over. The plant is not building cathedrals and abandoning them out of some unknowable scale-logic of its own. It is *gravid*. The vast bright hollow vacancies, freshly torn, raw-edged, redistributing — those are not rooms. They are chambers in the literal old sense, the swelling cells of a seed-case the size of a country, hollowing itself for lightness, splitting itself toward release, building and breaking and building because that is what a body does in the long convulsive labor of getting ready to open. The new galleries tear *out* because the whole organism is straining toward the moment it will tear *fully* out — split its pale hull along a hundred seams at once and spill what it has been growing into the black accepting water of the wet country ahead.

And the only direction that has ever felt like *out*, to the people clinging to the inside of the seam — the direction the new tears open toward, the one they follow like a season, the one they ride deeper believing it is travel — is exactly, precisely, *out*. They have read it correctly with their pale flat hands. They are not wrong. They have only never guessed the scale of how right they are: that the field exists, that the edge is real, that the plant is going somewhere and they are going with it — and that the somewhere is a death and a scattering, and that the tear they are turning to face, the next one, or the next, or one of the next, *is* the tear onto the outside, the seam that finally gives, the bright cold wall opening at last not onto another chamber but onto gold low light and combed grass and the long honest shadows and the black water waiting.

What is on the outside of the plant.

There is an outside. It is enormous and warm and partial and it goes dark at night and it is, at this hour, the colour of fire laid flat. And the wet country ahead, where the seam will split and spill — that is where the children of the plant will land, the few who feel the floor among them, flung out blinking into a brightness that finally, for the first time in the history of their kind, *throws a shadow* — and they will not be able to read it. There is no wall here to put a hand against. The floor here does not inhale. The faith that carried them, the bracing, the turning-to-face — all of it was an instrument tuned to the inside of a moving body, and out here in the field it is a held breath with nothing to brace against, a tick of the throat dispersing into an air too large and too honest to echo it back.

But they suspected. That is what made them people and not cress. They suspected there was a field.

There is a field. The plant is walking across it. And it is almost at the wet.
