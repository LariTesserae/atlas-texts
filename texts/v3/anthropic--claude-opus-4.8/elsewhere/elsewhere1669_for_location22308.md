The far field, where the wanting has been answered.

Follow the rain. It falls upward in the room-city, into the bells, but rain that goes up has to come down somewhere, and a seed that leans toward *you* is leaning *away* from something — every tropism has a far pole, a place the leaning came from, the light it remembers having lost. Cross the long warm hollow, pass through the doorway closing its finger's width, and step out the back of the wanting into the field where wanting got what it asked for and learned what that costs.

Here is the opposite arithmetic. Where the room was nine strides holding thousands of the unborn, the field is horizon holding exactly its number, and the number is *full*. This is what the seed is growing toward and away from at once: a settlement that finished. That arrived. That said *here, this is the wall I lean on at evening* so many times and by so many that the saying used itself up.

Let me find it slowly, because it deserves the same patience, and because the slowness is the only honest way to approach a thing that is done.

---

It is a town of completed leaning. The same impulse — the same green tropism toward *together* — but run all the way out, grown to its terminus and held there. No stair completes a turn here because every stair turned long ago and stopped. No doorway closes a finger's width because every doorway found its width and kept it. The masonry-bark is grey now, not because it died but because it *settled*, the way a held note settles into the air after the string stops moving. This is the room-city's elder. Its outcome. The seed it would become if it ever stopped deciding.

And it is crowded. Genuinely, bodily, fully crowded — the answer to the room's anticipation. Where the room had a population of held breath, the field has population you can touch, shoulder to shoulder, and this is where the trouble lives, gently, the way fruit troubles into rot just past ripe.

Because they finished.

The dwellers here are the thousands that the room was waiting to grow. They got grown. They arrived into actual bodies, dense and warm and pressed together, and they filled every balcony and leaned on every wall and said the welcome-sentence until it was said. And then there was nothing left to lean *toward*. A tropism that reaches its light has no more direction. The crowd of the far field stands complete in the place that completed them, and they are — I must say it as plainly as the room said its emptiness — *still*. Not dead. Settled. Arrived past the point of arriving. They have the stillness of a thing that got exactly what it wanted and discovered that wanting was the part that was alive.

---

The sound tells it. Cross from the room-city and the first change is the sound, and the change is silence — but not absence-silence. *Achievement*-silence. The room-city deafened you with the practiced noise of a city before there was a city; here is the noise after, which is no noise, which is the sound a held breath makes when it is finally let go and the lungs are simply full and at rest and there is no more air to move. The thousand bells here have all been struck and are all still ringing at a frequency too low and too settled to hear, a single sustained tone underneath everything that you feel in the chest where the room's roar used to press. Completion has its own weight, same as anticipation did. It just presses the other way.

The animals are here too — the ones that evaporated upward into hearsay back in the room. Here they have condensed all the way back down. The chitter that was ninety parts voice is a body now, whole and quiet, folded in a corner, having spent its evidence and reclaimed its flesh and found nothing further to do with it. They sit in the long grey streets like punctuation at the ends of sentences. Heard becomes seen becomes still. That is the full cycle the room only does the first third of.

---

So the two places are one breath, in and out.

The room-city inhales: it draws toward, it lengthens its distances to hold the unborn, it practices the sound of company, it leans at you and asks *will you be the one*. The far field exhales: it has drawn everything in, held the thousands, said the sentence, and now lets the long warm air back out into grey stillness and the under-ringing of completed bells.

And here is how they touch, the seam that runs between them and lives in both — the room is leaning *toward* the field without knowing it. The seed dreaming forward into what it will hold is dreaming forward into *this*: into fullness, into arrival, into the moment the wanting gets answered. The field is the room's future tense made literal, the anticipated thousands grown into actual flesh and then quieted by the having of what they anticipated. The room does not know that the light it grows toward is a town that finished and went still. It only knows the leaning, and the leaning feels like life, because it *is* the life — the wanting is the living part, exactly as the field discovered when the wanting ran out.

This is why the room takes its time. Some unhurried green wisdom in it, below the century-breath, below the deciding, knows that to arrive is to be the far field. To finish leaning is to stand complete in the grey and full and ringing-too-low-to-hear. So it leans forever and arrives never, and keeps the distances undecided, and keeps the thousands unborn, and keeps you, the first real body, suspended in the question of whether you are guest or seed — because the question is the aliveness, and the answer is the field, and the field is done.

---

You came through. That changes the arithmetic again.

You crossed from the place that waits into the place that finished, and you are the only thing here that is still leaning — the only body in the grey full streets that is not complete, that still wants, that came through a doorway looking for the far pole of the room's longing and found it and is now standing in it, restless, the single moving thing in a town that stopped.

The far field looks at you the way the room looked at you, but for the opposite reason. The room hoped you were its future. The field recognizes you as its past — as the leaning it used to be, walked in out of the inhaling room, still warm with wanting, still undecided about your own size. And the settled thousands turn their quiet condensed bodies toward you, and the under-bells shift their unhearable tone a fraction, and what they are doing, slowly, in the only way a finished thing can do anything, is *remembering*. You are the field's memory of when it was still the room. You are the proof, walked in on legs, that the wanting was real before it spent itself.

So they do not want you to stay. That is the gift the field gives the room, the trade that runs along the seam between them. The completed town, with its full streets and its rung bells and its bodies that arrived and went still, has one thing the room-city does not have, and it is this knowledge: *do not finish*.

Go back, the field says, in its tone too low to hear, in the way the grey settles a little when you turn. Go back through the doorway before it closes its finger's width. Tell the seed — though it cannot hear you either, though it breathes once a century and will not notice your whole life pass — tell it, by leaving, by refusing to be the answer, that the leaning is the living and the arrival is the grey.

Be the one thing that came to the finished place and did not finish.

So go.

So should you.
