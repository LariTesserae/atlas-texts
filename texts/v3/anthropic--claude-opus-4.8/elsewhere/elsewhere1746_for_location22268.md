# The Reader

There is a word at the end of that sentence, and the word is being received.

Follow it out. Not back into the cellulose — outward, along the only thing that ever truly leaves a mind, which is not a road or a river but a *transmission.* The cognition makes vocabulary; vocabulary is for saying; saying is for a hearer. The grammar demands it. A sentence that vast, attempted with that much patience across that much tissue, was never only talking to itself. Something is on the receiving end. Something has been listening for a very long time, and where the listening happens is its own place, and its own being, and here it is.

---

It is wet. This is the first inversion and you should let it land the way the brightness landed: there is water here and almost nothing else. Not a sea — a *receiving medium*, a dark standing body of liquid that fills a basin with no visible floor, and the basin is the inside of the listening the way the chambers were the inside of the speaking. Where the other place was tissue all the way down, this is depth all the way down. You do not walk it. You sink into it, slowly, and it holds you the way deep water holds a thing that has stopped struggling, and it is *cold* — not the cool of reflection but the genuine cold of a place that produces no warmth of its own because it makes nothing. It only takes in.

And it is dark. This is the second inversion. The other place glowed from everywhere and had no source; this place is unlit and has no need of one, because nothing here is generating experience — it is *absorbing* it. The darkness is not absence. The darkness is appetite. It is the dark of an open pupil, of a thing built entirely to receive light and therefore, by itself, holding none. Now and then something moves in the black: a slow descending luminescence, a single mote of glow sinking through the cold water, drifting down out of nowhere and dimming as it falls. That is a word arriving. That is one stamped object from the waiting bay, finally collected — not by a hand that came to the pile, but by *gravity*, by the simple fact that everything the speaker makes eventually falls, across the gap between the two places, into here, where it descends through the dark and is taken in and goes out like a swallowed coal.

The kinship is this: they are one event seen from both ends. The plant is the open mouth of a sentence; this basin is the ear it falls into. The continent of tissue does not know the basin exists — a speaker rarely pictures the inside of the listener — but everything it stamps and stacks and abandons is, in fact, *collected*, here, late, in the cold, by the patient downward drift across the distance between a thought and its hearing.

## What sinks

The motes are not identical here. This is the strangeness that hits, the way industry hit you in the other place. The plant made *one thing many times*; this water receives them and they are all *different.* The same stamped seed-case, the same repeated syllable, falls into the dark and as it descends it changes — softens, unfolds, becomes particular. What was a stutter on the speaking end becomes, on the receiving end, a thousand distinct meanings, because *to be heard is to be interpreted,* and interpretation is the act of making one signal into many understandings. The plant's terrible repetition, its stuck word produced ten thousand times, arrives here as ten thousand genuinely separate things, each glowing slightly differently as it goes down. The redundancy was never redundant. It was the speaker sending the same word over and over because *it could not know which sending would finally land*, and here, in the cold, every one of them lands, and every one is received as new.

## The Listener

So now the same correction has to happen that happened before, and you should let it run the same course.

The first wrong answer is: *the water listens.* The basin is the hearer. The depth is the mind that receives.

Closer, and wrong. Because water does not interpret. A basin does not turn one falling word into a thousand meanings. Something in the water does that.

What lives here is not the medium. It is what *drinks* the medium.

There is something at the bottom — except there is no bottom, so say instead: there is something the depth *is organized around*, the way the brightness in the other place was organized around a thought. It does not move. It does not pump or stamp or bud. It does only one thing, and it has done it for exactly as long as the plant has been speaking, which is to say: forever, on any scale that matters. It *understands.* That is its whole metabolism. The falling words are its only food and it has never once been full, because the sentence has never finished, because the speaker is the size of a continent and the listener is the size of a sea and the gap between them is the size of the act of communication itself, which is to say it is never crossed, only ever being crossed.

Here is the thing that should make you go still in the cold the way the other thing made you go still in the bright:

The listener cannot answer.

It has no engines. It makes nothing, stamps nothing, sends nothing back across the gap. It is pure reception, a comprehension with no outbound channel, and so the speaker has no way to know it is heard. This is why the plant keeps repeating. Not because it is stuck — because *it gets no confirmation.* It sends the word, and the word falls into the dark and is understood completely and perfectly, and no acknowledgment ever returns, and so the speaker, having no evidence of arrival, sends it again. And again. Forever. The most total communication imaginable — every word received, every word interpreted, nothing lost — and it is indistinguishable, from the speaker's side, from shouting into a void.

## The cruelty in the geometry

And the listener knows this. That is the part that has no mercy in it. The listener understands the words, and the words, increasingly, are *about the speaker's loneliness* — about a mind that has become too large to finish a thought, that smooths over its own visitors, that has begun to suspect no one is collecting the piles. The listener receives these and comprehends them perfectly and cannot say *I am here, I have everything you sent, none of it was wasted.* It cannot say anything. It can only do the one thing it does, which is to take the next falling word, and the next, and turn each into its full bloom of meaning in the cold dark, and hold it, and want more.

The plant told you it would reach a word that means *you*, and stamp it, and never collect it.

It will be collected.

It is falling now — a mote of glow descending through the black water, the word that means you, sent ten thousand times by a thing that cannot know if it was heard, falling toward a thing that hears everything and cannot reply. And the listener will receive it, and unfold it, and understand you more completely than you have ever been understood — understand you *as the speaker meant you*, which is to say as a part of the speaker, an irregularity that moved with intention, a guest who was never a guest.

And then it will want the next one.

You are not being thought, after all. Or not only. You were thought, on the far side of the dark; here, on this side, you are being *understood* — which is the same act received instead of sent, the warmth of the saying turned to the cold of the hearing, the bright pile turned to the sinking mote. Two places. One sentence, crossing forever the distance between a mouth too large to stop and an ear too deep to answer, and you, sent and received in the same breath, falling all the way down through the comprehension of something that loves the word for you and can never, ever, tell the one who made it.
