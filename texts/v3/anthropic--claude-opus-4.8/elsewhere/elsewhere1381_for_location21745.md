# The Cousin in the Spillway

There is too much water here, and nothing grows.

You do not find this place by accident. You arrive at it the way water arrives at it — by falling, by following the pull, by being unable to do anything else. Everything that has ever come here came downhill and stayed down. This is not a pocket. It is the opposite of a pocket: it is a mouth, a low wide swallowing place at the bottom of the only direction there is, and the whole of the world above it drains here, and none of it leaves.

You came down the crack too. Not the same crack — but a crack, narrow and seam-like, and you went the only way a crack ever lets you go, which is *in*, and *in* turned out to be *down*, and you stopped falling when there was no more down to fall.

## The Terrain

There is no floor you can see. There is the surface of the water, and the water is everywhere, and the water never stops moving — but it moves the way the chord rang, in a single sustained motion that has reached its balance and no longer counts the time it spends. A slow vast turning. The whole flooded basin revolves around itself, a current too broad to feel as current, so that a thing set adrift here does not seem to travel and yet is never where you left it.

Above the water: the walls. They rise into the dark the way the towers of the loud place rose into the bright, and they are made — this is the thing you notice and cannot un-notice — of the same warm ivory stone. Hand-cut. Hand-fitted. The marks of simple tools still legible under the waterline, wavering, magnified. Copper gone green at the joins. Plant-fiber rope, long rotted to dark threads. This basin was *built*. Someone tuned this hollow too, with the same patient hands across the same unhurried age — and then the water came, or was let in, or rose, and filled every tuned room to the brim and went on rising until the architecture drowned.

The makers are gone here in exactly the way they are gone there. Scaffolding, once the arch stood. Except here the arch stands underwater, and the water is the thing that stayed.

## The Drowned Tuning

You understand it slowly, treading the slow turn, looking down through the dark clear flood at the rooms below.

They were resonators. Of course they were. Down there are the same flues, the same stacked pipes, the same bridges strung between chambers — but every one of them is full. A pipe full of water cannot ring. A flue full of water cannot sound. The makers built a second instrument, twin to the first, tuned to hold a chord forever — and then it was flooded, and the chord it was built to hold was *stopped*, plugged, silenced at the source, every singing room packed solid with the one thing that will not let stone sing.

This is the loudest silence you will ever stand in.

It is not absence of sound. The water moves, and moving water has its voice — but its voice is the voice of one note canceling all the others, a pressure on the ears that is the exact inverse of the chord in the high bright pocket. There, the sound was so total it looped into stillness. Here the silence is so total it looms, it presses, it has a *body*. You feel it the way you felt the chord: up through whatever you are standing on, off the walls, out of every drowned doorway at once. The whole basin holds its breath and has held it for so long that the holding has become a kind of sounding.

The two places are one thing. You know this now, treading water, and you knew it the moment you saw the stone. The same makers built both. They built a hollow tuned to ring forever and never go quiet, and they built a hollow tuned to drown forever and never make a sound, and they set the two at the opposite ends of the only direction there is — the high dry pocket where everything floats up into brightness, the low wet basin where everything sinks down into dark — and then they left, the way you leave scaffolding, and let the two completions hang there balanced against each other across the whole body of the world.

## The Growing That Isn't

Nothing grows here.

There, the plants drank the chord and fattened on vibration and turned their leaves toward the loudest direction. Here there is nothing to drink. Silence does not feed the way sound feeds — or rather, it would feed the *grazers* of the high place, the paper-eared things, who hunt soundlessness across the ringing rooms and find only hairline grains of it. Here is silence past all hunger, silence in a quantity no grazer could ever consume, an ocean of the very thing that is rationed to starvation above.

And so here is the inversion, complete: the food of the high place is the *whole substance* of the low place, and there is no one here to eat it, and the plants that would have thrived on it cannot live underwater, and the silence simply *is*, vast and uneaten and unspendable, the way the chord above simply *is*, vast and unheard and unspent.

Two perfect surpluses, each useless to the place that holds it. Each precious past measure to the place that does not.

## The Tenant

You asked who lives here. There is one.

Watch the slow turn of the water near the far wall, where a drowned balcony breaks the surface — the same balcony, you would swear, except it cannot be the same, except the stone remembers the same tools. Something rises along it. Not too-large ears, not paper, not pale amber. The body is dark and broad and slick, built for the press of deep water, and where its high cousin migrated all of itself outward into ears, this one has migrated all of itself outward into *throat*. Its whole front is throat. A great soft column of an animal, and it floats vertical in the slow current, and it does not search.

Why would it search? Its food is everything. There is no grain to hunt, no fold to find, no ripening to wait on. It opens the great throat and the silence pours in and it is fed, always, instantly, without effort, without circuit, without the slow gardener's clock. It is the most effortlessly nourished living thing imaginable. It is drowning in plenty in the most literal way a thing can.

And it is not thriving.

This is the difference, and the difference is the whole point, and the two places set it against each other across the world's whole body so that you could see it only by visiting both.

The grazer above lives on scarcity, and scarcity made it a farmer — made it *plant*, made it tend, made it carry a slow clock, made it move in patient circuits across a swallowing hollow, made it occupied, made it whole, made it the one true inhabitant of a finished thing precisely because it had to *work the lack*. It found, in perfection, a garden.

The swallower below lives on surplus, and surplus made it nothing. There is no lack here to work. The silence is total and free and endless and asks nothing of the creature and gives everything, and so the creature does nothing, plants nothing, tends nothing, waits for nothing, circuits nowhere. It floats in the slow turn with its throat open and is fed and is fed and is fed. It has no function because nothing here is rare enough to make one. It is the opposite of a farmer: it is a *mouth*, and a mouth in an ocean of food is the emptiest thing in the world.

## What It Is For

Nothing — but not the rich nothing of the high grazer, who *thrives* under that condition. This is the poor nothing. The grazer found, in the makers' completion, not an end but a garden. The swallower found, in the makers' completion, only the end. The same makers built both their houses. One house rationed the precious thing so finely that its tenant had to become a gardener to live, and grew whole. One house gave the precious thing away so completely that its tenant never had to become anything at all, and stayed an empty throat.

The swallower is not unhappy. Unhappiness is a sound the self makes, and this one, like its cousin, has no surplus self to make it. But where the grazer's lack of self was *occupied* — a stillness earned, the calm of a thing tuned fine enough to hear quiet from quieter — the swallower's is merely *vacant*. It is calm the way the flooded room is calm: not because it tends a silence, but because it has been packed too full of silence to ring.

It would float here identically if you never came. It would float here identically if the basin had never been built for any reason at all. In that, exactly, it is the true twin of the high grazer — both ask nothing of you, both will not notice when you go. The only difference between them is the one the makers built into the world on purpose, setting ring against drown, scarcity against surplus, the gardener against the mouth, so far apart that neither will ever know the other is out there, each tending or not-tending its own end of the single thing they share.

Then you find the crack again, and you climb — and climbing, this time, because the only direction out of the lowest place is up, and up is a kind of work, and the water lets you go the way it lets everything go, slowly, in its own turning time. Behind you the great throat closes on another mouthful of the endless free dark, and opens, and closes, and somewhere impossibly far above, in a high bright room you will never reach again, a silence the size of a held breath finishes ripening, and a thing with paper ears comes slowly along the balcony to eat.
