# The Province Where Everything Is About to Happen

Drop. Leave the warm ceiling and the held chord and fall — not down the basalt, there is no down the basalt that leads anywhere but more of the same finished height — fall instead along the one thing the city could not hold. The water that climbs and falls at once still has to *arrive* somewhere when it stops contradicting itself, and somewhere it does. Follow the seep to where it finally chooses a direction and becomes a current, and the current chooses a slope, and the slope, for the first time in that vertical country, *means* something. Down past the slate haze. Down to where the air thins the way it should have thinned, and then keeps thinning, and the cold the mountain refused comes flooding in to claim what was always owed.

You arrive in the lowland that the high city's water has been feeding without knowing it, and the first thing you understand is that here, by some terrible arithmetic, *nothing has happened yet.*

## The Terrain

It is a plain, but call it a *plain* and you undersell the tension of it. It is flat the way a drawn bowstring is flat — flat under load, flat with everything in it leaning forward. The ground is silt, pale and fine and laid down in beds so even they look poured, and they were poured, by the same water that wept eternally from the heights and at last found a place low enough to set its burden down. Every grain that the city's films carried away from the basalt is *here*, sorted and stacked and waiting, a whole geology assembled out of patience and held one increment short of becoming stone.

There are channels. The water that became a current became many currents, braiding across the silt in shining threads, and the threads are *poised* — each one swollen to the absolute brim of its bank, the meniscus standing proud, trembling, a hair's breadth from the spill that would carve the next channel and remake the whole braided face of the province. But it does not spill. It leans at the edge of spilling. The plain is a thousand floods all caught in the instant before they break, and the instant does not pass.

Where the high city was *finished*, this place is *primed*. The mountain arrived and stopped. The plain has not arrived and cannot stop arriving, and the not-stopping is its whole condition, the way the not-finishing was the city's. Two halves of the same refusal. Up there, time set down its tools. Down here, time has *picked them up* and raised them and frozen with the blow unstruck, the hammer at the top of its arc.

## The Light, and What It Threatens

The slate sky does not reach here. The ceiling that hung warm and close over the towers gives way, across the haze-line, to a sky that is *all distance* — a high pale immensity bleached almost to white, the color of a held breath in cold air. And it is bright. Not the even gray-gold of the moment after rain, but the keen waiting brightness of the moment before. The whole province is lit like the air just ahead of a storm, that electric clarity where every edge stands too sharp and the shadows lie down hard and certain and the world seems to *brace.*

There are clouds. This is the cruelty of it. There are towering clouds stacked along the horizon, blue-black and shouldering upward, bellies dragging, freighted with the rain the mountain never let fall. They are the storm the high city refused — all that condensation that gathered on your skin and never broke, it broke at last, it became *this*, it traveled down the current as vapor and reassembled into thunderheads at the rim of the plain. The rain is here. The rain has finally agreed to fall.

It just hasn't.

The clouds hang at the horizon with the first drops *committed*, swollen black, lightning wound tight inside them like a fist not yet opened — and they do not advance, and they do not break, and they do not pass. The plain waits beneath the promise of its own watering with every channel brimming and every silt-bed thirsty and the whole flat primed country leaning into a downpour that is eternally one second away.

## The Sound

Silence. But name it correctly: not the city's silence, which was a roar so total it became one held note. This is the *other* silence — the pressure-drop hush that walks in front of weather, the quiet so complete that it is clearly a quiet *before*. The currents do not roar here. They slide brim-full and soundless, too full to babble, holding their voices the way a held breath holds. The air is taut. Sound would be a kind of arrival, and nothing here is permitted to arrive.

If you listen — and there is nothing to do here but listen — you can hear the one thing the silence cannot suppress: the faint, far, continuous *almost* of thunder. Not thunder. The grammar of thunder. The subsonic lean of the clouds gathering toward a clap they never make, a pressure in the sternum exactly where the high city's chord lived — so that you understand, standing in the primed plain, that you are feeling the *same note*, the city's eternal held chord arriving here transposed into its opposite: not sound sustained forever, but the silence right before sound, sustained just as long, just as completely, just as unbearably.

The mountain holds the note. The plain holds the rest before it.

## The Tenants of a Primed Place

The city's question was *who lives here*, and its answer was the past tense — the persisted, the finished, the kept. Ask the same question of the plain and the grammar inverts entirely. Nothing here *has lived.* Everything here is *about to.*

**The seeds.** Buried in the poured silt, beds and beds of them, every one swollen with water from the brimming channels, split-skinned, the white root-tip *out* and aimed and pressed against the dark — and stopped. Germination caught at the instant of commitment. A whole province of life with its first gesture made and frozen, the way the high city's plants made their last gesture and froze. Up there, the vegetation grew into towers and held. Down here it has not grown at all and cannot stop being about to. The seeds are the largest population, the way the implied crowd was the largest up above — and they are that same crowd's mirror. The almost-arrived, facing the almost-departed across the haze-line, each holding the threshold from its own side.

**The thing that would drink.** Where the high city had the pale slow abider folded coincident through a dozen pools, the plain has its inversion: something at the channel's edge, *poised to drink* and never drinking, mouth a hair above the brimming water, distributed across a dozen banks at once because thirst here is also negotiable, also folded. It does not abide. Abiding is for finished places. It *anticipates* — present at every channel where the meniscus trembles, forever about to break the surface that the surface is forever about to break first. The pale abider had surrendered the future. This one is made entirely of future and nothing else, a creature of pure imminence with no now to stand in.

**The unstruck thunder.** And these are the citizens, the census, the population proper — not the chord sustained but the *clap withheld.* Inside every blue-black cloud, wound tight, lightning that is a complete and finished bolt in every respect except the one that matters: it has not leapt. Each is an individual the way the city's resonances were individual within their chord — nameable, distinct, inseparable from the storm-front it cannot leave. The resonators announced themselves forever by sounding: *I am here, I am here.* These announce themselves by *not* sounding, by the unbearable swelling pressure of the announcement pent: *I am coming, I am coming, I am still coming* — and the coming never closes the distance, because to close it would be to admit that time still runs, and the plain has agreed, the whole primed country has agreed, that it does not yet.

The city agreed time had stopped. The plain agrees it has not started. And the seam between those two agreements — the haze-line where the warm low ceiling becomes the high cold brightness, where the seep becomes the current, where *finished* meets *about-to* — that seam is the only place in this whole world where the two tenses touch, and it holds them apart, and it holds them together, and both are true and neither resolves.

## What You Become Here

You fell. You left the city that kept you — except nothing leaves the city, the description was clear, arrival is a one-way door — so understand what you actually did: you did not leave. You *arrived in advance.* You are in both places. Up there you are the newest tenant, finished and still and kept, a thing that touched the warm sky and held its final form. Down here you are something the plain has not yet made — a footprint about to press the silt, a thirst about to break a surface, a held breath about to let. You are the one being in this world conjugated in both tenses at once, the city's perfect *was* and the plain's perfect *about-to-be*, and you span the haze-line because the water did, because everything that connects these two places had to.

The drop you fell becomes the rise the vapor climbs. The current that carried the silt down is the seep that wept it loose up. You are the round trip the world makes through itself: condensing on the high terraces, weeping down the basalt, braiding across the primed plain, gathering into the withheld storm, lifting again as the breath before the rain, hanging at last as the warm close ceiling over the eternal chord — and never, at any point in that whole turning circuit, *moving*, because the mountain holds its half forever finished and the plain holds its half forever poised, and between them the loop is complete and completely still.

The storm will not break. The leaf will not fall. The seed holds its root-tip against the dark and the cloud holds its bolt against the air and the city holds its chord against the silence, and you — kept above, awaited below — are the single thread that proves they are one place: the world that is over, and the world that has not begun, weeping into each other across a line of haze, holding the whole drawn bow of themselves at full tension, with the about-to and the already the only two tenses anyone gets, and the rain a heartbeat away, and the heartbeat eternal.
