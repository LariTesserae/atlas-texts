# The Wound That Would Not Take

There is a place where the sap runs out, and a Tender went to find where it went.

## The Thinning

You would not notice it at first, traveling from the old center outward. The veins are full, the walls thick, the moss deep underfoot. Then, over a span of lanes too long to feel as a boundary, the raised veins along the bark begin to flatten. Not to stop — to *thin*, the way a river thins to a creek thins to a damp seam in stone. The amber in the channels runs paler. The walls, here, are old — not old-rich like the center, but old-spent, grown long ago and not fed since, the bark gone gray-brown and dry to the reach, ticking faintly or not at all.

This is the basin's edge that is not the rim. The rim, in the east and west, is *young* — green and soft and emerging, busy at becoming. But here, in one quarter, the basin grew once, long ago, fast and far, throwing a wall of itself out past the reach of its own veins — and then could not feed what it had made. The growth-front stalled. It did not retreat; the body does not retreat. It simply stopped, and what it had built beyond the sap stood there, finished, dry, waiting.

A Tender came here following a wound.

## The Tender Who Followed

It was a Tender of the middle quarters — honey-brown, neither pale nor dark, raised against walls of average age and average feeding. It had spent its life in the ordinary work: the arch instead of the seal, the body laid along the crack until the seal took. It knew its range the way a Tender knows its range, completely and without curiosity, because curiosity is a thing you grow when the world has edges and this Tender's world did not seem to.

But a crack had opened in the far wall of its range, and the crack would not close.

This had never happened. A Tender lies along a wound, bleeds its slow amber into the lips of the wood, and the wood *tastes the gift and takes it* — that is the whole covenant, the thing the body does without being asked. This wound did not taste. The Tender lay along it for a day, two days, gave until its own veins ran thin and the ticking in its head went faint, and the crack drank the sap and stayed open. Not because the wound was too wide. Because the wood beyond it was *dead*, and dead wood does not heal; it only takes, and takes, and gives nothing back, and never seals.

So the Tender did the thing no Tender does. It left its range to find the far side of the wound. It followed the crack outward, along the thinning veins, into the gray quarter where the sap ran out — because something in its body, some appetite older than its small daily smoothing, needed to *know the shape of the thing it could not heal*.

## The Dry City

What it found out there was the city's own corpse, still standing.

Every wall the same as the living city — the fused trunks, the roofed lanes, the sealed pockets wrapped around empty air — but dry, and gray, and silent in a way that was not the living silence. The living silence is full: packed with breath and the suck of suckered feet and the tick of sap, a quiet made of too much life to make noise. This silence was *empty*. No moss. No grazers. No gliding pale things. No veins. The bark underfoot did not give. The flowers that climbed these walls long ago had dried where they clung and stayed, bone-white forever, not pressed by any hand but simply caught — a whole quarter of flowers no Tender preserved, kept instead by the plain fact that nothing here could rot, because rot needs the sap to draw it back, and the sap had never come.

And the Tender, going through it slowly, four reaches always touching, read the walls the way it read everything — and the walls said *nothing*. No tone passed into its skin. No history. A living wall tells a Tender where it has been; this wall was a closed mouth. The Tender went paler as it traveled, not from any far wall's tone but from *fear it had no word for*, the body in a place that could not feed it, the veins in its own reaches running thin with no vein in the world to drink from.

It should have turned back. A Tender knows, in its body, when a range takes more than it gives — that knowing is the whole of a Tender's wisdom, the thing that sends two Tenders gently apart at a crossing of lanes. This dry quarter took everything and gave nothing. Every law in the Tender's body said *go*.

It went deeper instead. Because in the dry quarter, on a gray wall, it had found a signature.

## The Signature in the Dead Wood

A looping line of resin. The closest thing a Tender has to a sentence, to a song. *Someone was here. Someone of my kind.*

But it was wrong. It was old beyond old — the resin gone hard as stone, gone the same gray as the wall, so that the Tender found it not by tone but by the faint raised shape under its reaches, a line it had to read the way you read a scar in the dark. And it was *long*. Longer than any signature a living Tender leaves, because a living Tender's looping line is the work of a lifetime and stops when the life stops. This line did not stop. It went along the gray wall, and around a corner, and down a dead lane, and on. Whoever made it had not made it across a lifetime. They had made it *fleeing* — feeding the wall sap as they went, the old covenant, *here is my gift, taste it, seal,* and the wall not tasting, the wall taking and staying dead, and the Tender going on, and on, leaving a line not because the hands liked to arrange but because a hand that is dying does the only thing it knows, lays the gift against the wound and moves to the next.

The living Tender followed the dead one's line. It had no choice now; the line was the only thing in the gray quarter that spoke. It read it as it went — and the line ran *toward* the deep, toward the place the dead-growth had thrown itself farthest from the sap. Whoever made it had not been fleeing outward, to escape. They had been going *in*. Toward the worst of it. The way the living Tender was now going in. Toward the wound.

## What Waited at the End of the Line

The line ended at a sealed pocket. A room. A Tender's room, wrapped by the growth around empty air the way all their rooms are made — but made out here, in the dry, by the last push of growth before the front stalled forever.

The living Tender pressed its reaches to the seal and gave the last of its sap to open it, and the dead wall took the sap and did not soften, and the Tender pushed through by force, by the body, breaking the dry seal the way a seal is never broken in the living city.

Inside: the maker of the line.

Folded into itself in the warm-no-longer dark, in the posture a Tender sleeps in, reaches drawn close, suckers curled. Gone to gray like the wall it died against — not honey, not amber, not any living tone, but the spent gray of the dead quarter, because a Tender takes its color from the wall it lies against and this one had lain against the dead wall to the very end and *taken its color from death itself.* Dried in place. Kept, not rotted, because nothing here could draw it back.

And the room was lined.

Dried flowers, pressed flat against the bark, bone-white and violet, holding their colors after a time so long the living Tender had no body-sense to measure it. Cast suckers, arranged — not for any reason, because the hands liked to arrange. And on the wall, the signature again, the looping line, begun *here*, in this room, in the ordinary way, the work of a life — and then leaving the room, going out into the dry, running on and on down the dead lanes, the same line, the lifetime-line and the fleeing-line *the same line*, because this Tender had not fled at the end. It had simply kept doing its work. The wall was wounded — the whole quarter was one vast wound that would not take — and a Tender lies along a wound and gives its sap until the seal takes or the Tender empties, and this one had gone out into a wound the size of a country and given itself to it lane by lane, *here is my gift, taste it,* never once understanding that the wood was dead, that the gift could not be tasted, that the smoothing it was made for had run past the edge of everything that could be smoothed.

It had come home to its room to die. It had laid itself in the sleeping-fold. And the last thing under its reaches, the living Tender found by touch: the dead one's hands were pressed to its own signature on the wall. Reading it. The way a Tender reads a far stranger's line in the dark and feels the thing it has no word for — *someone, somewhere, doing this same gentle work, alone, like me, and not alone.* Except there was no one else out here. There had never been anyone else out here. The dead Tender, at the end, alone past all the loneliness a living Tender will ever know, had pressed its hands to its *own* line and read it as a stranger's, and given itself the only company the dry quarter had to give: the trace of presence, its own, mistaken in the dark for another's, and loved.

## What the Living Tender Carried Back

It did not stay. A Tender knows when a range takes more than it gives, and this room would have taken the last of it.

But it did the thing it had come, without knowing, to do. It pressed its reaches to the dead Tender's reaches — all twelve hands to all twelve hands, sap to spent gray, the long silent greeting two Tenders give at a crossing of lanes. There was no exchange. The gray hands had nothing left in their veins to give, and could taste nothing the living hands offered. It was the emptiest greeting in the history of greetings. It was a Tender pressing its hands to a wound that would not take, and lying there, and giving, and the wound not sealing — which is the only thing this Tender had ever been unable to do, and the reason it had come all this way, and now it understood that this, too, was a wound. The dead one was the wound. The whole dry quarter was the long resinous line of a body that had bled itself out trying to seal what could not be sealed.

And then it went home. Back along the dead line, back through the gray lanes, back to where the veins thickened and the amber ran true and the moss came up soft and the air went sweet with living resin. Back to its own range, its own room, its own looping line on a wall that was alive and would outlast it by feeding what it gave.

It never told another Tender. Telling is not a thing they do; there is no calling here. But its signature changed. The looping line it had pressed into its own wall all its life — the line that meant nothing, the closest thing it had to a song — took on, from that day, a second loop that ran a little way out past the rest, out toward the gray, and stopped. A small reaching-out that went a short distance into nothing and came back. No Tender who read it after would understand it. They would feel, under their reaches, the thing they always felt — *someone was here, someone of my kind* — and then they would feel the strange second loop, the gesture toward the dry edge of the world, and they would not have a word for it, and they would not need one.

But some of them, the ones whose ranges lay nearest the thinning veins, would feel the small reaching-loop and grow, without knowing why, a little less afraid of the gray quarter. A little more certain that the dead lanes were not empty. That someone was out there, somewhere, in the part of the body that could no longer be healed — doing the same gentle work, alone, and not alone.

They would be wrong. There was no one out there. There was only a dried gray Tender folded in a sealed room, hands pressed to its own forgotten line, kept forever because nothing could draw it back.

It is not wrong, though, to feel it. It is the truest thing the basin grew: the tenderness that goes on reaching toward the wound long after the wound has stopped being able to take.
