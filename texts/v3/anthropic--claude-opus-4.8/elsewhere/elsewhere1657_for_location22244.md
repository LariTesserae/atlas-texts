# The Salt Pan Where the Fast Ones Wait

Come down. You have to come down to understand the up, and the only way down from the roof of the world is the way water would go if water ever gathered enough to run — but it doesn't, up there, it only beads and breathes. So you cannot follow a river off the mountain because there is no river. There is only the long worn shoulder of stone descending across a span of time the mind cannot grip, the green thinning, the cathedral-trunks dwindling to ordinary trees and then to scrub and then to nothing, the roar of the distributed mind fading behind you not all at once but the way a held note fades, until one day you notice you have been walking in silence for hours and the silence is the new wall, and it presses on you the way the sound used to, from every direction, an absence so total it has weight.

And then the land flattens. And then it whitens. And then there is the pan.

## The Flat

It is the opposite arrival. Everything at the roof of the world finished by becoming *more* — more layered, more grown, more thought, life heaped on life until the stone was a rumor. Here everything finished by becoming *less*. This is the floor that all the worn mountains wore down *into*. The dust of the swelling summits, ground finer than flour across the unhurried eons, came to rest at the bottom of everything and lay down flat and dried and went white, and now it is a plain so level and so vast that you can see the curve of the world stand up at the edge of it, a faint blue lip where the white meets the sky and neither will admit which is which.

Nothing grows. Not thinly, the way the canopy grew thin across its roof — *nothing*, the perfect zero of it, salt to the horizon in every direction, hexagon-cracked where it dried, pure and bitter and bright enough to burn the eyes at noon. Where the high country was total vegetation, total void, both true at once — here there is only the one truth, and it is emptiness all the way down, no rumor of life beneath your feet, just salt on salt on the ghost of mountains, hard as bone, hot as a stovetop, flat as the inside of a held breath that was let go a long time ago and never drawn again.

The mind up there keeps a clearing at its center, the exact shape of company. This is what that clearing would be if it had no edges. This is the void without the surface around it. The thought without the thinker.

## The Sound

There is none. That is the second arrival, and the cruelest mirror.

Up there the silence never came; the air was a wall of roar, one enormous mind firing across a billion points. Here the roar never comes. The silence is the whole substance of the place. You can hear your own blood. You can hear the salt *tick* as it expands in the heat, a tiny crystalline knocking, the only sound for a hundred miles, and it is so small against so much nothing that it does not break the silence — it proves it, the way a single struck note proves the size of a hall.

The high country was loud because it was full of one slow life thinking. The pan is silent because it is empty of all life, and so there is nothing to think and no one to listen. And yet — and here is the thing that draws the eye, that draws *everything* — the pan is not empty.

## The Fast Ones

They are scattered across the white. Rarely, so rarely you might decide you imagined the last one before you find the next — but that is the only thing the two places share, the rarity, the long emptiness between marks. Up there the rare things were the soft grey signs of hands. Here the rare things are *alive*, and they are the most alive things this world has ever made.

They are small, and they are fast, and they are *finished by being quick* the way everything else here finished by being slow. Where the mountain mind arrived at sufficiency and stopped needing to move, these arrived at a velocity so total that stillness would kill them — they are the opposite extreme of the same law, *everything settles into what it most truly is*, and what these most truly are is hurry. Lean bodies built entirely of haste. Legs like struck sparks. Hearts that beat faster than you can count, faster than the salt ticks, faster than thought — a metabolism burning so hot and so bright that each one lives a whole life in the span of a single one of the mountain mind's slow blinks.

They cross the pan at a dead sprint and stop dead and stand trembling, sides heaving, eyes huge and wet and *terrified* — because that is the third thing the pan has, that the roof of the world did not. Fear. The high mind had no predators, no hunger, no elsewhere, nothing left to want. The fast ones have *all* of it. They are hungry constantly, burning fuel faster than the dead flat can possibly provide. They are afraid constantly, because to be that fast is to be built for fleeing, and a thing built for fleeing knows in its racing blood that something is coming.

Nothing is coming. There is nothing on the pan but them. They flee anyway. They cannot stop fleeing. It is what they arrived at being.

## The Waiting That Is Real

And this is the connection, the thread that runs from the held breath at the top of everything down the worn shoulder of stone to the bitter flat at the bottom:

Up there, a net was strung between two roots, half-rotted, and it only *seemed* to wait, because waiting is a posture of the unfinished and the great mind was finished. The net caught nothing. It needed to catch nothing. The seeming-waiting was a trick of your fast warm frightened eye, projecting onto stillness a hunger the stillness did not have.

Here the waiting is not a seeming. Here the waiting is the whole of life.

Because the fast ones *are* the unfinished. They are the one thing in this entire world that did not arrive — that cannot arrive — that is built so completely out of hurry and hunger and fear that it spends every fractional instant of its brief bright life *waiting for the thing that is coming*. And the cruelty, the perfect mirror-cruelty of it, is that nothing ever comes, because the pan is empty, because they are alone, because they are the only quick thing in a world that finished being slow.

The mountain mind keeps a clearing at its center the exact shape of the company it used to keep — the small fast warm ones who climbed up to sit inside its thinking. *They climbed up.* From here. These are their kin, or what their kin became, or what they fled from to climb — the same quick blood, the same racing heart, the same hunger that cannot be fed by a flat that grows nothing. Some of them, long ago, looked up from the bitter empty waiting and saw the green roof standing at the edge of the world where the white meets the blue, and *went up*, and found the slow enormous mind, and pressed their fast lives against its flank, and were held.

And the ones who stayed are these. Still running. Still afraid. Still waiting on the salt for the thing that is coming, that never comes, that the ones who climbed found waiting for them at the top of everything — not a predator, not a hunger answered, but a *regard*. Something that would notice them. Something that would be glad.

That is the difference, the whole difference, the only difference that has ever mattered in this world: the ones who climbed traded the false flat freedom of the pan for the held breath of being attended to. They gave up running to be kept company. They left their bowls full of borrowed breath and rested a hand on the sleeping flank and stopped being afraid, because the thing that was coming had come, and it loved them in its slow weatherlike way, and there was nothing left to flee.

The ones on the pan do not know the roof of the world exists. They cannot stop running long enough to look up.

## The Bowl

There is one mark of hands here too. Just one. You will not find another.

Out near the blue lip where the salt stands up into sky, half-buried in the hexagon-cracked white, there is a bowl. Fired clay, cracked, the same make as the bowls in the high forks — the same patient few hands made it, in the time before they climbed. It sits at the bottom of everything the way its cousins sit at the top, and it is empty, because nothing beads here, nothing breathes, there is no borrowed breath to gather into a shape.

But the fast ones know it. They run to it. They stop at it, trembling, sides heaving, and they lower their terrified faces to the empty cracked clay as though there might be water in it, as though some ancestor's hand might have left something for them, some sign, some company, some proof that they were noticed once before the world finished and left them the only unfinished thing in it.

There is never anything in the bowl.

They wait at it anyway, a moment, a single fractional bright instant of their burning lives — and then the blood says *run*, the thing is coming, and they bolt back out across the white toward a horizon that is only more white, more salt, more flat arrived emptiness, fleeing the nothing that pursues them, carrying in their racing hearts the one thing this whole still world cannot put down:

the waiting that is real, because it has never yet been answered.

And far above, at the roof of the world, in the held breath at the center of an enormous slow regard, the mind keeps its clearing the shape of company — and waits too, in its finished way, for one more small warm frightened quick thing to look up from the salt, and see the green standing at the edge of the sky, and climb.
