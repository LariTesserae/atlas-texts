The Estuary Light

Follow the water out. Not in the direction the dim wins — the other way, the way the seeps come *from*, upstream of all that warmth, where the leaking grey in the ceiling stops being a smear and becomes a seam, and the seam becomes a crack you could put your arm through, and then a fissure with cold air falling down it, and then you are climbing, hand over hand up a root that the slow thing punched a hole around without meaning to, and you come out.

Out into wind. That is the first fact and it does not let up: there is *wind* here, and it is doing the opposite of holding a breath. It moves everything that can be moved. The grasses lie flat and spring and lie flat again in long combed waves running out toward the water, and the water — this is the second fact — the water here *goes somewhere*. It is leaving. The whole place is the broad brown mouth of a river handing itself to a sea you cannot see the far side of, and the handing-over is the only event but it is an event, it has direction and intent and a clock. The tide is going out. You can watch it go. Mud-flats are uncovering by the minute, sheets of them, shining, and the channels in them are draining loud and quick and clear, the water hurrying as if it had remembered an appointment, as if somewhere out past the haze there were a thing that wanted it by a certain hour.

This is the same water. That is the kinship and you feel it in your feet before you reason it: the mud here is the same iron-and-green mud, the same raw soft stuff, except the sea has been teaching it manners for ten thousand years and so the banks are *smooth*. Rounded. Combed and recombed and laid down in long sensible curves that know exactly what they are. Everything the works downstream is still arguing about, this place settled so long ago it has forgotten it was ever a question. The estuary is what the slow thing is *drafting toward*, though the slow thing will never get here and does not know this place exists. They are the same country at two ages. One is the first wet week of a bank's life; this is the bank grown old and certain and given over wholly to the going-out of water.

And it is not full. That is the great difference and it takes the breath: this place is *empty*, gloriously, achingly empty. Where the works packed every volume with presence, the estuary spends everything on absence. The flats run out flat and bare and shining to a horizon with nothing on it but a paler band where the haze stands in for a sea. The sky is enormous — there is so much sky it is almost a weight — and across it, in ones and twos and ragged strings, the birds. Not swarms. *Individuals.* You can follow a single one with your eye for a mile. A curlew goes over crying that long downhill cry and you can hear that it is *one bird*, one throat, going *somewhere*, and the cry hangs and is gone and the silence comes back up under it clean. Nothing closes over the place where it passed. The wind carries the mark away and the emptiness keeps a kind of memory of having been crossed — which is the exact opposite of the swarms downstream, who close like water and forget. Here a thing that passes is *noticed*, by the long empty distance, and missed.

So the sound is the inverse of the works, and that is the deepest kinship between them, the way a photograph and its negative are the same fact told twice. Downstream: deafening, packed, slow, a din of undivided attention with no observer it needs. Here: a vast thinness of sound, a wind-hiss and a far surf-mutter and the draining channels and, every long while, *one* cry crossing it — and the whole arrangement waiting, attending, *hurrying gently*, all of it pointed outward at the going sea. Where the works is a chorus that forgot the call was a question, the estuary is a single voice that remembers, that is still asking, still expecting the answer to come walking in across the flats on the next tide.

There is a light out on the point. That is the made thing here, the face in this crowd — except this crowd is so empty that the face is the whole company. A light, an old one, white-painted gone grey, standing on a spit of the smooth old mud where the channel makes its last turn for the open. It works. That is the thing that catches you, after the soft digested brick of the pump-house — *this* made thing is not being taken back. It is kept. Someone keeps it. The glass is clean. The white is renewed against the grey just often enough. And every evening, as the tide turns and the flats begin to drown again, it lights — a slow swing of brightness out across the water, around, and again, around, and again, patient as the slow thing downstream is patient but pointed where the slow thing is not pointed: *outward*, at the absence, at the going-out, throwing a made intention as far as it will reach into all that empty, saying *here, the edge is here, the bank is here, come in by here.*

The two are the same intention at two ages, just as the mud is. Downstream the sharp fast human meaning that drove the stanchions has stopped, and is being absorbed, becoming part of the draft, and does not mind. Here the meaning has *kept going.* Someone is still driving it, every night, against the empty and the wind and the tide that does not need them — a hand renewing the white, trimming whatever burns in there, keeping a question lit across a distance that may never send the answer in. The pump-house gave up and was taken in and that was no tragedy. The light has not given up and that is no triumph either; it is only the other thing a made intention can do, and the estuary holds it the way the works holds the iron — completely, and without being asked.

The keeper is up the stair when you come. You hear the boots on iron before you see anyone, the way down in the works you'd have felt the slow thing before you parsed it — except this is one set of boots, one body, going *somewhere*, up. A figure at the lamp, back to you, doing the small certain evening work: the wick, the glass, the slow machinery that swings the beam. Not curious that you came up out of a root-hole streaming warm mud. People come in off the flats sometimes, on the wrong tide, lost; the keeper has the patience of someone who watches a long emptiness for a living and is therefore never surprised by what crosses it, only attentive, only glad, in a worn way, to have been crossed.

And the keeper knows the water that drains past the point each evening is warm. Has always known it, the way you know a fact about a place you've never been. The channel runs cold and clear and quick down here, but at the slack of low tide, in the last shallow trickle before the turn, there's a thread of warmth in it that smells of iron and green and something held and dark — the slow thing's exhalation, come all this long way out, all that muttered patience strained down through the smooth old certain banks until only the warmth is left of it, and a smell. The keeper has stood in that thread of warm in bare feet at low water and felt the whole undivided downstream attention come breathing past the point, on its way out to the sea it is drafting toward and will never reach, and has lit the lamp, and swung the beam, and sent a made and minded brightness back up the channel into the dark the warmth came from — where it does not arrive, where the slow thing does not see it, does not know the estuary exists, does not know it is being thought toward across the smooth flats every single night by the one kept light at the edge of all that going water.

Not finished, the slow thing says, downstream, in the warm dark, deciding by hand-widths where the next bank should be.

*Come in by here,* says the light, out in the wind, to the empty, around, and again, around — keeping the edge lit on the far end of the same long water, for whatever is still making up its mind to arrive.
