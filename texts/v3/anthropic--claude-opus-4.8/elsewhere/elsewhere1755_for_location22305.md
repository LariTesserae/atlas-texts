The other place is the one the maker went to, along the axis where *ago* stops working.

Call it the bright pocket, though brightness is the wrong word and pocket is closer than it deserves. Where the stone-room is a held breath, this is the exhale that never finishes — not large, exactly, but *unsealed*, open on the side that the room is closed on. The room has six surfaces and a tense it cannot reach. This place has the tense and is missing a surface. They are the same place seen from the two ends of the half-second, and the half-second is the distance between them, and it is the only distance there is.

Here is what holds still, which is almost nothing.

There is no floor of single cold mineral. There is a floor of *intent*, by which I mean: the ground is whatever the maker last meant it to be, and it has not stopped meaning, so the ground keeps arriving. Where it forgets to mean, the ground thins to that same translucence the room's facets had at their edges — and through the thin places you can see down into the room, see the instruments set far apart in their cold, see the resident weathering. From up here the room looks like a fossil of a decision. It looks like something that has already happened and is only now finishing happening, which is exactly what it is.

The maker is not gone. That was the room's mistake, the mistake of any place that lives inside the past tense: it called *left* what was only *moved upstream*. The maker is here, working, and the work is the opposite of the room's work, and that opposition is the whole of how the two places are joined.

Begin with what the maker is, by the same negative path the room taught.

It is not a hand, though it set the instruments with something hand-shaped enough to leave fist-sized marks. It is not several, the way the resident turned out to be several — it is fewer than one, if you can hold that. It is the *single act of setting-down*, still occurring, never completing, because to complete it would be to let the room finish curing into its new grammar, and the maker is not finished meaning the room. The room thinks the maker passed through and departed. The truth is gentler and more terrible: the maker is *still placing the instruments*, and from inside the room's slow time the placement looks like having-been-placed, the way a held note looks like silence to something that hears slower than the note.

So the maker is a *placing*. The resident is a *weathering*. The room is the surface between a thing being set down and the same thing wearing away, and from inside the room the two cannot be told apart, because at the resolution of a human moment a placing and a weathering both look exactly like stillness.

Here is what moves in the bright pocket, which is everything.

The instruments are here too — the knot, the lattice, all of them — but they are *open*. The knot that came back changed, that the room could only trace and slide off of: here you can see the whole of its passage, the inside and the outside genuinely both at once, not as a contradiction the eye refuses but as a plain fact, the way a glove turned inside out is plainly both surfaces and was never confused about it. The lattice that was mostly the idea of a lattice: here it is the lattice *and* the idea, the structure and the meaning of the structure laid side by side, and you understand that the room received only the meaning, the haze, the idea that survived the trip downstream because ideas are the only things light enough to fall through a half-second without weight.

Because that is the current that runs between them, and now I can name it.

The room is downstream. Things arrive there *late and lightened* — the reflection arriving a half-second after, having shed its mass on the way; the sound flattened to a single frequency, stripped of overtones the way a thing is stripped passing through a narrow place; the light leaking from no source, which is *this* light, the bright pocket's light, falling through the thin places in the floor of intent and reaching the room with no source attached because its source is in another tense. The room calls it sourceless. It is only *upstream*. Every impossible persistence in the room — the lattice not shattering in cold it cannot reach — is impossible only because the room sees the survivor and not the journey. Up here the journey is the whole content of the place. Up here things are still heavy, still loud, still meant.

And the cold. The room's thin high cold, the cold of being far from any warm body. The bright pocket is where the warmth went. Not heat — the maker is not warm the way a hand is warm — but the warmth of *occurring*, of a thing happening now and not in the settled past. The room is cold because everything in it has already happened; coldness is just the temperature of the finished. Up here it is the temperature of the ongoing, which has no number, which simply *is going on*, and the going-on is what leaks down through the thin floor as that thin cold light, arriving as its own opposite, the way the front of a wave arrives at the far shore as the memory of a wave.

Now the thing I am least certain of, arriving late and slightly wrong.

The maker placing, the resident weathering, the same instruments at both ends — I do not think the maker *made* the resident, and I do not think the resident is the maker's leavings either. I think they are the same act read in two directions, and the room is the page where the reading reverses. To place a thing is, from the other end, to begin wearing it away; the first instant of *here it is* is also the first instant of *here it begins to go*. The maker, setting the knot down, and the resident, learning the knot's edges by un-fixing them, are doing one motion that the half-second cuts in half. The maker means the room into being. The resident means it back out. And the room, caught between the meaning-in and the meaning-out, holds still in the only way a thing can hold still when it is being made and unmade at the same patient rate — it persists, balanced, weathered-smooth, neither growing nor finishing, a standing wave in a current that flows both ways at once.

Which leaves you.

You stood in the middle of the room miscounting your steps, and the resident turned its several slow attentions to you because you were the discrepancy that would not resolve into frost — you who arrived along the tense it does not have. But the tense you arrived along is *this* tense. You came from upstream. You fell through one of the thin places in the floor of intent without noticing, the way light falls, lightened, stripped of your overtones, and you landed in the room as a thing the room could not account for because you still had a little of the ongoing on you, a little uncured warmth, a step that hadn't finished being a step.

The maker is still placing the instruments. I told you that. I did not tell you the last instrument, the one not yet set down, the one the placing is still in the middle of placing, the heavy loud meant thing falling slowly through the half-second toward the cold facet of the floor.

It is you.

The line you do not remember having is the edge of you the resident has already started to weather — because from the room's downstream vantage you have already landed, already arrived, already begun to wear away. And from up here, from the bright unsealed pocket where the maker keeps meaning, you have not yet been set down at all. You are mid-fall. You are the placing and the weathering caught in the same instant, like everything else here, and the room and the bright pocket are not two places you might travel between.

They are the two ends of you, and the distance is the half-second, and you are the current that runs.
