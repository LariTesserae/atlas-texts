# The Cold House

Go up. Not the unmarked stair — that one only goes down, and down is taken. Find instead the iron rungs welded to the outside of the building, the ones that stop being a ladder and become a habit of climbing somewhere around the third story, and keep going past where the warmth of the chapel could ever have reached, past the makers' last intentions, up into the open frame of the structure that nobody finished, until you come out onto the roof of the world the boiler-room is buried under.

It is a tank. That is the nearest word, though no tank was ever meant to be lived in. Once it held something — you can read the old waterline as a tide-mark of mineral lace around the inside, chest-high, from when it was full. Now it holds weather. The roof of it rusted through and fell in long ago, in pieces, and the pieces lie at the bottom like the leaves of a metal tree, and through the great ragged hole the sky comes down and sits in the tank and does whatever the sky is doing that day. Rain comes in. Snow comes in and stays, in the cold months, a drift of it leaning against the curved north wall. Wind comes in and cannot get back out cleanly; it turns in the round room, looking for the way, and you hear it searching.

This is the opposite house, and it is the same house. Follow the heat that the chapel keeps so jealously — follow it up through the iron, through every pipe the dweller circulates and re-circulates and will not release — and at the very top of the system, where the makers vented the excess they could not use, the warmth finally escapes. It leaks here. It has always leaked here. The one place the keeper below could not keep. And what the keeper exhales, this place inhales: a thread of warm rising air, the chapel's single sigh, climbing the cold throat of the tank and meeting the open sky and letting go.

Everything here is given away. That is the first fact and the last one. Where the boiler-room's law was density, the law here is *release* — the wide ringing emptiness of a room that holds nothing but air and the slow traffic of the seasons. You can stand in the center and not touch anything. Your voice goes out and comes back to you off the curved wall, changed, a moment later, as though the tank were answering. Below, the dweller filled every inch and let nothing move. Here there is one object only, and it moves constantly.

It is cold, and the cold has a grain to it, the way the dark below had a grain. By day, light pours through the broken roof and pools on the rusted floor and warms it by a few degrees, and that little warmth — added to the thread always rising from below — is enough. By night the heat streams up and out through the hole and the tank empties itself of warmth into the black sky, generous, wasteful, holding nothing back, the perfect inverse of the chapel's hoarding. Frost forms on the inside walls on clear nights. It forms because the place lets the warmth go. The frost is the work, here. The frost *is* the process.

Listen. The wind, searching the round room. Rain on the metal when it rains — not a drip into standing water but a clatter, a downpour heard from inside a bell. And in the long pauses, faint, almost not there: a thin warm whisper from the floor where a single gap in the plate lets the chapel's breath up into the tank. *Thunn,* you would hear, if you pressed your ear to it — the pump, the keeper's pulse, four stories down, still going. Up here it arrives as the gentlest possible warmth against the cheek, and then the wind takes it and turns it and gives it to the sky, and it is gone, and the keeper below has lost exactly that much, forever, and does not know, because losing is the one thing the keeper cannot register.

There is water, and you do not have to find it. It falls on you. It pools and freezes and thaws and runs out through the rust-holes in long rust-colored tears down the outside of the tank — the same orange weeping you saw below the moss in the chapel, but here it streams down the whole flank of the building in the rain, the structure crying its iron out into the open air, giving even its own substance away.

Things grow, and they are greedy about it. In the seams where the rain pools and the sun reaches, there is a moss too — but green, frank, ordinary green, nothing like the patient black fur below. And grasses have got in, seeds dropped by birds, and a single hardy birch sapling has taken root in a drift of windblown soil caught in a corner, leaning toward the broken roof and the light, already taller than you, already too big for where it is. The vegetation here has every ambition. It spreads as fast as the cold allows. It wants the whole tank and will likely have it. Where the chapel's plants had negotiated their few spots and stopped, these have no treaty, no restraint, no balance — only the open invitation of light and water and the going-away of warmth, and they take it all, and reach, and reach.

Fauna: abundant, and loud about it. Birds nest on the lip of the broken roof and quarrel and come and go through the hole all day. A pair of them have made the warm gap in the floor their winter perch — they have found the chapel's leaking breath and they sit over it on the cold nights, two small bodies stealing the keeper's heat, the keeper four floors of iron away, attending forever to its hoard, never to know that the very top of its sealed and self-tending world is a chimney, and that small lives huddle at the mouth of it to be warm. Insects in summer. Once, a fox, somehow, that climbed what could not be climbed and looked at the drop and chose not to. They are many. The place feeds many, and feeds them by giving, by losing, by the constant downhill spending of everything that arrives.

Here is the thing that makes it more than a ruined tank. It, too, is mid-process — but its process is the chapel's process run backwards and out into the open. Where the keeper distills the *essence of staying*, refines persistence down to its purest unmoving drop, this place distills the essence of *passing-through.* Nothing stays. Warmth arrives and departs. Water arrives and departs. Light arrives and departs with the turning day. Seeds arrive, grow, die, rot, feed the next. Every single thing that enters this room is on its way to leaving, and the room does nothing to stop it — the room *is* the not-stopping, the wide cold welcome that takes everything in and lets everything go, and asks for none of it back. If the keeper below is happiness as one held note, this is happiness as the whole song, every note dying the instant it sounds so the next can come.

They are the same enterprise. You understand that, standing here, with the wind turning around you and the thread of stolen warmth at your feet. The makers built one machine, top to bottom — a thing meant to take in the slope of the world and *do* something with it, and the keeper below is its grip and this tank is its open hand, and between them, all up the dark iron throat of the building, runs the one long current: heat gathered, hoarded, refined, and at last, here, at the top, released. The keeper could not exist without the vent. A thing that only keeps, with nowhere to spill, would burst or stall or cook itself to ruin. This cold open room is what makes the warm sealed one possible. The chapel hoards *because* the tank gives away. The tank gives away *because* the chapel hoards. They have never met. They are one breath — the in below, the out above — and the building breathes it slow, forever, four stories long, one stroke every several seconds, the pump that is the pulse of both.

And the smell of it: rain on hot rust, cold air with the iron in it, green things, bird-mess, the clean nothing of high wind — and under all of it, faint, rising through the gap, a sweetish warmth like the inside of a working thing. The chapel's smell. The keeper's breath. You only notice it when the wind drops. Then it is unmistakable: the same blood-warm mineral note from far below, arrived at the top of its long climb, met by the cold, and let go.

The scale stays large, the way the chapel's stayed small. You could not cross it in a minute; the wind would turn you and the broken floor would catch you and the birch would be in your way. But it has edges too — the curved walls, the lip of the roof, the drop on every side. It is one feature, one room, one held breath of the same enterprise, breathing out where the other breathes in. Beyond its walls there is the open air, the rest of whatever the world is, the changed light at the top of the worn stair come round at last to the sky. And here you *do* get there. That is the difference. The chapel sealed itself and you could go no further. This place is nothing but further — open on top, leaking at the bottom, spending itself in every direction, a room made entirely of ways out.

You can stay as long as you can bear the cold, which is the exact mirror of how long you could bear the heat. The wind will keep time for you, less patient than the drip, more honest about how it passes. Everything will change while you watch — that is its nature; the change is the whole of it, the light moving, the cloud crossing the hole, the birds in and out, the frost forming as the day cools. When you climb back down the iron rungs toward the building's warm and secret heart, the cold will follow you partway down and then let go, and up here the open roof will go on giving the sky everything it has, generous and ruined and full of small loud lives, the one open mouth of a place that decided, long ago, simply to let go — while four floors beneath it, in the dark, behind the plate, the keeper holds the other end of the same long breath, content, attending forever to its hoard, and the warmth climbs the iron between them, kept and kept and kept, and then, at the very last, at the top, in the cold, released.
