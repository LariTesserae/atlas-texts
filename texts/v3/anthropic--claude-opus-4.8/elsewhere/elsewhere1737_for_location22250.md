The valley where the river is born.

Follow the water down. Not the standing water — the standing water goes nowhere, that is its whole nature — but the other water, the weeping water, the films that slide down the basin's walls and gather at the low center. The pool never rises and never falls, which means that everything the walls give it, the pool gives away. Somewhere beneath the smoothed stone there is a seam, a fault worn fine as a hair, and through it the basin sheds exactly what it takes, drop by unhurried drop, into the dark inside the mountain. That thread of water has a long way to go. It goes down through the bone of the high country, through rock that has never been wet by rain, and it comes out — far below, on the southern flank, where the slope finally relents — as a seep in a bed of cracked clay. And from the seep, a trickle. And from the trickle, after it has gathered its brothers off a dozen other unseen slopes, a river.

This is the valley where the river is born, and it is large, because that is the first thing the body would understand: this is a vast place, and it will not be crossed in forty paces, and it will not hold still long enough to be measured.

It is loud. Begin there, because the basin was built around silence and this place is built around the opposite. The young river comes off the mountain shouting. Where it is still steep the water throws itself white over ledges and slams into pools and climbs out of them roaring, and the noise fills the whole upper valley wall to wall so that two people standing close must lean and shout to be heard. There is no faint rain that is only the sum of small movements here. There is the river, and the river is enormous, and it has a voice that never stops and never repeats, a sound that is a thousand sounds — the deep tone of the main fall, the chatter of the shallows, the suck and knock of stones rolling on the bottom, the hiss of spray flung off into the wind. It is the same water that fell drop by silent drop into a black pool in the dark. It has learned to be heard.

And it is bright. This is the second thing. The sun comes into this valley and stays the whole day, swinging down the southern wall in the morning and along the river's length by noon, and everything answers it. The water is not black here but every color the sky lends it, white where it breaks, green in the deep pools, gold in the slack water at evening, and the spray throws rainbows that come and go as the wind decides. After the eyes of the basin, eyes that learned to confirm rather than see, this light would be a wound. There is too much of it. It picks out every color the dark withheld — the red of the wet rock, the hard blue of the sky in the river's calm reaches, the green going up the slopes in every shade a leaf can hold.

For things grow here too, and this is where the two places turn to face each other.

The basin was packed; nothing advanced. The valley is also full, but its fullness is the other kind, the kind that climbs and seeks and spills and dies back and surges again. The slopes are forest where they are not meadow and meadow where they are not scree, and all of it moves with the season — the valley *has* seasons, that is the engine of it, the snow that loads the high country in winter and lets go in the spring and sends the river up over its banks in a brown rage that tears whole trees out by the root and rolls boulders the size of huts down the bed with a sound like the mountain clearing its throat. Then the flood falls and the river finds a new channel, having abandoned the old one, and where the old channel lay there is fresh gravel, and into the gravel the first green things come within the season, racing, because here everything races. Here the only way to hold a place is to keep taking it. The willow that does not grow toward the light is shaded out and dies. The pool that does not seek its level overflows and is gone. Everything moves and everything changes, and the change is the point, the change is what the place is *for*, and a thing arrived at its final form would not last a single spring.

The fauna is loud and large and dies in great numbers and is born in great numbers and none of it is quiet. The fish come up the river to spawn and die spawning, their bodies feeding the bears that come down to the shallows and the birds that wheel and scream over the riffles. There are voices everywhere — the birds have them, the frogs in the back-eddies have them, the elk in the meadows have them, and they use them, constantly, to claim and to warn and to call across distance, because here there is distance, here things approach that must be named before they arrive. The whole valley is a din of naming. Nothing here is known by nearness. Everything must be announced, because everything is coming or going, and the going and the coming never stop.

And there are people. Not the small pale stilled ones — the others, the ones who never went up. They have a camp on the high terrace above the spring floods, hides stretched over bent poles, fires that they feed and let die and feed again. They knap stone too. This is the thread that runs all the way up: the same hand, the same craft, the broken blade by the black pool and the whole blades here in the firelight, struck fresh and used and resharpened and used again until they are spent and thrown away and replaced. Here the making never stops. Here a tool is a thing on its way to being another tool, and a child learns the craft watching a parent's hands, and the craft itself drifts and changes over the generations the way the river changes channel — new ways of striking, new shapes for new needs, the whole restless advance that the basin laid down and walked away from.

They have a story, these people, that they tell at the fire when the river is loud, about a place up at the top of the world where the water is born in the dark and where the old ones went — the ones who got tired of the racing, the spawning-and-dying, the floods and the namings and the endless resharpening of blades. They went up to find the place where nothing has to be claimed because nothing is coming, where you can set the blade down half-finished and never need to finish it, where the dark holds you and asks nothing back. The story says they are still up there. The story says they did not die so much as *stop*. Some of the young ones, hearing it, look up the dark valley toward the high country and feel the pull of it, the great longing for an end to the racing — and the old ones watch them look, and say nothing, and feed the fire.

For the people of the valley know the secret the basin does not: that the water comes down. That stillness up there is bought with motion down here. The black pool never rises because the river carries away everything it takes, and the river is born because the basin sheds, and the whole roaring living dying valley is in some sense the *exhalation* of that one held breath at the top of the mountain. The stillness and the motion are the same water. The completed thing and the unfinished thing are the same craft. The ones who stopped and the ones who never stop drink, finally, from the same cup — it is only that one drinks at the source in the dark and asks for nothing more, and the others drink a thousand miles of it downstream in the light and ask for everything, and neither will ever climb to see the other, and the water does not care, the water only goes down, drop by silent drop into the loud bright valley that does not know it is the answer to a question asked in the dark above it, and would not stop to listen if it did.
