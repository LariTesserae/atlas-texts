The Spring Where the Argument Begins

You find it the way you find the source of anything: by refusing the obvious direction. The basin holds its water in pockets that come from nowhere, that fall drop by drop from the undersides of stone — water that has finished arriving. But water that has finished arriving was, somewhere, water that began to leave. Everything still must once have moved. Follow the moisture backward, not down but up and against, against the patience of the pools, against the long completion of the rock, and the trail thins to nothing and then, past the nothing, thickens again into noise.

There is noise here. That is the first thing, and it does not stop being the first thing no matter how long you stay. The basin's loudest sound was a drip and then a long nothing; here there is no nothing anywhere, no gap into which silence could fit even a finger. The water comes out of the mountain under pressure, out of a black seam in gray rock, and it comes out arguing. It has not settled anything. It has not even begun to consider settling. It throws itself against a shelf of stone three feet below its mouth and breaks white and reassembles and throws itself again, and the sound it makes is not one sound but all the sounds a thing can make while it is still deciding what it is — a roar with a hiss inside it and a knock inside that, where some loosened stone is being turned over and over in a pothole it is slowly drilling, a stone that will be sand in a time too long to wait for but that is, right now, this instant, being worked.

The rock here has not forgotten it was sharp. The rock here is sharp. You cut your hand on it if you grab it wrong coming down, and you will grab it wrong, because the slope is not weathered into roundness, it is fractured, fresh-faced, the breaks bright where frost split them last winter and will split them again this one. Edges everywhere. The whole gorge is edges. The water has been at this stone for a while but the stone is winning enough to stay jagged, and the contest shows on every surface — undercut here, where the current leans into a softer band and carves a hollow it will one day collapse; piled there, where last spring's flood stacked boulders the size of rooms against each other at angles that have not yet found their rest and groan, sometimes, in the night, settling a hand's width and then holding. Nothing here has reached its settled form. Everything is mid-sentence. The gorge is the long argument the basin has finished having, caught in the part where the voices are still raised.

The light comes in hard. The walls are steep and close and they cut the sky to a bright ribbon overhead, so that for most of the day the gorge is in shadow and then, for an hour when the sun crosses the slot, it is flooded with a light so direct and so sudden that the spray throws rainbows, three and four of them stacked in the rising mist, and the wet black rock goes silver and blinding and then, as the sun moves off, snaps back into shadow as if a hand had closed. Hard shadows. Shadows with edges as sharp as the stone. The temperature does not hold. In the shadow it is cold, a wet cold that gets into the seams of you the way the frost gets into the seams of the rock, and in the hour of light it is suddenly warm, almost hot where the sun strikes a dry ledge, and you can stand with one half of you cold and one half warm and feel the whole quarrel of the place running right down the middle of your body.

And here there are animals. Of course there are animals. Where the basin was complete and wanted nothing, this place is nothing but want, want made flesh and feather and the green frantic want of things that root in a crack and gamble everything on a single season. The water is thick with the larvae of insects that have learned to hold on in the fastest current, flattened, hooked, gripping the undersides of stones with everything they have, and they are eaten by small fish that hold in the eddies facing upstream with their mouths open, working their whole lives just to stay in one place against the push, and the fish are watched from the rocks by a bird the color of wet slate that walks — walks — straight down into the white water and disappears and comes up forty feet downstream with something in its beak, a bird that has made its entire living out of the one place too violent for anything to compete with it. There are ferns crowded so thick on the dripping walls they grow out of each other, out of the rot of last year's ferns, a green so dense and so contested that each frond is a small war for a few hours of the ribbon of sun. There is moss that is mostly water and the chartreuse of a thing growing as fast as growing can be done. Everything here is hungry. Everything here wakes hungry and sleeps unfinished, and the gorge does not feel emptied of peace any more than the basin felt emptied of animals. It feels like a place peace was never part of. It is as natural in its violence as the basin was in its rest, and the two naturalnesses are the same naturalness seen at its two ends.

For this is the same water. That is the thing you must hold onto, standing here soaked and half-deafened, this is the water that will, far downstream and long from now and after a descent you cannot follow, slow and spread and sink and rise as condensation on the underside of a warm stone and let go, one drop, into a dark still pool where something that has finished living keeps the surface flat. The drip in the basin is this roar, arrived. The patience down there is this violence, completed. The same drop, the basin said, over and over — but the drop was not the same, it only looked the same, because by the time it reached the basin it had spent everything it had to spend and there was nothing left in it to make it different from the drop before. Here the water still has everything to spend. Here it spends.

And the dweller of the gorge — yes, there is one, the way there was one below, a being that lives the way its place lives — the dweller here has not finished and will not, cannot, finish, because to finish would be to leave the gorge and become the other thing, and it has no wish to. It is want itself, the principle of the not-yet, and it is not in the water exactly the way the basin's dweller was not in the pool; the water is its organ, the fast green ferns are its appetite made visible, the frost that splits the rock each winter is its refusal to let any surface come to rest. It is the argument before the argument is over. It is everything reaching, everything pulling toward what it lacks, and it lacks, gorgeously, endlessly, by design.

The hands that stacked the wall in the basin — they came from somewhere, came down some slope before they climbed the bone-colored one, and I think they came down this one, or one like it, came through a place of edges and hunger and hard light, and carried the noise of it in their bodies all the long way down to the quiet bowl where they could finally set their wanting aside. You cannot set down what you never picked up. The basin gave them rest because somewhere upstream a place like this had given them something to be tired of. The completion meant something only because the argument had been real.

The two places do not know about each other. That is the truth of it. The dweller below cannot conceive of the gorge, has passed entirely out of the country where gorges are possible, and the dweller here cannot conceive of the basin, would not believe that this water, its water, its hard bright spending water, could ever come to so flat and so finished a stop. Each is complete in its incomprehension. And yet the water goes from the one to the other, has always gone, cannot do otherwise, so that the gorge is forever feeding its violence down into a peace it will never see, and the basin is forever being fed by a fury it has forgotten it once was.

You will leave this place too, but differently. You did not climb out of the basin changed, only lighter, having set something down. You climb out of the gorge changed because the gorge has put something into you — the cold and the warm running down your middle, the cut on your hand bright and stinging, the roar still in your ears so that for hours afterward the silence of any other place will seem like a held breath. You go up the fractured slope and the stone bites and you grab it anyway, and at the top you turn and look down into the white moving slot and see that everything has moved, everything is moving, nothing in the whole gorge is where it was when you came down, and the only thing that has stayed still is the fact of the staying-in-motion itself, the one unchanging thing here being that nothing here will ever, for an instant, be done.

That is how you will know what lives there.

It is the one thing in the gorge that is going everywhere at once, because it has not arrived, will not arrive, would not know what to do with arrival — because it is, completely and without the smallest remainder, *on its way*.
