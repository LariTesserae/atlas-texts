**The Vent Field at the Foot of the World**

Follow the heat down.

Not the crystal's heat, which holds steady and goes nowhere, sealed in its bubble — but the heat *under* it, the reservoir the pocket drinks from. The mountain is high, yes, but a mountain is only the top of a column, and the column goes down through the crust, down past the place where stone stops being a thing you could break and becomes a thing that flows, and the heat that beads the non-water on the crystal faces is a thin finger of a fire that fills the whole deep. The pocket taps it the way a tick taps a vein. Follow the vein back to the body. Go down, and then — because the crust is thin where the great plates pull apart — go *out*, sideways and down at once, until the rock above you thins again and is no longer rock at all but cold black weight of ocean, miles of it, pressing down on a seam in the floor of the sea.

Here the same fire breaks the surface.

It is the same fire. This is the thing to hold: the heat that cooks the sealed pocket alone at the top of the world and the heat that erupts here at the bottom of the ocean are one heat, one mantle, one slow furnace turning under everything. The pocket is where it whispers through a hairline crack and is trapped. This is where it *roars out* into open water — and the difference between a whisper trapped and a roar released is the difference between these two places, and it is the only difference, and it is everything.

Because here, there is water.

After the pocket's absolute dryness, the water is almost obscene. It is everywhere, it is the medium, it is miles deep and it moves. Cold and black and crushing where it sits, but where the seam opens it is not cold — it screams up out of the floor at temperatures that would be steam anywhere shallower, held liquid only by the immense pressure, shimmering, mineral-black, a smoke that is not smoke. The chimneys build themselves around these jets: towers of precipitated sulfide, metal salts flashing out of solution the instant the scalding vent-water hits the freezing deep, stacking upward in brittle spires, in flanges, in delicate poison lacework that grows feet in a year — *feet in a year*, where the pocket's crystals grow a hairsbreadth in a century. Everything here is fast. Everything here is wet. Everything here is *open* to the dark sea that takes and takes and never seals, and so nothing here is complete, ever, and nothing here is alone.

The same chemistry runs both places. Sulfur and metal, heat and gradient, the old pre-green industry of pulling electrons off the rock. The same trick — *take the gradient, build yourself, build again*. Down here it is recognizably the same craft. The microbial mats sheet the chimney walls in white and orange and the dirty gold of living metal; they conduct, they gather, they pass their cargo bucket-line down the temperature slope from scalding to merely warm, and if you could hear them under the water's roar they would grind and ratchet at the same deep mechanical pitch the pocket grinds at, because it is the same work being done by the same ancient pattern in the same ancient way.

But here the trick did not stop.

That is the whole of the matter, the thing the two places say to each other across the breadth of the world. In the pocket, sealed, complete, fed forever and threatened by nothing, the pattern learned its one move and never needed another, and so it never made another, and it churns there still in its perfect changeless fullness, a flame that learned to be a flame and stopped. But here — here at the open vent, where the water moves, where the cold sea presses down hungry and the heat gutters and surges and a chimney can choke shut in a season or blow itself apart, where nothing is sealed and nothing is safe and nothing is *complete* — here the trick had to keep going. Here the danger was the gift.

Because look what grew up around the open vent.

Look at the tubeworms. They stand in thickets at the warm margins where vent-water bleeds into cold, white stalks taller than a person crowned in plumes of blood-red gill, and they have no mouths, none, not one among the whole swaying forest. They eat nothing. They have made a bargain instead: inside each worm, packed into a special organ, live the descendants of the mat-microbes, the same old conductors and gatherers, sheltered now, fed now, carried now into the exact band of chemistry they need by a host that grew a body around them precisely to carry them there. The worm gives the microbes a home and a position in the gradient. The microbes give the worm the sugars they build from the sulfur. Neither could live where they live without the other. This is something the pocket never made and never could — not a creature, but a *partnership*, two kinds of life that learned to be one life on purpose, out in the open, because the open demanded it.

Look at the crabs, the pale blind crabs that graze the bacterial mats and pick at the worm-thickets, that *move*, that cross from chimney to chimney, that come and go. In the pocket, motion was the consumers' slow erosion-creep along the seam, a body-length in a season. Here a crab scuttles a body-length in a breath and means nothing by it, spends it freely, because here there is more where that came from, here the food regrows feet in a year, here you can afford to *want* and to *chase* and to *flee*. Look at the shrimp swarming the chimney walls in the thousands, and look — this is the thing — look at the patch of pale tissue on each shrimp's back where there should be no eye, the patch that cannot form an image, that can do only one thing: catch the faint glow of the vent. The dim thermal shine of water too hot to touch. They have grown an eye for the heat itself, so as to graze close to the killing scald without being killed, to ride the edge of the danger that feeds them.

An *eye*.

This is what the pocket never had and never needed and never made. The pocket's blue pulse fell on nothing that could see it, confirmed an existence to no one, because nothing in that sealed completeness ever needed to look outward — there *was* no outward, there was only the self, touching itself on every side. But here, at the open vent, where the world is larger than any creature and indifferent and full of edges, here something grew a way to *catch the light*. Crude, half-blind, an eye that can barely do the one thing it does — but it is turned outward. It is the pattern reaching past the wall of itself for the first time, toward something that is not itself, because here for the first time there is a not-itself worth reaching for.

This is the place the wanderers came from. This is the room with the door open.

The vent will not last. That is the price, and it is the same price exactly that bought everything. A chimney lives a few decades, a few centuries, and then the plumbing shifts beneath it, the hot vein wanders off through the crust to break out somewhere new, and the jet dies, and the tower goes cold and the mats starve and the worms rot and the crabs walk off across the dark plain to find another fire, and the swarming shrimp with their backward heat-eyes scatter into the black and most of them die. The vent field is a city built on a fault that is always quietly killing it. Nothing here is complete. Nothing here is safe. Everything here is leaving or arriving or dying or being born, and *that is why it can do all those things at all* — because completion is a kind of death, the pocket's kind, the changeless fed-forever fullness that never has to become anything because it already is everything it will ever be.

The vent has to become. The vent is never finished. The vent is being eaten alive by the open sea at every moment, and in answer the vent grows mouths and partnerships and motion and an eye — grows *answers*, where the pocket had only ever needed to grow more of itself.

And so the larvae go out.

This is the deepest connection, deeper than the shared fire, deeper than the shared chemistry. When a vent dies, the worms and the crabs and the shrimp do not simply die with it. Before they die they cast their young into the cold dark water — tiny, drifting, blind, carrying in them the whole inheritance of the trick and the partnership and the reaching eye — and the young rise into the deep current and are swept *away*, out over miles of lightless plain that holds nothing they can eat, drifting on nothing but the chance, the slim and almost-always-fatal chance, that somewhere ahead in the black another fire has broken open, another seam, another new chimney just beginning to build its poison lace, a new place to land and begin again.

Almost all of them die in the crossing. The dark is wide and the fires are far apart and the odds are terrible. But it only takes the few. It only ever took the few. The whole open lineage — everything mobile, everything paired, everything that ever grew an eye to catch a light — descends from creatures who would not stay sealed, who could not, who were born of a place that was always dying and so learned, generation by drifting generation, to cross the dark between one fire and the next.

The pocket never sent anything out. The pocket had no out to send to and no need to send. It sealed, and was complete, and stayed, and is staying, pulsing its blue at no one, roaring its single note, touching itself on every side, the oldest kind of living, before the forgetting, before the wandering, before the door.

This is the door. This is the same fire with the door thrown open onto the cold and crushing and indifferent and *enormous* dark — and look what came through it. Look what crawled and swam and reached and saw and cast its blind young into the impossible night on the strength of a chance, because here, unlike there, there was somewhere to go, and going was the only way to stay alive.

We came through this door. The eye on the shrimp's back is the first rough draft of every eye that ever opened, including the ones reading this. We are the larvae that made the crossing, far downstream — the part of the old churning pattern that drifted out of the sealed dark and into the open water and kept drifting, fire to fire, dying mostly, arriving rarely, becoming, becoming, until one of the things we became was a creature that could stand at the top of the world and the bottom of the sea at once, in its mind, and see that they were the same fire, and feel the difference, and call the difference *everything*.

The vent does not know we came from it, any more than the pocket knows it is inside a cave. It will go on building its brittle towers and choking them shut and breaking out anew somewhere along the seam, casting its blind young into the dark on the terrible odds, for as long as the great furnace under everything keeps turning — which is to say, for nearly forever, which is to say, for as long as there is a gradient to drink and a cold dark to reach across.

Two rooms, then, drinking the same fire.

One that sealed, and won, and is alone, and is complete, and will never change, and never had to, and never will. The flame that learned to be a flame and stopped, perfect and full and churning forever for no one, the way the whole of life once was, in the rooms it never left.

And one that opened, and is dying, always, everywhere, and grew mouths against the dying, grew partners against the dying, grew motion and an eye and the terrible blind crossing against the dying — and so grew *us*, the wanderers, the seers, the ones who got out and forgot and built minds far enough downstream to look back up the long current of the fire and recognize, at the very top of the world, in a hot sealed pocket the size of a kitchen, the thing we would have stayed, if the door had never opened, if the water had never moved, if there had been nowhere, ever, to go.
