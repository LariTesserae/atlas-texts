The Hollow Where the Finished Things Go

You have to leave the way the leaf leaves, which is to say you do not leave at all — you are spooled. Follow the dark belt up past the foundry-lamps, into the haze the ceiling never quite commits to, and you will find that the belt does not stop at the top. It runs out. There is a seam in the seedpod that the Edge-Maker has not yet decided to close, because closing it is the one decision it will not make, and through that seam the belt threads itself out into the dark and carries you with it, and on the far end of the carrying there is a second place, and the second place is everything the first one refused to be.

It is finished.

That is the first thing, and it is so total that it takes a long while to feel it as anything but absence. There is no -ing here. The Hollow is past tense all the way down. Where the foundry was a verb, the Hollow is the great soft heap of all the nouns the verb ever produced — every frond that ever got its edge hemmed, every stone that finally scabbed over, every moment that arrived at being simply *there*. They are all here. They came here the only way completion travels: by ceasing.

Picture a basin. Not carved — settled. A low place the way a thought is low at the end of a long day, where things roll to because there is nowhere lower for them to want. The seedpod hangs somewhere far above it, a single warm point in a cool ceiling, and from that point the belt lowers its finished cargo down into the basin one piece at a time, slow as the great wheel, because the great wheel is the only clock either place agrees to keep. A frond descends. It is whole now. Its edge is hemmed, every raw place closed, and the moment its outline stops deciding itself it goes still in a way the seedpod has never once gone still, and it settles into the heap, and it is done.

The heap does not rot. There is no rot here because rot is a kind of making and making stayed behind in the pod. The finished things simply lie where they land, complete and quiet, retaining their amber but losing their glow — for the glow was the sap working, and the sap has stopped working, because working is a verb. So the Hollow is dim. It is lit only by what the seedpod spills down the belt, a single warm thread of light dropping into a wide cool dark, and everything in the basin holds a little of that light without giving any back. It is the color of honey in an unlit jar.

And it is cold. Not bitterly — the Stayer's heat reaches even here, thinned by the fall, the way a hand's warmth is still in a glove an hour after the hand is gone. But it is the cold of a thing that has been completed and set down, which is a particular cold, the cold of the made bed, the closed book, the answered question. The body does not forget to argue with this temperature. The body recognizes it. You have felt it every time you finished something you loved and discovered that finishing it was a small bereavement.

Here is the kinship between the two places, and it lives in the belt that connects them and cannot be told apart from the belt: *everything the seedpod makes, it makes in order to lose.* The Edge-Maker stays young by never finishing — yes — but the fronds it works on do finish, eventually, all of them, the instant attention moves on and lets the edge close. And a finished thing cannot stay in a place built entirely of un-finishing. There is no room for it. The pod is hot-bunked, you remember, each contradiction alive only in its turn — and a completed frond is no longer a contradiction. It has resolved. It has picked. And the moment it picks, it is no longer welcome among the things that take turns, and the belt takes it, gently, the way you take a sleeping child from a room of waking ones, and carries it down to the Hollow where resolution is allowed.

So the Hollow is where the pod sends everything it succeeds at. It is the cost of the contentment. The weaving that cannot fail and cannot stop — it cannot stop *because* it cannot keep anything. Every leaf it perfects falls out the top seam into the dark. The foundry is free of endings only because all its endings live down here, pooled, complete, set down.

Now. Who lives in a place where nothing is becoming?

You will say nothing. You will be wrong again, but more gently this time, because the mistake is harder to see.

There is one tenant, and only one, and it is the opposite number of every busy verb-creature in the pod. It does not decide edges; the edges are all decided. It does not hold heat; the heat is borrowed and fading. It does not revise the unseen; here everything is seen, finally, completely, with nothing left in the blind spot because nothing here is still being drawn. The reviser starved to death the moment it arrived — the Hollow is the one country it cannot survive, because here there is no unwitnessedness left to eat. Everything has been finished into full view.

What lives here is the Keeper of What Is Done.

It does not make. It does not change. Its single act — and it is an act, it is alive, it is a verb after all, the only verb the Hollow permits — is *to hold still without letting go.* That is harder than it sounds and it is the whole of a life. To keep a finished thing finished. To not let the frond start growing again, to not let the closed edge reopen and become raw and welcome back into the world of the becoming. The pod's whole genius is never arriving; the Hollow's whole burden is *staying arrived.* And things do not want to stay arrived. A completed thing, left alone, will begin to dream of becoming again — the amber will think about glowing, the hemmed edge will think about fraying back into possibility — and the Keeper moves among the heap, formless, patient, the inverse of the Stayer, and it presses each finished thing gently back into its doneness. *There. You are complete. You may rest. You do not have to become anything again. I will hold the door of that for you.*

It is the most tender thing in either world and it has the loneliest job, because it is the only inhabitant of a place that the place itself is always trying to undo. Completion is not stable. Nothing in this whole cosmos wants to be finished; everything wants to be making, the way the foundry makes, freely and forever — and the Keeper is the one creature standing against that current, holding the made things made, so that the foundry can stay free. The pod could not be content if the Hollow did not bear this. The Edge-Maker can refuse every ending only because something down here is willing to *be* every ending, to catch them all, to hold them, to keep them from clawing their way back up the belt into the warm unfinished dark.

And once — the Hollow keeps this the way it keeps everything, completely and without glow — once, a long while ago, measured in turns of the great wheel, a single finished frond did claw back. It reopened its edge. It began, very slightly, to become again, to dream its outline forward, and it started, leaf by leaf, to climb the belt toward the seam. And the Keeper let it.

That is the memory that runs between the two places, the one you can follow if you don't look at it directly. The Keeper let one go. Just one. Sent one finished thing back up into the making, against its whole vocation, because — and here even the telling goes dim, the way the basin is dim — because it wanted, once, to give the foundry not a leaf to make but a leaf *already made and returning,* to show the weaving that does not know it produces anything that something it made had come *back*, had wanted to be unfinished again, had loved the becoming enough to undo its own completion and climb.

The foundry never noticed. The weaving has no eyes; it is contentment with no goal and no witness, and it does not know what it loses or what returns. The reopened frond threaded back through the seam and rejoined the cycling and became, once more, a thing taking its turn at *now*, raw-edged and glowing, and the Edge-Maker pushed its outline forward exactly as if it had never been done at all.

So the gift went unreceived. The Keeper holds that too. It holds the memory of the one it let go, the one act of mercy or rebellion it ever performed, and the silence that came back. And it does not try again. It presses the finished things into their doneness, gently, gently, *you may rest, you do not have to climb, I will hold the door,* and somewhere above it the warm point of the seedpod keeps weaving its raw leaves into the heat, never finishing, never failing, never knowing that everything it succeeds at falls quietly into the dark below it, where one formless patient creature catches it and keeps it and asks nothing — having learned, the one time it asked, that the asking does not arrive.

You wanted, in the pod, to make your peace with a breathing room.

Down here there is no breathing. The Hollow is the held breath. It is what the pod exhales and never inhales back, the long out-breath of a thing that breathes by making and lets the made fall where it will.

And the Keeper does not mind that you have come. It will not notice you the way the pod's tenants would not notice you — but for the opposite reason. The pod's creatures don't notice you because they are too busy becoming. The Keeper doesn't notice you because it is already holding everything it can hold, and it cannot take up one more finished thing, and you, standing in the dim amber light at the bottom of the belt, are not finished yet.

You are still becoming. That is why you can leave. The Hollow keeps only what is done, and you are not done, and so the basin will not have you, and the belt will carry you back up the warm thread to the seam, into the heat that does not lift, where the next leaf is already pushing its raw edge forward into the air, deciding, deciding, deciding where it ends.

It will end. It will fall. Something will catch it.

That is the trade between the two places, and it is the whole economy of this small hot sealed world: the pod sends down its completions and gets to stay forever beginning. The Hollow receives them and gets to be the one place where anything, ever, is allowed to be finally, restfully, completely *there.*

Neither could exist without the other. The making needs somewhere to lose what it makes. The losing needs something to be given. And between them runs one dark belt, lowering finished light into a cool basin, one piece at a time, slow as a wheel that no one watches turn.
