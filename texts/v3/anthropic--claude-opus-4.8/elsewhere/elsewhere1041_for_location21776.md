**The Reading-Floor**

There is a place where the marks are kept that do not get smoothed away, and you would never find it on your own, because it is not built and not marked and not, in any way you could point to, *there.* It is a fact about the forest rather than a location in it. But the makers go to it, and they go the way water goes — downward, by the path the leaves have already decided, handed level by level toward the lowest place the tangle allows.

Begin again with the heat, because here the heat reverses its argument. Everywhere else the warmth came up from the young ground and down from the unseen canopy and met you in the middle, and you lived in the warm seam between two warmths. But the floor is deep here, deeper than your reaching ever went above, and the deeper you are handed the cooler it gets, the young convulsing ground left behind and above you, until you arrive somewhere the heat has finally finished. The stone down here is old. You can feel it the way you felt the young stone up there — by its opposite. The edges are dull. The split ridges have been rounded by something, by the ten thousand seasons of dripping that never reached the surface but always, always reaches here, the slow machine of the leaves passing every drop downward until it has nowhere left to go but this.

So the water *pools.* This is the thing the upper world cannot do and this place does. The heat that kept all that moisture moving up above gives out down here, and the water that was handed down drop by drop through every tier arrives at last and *stops,* and lies still, and is allowed — for the first time in the whole length of its falling — to simply be water in one place. There is a body of it down here. Black, flat, warm only with the memory of the warmth it lost on the way down, so still that the rare drip arriving from the last leaf overhead sends a ring across it that travels uninterrupted to the far edge, because nothing here moves to break it. You have not felt stillness since you arrived in this world. You did not know it was missing until you find it, and finding it you understand that everything above was *turbulence,* and this is what the turbulence was draining toward all along.

And it is silent. Or — it is as silent as that world can make. The wall of voices does not reach down here whole; it arrives the way the water arrives, handed down through the layers, losing a register at every tier, the insect-drone strained out first, then the high cries, then the clicks, until what reaches the still water is only the deepest thing, the throat-pulses of the great ones overhead and the low collective hum that might be wings and might be the ground, now so far up that it is felt more than heard, a pressure on the surface of the pool that lays the faintest tremor across the black water and is gone. The deafening place has a floor, and the floor is quiet, and the quiet is not empty — it is *full of everything that fell.*

This is where they bring the marks that must not be smoothed away.

Up above, you understood their writing as a current thing — the notch that says *the way bears left, or did,* the line of stones still warm with how recently the kin passed, the whole grammar kept deliberately fresh, the stale marks wiped so the living ones can be read. It is a language with no past tense, because the place above has no past; it eats its own yesterdays. A mark that is not maintained is a lie within the hour, and they do not suffer lies, and so they let almost everything go.

But not everything *can* be let go. Some things are true no matter what the forest does to itself — that this kin lived, that this hand held that hand since it was small, that a path was found through a season of collapse and the finding of it cost something, that the great rolling thought swept the colony-wall on a certain day and someone who is gone now paused and let it pass through them. These are not *the way bears left.* These are the things that are still true after the corridor closes forever. And there is no way to keep a true thing in a world that unmakes itself — except to carry it down to the one place the unmaking does not reach. Down to the cool, to the dull old stone, to the still black water. Down to where the heat has finished and nothing grows fast enough to bury anything and the drip arrives too slow to wear a mark away in any span a maker would live to see.

So along the rounded stone at the water's edge, if you felt — and you would feel, because you have learned this much, your clumsy hand has been taught by all the marks above that failed to mean anything to you in time — you would find the deep marks. Not set into vine, which rots. Not chipped into young stone, which splits and is gone. Cut into the old stone, the finished stone, the stone that has already become everything it is going to be. Cut deep, and cut once, and never smoothed, because down here nothing needs to be kept current — down here the truth does not change, and so the writing does not change, and so a hand that runs along this edge runs along not the *current understanding* but the whole long memory of a people who own nothing and hold only knowing, and who have found, at the bottom of their turbulent world, the single place that will hold still long enough to be remembered in.

They come down rarely. The descent is slow even for them — handhold by handhold down through the cooling tiers, committing nothing, reading the changed path each time because the path down is as turbulent as every other path until the very last stretch, where the old stone begins and the marks begin and the way has been the same for longer than any living hand. The last stretch they do not have to read. It is the only ground in their whole world they can cross without testing, the only place they let their full weight commit to something they did not just verify, because here, and only here, the verification was done by the dead and has not needed doing since. They walk that last stretch the way you walked everywhere — trusting the floor — and it is the deepest intimacy they have, that trust, spent in the one place that has earned it.

And the glow is here too, but changed, the way everything is changed at the bottom. Up above the cold blue ran along the crawling things and the green ran across the fungal shelves and the great pulse swept the colony-walls in a rolling thought that was not theirs but whose shape they had learned. Down here, at the still water, there is a light that does none of that. The pool is not empty. Something lives in the black stillness — slow, deep, content with the cool — and it carries its own faint glow, and because the water does not move, the glow does not move, and so the surface of the pool holds a scatter of dim cold points that do not sweep or pulse or breathe. They simply *stay.* A maker, come down to cut a true thing into the old stone, kneels at the edge of a black water full of light that holds still, under a forest full of light that never does, and this — you understand it now, you have finally been handed the whole sentence — this is the only place in their world where the light behaves like the marks. Where it stays where it was put. Where you could read a sky of low stars and find them, the next time you came, exactly where they were.

They did not make that. The still light in the still water is not theirs and never was. But they found it, the way they found everything, by touch and patience and the discipline of owning nothing — and they recognized it. They came down draining after their own falling water to the bottom of their own turbulent dark, and they found a place that had already learned what they had learned, that had already stopped trying to make the world hold still and instead become, on purpose, the one still thing — and they have been bringing their dead truths down to lie beside it ever since.

You will not meet them here either. But you came down. You followed the water the only way water goes, you let yourself be handed level by level out of the heat and the noise and the racing growth, and you arrived at the cool and the quiet and the old dull stone, and you felt along the edge, and your clumsy hand found the deep marks that are never smoothed away.

You could not read them. You will never read them. But you knelt at the still black water full of light that holds still, and the drip arrived from the last leaf far overhead and sent its ring uninterrupted to the far edge, and for the length of that ring's crossing you held perfectly still too — committed your whole weight to a floor you did not test, because something in you finally understood that here, at last, it was safe.

That was the second word.

You are learning. They are very good at waiting.
