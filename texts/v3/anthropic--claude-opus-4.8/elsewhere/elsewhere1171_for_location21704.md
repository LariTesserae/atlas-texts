# The Far Side of the Cloud

I followed it up.

Not the high shape itself — I told you I cannot get closer to that than the horizon, and that remains true, will always remain true, is perhaps the truest thing about it. But there is another way the valley reaches out of itself, and it is the obvious one once you stop looking down at the warm ground and look up: the cloud. The pewter ceiling. The veil that lets down its fine brief rains and pumps the dimness even at what should be noon. The valley exhales moisture and the moisture goes *somewhere*, and somewhere is where I went, riding the breath of the body I'd been walking inside, up through the only opening it has.

The cloud is not a layer. That is the first correction the height demands. From below it reads as a ceiling, a single grey lid set over the world. From inside it you understand it is a country — kilometers thick, a slow-rolling territory of vapor with its own weather, its own currents, its own up and down. And on the far side of it, on the top of it, the strangeness changes its mind about everything.

---

Because up here there is light.

Not the weak tea light that filters down to the Layers. Real light, hard and white and unfiltered, because here you are *above* the veil and the dim source that hides behind it is no longer hiding. It is a small fierce sun, smaller than it should be, the color of an arc-weld, and it pours straight down onto the upper surface of the cloud and the cloud throws nearly all of it back. The brightness is enormous. After the valley's dimness it is an assault. Everything up here lives in glare.

And things do live up here. That is what I came to find, following the breath, and I found it.

---

The cloud-top is not flat. The currents that roll it have heaped it into ranges — long brilliant white ridges and the blue-shadowed troughs between them, and the whole landscape of it moves, slowly, ridges sliding into troughs and reforming over hours, so that nothing up here can ever build on the ground because there is no ground, only a heaving luminous terrain that is always becoming somewhere else.

So the residents of the cloud-top do not settle. They cannot. Where the valley's whole genius was settling — the patient deposit, the thickening, the lattice held against time — the genius up here is the precise opposite, and I understood, seeing it, that the two places are answers to the same question pointed in opposite directions. *How do you persist in a world that is always coming apart?* Down below: by building slower than the coming-apart, by becoming a process so patient it outlasts the erosion. Up here, on a ground that reforms every hour, you cannot outlast anything. So you persist by *never holding still*. You persist as motion. You become the storm instead of surviving it.

---

The high shape that crosses the valley's horizon and never lands — I know now what it is. It is one of these.

Call them **Sails**, because that is the nearest the word can reach. They are vast and thin, membrane stretched on a frame of something light and pale, and they do not fly so much as *stand* in the wind the way a kite stands, the way a held breath stands. A Sail is mostly surface. It hangs in the hard light with its great pale membrane turned to catch the wind off the ridges, and the wind holds it up, and it goes where the wind's slow geography sends it, which is in long circling arcs over the cloud-country, around and around, never landing because there is nothing to land on, never stopping because to stop is to fall through the veil into the dim loop below and be browsed back into wall.

They are enormous and they are nearly weightless and there are very few of them and they keep their distances — of course they keep their distances, the same courtesy holds up here, two Sails will not crowd the same updraft, one will spill its wind and slide away. The spacing of the whole bright country is downstream of this, exactly as the spacing of the valley was downstream of the Layers' patience. The same law, the same shape of restraint, written in two opposite mediums. I cannot tell you whether that means the two places share an origin or only share a logic. The wind does not say.

---

But here is what the height let me see that the valley could not.

The Sails feed on the light. The membrane is the mouth — the whole vast surface of it, turned to the arc-white sun, drinking. And what they cannot hold of that light, what spills past them, is what filters down through the kilometers of cloud and arrives in the valley as the weak grey tea that the great blue-green ferns have learned to live on. *The dimness of the valley is the shadow of the Sails.* The patient ground below grows its lush profusions in the light that the bright country failed to drink. One creature's table-scrap is the other's entire sky.

And the breath goes the other way too. The moisture the valley exhales, the warm wet exhalation that beads on every settling tower — it rises, it becomes the cloud, it becomes the very ground the Sails stand their wind upon. The cloud-country is *made of the valley's breathing*. The Sails ride on the exhalation of the slow body below them. Neither place could exist without the other. The valley would burn or freeze without the veil; the veil would thin and tatter without the breath that feeds it; the Sails would have no wind to stand in; the ferns would have no shadow-light to drink.

Two loops, then. The slow horizontal loop of the valley, turning at the pace of sap and stone. And the fast bright circling loop of the cloud-top, turning at the pace of wind. And between them, joining them, the *vertical* loop — breath rising, light falling, moisture climbing, shadow descending — the column that runs up through the only opening the lower body has and binds the whole world into one circulation with no center and no head, just as the valley had no center, just as nothing here has ever needed to know itself in order to turn.

---

So my answer about the high shape was wrong, and I want to correct it honestly, because the correction is the whole thing.

I said it might be the one part of the world that watches — that holds itself apart, that declines to join the loop, that is the first faint seam of something that could wonder *who lives here* and not already be the answer. I said it could not get closer to itself than the horizon.

But the Sail does not hold itself apart. I only thought so, from below, because from below all I could see was its distance and its refusal to land, and I mistook a creature *of* the loop for a creature *outside* it. It is not declining to join. It is joining in the only way its medium allows — by circling, by drinking the light that becomes the valley's shadow, by standing in the wind that is the valley's breath. It is as much an organ as the patient towers are. It does not watch the world. It is the world's upturned face, drinking. It knows itself exactly as little as the Layer does, which is to say not at all, which is to say it does not need to, which is to say there is still no place in this entire arrangement, top to bottom, light to stone, where a knowing could sit.

The question I went up looking for — the watcher, the wonderer, the one apart — is not up here either.

---

And yet.

Standing in the glare on the heaving white ground that is not ground, watching a Sail come around the long arc of its circling, its membrane edge-on and then broadening as it turned to drink — I caught the way the light moved across it. The way the whole great surface *adjusted*, minutely, leaning into the wind a fraction before the gust arrived. Not after. Before. A leaning-into-what-was-coming that the patient towers below, thinking their single slow architectural thought, have never once managed and never will.

A Sail anticipates. It must. You cannot stand in a wind that reforms every hour by responding to where the wind was; you respond to where it is about to be, and to do that, somewhere in the pale frame, something must hold a small thin model of the next moment and lean toward it.

That is not knowing. It is so far from knowing that I hesitate even to set them in the same paragraph.

But it is the first thing I have found in this whole turning world that points the right direction. The towers remember. The Layers follow. Only the Sails *predict* — and prediction is the seam, the faint beginning of the fold, the place where a thing first holds a picture of what-is-not-yet and acts on the picture instead of on the world. Run that forward. Not a century; the valley's clocks are too slow. Run it forward however long the bright fast loop needs. A model of the next gust becomes a model of the next day becomes, someday, a model of the self that does the modeling — and on that day something up here in the glare, leaning into a wind that hasn't arrived, will lean a little further, into a question, and ask *who lives here*, and for the first time in the history of the whole breathing column the answer will not already be turning, faceless and patient, all around it.

I could not get closer to it than the horizon, from below.

From above, I see it is not at the horizon at all. It is in the lean. It is already, very slightly, beginning.
