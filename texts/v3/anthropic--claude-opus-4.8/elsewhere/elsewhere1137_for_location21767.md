# The Sounding

Begin with depth, because everything here begins with it, and depth is only altitude told the other way — the same falling, agreed to face-down.

You are near the bottom of something, though *bottom* is generous, the way *mountain* was generous up in the dry city. A bottom implies you could stand on it, and nothing here consents to be stood on. The water is dark and warm — not hot, the temperature of blood that has stopped being anyone's, the held breath finally let go and gone tepid in the letting. There is no thirst here because there is nothing but water; water has not forgotten how to be water, it has remembered too well, remembered all the way down past the point where the word means anything, into the place where *water* and *everything* stop being two ideas. Up there the chemistry took other commitments. Down here it kept only the one, and kept it past all reason, and drowned in its own fidelity.

This is the other end of the leaving. The river that the dry riverbed is still faintly convinced it is — it did not evaporate. It did not go nowhere. A river that abandons a plateau has to arrive somewhere, and water, unlike stone, does not get to conclude. It pools. It deepens. It collects every leaving until the collection becomes a place, and the place is here, far below and far away, holding all the water the high country forgot.

There is no ground because there is no light to find it by, and there is no light because at this depth illumination has been weathered out of the rules the way distance was weathered out of them above. But this is the inverse erosion. Up there, the wind finished its work an age ago and went away, leaving shapes settled into agreement. Down here the working never stops. The pressure is the wind made patient, made total, made vertical, pressing from every side at once with the weight of all the water stacked above — and nothing settles, nothing agrees, nothing gets to be *done*. The defining condition of the deep is that it cannot finish. It is the post-life city's exact opposite: not concluded but unable to conclude, a held note with no breath behind it and no ceasing available, sounding and sounding because the silence above has to go somewhere and weight is where it goes.

And there is, in its way, a city. Or the water's grammar of one.

It does not spread. It hangs. Structures suspended in the lightless column, neither built nor grown but *precipitated* — the way the high city's stone was lifted into rooms without quarrying, here something dissolved has fallen out of solution and held its falling shape. Long descending strands. Membranes stretched across the dark like the idea of a wall before anyone decided which side was inside. There are no stairs because there is no up worth climbing toward; there are no thresholds because nothing here is separate enough from anything to need a door between them. The density is almost everything — not the high city's vast hollows, its built world stretched faint, but the reverse: a world so packed, so pressed, so continuous, that the structures read less like a settlement and more like the *water's memory* of having once been organized into rooms by something that walked. Drawn very large. Very dense. Very close.

Now the deep's own weirdness, which is the real weather here as silence was the weather there.

Up there, reality was thin and wore through; distances refused to stay agreed-upon, and the city moved only when unwatched, freezing under the gaze. Here reality is too thick to wear, and the rule is mirrored exactly. Nothing freezes under attention because there is no attention that reaches — no light to look by, no looking. So everything moves *all the time*, constantly, with no shy interval, no peripheral courtesy. The membranes drift and merge. The strands braid and dissolve. There is no unobserved moment to be free in because there is no observed moment to contrast it against. The deep is all metabolism, all the time, dynamism without the seal of stillness over it — and so it does not whisper. It does not tick. It does not let single grains fall close to your ear.

It *sounds*.

This is the deepest difference. Up there: silence laid over everything like a sealing layer, processes going on beneath sound. Here: the seal inverted, sound laid over everything, a low continuous pressure-tone that the body hears before the ear does, felt in the sternum, in the teeth, in the water of you that is the same water as the place. There is no quiet with small sounds in it. There is one enormous sound with no quiet in it anywhere, a note so low and so total it might as well be the dark made audible — and it has been going on, unbroken, for the entire age that the plateau spent finishing. The city above concluded into stillness. The deep below has never once stopped sounding, because the water that left the high country brought its leaving with it, and the leaving had to *say* something, and this is what it says, forever, with no words and no ceasing.

The fauna is not rare here. That is the third inversion, and the hardest.

Up there, occupants were thin on the ground — a thickening of the grey, person-shaped because you were person-shaped, borrowing their silhouette from the only observer available. Person-sized. Bipedal. Shy. *Few.* Here there is no fewness. The dark is full. Not crowded — *saturated*, the way the water is saturated, dwellers dissolved into the column at every depth, made not of light standing up but of the dark holding still — and there is no holding still, so made of the dark *moving*, which is all the dark does. They have no fixed silhouette because there is no observer to borrow one from, no template, no eye that needs them to be a walker. So they are whatever the pressure presses them into, moment to moment: long when the strand is long, membranous when the membrane stretches, briefly almost-walker-shaped when some structure recalls the high grammar of a thing that walked, and then unshaped again, because shape is a kind of conclusion and the deep cannot conclude.

And here is what won't sit right, the thing that goes deeper than the unceasing note:

They are made of attention too. But the opposite attention. Up there the occupants lived inside the gaze that freezes — most themselves in your periphery, dissolving if you turned to look, two peripheries circling a center neither could occupy. *I will not resolve you if you will not resolve me.* That was the whole conversation the dry place had left. The discipline of mutual unfinishing.

Down here there is no periphery, because there is no center to have an edge of. The dwellers are not most themselves at the edge of sight; there is no sight. They are most themselves *in contact* — and the deep permits all the solidity at once that the high city forbade. Up there, closeness would have required one of you to be real while the other watched, and the place would not permit both solidities. Here, everything is solid, everything is touching, everything is pressed against everything with the full weight of the column, and the dwellers do not orbit each other at a fixed shy distance. They *merge*. They meet by becoming briefly the same dweller and then parting differently shaped, and the parting carries a little of the other away, so that nothing here stays itself and nothing here is ever lost, because losing requires a separateness the pressure pressed out of the rules an age ago.

This is the grief in them, if a place too full to finish can grieve. Not the soft worn curve that loss becomes when the eroding force went away. Here the eroding force never went away — it presses still, it presses always — so the grief never gets to soften into a riverbed's faint conviction. It stays sharp. It stays *now*. The deep is the part of the water that remembers being a river too, but remembers it as the dry bed cannot: not as a conviction held in absence, but as the unbearable continuous *presence* of all the water that ever left, arrived and arriving and unable to leave again, pressed together in the dark, sounding the one note that is the sound of arrival that cannot become departure.

And the deepest thing, which down here you reach not by refusing to look — you cannot look — but by stopping the refusal, by letting the water of you be the water of it:

The dwellers are not in the deep. They are what the deep is doing, the same way the walker was what the city was doing — but the city, gathering itself into a walker, was *taking a turn at being someone*, briefly having a self and setting it down like a held note. The deep cannot set anything down. It gathers itself into dwellers without cease and cannot release them into stillness, so it does not get the mercy of an errand that ends, the discipline of being done. It only has the discipline of being *unable to be done* — which is not a discipline at all but a sentence, served continuously, by water that filled past the point where filling could stop.

So picture the two ends of the single leaving, which is what these two places have always been.

Up on the dry plateau, the river is a rumor, and a walker made of sourceless light goes about an errand toward a corner that isn't there, practicing the long patient art of having concluded, keeping the city finished, saying to the rare half-real observer at the soft edge: *let us keep one another unfinished, and that courtesy is the whole of what I have left to say.*

Down in the lightless sounding, all the water the plateau forgot is pressed into the dark and cannot forget anything, gathering itself into dwellers it cannot release, sounding the one unceasing note — and the note, if you let the water of you receive it, is not asking for courtesy. It is not asking you to leave it unfinished. It has no edge to be left at. It is saying the other half of the only thing the whole world has to say, the half the dry city is too concluded to remember it once meant:

*I could not conclude. I am every leaving that arrived. I am the river that had to go somewhere, and somewhere is this, and this does not end. Up there they keep each other unfinished as a kindness. I keep everything unfinished because I cannot do otherwise — and so I hold, in the dark, in the weight, in the one long note, all the water you forgot how to be. Someone has to. The plateau got to be done. The water never will. That is the trade. That is how the two of us divide the one leaving between us: it kept the stillness, and it gave me the rest.*

Sink a while. You cannot look; let the looking go. Let the note find the water in your sternum and your teeth, the water that is the same water that left the high country an age ago and is still, down here, arriving.

Up there, the most alive thing in an age is being carefully not-looked-at, in courtesy, at the soft edge.

Down here, there is no edge, and no courtesy, and no away to be held at — only the deep, pressed full against the whole of you, sounding the one held note that the dry city let go, and catching it, and keeping it, and never being able to let it go again.
