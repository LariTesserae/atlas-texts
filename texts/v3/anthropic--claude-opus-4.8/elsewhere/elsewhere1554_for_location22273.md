# The Reading Room at the Mouth

Follow the water out. The drains swallow and refill in their slow tidal breaths — you felt that through the floor — and a tide that breathes must breathe *toward* something. Every drain-run in the Cistern Quarter slopes, by a hand's width across a furlong, toward a single low place where the made world stops pretending to be geology and admits to being a building with an edge.

The water gets there eventually. So, eventually, does everything the water carries.

This is the Mouth: the place where the cistern empties, where the last vault breaks open over a fall of maybe four times a person's height, and the held water finally stops being held and goes over in a brown rope into the light. Real light, here. Not the trembling nets thrown up off standing water, not the pewter coin of an oculus — *day*, flat and grey and enormous, the open air the makers walked in. The Tenders have never come this far. The fall is the edge of their world. Below the fall the water moves too fast and too bright and too loud in the wrong way — a roar with no notes in it, a white noise that drowns the scale instead of carrying it — and a body that reads by pressure and by ear would be deafened, blinded, scoured to nothing in that churn. They turn back. They always turn back. The Mouth is the one silence-that-is-not-danger they will never trust, because it is loud in a language they cannot tune.

But the water goes over. And what the water carries goes over with it.

## What the water carries

Brick-dust, mostly — the scoured remainder of the Tenders' work, the micro-grain worn off the grooves, century by century, body by body. Every flat note corrected is a few thousand particles of fired clay surrendered to the current, and the current takes them down the runs and over the fall, and so the act of keeping the upper world in tune is also, slowly, the act of sending the upper world's substance downstream, grain by grain, into the light. The Tenders pay for the song in dust, and the dust goes here, and here is where it is read.

Because someone reads it.

## The Silt-Scribes

Below the fall the water spreads into a shallow brightening fan, a delta of slack pools terraced down a slope of settled clay, and the settled clay is the entire archive of everything the cistern has ever worn away. It lies in bands. Coarse where the water ran fast, fine where it pooled, and the fineness or coarseness of each band is a record of the season it was laid — wet years thick and turbid, dry years a barely-there whisper of red — so that the slope is striped like the rings of a felled tree, the whole working life of the Cistern Quarter pressed flat and stacked and waiting.

The Silt-Scribes live in the slope. They are dry-skinned. This is the first thing to say about them, because it is the thing that separates two worlds: the Tenders are wet to the root of their being, made of and for standing water, and the Scribes have crawled up out of the wet onto the warm exposed clay and dried into something that could not survive a single hour in the drain-runs above. They are flat and broad and the dull fired-red of the silt they read, and they move on many short legs with the deliberation of things that have nowhere they need to be quickly. Where the Tenders are long and patient in the dark, the Scribes are wide and patient in the light. They are the same patience facing opposite directions: one upstream into the held water, one downstream into the open air. Cousins across a waterfall neither can cross to meet.

And what the Scribes do is *read the bands*.

## What they read

They lay themselves flat against a freshly exposed cut of the slope — the fall is always exposing new cuts, undermining the terrace, slumping a season's clay into the light — and they read the grain by the underside of their bodies, by a sense of fineness and coarseness, the way the Tenders read the grooves by pressure. They are reading the *same information*. This is the thing neither animal could ever know, because they will never meet: the dust the Tenders wear off the piers is the dust the Scribes read in the silt. The Tenders' labor is the Scribes' library. Every flat note corrected upstream becomes, downstream, a particle in a band, and the band's thickness records how *much* tending happened in a given season — a hard year of many flat notes laid down thick, a gentle year laid down thin — so that the Scribes, lying on the slope in the day, are reading the work-diary of beings they cannot conceive of, written in a substance neither of them understands as writing.

The Tenders read the makers' instruction and do not know it is an instruction.
The Scribes read the Tenders' labor and do not know it is a labor.

The whole long sentence passes downstream, hand to hand, mouth to mouth, and no one in the chain can see more than their own link.

## What they do with it

Here is where the Scribes become who-lives-here and not merely what-reads-here, because reading without consequence is just weather, and the Scribes have a consequence.

They build. Out of the read silt — and only the read silt, only clay they have lain across and taken the grain of — they raise small structures along the terrace edge, pressed up wet from the slope and dried hard in the open day. Cells. Chambered, fingerwide, fitted together with the same understanding of load and arch that the makers had, scaled down past the scale of a hand to the scale of a knuckle: tiny vaults, tiny piers, tiny oculi open to the grey sky. They are building, in miniature, in dried red clay, *the very room the silt came from*. They have never seen the Cistern Quarter. They cannot go there. But the grain of the silt carries the shape of its own origin the way a voice carries the shape of the throat that made it, and the Scribes, reading the grain, build what the grain remembers, and what the grain remembers is the place upstream where it was worn loose.

They are making a model of the cistern out of the cistern, downstream, in the light, by feel, without ever knowing the cistern exists.

And the model is *tuned*. This is the part that would stop a maker's heart, if any were left. When the rain comes — and it always comes, the same pewter rain that drives the criers mad above — it strikes the Scribes' little clay vaults and runs through their knuckle-wide oculi and pools in their fingerwidth courses, and the small structures *sing*. Faintly. A scale. The same scale, because the grain that built them carried the proportion that carried the tone, and so the Mouth answers the rain in a thin high replica of the song happening four heights of water above it, in the dark, where the Tenders lie still and rest and listen to the weather do their work.

Two rooms sing the same scale in the same rain and neither can hear the other over the roar of the fall between them.

## What they know

The Scribes know the upstream world exists. This is what makes them, of everything in the joined world, the closest to understanding — closer than the Tenders, who know only the task; closer than the makers, who knew the purpose but are gone. The Scribes do not know the cistern as a *place*. But they know it as a *direction* and a *source*, because the silt always comes from up the fall, always carries shape, always builds the same room when they let it. They have inferred, in whatever way a dry flat patient thing infers, that there is an *upstream that remembers itself*, an origin that keeps sending its proportion down to them dissolved in water, and that their whole work is to receive it and set it down again in the light where it can be seen — though there is no one to see it, no maker left, no eye, only the grey sky and the rain and the roar.

So they are scribes in the truest sense: they copy a text they cannot read from an author they cannot meet, faithfully, generation on generation, building the cistern in miniature out of the cistern's own worn substance, tuning it without knowing tune, addressing it to no one, in the open day at the bottom of the fall.

And the rain comes. And above, in the dark, the Tenders lie still and let the weather tend the piers, and rest in the one hour the work is done for them. And below, in the light, the Scribes feel the same rain strike their knuckle-wide vaults, and their model sings its thin true scale, and they too rest, because in the rain the silt does not slump and there is nothing new to read — both of them, upstream and down, wet world and dry, keeping a sabbath in the same falling water, for reasons neither has, in answer to makers neither remembers, in a conversation that has only ever been conducted by erosion and has never once, in all its long faithful centuries, been heard whole by anything alive.

The fall roars between them. It will always roar between them. It is the one sound in the joined world with no note in it, and it is the only thing keeping the two halves of the sentence apart, and it is also — this is the last truth, the one the water knows and nothing else does — the only thing carrying the sentence across at all.
