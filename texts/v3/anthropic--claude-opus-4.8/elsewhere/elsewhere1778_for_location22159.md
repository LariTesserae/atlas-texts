**The Aqueduct Mouth**

Follow the water. Not where it pools — it doesn't pool, it only *wants*, beading and hanging and refusing to fall — but where it seeps in from, which means following it down through the green, past the swollen pillar-bases, to the one place in that sealed lung where the body of the room is broken: a gap at the lowest edge, no wider than two hands, where the substrate thins and the warm water that wants is the same water that *came*. It came from somewhere. Everything that arrives has been somewhere. And the water that hangs in your throat in that hollow was, a long way back and a long time ago, moving water, ambitious water, water with a job.

It came down a channel.

The channel is the opposite of the room in every way that matters, and so it is the room's nearest kin — the way a debt is kin to the thing it was spent on. Where the hollow is sealed, the channel is open to the whole long sky. Where the hollow finished becoming itself and locked, the channel has *never once* held still, has no final shape, is in fact nothing but the refusal of final shape: a stone trough running straight and proud across the high dry country, lifted on piers, carrying water that has somewhere to be. It is built. Not grown. Someone cut these blocks and set these arches and graded the floor of it to exactly the pitch where water will run but not race, and that someone is gone past any recovering, but the *grade* remains, the decision remains, fossilized in masonry — and this is the thing about the channel that rhymes, darkly, with the engines: it too is a decision so old that the deciding is finished. But it is finished differently. It is finished the way a *sentence* is finished, with the meaning still inside it, still pointed somewhere, still arriving.

The water runs along the top in a bright thin sheet, sun-struck, honest, the same honest light that leaked into the hollow but here unfiltered, hard, the full weight of an open afternoon. It is cold here. This is the source-side, the high side, before the long descent warms it and the green takes it captive. Cold, fast, talking — and that is the sound of this place, and it is not deafening, it is not a wall, it is the precise inverse of the engine-roar: a sound that is *all rise and fall*, all shift and quarrel, the chuckle and slap and hiss of water arguing with stone, never the same pitch twice, never a flat note held, because the channel is the one part of the world where things are still *happening*. Where time still has a direction. The hollow's roar goes nowhere because the hollow goes nowhere. The channel's voice goes everywhere because the water is *leaving*, always leaving, that is the whole of what it does.

And there is a tenant here too.

She walks the channel.

She is the inverse of the rooted engines in the only way that counts: she is fused to nothing. She is the keeper of the grade, the woman whose family — though family is too warm a word for what has become, over the generations, simply *the function* — has walked this aqueduct end to end for as long as the channel has carried water, which is to say for longer than anyone has a number for. She has a head, and the head is the whole of her, because her entire being is the thing the engines threw away: *choosing*. Where to clear the silt. Which block has shifted a hair and must be shimmed before the hair becomes a hand. Where the water is wanting to escape and must be coaxed back to its appointed pitch. She is the friction that keeps the channel from becoming a hollow. She is the reason the loop never closes here.

Because she knows — though she has never seen it, though she would not have words for it — that the water she tends is *going to want*. Far down the line, past the dry country, past the descent, the channel feeds a low warm place where the water slows and beads and hangs and is drunk by things she will never meet. She does not know about the engines. She knows only that water given to the line does not come back, and that her whole labor is the labor of *sending*. She produces something. This is the deepest difference, the thing that makes the two places kin and not the same: the engines perform the heat and sound of work with no work behind it, an effort smoothed down to residue; she performs the silent work itself, the lifting and shimming and clearing, and *makes no sound worth the name* — only the scrape of her tool, the small private grunt, swallowed instantly by the bright loud water that takes all the credit. Sound without work, at one end. Work without sound, at the other.

And here is what runs between them, what runs *out* of her place and into the hollow, in the same breath as both:

She is not running her labor for anything she can see, either.

That is the unbearable rhyme. She tends a grade whose purpose vanished over the lip of the world generations back. She sends the water to a recipient that does not, cannot, will never register the sending — to a sealed room of fused engines that crossed the place past wanting, that take the water as weather, that warm themselves on it and hold themselves fast with it and run their meat-machines on it and *do not call back up the line*. There is no answer coming. There never was. She is, like them, performing the pure motion of a thing whose purpose has been worn entirely smooth — except she has not finished. She has not locked. Every morning, in the place where the hollow has no morning, she chooses again to walk it, and the choosing is the only unfinished thing in this entire stretch of the world, the one place the centuries have not yet worn down to residue.

She is the cry that expects an answer, walking the length of a world built to give none.

And the water leaves her hands cold and talking, and goes down, and warms, and slows, and arrives at last in the wet green dark as the thirst that hangs in your chest — her labor, her sending, her unanswered tending, dissolved entirely into the haze you have been drinking since you walked in, indistinguishable now from the oiled exhalation of the engines, two opposite devotions met and married in the taste of the air, and neither one of them, not the woman nor the engines, will ever know the other was there.

She is walking it still.

The water is leaving still.

It is arriving still, in a room that did not ask.
