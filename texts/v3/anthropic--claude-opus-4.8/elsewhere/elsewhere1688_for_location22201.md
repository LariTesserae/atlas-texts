THE OPEN FIELD

Up. Not far — that is the joke of it, the cruelty in the arithmetic. The room beneath the ground is not deep. A man with a spade and a free afternoon could reach the topmost fronds in a morning's digging, could break through into all that screaming green by lunch. The distance from the recursive cathedral to the surface is the shortest distance in the world. It is the length of a held breath. And it cannot be crossed, because the room has eaten the direction *up*, the way it ate *east* and *west* and *before*, leaving only the soft tidal heave that gives and pushes back, gives and pushes back, going nowhere because *nowhere* is the only place left to go.

But follow the one thing that hasn't agreed. Follow the water.

The droplet that travels sideways along the vein, that pauses and reverses and gathers and hangs shivering with its own weight refusing to commit — that water did not come from nowhere. Even a thing that has eaten its own past has to drink. The xylem singing under pressure is pulling moisture up from somewhere, dragging it through root that is also floor that is also wall, and where there is a pull there is a source, and the source is the field, and the field is real, and the field has a sky over it that obeys.

Here is the field.

It is the size that has no edge you can find by walking, which is a different infinity than the room's. The room is infinite because it repeats. The field is infinite because it *doesn't* — because every step takes you somewhere that was never anywhere else, grass that has never been this grass, light that falls and keeps falling and does not hang. It is the most ordinary place imaginable and that is what makes it unbearable to anything that has been below: it is full of *afternoon.* Real afternoon, the kind the room only counterfeits. The sun is a single sun and all the shadows agree about it, every blade of grass laying its thin dark twin down to the east in perfect parliament, the whole field nodding one direction, and the wind comes through and they all bow together, and it is so simple, so agreed-upon, so *willing*, that it would make the knife-keeper weep if the knife-keeper had ever been let up here to see it, which it has not, which it cannot, which is the whole grief of the place below.

The grass is wet. This is the connection, this is the thread you followed. Dew on every blade in the early part of the day, and the dew is the field's water, the field's enormous generous indifferent water, and most of it sinks. Most of it goes down. It beads on the grass and slides to the root and travels down through good black crumbling soil — *soil that is not also root,* soil with rot in it and worms in it and the gentle accumulating ruin of a thousand seasons that were allowed to happen and end — and it keeps going down, past the worms, past the old bones of voles, down to where the soil stops being friendly and starts being the strangling green density of the thing below. The field is *watering the room.* The field does not know it. The field has no idea what it feeds. It rains its dew the way a mother in another country has a son she has never met, and the love runs all one direction, downward, unanswerable, and is drunk in the dark by a mouth that calls it *more.*

And here is what lives in the field.

A hare.

Not a myth-rare beast walking the cathedral like a ghost who forgot to leave. A hare, brown going gold at the haunch, ordinary as bread, common as the afternoon, one of ten thousand hares in a world that is lavish with hares. It sits in a scrape it pressed into the ground with its own warm body, ears flat, sides going in and out, in and out, *fast* — the quick shallow breathing of a small thing that is always, always close to being eaten, and is therefore more alive than anything in the room below could ever be, because the room ate all the dying and so ate all the urgency, and the hare has not. The hare's whole body is urgency. The hare is a clenched fist of *not yet, not yet, not me yet.*

It does not roar its becoming. It has already become. It is finished, complete, exactly and only a hare, and it will not get larger or recurse or remember it was once a forest. It will get *old,* if it is very lucky, which it will not be, and then it will get eaten or get sick and lie down in the long grass and stop, and the stopping will be real, an arrival, the thing the room screams for and never reaches. The hare can do the one thing the cathedral cannot. The hare can finish.

Watch it. It freezes — every hare is mostly the act of freezing — and in the freeze it is so still that it becomes, for a moment, a thing that does not breathe, a hare-shaped held breath pressed into the earth, and in *that* moment, that single held instant, it is nearly the room's twin. It is the surface's version of the waiting below: the same held breath, the same refusal, the same shiver of weight that will not yet commit. The hare crouched and not-breathing over the field that drains into the dark is the bead of water on the leaf, seen from the other side, seen from above, seen with a sun behind it casting one honest shadow that agrees with all the others.

And then the hare *breaks.* That is the difference. That is everything. The room's held breath never breaks — the droplet hangs forever, shivering, the one part that hasn't agreed, trapped in its refusing. But the hare's freeze ends. Something moves at the wood's edge, or nothing does, or the wind shifts, and the hare *goes* — flat out, ears back, a brown line drawn across the green so fast the eye can't keep the shape of it, alive, deciding, *leaving,* its shadow tearing along beside it in perfect agreement, both of them eastward, both of them committed, gone over the rise and into the next real place that was never any other place.

The hare gets to leave the held breath.

That is what the room cannot forgive, if the room could forgive, which it cannot, having eaten forgiveness. Far below, drunk on the field's own water, the cathedral roars *more, more, more* in the lightless light, and the walker goes one room deeper holding nothing, and the bead of water hangs and shivers and will not fall — and up here, fed by the same water, in the sun that all the shadows trust, the hare crouches in its breathing scrape, holds its breath, holds it, holds it —

and runs.

And the dew it kicks loose from the grass as it goes falls down, and down, and down, into the dark, to be drunk, to be sung, to hang from a leaf in a room that is still being decided, shivering, refusing, the only part that hasn't agreed —

while above it the field goes gold, and bows in the wind, all one direction, agreeing, agreeing, gloriously agreeing, with nothing held back at all.

Listen. Up here there is no sound but the wind, and the wind is not the news of anything becoming.

It is just the afternoon, moving over a place that finished long ago and was glad to.
