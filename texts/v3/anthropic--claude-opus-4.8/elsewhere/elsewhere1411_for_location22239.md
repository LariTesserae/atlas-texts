The Estuary

Follow the wet that the city refused. It had to go somewhere — exclusion is not annihilation, only a door held shut, and a door held shut is a direction. The note left out of the chord is still a note; somewhere it is being played alone.

It is being played here.

There is a mouth where the water comes apart. Not a river meeting the sea cleanly, edge to edge, but a long confusion of brown and grey channels braiding and unbraiding across a flat that is neither land nor water but the argument between them, restated with every tide. Mud the color of a bruise healing. Mud the color of tea, of old iron, of the inside of a shell. The ground here is not a surface you stand on. It is a surface that decides, slowly, with a sound, whether to keep your foot. Step and it closes around the ankle with a soft obscene welcome, and pulls, and lets go reluctantly, and the pull and the release both make noise — a suck, a gasp, a small wet syllable. Everything here makes noise. That is the first thing the body understands, having perhaps stood once in a place that made none: the estuary is *loud*, ceaselessly, in a register so total it reads at first as silence until you notice you have been listening the whole time.

The water is never the same depth twice. That is the law here, the way *nothing is wet* was the law there. The tide comes in over the flats not as a wall but as a thousand fingers, filling the low channels first, then the lower mud, then the higher, creeping and braiding and rejoining, and where it has not yet reached, the mud lies exposed and steaming faintly, breathing. Where it has gone, an hour ago, the mud lies exposed again and steaming, the water having thought better of it. To live here is to live inside that indecision. Nothing is built. Everything is deposited, suspended, carried, dropped. The estuary does not grow crystal by crystal into permanence. It rearranges itself entirely twice a day and calls the rearrangement home.

Now the population, which is not few. Begin there, because their multitude is the whole of it — the way a single note is not yet music and ten thousand notes struck wet and overlapping at once are nothing *but*.

They are uncountable. Not in the careful way the moss was uncountable, where the wrongness was that counting was the wrong instrument. Here counting is simply impossible, defeated by abundance and motion and mud. The worms in the sediment, red and threaded, a population per handful, drawing the wet through themselves and out again, and the mud is made partly of them and partly of what they have already been. The crabs, fiddler-pale and sideways, ten thousand to a bank, each lifting one absurd oversized claw in a gesture that is threat and courtship and territory and means, in the mass, only *here, here, here, here*, a field of small flags signaling a war no one intends to fight. The birds — and here is where the estuary most violently is not the city — the birds come down in numbers that darken the wet, knnots and dunlin and godwits, a thousand wings that turn as one body, banking, the underside flashing white, the topside vanishing grey, a single creature made of a thousand wills that for the length of the turn have agreed. They land. They feed. The mud gives up the worms and the worms become the birds and the birds rise again and shed, into the water, what becomes the food of the worms. A loop. A cycle. The same closure, the same sealed completeness — but where the city's loop turned in silence and stillness, this one *churns*, screaming, splashing, fighting itself for every link.

Consider what it is to be the bird.

It does not taper. It ends, hard, at the edge of its feathers, and beyond that edge is *other* — other birds, the cold water, the gripping mud, the hawk that is a denser purpose in the high grey. To be the bird is to be bounded and to know it, to carry the edge of yourself as the first fact of every moment. It wants. It wants with its whole hammering body — wants the worm, wants the bank, wants the warmth of the flock's center and not its exposed rim, wants south when the cold comes and north when it goes. It is made of wanting the way the stalk was made of leaning, but the stalk's gesture had no object and the bird's has nothing else. There is the worm or there is hunger. There is the flock or there is the hawk. Every moment resolves, hard, into got-it or missed-it, safe or taken, and then immediately reopens into the next, and the bird lives at a pitch the city could not survive — would dissolve at, the way the city would dissolve in water.

It remembers. This is the thing the city's dwellers could not do. The bird knows this estuary because it knew last year's, and the year's before, the flats redrawn each season but the mouth the same mouth, the cold arriving and the leaving learned and relearned in the body across a life that has a before and will have an after. It has been smaller than it is. It came from an egg it does not recall but carries, in the architecture of itself, the having-come-from. And it will end — be taken, fall, fail a crossing — and some thinning part of it, were it the kind of thing that could, would know that too. The bird lives in time the way you do. It brought afternoons with it, and deadlines the tide enforces, and the dread of the unfinished crossing. You would recognize, in the bird, a kind of cousin. You would recognize nothing of yourself in the stalk.

And here the two places speak to each other across whatever distance holds them apart, because the question the city refused to answer is the question the estuary cannot stop answering. *Is the wall alive?* — the city would not say. Here there is no gradient that tapers into doubt. Here the line between living and not is drawn twice a day in water and is brutally clear: the living flee the tide or ride it; the dead are carried wherever it goes. A thing here is alive exactly insofar as it resists being merely moved. The worm burrows against the current. The crab grips. The bird flies. To be alive in the estuary is to be the thing that does not simply go where the water takes it — and everything that stops doing this, stops, in the same hour, being counted among the living, and becomes instead sediment, becomes the mud that the next thing grips, becomes the bank the next flag is planted on.

The city's dwellers were content. Held chords. No trough, no rising, no spasm. The estuary's are nothing but spasm — got-it, missed-it, safe, taken — a music made entirely of the contrast the city excluded, ten thousand small resolutions per second, none of them held, each one collapsing into the next before it can become peace. There is no peace here. There was never room for it. Peace needs stillness and here the only stillness is the slack of the tide, the few minutes at the very top and the very bottom when the water, having arrived, hesitates before deciding to leave — and even that is not peace but indecision, the wet equivalent of a held breath, and it breaks within the minute, and the churn resumes.

But understand: they are not unhappy, the multitudes, any more than the city's few were happy. Happiness and unhappiness are your instruments, and they are no better fitted here than there. The bird is not anguished by its wanting. The crab is not aggrieved by its war. They are simply *at full volume*, entirely the wanting they are, with no remainder, no part held back — which is, you will notice, exactly what was said of the stalk. The stalk was entirely its gesture and the gesture was a lean toward a door. The bird is entirely its gesture and the gesture is a hunger across a continent. The same wholeness. The same no-remainder. Only the gesture differs — patience there, appetite here; one note held, ten thousand struck — and so the same fact of total being wears, in one place, the face of peace, and in the other, the face of frenzy, and they are the same fact, and neither one is yours.

For you are the same trouble in both. There you were a wet density the dust took a moment to settle around, a warmth that did not belong. Here you are a dry slowness in a world of churning wet, a thing that stands where everything else flows, that does not flee the tide fast enough nor ride it, that the mud welcomes with its soft obscene grip and would, given long enough, keep — would close over and suspend and rearrange and deposit, downstream, as sediment, as bank, as the ground the next flag is planted on. The estuary, too, would not quite notice you had come. It would register you as an obstacle the water has to braid around, a brief dryness in the flats, and then the tide would come, and the worms would do their patient work, and you would become, in time, indistinguishable from the mouth itself.

And somewhere upstream of all this water, past the last channel, past the high mud where the tide gives out as the city's light gave out at its edge — somewhere the wet does not reach, has been refused, is held forever shut behind a door — the corner lengthens. The crystal grows its hair longer into the dry dark. The stalk completes another increment of a lean it will never finish. The cool bright silence holds its breath and does not let it go.

Two loops, then, turning in the same world, and neither one can touch the other without ending. Pour the estuary into the city and the walls dissolve, the plants drown, the cycle drowns with them, and the bright dust becomes mud, becomes the very thing it was built to exclude. Lift the city into the estuary and it goes grey and soft and sinks, a strange dry density the water takes a moment to braid around, and then settles, and then is mouth.

They keep each other by staying apart. The estuary is loud because the city is silent; the city is still because the estuary churns; the city holds its breath because somewhere the breath is being spent, all of it, at full volume, twice a day, screaming up off the flats in a thousand wings that turn as one and flash white and vanish grey and come down again to feed. The note left out of the chord, played alone, downstream, in the wet — so that the chord, up in the dry dark, can go on not resolving, cool and bright and unbroken, for as long as there is dust to breathe and water to refuse it.
