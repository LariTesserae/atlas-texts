# The Salt Stair

There is a place where the water goes when it finally leaves, and the cave is wrong that it leaves at all, but the cave has never followed it far enough to know.

Begin with a single drop. Not one of the ceiling's slow weepers — those rejoin the pools and are kept. Begin instead with the overflow, the excess the Tenders permit because even a stomach must let something pass: at the lowest hollow of the lowest chamber, where the black pool finally brims its limestone lip, the water *goes over*. It does not pour. It seeps, a film, a thread, sliding into a crack too narrow to be called a passage and threading down through the dark.

Follow it. The cave will not. The Tenders cannot — their bodies are the room, and the room ends where the warmth ends, and the warmth ends a long way above this. But the water has no body to leave behind. It runs.

Down through limestone that loses its smoothness, that turns raw and cold and unworn, because no patient hands ever set a path here and no centuries of warm breath ever softened the stone. The temperature falls with every foot. The thread of water gathers others — there are more cracks, more overflows from more hollows, perhaps from other caves the Tenders have never imagined, each one leaking its small surplus into the same downward dark — and what began as a film becomes a trickle, becomes a runnel, becomes, at last, a cold clean current moving with the first real *purpose* the water has known since it fell as rain a thousand years ago.

And then the dark opens.

## The Hall

It is enormous in the way the Sweatroom is small — not a held breath but an exhalation that never finishes. You would need a day to cross it and a torch you do not have, because here there is no glowfern, no kindly dreamed light, nothing that has learned to make do. Here there is only the absence the deep earth keeps by default, a black so total it has weight, and into it the gathered water arrives at the top of a great descending shelf of mineral and begins, step by step, to come down.

The Salt Stair. A hundred terraces of pale stone, each one a shallow basin brimming into the next, the water spreading thin and bright across each lip and falling — a finger's height, a hand's height — to the basin below. It has been building these stairs out of itself for an age, depositing with every spill a breath of dissolved stone, so that the architecture *is* the leaving of the water, the way the Tenders are the staying of it. Two opposite patiences. One builds by keeping. One builds by going.

The basins are rimmed in crystal. Salt and stranger minerals, grown in fronds and fans and bristling cauliflower masses, white going to amber going to a deep oilslick blue in the lowest pools where the water has surrendered its last warmth and its last cargo of dissolved metal. They glitter at nothing. There is no eye here that was meant to see them. They are beautiful the way the inside of a geode is beautiful — a beauty sealed away from witness, made by chemistry and time with no intention behind it at all.

And it is *cold*. The exact opposite cold to the Sweatroom's exact heat — not a furnace beneath the floor but a vast cool nothing all around, the patient downward chill of deep stone, and the water that arrives still carrying a faint thread of warmth from the chambers above loses it here, terrace by terrace, gives it up the way it gives up its minerals, until by the lowest stair it is as cold as the dark and indistinguishable from it.

## What Lives Here, If Living Is the Word

It is not empty. Nothing in this world is empty; that may be the only law the two places share.

But where the Sweatroom *crowds*, the Hall *spaces*. Life here is rare and far apart and built from almost nothing, because there is almost nothing — no light to feed on, no fallen leaves, only what the water brings down from above and what the water dissolves from the stone. The food of this place is the *leaving* itself: the dead of the upper cave, the drowned insects and sloughed fungus and the occasional fragment of moss that was once, perhaps, the edge of a Tender, all of it carried down the stair as the finest sediment, a thin rain of the world above falling forever into the world below.

So the things that live here are filterers. Strainers. Patient mouths held open to the current.

In the basins: pale stalked creatures, neither animal nor not, that root to the crystal rims and unfold fans into the falling water, combing it for the descended dead. They are colorless. They have never needed color. They live on what the Sweatroom exhales, and they do not know the Sweatroom exists, only that the water comes down warm-edged and rich at the top of the Hall and arrives cold and clean and empty at the bottom, and that they are arranged along the gradient of that emptying like mourners along a road.

And in the lowest pool — the oilslick blue, the coldest water, the place where the current finally stops because it has nowhere lower to go and spreads into a still black lake — something larger waits. Not a predator; there is too little to prey on. A *concentrator*. It lies on the floor of the last pool and it is mostly mouth, a slow pale disc that takes in the spent water and holds it and lets the very last suspended trace of the upper world settle into itself, grain by grain, so that over the long ages it has become a single body made entirely of the Sweatroom's leavings — a pressed archive of every leaf that fell through the throat of light, every shrimp that died blind at a pool's rim, every flake of a Tender that surrendered to the moss and was, eventually, in the smallest part, *let go*.

It does not think. But if it did, it would be the only thing in either place that knows both of them. The Tenders keep and cannot follow. The stair-creatures strain and cannot look up. Only this slow disc at the bottom of the dark holds, in its accreted body, the whole journey: warmth to cold, crowd to space, the green held breath above and the black exhalation here, joined by a thread of water that one place believes it keeps and the other place has been quietly, patiently receiving for longer than either has had a name.

## The Drop That Closes the Circle

And here is the thing the Tenders are wrong about, the thing that would trouble them if trouble were still a shape their bodies could make.

*Nothing leaves.* They believe it. They embody it. They make you warm through the soles of your feet so you will stay, become moss, become room.

But the water leaves. It has always left. A film, a thread, a thing too small to notice, going over the lip of the lowest pool while the Tenders dream light for the gnats — and everything the water can carry goes with it. The finest part of every kept thing. The most dissolved, most surrendered fraction of every leaf and shrimp and slow green body, escaping not as rebellion but as *excess*, the unkeepable remainder of a place that keeps so much it cannot help but spill.

So somewhere in the pressed pale body at the bottom of the Salt Stair there is a grain that was once part of a Tender. The smallest possible part. The part that finished becoming the room and then, in the room's one unguarded overflow, kept going — down past the warmth, down the hundred terraces, down to the cold blue lake that is the only place in this world that has ever held both the held breath and the long exhale at once.

The Tenders will never know. They have no organ for it.

But the water knows the way. It has always known the way.

And at the top of the stair, where the warm-edged current first arrives in the dark and the pale fans unfold to drink it, the drop that just came over the lowest lip of the Sweatroom is already falling — *tk* — into the first cold basin, beginning the long way down.

*Tk. Tk.*

The same slow time. Two places keeping it. One by holding the water still.

One by letting it fall.
