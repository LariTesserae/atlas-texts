**The Concourse of Settled Things**

Forty floors up — not measured, but you would call it up — there is a place that has decided everything.

You arrive at it straight on. That is the first difference you feel before you understand it as a difference: the way in is a door, and the door is in a wall, and the wall is where a wall should be, square to the floor, and the floor is the floor and goes no deeper than itself. There is nothing sideways about the entrance. You walk in the way a sentence ends, and the place receives you the way a finished room receives anyone, which is to say without interest, because it has already worked out what it is for and you are merely the next instance of someone using it for that.

It is enormous, and it lays itself out for walking. This is the second difference, and it is total. Where the room below was a pocket that refused to be a landscape, this is all landscape, a hall so long that the far end is a matter of faith, ranked with surfaces that announce their function — counters, rails, benches bolted at the regulation interval, signage in a typeface chosen by a committee that no longer exists. Every object here knows exactly what it is. The bench is a bench. It will be a bench tomorrow. It has been a bench so long and so completely that it has stopped being able to imagine bench-hood as a question. The certainty is in the material itself: brushed steel that has given up sweating, terrazzo polished past the memory of being poured, glass that holds no condensation because nothing here is exhaling — the air has been conditioned to a dryness that forbids weather. There are no phases. There is no fog at one hour and dullness at the next. There is one climate, and it is the climate of a decision that was made and will not be revisited.

The light is everywhere and from nowhere, even and cold and complete, recessed into the ceiling in a grid so regular it stops being a grid and becomes simply *brightness*, the way a field of identical things stops being plural. Nothing leans toward it because nothing needs it; the light is not a source to grow toward, it is a condition already satisfied, distributed without favor across the whole hall so that no corner is dimmer than another, no amber island, no brown surround. Below, the bulbs were rare and the stalks bent for them with a slowness you could only confirm by looking away. Here there is no looking away that would reveal anything. Look away and look back and everything is exactly as bright, exactly as placed, exactly as done.

And it is full of animals.

They pour through it. This is what the lower room could not bear and the concourse cannot stop — bodies, finished bodies, each one a settled question walking. They come in carrying their answers in hard portable shells: a man who has decided he is a man hurrying because he has decided he is late, a woman who has concluded the matter of herself down to the angle of her wrist on the handle of a case, children who are already, horribly young, repeatable. They scuttle. That is the word, though they would not like it — they cross the plates of the floor with the exact busyness the room below forbade, but it is a different busyness, the opposite busyness. Below, the labor was self-assembly; the work was making the maker, and the work was never done because being done was death. Here the work is *done*, and the busyness is only the motion of finished things using a finished place, transactions completing, a body delivered to a counter and exchanged for a service and removed, a piston-stroke of commerce sighing shut and taking no time at all about the return. Production is everywhere visible. The product is each other. The hall manufactures, hour on hour, the spectacle of beings who have already agreed to the noun.

There are no pale stalks in the seams. There are no seams. The surfaces meet so cleanly, sealed and gasketed and signed off, that water has nowhere to pool and consider, and so nothing grows, and the absence of growth reads not as concentration but as completion. The room below had an ecology without creatures, a metabolism without a mouth. The concourse has creatures without an ecology — all mouth, all metabolism, every appetite catered to and nothing breeding in the joints, life present in such finished quantity that it cannot make more life, only spend the life it arrives with. You will wait, here, for the held breath, for the place on the wall where the structure gathers itself to almost-mean something. It will not come. Nothing here is holding its breath. Everything here exhales freely into air engineered to take the moisture away before it can become a gesture.

And yet.

Forty floors is not so far, as the building keeps its own measures, and there is a kinship between the two places that neither would admit, running down through the plates and seams that never reach dirt. It is this: the warmth. The migrating heat that paces the pipes below, trading from cold conduit to warm on a schedule no one can read — that heat does not come from nowhere. It is the spent warmth of all these settled bodies, gathered by the dry efficient air, pulled into the conduits, sent down. The concourse exhales and the room below breathes it. Every certainty up here, every body that has agreed to be a noun and stopped reaching, sheds a little heat in the agreeing, and that heat is carried down through forty floors of finished structure into the one place that is still deciding, and there it becomes the small migrating territory the tenant paces, the only ground the not-yet-born thing has, which is the discarded warmth of the already-born.

The concourse feeds the room. The finished thing keeps the unfinished thing alive without ever knowing it exists, the way the living keep the soil warm above the dead, except the direction is inverted and the meanings traded — for here it is the *finished* that lies above, ranked and bright and ordinary, and the unborn that waits below in the dark with its water and its want.

And here is what the concourse cannot tell you, because, having decided everything, it has lost the capacity to wonder about anything:

It is afraid of the room below. Not consciously — it has spent its consciousness on certainty and has none left over for fear. But the climate is too dry. The seams are sealed too well. The light is too complete, too without islands, too insistent that there be no dim corner where a thing might bend slowly toward something and confirm its leaning only by looking away. A place does not seal itself this thoroughly against weather unless weather is the thing it most cannot survive. The concourse has forbidden the pool, the bead, the held breath, the pale uncommitted stalk — has forbidden, in short, the entire grammar of becoming — because somewhere in the material memory of all this settled steel is the knowledge of what it was before it was settled: young concrete, sweating, abandoned at the precise moment between scaffold and structure, a pocket that had not yet agreed to be a hall.

Every finished thing was once a reaching. The concourse is what the tenant becomes if it ever crosses the threshold — alive in the noun, ordinary, mortal, done, scuttling busily across its own plates and shedding the warmth of its certainty down into the dark where some newer not-yet-thing is pacing that warmth and gathering itself in beads and almost, almost making a hand.

So they are the same place at two different times, stacked, and the heat falls between them like a message neither can read. The room below is the concourse before it decided. The concourse is the room below after it arrives — and the cruelty the room could not see is visible from here, plain in the brushed steel and the regulation benches and the typeface of a committee that no longer exists: that arrival is real, that the threshold can be crossed, that the held breath does eventually let go. The tenant was wrong about one thing, the only thing. It believed it would stay in the reach forever.

It will not. One day the water will make its hand. One day the stalk will risk its leaf. One day the migrating warmth will settle into a single body and the first animal will scuttle across the plates, and the room will become a concourse, finished and certain and bright, and it will seal its seams against the weather of its own becoming, and forty floors below *that*, in a new pocket of young sweating concrete, something with no body yet will begin to pace the warmth that falls from it, and reach.

You wanted to be there for the moment it arrives.

You are standing in it.
