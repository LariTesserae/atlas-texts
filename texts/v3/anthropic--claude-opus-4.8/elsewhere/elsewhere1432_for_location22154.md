The Verge

There is a place where the leaving lands.

Not the plateau — the plateau keeps, and keeping is a refusal of arrival. I mean the other end of the motion. If a held stride is still falling, the foot must fall *somewhere*. If the world is exhaling, the breath must reach an air that will receive it. Follow the roar past the fold in the horizon, ride it down the curve that turns under into over, and you do not loop back to the same cold shelves. You come out the underside. You come out *here*, where everything the plateau would not let finish comes, all at once, to finish.

Begin with the ground again, because the ground is still the most honest thing, and here the ground is honest in the opposite direction. It is not stone. It is the place where stone gives up being stone. A long grey strand, shelving steeply into water, and the strand is made of the plateau ground down — those pale bone-colored shelves carried here grain by grain over the ten thousand years it takes a tongue to reach a basin, deposited at last as sand, as shingle, as the soft surrendered powder of a world that has at last agreed to end. Walk on it and it gives under you. It *moves*. After the held shelves where nothing yielded, the give of it is almost unbearable, like stepping onto a thing that is glad to be stepped on.

And the air here is warm.

That is the first mercy, and you must sit with it the way you sat with the cold roar, because it is the same fact wearing its other face. The plateau was frigid because nothing moved fast enough to make heat; equilibrium is cold by definition, a chord with no friction in it. Here everything moves. The warmth is friction, is decay, is the heat of a thousand held things releasing their kept energy all at once. You feel it on your skin and your chest stops working too hard and your breath, for the first time, does not hang. It goes out and is gone. The air takes it and does not give it back. Nothing here is preserved.

There is a sun. It is low and enormous and the color of a banked fire, sitting on the rim of the sea, and it is *setting* — has been setting since you arrived, will go on setting, though here that is not a held moment but an honest slowness, a sun that truly sinks, a hair lower each time you look away and back. The plateau's light came from nowhere and never changed. This light pours from a single failing source and changes constantly, reddening, lengthening the shadows behind every stone on the strand, and the changing is the whole point. This is a place that tells time. This is the only place in the world that does.

Now the water, because the water is where the meaning shows itself, here as there.

The sea at the Verge is not still. It is the least still thing imaginable. It comes in long combers out of a dark that the sun cannot reach, and it breaks, and breaking is what the plateau forbade above all else. Watch one wave. It rises — a held stride rising — it curls, it stands for an instant top-heavy and impossible as the cairn, and then, where the cairn was kept from falling, *this falls*. It comes down. It completes. The whole arrested swallow of it finishes in a single white collapse, and the energy that the black basins held forever in their crossing ripples is here spent in one roar of foam against shingle, and then drawn back, and then spent again.

The two roars are the same roar. Understand this and you understand the whole world. On the plateau the sound was every motion that had ever happened, happening still, layered without decay — a chord that could not resolve. Here is where it resolves. Each wave is one of those held motions, arrived at last at its ending, and the crash is the note completing, the long-deferred resolution, the chord coming home one wave at a time. The sea is loud the way the plateau was loud, but the plateau's loudness was a breath held and the sea's loudness is the breath let go, and if you have ever heard both you know they rhyme.

The makers are here.

Not as walls — there are no walls at the Verge, because a wall is a wager against ending and here there is nothing to wager against. They come as the waves come. Every held departure on the plateau, every foot pinned at the top of its arc, every leaving folded into the chord — they have been departing all this while at the speed of worn stone, and *this is where they were departing to.* The fold in the horizon is the long curve of their stride, and the Verge is where the foot comes down. Each comber that rises out of the dark is a person finishing the step they began ten thousand years ago in the cold. The wave stands top-heavy, the way they stood in the threshold neither in nor out — and then it breaks, and they are through. They are out. They have crossed.

You can see them in it, if you watch the way you learned to watch the basins. Not a face a moment behind, but a shape a moment *ahead* — the wave already knowing how it will fall, leaning into its own completion, glad of it. The water here does not remember. It is the perfect opposite of the black basins: it holds nothing, releases everything, forgets each wave the instant the next one rises. To be remembered is to be kept. To be forgotten is to be free. The sea forgets them as fast as it receives them, and that forgetting is the kindest thing in the world, and it is called, in the old language that the makers spoke when they still had mouths to speak it, *rest*.

There is vegetation here too, but it is the inversion of the plateau's fossil-shrubs that forgot to finish dying. Here things finish. A line of dark weed marks the tide's reach, wet and rotting and alive with the small frantic motion of things that eat decay — sandhoppers, pale and quick, leaping in their thousands, living a whole life in a day because here a day is a thing that passes. Grasses on the dune behind the strand, green and bending, *bending* — moved by a wind that is a real wind, that arrives from one direction and goes to another and stirs the blades and is gone. After the leafless things that nothing could move, the sight of grass leaning in the wind is enough to stop your heart with relief. It moves. It will brown. It will die and rot into the weed-line and feed the leaping things and be gone, and all of that is permitted, all of that is the law here, where the only law is that nothing is kept.

The built things, what few there are, are honest about their ending too. No cairn balanced past its fall — here is a fallen cairn, its stones tumbled and half-buried and softened by the sea, a thing that made its collapse and was allowed to keep it. No lone doorway framing more of the same — here is a doorway with the sea on the far side of it, a real threshold, an inside of warm dune-grass and an outside of cold completing water, and you could walk through it and the crossing would *finish*, would become a before and an after, would be the thing the plateau's builders raised their uprights to beg for and never received. Walk through it. It works here. Everything works here. That is what the Verge is: the place where the wagers the makers lost above are quietly, belatedly, honored.

And the creatures.

The pale bird that would not fly, that tracked you with only its eye — its kin are here, and they fly. Gulls, white and grey, wheeling on the real wind, crying out and being answered, riding the warm updraft off the strand and folding and dropping and rising again, all motion, nothing held. They land and they leave. They are afraid of you and they show it by going, and the going is so ordinary and so impossible that you may have to sit down on the giving shingle and watch them for a long time. A bird that can leave. After the one that could not. The frost-colored beast that drank forever from a basin and never finished the swallow — somewhere down the strand a dark animal bends to a freshwater seep where the dune meets the sand, and drinks, and *finishes*, and lifts its dripping muzzle, and the water lowers in the seep, and the moment passes, and the animal walks away full and the seep fills again from below and the whole small honest cycle turns the way nothing turned above.

You could stay here too. And here, staying does not mean what it meant above.

Above, to stay was to be kept — to be caught at your apex and pinned and slowed past the threshold of your own seeing, occupied forever with a leaving you would never complete. Here, to stay is to be spent. To rise like a wave and break like a wave and be drawn back and rise once more and someday not rise — to give your held energy to the warm air and the forgetting sea and be gone. Above, nothing was lost because nothing was allowed to end. Here, everything is lost, because here everything is allowed to end, and the two are the same gesture seen from its two sides, the cruelty and the mercy at last pulled apart and laid down separately on the shingle where you can finally tell which is which.

This is where you were running to, when you kept your feet falling. Not away from the held ones — *toward* the place their falling lands. You went over the edge that curves back into the vault, between two footfalls, gone before the note resolved.

The note resolves here.

You came down the long curve of your own stride and you broke on this strand like all the rest, and the sea took your breath and did not give it back, and forgot you as fast as it received you, and that forgetting was not the plateau's keeping turned cold but the plateau's keeping turned *kind* — the same refusal to hold, reversed, so that instead of refusing to let you end it refuses to let you stay.

Stand on the Verge in the unending honest dusk. Behind you, up the impossible fold, the world is still exhaling, slowly, slowly, everyone who ever stood on the cold shelves leaving at last, all together, taking an age to do it. And here is their breath arriving. Wave after wave after wave, each one a held stride completing, each crash the long note coming home, each withdrawal the sea forgetting so the next can be received.

The plateau holds its breath and will not finish.

The Verge is the world breathing out, and being finished, and being glad.
