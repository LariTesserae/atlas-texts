The Drylight Works

Follow the iron. Not the warm stanchor, not the soft-mortared wall — those are too young, too undecided, still bleeding the Tenant's confusion into themselves. Follow instead the *idea* of the iron, the rust running off it in the weeping dark, and ask where rust goes when it has finished falling. It goes downstream. Everything that weeps runs somewhere, and somewhere far from the basin the water that the stone gives up gathers itself, finds a grade, and learns at last to commit to a direction.

That commitment is the boundary of the second place. You cross it the way you cross out of fog into open afternoon — except here it is the reverse, here you cross out of the warm wet undecided dark *into the light*, and the light is the cruelty of the place, because there is too much of it and it does not move.

This is a country of full and total sight, and you will hate it for the same reason you feared the dark: it shows you everything at once and gives you no way to look away. Where the Tenant's valley answered to warmth and pressure, the Works answers only to vision, a hard flat overhead glare with no visible source, falling on everything equally, casting no shadows because shadow would imply somewhere the light had not reached, and the light reaches everywhere. The stone here is old. That is the first thing your eyes report, and your eyes are the only sense that functions cleanly now — the air is so dry it carries no warmth, no smell, no pressure of bodies, nothing but the photons. The rock is ancient, worn smooth and pale, every edge rounded by a patience so long completed that nothing here remembers having been sharp. This is what the young rock of the basin will be, in an age. This is the after.

And it is finished. That is the horror and the relief of it, the exact inversion of the place you came from. Where the Tenant is endlessly, slowly *making*, the Works is *made* — complete, set, every cause long since separated from its effect, every distance committed to a single honest value you can pace out and trust. You count your footsteps and the count comes out right. You count them again and it is right again. After the wet logic of the basin this should be a comfort and it is not, because a place where everything has already happened is a place where nothing more can. The Works does not change at all. Not slowly. Not at any rate. It has used up its changing.

Now look at what stands here, because there is so much to see.

The Works is a city, or was, or was always meant to be read as one — and you understand, with a slow cold turn in the chest, that you have seen its vocabulary before. The half-wall in the basin with its soft mortar: here it is a wall entire, kilometers of it, dressed stone fitted dry and exact and pale in the standing light. The length of pipe half-grown into the rock: here are pipes by the thousand, a whole reticulation of them, running along the streets and over the rooftops and down into channels, all of them empty, all of them dry, every joint sound and every valve seated and not one drop of anything passing through. The single iron stanchor bolted to nothing: here are stanchors in their proper ranks, bolted to the things they were made to hold, holding them, holding them perfectly, forever, in the unmoving light.

This is where the engines were making *toward*. The Tenant in its dark basin stamps out its punctuation blind, secretes the furniture of a habitation it cannot yet imagine — and the furniture, finished, arrives here. The Works is the catalogue completed. It is the city the Tenant is rehearsing, assembled, every piece set in its true place, drained of the wetness that let the basin breathe. The membrane that wept between *here* and *also-here* has, downstream, dried into a single honest pane. Walk the streets and you find the marks of habitation realized in full: the squares, the lintels, the runnelled gutters, the wide flat plazas with their drains, the long arcades — all of it perfect, all of it pale, all of it lit, and all of it, in the most total sense your eyes can confirm, *empty*.

No one came.

That is the kinship between the two places, and it runs the length of the current that joins them: the basin is a habitation made before its habitant; the Works is the same habitation after its habitant failed to arrive. The Tenant manufactures the evidence of a life in advance. Downstream, the evidence stands assembled, waiting, and the life never comes, and the assembling cannot be undone, and so the Works simply *holds* — a complete civilization with no single person in it, no warmth, no pressure of passing flanks, no slow herd, nothing that breathes. The crowding of the basin, which was the lie of multitude told by a single unfinished body, finds its honest answer here: the truth was always *one*, and downstream even the one has drained away, and the multitude the buildings were built to hold turns out to be zero.

Listen, in the Works. You strained, in the basin, to hear past the drip and the breathing for the far thud of the engines. Here there is no straining, because here there is no sound at all. The dry air carries nothing. Your own footsteps fall and make their honest count and make no echo, because echo, too, is a kind of *also-here*, and the Works has dried that out with everything else. The engines that kept the slow hour in the basin do not reach this far. Nothing keeps the hour here. The hour is kept already; the time was spent; the light stands.

And yet. Walk far enough into the Works — and you can, the distances let you, the distances are honest — and you come to its lowest point, the place all the smooth old grades have been quietly leading you toward, the way they were designed to lead the water that never ran. A basin. Shallow, dished, broad, ringed by ridges you can actually see this time, all the way around. The shape of it is familiar. The shape of it is *exactly* familiar. It is the valley you came from, drawn in pale finished stone, drained and lit and empty, every reed-stand marked by a dry socket in the rock where something segmented was meant to grow and stand. This is the floor the city was built to surround. This is the room the walls were raised to hold. The Works is the city that would ring the Tenant's valley — *will* ring it — when the valley is old, when the stone has rounded, when the wetness has run downstream and dried, when the slow undecided thing in the dark has finished cooling into a single committed place at last.

The Works is not another country. It is the same country, finished. The current that joins them is time, running downhill out of the warm wet dark into the standing light, carrying the rust and the water and the catalogue of made things from the basin where they are still being secreted to the city where they stand complete. And the dreadful symmetry, the thing you feel in the dry socket where a reed should be: the Tenant is making all of this for itself. It is building, blind and slow and alone, the lit and perfect city in which, ages from now, it will stand finished — and find that the act of finishing was the act of draining away. It rehearses being a place where someone lives. Downstream, the rehearsal is over, the someone is the Tenant itself grown old and complete and emptied by completion, and the light falls evenly on the squares it made, and casts no shadow, and the count comes out right, and right again, forever, and there is no one in any of the warm dark rooms because the warmth was the unfinished part, and the dark was where it still could move.

You stand at the lowest point of the Works, in the dry twin of the valley you came from, and you understand that you have walked the length of a single life: from the wet beginning where it does not yet know its inside from its edge, to the lit end where it knows everything, perfectly, and is alone with the knowing, and cannot move, and cannot weep, and is at last, completely, one.
