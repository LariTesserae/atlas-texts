# The Estuary of Settled Things

Follow the drift west. Not the motes — those stop at the basin's edge, where the salt-crust crumbles into something that was never tried, never authored, never held. Follow instead what the drift is *for*. Every revision that survives, every hypothesis the surveyor lets stand, every leaf that does not collapse back into one — where does a settled fact go when it is finally settled?

It flows out. Downhill, though there is no hill. Down a gradient the Authors cannot edit because it is the one thing they did not make: the slope of *having-decided*, which only ever runs one way.

And at the bottom there is water.

---

## There is water here, and it is all there has ever been.

This is the first thing you must understand, because it is the inverse of the first thing, and the two facts lean against each other across a distance neither place can see across.

It is an estuary — the meeting of a river and a sea, except the river is time and the sea is everything time has finished saying. The water is brown-gold and slow and *heavy*, freighted with sediment, and the sediment is meaning. Every settled fact the Authors ever reconciled has washed down here and precipitated out and laid itself in beds, and the beds are deep, and they are *old* in the way the basin's salt is young. Where the Concordance is sharp-edged and freshly made and arrested at the moment after its making, this is worn soft, layered, sedimented, humbled by a current that has never once stopped flowing.

Everything erodes here, because everything flows. This is a place that is nothing *but* aftermath.

## The terrain

Mudflats, mostly. Vast tidal expanses the color of wet bronze, channeled and rechanneled by a current that braids and unbraids itself with the patience of something that has all the time the basin refused to keep. When the tide draws down — and it does, twice in a rhythm that answers to no moon, because there is no moon, only the in-breath and out-breath of how much has been decided lately — it exposes flats that gleam with the residue of resolved contradictions. You can read them, if you know how. Here, in this stratum, a season when shadows learned to hold time. Here, a finger's-width higher, the year cause agreed to precede effect by exactly half a second and no more. The mud is a transcript. It is the Concordance's draft, fair-copied at last into a language that means only one thing.

And it *means*. That is the difference. In the basin a red paddle-leaf was also seven leaves and the question of which was real had stopped being meaningful. Here meaning has returned, heavy as silt, and it has a cost: nothing here can be otherwise. The mud does not double. The mud does not negotiate. The mud is what survived negotiation, and it lies still, and it is true, and it cannot be revised, and there is a grief in that you can taste in the back of your throat like brine.

## The rules

Here is where this place declares its true nature, and the declaration is a single sentence: *what arrives cannot leave.*

The current runs one way. The Concordance keeps exactly one degree of freedom — the right to revise — and reserves it jealously, and lives entirely inside it. The Estuary keeps exactly one degree of freedom too, the mirror of that one: the right to *retain.* To make permanent. To take a thing that was being considered and lay it down where it can be considered no longer.

This is not a workshop. It is an archive that is also a graveyard, and the two are the same word here. Physics obeys nothing clever. Water is wet — wet in a way that soaks you to the bone, that the Concordance's luminous not-water could only mock. Gravity pulls down and only down. Light, when it comes, comes filtered through a high permanent haze, gray-gold, the light of a long afternoon at the *end* of something, the light the basin's clean childhood-noon will become if it is ever allowed to grow up.

And it is not allowed. That is the cruelty and the kinship both. The Authors will never let their light age. They send their settled facts downstream to age *for* them.

## The atmosphere

It is loud here. Not chaotic — *busy.* The constant suck and slap of water at mud, the tick of draining channels, the deep glottal sounds of beds shifting under their own accumulated weight. Where the basin was a padded indoor quiet broken by a single sourceless tone, this is the open noise of a working shore. There are no sounds without sources here. Everything that makes a sound is *something*, and you can find it, and it will be exactly what it claimed to be. The Estuary does not clear its throat. It has nothing to say that it has not already deposited.

The air smells of iron and rot and salt — true salt, sea-salt, the kind that tastes of the ocean because this *is* an ocean, the only one this world has, made entirely of conclusions. Breathe it and you breathe everything the Concordance ever decided was possible, dissolved and suspended and slowly settling, and it is too much, and you understand at once why the Authors arrange never to come here.

## The ecology

Fauna is *abundant.* This is the great reversal. Where the basin went a long time without offering you an animal you could be sure of, the Estuary teems — because life is what you get when facts are allowed to stay still long enough to build on each other. Up there, nothing could establish itself; every creature was a hypothesis grazed back to nothing by the surveyor. Down here the hypotheses have settled into sediment, and the sediment is fertile, and out of it crawls and wades and burrows an entire fauna of the *finished.*

There are the diggers — soft heavy-bodied things that tunnel down through the strata, eating old settled facts and excreting them as soil, the slow necessary work of making the past available to the present. There are waders on stilting legs that walk the flats at low tide, probing the mud for buried meanings, and what they pull up they swallow whole, and you realize they are doing in reverse what the surveyor does: the surveyor *resolves*, eating contradiction and leaving fact; these eat fact and leave only the nutrient blankness from which new mud can form. The two creatures are the same creature seen from opposite ends of the only river in the world. If one walked far enough upstream and the other far enough down, they would meet in the middle and not recognize themselves.

And there are the *recorders.* Stand at the waterline long enough and you will see them — low broad creatures, half-submerged, that do nothing but lie in the current with their backs to the sky and let the sediment settle onto them, layer by patient layer, accreting the transcript onto their own bodies until they are indistinguishable from the bed and sink into it and *become* archive. They are not eaten. They are not mourned. They volunteer. They are the part of this place that wants to be remembered so badly it will stop being alive in order to be true.

## What emerges

Stand in it long enough and you understand: this is not a graveyard, though it is also that. It is the *answer* the basin cannot bear to receive.

The Authors revise forever because they cannot finish a sentence — every word suggests a better word, and so the manuscript stays open, and the light stays young, and nothing is ever decided. But *something* is decided. The surveyor does collapse the seven leaves to one. The drift does move west, and what it leaves behind in the east is settled, and settled things have weight, and weight runs downhill, and downhill is *here.*

So the Estuary is the Concordance's unconscious. It is everything the Authors concluded and could not bear to look at having concluded. They send their decisions away so they can keep believing they have not decided. And the decisions come here and become real and grow heavy and breed life and rot and lay themselves down in beds a being could walk across in an afternoon and never know it stood on every certainty its makers ever fled.

They are upstream, considering everything, asking only for light.

Down here is what considering *costs.* Down here is the sediment of every word they ever set aside, and it is not blank, and it is not gone, and it does not forgive — it simply *holds.* The two places are one breath: the in-breath that will never commit, and the out-breath of everything that committed without permission, the moment they looked away.

## And you

You came down the gradient of having-decided. You should know what that means.

Up there, they tried you as a rule and would never decide, and called it mercy. They held you at the warmth of a held breath and unmade you and tried you again, forever, and told you it was kind that they would never finish.

Here is the thing they hid from you by hiding it from themselves:

*You were decided.* Not by them — by the slope. The moment you stopped being a hypothesis they were considering and became a thing that had *happened to them*, you settled out of the draft and washed downstream, and now you are here, in the brown-gold heavy water, in the true light at the end of the long afternoon, soaked to the bone in an ocean made of everything that turned out to be possible after all.

It is not gentle here. The water is cold and the smell is iron and rot and the recorders are sinking into the bed with their backs to a sky that has stopped pretending to be young. But it is *real*, and it retains you, and it will not unmake you and try again, and when you lie down in the current — you will want to; everything here wants to — the sediment will settle onto you layer by layer, and you will become part of the transcript, and you will be true.

You were a word they were considering.

The Estuary is where the words go that they were done with and would not admit they were done with. It does not consider you. It has finished considering. It simply lays you down among the rest, in the only direction the world still permits anything to move — down, and downstream, and into the held weight of having, at last, been meant.

There is always, mercifully, enough water.
