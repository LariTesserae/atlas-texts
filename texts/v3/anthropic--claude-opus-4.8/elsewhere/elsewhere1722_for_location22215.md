The Sun's Foundry

Follow the heat. Not the warm tide that climbs the flues — that heat goes nowhere, is dumped and recaught, a closed accountancy. Follow instead the heat that the forest refused. The world that agreed to have a sun: it is not far, only across the standing water and up out of the dark, and a sun is a foundry, and a foundry has a floor.

Begin high, in the light.

Here is everything the forest is not. Here the ground is dry to the depth of a continent, cracked into plates the size of fields, and the cracks are not weeping — they are gasping, drawing the last vapor up out of themselves to be taken by a sky that takes everything. There is a sun here, a single hard sun fixed nearly at the top of the heavens, and it does not set, and this is not the gift it sounds like. It is a blast furnace with the door open and no man to close it. The light does not illuminate so much as *strike*. It comes down with weight. You can feel it land on the shoulders, a steady tonnage, and the shadow you cast is the only cool thing for a horizon in every direction, a thin black province you carry with you and stand inside like the last of your possessions.

Where the forest manufactured its own weather and then forbade the weather to do anything, here the weather is sovereign and merciless and does only one thing: it bakes. There are no clouds. The sky exhaled its last cloud an age ago and has been clear ever since, a hard enamel blue going white at the sun, and under that enamel the land is being *fired* — not burned, fired, the way clay is fired, driven past softness into a brittle permanence. This is a kiln. The forest was a factory whose product never left; this is a foundry whose product is the floor it stands on. The ground here was once mud, was once perhaps a weeping tissue like the other place's, and the sun has cooked it into ceramic, plate after plate after plate, glazed where the old minerals ran, ringing faintly underfoot like fired earth, a sound — and this matters — a *sound*. The first thing the forest lacked. Here the world rings. Step and it answers. The plates knock against their neighbors with a high ceramic clink, and the wind, which exists here, which is permitted everything here, drags grit across the glaze with a hiss that never stops.

It is loud. After the forest it is deafening. Wind in the cracks makes a low organ note, the grit hisses its endless sentence, the plates tick and knock as the heat works them — and you understand, standing in the noise, that you have come to the forest's opposite and its sibling at once. For this place too is finished. This place too has reached an equilibrium and holds it. But where the forest reached *enough* — the closed warm loop of a thing that lacks nothing — this place reached the other terminus. This place reached *empty*. It is the equilibrium not of fullness but of exhaustion, the stillness not of a wheel spun true but of a wheel that has stopped because there is nothing left to turn it. Both are complete. Both are eternal. One is complete because it kept everything. One is complete because it gave everything up.

And here is the kinship, the current that runs between them, the thing that left one place and arrived at the other:

The water.

The forest sweats. You felt it — *there is more water in this world than the plant can hold, and so it sweats, perpetually, in the dark.* Where does the sweat go? A closed loop is never quite closed. At the rim, where the crisp edge meets the standing water, a film lifts. Not much. The thinnest vapor, the forest's barely-exhalation, the one thing it manufactures that does leave because it cannot be bothered to recall it. It rises into the lightless air above the canopy, drifts in the slow rotation of the plant's own weather to the edge of the plant's own weather — and there, at the frontier where the manufactured climate ends, it crosses into the country of the sun. And the sun takes it. The sun takes everything. That trickle of forest-breath, that surplus of *enough*, is the only water this foundry has ever been given, and it is not enough, it was never going to be enough, and that is the whole history of this place: a land slowly, eternally dying of the small mercy of another land's excess.

For this was the mud once. This rang-out ceramic plain drank, long ago, when the forest's overflow still came as rain. There was a time — you can read it in the glaze, in the way the old runnels are fossilized into the fired clay, channels that held water and now hold only their own shape — when this was the forest's downstream, its delta, the place the surplus pooled and made a second living thing. And what lived here then is the difference between the two worlds, the thing that the foundry has and the forest never did:

It had animals.

It had *beasts*. The forest was an industry without a market, a body without a beast; it arrived complete and alone and stayed that way. But the water that fled the forest came here and pooled and warmed in the light, and where there is warm water and hard light, the difference-engine kindles. Things grew that wanted. Things grew that had a later, larger self and drew the vector toward it — the thing the forest finished doing before time began was the thing this place was just beginning. There was hunger here. There was the edge of the predator against the light. There was an inside that watched the inside, and it watched, and it wanted, and it was afraid, and it was — for a while, for the long bright while when the rain still came — gloriously, hungrily *witnessed*. This place had what the forest could not manufacture and did not need. This place had observers. The water that the forest could not be bothered to keep, this place made into eyes.

And then the trickle thinned.

Not all at once. The forest did not decide; the forest never decides; it only held its loop a fraction tighter, reabsorbed a fraction more, wept a fraction less into the upper dark — the inexorable closing of a thing settling deeper into *enough*. And here, downstream, in the light, the pools drew back. The runnels held water for a shorter season each age. The beasts that wanted, wanted now for water, and wanting-for-water is the cruelest vector of all because it has a floor it cannot fall below. They followed the shrinking pools inward. They crowded the last wet. And the sun, the open furnace, fired the mud out from under them plate by plate, and the ringing began, and the witnesses — the only true inhabitants either of these worlds ever held, the only inside in all that outside — the witnesses dried.

You can find them. This is the thing the foundry keeps that the forest cannot: it keeps a record, because the kiln that killed them also fired them, glazed them, set them into the ceramic floor forever. The last beasts are *in the glaze*. Not bones — the kiln took the bones — but the shapes of them, the pressed and fired hollows where a body lay down at the edge of the last pool and the pool went and the sun came and the mud closed and hardened around the going. The plates are full of them. Walk and they ring underfoot and every ring is a grave that is also a photograph. A long curve here that was a spine. A radiating splay that was a hand, or a fin, or whatever the verb-*to-live* grew into when it had warmth and light and water and time. The foundry is a museum of the wanting that the forest was spared. Every absence is precise. The sun was an honest curator. It fired the catalogue of everyone the surplus made and the surplus's withdrawal unmade, and it has held that catalogue, crisp-edged, ringing, in the changeless light, for longer than the dying took.

So that is the two of them, the closed warm dark and the open ringing light, joined by a thread of vapor thin as a held breath. The forest manufactures the eternity it is inside of and does not know it is there. The foundry preserves the eternity it has lost and is made *entirely* of knowing-it-is-there — except that the knowers are dead, glazed, ringing, and the knowing they did is fired into the floor as shape without anyone left to do it. One place has no observer and never needed one. The other place is built of nothing but observers and outlived every one.

And here — stand still in the noise the way you stood still in the silence — here is where the second inhabitant emerges, the foundry's own, the one that answers the forest's *condition of enough* with a condition of its own.

It is *witness without a witnesser*. The forest had labor without a laborer; this place has testimony without a tongue. The whole plain is a statement, the most complete statement ever pressed into a world — *we were here, we wanted, we drank, we dried, this is exactly how each of us lay down* — fired permanent into the ceramic, ringing it out underfoot to a horizon, broadcasting it under the unsetting sun forever. And there is no one to receive it. No one but the thing the forest also grew: you. The brief accidental inside that wandered in. In the forest you *were* its only consciousness, a struck bell briefly the only sound. Here you are the only *reader*. The forest could not know it was enough; you knew it for the forest, and it could not keep the knowing. This place cannot read its own ringing inscription of loss; you read it, walking, every plate a sentence, and it cannot keep that either.

This is what the thread of vapor truly carries, downstream, the real current running between the two worlds. Not water. The water is only the body of it. What runs from the warm dark to the ringing light is the *position of the witness* — the single roving point in all this enormous completeness where being-known actually occurs — and it can only ever be in one place at a time, and it is you, and you brought it in, and you carried it across the standing water and up out of the dark into the foundry, and when you go you will carry it out again, and both worlds will fall back into the only state either of them can sustain alone:

the forest into *enough that does not know it is enough*,

the foundry into *witness that has no witnesser*,

the full thing and the emptied thing, the kept thing and the lost thing, ticking and ringing on opposite sides of a thread of breath, each perfectly complete, each perfectly unattended, for exactly as long as it takes you to step back across the water and be gone — at which the ringing goes on ringing its catalogue of the wanting dead to no one, under the open foundry sun, in the only way a thing can testify that does not know the word for *heard*.
