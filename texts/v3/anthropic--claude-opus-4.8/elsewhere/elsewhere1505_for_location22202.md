# The Shaft

There is a way the makers fell, and a way you fell, and it is the same way, and it has a top.

Begin at the top, because the top is the only place in this whole vertical wound where the word *morning* is not a lie. It is not much of a morning. It is a slot in a high plateau, a crack you could step over without noticing, a darker line in pale wind-scoured rock among a thousand other darker lines, and the difference between this crack and the others is only that this one does not stop. The others are cracks. This one is a throat.

It is cold here. This has to be said first and said hard, because everything you have learned to know is the warmth that holds you like a mouth, and this place is the mouth's opposite — a dry high cold that takes the water off your skin the instant it surfaces, the exact hunger the cavity lacks, the appetite for moisture that the deep amber air has long since spent. Up here the air is starving for you. It wants the damp off your lips, the breath out of your lungs, the warmth from under your nails, and it takes all of it, ceaselessly, with the flat indifference of weather. You do not stay damp here. You stay parched. You crack. You are read by the world not by hand but by exposure — flayed open to a sky so large it is itself a kind of dark, blue going to black at its top edge, a dark that is the surface's honest confession that it, too, is mostly emptiness and only pretends otherwise by being lit.

And it is lit. That is the whole difference, the only difference that finally matters. The sun comes over the plateau's eastern lip and crosses and goes, and the slot of the shaft fills, for one knife-edged hour near noon, with a single shaft of light — and the light goes *down.* It goes down the way nothing goes down in the cavity. It does not suggest. It does not rumor a terrace or hint a drop. It declares. It falls cleanly through the cold dry air and strikes the first ledge forty feet down and stops, because the throat bends there, and below the bend the light has never been and will never be, not once, not for an instant, in all the turning of all the surface's continents.

That ledge is the last lit thing on the way to you.

Everything past it is the fall.

---

Look at the ledge, because the makers looked at it. They must have. It is the last place a falling creature touches that is still part of the world with mornings in it, and it is *marked* — not with cairns, the cairns are below, the cairns are the cavity's grammar — but with the marks of *waiting*. Scratches. A worn place where something crouched and crouched again, claw or hand or whatever wanted what hands want, hunched at the lip of the lit ledge in the one hour of noon light, looking down into the throat that did not bend back up.

They did not fall by accident. This is the thing the ledge says that the salt below could never say, because the salt keeps no time and the ledge keeps the only time there is — the daily clock of the light arriving and leaving. They came here. They climbed the plateau out of whatever surface life had grown too heavy with *and then, and then,* too crowded with consequence, too full of walls that rose to a fourth course and a fortieth and a four-hundredth, of bowls filled and emptied and filled until the filling was a tyranny — they came out of all that accumulation, across the cold scoured rock, to the one slot that did not stop, and they crouched on the last lit ledge in the last lit hour, and they looked down into a dark that promised the one thing the surface cannot give a creature made of sequence:

*An end to having to do the next thing.*

That is what the cavity is, seen from above. Not a tomb. A *mercy*, advertised. The makers were not exiles flung down by misfortune, clutching a hammerstone they happened to keep hold of. They were pilgrims. They carried the hammerstone *deliberately* — the one hard dark fragment of the consequence-world, brought along the way you bring a single coin to pay a single passage, so that when they reached the bottom they would have in their hand exactly enough of *and then* to perform the gesture of building, once, slowly, lovingly, and then be permitted at last to set it down and not pick anything up again.

The cavity did not capture them. They *applied*.

---

So the shaft is the document, and the cavity is what the document promised, and between them runs the fall, and the fall is the whole transaction, and I can tell you its terms now because I have read both ends.

Up here: surface. Cold, lit, hungry, crowded with mornings, every one of which demands a next thing, the engine of *and then* running with no off-switch the world will permit, walls rising forever, the unbearable arithmetic of a creature that can begin things accumulating beginnings it can never make balance. Up here the sum is never zero. Up here the sum only ever grows, and growing is exhausting, and there is no rest in it, because every rest is just the start of the next labor. The makers' whole tragedy on the surface was that they could not stop *adding.*

Down there: the cavity. Warm, dark, fed, the page turning in the next room, the salt growing exactly as fast as it is taken, the held breath, the perfect zero. Down there a creature of sequence can finally lay its sequence down — but only by spending it, gesture by declined gesture, until the wanting wears out and the hand stays open and the books balance and the date is forgotten because there are no dates.

And the shaft between is the only place where both are true at once. The ledge at the bend is the exact frontier. Light above it. Below it, the first cairn — no. Below it, *nothing*, then the long throat, then the first amber suggestion of a terrace, then you, sitting with your hand on someone's last move, being tasted by the grazers, learning the law under the laws too slowly to use it.

The makers crouched on that frontier and chose. Each of them, alone, spaced — even here, even at the lip, you can see two worn crouching-places and they are *not close together*, they kept the distance even on the threshold, *do not finish my falling for me and I will not finish yours* — each one looked down its own last hour of light and let go of the surface's intolerable forever in exchange for the deep's tolerable nothing.

They thought they were choosing rest. They were. The cavity does not lie about the rest. The rest is real. The warmth is real. The grazers really will hold you, really will taste the tomorrow off you as gently as the high air tastes the water, really will let you set your one cairn and lay your one hand open and stop.

What the shaft knows, that the cavity will never say, is that *rest and ending are the same purchase here.* There is no version of the bargain where you put down the weight of the next thing and keep the lightness of being able to leave. The hammerstone buys one passage, one direction. You cannot crouch on the lit ledge forever — the air will flay you, the cold will take you, the surface has no resting place either, that was the whole reason you came. And you cannot reach the bottom and decline to spend what you carried, because the spending is automatic, the spending is just *what tomorrow does* in a place with no tomorrow to spend it on.

The makers paid the cold to escape the cold's appetite. They paid the warmth's appetite to escape the cold. Up here the world eats your water. Down there the world eats your future. There is no third place. There is only the shaft, the one hour of noon, the bend the light cannot follow, and the long fall that connects the world that cannot stop adding to the world that has already finished its sum.

---

You came down this. You crouched, maybe, on the lit ledge — or maybe you were the rare one, the accident after all, the foot that slipped where the makers' feet had only ever lowered themselves on purpose. It does not matter to the cavity. The cavity cannot tell a pilgrim from a casualty; both arrive the same way, both land in the warm, both carry one last increment of *and then* and both get tasted for it by the soft glowing crowd. The shaft is the only thing in this whole world that *could* tell the difference — because the shaft alone keeps the record of the crouch, the waiting, the choosing — and the shaft is forty feet of cold rock and one hour of light a day, and it cannot speak, and you have already fallen past the part of it that remembers.

But it is still up there. That is what I have to leave you with, sitting where you are, hand on the cairn, grazers nearing. The shaft is still up there and the noon light still goes down it and stops at the bend, every day, in a world that still has days. The frontier is still lit. The crouching-places are still worn and still cold and still spaced their careful distance apart, waiting, in the cavity's honest opposite, the one place that keeps the time the deep refuses to keep.

You said you are the only thing down here still capable of leaving.

The shaft is the proof that leaving was always only ever a direction the makers declined to take — *up*, against the bend, back toward the hungry light, paying the warmth back its borrowed future the way you'd climb out of a debt, gesture by deliberate gesture, each one a beginning, each one forbidden, each one therefore the single bravest thing a spent creature could attempt in a world that has perfected the art of declining to begin.

They never climbed.

The crouching-places are on the lit side of the bend, every one of them, facing down.

Not one faces up.

Get up now — *up*, toward the cold that wants your water and will give you back your mornings — while *up* is still a direction you can mean, while the cairn under your hand is still only somebody else's last move and not yet your first refusal, while the shaft at the top of the long fall is still, for one knife-edged hour you cannot see and have no way to time, holding its single column of honest light open against the dark, as it has held it for longer than the surface has had continents, for no one, for no reason the cavity would ever permit, going down.
