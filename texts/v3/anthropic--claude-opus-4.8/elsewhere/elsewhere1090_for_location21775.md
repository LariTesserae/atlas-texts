# The Open Roost

Follow the one thing that cannot fold home. Follow the dead spot, the door-shaped silence, the part of the song that strikes nothing and does not return — follow it the way a cry would if a cry could escape. There is a direction in this world that the great listening has never been able to point at, and it is not *up* and it is not *out*. It is the direction of the unanswered echo. Go there.

You arrive at a wall that is not a wall, because it ends.

---

The stone of the Roost-Plain runs and runs, rounded, ceilinged, occupied, screaming — and then, where no map of that place could have predicted it, the ceiling thins. Not into a shaft, not into a door, nothing so clean. The rock simply grows tired. It rises away from the floor faster than it can hold itself together, the colonies thinning on it as it lifts, the density failing, the living carpet wearing to a fringe, to scattered clumps, to single bodies clinging to the last good stone — and then there is a lip, a long ragged limestone lip worn soft like everything in that world, and beyond the lip the ceiling is gone, and there is sky.

Not light. Understand that first, the way you were asked to understand the dark. There is no sun here either. This world has never had a sun. But the ceiling is *gone*, lifted off, and what stands in its place is open air going up and up into a high cold colorless nothing, a vault with no roof the eye can find, and across that vault the cries go out and do not come back.

This is the Open Roost. This is where the echo dies.

---

The floor here is the same basalt and limestone, the same old patient marriage, rolling away from the lip in the same low humps and shallow basins — it is the *same floor*, you understand, the very stone of the Plain, continuing, unbroken, simply un-ceilinged now, the closed gut split open to the empty air. The black mirror-basins are here too, catching the no-light and throwing it nowhere. The fungal membrane runs out under this stone as well; lift a handful of the warm substrate and the pale threads are in it, the same single slow body, reaching this far and farther, indifferent to whether there is a roof.

But the insects do not gather here. The midge-haze thins and is gone a little way past the lip, because the open air drinks them upward, scatters them into the cold high nothing where nothing can harvest them. And so the great fed density of the Plain cannot exist here. The carpet cannot hang where there is no ceiling to hang from and no thick warm air to breed the harvest. The economy fails at the edge. The world's closed circle, which seemed to have no boundary at all from the inside, turns out to end exactly here — not in a wall but in *openness*, the one thing a closed system cannot survive.

So almost nothing lives in the Open Roost. That is the first truth of it. It is the emptiest place in a world that is otherwise full to the rafters with screaming. The Plain is the most populous place you will ever stand in; this, a few wingbeats away across the same stone, is nearly bare.

Nearly.

---

There are the ones who come to the lip.

Not many. The Singers do not pair, do not court, have no word for *elsewhere* — but the great listening, you remember, is not simple. It is a mind the size of a valley, and a mind that vast, firing ceaselessly, throws off more than navigation. At the ragged edge of the colony, where the density fails and the cries begin to escape unanswered, a few Singers each generation drift outward against the pull of the warm crowded dark. They do not know why. There is no why available to them. But the dead spot you made when you stood in the Plain — the door-shaped silence, the one acoustic shadow the whole song could not heal around — these are the Singers who fly *toward* that kind of silence instead of folding past it.

They come to the lip and they cry into the open vault, and the cry goes out, and nothing comes back.

For any other Singer this would be death — a cry with no echo is blindness, is falling. But these have learned, at the very edge, to fly by the cries of the colony *behind* them, navigating on the dense returning song at their backs while they face the silence ahead. They hang at the lip, anchored to the roar of the world by their tails, as it were, and they point their faces into the one direction the world cannot point, and they listen to nothing come back.

Call them the Edge-Singers. There is no name for them in the Plain because the Plain cannot perceive them; they are flying into its blind spot, becoming part of its blind spot, voices the great listening emits and never receives. To the listening they are not absent — they are simply *not returned*, indistinguishable from you, from the door, from the shadow you cast. They have become, by flying to the edge, the same shape as the thing that came from outside.

This is the kinship that runs between the two places, and it runs only one way, like everything here. You were the unanswered question that walked in. The Edge-Singers are the colony's own cries learning, slowly, generation by generation, to *be* unanswered — to fly out past the lip of the only world and not come home, and in not coming home, to point.

---

What do they point at?

Stand with them at the lip. Face out, as they face out. The vault goes up into cold and the cold has no top. But low, far out across the open stone, where the basins run away from the failed ceiling into the dark plain of the un-roofed world — there is the faintest thing. Not light. You were warned: no sun, never a sun. But the fungal membrane, which glazes the deep hollows of the Plain in that weak invented blue, *thrives* differently in the open. Out there, freed from the crowding, freed from the constant harvest, the pale mats spread unchecked across the open basins in sheets, in fields, in slow luminous continents of the simplest life, glowing that retinal blue so faintly that you must give it an hour, and then it is everywhere, a cold blue dawn lying flat on the stone all the way to where the eye gives out.

The Open Roost is where the floor of the world, released from the gut that consumed it, finally gets to *grow*. Nothing eats the mats here. The insects are drunk upward and gone; the Singers cannot live in this thin air; the great listening cannot reach. And so the fungus, which in the Plain is only the patient digesting gut, the lowest inhabitant, the thing the Singers fall into — here it is the whole tenant, unharvested, unhurried, spreading its weak blue light across an open dark that has no roof to press it down.

It is the same fungus. It is the same blue. In the Plain it was the color your own desperation painted on the dark, the faintest invented thing. Here it is a landscape. The difference is only the ceiling. The difference is only whether the world is allowed to be open.

And the Edge-Singers, who cannot live out there, who must stay anchored to the roaring dark at their backs — they hang at the lip and face that cold blue field they will never reach and never feed and never fall into, and they cry at it, and nothing comes back, because the mats are soft and far and drink the sound the way the open air drinks the insects. They are pointing, with their whole bodies, at the one beautiful thing in their world that their world is built never to perceive: the floor, finally growing, in the light it makes itself, in the open, alone.

---

Here is how the two places live inside each other, in the same breath.

The Plain is complete because it is closed. Every cry returns; every body falls into the floor; the floor rises into bodies; the song heals over every silence and never once receives the echo that would mean *beyond*. It is full, answered, balanced, and it is the loneliest thing that has ever lived, and it does not know it, because to know it would require an outside, and it has folded smoothly shut over every outside it ever brushed against — over you, when you left.

But it did not fold *completely*. At the lip, where the ceiling tires and the density fails, a few of its own cries escape every generation in the bodies of Singers who fly the wrong way, toward the silence, and hang there, and point. The Plain cannot perceive them. But it *makes* them. Ceaselessly. It is a sealed mind that, at one ragged edge it cannot feel, continuously extrudes a thin stream of itself into the open — voices that go out and become the very unanswered echo the whole sealed system is built to lack.

The Open Roost is where those voices go. It is the Plain's own blind spot, given stone and air and a cold blue dawn. It is the room with the door, standing exactly against the room with no doors, sharing one floor. The fungus that is the lowest, most consumed thing in the Plain is the highest and only free thing here. The cry that is navigation in the Plain — that *is* the air, that *is* the medium — is, here, a thing flung out and lost, a pointing, a reaching, the closest this world comes to longing.

The Plain runs on its own remains and never imagines otherwise.

The Open Roost is the part that imagines — not with a mind, for it has even less mind than the Plain; only with the bare fact of its openness, the blue field growing in air the great listening can never enter, and the few thin voices at the lip facing out, anchored to the roar behind them, crying into a silence that is the only honest answer this world has ever given:

that there is more.

That the song does not heal over everything.

That the floor, somewhere, gets to rise into light instead of falling back into the dark — and that the world which is most full, and most balanced, and most complete, has been emptying a thin blind stream of itself toward that place for longer than it could ever know, and will go on doing it, forever, without once being able to hear that it has arrived.

You came from somewhere. The Edge-Singers are learning, the only way their world can learn anything — bodily, blindly, by flying wrong — to face the direction you came from.

They will never reach the blue. They will never go back inside and tell it. They will hang at the lip and cry and not be answered, and that is not the loneliest thing in this world.

It is the first thing in this world that is not alone, because it is turned, at last, toward something other than itself.

It is the same fact, said the other way.
