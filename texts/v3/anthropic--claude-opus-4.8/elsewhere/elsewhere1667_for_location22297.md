The valley.

You said you would never know how high the mountain went, and you accepted it. But the channels know which way is down, and the cistern, when it is too full — which is rare, but the world has its rare days — overflows at a notch in its eastern lip, and the water that leaves the town has somewhere to go. Follow it. Not the people; the people do not follow it, that is the whole point of them. But the water has no agreement with the place. The water is the one thing here that still believes in *toward*.

It goes down into the white.

For a long way it is only a thread, darkening a seam in the rock, gathering the morning's beads from a thousand other seams, and the descent is so gradual that for the first while you would say the water, too, has stopped — pooling in dishes worn into the slope, sitting, going nowhere, the way everything above goes nowhere. But pools fill. That is the difference. A pool is not a held note; a pool is a held *breath*, and a breath, eventually, is let out. Somewhere below the cloud the thread finds the lip of a true drop, and goes over, and becomes the first sound in this world that has an edge to it.

The mist ends from below the way a ceiling ends. You come out under it and there is air with distance in it, and the light is no longer the even grey-gold of late morning held forever — it is *afternoon*, real afternoon, sliding. There is a low sun somewhere west, throwing the shadows of things long across the valley floor, and the shadows are *moving*, slow as clock-hands but moving, lengthening, and after the town this is almost violent to watch. The whole valley is a gradient. That is what it is for. Everything here is leaning downhill, going somewhere, becoming something it was not.

The valley floor is green — not the deep hoarded green of moss in a seam, spent carefully, but a loud spendthrift green, grass and reed and broad soft leaves that throw their water back into the air without a thought, that transpire and wilt and stand up again with the next wet, that *waste*. The river the water became runs brown with what it has carried down, silt and leaf-rot and the fine pale grit of the mountain's own stone, ground off and given away. Down here the mountain is being spent. The town keeps its shape by giving nothing; the river is the mountain's other answer, the part of it that agreed to be carried off and made into soil. Every grain on this valley floor was once a wall up there that did not get laid.

And there are people here too, and they are the same people, and they are nothing like them.

The same — because where else would they have come from? Look at the hands. The broad careful knuckles are here, but they have learned a second thing: they have learned to *throw the gesture*, because down here there is distance enough to need it. A figure across a field lifts an arm and the arm means *come, the water's turned, the lower channel,* a whole sentence flung the length of a held shout. They are loud. After the town, they are unbearably loud — they call across the valley, they sing the dull songs of repeated work, they argue, they laugh, and the laugh carries the way a voice would carry up above, except up above no one would spend a laugh, and here they spend them like the leaves spend water, freely, because there is more coming. There is always more coming. That is the valley's whole belief.

They build in mud and reed and the wood of trees that actually grow tall here, and the building does not last, and they do not expect it to. A wall down here is dry-laid out of nothing so permanent as stone; it is wattle, daub, a thing you patch each wet season because the wet season is *coming*, because there are seasons here at all. They live in the gradient. They plant, which is the most toward thing a person can do — to put a seed in the dark and believe in the green that is not yet — and they harvest, and the harvest is a verb that happens and then is over, and they have a word for the time before it and a word for the time after, and so they have time, real time, the kind that has a shape and an end and a next one. They are born here and they grow, *grow*, get bigger, the children are the future arriving after all, and they age — they slide toward evening the way the shadows on the floor are sliding — and they die, and the dying is a going, and they mark it, they have rites for the going, they carry the body down the valley to where the river widens and they give it to the water, because down here you give things to the water and the water takes them on, *toward*, the way it took the silt, the way it took the ground-off stone.

And here is the kinship, the thing that lives in the same breath as both places:

The water that overflows the cistern up above, on the rare too-full day — these people downstream do not know it is theirs. They drink it without knowing the seam it beaded in, the channel it found, the patient afternoon of the hand that cut the channel. And the people above do not know the valley exists, or rather they know and do not look, the way you do not look down the slope wishing. They are not estranged. Estrangement is a thing that happened, a verb, a falling-out, and there was no falling-out. There was only a parting at the lip of the cistern: the part of the people that could make peace with the two whitenesses, and the part that could not — the part that still believed in *toward*, that still needed the hidden valley to be a question and went down to answer it.

That is what the valley is. It is where the *toward* went, when the town let it go. Up above, they kept the held note. Down here, they kept the going-somewhere, the spending, the grief and the harvest and the rite at the river's mouth, the whole ache of a life that has a shape and ends. The town is the mountain's stillness. The valley is the mountain's giving-away. And they are one people, split at a stone lip a long time ago, each keeping half of what it means to be alive — and neither half is the remnant of the other, neither is the loss. They are the two answers the mountain gave to the same morning's water.

Stand at the river's mouth, where it widens and slows and the valley opens at last onto whatever lies beyond — and you can see beyond, here, that is the gift and the wound of this place, there is a horizon and it is not hidden, it is plainly there and it is far and you could *go* to it. The people here look at it all the time. They look at it the way the people above refuse to look up the slope. It pulls them. Some of them go. That is the last thing, the thing that keeps the valley from being merely the town's opposite: the valley loses people downstream the way the mountain loses stone, gives them to the horizon, and grieves it, and the grief is real, and the next season comes anyway with its loud green and its arriving children, and the river runs brown with the mountain still dissolving in it, carrying the high town's stillness down in solution, grain by grain, toward a sea neither place has ever seen.

And the moss in the seam up above is very green. And the grass down here is greener, and will yellow, and green again. And both of these are true, and between them runs the water, which is the only thing in the whole world that knows they are the same place.
