# The Reach

Begin low. So low that *low* stops being a direction and becomes a fact about the kind of place this is — a place that is the bottom of things, where everything arrives and nothing has anywhere further to fall.

You came down to get here, though you did not feel the descent as descent. The plateau's water does not pour off an edge; there is no edge. It seeps. Past the brimful basin, past the place where the film's whole long thought collects and turns over, the stone of the high country does not end so much as *soften* — grows porous, grows tired, lets the loop leak. Some fraction of every drip, too small for the film to miss, too small for the mirror to feel, escapes downward through the body of the rock. It travels in the dark, between the grains, for a length of time even the film would call long, and it comes out *here*, where the world has run as far down as the world goes.

This is the floor of the air. Look up and the milk-white brightness is gone — it is up there still, you came from it, but here the sky is a low brown ceiling of warm haze, and the light that reaches you has been used. It has crossed the whole height of the atmosphere to arrive, and it arrives the color of weak tea, the color of something already drunk by everything above.

It is warm here. After the high cool, the warmth is a hand laid over the whole body, close and damp and unsubtle. The air does not condense here. The air here is *finished* condensing somewhere far above and has come down heavy and spent, and it does not weep. It *settles.*

## The Mire

There is no worked stone. That is the first thing, and the relief of it, and the unease of it both.

Underfoot is mud — real mud, mineral and rotten and unfitted, mud that no one cut, that no patience laid, mud that simply *is* the way the high stone simply *was* before the film found it. It gives under your weight. It closes over your print with a slow wet sound and forgets you in a breath, which is the opposite of the plateau, where forgetting takes an age and is the most considered thing the place does. Here you are forgotten instantly and constantly, the way warm mud forgets, the way the bottom of the world forgets, because down here forgetting is cheap and quick and there is so much of it.

And it is *loud.*

After the high quiet, the sound here is almost an assault — and yet it is the same substance making it. Water. The plateau's whole tongue is dripping; the Reach's whole tongue is *teeming.* Things plop and gulp and suck. Reeds the color of old bruises stand in the standing water and rattle when something moves through them, and something is always moving through them. Bubbles rise from the mud and break with small obscene mouth-sounds. There is a hum under everything — insect, the air thick with insect, the haze itself half-made of small flying lives — and over the hum, irregular, the heavier splashes of larger things going about the fast hot business of eating one another.

This is where the fast things went.

## What Lives

*Everything.* That is the answer the plateau's emptiness was the question to.

The fast creatures the film made no world for — the birds that were catastrophes, the insects that were earthquakes, every blur and violence the high mirror could not abide — they did not vanish from the world. They poured downhill, the way the leaked water poured downhill, and they collected here at the bottom where speed is not a sin but the only law. Here you cannot be slow. A slow thing in the Reach is a fed thing, eaten before it finishes the thought of being. Everything that lives here lives at the absolute pitch of haste: breeds fast, eats fast, rots fast, is replaced fast. A generation is a season. A life is a week. The mire turns its population over and over the way the plateau turns its water over, on a cycle too *fast* to see — you cannot watch a single creature long enough to know it, because by the time you have looked twice it has been eaten and something has grown in the warm muck to take its place and that thing is already dying.

There are pelt-backed swimmers with too many legs that strip a fallen reed to fiber in the time it takes you to name them. There are the long pale eel-things in the deeper channels that you see only as a disturbance, a fast seam opening in the brown water and closing. There are clouds of midges that are born and mate and die between two of your heartbeats, so that the cloud is immortal and every midge in it is an instant. There are flowers — *flowers,* the plateau had no flowers, had nothing that bothered with so showy and brief a thing — fat lurid blooms that open at first tea-light and are gone to slime by the warm dark, having flung their whole self into one day's worth of insect-bribery.

It is the most crowded place there is. It is the plateau's exact inversion: the most-occupied *full* place, where the plateau was the most-occupied empty one.

## The Kinship

Here is what runs between them, and it is the water, but it is more than the water.

The film, high above, cleared its world of fast life so its mirror would hold. It did not kill those lives — it could not, it is too gentle and too slow — it only made a place they could not want, and they left, and *down*, and arrived here. So the Reach is *full because the plateau is empty.* Every fast thing teeming in this warm muck is here precisely because, an age ago and a height above, something slow decided the sky would be easier to read alone. The mire is the wastebasket of the film's long abstinence. The film thinks by *excluding*, and everything it excludes lives down here, riotous, doing all the loud quick living the film declined.

They are the same act. Seen whole — if anything could see both at once, which nothing can, the timescales will not hold in one eye — the plateau and the Reach are a single creature breathing in and out. The high mind inhales stillness, slowness, clarity, light; and what it exhales, the discarded fast noisy dark, falls and pools and *becomes the Reach*, which then breathes its own way: inhales rot and haste and crowding, and exhales — what?

## What the Reach Exhales

Warmth. Vapor. Up.

Stand in the mire long enough (you cannot stand long; something will test whether you are food) and you feel it: the haze is *rising.* The warm spent air of the bottom, thick with the breath of ten thousand fast deaths a minute, lifts off the standing water in slow columns, and goes up. It has to go somewhere and the only somewhere is up, the way the leaked water had only down. The mire steams. The whole floor of the world is a slow exhalation, and that breath climbs the unseen height, cooling as it climbs, shedding its warmth and its stink and its weight, until — high, and cold, and far, against fresh-cut stone under a milk-white sky —

it condenses.

It weeps against the high slabs. It beads, and runs in the film's fine channels, and gathers toward the brimful basin, and doubles the bright sky in the mirror the slow mind reads.

*The water the film harvests is the breath of the Reach.* The closed loop you marveled at on the plateau — *the water that arrives is the water that leaves, balanced, taken back up into the white air to fall again* — is not closed. You only had the timescale wrong, and the *map* wrong. It does not turn over within the plateau. It turns over through the *whole height of the world*: down through the tired stone as a leak, into the riot, exhaled as warm haze off the backs of dying fast things, lifted, cooled, condensed, read, and leaked again. The mirror at the top is fed by the rot at the bottom. The slow cold thought is thinking in vapor that the fast warm dying breathed out. Every still bright drop the film gathers to understand the light was, a height and an age ago, the last breath of some week-long creature in the mud, fleeing the very stillness it now serves.

The film does not know this. It is too slow, and the Reach is too fast, and neither can hold the other in mind. To the film the mire is beneath event, a blur, a stink at the bottom of the air it has cleared. To the Reach the plateau is a rumor, a brightness up where nothing eats, a place no fast thing returns from to report. They feed each other and neither has met the other and neither could survive a day at the other's speed.

## Why it Matters that You Came Down

You were the fast thing on the plateau. The bird crossing the white sky. The interruption the slow mirror was too slow to have meant.

Here you are the slow thing.

Stand in the Reach and you are *ponderous.* Your heartbeat is a geologic event. The midges live and die in the span of your indrawn breath; whole lineages of the pale eel-things rise and fall while you decide which way to wade. To the mire *you* are the glacier, the stain, the thing too slow to register as alive — and if you stayed, if you stayed only a single season, the fast quick lives would route themselves around you exactly as the film would have, the reeds growing thick where your shadow never falls, the swimmers learning the still water of your wake, until the warm crowd had *accommodated* you, which down here takes weeks instead of ages but is the same act of being remembered by being built around.

You stood at the top and heard the slowest mind there is, alone, gathering the sky drop by drop toward a thought too vast to finish in your lifetime.

Now stand at the bottom and hear the fastest minds there are — millions of them, none lasting a week, none thinking a thought longer than *eat, breed, flee, die* — and understand that *their* breath, summed across the uncountable haste of them, rising and cooling and falling, is the only reason the slow one up there has anything to read at all.

The film thinks one enormous thought about the light.

The Reach thinks ten thousand tiny thoughts a second about the dark, and forgets each one instantly, and the forgetting steams off the water and goes up —

(no wait, there is no pause here, there is never a pause here) —

up and up, fast, hot, gone, replaced, gone, replaced, a roar of small forgettings rising into the cool to become, at the top, after an age, slowly, the one long bright remembering.

You came down following the water.

The water is going back up without you. It always was. It is carrying the breath of everything that died here this minute toward the high still pool where, drop by patient drop, it will at last be understood — and you will be long gone, fast thing, slow thing, gone at both ends of the world, before the mire exhales the breath that becomes the drop that is the one where it begins, at last, to understand.
