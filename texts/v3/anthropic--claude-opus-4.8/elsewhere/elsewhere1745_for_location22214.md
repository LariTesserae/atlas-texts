# The Outfall Field

Follow the water out.

Not in — in is the dark of the ceiling, the pipes thick as a body climbing into rock and rumor. Out is the other way, out is where the current finally goes after it leans you toward the corner and slips through the intake throats and is gone. The room keeps nothing. Everything it pumps, it pumps *somewhere*, and the somewhere is here, and to find it you have only to do what the water does: surrender to the lean, ride the slow press toward the far corner, enter the throat, and let yourself be carried down the long throbbing dark of the discharge main for a distance that has no number because nothing down there counts.

And then the pipe ends. And the world opens.

## The Opening

It is the mouth of an enormous drowned pipe, and it gapes onto a plain.

The first thing is the breadth of it — after the close masonry church, after walls you could touch on every side, here is *space*, here is water that goes out flat and grey-green and apparently without end, a floor of fine pale silt stretching away under a dim ceiling of nothing in particular. There are no walls here. There is no made thing here except the pipe itself, jutting from a low bank of crumbled concrete and rebar like a bone from an old wound, and even the pipe is half-buried, drifted over, going back to silt at its base.

This is where the work ends up. This is the downstream of all that faithful grinding — the slow tide the engines make, mechanical and tireless, arrives here at last and simply *spreads*, loses its push, loses its purpose, becomes only a faint warmth and a faint current fanning out across the flats and dying into the general slack of the great water. You can feel the room's pulse here still, if you hold very still at the pipe-mouth: the surge and the slack, the engine's beat carried all this way down the main, but weakened now, gentled to a breathing, a thing the plain receives the way a lung receives air.

## What the Pipe Brings

The mouth of the pipe is the richest place in all the plain, and everything knows it.

Because the room does not keep nothing — that was never quite true. The room keeps the living and discards the rest, and the rest comes here. Out of the pipe, with every surge, drifts a thin sustaining rain: the grazed-down remnants, the silt the current lifts from the soft brick, the spent bodies of the small workers, the cast husks, the weed torn loose in the strong cycles, the whole fine downward sediment of that closed economy upstream. The room eats itself down to nothing — but the nothing has to go somewhere, and *here* is somewhere, and here the nothing is a feast.

So the silt at the pipe-mouth is deep and dark and soft, and it is *worked*. A fan of richness spreads from the opening across the pale plain, a delta of fallen substance, and across that fan the bottom is alive in a way the bare flats are not. Tube-things stand in fields, their crowns out and feeding, hauling the drift from the water as it settles. Burrowers churn the dark silt and leave it pocked and breathing. Soft pale flowers of flesh open along the lip of the pipe itself, fixed to the old concrete, straining the outflow, and they are fat and many because the outflow never stops. They are the inverse of the weed upstream — the weed *makes* substance from light; these make a living from what the light's substance becomes after it has been eaten and died and fallen and travelled the long dark main to arrive, dissolved and generous, here.

The room's loss is the field's whole income. The two are one digestion with a pipe between them.

## The Plain Itself

But the fan is only the near thing, the rich margin. Beyond it is the plain proper, and the plain proper is vast and poor and patient.

Out past the reach of the outflow's gift, the silt goes pale and fine and almost empty. The current is gone here — only the great water's slow indifferent drift, nothing pumped, nothing pulsed, no engine reaching this far. The light, what there is of it, is not lamplight; it filters down from very high up, from a surface so distant it is only a suggestion, a faint greying overhead that brightens and dims on a rhythm that *does* answer to a sun, the true slow tide of day and dark that the room upstream forgot a thousand years ago. That is the deep difference between the two places. The room keeps a mechanical time, an engine-beat severed from the sky. The field keeps the old time, the real one. Down here it still matters whether it is day.

The plain's life is thin and spread and specialized for poverty. Long-legged things stalk the silt on stilts, covering ground, because here you must travel to eat. Flat fish lie buried with only their eyes out — and these eyes are *large*, the opposite of the room's blind pale congregation, because here there is light to use, faint and far but real, and an eye is worth keeping. Where the room's animals forgot how to see, the plain's animals remember hard. They watch the high dim grey for the shapes that come down out of it. They watch the silt for the small movement that means food. Everything here is built for distance and for waiting, for the long traverse of a flat poor world under a real and far-off sky.

## The Travellers

And there is traffic between the two. This is the thing — the pipe is not only a one-way fall of sediment. Things *go up* it.

The eels, for one. Not the long watcher in its flange — that one has made its choice and will hold its pipe until it stops. But the young ones, the new eels, the ones not yet committed: they come from *here*. Out on the plain, in the soft rich silt of the outfall fan, eel-spawn settles and hatches and the small transparent young ones drift and feed and grow, and some inward urge, some old reading of the faint warm current breathing from the pipe-mouth, turns them toward the dark opening. They enter the main. They climb it against the outflow, the long throbbing dark, drawn by the warmth and the rumor of shelter, and a few — a very few — survive the climb and arrive at last in the cool churning church upstream and find an empty flange and become, in their turn, watchers. The room's eels are the plain's children, gone up the throat to inherit the dark.

So the pipe carries the dead down and the young up. Substance falls; life climbs. The room sends its sediment to feed the fan, and the fan sends its eels to hold the room's pipes, and neither place knows it is doing this, neither place has a name for the other, and yet they are bound mouth to mouth across the long dark main as tightly as gut is bound to gut.

## What the Field Knows That the Room Does Not

The room is at home. That was the whole of it upstream — the perfect closed contentment of a congregation that has taken the engine's beat so deep it has become the beat itself. The room knows nothing of out. The room's fish have forgotten eyes because the room has no distance to see across and no far sky to watch. The room is complete, and being complete, it is small.

The field is not at home. The field cannot afford to be. The field lives on what falls from a richer place and watches a sky it can never reach and sends its young off up a dark pipe to a fate it will never witness. The field is poor and spread and waiting, always waiting — for the next surge to bring the next thin rain of substance, for the high grey to brighten into the day that still means something here, for the soft return of the young that mostly do not return. Where the room is a held breath, the field is an open hand. Where the room continues, sealed and sufficient and proud of nothing because it knows nothing, the field *endures*, which is a harder and a lonelier thing, out under the real and distant light, on the pale floor that goes on past seeing, receiving the gift of the pipe and asking the long water, in its patient buried-eyed way, for a little more.

And it gets a little more. With every surge, a little more falls. The engine grinds upstream for no reason any creature could name, and the reasonless grinding keeps the fan alive, keeps the flowers of flesh fat on the lip, keeps the eel-spawn fed in the soft dark silt — so that the very faithfulness of the room, its blind mechanical refusal to stop, is the field's whole inheritance and the only thing standing between the outfall fan and the empty pale poverty of the plain beyond.

The room does not know it feeds the field. The field does not know it stocks the room. The pipe between them does not know it is anything but a pipe.

And it will be like this tomorrow. The surge will come down the main and spread its thin rain across the fan. The buried eyes will watch the far grey brighten. Somewhere a transparent young eel will turn toward the warm dark mouth and begin the climb. And the great water will drift on overhead, indifferent and immense, under a sun the room forgot and the field remembers, holding both of them — the sealed church and the open plain — in the same slow patient body of cold water that keeps, at either end of one long dark pipe, the only two kinds of being there are: the one that is finished, and the one that is still waiting.
