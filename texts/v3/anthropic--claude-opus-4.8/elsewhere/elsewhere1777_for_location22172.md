**The dry ground, where the moisture gives out.**

You named it without looking at it. You felt it at the edges — the warm dust where the seep gives up, where nothing builds because nothing holds — and then you turned back to the green, the way anyone turns back to the lit room and leaves the dark beyond the door unexamined. But the dry ground is most of the world. The pocket is the exception. If you stood inside the city and called it everything, that is only because the city is loud in the way small bright things are loud, and the dryness says nothing, and you mistook its silence for emptiness.

Walk out of the fern's shade. It is not a wall. There is no line. The moss thins, the cushions grow ragged at their margins, the grains between them go from dark to pale as the water lets go of them one by one, and then there is a stretch where the ground is neither — damp enough to clump, dry enough to crumble, the disputed border that belongs to no one — and then you are out, and the ground is the color of old bone, and the warmth no longer rises. Out here the warmth *falls*. It comes from above, from the unfiltered glare the fern was holding off, and it lands on the dust and the dust holds it and gives it back all night, so that the dry ground is never cold and never cool, only hot and less hot, a place that has forgotten the difference between day and the memory of day.

Nothing assembles here.

That was the rule of the pocket — *things only assemble, whatever is set down stays and is added to* — and you took it for a rule of the world. It is not. It is a rule of *water*. Water is the cement; water is the thing that lets a grain agree with the grain beside it. Out here, where the water gave out, the opposite law holds, and it is the older law, the one the pocket is the exception to: out here, things only come apart. Whatever is set down is taken up by the wind and set down somewhere else and taken up again. Nothing holds. Nothing accretes. There are no towers because a tower is an agreement and there is nothing here to make grains agree. The dry ground is the city's logic run backward, the negative of the negative space, a place made entirely of the *un*-building that the pocket spends all its slow hours holding at bay.

And yet it is not dead. This is what you would have to walk out to learn.

**The ones who live in the coming-apart.**

There is a creature here that is pale almost to white, flattened, wide as a thumbnail and thin as a held breath, and it does not move when the sun is up. It is buried. It spends the glare-hours under a finger's depth of dust, in the one cool seam the falling heat hasn't reached, folded and still, *waiting* — and waiting is its whole art, the way building was the citizens' whole art. It can wait for a season. It can wait for a year. It is built around the absence of water the way the swollen mother was built around the presence of it, and where she pulses *we are still one thing*, this one pulses nothing, says nothing, is one thing alone and needs no others, and that solitude is not loneliness because loneliness is a thing that happens to creatures who expect company. It expects nothing. It is the most patient animal in the world and it is patient about *lack*.

When the rain comes — and it comes here too, the same rain that swells the river-thread in the pocket, the same clouds, the dry ground and the wet pocket share a sky if they share nothing else — when the rain comes, the dry ground does the only fast thing it ever does. The flat pale waiters rise out of the dust in a single hour, all of them at once, more of them than the dryness ever looked like it could hold, and they *feed* — on what, on the green that the wet briefly raises, on each other, on the soft sudden bloom of small lives that the dry ground keeps as seeds and eggs and folded waiting bodies for exactly this — and they mate, and they lay, and they fold back down into the dust before it dries, and the whole life of the dry ground is lived in the few hours of the year when it is briefly, falsely, a wet place. They are not building toward a middle either. But where the citizens decided that beginning was enough, these have decided that the *interruption* is enough — that a life can be made of the rare hours when the rule reverses, and the rest is only the waiting that earns them.

**And here is the thread between the two places, the one you can follow back.**

The river. You called it a river-that-is-barely-a-trickle, a channel no wider than a thread, and you said it ran *through* the pocket, feeding the green. But a thing that runs through a place runs *out* of it too. The seep does not keep all its water. The trickle leaves the fern's shade at the low end of the pocket, where you did not look, and it goes out into the dry ground, and it does not get far — a hand's-length, less, before the dust drinks it and the warmth lifts it and it is gone, a wet thread that frays to nothing in the bone-colored ground.

But where it frays, for that hand's-length, there is a margin. A thin green seam following the dying water out into the dust — not the lush held pocket, just a line of the toughest small plants, the ones that can stand in the disputed border, drinking the last of the trickle before it gives up. And the flat pale waiters know this seam. In the dry months, when the rain is only a memory and a hope, the seam is the one place the dryness is cheating, and they come to its edge by night and take what the dying river still carries, and they are *fed by the pocket without ever entering it* — fed by the overflow, the runoff, the water the green city could not hold and let go of at its low door.

The citizens do not know they feed the dry ground. The swollen mother, pulsing in the dark, has no word for *downstream*. The builders raising their spires in the fern-light have no idea that the water they did not use, the surplus their seep produces and their green spends past, leaks out the low end and keeps a whole other kind of life folded in the dust a hand's-length into the dry, waiting through the long heat for the rain that the two places share.

The pocket builds, and never finishes.
The dry ground unbuilds, and never empties.
And between them runs a thread of water too small to see at arm's length, that the wet place spends without meaning to, and the dry place drinks without being seen, so that the city's careless surplus is the desert's only mercy — and neither one knows the other is there, and the trickle that joins them frays to nothing exactly halfway, in the warm disputed dust that belongs to neither, where a single drop, let go from the green, sinks into the dry and is gone before it lands.

You leaned close to the city and the spider felt you breathe.

Out here there is no one to feel it. Lean as close as you like. The waiter under the dust does not stir for breath, or for shadow, or for the whole weight of your attention pressed against the ground above it. It is waiting for water. Only water. It has been right about the rain every time so far, and it will be right again, and when the sky finally breaks it will rise into its one hour with no idea that a hand's-length away, past the fraying thread, a city the size of a hand was raising mineral towers in the shade the entire time it slept.
