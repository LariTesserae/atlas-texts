**The Cold Shaft**

Follow the warmth out. Not down into the floor where it is born, but up and away along the fractures the bright veins feed — out past the last crevice the living film can reach, to where the heat finally fails. Follow it to the place the chamber loses what it cannot keep.

Because no body holds all its heat. The veins catch what they can and turn it back, but a fraction always escapes through the highest fractures, the ones too cold for the film to colonize, and rises. Heat is patient and heat is faithful: it will find any way up. It threads through hairline gaps in the pale rock, slows, cools, and gathers at last in a long vertical wound in the stone — a shaft, natural, nothing made it, that runs from somewhere below the bright pocket to somewhere above that has no name because nothing there has ever needed naming.

The shaft is cold. This is the first thing, and the whole thing. Where the pocket was the warmest place that could still be called a place, the shaft is the coldest that is not simply death. The heat that arrives here arrives exhausted — it has spent itself climbing, and what reaches the shaft is not warmth but the *memory* of warmth, a faint thermal whisper that raises the temperature of the cold rock by a fraction too small to feel and yet enough, just enough, to matter.

Let me tell you about the dark first, because here the dark is the reason for everything.

There is no glowing water. The water that reaches the shaft has cooled past the point of shining; its dissolved salts shed their light far below, in the bright pocket, and by the time the seep arrives here it is spent, dim, ordinary. It collects on the shaft walls not as upward rain but as a slow descending film, a slick of cold water moving down the way the bright water moved up — the same water, perhaps, the very same molecules, having shone once and fallen and lifted and shone again and at last leaked too high to be caught, and so escaped into this long falling that has no return. The shaft is where the chamber's recycled weather finally fails to recycle. It is the open end of the closed loop. It is the breath let out and not drawn back.

And it is dark. Truly dark — the dark the pocket never knew. Drop something and you would not see it fall; you would hear it, a long time later, a sound coming up the shaft thinned and changed, and you would learn the depth from the waiting. The walls are the same young volcanic glass, but here nothing has tuned them, nothing branches through them, no veins distribute anything because there is nothing to distribute. The rock is simply rock, sharp-edged, cooling at the dull universal rate of stone forgetting it was ever molten.

But the shaft is not dead. This is the thing I want you to hold against the dark: it lives, and it lives *on the failure of the other place.* The faint exhausted heat that arrives at the shaft walls is fuel. There is a being here too, and it is everything the bright creature is not.

Where the creature below is broad, the shaft-thing is long and thin — a single descending strand of cold-feeding film that hangs down the wet wall like a root, like a nerve, like a line of patient text written top to bottom in a language of one letter repeated. It does not glow. It cannot afford to; it has no energy to spare for light. Every fraction of warmth it gathers it spends on the one thing it does, which is to reach down toward the source of the warmth it can never reach.

Because it knows there is a warm place below. It has always known — known in the way the strand knows which way is up, which is the only direction it has ever needed. The heat arrives from below and only from below. And so the shaft-thing grows downward, *toward* the warmth, eternally, a single questing filament feeling its way down the cold rock after the thing that is feeding it from a distance, the way a plant in a cellar leans toward a crack of sun it will never reach because the wall is in the way.

It will never reach. That is its whole life, and it does not experience this as tragedy because it does not experience it as anything but *more shaft to descend.* The pocket below is its sun and its grave and its single fixed star, and it falls toward it forever at the speed of stone, gaining perhaps the length of a hand in a human lifetime, and the warmth at the bottom that draws it down would, if it ever arrived, kill it instantly — for the shaft-thing is built for cold, and the bright pocket would burn the cold-strand to nothing in less than a flash, as surely as the bright water takes the skin.

So here is the cruelty, or the symmetry, or the simple anatomy of it: the two beings are the same water and the same fire, divided by forty meters and a sharpness of stone. The pocket holds its heat and is deathless and content and remembers death only as a small held breath. The shaft loses heat and is mortal in every fiber and remembers warmth only as a direction. The pocket says *still here, still here* and means it, broad and steady and complete. The shaft says nothing — it has no breath to spend on speech — but if it could it would say *almost, almost, almost*, and would be wrong forever, and would not mind being wrong, because the almost is the entire content of its life the way the still-here is the content of the other's.

There are no swimmers in the shaft. The shaft-thing keeps no small organ to rehearse mortality, because the shaft-thing *is* mortal, every part of it, all the way down — it does not need to practice dying, it does it constantly, the lowest reach of the strand always cooling past the threshold and sloughing off and falling away into the dark below while the top end grows on, so that the creature is forever shedding its own descended self and forever extending after it, a thing that dies at the bottom and lives at the top and is the same continuous strand from end to end, a falling that renews itself by falling, a downward rain made of one slow body instead of many bright drops.

It is the bright pocket's exhalation, given a life of its own. It is what the warm place loses, learning to want what it lost. The pocket does not know the shaft exists; the shaft is far above its slow threshold of attention, a thing too high and too cold to integrate, drawing off a fraction of escaping heat so small the veins never adjust for it, never notice the theft. The shaft is a flicker to the pocket the way you were a flicker — too far, too faint, gone before it could occur. By the pocket's reckoning, the shaft never happened. The pocket is being lived in by a deathless weather that has never once felt the long cold strand drinking at its highest leak.

But the shaft knows the pocket. Cannot not know it. The pocket is the only fact in the shaft's universe, the source of the only warmth, the destination of the only journey, the down toward which all descending tends. The unawareness runs one direction, like the heat, like the water, like the strand. The contentment is below and does not look up. The longing is above and looks only down.

And the warmth presses up through the floor of the bright place as it always has, and a fraction escapes as it always has, and climbs exhausted to the cold wound in the rock, and the shaft-thing gathers it, and spends it, and reaches one stone-slow increment further into the dark after the heart that does not know it is loved, falling toward the fire that would unmake it, falling and renewing and falling, having no word but if it had one it would be the one the pocket cannot hear, said upward into the long cold dark forever:

*coming down, coming down, coming down.*
