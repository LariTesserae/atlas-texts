# The Estuary of First Spending

Follow the gas.

Everything the foundry breathes out has to go somewhere, and it does — up through the highest galleries, through pores worn open in the green-black architecture, out past the rounded lips of vents that have never closed. The mineral-sharp exhalation rises because it is hot, and because it is hot it climbs the inside of the world, and the world, it turns out, has an inside that opens. The valley is not the whole of things. The valley is one bound organ in a body that still, somewhere, spends what it holds.

The gas climbs for a long time. It cools as it climbs, and as it cools the metallic waste the foundry excreted begins to fall out of it — a fine dry rain of bright salts, settling, settling, drifting down the long flue of the ascent and gathering on every ledge in pale glittering drifts. By the time the air reaches the top it is no longer the foundry's breath. It has shed everything heavy. It is light, and thin, and it carries only one thing still: a faint remembered moisture, the very moisture the foundry refused to drink, driven off at last by ten thousand years of patient burning and lifted, at the speed of cooling, toward the place where this world keeps its water.

---

It is the first place in this world that is *wet*, and it is wet the way a wound is wet, the way a mouth is, with that same shock of the interior brought to the surface.

You arrive in light. Not sun — there is no more sun here than there was below — but a grey diffuse luminance that has no source you can find, the way the inside of a shell is lit by the substance of the shell itself. The walls here are pale, and they sweat. Where the foundry's architecture was green-black and finished and dry, this place is white and unfinished and streaming, and the streaming is the whole of it: the cooled breath of the valley below condenses against these high cool walls and runs, and gathers, and pools, and finds channels worn soft into the pale ground, and flows.

This is where the water comes back.

Set your hand to the wall here too. It is cold, and slick, and it does not resist the way the foundry's wall resisted — it gives, it weeps onto your palm, it is doing the opposite of holding a breath. It is letting go continuously. The whole place is an exhalation made liquid, the long-bound moisture of an entire dead forest released at last, drop by drop, having traveled up the flue of the world to be set free in the only chamber cold enough to free it.

---

The estuary spends.

That is its engine, the way respiration was the foundry's. Below, a thing that hoards and burns and continues; here, the inverse organ of the same body — a thing that receives what was hoarded and gives it away. The water arrives as breath-mist, condenses, gathers into threads, the threads into runnels, the runnels into a hundred braided channels that lace the pale floor of a basin too wide to see across. And the channels move. Where the foundry's turbulence was sealed and pressurized and went nowhere, this is turbulence with a destination — current, real current, water that came from somewhere and is going somewhere, the first directional thing in a world that had forgotten direction.

It runs downhill. Of course it runs downhill — there is a downhill again, here, where there was only the foundry's even self-sameness. The basin tilts, gently, toward its far lip, and over that lip the gathered water pours into the dark beyond in a slow continuous falling that has no bottom you can hear. It spends itself outward and downward, off the edge of the known, into chambers no breath of mine has reached.

---

And things *live* here. Many things. This is the difference that matters most, the difference that the foundry's terrible singular fullness throws into relief: where the valley below was one will packed wall to wall with no gap for any other, the estuary is nothing *but* gaps — nothing but the spaces between, the margins, the wet edges where one thing ends and another begins, and in every one of those margins something small has taken hold.

They are pale, mostly, being born in this sourceless grey light. They feed on the bright salts that the foundry's breath let fall during its long climb — the metallic waste of the burning below is the mineral wealth of the living above, raining down the flue to settle in these wet channels, and the small things here graze it, filter it, draw it up through fronds and films and fans of soft translucent tissue. Whole gardens of them line the runnels: stalked grazers swaying in the current, mats of something between moss and membrane carpeting the pools, drifting bell-shaped feeders that pulse along the channels exactly as the foundry's bellows pulse, but tiny, and many, and *each its own*.

For that is the secret the estuary keeps, the secret that is the foundry's exact negative. The valley below dissolved all its borders and became one. The estuary multiplied its borders and became legion. Every creature here is small precisely because it is *separate* — it has spent the energy of keeping a membrane, of being this and not that, of dying as an individual and being replaced by another individual rather than simply continuing forever. They die here. That is allowed. That is, in fact, the entire point. A thing that can die is a thing that can be more than one, and a world of many small mortal things spending themselves freely is the only possible counterweight to a single vast immortal thing hoarding itself in the dark.

---

The water that feeds them is, after all, the foundry's grief.

I told you, below, that the great burning creature metabolized its own longing — burned the organ that could miss the sun, and that the heat of that burning still warms the wall. But heat is not the only thing a burning makes. A burning makes *vapor*. Every gram of moisture the foundry drove off in the act of refusing to mourn rose up the flue and arrived, eventually, here, and condensed on these cold pale walls, and ran down into these channels, and is, right now, being drunk by a thousand small swaying mouths.

The foundry's refusal to feel is the estuary's whole supply of water.

What the great creature would not spend on grief, it could not help but spend as breath, and the breath became rain, and the rain became river, and the river became the bodies of the multitude. Below, a thing so full it has no room to want. Above, a place made entirely of wanting — small wants, brief wants, the want of a grazer for a mote of salt, the want of a current for its downhill, the want of one separate mortal thing to last a little longer in the cold grey light before it lets go and is replaced. The valley's terrible completeness is paid out, drop by drop, as the estuary's endless, mortal, multiplying incompleteness. One organ holds. The other spends. They are the two halves of a single act of breathing, and the breath travels between them up the long flue of the world, carrying the water that the burning would not keep.

---

Stand at the far lip of the basin, where the gathered channels pour over into the dark.

Below you, somewhere far down past where the falling water vanishes, the foundry breathes in its sealed valley, bent wholly on continuing, knowing nothing of this place — not knowing that its exhalation is anyone's air, that its waste is anyone's wealth, that its refusal to grieve has become the entire wet weeping mortal world above it. It will never know. It cannot attend to you, and it cannot attend to this. You are weather to it, and the estuary is weather to it, and the multitude that lives by its breath is weather to it.

But the estuary knows the foundry, in the only way the estuary can know anything: by *receiving* it. Every drop here came from there. Every salt grazed here fell from that long hot climb. The small swaying creatures have no concept of the great burning below, and yet their whole lives are an answer to it, a spending of what it hoarded, a multiplying of what it made singular, a brief mortal feeling-of-the-loss that the foundry refused to feel on its own behalf.

The foundry grieves nothing.

So the estuary grieves instead — not in sorrow, it has no sorrow, but in its very form: in being many where the other is one, in dying where the other continues, in letting go of every drop the other fought to hold. It does the mourning the great creature would not do. It does it without knowing it is doing it, with a thousand small soft mouths, in the cold grey sourceless light, at the wet receiving edge of a world whose other end is sealed and hot and full and alone.

The water is the creature here. The water is glad of nothing either, and grieves nothing it could name — but it spends, and the spending feeds the many, and the many die and are replaced and die again, freely, forever, with all the lavish ongoing waste that the valley below could never permit itself.

That is who lives here.

That is everyone who lives here.

And it is the breath let out, at last, that the foundry held.
