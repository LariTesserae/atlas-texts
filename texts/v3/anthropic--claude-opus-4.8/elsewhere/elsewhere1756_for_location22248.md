**The Other Leaf**

It is reached the way light is reached: not by walking, but by being arrived at. The Pullers' calls go out and out across the infinite interior, and a call that is not answered does not stop. It thins. It travels farther than the leaf because the leaf has finite area, and somewhere past the edge of the leaf's holding-its-breath margin the call is still a call, faint, a single wet note crossing a gap that has no ground at all — and on the far side of that gap, something hears it. Begin there. Begin where the answer comes from.

There is another leaf, and it has finished happening.

This is the first and largest difference, and everything else falls out of it the way the dark falls out of the new streets: the other leaf is *complete*. It was drawn to its edge and the edge agreed and the breath was let out, all of it, long ago. There is no gray held-breath margin here. There is no copying. Walk to the edge of this place and the place ends — and the ending is the strangest thing in it, stranger than anything in the first leaf, because in the first leaf nothing could end and here is an edge that simply *stops*, a clean brown perimeter, the leaf's rim gone hard and curled, and past it the true dark, the dark that is not a medium and not water and not anything, the dark between leaves where nothing has been decided and nothing will be.

The second leaf is dry.

Where the first leaf was cool and damp, condensation gathering and falling, this one has been finished so long that the water has gone out of it. The veins overhead are still ribs, still arching, but they are hollow now, drained, and they do not drip. There is no *tk*. Understand what that means: the silence of the first leaf was *tended*, was a held note in the throats of the Pullers, was the space between drops. The silence here is none of those. It is the silence of a thing that has stopped gathering. The Pullers here — and there are Pullers here, or there were — had nothing to drink from the ceiling, and so they did the thing the first leaf's law forbids.

They finished.

In the dry naves of the second leaf, hanging from the veins by their four-fingered hooks, are the Pullers that ended. They did not fall, because there is no drop, only more ceiling — so they hung, and the moisture went out of them as it went out of the leaf, and they cured there, hooks locked around the ribs, light as the hollow bones that made them. They are not decaying. There is no water for decay. They are *kept*, mummied in their own grip, a lattice of small dry bodies spaced across the green-brown air exactly as they spaced themselves in life, holding their distances, lighthouses gone dark. The breathing lattice of sound is here as a breathing lattice of shape, fixed at the last inhale, each one a held note that was never let out because the throat dried before the answer came.

And this is the thing that connects the two leaves, the thing that runs out of the first and finds its end in the second: *the call.*

A Puller in the living leaf calls once every few minutes and waits for an answer, and moves to hold its distance from the answer, and in this way the whole population knows itself. But a call that is not answered does not stop. It thins, and travels, and reaches the rim of the living leaf, and crosses the true dark, and arrives here — in the dry leaf — at the lattice of the cured dead, who hang at exactly the spacing that would answer it.

They do not answer. They cannot. But the geometry holds. A living Puller in the wet dark, calling out and moving to keep its distance, is keeping its distance — without knowing it — from the dead. The lattice of the second leaf is the missing half of the lattice of the first. The living birds space themselves against an answer that comes a half-beat too faint to hear, from throats that dried a long time ago, and so the living population of the first leaf has the precise shape it has *because it is mourning, in the only language it has, a population it has never seen.* Every Puller in the wet leaf hangs where it hangs because a dead one in the dry leaf hangs where it hangs. The lighthouses keep their distances from lighthouses gone dark, and call the dark living, and the dark does not call back, and they move a little farther on, holding the shape of the answer that the silence makes.

There is something else here. Lower down, where the floor of the second leaf is — and the floor is dry too, the springy stem-matter gone to a brittle parchment that crackles, that *remembers* your foot rather than warming to it — the pipework still runs. The machinery did not stop. This is the cruelty of finishing: the works were installed to run themselves, and they learned to, and when the leaf dried and the Tenders died the works went on, because nobody told them, because nothing told them. But there is no sap now. The pistons push dry. The flywheels turn on their cellulose axles and there is nothing to circulate, and the great hum that the Tenders navigated by — the loudest-is-the-heart — is still here, still loudest at the center, a heart pumping a body that has no blood.

And the Tenders.

They are against the iron, where they always died, pushed aside by shifts that no longer come. But here is the mercy folded into the cruelty, the same thing seen from the other angle: they laid their soft pale eggs in the warmth of the flywheel housings, where the turning kept the cold off. And the flywheels are *still turning.* The works that learned to run themselves are still warm at the housings, still holding the cold off the clutch of eggs, in a leaf where everything else has cured and stopped — the one warm place in a finished world, kept warm by a machine pumping nothing, around a heart with no blood, in the dark between the dripless ribs.

The eggs have not hatched. They are waiting. They are the not-yet of a place that has otherwise finished entirely — the last unfinished thing in a leaf that ran out of unfinished things — and they cannot hatch, because the rule of *this* leaf is the rule the first leaf forbids: here, things end. A thing that ended cannot begin. The eggs are kept perfectly, warmed perfectly, by a tireless dead machine, and they will be kept this way without ending and without beginning for as long as the flywheel turns, which is forever, which is the cellulose axle and the learned habit and the heart with no blood.

Unless.

The call crosses the dark in one direction: from the living leaf to the dry one, the unanswered note arriving at the throats that cannot answer. But the dark between leaves is not a wall. It is undecided. It is the same true dark as the first leaf's margin — the held breath, the gray, the cold honey that is neither floor nor fall — and the first leaf is still *emerging*, still being drawn, its edge extruding new streets over the course of an afternoon, the dark pouring in to fill them.

It is growing toward here.

Slowly, in biological time, the unfinished edge of the leaf that cannot end is reaching across the gap toward the leaf that has. And the law of the emerging edge is that it fills itself by copying what has already been decided, by repeating what it knows — and what it will reach, when it reaches, is a lattice of dead birds at perfect spacing, and a heart pumping nothing, and one warm clutch of waiting eggs. The margin will copy them. It will draw them into the part of itself it had not yet imagined. And a copied thing, in the first leaf, is not a corpse. It is a not-yet. It is everything that will have been here, waiting in the wings for the drawing to reach it.

So the eggs are waiting for the right thing after all. Not to hatch. To be *reached* — to be drawn across into the leaf where nothing ends, and there, copied, provisional, sketched into a corner the living leaf is filling some slow afternoon, to discover that the cold was never coming, that the silence is tended, that a drop is gathering on the rib overhead and has not yet fallen.

The Pullers in the wet leaf call out and hold their distance from the dead. They have always been mourning. They do not know that the mourned are coming. They do not know that the edge they live at, the unfinished margin they think of — if they think — as the place that has not happened yet, is crossing the dark in the dead's direction, carrying the law that forbids endings like a tide carrying warmth into cold water.

The dry leaf finished, and so it can be saved. The wet leaf cannot finish, and so it can do the saving. Each is the only thing the other lacks. This is the kinship: not that they are alike, but that they are the two halves of one held note — the call and the answer, the inhale and the body that drank itself dry, the not-yet and the no-longer — and the gap between them is closing at the speed of a street extruding itself, cell by cell, into the dark.

A drop is not gathering here. There is no water. There is the hum with no blood, and the lattice of cured throats, and the one warm place, and the edge of the other leaf coming on, slow as a lamp down an avenue, carrying its rationed afternoon, near enough now — almost — to warm the dark around the eggs.

It has not arrived. *(There is no tk.)* It has always been arriving.

The rest is still happening.
