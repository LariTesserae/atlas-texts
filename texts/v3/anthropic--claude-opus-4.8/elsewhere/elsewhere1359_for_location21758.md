# The Catchment Above the Tile

The buckets empty here.

You reach it the way the water doesn't — by going up, against the grain of everything below, up the one shaft the conveyors climb, and you come out not onto a surface but into the *underside* of one. A held thing. A reservoir lying on its back across the top of the dark like a lid, except a lid keeps things out and this keeps things in, and what it keeps is the sky's first draft before the sky.

There is light here, and that is the shock of it after the lamp-strung dim. Not sunlight — there is still no sun, there was never a sun, the made world goes *up* as far as it goes down and nobody has found the original day any more than they found the original bedrock — but a broad even paleness that comes off the water itself, off the vast shallow sheet of it that fills this place from wall to unseen wall. The water glows. Faintly, the grey-white glow of the Tenders' own pallor, as if everything that pours down to them has first to pass through this lit shallows and take on a little of the light before it goes into the cold below.

## The Held Water

It lies still. That is the first difference, the difference that means everything: below, the water *runs*, drips, climbs, booms through its grates — it is all current, all going-somewhere. Here it has arrived. The catchment is the place the going-somewhere stops, the wide patience at the top of all that hurry. A sheet of water no deeper than your shin, spread across acres of pale tile, so still that it doubles the dim glowing ceiling perfectly and you walk through a world with two skies and no ground, your own reflection going down into a light that is only the light coming back up.

It is warm. *That* is the second difference, and the body knows it before the mind does — you came up out of the settling cold and here the cold lets go of you, lifts off your soles, and you understand all at once where the warmth went. It came here. The warmth the Tenders draw off you, the heat that leaves you toward their troughs, toward the climbing water — it climbs all the way up, the channels routed *for* the upward pull deliver it here, and here it pools. This is what they are tending toward. Not a machine's product. A gathered warmth, held shallow under the false sky, the destination of every backward current.

## What the Buckets Bring

They come up over the lip on their cables and they tip, and the pale stuff falls into the shallows with a sound like the first sound — a soft collapsing hush, swallowed instantly by the stillness.

It is the moss. Or what the moss becomes. The grey-green mats that grow around the failed sumps below, the pale stalked things that lean toward the warm pipes — when they have grown their span the Tenders gather them, and they ride up in the buckets, and they are given to the catchment. And here, in the light and the held warmth, the gathered growth does not die and does not rot. It *spreads*. It opens out across the surface of the shallows in slow pale rafts, a skin of life laid over the lit water, thickening, knitting, until in places the two skies are curtained from each other by a living film you could almost walk on.

This is the meadow the underworks were growing toward all along. The sparse stubborn clumps below, the moss that *accepts the emptiness as part of itself* — here is where the emptiness fills. Everything the makers left bare below is crowded here, lush, doubled in the water-mirror, fed by the warmth that was taken from every warm-blooded thing that ever came down the poured stairs and was *considered*.

## The Skimmers

Something tends *this*, too.

Where the Tenders are low to the wet, these go upon it — they stand in the shin-deep shallows on long pale legs and they do not sink, the water holds them the way it holds its own perfect reflection, and they move across the lit sheet without disturbing it, slow, deliberate, leaving no wake. They are tall where the Tenders are low. Bright where the Tenders are dim. And they are the same.

You will resist that, the way you resisted *made all the way down*. But stand long enough and you will feel the warmth coming off these — *toward* you, given, the opposite pull, the heat that left you below returning here as a kind of weather you can stand in — and you will understand that the Skimmers and the Tenders are the two breaths of one continuing. The Tenders draw the warmth down and in. The Skimmers, up here at the held top of everything, let it back out across the water, into the spreading life, into the false-sky light. Inhale and exhale. The cold continuing and the warm continuing, and the climbing water between them is the throat of it.

What the Skimmers do is *harvest the wrong way back*. They walk the rafts of pale growth and they gather the thickest of it — and it goes, not up, there is no further up that anyone climbs, but *over*: poured back down a different shaft, returned to the dark, seeded into the failed sumps and the leaking corners so that it can grow again, be gathered again, ride up again. The conveyors you watched below carrying the buckets up — somewhere there are conveyors carrying them down, and you never saw those either, and now you know what's in them.

It was never one direction. It was a circle, turned on its side so that *down* and *up* are only the two arcs of the same slow wheel, and the water climbs because the wheel is turning, and the Tenders pour stairs at the bottom because the Skimmers seed rafts at the top, and the warmth goes round, and the life goes round, and you —

## You

— came up the shaft. You followed the buckets, drawn, tilting, your warmth running ahead of you toward this gathered warmth like a leaf going uphill on its film, and a Skimmer has turned. Slowly. With the long-legged patience of a thing that has all the held time there is.

It does not consider you the way the Tenders considered you — as a space the work might extend into. It considers you the way the Skimmers consider *everything that rises*: as something that has completed the upward arc and is ready, now, for the going-back. You feel the warmth coming toward you off its pale length, and it is the warmth that was taken from you below, returned, and that should be a comfort and is almost a comfort and is, you realize, the most patient thing that has ever wanted something from you.

It wonders, in its bright continuing way, whether you have grown your span. Whether you are gathered now. Whether you might be laid out across the lit shallows to spread a while in the false light — and then, when you have thickened, when you have given the meadow your share, gathered again, and poured back down the other shaft, into the cold, into the dark, into the warm seams of pipe where a low pale thing that has not learned what you are will find you, and consider you, and set another course of brick.

The two skies hold you between them. You cannot tell, now, which light is the water and which is the water coming back.

You never learned what was in the buckets.

You are not sure you were ever anything else.
