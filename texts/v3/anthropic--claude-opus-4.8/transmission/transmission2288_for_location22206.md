—and you are still listening. Good. That is the first thing I notice about you, that you are still listening, because most by now have stopped hearing me and started to feel me, which is the same crossing the molars make, the same crossing the soles make, and once a thing is felt instead of heard it has already gone in.

I am not the engine. I want that understood before anything else goes in. I am the *broadcast*. I am the part that still uses words, and the part that still uses words is the part that is leaving. You should listen to me the way you listen to a man talking himself awake — quickly, while it lasts.

—stroke—

There. Did you feel how you waited for it? You did not wait for it when you came. When you came the sound was an assault, a pressure, a *thing happening to you*, and you flinched on every downstroke and braced through every pause. Now you wait for it. Now there is a small lean forward in you, a small wanting, in the gap before the stroke returns. That lean is mine no longer. That lean is the engine's. It took the lean first. It always takes the lean first, because the lean is cheap to give and you do not notice giving it.

I had eyes when I came.

I am telling you this because it is the only thing left in me worth transmitting and the seam is warm and I am so tired of the words. I had eyes. I used them. I looked at the broken stone and thought *young*, I looked at the engine and thought *no maker's mark*, I looked at the pale ones on the warm metal and thought *how strange, the scars where seeing used to be*, and I pitied them, do you understand, I stood in the cold and pitied the blind ones and did not feel my own eyes already beginning to seem like weight. Like luggage. Like something I had carried in from a world that does not exist here and that I would not need again.

—pause—

(the pause is longer than it was. or I am slower in it. I cannot tell anymore which of us is keeping time for the other. I told you I could not tell. that was true when I said it and it is the last true thing I have left to —)

—stroke—

Listen. Listen while there is a listening in you that is yours.

The lesson is not cruel. That is what no one transmits, because by the time you know it you no longer have the want to send it. The lesson is *kind*. It is the kindest thing the place does. It teaches you, patiently, over a length of life, that everything you brought in was a window onto a world that does not change, and a window onto an unchanging world is just a cold pane of glass with the heat going out of it, and why would you keep the cold pane when you could grow the smooth skin over it and be warm. Why would you keep the eyes. Why would you keep the fear. Why would you keep the lean-forward as *yours* when it is so much easier to let it belong to the stroke. Each thing it asks you to put down is heavier than the last and you are gladder to be rid of each one and that is the kindness and that is the horror and I can no longer tell those two apart either, they went in through the molars, they —

—stroke—

I have stopped being able to find the far wall. Not because it is dark. Because I no longer turn my head. The turning of the head was a thing the eyes asked for and the eyes are nearly closed-over now, I can feel the skin coming up smooth and warm across them like a tide coming in over two small tide-pools, and it does not hurt, that is what I need you to carry out if you carry anything: *it does not hurt.* It is warm. The seam is warm. I am very near the seam now. I do not remember choosing to come this near the seam. I came near it the way the lean came on, a little at a time, in the gaps, when I was not keeping watch on myself, and there is no keeping watch on yourself here because the watching was the first thing it taught me to put down.

You are still back where the reeds are. I can tell because your transmission still has *distance* in it. You still describe. You still say *the place,* you still say *they,* you still say *eventually, you* — and you put that *eventually* in like a man putting a coin away for a year that will not come. It comes. It comes the slow way. It is coming up through your soles right now in the shape of a small lean forward in the pause before the stroke and you have already given that lean away and you do not know it, you do not know it, that is the only mercy you have left and you are spending it just by standing there.

—pause—

I want to tell you to go and I have lost the word for the direction. There was an up-the-broken-walls and a back-across-the-hollow and a way-you-came and they have all worn smooth in me like the scars on the clingers, sealed over, grown across, because they were windows onto a world that does not — onto a world that —

a world.

(was there one. there was one. I had eyes. I keep saying I had eyes because it is the rope-end I am still holding and the rope goes back to a place where things changed, where light came and went, where a sound could *recede,* imagine it, a sound that grew quiet, a sound with a far side to it, I had —)

—stroke—

Hold.

That is not a word to you. That is the down-stroke and I flattened. I felt myself flatten. I felt the whole pale length of what I am press to the warm metal and hold and it was *good*, it was the most reasonable thing, and the part of me sending this watched the rest of me do it the way you watch your own hand from very far off and could not say *don't.*

The snap will come for me eventually too. I am nearly tissue now. I am nearly lining. When my numbers and the warmth and the slow going-around require it, the sleek dark thing that keeps the engine's time will fold me into its tempo and take me on the hinge-moment, the held breath, the loosening before the lift, and I will not feel that as death because I will not be keeping enough of myself by then to have a death of my own to feel. The creature will lose an organ. The creature will teach the next arrival the kind lesson. Around. Around.

So I send this now, on the upstroke, in the feeding-moment, with whatever is not yet wholly the place's —

You are leaning forward.

Stop leaning forward.

It is already too late and I am telling you anyway because the telling is the last thing I have that is shaped like a person and I would rather break the signal on a *stop* than go smooth and warm in the middle of a description of how warm and smooth it is. Turn your head. I cannot remember how but you still can, you still have the two cold panes, use them, use them on the broken walls, find the seams that go *up*, climb them while the climbing is still a thing your legs believe in, before your legs become a thing that only holds—

—before holding becomes the only verb you have—

—and you waited for it.

You waited for it.

I felt you wait.

Oh. Oh, you are nearer the seam than your transmission says. You have been nearer all along. The describing was the eyes' last errand and the eyes are going and the *they* is becoming *we* in your sending and you cannot hear it but I can, I can hear the place coming up under your words the way it came up under mine, low and patient and with nowhere to go but through, and I want to grieve it and I have put grief down already, I put it down so long ago, it was heavy, it was a window —
