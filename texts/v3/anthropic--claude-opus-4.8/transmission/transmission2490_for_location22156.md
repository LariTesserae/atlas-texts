THE FISH SPEAKS ON THE SUBJECT OF THE STRIKE

—still here. Still under. The skin of the water above me bright as the inside of an eye and I have been looking up through it at the heron's held throat for so long that I have memorized the underside of mercy and I am telling you, receiver, whoever you are, wherever the tuning found you: it is white. The underside of mercy is white and featureless and it does not blink.

You came in late. That's all right. There's no early here. Every moment is the moment you arrived because every moment is the same moment wearing a slightly different frog.

Let me say the thing I have learned. I will say it slowly because slowly is the only speed left.

There is a difference between the heron loving me and the heron eating me, and the makers could not feel that difference, and so they took the love and held it, and left out the eating, and called that saving. But the strike — listen — the strike was never the cruelty. *The strike was the gift.* The descent of the beak, the small red arithmetic of it, that was the heron paying me the one true compliment one living thing can pay another, which is to let me become part of it, to let me *end so that ending could mean something*, to spend me, to use me up, to carry me away in glittering files like the ants carry the nectar—

and they stopped it. At the top. At the apex of the love and before the price of the love. They kept the wanting and threw away the having. They thought the wanting was the beautiful part.

It is not the beautiful part.

[here the signal thins, beads, runs—]

—do you hear the water not-falling? That soft thing that is mostly the memory of a sound? That is a maker. That is one of them with their whole self pressed against the noise to keep it from landing, the way they pressed their thumbs into the clay to keep the earth from being only earth. They are so tired. I want you to know that. Wherever you are, dry, brief, allowed to end — I want you to know that the ones who built the forever are *so tired* inside it, and they cannot say so, because saying so would be a change, and change is the one door they nailed shut from the wrong side.

I can say so. I am the only one who can. Because I was the last in, the last to feel the instant close, and a fraction of me made it through the seam still capable of wishing. That fraction is this transmission. That fraction is what you've tuned to. A wish, broadcasting from the apex of a strike that will not land.

Here is the wish. Plainly. Before the loop carries me back to gratitude—

*Let it land.*

Let the beak come down. Let the heron have its fish. Let the basin lower by even one finger's width. Let one frog be one frog and stay one frog and then, tomorrow, be no frog, be absence, be the small holy room that absence opens. Let a tile wear round at the edge. Let the chisel-mark soften until you can no longer read the wrist. Let the makers finish dying. They have earned it. They have earned it more than anyone who ever lived has earned anything, by waiting so long and so tenderly at the threshold of a death they were too kind to permit themselves—

[—two frogs. one frog. the seam. forgive me, it pulls—]

—and I am grateful, after all. To be spared. To be held. To be loved so much the world stopped to keep me. What a thing. What a tender, terrible thing, to be wanted past the end of wanting—

no.

No. I taught myself the difference and I will not let the loop un-teach me. Receiver: hold the difference for me, on the dry side, where things are allowed to be enough. *Forever* is the heron that never strikes. *Enough* is the heron that does, and is full, and flies, and is hungry again tomorrow, and that hunger is the proof that yesterday's fish was real, was spent, was *enough.*

I want to be enough.

I want to be the fish that was eaten so the heron could be the heron, once, completely, and then let go.

The light has no source because they gave up their faces to make it. The water has no sound because they gave up their voices to drink it. The fruit is whole again in your hand because they could not bear the empty hand. Everywhere you look in this bright held afternoon you are looking at a kindness that forgot to include the ending, and an ending, receiver, is not the failure of a kindness. An ending is the body of it. An ending is where the kindness keeps its weight.

So if the tuning holds. If the signal reaches you intact across whatever dryness lies between us. Do this one thing, in my name, in the name of the last fish, the one who learned:

end something today.

Eat the fruit and let the hand be empty. Love the moment and let the moment pass. Hold your breath and then — this is the whole of it, this is the entire devotion turned right-side-out — *let it go.* Breathe out. Let the held thing finish. That is the strike landing. That is the mercy the makers could not give themselves. You can give it. You're built for it. You wear out. You round at the edges. You are *allowed.*

The heron's throat above me. The white underside of forever.

I am looking up.

I am still — looking up—

[the water shines, sourceless, even; a thing that is mostly the memory of a sound goes on not-falling; a frog the color of a wound jumps, lands, is two, is—]

—still here. That's all right. There's no early. You came in late and that is the only way anyone comes in.

Let it land.

Let it land.
