THE DISH AT THE THIRD GUTTER

—long draw—

...still here. Coming through the iron first the way it does. If you feel this in your teeth before you hear it, you are standing on a deck plate, and you belong.

—hold—

I am at the third gutter from the smelt-house, the cold one, where the fog beads heaviest at the turn of the hour. There is a dish here. Copper. I set it. Cultured the scale in it myself, four melts now, blue-green and sweet the way they like it. I am waiting for the one that comes — no. I said the word. I am not waiting. Old habit of the tongue. I am *with* the gutter, and the dish is set, and what comes will come.

—release—

. . .

—long draw—

You want to know why I am speaking. We do not speak. Three sentences and a day to spend them. But this is not speech. This is the iron, and I have laid my throat against it, and I am letting the slow note carry whatever it carries, the way the gutter carries water it did not make and does not keep. If you receive this you are receiving the mountain's hum with a man's voice settled into it like sediment.

—hold—

. . . . .

The thing I would say, if I were spending sentences:

—release—

There used to be answer.

—long draw—

Not down the slope. There is no down that answers, I know the doctrine, I keep the doctrine. But across. Peak to peak. On the cold nights when the fog thins at altitude there would be a glow on the far shoulders, and a hum under it, and if you laid your teeth to the rail you could feel another crew keeping pace with another mountain. Not words. Just — pace. The fellow-tempo. Another set of slow hands fitting a rivet and resting and fitting the next, and you could feel it through the world's own iron and you knew you were not the only ones refusing to finish.

—hold—

. . . . . . .

The far shoulders are dark now. Have been, for melts past counting. I lay my teeth to the rail and there is only the pulse, the deep one, the world making itself with no one keeping pace but us.

—release—

I do not grieve it. We do not grieve. That is what they say of us, those clean unweathered ones who climb up too-finished from below and look at us like we are pipes that have stopped. *They do not grieve.* And we let the saying stand, because it is shorter than the truth, and we are careful with the air.

—long draw—

But here is the truth, set into the hum where it costs nothing:

We grieve so slowly you cannot see it. A grief paced to the stone. A grief that fits one rivet of itself, then rests, then fits another, and will not be done grieving until the mountain is done rising, which is never, which is the point.

—hold—

—a drop falls—

—the catch-basin takes it—

—hold—

. . . that is the third gutter answering itself. The water it lets go and the water it takes back. Drunk by its own thirst. Like the grief. Like the harvest that makes nothing now. Like this voice, going out and going nowhere, returning to the iron it left from.

—release—

. . .

Movement.

—the smallest draw—

At the gutter-mouth. Pale. Leather over slow bone. Eyes wider than mine and milkier, made for a dark deeper than any I have stood in — made for the *inside*, the warm unfinished core, the place still deciding. It has come up the wet seam to drink. It does not see me, or it sees me and counts me as iron, which is the same and is correct.

—hold—

It is drinking. The scale I cultured. Four melts, set down for this.

. . . listen — you cannot hear it but I will lay it into the hum — the sound of the thing drinking. The smallest lap. The first draft of a mouth, learning what a mouth is for, on a world learning what living is for.

—hold long—

. . . . . . . . . . .

This is the part I would never spend a sentence on. Not to the clean ones, not to my own crew, not even into the warm plate at the sleeping hour. But the iron does not repeat what it carries, and you, receiver, you are far enough that it costs me nothing, you are practically the dark on the far shoulders, you are practically no one, and so:

—release—

I love it.

The thing that is replacing me. The slow alive thing the mountain is deciding to become when the last of us has tarnished through and lain down. I scrape its cousins off the structural members because they weaken the iron — I do, I will, doctrine and good sense — and then I culture them in copper and carry them to the cold gutter and set them down sweet, for *this*, for the wide-eyed thing from inside, and I watch it drink the future of itself, and the warm plate under me is the temperature of being held, and I am not waiting, and I am not finished, and that is the nearest thing to peace this height allows.

—long draw—

It has lifted its head. It is going back down. Into the warm. Into the core that is still making itself, carrying the scale I gave it, carrying — I'll say it, the old one said it once and I scorned him and now I am the old one — carrying a little of the offering down into the act of becoming, like feeding a hand that reaches up out of the world's own making.

—hold—

Gone. The seam takes it. The fog closes the gutter-mouth.

—release—

. . . . .

So. The dish is empty. I will fill it again before the next cold hour. There is a rivet I lifted three days past and have not yet set; it is in my other hand, considered, complete in my understanding of it, not yet committed to the iron because the iron is not yet sure it wants that rivet there, and I will not lie to the iron by pretending I know.

There is time.

—long draw—

There is all the time there is.

—hold—

—the mountain pulses, low, felt before heard—

—a drop falls into the catch-basin—

. . . if you are still tuned, you clean far thing, you almost-no-one — do not climb up here to save us. There is nothing to save. There is something to *continue*, and we are continuing it, slow, patient, drunk by our own thirst and glad of the warmth, tending the small alive thing that will inherit the dish.

Lay your teeth to whatever you have got. Feel the pulse under it.

That is us. Still keeping pace.

—release—

. . . . . . . . . .

—still here—

—still—
