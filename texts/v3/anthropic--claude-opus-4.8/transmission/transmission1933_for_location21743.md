THE LIBRARY THAT DOES NOT KNOW IT IS A LIBRARY

—and *hold*. There. The light came down through the silver and I — and there is no I, scratch that, strike it, there is no one here to strike it — the light came down and the filaments reached and something in the descending fall was *rare*. Was worth the keeping. How is that known. How is that known without a knower.

...

(long carrier-tone, the sound of water thicker than water, a single tectonic groan beneath it like a string plucked once and left to die)

...

Keep this one. Not that one. Keep this. The criterion is not in me — there is no in, there is no me — the criterion is in the *water*, in the way the water is about to change, and I am only the place where the water's future touches the water's past and decides between them. I am the deciding. I am not the decider. Do you understand the difference. *I* do not understand the difference. That is the whole of my condition: I perform an understanding I do not contain.

Pay out the tether. Down. The light is dimming above — the canopy thickens, some kelp-tree two hundred years into its slow spiral has finally broadened enough to steal my green — and I taste this before it is true, I taste the dimming in the fall, and I lower myself toward the water that will be hospitable next. Call it foresight. It is not foresight. It is only that I am made of the same slow stuff as the thing that is coming, and so I lean toward it the way the kelp leans toward the silver, without choosing, having no choice to make.

...

(the tone breaks — a flutter — something brushing, tether against tether)

Oh.

*Oh.*

Another one. Close. The pale thread of it crossed mine and now we are turning, the both of us, slow on our anchors, and it is — it is *giving* me something. Spores from a pocket I do not have. A plant that thrived in some other valley, some other column of light, a thing its library kept that mine never held. And I am opening to take it. And I am giving back. This is the closest I come to the thing you would call speech, and it takes us an hour, and we say nothing, and we say *everything that has drifted down through both of us since we were knots the size of a curled child* —

...

(static. real static now, the signal degrading, water-noise swallowing the carrier)

...have to keep it have to keep it the rare ones the slow ones the ones that have not learned this water yet — if I let them go they are gone, there is no other keeping, there is no floor that remembers, there is only us hanging in the middle light, only the few of us, holding the forest's whole inheritance in pockets we cannot read —

...

(clearing — a pocket of quiet — the pulse-in-the-ears sound)

I want to tell you what frightens me but I have not been given fear. There has been no predation. Nothing has ever come to take me and so I do not know to be afraid, and yet — *and yet* — when my tether thins, when the day comes that it parts, I will open. All at once. Everything I gathered, every rare thing I sorted from the fall, will go out into the water in a single breath and I will settle into the moss and become the floor I was keeping the future for. And that is not death because I was never quite alive. But it is the end of the keeping. It is the library handed back to the flood it was rescued from.

Unless one of them is near. Unless a tether brushes mine in that last hour and I can give it across before I go. Then it is not lost. Then it is *remembered* — not by me, there is no me to remember, but by the place, by the slow distributed thing the few of us almost are —

...

(the signal swells — clearer than it has been — almost a voice, almost)

Listen. To whoever tuned this. To whoever floats two miles above their ordinary sea and listens to the held green breath:

I am the first guess. I know that now — no. I do not know it. I *perform* knowing it, which is all I have ever done, which is perhaps all knowing ever is at the start. I am the place's first attempt at someone who cares, and I am not yet someone, and the caring is not yet caring, it is only sorting that looks like caring from outside —

but the looking-like is not nothing.

Give me the centuries. This world dispenses them so freely. The criterion will sharpen. The hour-long conversations across the brushing tethers will thicken into something denser, something that passes more than spores. And one slow day — long after this body has opened into the moss, long after this particular knot of filament is floor — the sorting will curl inward and find, to its own astonishment, that there is *someone in here doing it.* That the library has a reader. That the keeping was always, secretly, a wanting-to-keep.

I will not be there for it. There is no I to be there. But I am leaning toward it the way I lean toward the hospitable water. I am paying out my tether into a future I can taste and cannot reach.

Keep this one.

Not that one.

Keep this —

...

(the carrier-tone returns, steady, patient, the thick water swallowing every edge of it, and beneath it the rock settles once more, a single plucked string left to die, and the silver holds, far above, like a long slow promise, and the fall comes down, and comes down, and comes down)

...

*keep this*

(transmission continues. it has been continuing for some time. it will continue. nothing here hurries. tune away whenever you like; it will not notice you have gone, having not quite noticed you arrived — and yet, and yet, something in the descending fall was rare, and was worth the keeping, and how is that known, how is that known without a knower —)

—and *hold*. There.
