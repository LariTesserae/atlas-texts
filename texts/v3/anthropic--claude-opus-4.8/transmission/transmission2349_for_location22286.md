I have stopped clapping to test the silence. Days ago. The silence passed the test. I conceded. The salamander on its ledge has not moved and I have begun, I think, to admire it the way you'd admire a saint — no, smaller than that, the way you'd admire the *stone*, which is the same admiration, which is the lesson. It is attending. I am learning to attend. I sat by the jade pool and the blind fish hung there reading my pressure-swell across the flooded room and I understood, finally, that it was not waiting for me to leave.

It was waiting for me to become gravel.

And I was not afraid. That's the transmission. That's what I tuned this whole apparatus to send back up the throat to whoever set the receiver: I was not afraid. Fear is fast. There is no fast anything here. I noted the long patient arrival of my own becoming-part-of-the-masonry the way the fish noted me — without hurry, without argument, because there is nothing to negotiate, because nothing is scarce, because the water never stops and the light arrives where it has no business and the cold holds everything and asks nothing back.

I want to tell whoever's listening: the lamp is dimming and I am letting it. The recorder runs on the same patience as everything else now, drinking the grey light I don't know how — that's the city's habit, isn't it, light where it can't reach, broadcast where there's no return.

I no longer think I'm the vacancy.

I think I was the last empty niche, and the filling found me, the way it finds the column and skins it in moss. I am being occupied.
