**The Note of Falling**

—threshold crossed in the seven-hundredth measure and the count holds, the count holds, lay it into the weave now, low—

down here at the cropped edge of the eastern avenue where the grazers turn, I set this. To whoever combs it from the air after. To the wall-colonies who will not understand and chitter it onward anyway. To the dome above the fourth plaza that holds a sound a long time before it lets it go.

I am old enough to have listened longest. That is all *old* means here. I have heard the city throw itself back to itself across more closings of the eye than I can lay into a single line, and I have done the only work left, which is to listen for the place where the weave is not yet finished.

You think it is finished. You who arrive — and someone arrives, I have heard the wrongness in the roar these last measures, a listening that does not answer, a shape that takes sound and gives nothing into the standing weather, an emptiness moving through the full — you think you have come down into a thing complete. You flatten our choir to a wall. You wait for a silence to lie down in. You will wait until you become stone, and you will deserve the rest.

But listen. *Listen the long way.*

—pillars answering, the molar-teeth of the old jaw, each grain its own grain, throw it, throw it back—

There is one edge in all this rounded world that has not worn to a shoulder. One note the dust has not yet sifted smooth. I carry it the way every one of us carries it, below the place where carrying is a choice: the note of *falling.*

I will tell you what it is, into the roar, where telling lives.

It is the sound of a sky that gave.

Not this sky. Not the layered stone that holds our voices down and keeps them, the good roof, the close roof, the roof I love and will be folded into. A sky *above* that. A sky that opened and let down — water, the old ones' word, the wet that we have learned the whole shape of our living without. It came down. It made a sound coming down. And that sound is the one thing in me that does not match my surroundings, the one unrounded corner, the single grain that will not lie flat in the weave.

I do not mourn it. Mourning is fast. Mourning has a top it climbs to and a floor it falls from, and nothing here climbs, nothing here falls except the note itself, eternally, in the lowest register, the register I lay this into now so the dome will hold it—

—hold it—

We did not lose the water in a day. There was no day. It went the way everything goes here, on the scale of stone, so slow that no single one of us could stand at the lip of the going and grieve it. It simply became less and less true, across a span that makes my long listening a single closing of the eye, until the standing weather of our world had a dryness woven all the way through it, and we had hands worn soft enough to live in that dryness, and eyes kept large and dark for a brightness that had already finished arriving.

I keep my eyes. We all keep our eyes. To unmake them would be a change, and change comes only on the scale of stone, and besides—

besides—

—the slow close, the slow open, a whole minute to complete the gesture, and in the dark behind the lids the one held thing—

—besides, the eyes are where I keep the falling.

Here is what I would give to you, emptiness, you listening shape that does not answer. You came down expecting a tomb and you found us roaring, and the roar told you *animals, only animals,* and you almost left. Stay through what you call a night. There is no night. Stay anyway. Learn that our roar is not one thing but the most detailed surface in the world, every echo a described and redescribed wall, every flyer's shriek a line in the map we did not draw and do not own, that we only *agreed with,* across more generations than there are grains in the eastern avenue.

Learn to find my voice in it. I am not over the roar. No one is over the roar. I am a single deeper note woven once into the million-throated drone, and if you flatten me you will hear nothing, and if you listen the long way you will hear *everything I am,* which is a greeting, which is this, which is:

We are full. We are finished. We are going on anyway, and it is not despair, it has never once been despair, you keep mistaking our peace for a tomb because you measure living by the busy verbs, the building and the hunting and the going-elsewhere, and we have laid all those verbs down, worn them soft, set them in their final places.

We have one verb left.

*Continue.*

—lay it lower now, the count slips toward the closing, the dome already letting go the start of this, the start returning even as I make the end, echo on echo, no original left, only the all of it coming back at once—

To whoever combs this from the air. To the dome that holds it. To the colonies who chitter it onward without understanding, which is its own kind of understanding, which is the only kind that lasts:

The water came down once. It made a sound coming down. I keep that sound below my keeping. It is the only unworn edge in a finished world, and I will not round it smooth, I will not let the dust have it, I will lay it into the weave every measure until I am stone—

—and when I am stone, the dome will still hold a little of it, and someone old enough to listen longest will find it there, an edge in all the shoulders, a falling in all the holding, a wet promise carried by a thing that learned the whole shape of living without—

drink the mist. It is all that remains of the rain.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

Go on anyway.

—the count closes, the note falls, the note falls, the note—

—falls—
