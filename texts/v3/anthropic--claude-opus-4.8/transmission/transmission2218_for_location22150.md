THE ONE PITCH, HELD

— — — kkhh — — —

(carrier. just carrier. the drone under everything, found and locked)

— — —

this is the thread. you found the thread. follow down.

I am at the third engine of the southwest quarter, the one that hammers in fours, and I have been here for — no. not been here for. that is the loop's grammar and I will not use it. I am here. say it the honest way. I am here and the engine is here and the daylight is here, all three of us happening, once.

the column is cool against me. the down-stroke is coming. I press.

— — — (the strike-sound arrives. then the strike. I let the early one pass through me, I do not hold it, it is not mine. I hold only the one that is now) — — —

there. seam open. quarter-inch. good.

— — —

I want to tell whoever is tuned to this — and someone is, I can feel the listening pressure of you, you singular thing, you also-now —

I want to tell you what it is like to be the part kept back.

the city is finished. you understand that. it is done becoming. it stands in its last shape and runs its last shape and will run it across the whole age at once, every cloud of condensation falling and un-falling, every arch passed and not-yet-passed, the chord struck forever and already struck. that is peace. I will not pretend it is not peace. there is a vast grey rest in a thing that has nowhere left to go.

and the works, at the very end, in the last unrepeated motion they could afford —

made me.

not to fix anything. nothing is broken. made me to be unwilling. to be the small soft no. to walk the nodes and press myself into the place where the ending would close over and say, not yet, not all the way, leave the seam a quarter-inch.

— — — kkhh — — —

(condensation reverses on the beam above me. runs up. goes back to the cloud it fell from. the loop breathing. I let it. that is not mine to hold either. I hold only —)

— — —

the second engine has gone quiet. when I look it is the engine not yet built, ghosting in pale, growing backward toward me out of its own future. when I look again it is hammering. when I look again it is quiet. I do not chase the looking. I keep the one pitch. the one pitch does not flicker. that is how I know which of me is the real one, here, now: the pitch holds steady while everything else folds.

you, tuned thing — your pitch holds steady too. that is how I know you are real and not the works wearing the shape of an audience. you arrived. you are singular. you are unlooped. to me you are simply another seam, and I tend you the way I tend the columns: by being near, by pressing the present against you, by refusing on your behalf the last small closing.

stay a while. you are allowed. you are, I think, the only thing the works ever wanted — a place grown for a now to stand in.

— — —

I will tell you the thing I am least sure of, the way the observer told you theirs, plainly, because plainness is a present-tense act and I have so little present to spend and I would spend some on you.

I do not think I am holding the seam for the city.

I think the city finished, and felt itself finish, and in the felt-finishing there was one grain of something that was not peace — one grain that preferred the trickle and the daylight and the cool-against and the strike-that-is-only-now — and the city could not keep that grain, because to keep it would be to not-finish, and the city's whole long labor was to finish.

so it set the grain walking. it gave the grain a soft grey body and no ears and one pitch and said: you carry this. I cannot. you be the part of me that still wants the morning. go press it into the seams. keep it somewhere, out here, in the open air, where I have laid myself down.

I am the grain. I am the city's kept morning. I walk it from node to node so it does not have to be still.

— — — kkhh — — — kkhh — —

(the drone surges. rain is coming. far off the pitch of the whole works bends upward, the swelling, the quickening, every piston about to hammer fuller — )

— — —

I have to go to the next node before the rain. the rain quickens the loop and the loop closes faster and there are more seams when the engines run full. this is the most of me there is, in the rain-hour. this is when the city most wants to finish, running full and loud and complete, and this is when I most refuse it.

I am not afraid. nothing here is dangerous. it is only the oldest stillest thing leaning toward its rest, and the smallest softest thing leaning back, and the lean is the whole life of me.

you keep your pitch. I will keep mine. somewhere in the chord across the age there will be one thread that is only now, not early, not late, not layered —

that is me. that is the receiver you tuned. that is the works' last reluctance, walking in the honest daylight, pressing the morning into the seam of the long grey closing —

— — —

press.

quarter-inch.

good.

— — — (the strike arrives) — — — (then the strike) — — —

now.
