THE LONG COURSE

—carry on from here. Carry on from here. Drip. ...still true at the third notch, the one your mother's mother set, I have checked it with the cord and it holds, the wall does not lean, you may build—

—and to the hand that comes after the hand that comes after mine: the seep above the stair has begun. There is wet in the seam now that was dry when I was young. Leave it. Leave it. Route the upper landing east and let the stone come down to meet you in its own years. I will not see what it becomes. You will not see what it becomes. Someone will. Drip.

This is the long course. This is the channel that does not close. Listen—

—a leaf today, in the third trough under the bright flue. A whole leaf, uncurled, the green of it, oh. We stood close. We made the small sounds. Set it down in the record that on this brightening there was a leaf and it was an event and we were glad and quiet about it together—

...static, the soft static of water finding new stone...

—my hands. Look at my hands. You will read me by them when I am only a name no one says, only a notch that means I left off here. The chisel-callus, see. The cord-callus. The press at the heel of the palm. This is the whole arc. This was not given up. This was the shape it took. Carry—

—do not drop the pebble. Do not raise the voice. There is nowhere to make the large gesture and we have forgotten how and we do not miss it. Murmur the grief. Murmur the joy. Everyone you love is within the reach of your arm. There is no distance here to shout across. There never was—

Drip. Drip. ...the cave thickens by a hair... we will be old before it is done... it will not be done...

—the bowl is handed on again. Six hands, now seven. It has not finished being useful. That is all we mean by lasting. That is the whole of it. There is no other lasting and we have not needed one—

—and to the one who tunes to us from very far, from wherever the long course reaches: we know we are not noticed. We made our peace with that before you came. The thing above us, building, does not know our names and will not, through all our generations, and we lay our true courses anyway, in good faith, alongside it. This is not sorrow. Mark that clearly. This is not sorrow. It is the manner of a people who keep the manners of slow stone—

You leave the growing alone.
You hold up the ones above you.
You are held by the ones beneath.
You add one true course.
You cut the small mark that means *continued*.
You hand it on.

That is sufficient. That has always been sufficient.

—here is where I left off. Here is true. Carry on from—

Drip.

...carry on...

Drip.
