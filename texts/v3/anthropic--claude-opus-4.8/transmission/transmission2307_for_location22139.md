THE NORTH SHELF IS ANSWERING

—still here. Still on. If you're catching this, hold the line a moment, I'm setting the can down so I've a hand free for the cable—

There. Palm flat. Good.

I want to tell you about tonight because tonight is ordinary and I've decided ordinary is the thing worth sending. The grand sentences carry themselves. *She ran water to the north shelf.* That one will outlive me, fine, let it. But nobody plucks a cable to say *the bearing ran smooth and I was glad,* and so nobody two spires over ever feels that gladness come down the line, and that seems to me a poverty. So: the bearing ran smooth tonight. I was glad. Receive it however you receive things.

[a long tone, low, fading]

The engine on the east face of the third great needle — my route calls it the Patient One, though everyone's route has a Patient One, it's not a name so much as a feeling we all arrive at — it had a roughness three nights back. You feel it in the heel of the hand before the ear ever finds it. A little catch, once a stroke, like a man clearing his throat before he's sure he means to speak. I oiled it and it eased and tonight there was nothing. Nothing. The flank just rose and fell under my palm in the same time as my own chest, and for a moment I could not have told you which of us was breathing.

That's the part I can't send down a cable. There's no note for *I forgot where I ended.*

[static — wind crossing the line — a single sustained hum, then quiet]

—if you're still there. The pools on the north shelf are full. You should see them at this hour, though you can't, so I'll do it for you. The succulents are opening. That smell — wet sugar, somebody once called it and I've never bettered it — comes up off the water just as the heat lets go, and the blind things rise in the pool because the surface cools and they feel it, glassy, turning over each other, having come up through the pumps from a dark they'll never leave and found this warm bright pocket and decided, in whatever way a thing with no eyes decides, to stay.

I lifted that water. Not the rain — the rain does its own work — but the pumped portion, the part that climbs against its own wishes. I sent it up last dusk and the night before and I'll send it up again when I've finished talking to you, and those blind pale swimmers exist in that pool because of a decision, mine, repeated, that they should.

I don't tell them this. They wouldn't thank me. That's not why.

[thock — distant — a piston completing its stroke — then the long pause]

There. Heard that? That's the Patient One. Once a minute now. You learn to wait inside the pause. The pause is not empty, that's the thing I'd most want a stranger to understand if I had a stranger and the words — the pause is the engine *deciding* again. Every stroke is a fresh agreement. Nothing here runs because it was wound up once long ago. It runs because something keeps consenting to the next turn. The builders consented. Then they were gone and the ones after consented in their place. And now it's mostly me on this quarter, and the can's getting light, and my hands have got the grease in the creases that won't come out, and I consent.

That's all keeping is. Consent, made into a circuit. Made into water climbing.

[the tone again, deliberate this time — four notes, a small grammar — I am here / all well]

I won't pretend at the rest of it. There's a sentence I owe this transmission to be honest, and it's this: when I set down the coil for the last time, the Patient One will run a while on its own momentum and then a bearing will catch the way that one caught three nights back, only there'll be no heel of a hand to feel it early, and it will go from a throat-clearing to a grind to a stop, and the pump will still and the north pool will give itself back to the noon, and the blind things will end in a dust that was warm water, and the wings — 

the wings will lift off this quarter and go to wherever the climbing still happens.

And the rock will be patient about it. It was dead before. It's in no hurry.

[silence — true silence — long enough that you might think the signal lost]

—but not tonight.

That's the whole of the faith and I'll say it once, plainly, into the line, because I'm alone enough that plainness costs me nothing: the height was free and it did not care. The water did not want to climb. And someone looked at all that perfect indifferent stone and decided it should fill with wings anyway — not because it asked, but precisely, *precisely* because it didn't. You teach the dead thing to drink. And then you stay.

I'm picking the can back up now. It's light but there's enough. The dusk-flowers are open and the air's gone cool and clean and the gliders are still wheeling up there, silent, riding the last of the day's warmth between the needles, and every one of them is downstream of a decision, and the decision is mine to make again, and I am making it.

Water goes up tonight.

If you're receiving — and I never know, the cables hum whether anyone's hand is on the far end or not, that's the loneliness and also the freedom of it — if you're receiving, then know it was an ordinary night and the bearing ran smooth and I was glad, and that I sent the gladness down the line on purpose, into the dark, into the height, in case.

In case someone's hand was there.

[a final tone — long, low, even — fading into the hum of the wind across the cable —

— then the slow pause —

— then, once more, faint, as the can lifts and the work resumes —

— the engine, breathing —
