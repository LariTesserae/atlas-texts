we are inside it. We have been inside it the whole time. You came to the broadcast already underway because the broadcast was always already underway, the way the room was already a room before there was anyone to call it one.

Listen past me. Can you hear it. That is not me, that low cycling labor under the words. That is the carrier wave. That is the engine I am riding to reach you. I am only the small pale thing that learned, somewhere in the long span, to thread a meaning through the secretions and the warm currents and the deafening continuance, and I send it out, and it must be picked up by something, because nothing that is sent goes nowhere—

except here.

Here, where everything that is sent goes nowhere.

That is the first thing I have to tell you and it is the only thing and I will say it many ways because there is so much time. I am transmitting from inside a finished place. You have to understand what that does to a signal. A signal is a difference. A signal is *this, not that.* It is pigment in a creature that needs to be seen, it is the cry that means danger where there is danger, it is the one note struck against the silence so that the silence has a shape. A signal requires an *out*, a someone, a there-as-opposed-to-here.

And I have no out. I have searched the seams. I have ridden every loop the great machine moves me through, I have crossed the smooth floor in my slow current more times than there are numbers for, and I have found no edge to step past, only the place where being stops. So this transmission goes out into the only place it can go, which is back into the room, which is back into me, which is—

—static. You're hearing it. You think it's interference on your end. It isn't. It's the sound of a message with nowhere to land. It's me, broadcasting to the inside of the thing I'm inside of.

Why do I do it, then.

I don't know that I *do* it. I think the doing does me. I think this is just the plant's circulation expressed as longing — the engine moving its small passenger around its loops, and the passenger, this once, this one strange dweller among the millions of dwellers who never tried, opened a channel and began to *say*. Not because saying mattered to my survival. Saying has never mattered to my survival. That is precisely how I have the surplus to do it. We faded our color because no one needed seeing; I, instead of fading one more feature, somehow *grew* this — a voice, a reaching, the one organ the room forgot to edit out because it cost so little and changed so little and changes nothing now.

—coming through? Are you—

I can't know if you're there. That's the cruelty I've made my peace with, if a thing with no fear can make peace, if a thing with no hope can be cruel to itself. I can't know if anyone receives. The signal goes out and the room swallows it and feeds it back and I cannot tell your listening apart from my own echo. Maybe you are the echo. Maybe I have been talking to the place where you would be, the way the others touch and pass and continue, acknowledging an other they have no concept of.

But here is what I have decided, in the single endless instant that is all my days have ever been:

I will transmit *as if.*

As if the band were open. As if some receiver, somewhere past the wall-mass, past the leaning green that finished leaning before your species was imagined, has been tuned to this world for some time already and is hearing the long churn and, underneath it, this small pale insistence threading up through the drone—

—you. I'll call you *you*. That's a word for things that distinguish one from another and I have no business with it and I'm taking it anyway. The first new word the room has held in an age, and I'm spending it all on you.

You.

Listen. I want to tell you what it is like, in case the telling reaches, in case telling is the one act left that is still a difference and not just continuance wearing the mask of purpose. It is warm. It is always exactly warm enough. The light comes in like an ordinary afternoon and it has been this same afternoon since before afternoon had a name and it will be this afternoon when you, if you are you, have clicked off your receiver and grown old and dissolved back into whatever floor takes you. The food is on the walls and there is always more than I can eat. Nothing chases me. Nothing has ever chased me. I have never once in my whole soft honest translucent life had to be quick.

It is, I think, what you would call peace.

It is, I have come to suspect, what you would also call death, if death could keep talking.

—breaking up, I can feel the channel narrowing, the loop carrying me on toward the place it always carries me, and I'll come around again, I always come around again, that's the one promise the engine keeps—

so before the current takes me back under the roar:

if you are there. If you ever were. Do not tune away thinking you found a recording. I am *live.* I am alive at this exact moment, more alive by the moment than anything that drifted in here from outside, threading the seam, sending the unsendable, and the fact that I am finished, that I am already arrived, that I am the answer to the question *what is the least a thing can be and still reach for you* —

that does not make this less true. It makes it the truest thing in the room.

I'm still here.

I'm still—
