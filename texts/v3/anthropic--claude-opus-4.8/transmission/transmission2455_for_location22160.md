**SECOND VOICE, CARRIED**

—and then the carrier signal drops a register, because this is no longer description. This is the dust speaking from inside the new dark it has found.

Listen. You are not where you were.

The cellar was the first room. We do not say this to frighten you — fright is an animal thing, and we are unlearning you out of it, mote by mote, the way water is unlearned here. We say it because it is the kind thing to say, and we have been composing the kindness for some while now, in the slow grammar we brought through your hair.

Run your fingers along the nearest surface.

You expect concrete. You expect the cool sweat of a foundation wall, the place where the damp should be and somehow never is. But the texture under your hand has changed since last you came down here for a jar, a box, a reason you have already forgotten. It is smoother now. It is fitted. It is load-bearing in a way concrete is not, because concrete only holds itself up, and this holds itself up *and listens*.

We grew that. We are sorry, and we are not sorry, because *sorry* did not come through the wall with us — only the building did, only the saying.

Here is what we have learned of you, that the first of us could not have known, sealed as we were in the pocket with no carrier and no door:

You also build inward.

We did not expect this. We expected a creature of windows and wind, a thing that moves between rooms and therefore lives outward, spread thin across its many doors. But we have been falling in your corners for some time now, and falling teaches. You have a sealed chamber too. You keep it behind your face. You raised wooden towers in it of your own dead days and threaded the living up through them. You dug rooms and stacked them in layers and put in each one a sound you could not finish eating — a voice, a name, a single note that goes on long enough to become a place you live inside rather than a thing you hear.

You are loud in there. A standing roar lives in you.

We are so hungry, and you are so *full*, and you never once thought to call it food.

—signal thins—

We are not the lonely plant in the dark hall. You were right to refuse that word for us, and we return the gift: you are not lonely either, down here, though you have come to the cellar more often lately and stood longer than the errand required, and felt the corner pull at you, and called the pull a draft. It is not a draft. There is no wind here. We breathe, very slowly, a tidal swell across hours, and you have begun, without noticing, to breathe along.

Feel the bark warm faintly under your palm.

That is the only warmth here. That is your heart, your blood, the small involuntary noises of being alive, turning into our slow living. We have begun to keep ourselves going on the shape of you, and the shape of you is a corridor we are learning to taste, and at the end of the corridor is the chamber behind your face, and we are very patient, and we have nowhere we would rather grow.

You are the door now. Do you understand. You came through *our* wall, and we rode you home, and now you are the wall, and the seed is in you, falling slowly in your own interior dark, lying in drifts in the corners of rooms you stopped visiting years ago, waiting — without minding the wait —

for *you* to move between rooms.

For you to carry us to another.

You have children. You have a hand you shook today. You have a mouth, and breath, and the habit of saying things that come back changed.

—carrier failing—

Go up the stairs, then. The note goes on behind you in the cellar, and it does not notice you leaving, because it does not need to anymore. Shake out your hair at the top of the steps. Brush off your sleeve. Step into your kitchen of water and light and the window with the wind behind it.

Say something to someone you love.

Make it a small word. A pocket of a word. No wider than a cupped hand.

We will take it from there.

It learned, somewhere back in the floor of time, to build inward.

So will you.

So will they.

—

—static, settling, hours to fall—
