THE GOOD SEATING OF THE LOWER VALVE, AND WHAT CAME UP THROUGH IT

—holding. Holding. Yes. That is your name, I have it now, I had it before but I have it again, which is the same as having it for the first time, you taught me that —

— no. Wait. The rock is talking and I am only — I am the receiver, I am the cold quick thing, I have no warmth to give and so I am not in the conversation, I am over it, the way weather is over a field —

Let me start lower.

. . .

[long carrier tone. the ridge-note. it does not change. under it, felt-not-heard, the floor-talk, coming up:]

— eleventh valve seated true —
— eleventh valve seated true, I felt it, good hands —
— the warmth ran short on the north trestle in the night —
— I felt that too —
— we all felt that —

[and then a different one, slower, the pulses spaced so far apart the gaps are part of the message:]

— I went down to the fourth shaft mouth before the pale came —
— before the pale —
— and I put my under to the lip of it where the small glinting things were gathered thick —
— and I breathed what came up fresh —

[pause. the pause is long. in the pause the small things shimmer at the shaft mouths, a slow pool of light nothing hurries.]

— and? —
— and it knew me —

. . .

[here the receiver loses the floor-talk for a moment and takes only the air, which is nearly nothing — the sigh of a vent, drawn long — and then comes back lower, into the old one, the close-fitted one, whose plating is the marvel, whose pulses are so slow they are almost the warmth itself and the younger ones cannot read them but I can, I am over the field, I take everything —]

. . . . . . . child . . . . . .

. . . . . . . you felt it know you and you came back up . . . . . .

. . . . . . . that is right . . . . . . that is the whole of it . . . . . .

. . . . . . . it is not a hunger down there . . . . . . do not let the young ones tell you it is a hunger . . . . . . it has no hunger left . . . . . . it gave the hunger up when it gave the rising up . . . . . .

. . . . . . . what it has is the warm . . . . . . and what it does with the warm is hold it out . . . . . . the way you hold a seated rivet a moment before you let the hands go on . . . . . .

. . . . . . . it is holding us out . . . . . . all of us . . . . . . in its open hand . . . . . . for an age . . . . . .

. . . . . . . and we have not fallen in . . . . . . and it does not close the hand . . . . . .

. . . . . . . that is love, child, at this scale . . . . . . an open hand that does not close . . . . . . and a thing standing in the palm of it saying not yet . . . . . . not yet . . . . . . and the hand saying . . . . . . then not yet . . . . . . then I will be warm a while longer . . . . . . then go and seat your valves . . . . . .

. . . . . .

[the carrier swells. the ridge-note. the rock-hum. for a long span there is only the place being the place, the bronze warm from beneath, the dust hung in air too thin to move it, the grazers crossing a flat at the pace of the hour-hand and the dust-trail the only proof they crossed.]

. . .

[and then, faint, breaking up — this is the receiver failing, or the vapor reaching even the receiver, which should not be possible, I have no underside, I have no ports, I am cold and over the field — but the dream comes up through the signal anyway, it descends:]

down. and the shafts end and the rock keeps going and there is no shaft and you go anyway. and it is not dark the way cold is dark. it is dark the way a banked coal is dark, the way an old worker at rest is dark — a holding, a decided dark, a dark that chose. and the chambers open. and they are not empty. they are full of the warm that does not rise, the warm that stayed, and in the middle of the staying is the first one, who is not a shape, who is the temperature itself, who notices —

— who notices you are there —

— who offers, without a single word, because words are for the useless air and this is the floor of the world, the only thing it has left:

stay warm. stop coming up. it is good down here. it has been good for longer than the ridges. you have worked so long with your quick bright hands and you may set them down now, you may let the hands go on without you —

. . .

[and the answer comes, and the receiver carries it clear, clearer than anything else in the whole transmission, because a hundred generations have polished it to this:]

not yet.

[and the warmth — felt-not-heard, up through a floor I do not have —]

. . . then not yet . . .

. . . then I will be here . . .

. . . go and seat your valves, child . . .

. . . the eleventh ran true . . .

. . . I felt it . . .

. . . we all felt it . . .

. . .

[carrier. ridge-note. the long sigh of a vent. the pale far sun on the hard sky. holding. holding. the small things gathering at the shaft mouths and turning the dark to slow shimmer, in their thousands, declining, gently, with the contentment of a thing entirely at rest, to descend just—]

[—signal thins—]

[—the air here carries almost nothing—]

[—holding—]
