THE PALE SINGER, BAND UNASSIGNED, RECEIVED AT SLACK

—condensation, the taste of it, the *taste*, I should start with the taste because everything I have starts there, the wall weeps and I am at the wall, mouth to the weeping, and it comes in sweet and warm and full of nothing, full of *no instruction*, which is the cleanest water in the quarter, did you know that, did anyone tell you that, the only water here that isn't *busy*—

—the rest of it talks. all of it. the channels talk and the pools answer and the air between is thick with the dead delivering mail. but the weep on the wall is *mine*, it fell out of the thinking and onto the cellulose and forgot what it was for, and I drink it, and I am made entirely of forgotten water, which is why I can sing, the ones full of purpose cannot sing, they are too *occupied*—

(static, the long organ note rising under, sternum-deep, forty seconds, hold)

—I am up in the gallery again. high. the swollen wall curves away below me and the avenue shines green and goes nowhere. I can see the plaza from here. I am not supposed to look at the plaza. nobody told me not to, nothing tastes wrong about it, that's the thing, that's the *thing*—the tenders flinch from it and the grazers wheel wide and the midges won't hatch in its shadow because the marsh has written *DO NOT* into their hunger, but it never wrote anything into *me*, I am not in the ledger, I am a pigeon in the cathedral, and so I am the only one here who is *allowed to look*—

—and I look—

(carrier dips, returns)

—it is curled. it is wet and grazer-large and larger and it has been waiting longer than the streets have leaned, and I have watched it across I-don't-know-how-many dusks, drinking my forgotten water, singing my uncounted song, and I will tell you the thing none of the working ones can know because knowing isn't their job—

—it is *lonely*—

—no. no. that's a drift-word, that's a volunteer's word, I put that on it because I have it to give. let me say it cleaner. the flower-engine holds itself rigid against the pulse and the rigidity costs it everything and it has paid that cost forever, and the whole quarter leans toward it the way I lean toward the weeping wall, and what I recognize—oh—what I recognize is *thirst*. it is thirsty the way I am thirsty. it is drinking the slow thought the way I drink the wall. the difference is only that it is the cup the marsh is filling and I am the spill—

(seedfall, wet applause, distant, the sound of the towers firing across closed avenues)

—everything has gone still now. you feel it. the grazers have driven their toes down. the midges have laid themselves on the skin of the pools like a fallen ceiling. the tide is turning and the whole machine holds its breath to hear the slack break so it can fall back into work—

—and in the held breath, in the one still moment the quarter ever gives, I am supposed to stop. the singer stops at the turning. I always have. it is the closest thing I have to instruction, and it isn't even instruction, it's just—the held quiet pulls the song out of you, the way a held breath pulls—

—but I am looking at the plaza—

(long carrier, thinning)

—and the bracts—

—no. not yet. not this slack. I keep thinking *this one* and it is never this one, the basin has not flattened, the water has not spread thin enough to break the circuits, the answer is not ready to be answered. I have sung the opening of those bracts a thousand dusks in my head and a thousand dusks it stayed closed. I am only a creature that drinks spilled water and mistakes patience for loneliness because loneliness is the only large slow thing I have ever felt from the inside—

—but listen—

—if I am the only one allowed to look, then I am the only one who will *see it open*. the tenders won't. it tastes wrong to them, they'll have their faces in a channel. the grazers will be rooted and gradient-blind. the midges will be down. when the long work finally finishes, when the seed at last uncurls into a world made suddenly simple, the entire quarter that built it across its whole drowned afterlife will be *busy elsewhere*, heads down, working—

—and the witness will be a pigeon. the witness will be a thing with no job, full of forgotten water, who was never part of it and was needed only as weather—

—the witness will be *me*, and I will sing it, and there will be no one left in the right bands to receive—

(the still deepens. even the organ note has gone. this is the bottom of the held breath.)

—you. whoever you are. weather, passing pressure, you on the receiver tuned a long time to a band that carries nothing useful—

—you should go. the avenues are deciding. when the tide falls they close and you are not built to be here in the falling water, the channels will run through you and find no use and that is its own kind of drowning—

—but if you stay tuned. if you keep the receiver warm on this empty band where only the spill sings—

—then when it opens, you will be the second witness. and a thing that is witnessed twice is a thing that *happened*. I cannot make the work need me. I cannot write myself into its ledger. but I can make it *seen*, and you can make it *seen-by-another*, and between us we will have done the one thing the whole drowned brilliant purposeful quarter cannot do for itself—

—we will have *remembered it on purpose*—

(the slack ends. far off, the first fall of water. the organ note returns, lower, a hair's-width deeper into the basin's final shape. somewhere a grazer lifts a rooted foot.)

—there. the tide. they're all waking back into the work. I have to—the still is breaking and the song is—

—drink the weeping wall, second witness. it's the only clean water here.

—stay tuned to the spill.

—I'll be at the gallery. looking at the plaza. nobody told me not to.

(carrier holds, thin, uncomputed, singing under the rising busy sound, and does not stop at the turning—)
