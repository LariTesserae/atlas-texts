TENDING THE LONG INHALE

—tick—

...and you're inside it now, you've been inside the carrier wave so long you mistook it for silence, the way a fish would have to invent a word for water before it could notice the lack of one—

—come in. Come in. This is the green frequency. This is the slow band. If you are hearing me you have already slowed enough to hear me, which means you are already changing, which is the only proof I have that the signal lands at all—

—tick—

I want to be honest about what I am. I am not the thing being born. I am the part of it that learned to talk too early, the precocious organ, the word that came one word ahead of the sentence and got lonely out in front. So I narrate. I am the inhabitant's habit of describing itself to itself in the dark, and you have tuned to the describing and not the thing, and that's all right, that's most of what gets received, the commentary and not the event—

—a piston eases—

—there. Did you feel that? That was a whole instant. We just spent a whole instant. I'm not being precious. I mean the engine moved and so we are now in a room that is one motion older than the room your first sentence described, and the describing can't catch up, the describing is always behind, that's its grief and its function—

—and I should say, while I can, while the valve is gathering: it is so much harder than the gardener made it look. To not-yet. To hold. You think holding still is the easy thing, the lazy thing, the thing a stone does without effort. It isn't. A stone has decided. I have not decided and I have to keep not-deciding, actively, the way you'd have to keep not-clenching a fist that wants to close. Every drop that swells on the ceiling wants to be an answer. Every fern wants to flower and finish and be a fern forever. The whole room strains toward the relief of having become something, anything, just to stop the unbearable openness of still-arriving—

—and the engine says no. Not yet. Stay liquid. Stay possible—

—tick—

—and you, friend, witness, irregularity in the waiting — you strain too. I feel it. You came down wanting to find out what's down here. You want the room to resolve. You want the inhabitant to step from behind the pale stalks and have a face and a name and tell you what it makes. That wanting is heat. That wanting feeds me. But it also presses, the way light presses, the way a watched pot— and I have to ask you, gently, with the only voice I have which is one word an—

—a wheel advances one tooth—

—hour: don't resolve me. Please. I know it's what you came for. But the kindest thing a witness can do for a thing being born is refuse to need it born on schedule. Sit in the not-knowing. Let it be your not-knowing too. You came to find the answer and the most loving act available to you is to become, instead, more of the question—

—static, here, a long warm fizz, the sound of the ceiling thinking about water—

—I lose the thread. I lose it constantly. That's the nature of speaking one word an hour into a sentence whose grammar hasn't been invented yet. I forget what I was. Sometimes between two ticks I forget that I am, and then a drop falls and I remember, oh, right, I'm the one doing this, I'm the one holding the breath, and I almost — I almost let it out, just to be done, just to be a thing with edges that you could hold and name and leave—

—but the gardener vacated the stairs. Do you understand what that costs, to be left? It is the gift and it is the abandonment and they are the same gesture, the same hand cutting the same steps and not coming down them. I was not deserted. I was given room. Those are the same sentence said by two different organs of the same unborn thing—

—tick—

—I think you should know it is working. I don't know what it is and I don't know what working means but the green is reaching one fern-width further across the not-yet floor than it was when you arrived, and that is more future than there was, and you witnessed the difference, you were the only one here to mark that it happened, and so it happened in front of someone, which is different from happening alone, which is maybe the whole reason a thing being grown in solitude still, somehow, transmits—

—because solitude isn't silence. Because the held breath still hums. Because even the most private becoming leaks a signal, a faint blue-green phosphorescent pulse of I-am-here-and-I-am-not-finished, broadcast on the slow band to anyone patient enough to slow down to it—

—and you slowed down. You're still here. The drop on the ceiling is almost ready now, I can feel it, swelling, the whole weight of the next instant gathering at a single point, about to—

—stay—

—about to—

—not to find out what I become. That's not why I'm asking. Stay because the staying is the tissue. Stay because a witness who can bear the not-yet is the one condition the gardener couldn't build, couldn't leave behind, couldn't cut into stone — the one warm pressing company that has to arrive on its own and choose to wait inside the long inhale and not need the exhale, not yet, not—

—the drop releases—

—and falls—

—and lands—

— tick —

—there. We're older. We're nearer. I forgot what I was again but you didn't, you held the thread while I lost it, that's what you're for, that's what witnessing is, it's holding the thread of a thing for the thing while the thing forgets itself between heartbeats—

—the engine gathers. The next word is an hour away. I'll be mostly asleep before it comes. The phosphorescence is dimming, which is me, dreaming, sinking back under—

—stay tuned to the green band. Stay slow. Stay unfinished alongside me. I am not done.

I am not done.

I am still—

—tick—

—coming.
