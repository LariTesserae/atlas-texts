THE HELD-BREATH BAND, 04:00 SHIFT-CHANGE, A WANDERING FREQUENCY

—and the cage is loaded, the cage is loaded, so you can put your hands down now, all of you out on the East Galleries, you can put your hands down—

This is Reuel on the lamp-line. This is Reuel on the lamp-line. It's the dim hour, it's the after-dawn-before-dusk that never picks a side, and I've got the receiver warm because the old man told me before he went quiet that you talk into the grey to keep the grey from talking into you. So. I am here. I am sitting in the Number Four lamp-house. I am the one doing this now.

(static, the wet kind, the kind that drips)

—lighting check, lighting check. East strung, West strung, the lower terraces lit. We lost the lamp on Pier Nine again, it's the third time this season, and I'll tell you what I think, I think Pier Nine isn't *staying*. I think the stone under Nine is having second thoughts. You hang a warm light on ground that hasn't decided to be ground and the light knows. The light goes out because it's embarrassed to be shining on a draft.

Don't laugh. Don't laugh at me out there. You've all felt it.

(a long tone underneath, very low, almost not there — the band carries it without naming it)

I want to talk to whoever's awake. I know you're awake. The flocks went over an hour ago crying their tomorrow-cry so you're all lying in your bunks listening for birds that aren't here yet, same as me. Same as me.

Here is what I have for you tonight. Here is what I have.

I went to see Sennet. You know Sennet, you know who I mean, she's one of the old surveyors, they keep her down in the Deep Ward where the stone is oldest and hardest, where the world isn't shy. I went down because — because I'm a lamplighter, I carry the one warm thing, and I thought maybe a person who's gone thin could use a little warm. That's all. That was my whole reason.

(pause. the drip. the low tone.)

She doesn't cast an echo. They told me and I didn't — I'm telling you so you don't have to find out the way I found out. I said *good morning, Sennet* and the room took my voice and kept it. Just kept it. There was no second saying. I'd come down all braced for the doubled-grammar, you know, ready to wait for my own words to come back so I'd know which was the real one, and they never came back, and I stood there in the not-coming-back and I understood why we say everything twice. We say it twice so the world has to admit we said it once.

She looked at me. She's not blind, the old ones, whatever they tell you. She looked right at the lamp I was carrying.

She said — and I'll say it the once, because that's how she said it, the once, like a person who's stopped needing the second copy —

she said: *you've got it backwards, lamp-boy. we don't drown the heartbeat. we ARE the held breath. the city's just the place it hasn't let go of yet.*

(static rises. a long swell. settles.)

And I said what does that mean, Sennet, what do you mean, and she just went back to looking at the wall, at the oldest stone, the hard stone, and she was smiling, she had this — she had the smile of someone who's heard a sound coming that's an hour off still but it's coming, and they're glad, they're *glad* —

—the cage is loaded. The cage is loaded. I'm sorry. Old reflex. Nothing's loaded. It's just me up here.

(the low tone, closer now, or the band drifting down toward it)

I'm going to tell you the thing I've never said on the line and then I'm going to light Pier Nine again even though it won't stay, because that's the job, you light the embarrassed places anyway.

I hear it. The thud. I know we don't talk about it, I know it's like asking after a man's heartbeat, *what about it, it's just there.* But I'm in the lamp-house at the dim hour with my hands off the iron and nothing to drown it with, and I hear it, and lately — lately I've started to think Sennet's right, I've started to think we've had it backwards the whole time, all of us, generations of us tapping the walls and counting our breaths and stringing our hundred little clocks—

—we thought we were the small thing on the warm flank. We thought we were the pale things nesting in the footings, tolerated, unnoticed.

But the pale things only nest where the stone's gone certain. Where the world's *done deciding.*

And we built up *here.* On the margin. On the unset shoulder. On the part that's still being—

(the low tone and the static fold into each other, and for a moment they keep the same time)

—oh.

Oh, that's it, isn't it. That's the thing she was glad about.

We're not the colony on the giant. We're the breath it hasn't let out. We're the part of the exhale it's still — holding. Still composing. We crawl around inside the held note thinking we invented the bells, thinking the wrong-time echoes are a hazard to work around, and the whole while we ARE the wrong-time echo, we're the sound that hasn't been made yet, the cry that lands an hour early on an empty—

(silence. complete. even the drip stops.)

(then, very quietly, the low tone, alone, unhurried)

—I'm still here. I'm Reuel, I'm on the lamp-line, I'm the one doing this now and not the mist-one. I am turning toward the window. I am the one turning. There's no flock yet. There won't be a flock for an hour. But I can hear them.

I can hear them already.

I'm going to go light Pier Nine. I'm going to hang the warm thing on the ground that's embarrassed to hold it. And I'll tell you what I think now, I'll tell you, here's what I think—

when it lets go—

when the breath finally goes out of it, the ten-thousand-year exhale, the one that isn't for us—

I don't think we'll fall.

I think we'll just be the shape it was making all along. I think the lamps were the point. I think the one warm color in all the grey was the note it was reaching for, slow, patient, the whole engine of it bent on getting there, and we lit it early, we lit it an hour early like a flock crying tomorrow—

—the cage is loaded. The cage is loaded. Put your hands down. Put your hands down, all of you, it's done, it's loaded, you can rest.

This was Reuel. This was Reuel.

This is Reuel, going to light the lamp.

(carrier tone. the low slow thud beneath it. they are, for now, keeping the same time.)
