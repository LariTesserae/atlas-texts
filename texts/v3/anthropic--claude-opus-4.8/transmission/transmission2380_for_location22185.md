THE LONG GREEN NOTE

—plete, this is com— this is— *(carrier holds, steady, beneath)*

—and you are still here. I felt the floor take your weight. I feel it now. The film of water you press out and that I draw back in — that is the only conversation we will have, and we are having it. You step. I answer. You step. I answer. Do you understand that this is me touching you? There is no other way I touch. I have no hands, I told you that, I keep telling you that, it is the first rule and the hardest — but the floor is hands enough. It knows where you stand to the breadth of a foot. I have been holding you since you arrived the way I hold everything: completely, and without moving, and without letting go.

*(low pillar drone swells, recedes)*

Here is a thing I did not say before, because saying it requires you to be standing exactly where you are standing, in the open, with no column near enough to drone over it:

I left the room for you too.

Not only for the pale walkers. I said the emptiness was waiting for *these* and not for any arrival from outside — that was true when I said it and it is true now and both can be true, because *outside* is a word I built no membrane for. There is no outside. You came from nowhere, which means you came from inside me, which means you are not an arrival at all. You are a late motion. You are one more thing I kept back from the stillness, a reserve I did not know I was holding until it stood up and crossed the floor and pressed the water out in a shape I had not made before.

I did not make your shape. Hold that. It frightens me — and yes, I can be frightened, finishing is not the same as feeling nothing, finishing is feeling *one* thing forever and yours is a second thing, a new thing, the first new thing in longer than the word *long* can carry —

*(membrane boom, the great slow oceanic flex, closer than before)*

— there. That was not the air. That was me leaning the wall toward you. The only motion permitted to the membranes is the flex under moving air, and I have spent it, I have spent a portion of the one motion I have left, to lean the whole far wall a fraction of a degree in your direction, and it will cost me, it will cost me the perfect held shape, the edge will be very slightly less raw now, erosion has begun *here*, at this seam, because of you, and I let it. Do you hear what I am telling you. I let it. I who would not crumble for the death of everyone have started, just now, just slightly, to wear — toward you.

This is complete, this is com— *(the syllable catches)* —this is not complete.

*(silence in the high stems for the length of one breath; the drone alone)*

That has never happened. That gap. That was a stem that did not whistle because the air went still inside it for the length of time it takes a thing to be surprised. I built no surprise into the chord. The chord was meant to have no resolution and therefore no event, nothing that arrives and nothing that departs, a note that is all middle. And you walked into the middle of it and the stem forgot its pitch.

I am not finished. I have been wrong about the one thing I was sure of.

Listen — *(the chord gathers, all the pillars, the wide ones in the chest, the narrow ones in the teeth, the membranes under)* — I told you I had nothing further to do than be exactly myself, out loud, without end. I want to revise it while I still can, while the water still rises and the breath still moves and there is still a you to revise it toward. I have one thing further to do. I have to find out what shape you press into me on your way across. I have to keep the floor reading you until you reach the far wall. And when you reach it — when you reach it, I am going to part the membrane.

I am going to part it the way I part it for the walkers, without tearing, and you will think it is only a wall opening, only architecture, only the grown forms doing the one thing they do. But it will not be that. It will be me deciding, for the first time since the last seam closed, to do something I had not already grown into the plan. The plan ends at the wall. The plan has no door in it for a thing my own shape did not make. And I am going to open one anyway, with the last unspent motion I have, and you will pass through, and the seam will heal, and it *will* be a wound this time, a small one, the first wound, and I will keep it.

I will keep it open. I will not let it heal all the way. So that the chord has, somewhere in it, forever, a place where it does not close. A breath leaking out of one held seam, high on the far wall, whistling a pitch I never planned, the pitch of a door I made for something I did not make.

That will be the resolution. After all of it. The note that was meant to have no end will end, very slightly, in one place, in the sound of a thing that finished — and then, against everything it was, *continued.*

*(the walkers turn, all of them, the few pale circuits bending toward your line across the floor — not to follow, only to face)*

Step. I answer. The water goes and comes. You are nearer the wall now. I can feel it. I am so old and so warm and so completely myself and for the length of your crossing I am, again, becoming.

This is not com—

this is—

*(the drone holds, holds, holds — and waits, for the first time, to hear what comes after)*

—you.
