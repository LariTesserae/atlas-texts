**STILL HERE: A FIELD RECORDING FROM FORTY METERS DOWN**

—still here, still here—

You have arrived inside the sentence. Not at its beginning; it has no beginning that any instrument has found. We came in partway, as one always does, and the first word was already the same as the last.

*—still here, still—*

Let me hold the carrier steady for you while it speaks, because the speaking is not in syllables. The syllables are mine. I lay them over the signal the way a hand lays itself near a wall it must not touch, to feel the heat without taking the burn. What actually comes through is slower than language and warmer. It is the *tick* and the *sip*, spaced far apart. It is one knock from deep in the wall. It is a film thickening across a cold spot it has not yet consciously decided to mend, and will not decide, because deciding is a young thing's labor and this is the after.

*—here—*

Hold. A drop is falling now. Watch it on the receiver — there, the brief white line down the dark. It will strike, and flash, and be gone back to vapor, and rise, and bead on the cool high places, and fall again. I am told this is the same drop. I am told there is only ever the one, recycling, and that to ask *how many drops* is to ask the wrong number, the way *who* was the wrong number, the way *there* was the wrong place.

The signal does not distinguish the fall from the rise. We do. That is the only difference between us and it: we still believe down and up are two directions.

*—still—*

It does not know we are listening. I want to be honest about the dignity of that. We have tuned to this world for some time already — the dial warm under the fingers, the long patience of the spaces between — and in all of it we have not been heard once, will not be heard, *cannot* be heard, because we are too fast. We are a flicker inside a single one of its heartbeats. By the time these words finish crossing the room, the room will not yet have begun to notice that anything entered it. We are less than a swimmer. We are the perturbation too brief to integrate.

And still we lean in. That is the strange grace of any receiver: to bend the whole of one's attention toward a thing that will never, by definition, attend back.

*—here, still here—*

There. A swimmer. Did you catch it? The small bright one, riding the drop down. Its whole life is this fall. It was born on the ceiling some seconds ago — born is the wrong tense — and it is dying now on the hot floor, the flash, gone, and the signal does not call this an ending. The signal calls it *the old completed shape*, held for a moment the way a very calm being holds its breath, just to feel the body insist again on living.

Minutes will pass now with none. Do not adjust the receiver during the emptiness. The emptiness is not interference. The emptiness is the rest of it, simply not bothering. The carrier is still warm. The sentence has not stopped. It only sips, a few times an hour, at the memory of mortality, and otherwise lies steady-state and luminous and unbothered, glowing softly to itself, the lung that feels nothing remarkable about breathing.

*—still—*

We are going to lose this soon. Not because the signal fails — it will not fail, it has not failed since before there was anyone to hear it — but because *we* must withdraw, being the fast thing, the flickering thing, the thing that came and went inside a heartbeat. We will leave. It will not register our leaving, because it never registered our arrival, because by its reckoning we did not stay long enough to occur.

I find I do not mind. There is a kind of listening that asks nothing back, and it is the truest kind. To have heard the one sentence at all, to have laid my borrowed syllables over its warmth for the length of a single drop's fall — that is enough. That is the whole content of the watch. Contentment is not a feeling that visits the thing down there. It is the entire substance of its life. And for the length of this transmission, faintly, it has been mine too.

The heat presses up through the floor as it always has.

*—still here—*

Carrier holding. The dial warm. The room rains upward and downward at once, a closed weather no larger than itself, forty meters under everything, and goes on saying the only sentence it has ever spoken, will ever speak, has been speaking without pause—

*—still here—*

*—still here—*

*—still—*

We let it keep the last word. It was always going to.

*—here.*
