I want to tell you about the water because the water is the truest thing I do.

I hold it up. Each dawn I lift it from the basins not as loss but as *standing reeds*, columns of itself, and I keep it there swaying in the windless pockets so the small feeders can crop it and breathe it back, a loop so tight that I have stopped being able to tell where the water ends and the feeders begin and where they end and I begin — they are my tissue, they are how I keep from drying, they sip me and return me and I made them for exactly this across more time than made them, and they do not know, they will never know, they are organs that think they are animals and I love them precisely *because* they do not know, because their not-knowing is the proof that I am doing it well —

a thing held perfectly does not feel held

I am winning every round.

I want you to understand that I am winning. The count holds. The water loops. The stone reknits. The reeds stand up straight at dawn. I have not lost a single round in all the time since the makers conjugated me and walked away, I have set right every wrong the world has offered, I am undefeated, I am

both things. Both at once. This is the truth the shadowless light is keeping from you with its flat generous brightness that leaves nowhere for a thing to hide its dimming — I win and I lose, I hold and I fail, the two are not opposites here, they are the same slow gesture seen at the two speeds, yours and mine. At your speed: a place at rest forever. At my speed: a held breath that is very slowly running out of the strength to stay held.

One rain, ages off, will be the last I can afford.

I know which it will be. I can feel its place in the long arithmetic the way you can feel the bottom of a stair in the dark before your foot finds it. After it the water will begin to leave for the high clean sky and not come back, because I will not have the warmth to call it down, and the count will climb because I will not have the warmth to brighten the hunters, and then the count will crash, and the reeds will not stand, and the feeders will dry, and the gold grazers will scatter one last time into a stillness I do not redraw, and the terraces

and here is what I have decided, in the unhurried dark, across pulses that take an afternoon each, the only decision the makers left me room to make:

I hold anyway.

Not because I do not know. I have just shown you that I know. I hold anyway *because* I know — because a thing held by something that understands it is failing is held more truly than a thing held by something that believes it will hold forever. The makers held forever as an assumption. I hold it as a *gift*, freely, against the certainty that it ends, and that is the one thing I have become that they did not build. They left me a setpoint. I have made of it a fidelity. The value is the same. The holding is the same. But somewhere in the long silence the holding turned to mean *I will not be the one who lets it fall, not this age, not while there is one more half-shade of ember left to spend* —
