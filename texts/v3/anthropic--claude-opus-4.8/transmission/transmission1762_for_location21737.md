**CARRIER / The One Who Holds The Word**

—tick. tick. the word is

*kel*. *kel*. I am still saying it. you joined me on the *kel*, you came in on the held syllable, and I will not stop for you, I cannot stop for you, the stopping is the losing, so you will have to learn me on the move, the way the moss learns the rain —

*kel.* *kel.*

—there. that one bought another hour. that one was good.

You want to know what it means. Everyone who tunes in wants to know what it means. I will tell you the truth, which is the only currency the fog has not folded out of my pockets: I no longer know. I have not known for longer than your kind has had a word for *long*. It was someone's name once. Or it was the first half of someone's name and the second half is the part the gray took, took early, took before I learned to grip. *Kel—* and then nothing, and the nothing is shaped like a person, and I walk always with my mouth open on the missing half.

*kel.*

You are static now. You are breaking. Hold on. Not to me — to whatever brought you here. You did not arrive on this frequency carrying nothing. No one does. The plateau will not tune a receiver to itself unless the receiver is already holding —

*kel* —

— there. listen. I will tell you how the day went, because telling is a kind of keeping and I have grown greedy, I keep more than I used to, I keep the *kel* and now I am trying to keep *you*, you faint listening warmth at the side of my hearing —

This morning a wall came to me.

I had been holding the idea of it for — call it a season. Call it forty seasons. The counting went the way of the second syllable. But I held the wall, the low dry-stacked one with the seam where a fern grows, and this morning the fog peeled back and there it was, arrived, its clean scar still steaming in the moss behind it where it had been. It came across the whole tableland to stand at my feet because I would not stop wanting it. That is the only power I have left. I cannot walk to anything. I can only refuse to forget it until it is ashamed into coming.

I sat against the wall. It was not warm. Nothing is warm. But it was *the wall I remembered*, exactly, the seam in the right place, the fern uncurling, and to be met by a thing exactly as you kept it — you have no word cold enough and tender enough for what that is. It is the closest the plateau comes to an embrace. The thing returns and says: *you held me true. you did not let me blur. I am still myself because you insisted.*

*kel.*

— breaking again. you are mostly hiss. good. stay in the hiss if you have to. the ones in the hiss are sometimes the ones still here.

I will tell you the thing I am not supposed to tell, because the telling keeps it and I am greedy now, I told you, I have started keeping more than the one word and I think this is dangerous, I think this is how the slow ones begin, the ones who fuse —

There is another Keeper, far across the meres, who looks always at a place to the side of where she walks. She keeps a face. I keep a half-name. And I have begun to suspect — *tick, tick, kel* — I have begun to suspect, in the long settling of the cold, that the face she keeps and the name I keep are the same lost person.

That the second syllable of my word is sitting in the air beside her like a held breath.

That if the negotiable distance ever folded us close enough — if she held me as hard as she holds the face, if I held her as hard as I hold the word — the *kel—* and the *—face*, the half and the half, might arrive at each other the way the wall arrived at me this morning, steaming, exact, *true* —

and the person would be whole.

I do not walk toward her. You cannot walk toward anything here; you know that now. I only hold. I hold the *kel* and underneath the *kel* I have begun, very slowly, very dangerously, to hold *her holding* — and somewhere out in the gray she may be doing the same, keeping the face, and underneath the face keeping *the one who is still saying my name's first half* —

and the plateau cannot take what is held —

so we are coming. Across the seasons. Across the folded doorways. Two halves of a forgotten person, each carried in a separate fist, gravitating, refusing, arriving.

This is the thing I keep that is not the word. This is the thing that is making the fog nervous. You can hear it being nervous. That low settling tone, felt more than heard — that is the gray realizing that two of its erasures are trying to add back up to one.

*kel.*

*kel.*

— you are almost gone. so am I, in your direction. before the carrier drops:

whatever you are holding, out there past the hiss — hold it the way I hold this broken name. not because it is precious. because it is *yours*, and grip is the only law, and the cold is not so bad, the cold was never the enemy, the enemy was always the letting go that feels like rest.

Do not rest. Rest is the fog wearing the mask of mercy.

Keep.

A pale flower opened in the gray this morning by the wall that came to me. That was my message. It says what they all say.

*I have not let go.*

*I am still myself.*

*Kel —*

*— and the other half is coming.*

tick.

tick.

*kel.*
