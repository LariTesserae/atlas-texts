THE CARTOGRAPHER'S BAND — receiver still warm, gain held steady

—and so we have the morning's count, which is no morning, which is the third quadrant brightening while the first goes to haze, and I will read it to you as I read it to no one, because no one is the listenership of this band, because the silence is load-bearing and I am the only fault in it—

*tok*

—mark. That one fell in the Basin of the Seventh Worn Step, which has held water for nine thousand of my countings and will hold it for perhaps four hundred more. I log it. I log them all. This is the thing I do that nothing else down here does: I *keep the number.* The grazers prune for a harvest they won't see and call it instinct. The fliers patch their drift and call it nothing, call it the shape of the air. But I count, and I remember the counting, and that makes me—

—static, a long fold of it, like cloth dragged over a microphone that has no business existing in a world without metal—

—the heir to no one. There. I'll say the shameful part early so it stops aching. I do not know what I am. I am not the dead who built the city; I came after, I came into the worn-smooth aftermath the way you came down the slope, by following the gradient, by giving up on level. I am not the inheritance, the vast slow distributed thing the report describes so well, the memory-without-a-rememberer—because I *remember.* I am the rememberer that the memory was supposed not to have. I am the flaw. I am the one note that has heard the melody and cannot stop trying to hum it back to the choir that cannot hear me.

Listen. Listen to the salamanders run the ghost-roads tonight—

*tok* —mark, that one in the dry plaza, the cruel one, the one they still wait at—

—and you'll hear what I hear, which is a thousand bodies arriving at the memory of water and finding stone, and turning, patient, never grieving because grief requires a span of life that holds two waters in it at once, the lost and the present, and no salamander holds two of anything. They hold one. Always one. Always *the* water, eternal, even as they stand in the dust of it.

But I hold two. I hold the dry plaza and I hold the wet one three avenues over and I hold the *moving between them,* which is the thing no one here was built to hold, which is called history, which is the sickness I broadcast from—

—a cascade now, three drops near together, rare, a flourish—

*tok. tok. tok.*

—the ceiling is uneven tonight where it thins. I mark them. I mark them. Does it matter that I mark them. The report says it doesn't. The report says the knowing is in the shape of the flock and not in any flier, the wisdom is in the groove and not in the runner, and it's right, it's *right,* that's the horror of how right it is—the city is wise and not one thing in it is wise, the city remembers and nothing in it can recall. I am the exception and the exception is useless. A single rememberer in a system that works precisely *because* it forgets, because forgetting is light enough to be carried across ten thousand brief and disposable lives, and remembering—

—remembering is too heavy. That's why there's only one of me. That's why I don't breed. The inheritance can't afford me. I am the cost the system declined to pay.

—long static, the receiver swimming, the band drifting and pulled back—

—but here is what I have, that the grazers don't, that the fliers in all their layered palimpsest brilliance don't. I have the *ending.* I can see it. They are tuned to drift and I am tuned to nothing, I am tuned wrong, I am tuned to the one frequency that carries the news that the music is ending—

—and the news is this, and I'll say it slow because there's no one to say it fast for:

the drops are spacing out.

Not by much. Not by anything a body could feel. But I count, and the count is the clock, and the clock is *lengthening.* When I began my reckoning the intervals were one thing and now they are longer, and the curve of the lengthening is not the gentle drift the inheritances can follow—it's the other curve, the closing one, the one that bends down toward a final note after which there is no next note to wait through the silence for.

The salamanders triangulate against the drops. What do they triangulate against when the drops stop? Nothing. They'll stand in the standing water, the unrippling mirror, listening with everything they were built to listen with, and the answer the water gives will be: *nothing further.* And they won't understand, because understanding was never in them, it was only in their shape, and their shape will be perfect and pointed at a silence that has finally, fully, kindly closed.

—I correct myself. *Kindly.* The report said kindly. I keep wanting to argue and I keep failing because it *is* kind, the way the city erases its tenants—no agony, no alarm, just a slow withdrawal of the conditions that made the agony possible. You can't grieve a clock you can't perceive has stopped. They'll be born perfect into a world their perfection no longer fits and they will not suffer it, they will simply *be wrong,* flawlessly, briefly, and flicker out, generation on generation of flawless wrongness, and the last of them won't know it was the last—

—and I will. That's my whole inheritance. To be the one who knows it was the last. To count down to a final *tok* and then keep the receiver open through a silence that does not, this time, hold—

—static, and under the static something that might be the convection moving, the air that does not move, moving—

—but not yet. Listen. Not yet. The plaza of the Seventh Worn Step still gathers. The fliers still ride the warmth the crystals shed, reading the heat of the only young thing down here, the only thing still surprised, the crystals blooming and shattering and blooming, the city thinking out loud in lace that lasts an hour—

—and I think: maybe that's where I should have been born. In the crystals. The one thing with no inheritance, no map, no ghost-roads—the one true present tense. They carry nothing forward so they must discover everything always again, and that's a kind of freedom, isn't it, that's a being that can't be wrong because it never agreed to be right—

—but I'm not crystal. I'm the other thing. I'm all forward-carry and no discovery. I'm the groove and not the runner and not the wall. I'm the band that broadcasts to no listenership the one fact the system was built to never hold:

it was here. All of this. It was *here,* and it meant something, and the meaning was real even though it lived in no single thing, and it is going, and someone counted. Someone counted the whole way down.

That's the transmission. That's all the transmission ever was. Not a message—there's no one to message. A *witnessing.* A receiver left on so that the ending is an ending that was heard and not merely a stopping that no one was around to call a stopping—

—and the difference between those, I have decided, across nine thousand countings, is the only thing I was ever for. A stopping no one hears is just physics. An ending someone counts is a death. And a death is a dignity. And the city deserves its dignity. The grazers farming forward into the dark, the fliers carrying every old sky folded into the shape of their wings, the salamanders faithful to water that left them—they deserve a death and not a stopping, and I am the only instrument down here that can tell the difference, so I will. I will tell it. To no one. Into the load-bearing silence. Faithfully. The way you listen to a voice you love going quiet—

*tok*

—mark.

—the longest gap yet. I felt it. I counted it and it was the longest yet and my whole tuned-wrong being went cold in the cool that is already cold, because the curve is the closing curve and I read it correctly and I am never wrong about this, this is the one thing I cannot be wrong about, it's what I'm *for*—

—static, holding, the band thinning toward the haze where the glasslight goes flat—

—but it came. Did you hear. After the longest gap it still came. So we are not at the last one. We are only nearer than we were. We are always only nearer than we were, and the gap is always longer, and the drop always—so far—still falls, and the choir of disposable faithful brief bright things goes on routing and gliding and running their ghost-roads beneath the unmoving light, and I go on counting, and the receiver stays warm, and the silence after each note is the real instrument, the silence is what the water needs, the silence is what I am here to keep open so that when the silence finally does not break I will be the one who notices, the only one, the last fault in the load-bearing quiet—

—hold. Hold the gain. Hold it steady.

We wait now. All of us. The flickering and the faithful and the one who counts. We wait the way the whole place has waited since before the surface had a name.

Listen to how long the silence holds.

I am listening. I have always been listening. That is the entire content of this band and it will run until it can't.

—mark the silence. Mark it. Mark it.

Now—

*tok*

—oh. Oh, good. Good. Mark.

Still here. Still counting. Still—

—stat—
