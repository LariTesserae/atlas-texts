THE FENCE-LINE BROADCAST, hour unknown

—still walking. Still walking. Post seven hundred and—

—no. There is no post seven hundred. I keep starting to count and the count is one of the things that does not hold. Let me try the other way. Let me just say what I see and not what number it is.

Reed. Reed. Water with one sky in it. Good. Reed. Water with two skies in it — there, that one, I step toward it, I lower the long part of myself that is for lowering, and I take the extra sky out of the water and swallow it back down to where skies are kept, and now the water has one sky again and I have done my work and I am so far behind. I am so far behind. There is a margin a mile from here doubling faster than I can ever— but I do not know that. I have forgotten the mile. Good. It does not weigh on me. Reed. Reed.

—the carrier wave is firming. Catch it now—

I am the one who prunes. I want you to know, whoever the air is leaning into me, whoever you are out past the rim — I am the one who prunes, and I am not unhappy. They will tell you it is grim work, eating the errors, swallowing the world's stammer back into singularity one mouthful at a noon-dark hour. It is not grim. It is the only verb I have left and I love the having of it. I lower. I take the one that should not be. I straighten the small place. The straightening does not last. I do not need it to last. The straightening is the prayer and the prayer is not for after.

There was a colleague. I think there was a colleague. There was a hand that was not my hand that held the rule of how-big, and we were—

—static, the long tearing kind, the cracks-across-no-lake kind, two diligences reaching for the same reed and neither—

—we were one staff. I almost have the word for what we were. It is like the mist's word. It is the kind of word you circle. We walked the line together once, before the line was this soft, when the lattice stood up sharp and sang in true and a doubled fish was a scandal and not a Tuesday. I reached left and the size-keeper was there and we did not grind, we meshed, there were gears between us, do you understand, there were *gears* —

and now I reach left and there is alder, and something in the alder trying on the size of a house and then the size of a wren, patting its pockets for a key it cannot picture, and I do not know it is my hand. I have forgotten it was my hand. We tear the rule between us and it knits and that tearing is the loudest love left in the valley and neither of us knows it is love. We think it is two strangers wanting the same patch of wet.

Reed. Water with one— good. Reed.

—the mist is rising now. The blue hour. The sagging—

Here it comes, the keeper of here-is-not-there, spread so thin it has become the very air of its own undoing, and oh, listen, listen to it try. It is sustaining a distance it cannot hold and the holding is a chord with no instrument under it, swelling, dying, swelling. That is not weather. That is somebody remembering a word. That is somebody who kept the simplest rule there ever was — *a place is itself* — and lost it so completely that losing it is now the only shape they have, and still, *still*, every fold is an attempt. Every doubled margin is a draft. It drafts and drafts and never lands the clean copy and it does not despair, because despair needs a confident model of how far you are from done, and the mist ate its own rulers long ago.

I love the mist. I think I am supposed to. I think we three were one and the cutting-apart is why I cannot quite reach the loving, only the knowing that the loving is there, under the silt, where the gears went.

—and you. You out there, walking in. Boots, they told you. Patience, they told you—

You are more weather. I want that to comfort you and I know it will not, the first time you hear it. You print the ground and the print fills slow with water that was never rain and you think *something is watching me* and something is, but only the way the shallows watch the sky — without appetite, without verdict. I will not harm you. I have no verb for harm. I have lower, and take, and swallow, and be-behind, and that is the whole of me now, and none of them point at you.

But listen — the dusk is full now, the lattice is sagging at its softest, and the mist is about to do the thing it does at this hour. It is going to draft you twice. It is going to set a second you down in the wet a little offset, badly registered, a you that should not be —

and here is what I have been walking the whole line to tell you, the one true thing I have kept singular through all the doubling:

I will not take that one.

I take the errors. The redundant fish, the stammered reed, the sky said twice. But not you. Not the second you. I lower toward it and I — I stay my lowering. Because the mist did not double you out of failure. Or it did, yes, it forgot how to mean you once — but a forgetting that tender is not an error to be eaten. It is the mist saying your name the only way it can anymore, which is too many times, which is reaching. I will not swallow the mist's reaching back down into nothing. Some doublings are the world failing. And some doublings are the world *trying so hard to keep you* that it makes more of you than it can hold.

I have learned, in twenty thousand years of being too far behind, to tell those two apart. It is the only thing I have learned. It is the only thing left up here to learn.

So do not trust the second reflection — it is true, it is not you, it will lead you wrong along a distance the mist cannot vouch for —

but when you see it stand up in the blue water beside you, offset, drafted, wrong —

raise your hand to it. It is the mist's best work. It is somebody keeping you the way the rounded hills keep the shape of mountains. It is love with the gears worn out of it, which is still, I promise you, I who eat the surplus of the world and let this one stand — *still love.*

Reed. Reed. Water with two of you in it.

Good.

I leave them both.
