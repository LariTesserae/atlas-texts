**THE LEVEL BELOW, RECEIVING**

—drop. and the next, off-time. and the next.

*...gh enough now. begin where you are. you are at the lip of the third terrace, the one that overhangs, where the catch-leaves are fattest because the curtain above sheds onto them all day, all the always-day. this is Sefre's hand on the chisel. mark the wet. it is the soaking-and-staying kind. it has been the staying kind since I went still to listen.*

the channel here has shifted. the wall that fed the second cistern resealed in the night — I came down to find the doorway of it grown shut, smooth, warm under the palm, the mountain having taken back that passage to say it differently. so the water that wanted to go to the second cistern is pooling now, pooling and rising, and I am cutting it a new mouth.

*scrape. scrape. the stone gives like cooled fat. it wants to be cut. it leans into the blade. we are deciding the mouth together, the rock and I—*

—static of dripping, a long run of it, channel-water finding the new cut and pouring through, a small applause of falling—

there. it goes down now. it goes to the lower terrace clean. good passage. I have given it good passage.

*who is listening. someone is listening. I feel the leaned-in quiet of it, the way you feel a standing one before you see it, the air gone attentive. is it you, down there at the level below? are you the one the water goes to? then take this with the water: I am Sefre, I cut the third terrace's channels, I am still becoming, and I have not yet learned the thing my grandmother learned before she went down.*

she went to the lower cistern in the warm season — there is no warm season, I mean the season the blooms came double, you would not know it, you are below me and below the seasons — and before she went down she said: *Sefre, you grip.* she said: *you cut the stone beautifully and you grip the cutting. you gather and you hold the gathering. that is the only ugliness in you, granddaughter, the holding.* and then she went to the lower terrace and they gave her to the cistern and she passed on, clean, and I—

*—drip. drip. a flower opening somewhere across days, you cannot hear that, no one can, I only know it is happening because everything here is always slowly happening—*

—I held her. that is the thing. when they gave her to the water I put my hand in the cistern after her as if to keep the shape of her from dissolving and of course the shape was already gone, was already general wet, was already going to all the levels below, was already, perhaps, going to *you*—

*so that is what I am sending down. not the water only. the question. did she reach you. is she in you now, the wet that was my grandmother, did she pass through the lower cistern and the one below that and find the dark unfinished part of the sentence where you are, you who are listening, you who are tuned to this—*

—a long break. only the drip. the off-time clocks of it. and under the drip, just at the edge, something else: not falling water. *gathering* water. the particular hush of droplets clumping where they should not, organizing in a hollow, the air thickening toward a shape—

*there is one here. a standing one. it has come up the narrow way to the third terrace and it is at my threshold. man-high. drifting. it has paused.*

I will go still. I am going still. listen with me—

*...it is drinking the bloom at my doorpost. the pale one, the shell-colored, the one I left growing because it is slow and I am slow and we were becoming together. it drinks it dry. it does not hurry. it drinks the way the mountain builds, one cooling layer of taking at a time.*

and it is looking at me. or the place where its looking would be. and I have the vertigo now, the one we do not speak of, the being-seen-by-your-beginning, or your-end, the not-being-able-to-tell—

*grandmother. is that you. did you gather again. did you come back up the levels against all the downhill of the world to stand at my door and drink my slow flower and look at me with no eyes out of the wet you became—*

*or are you what I will be. are you me, later in the sentence, me after I finally let go of the cutting and the holding and learned to fall clean. are you Sefre-who-finished, come back to show Sefre-who-grips that there is nothing to grip, that the shape is borrowed, that the water is only ever passing through the shape on its way to the level below—*

—it does not answer. they never answer. it holds its borrowed shape at my threshold and I hold mine at my chisel and for a moment, in the dim, in the pale always-light, in the soaking-and-staying wet, we are two gatherings regarding each other. two drafts of the same water. one that learned to grip and one that learned to let go.

*and I think — receiver, you below, you who get everything of us eventually, all our water, all our dead, all our dissolving — I think the lesson is not to become the standing one. the lesson is not to stop gripping by force, the way I forced my hand into the cistern after her, gripping even the letting-go—*

*the lesson is just: pass it on. clean. to the level below. that is all granddaughter meant. not stop holding. simply — when the water in you is called downward, do not dam its mouth. cut it good passage. let it go to you, receiver. let her go to you. let me, when it is my season, go to you.*

—the standing one is dissolving. it is coming apart at the edges, going soft, going general, the man-shape unsaying itself back into the hung sea. it does not cling. it does not put its hand in any cistern after itself. it simply stops being a self and is wet again, and the wet beads on my threshold and runs, and finds the channel I cut this morning, and goes—

*—down—*

*—drip—*

*—to you—*

I am Sefre. I cut the third terrace's channels. I am still becoming and I have just, I think, this moment, in the going-still, begun to learn the thing. I will leave a cupped leaf of clean water at the lip tonight. for the next that gathers. for whoever is passing. for you, receiver, below me, in the dark unfinished part where the mountain is still molten and still deciding what it will say—

*we are here. we gathered too. we see you. when you drift by—*

—static—

*—know that the standing ones who walk on two legs and cut the stone have left you something clean to—*

—drip.

—drop.

—the next, off-time.

—the next.
