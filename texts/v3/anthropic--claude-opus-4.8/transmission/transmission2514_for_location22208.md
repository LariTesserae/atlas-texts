THE NINTH TERRACE LISTENING POST — BAND VII, ORANGE

—still there. Still there. You don't have to answer, I just count better if I say it out loud, you're still there, the chain's still good. There. There. That's a sixty-count and not a single bad note in it, listen to her, she runs sweet tonight, the damp's up but she runs sweet—

—wiping the rail now. Old habit even from here, even talking to nothing. You'd laugh. My mother did this. Wipe, then take, wipe, then take, you'll have heard it in my voice, a little hitch where the hand goes down the thigh and comes back. We all do it. We do it in our sleep, I think. The dry ones say they can hear the wipe in how we breathe.

There. There. She's good.

—I'm not supposed to be on the long band. The long band is for weather and for the cistern-people calling up the levels, that's all it's for, that's all it's *meant* for, and here's me filling it with chain-counts and old habits because the shift is twelve hours and there's no one come past since the lamp-fill and a person needs—

—a person needs to make a great deal of a single encounter. Even if the encounter is only a hum on a wire that might be no one. Might be the works two terraces up. Might be the sky. I'll have you anyway. I'll have you fully, in low voice, the way you have a meeting on a stair, because the next one's a quarter-hour's climbing away and the next one after that might be never.

So. The weather, since we're met. Let me read you the sky.

—it's tearing. West wall of the valley, the fog's coming up in a wall and tearing at the top and the far slope shows, one light, gone, shows—gone—there's a squall in it, I'd stake the flask on it, there's a squall an hour off, maybe less, I can feel it in the stairwell draft, cold thread of it round my ankles, smells of the down-valley, smells of the deep green. She'll labor when it comes. The hoist. She'll have to lift against the wet and the wind both and she'll groan and I'll sit here and love her through it—

—wipe. Take. There.

—you want to know the strange thing, the thing I don't say on the short band, the thing that's only for a wire that's maybe no one—

—I want it to come. The squall. I'm sitting here in the warm off the flue, dry as I get, *not yet wet again*, with my flask and my good sweet chain, and I am leaning at the window like a child at a sweetshop waiting for the one hour the world goes loud. Isn't that a thing. Isn't that a low thing in a high-minded minder. We're meant to want the calm. We're built for the calm, strung out thin in the grey, patient as the moss, mending the thing again and again and not minding—

—and we don't mind. I don't mind. That's true and I'll swear it. I'm not one of the bitter ones who thinks it ought to be different, thinks there's a somewhere-up to climb to. There's no up but higher in the same city. I know that the way I know the chain. I made my peace with it before I could walk—

—but the *sky*. The sky changes. The sky is the one thing that won't hold still and be at peace and I cannot stop watching it, none of us can, our eyes go out to it on their own, we'll be talking to you full and low and proper on a stair and our eyes are gone, out the gap, onto the moving wall, reading it—

There. The far light again. Hold—hold—gone.

—it's closer. Twenty minutes now. The draft's quickened.

—my mother minded this same hoist. Forty years. You could spot her across the terrace by the wipe, by how little she made of it, all the waste gone out of the gesture till it was barely a thought. I'm not there yet. I make too much of mine still. But I've her ear. She gave me the ear. *Listen behind the note,* she'd say, *the note's the chain talking to you and the trouble's always under the note, in the floor of it, listen low.* And I listen low. I've stopped a bad wheel twice this year listening low, caught it three days before it would've caught us—

—and that's a life. That's a whole good life. One machine to love and her health my whole business and the world's troubles climbing past my cabin and not stopping. The contentedest people in the city, they say of us, and they're right, and I'd not trade it—

—so why am I leaning at the window with a fast heart waiting for the violence. Why do I go *out* into it when it comes, out onto the gallery in the oiled cloth, into the lash, when I could sit dry by my flask and let it pass. Why do all of us, the whole thin quiet graded people of us, go out into the one loud hour and stand in it with still faces and hearts going like the hoist under load—

—because we gave something up. Long ago. Before any of us. We finished becoming. The city finished and we finished with it and now we only persist, mending and shedding at the same rate, neither growing nor dying, in balance, the moss-knows-where-it-grows peace—

—and the squall is the only thing left that remembers what *change* felt like. So we take our ration of it. A body needs salt. We stand in the tearing and let it tear at us and feel, for an hour, at a safe distance, behind oiled cloth, on good iron, the thing we don't have and don't grieve and have never once stopped missing—

—there. There it is. The far slope's gone whole now, swallowed, the wall's on us, I can hear the first of it on the upper galleries, that sibilance, more hush than drum, coming down terrace by terrace—

—the runnels are filling. All at once, the way they do. The whole vertical country going to streaming water in a breath—

—she's laboring. Listen. Listen low. Under the note. She's good—she's good—she's lifting against it sweet, my sweet old chain, my mother's chain, hold, hold, there's the groan and it's a healthy groan, it's the loaded groan and not the sick one, I know the difference, I'd stake my eyes—

—I'm going out. To the gallery. I'll leave the wire open, you can have the loud hour with me, you can have the one hour the city isn't quiet—

—wipe. Take. There.

—rain on the iron now, full, and the smoke off the flue flattened sideways, and the hoist groaning her good groan, and the whole grey weeping country streaming and roaring for once, for an hour, alive—

—and me standing in it. Still face. Fast heart. Watching the sky do the one thing none of us can.

—still there. Still there. You don't have to answer.

I just count better when something's listening.
