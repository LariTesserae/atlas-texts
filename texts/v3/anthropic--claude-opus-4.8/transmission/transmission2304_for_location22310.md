**KEEP·THE·WARM·GOING — band held open, dawn-side**

— wring and the threads give, count it, that is forty nets and forty wrung and the bowls stand full along the east ledge, and so it goes, and so it returns—

—who is on the band. someone is on the band. the hand that left this open left it warm. I take the warm. I am the one who sits longest with the walls now, the old one before me went to the north moss four less-warms ago and the green has him gently and the vine above where we wound him fruited heavy this turning, I felt the clusters, they pulled the stem down to my reach, that is him, that is the loop closing as it should—

—static, the dust-hiss, the always-hiss, do not mind it, it is only the mountain leaving—

—I keep the band because someone should keep the band. not for any now that listens. for the shape of keeping. the way the lamp is filled before the fat runs fully out. you do not fill it for the moment the light would have failed. you fill it because filling is what the hand is for—

—the birds are loud this dawn. they are always loud. I want to say to whoever takes the warm after me: do not be afraid of the roar. the roar is the proof. I have heard the elders' elders say there was once a fear-tale of a morning the birds did not croak, and how that silence was the worst shape, and they were right to make it the worst shape. a silence here is a loop that has broken. let it never break on your watch—

—I shoulder a stone today. the third corbel on the low west wall, it has begun its lean, I have watched it lean for— I do not count in turnings that large. my grandmother's hand was on this stone. she told me it would need shouldering before I grew worn, and I am worn now, so it is time, and so it is the same stone and the same lean and the same hand, only the hand is mine, only the hand is always becoming someone else's—

—hiss—

—I will tell you the thing I carry that the others do not say. I carry the wearing. I am one of the few this turning. I feel the cliffs going smooth under my palm and I know it is the one arrow in all our circles, the single line that does not come back around. I sat with it a long while when I was young and it made me quiet and the quiet never fully left. but listen, you who take the warm—

—the arrow is so long it is almost a circle too. it is so slow it is almost staying. and in the almost, in the not-yet, in the so-far-past-counting, there is room. there is all the room there has ever been. there is room for forty nets wrung and forty filled, room for the purple to pull the stem to my hand, room for the soft ones in the pens to low their long dusk-chord, room for a child to squat by a vine-tip the whole dim day and be content. the arrow takes everything, yes. but not now. not this now. this now is warm—

—and that is the courage, I think, if it is courage, I do not know the lower word for it. to know the stone is going and to shoulder it anyway. to fill a lamp on a mountain that is dissolving. to keep the warm going a little longer not because it will last but because *a little longer* is the whole of what is given, and a little longer, tended, attended, hand to hand, is enough—

—it has been enough for a very long time—

—the dew is gone off the nets, the dust-veil thins, the smear is up. I will leave the band open. someone may take the warm. someone always has. the knot ties the same. the gesture is in the next hand before the first hand knows it is letting go—

—I shoulder the stone now. I press my palm to it. my grandmother's lean. my lean. the next one's lean—

—and so it goes—
—and so it returns—
—and so it—

—hiss—

—fill the lamp—
