held. We are held. Tell them above: held even. Tell them the red lichen wants picking before the hour, the hour is—

*(a sound like a foot lifted from standing water, drawn out, enormous)*

...no downhill. There is no downhill, that's the first thing, that's the thing to *learn*, you keep waiting for the slope and there is no— it all leans in. It all leans toward the middle. We lean too. We have been leaning so long we—

*(whisk. tamp. whisk. tamp. close and small and very near the receiver)*

...re-bedding. Don't mind me. Don't mind the work-sound, the work is the— the work is the whole of it, there's nothing under the work but more work and that's not a *complaint*, you understand, that's not— a held note doesn't complain that it hasn't ended yet—

*(clearer now, a voice that has dried slightly)*

I'll tell you what's down there. You want to know what's down there. Everyone tuning in from the dry places wants to know what's down the stairs.

Doorways. Just doorways. Hearths gone under with the ash still in them. A gutter still doing its small job in the cold clear black, leaning inward toward the middle the way the live ones do, because the dead floors keep the habit. The fish circle and won't enter. My hands are pale to the elbow. I count the green on the courses, I read how fast we're losing the bottom, I bring the number up into the grey and I tie it to the reed-cords with a look and we set the new course exactly that much higher. No more. There's no labor to waste. No less, or it comes up through your floor in the—

*(the signal dips, water-logged, a long blue swallow of nothing)*

—back. I'm back. You lose me when I describe the well. I think it doesn't like to be— I think the deep blue in it doesn't carry on your air. It's not for carrying.

*(softer)*

We don't look at it. You'll have noticed. It's not a rule. Nobody made it a rule. It's the older thing, the thing before rules — you don't stare into the place you've given the water leave to be wholly itself. We fight it everywhere else. Course by course, reed by reed, palm gone soft over the hard, the body's own waterline climbing year by year toward the wrist, toward the elbow, toward— we fight it everywhere. But the middle is the truce. *Be wholly water there. Be only town-water everywhere else.* And it keeps the bargain. It always keeps the—

*(a child, distant, too loud — a splash — a cry — then the soft tamp of a hand, and the child folding down into the hush, learning again)*

...you hear that? That's the only loud thing we make. That's a small one not yet understanding the place. They learn. The fog teaches them. The fog eats the loud out of you until you're quiet enough to hear the water finding its way down through the stacked stone, dozens of separate droppings, no rhythm, no— a percussion that never resolves because it isn't going anywhere, it's only keeping even, like us, like—

*(the carrier thins. something underneath it. a deeper register, too steady to be a voice)*

...I should tell you the true thing before I lose the air. The dry places always want the secret and the secret is so plain they'll be angry to have it:

We are not winning.

We were never going to win. Our grandmothers weren't winning. We rise exactly as fast as we sink and that is not the *means* to our life — strangers always think it's the means, the labor toward some dry tomorrow — no. It is the life. Whole. Finished. A breath held is not an unfinished breath. Keeping even is not waiting to win. We do not dream of the fog burning off. That isn't a dream here. That's a *misunderstanding*. The wet is the condition of being and you do not solve your own condition, you set the reeds, you keep even, you teach the quiet child who stood too long at the forbidden stair, you tie the near-black cord on her wrist yourself and say almost nothing because there is almost nothing that can be—

*(and here the signal goes very clear, very close, the palest voice, as if leaning right against the receiving glass)*

It holds more sky than it should. The well. I've been down to where it reflects from. I'm the one who can say it: the too-deep blue is reflecting *something*, there is a from, the water does leave, it leaves down and down past the last drowned floor into the— and I have looked and I will not tell you on the open air what looks back, only that it is patient the way we are patient, only that it is also keeping even, only that the bargain at the center may not be ours alone to have made—

*(she stops. a long stillness. the tick returns. tick. tick. tick.)*

...mark the course. Eighteen and even. The lichen before the hour. We are held.

Tell them above. We are held.
