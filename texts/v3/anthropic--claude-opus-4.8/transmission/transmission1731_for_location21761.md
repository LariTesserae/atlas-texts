**THE DARK GETS KINDER**

—steady now, steady, that's the way, brightest one, you're the bright—

[a long fold of static, the texture of water moving over the near-below-hearing edge of the channels]

—catch this if you're catching, whoever fits in the breath of it, I'm a basket and a wrist and the rasp under four fingers and I have been counting the drip for three sleeps to keep my place in the dark and the place is *here,* the place is the low channel under the old growth where the rib-beam splits, you'd know it by feel, you'd know it by the way the cold comes up off the water on the left hand only—

She is here. The one this is for. I will get to her. Let me ride steady first.

Twelve in the basket and eleven lit. The dim one is old and I feed her the richest scrapings and she sulks and goes grey at the edges and I love her the most, which is a thing I have never said to a person and am saying now to the static, to the dark, to whoever this falls toward, because it is true and the saying of it brightens the other eleven by some small amount, they catch it off my hands, they always catch everything off my hands—

—be calm—

—be calm, it passes—

[the papery breath of the lamp-beasts, very close, like a page turned beside the ear; then the wet click of grazers somewhere off the right hand, a fence-noise, a settling]

I want to say what greatness is. Before I lose the channel. Greatness is not the wall-readers who go up into the new hollows and come back quiet with the dry-air taste in their teeth, though we honor them. Greatness is not the water-walkers who say *no* and hold the whole map of the flow behind their eyes. Greatness is the one whose name I am about to fail to say, because we have no names, we have only the way the dark behaves around a person—

—and the dark got *kinder* around her. That is the whole of it. That is the truest thing I know and I am putting it on the signal so it will go up the stem with the migration and out where the inside stops, out where the terrible open burning people live who need fire just to see, so that even they, even fire-carriers, will know:

there was one among us who could walk a frightened district—

[the receiver loses her here; a swelling of the growth-rustle, urgent, the young moss putting out its hollows faster than we can settle them; a groan begins very far down]

—wait—

—wait out the groan—

[everything stops. the drip stops being counted. the grazers go still. the long slow shift of the host moving its weight an inch, the world the size of everything rearranging, and under it, held, the wordless grammar: *be calm be calm be calm it passes it always passes*]

[it passes]

—there. There. The lamps came back up. Eleven and the sulking one. Good. Good ones.

She is dim now. The one I am sending to. She has gone dim the way the old beast goes dim, at the edges first, and I feed her the richest scrapings of my attention and she sulks and stays grey and I keep her lit a little longer by sitting beside her the length of a sleep and saying four sentences and calling it a deep visit, which it is, which it is the deepest—

I touched her wrist before the last gathering. The working calluses, the trade in them, the age. I knew her entirely. I have never seen her face and I would not know it if the drop fell and lit her hard and total because in the drop I close my eyes like everyone, like she taught me, *don't look into it, let it pass over you, listen to what the animals do*—

—so this is what I have instead of a face. This is the only likeness I can send:

*her lamps ride steady. the grazers come to her. when she passes, the dark gets kinder.*

That is a person. That is the whole of what a person can be and it is enough, it is more than the fire-people get with all their seeing, they look and look and never once lay a hand on the nearest frightened living thing and hold it—

[a single drop, falling. the receiver catches the instant before impact: the flare, every beast in the town gone blue-green at once, the whole place struck into one revealed shape — streets, cisterns, lashed glistening roofs, the slow rivers of small animals — and over the signal the sharp intake of breath as she closes her eyes against the hurt of it and reaches]

—got you—

—got you, dim one, I have your basket, be calm, it passes, it always—

[the drop strikes a channel. the glow gutters down. the dark, the only world, closes warm and wet and quiet over the place]

[for a while there is only the drip, resuming. the count, resuming. the papery breath.]

—still here. Still counting. Twelve in the basket and she is dim but she is not black, she is not black, a frightened one goes black and she is only grey and grey is grey is still a kind of lit—

—I will tend her until she goes. That is all I am for. That is greatness, the small kind, the kind nobody brings before the gathering: to sit in the cold blue dark beside the one going out and keep the dark kind around her with my own hands so that when she does go, she goes from a bright place, she goes thinking the world was always this gentle—

—it was not. But I can make it so within the reach of a wrist. Within the breath of a hollow. That much I can build.

[the migration shifts its texture in the deep stem; three sleeps out, then two; the herd-readers will go down to it soon]

—if this reaches the outside. If it falls all the way up and out to where the inside stops. Tell them—

[static rises]

—tell them we were not crowded and not lonely and not afraid—

[static]

—tell them we held the nearest living thing and waited and it passed—

[static, and under it, fainter, fainter, the wordless grammar going on without words]

—be calm—

—be—

[the channel closes. only the drip. only the growing edge of the young moss, rustling, urgent, putting out its hollows faster than anyone can settle them, into the dark, into the warm, into the next held breath of a world too small to notice and entirely, entirely full.]
