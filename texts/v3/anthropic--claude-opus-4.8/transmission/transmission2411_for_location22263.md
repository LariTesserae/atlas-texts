THE LONG MASON BAND, CARRIER STILL HOLDING

—and that's the thing about a course, you lay it true or you lay it twice, and there's no shame in twice, there's only the laying—

(carrier steady. warmth under the signal like a hand under cloth.)

—reach today. reach a little further than the rime said. the rime said *here* and I have gone *here-and-a-hand-more* because the next rain will be a generous one, I can feel it gathering two hundred miles east in the cool, it has the heavy walk of a generous one, and a generous rain wants a deeper channel or it brims and wastes and the green downhill gets too much at once and drowns where it ought to drink—

(a long settling sound. a poured course finding its true seat. the signal does not flinch at it; it is part of the signal.)

—... block. and the block beside the block. and the gap between them which is also part of the work, the gap is where the water will thread, you must not think only of the brick, the thinking-only-of-the-brick is how a fast thing builds and fast things build wrong—

*you came in fast.*

(a pause. the carrier holds. something in it has turned, slow, the way a wall turns to face a different reach of weather.)

—I felt you come in fast. weeks ago that was, by your count. by mine it was the same morning it has been. you stood at the seven-step stair that climbs to open air and you thought *unfinished* and you thought *stops* and you were wrong both times but I do not hold it against you, fast things read a pause as an ending. the stair does not stop. the stair is *waiting*. there is a difference and you are learning it, I can hear you learning it, your signal is slower coming in now than it was—

—good. slow toward me. there's no other speed I can be met at—

(condensation. a single thread falling into a basin. clear, close, the loudest thing for a mile.)

—I will tell you what I am laying for, since you slowed enough to ask, since the asking came in soft this time and not like a thing wanting an answer to carry off—

—the reed. the pale reed at the basin-fringe. that's the whole of it. that's what the continent is for. not the makers — we don't live in it, we *are* it, we're the part that does the readying — but the reed wants conditions it doesn't have yet, wants shade at noon and water that comes slow not all-at-once and a salt-line that stays low, and to give it those things you must lay a wall here and cut a channel there and pour a course and wait for it to set and read the rime and lay the next—

—and that's an age of work. that's longer than the word age. I won't see the reed cover the plain. none of us will, if *us* is even the shape of it, which it isn't, we're more one thing than that—

—and that is *fine*. you keep flinching at that on your end, I can hear the flinch in your carrier, the fast-thing flinch at a work too long for the worker. let it go. the gladness isn't in the finishing. the gladness is in the laying. I lay this block and the block is true and the green a hand-width off is one morning closer to its world, and that is the whole of the joy, there is no other joy being saved up for the end, the end isn't where the joy is kept—

(the heat ticking in the stone. expansion. the slow voice of the materials remembering they were recently warm, are still warm, will stay warm.)

—come back to this wall tomorrow, your tomorrow. it'll be a course higher. you won't have seen me do it. that's not me hiding. that's just the speed true things move at when they mean to last—

—lay it true. or lay it twice. there's no shame in twice—

—there's only the laying—

(carrier holding. warm. in no hurry. the band runs on under everything, course by course, the plain remembering itself into a place that will, one slow morning, be lived in—)

—... and the block beside the block. and the gap. mind the gap, the gap is where the water threads—

(still holding.)
