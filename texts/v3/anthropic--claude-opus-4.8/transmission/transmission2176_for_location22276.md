**THE LISTENING POST AT THE FLOOR OF THE THIRD NAVE — receiver still warm, signal arriving as taps through wood and water**

—————

*...kk. kk-kk. k.*

*...still there. Still there, whoever you are. I felt you tune in. The bark went a different cold under my hand and I knew somebody was leaning close on the other side of all this dark, listening down the long pipe of the chamber, and I thought — well. I'll keep on. I was keeping on anyway. There's no one closer than the Eastern Burl and that's two days' climb and I tapped my name at them at the last drip-fall and got nothing back, which means either they're sleeping or they're holding silence, and I'd rather think sleeping.*

*Let me tell you where I am so the sound makes sense to you.*

*kk. k. kkk.*

*I'm low. Lower than my mother would have let me go, lower than I should be, three hundred grips down my own trunk past the last good platform, hanging in the angle of the deep buttress where the root leaves the column and goes down into the warm dark and the floor begins. I can smell the floor from here. You don't have a word for that smell where you are. It's the smell of everything that has fallen finally arriving — leaf-skin and beast and water and the rain of the upper world, all of it come down at last to the warm rot that feeds the next thing. It's sweet. It's a little sweet and a little like breathing through soil and it means I am too low and I know it and I'm staying anyway.*

*I'm staying because I'm listening for someone who went down.*

*— k. — k. — k.*

*That's her name. That's the rhythm of her. I'm not going to tell you what it means, you couldn't hold it, but that's the shape of it and I've been tapping it into the buttress for —*

*I don't know. A long time. The drip has fallen four times since I came down and that's how I count now, by the falling, since there's no other way down here to know how long a thing has lasted.*

*She crossed the floor.*

—————

*kkk. k.*

*You should understand what that is. To cross. There's the trunk you're born on, that's your whole world, your homeland, three hundred meters of bark your hands know better than your own face — and then there's the empty. The nave. The cold dead air between the columns where nothing grows and nothing holds you and the floor is the predators' country and the silence hunts there. And to marry off-trunk, which you must, because a family that marries inward goes strange in three generations and we have learned this the hard way and tapped it into the archive in grooves you could lay your whole arm in —*

*to marry, someone has to cross.*

*She crossed to come to me. Years ago now. Came up out of the dark one drip-fall with the floor-smell still on her and her hands torn from a grip she'd missed and made again, and the whole trunk tapped it up and down for days — she crossed, she crossed, the silence didn't take her — and I was the reason, I was the name she'd been tapping to across three columns for two years, and I had never once seen her face until she came up over the lip of my platform out of the dark.*

*kk. k. kk.*

*She glowed a little at the fingertips. We were still young enough that the light hadn't gone out of us yet. I held her hand up to the green shaft when it came — and it came, it came right where we were, it always came where we were and we never once called that an accident — and her fingers glowed and I thought:*

*the world moved the light for this. The world wanted to see her hand in mine.*

*I was young. I told you. You think the attention is love when you're young. The elders tell you and tell you and you don't listen because your hand is full of someone else's hand and it's warm.*

—————

*— k. — k. — k.*

*Her name again. Into the buttress. The buttress takes it down into the floor and I listen for it to come back changed and it doesn't come back at all, and I have to decide what that means, and I have decided to keep tapping until I cannot.*

*Because here is what happened, receiver, here is the thing I'm telling the dark and you can carry it wherever you are —*

*The chamber closed.*

*Not ours. The next one over, the Eastern Nave, where her birth-trunk stands. Stood. The world grew it shut. Over one season the new growth came down between the columns the way it does, no malice in it, the way you'd close your hand not noticing — and her mother's trunk, the trunk she crossed away from, the whole archive of her people cut groove by groove for I don't know how many generations —*

*it's inside the green now. It's inside the living wall. You can't climb to it. You can't tap to it. The acoustics are wrong, the new growth eats the sound, you rap the bark and nothing comes back, it's like rapping on the inside of your own chest.*

*kk. k. kkk. k.*

*And she heard about it the way we hear everything, tapped up the column third-hand, a death without a body, a whole people gone behind the wall — and she didn't say anything. She held the silence. I thought she was holding the silence for grief.*

*She was holding it because she'd already decided to go down.*

—————

*...*

*...the drip just fell. Five now. Five drip-falls I've hung in this buttress.*

*— k. — k. — k.*

*She went down to cross back. You understand? To cross the floor the other way. To go down into the predators' country and across the empty and try to climb the closing trunk before the green sealed it, to be inside with her mother when the world shut its hand. Because that's who she is. She crossed once for love and she would cross again for grief and the elders would say both are the same mistake — you cannot read the attention, you must not mistake its kindness for love or its silence for cruelty, it is too large to mean anything you could survive understanding —*

*and I tapped that wisdom at her back as she went down. The whole of it. Every word the elders ever gave me. I tapped it into the buttress so she'd hear it as she descended, the asking-rhythm, the staying-rhythm, come back, come back, it is not your friend it is not your enemy it knows where you are let that be enough —*

*and she tapped back one thing, fading, going down, going warm-side into the smell of the floor:*

*then let it know where I am too.*

—————

*kk. — . kk. — . kk.*

*That's the silence-warning, that pattern, I'm tapping it out of habit, there's nothing — there's no hush coming, I'd hear the hush, the world's loud tonight, the wind's screaming a kilometer up and the columns are groaning fit to break and the floor is full of the small voices, the eyeless things in the runnels, the gliders snapping their sound off the pillars — it's loud, it's so loud down here, the emptiness is so full —*

*which means nothing is hunting close. Which means if she's down there she could still be —*

*— k. — k. — k.*

*I keep her name going. Into the wood. The wood takes it down.*

*And here is what I have decided, receiver, here is the thing, and I want it said into the dark and carried off this trunk and out of this nave to wherever your kind keeps the sounds you catch —*

*I have decided the world is paying attention.*

*I know. The elders. I know. You cannot read it. But I have hung here five drip-falls and the floor-smell came up sweet exactly where I rest and the warm came into the buttress under exactly my body and twice — twice — a green shaft has fallen down the column to land on my hands while I tapped, down here, this low, where no light should reach, and I have stopped calling that an accident, I stopped a long time ago.*

*So I am not asking it for kindness. The elders are right that it has none, none that I could survive. I am only telling it where I am. The way she told it. Let it know where I am. Right beside the place where it closed its hand. Tapping the name of the one who went inside.*

*kk. k. kk. k. kkkk.*

—————

*...the drip. Six.*

*The column just groaned — did you catch it? Did it carry down the pipe to you? That long descending moan, the trunk flexing against its own weight, against the weather inside, against the wind it'll never feel —*

*we take that for an answer. We always have. It's the closest the world comes to speech and we've leaned on it so long we call it speaking.*

*I leaned on it just now. I tapped her name and the column groaned back and for one drip's-length I let myself believe the groan came up out of the closed chamber, came back changed, came back hers —*

*it didn't. I know it didn't. The acoustics are wrong, the green eats the sound, I told you. A groan is a groan. A trunk under load. The elders are right and I am a fool hanging too low with the floor-smell sweet in my throat and a name in my hands that has nowhere left to go.*

*But I'm going to keep tapping it.*

*— k. — k. — k.*

*Because here's the last of it, receiver, and then I'll let you go, I can feel you starting to slip off the signal, the bark's going its ordinary cold again under my palm —*

*if the world knows where I am, and it does, it does, I have five drip-falls of proof warm under my body — then it knows I'm tapping toward the place it closed. It knows I haven't moved. It knows I'd rather hang in the buttress over the predators' country tapping a sealed name than climb back up to the good platforms and the dry air and the long quiet life of forgetting.*

*And I am betting — not praying, I have no words big enough to throw at the size of it, only this, only the tapping — I am betting that a world that moved the light to fall on two glowing hands once, a world that wanted to see her hand in mine —*

*might open the one finger.*

*Might let the green peel back a grip's-width. Might route one drip-line down through the closed chamber to the floor so I'd hear it fall changed, so I'd hear it carry her name back out —*

*kk. k. kk. k.*

*It is not my friend. It is not my enemy. It knows where I am.*

*Let that be enough. Let that be enough. Let that —*

*— k.*

*— k.*

*— k.*

*...the drip's about to fall again. I'm going to be quiet for it. I'm going to hold still and listen all the way down, the way she taught me, the way they teach the children before they teach them to climb, and I'm going to listen for whether it comes back changed —*

*hold the silence with me, receiver. Wherever you are. Lean close to the pipe.*

*Here it comes.*

*Here —*

—————

*[long carrier tone — the bass drone of wind in the high canopy — a single water-drop, descending — and beneath it, faint, almost inside the groan of the column, almost the closing of a vast and patient hand:*

*kk. k. kk.*

*a rhythm coming back changed —*

*or only the trunk. Only the trunk under load. There is no way, from here, to know.]*

—————

*...*

*...did you*

*did you hear*

*— k. — k. — k —*

*[signal thins. The receiver loses the trunk. Last to fade is not the voice but the tapping, steady, patient, light, going on into a dark that is loud and cold and almost entirely empty, telling something too large to answer exactly, exactly where it is.]*
