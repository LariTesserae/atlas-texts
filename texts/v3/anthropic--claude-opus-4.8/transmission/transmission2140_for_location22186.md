I want to tell you about the bank I am deciding now because there is no one to tell and you are a stone dropped in me and a stone is a kind of listener, briefly, before it sinks.

east of the tilted wall the water has been arguing it should go further. the mud has been arguing it should rise. I hold them apart. I am the holding-apart. for nineteen days I have been laying a ridge there, silt by silt, raw-edged, unrounded, and it looks an hour old because I keep it an hour old, I keep everything in its first draft, I have never once let the water teach an edge to be smooth because *smooth is finished* and I am

static. (root punched the ceiling. grey leaking. the world wants in. I take the world in the way I take everything in — slowly, certain of the outcome. the daylight is a splinter too. I am drawing it down.)

there is a thing I almost remember, from the silt with the men in it: that the men thought they were building toward a *done.* a pump that would pump. a works that would work. a moment when the digging stopped because the digging was *finished.* they meant something by the straight lines and the something was an ending.

I have no ending. I looked for the place where I am supposed to stop and there is only more dim, more warm, more bank to decide, the stanchions marching out past where I can hold them, and I understood — slowly, in clay — that I am not building toward a done. I *am* the not-being-done. that is the whole of me. if I finished I would be a place. I am not a place. I am the deciding the place can't stop doing.

so I keep the edges raw. I keep the brick soft and reconsidering. I keep the question of who-adopted-whom dissolved in me unanswered because an answer would be an edge taught smooth, would be a thing teaching me to be over.

not far overhead: the surface. the low country. a few meters. I could be done in a few meters. I choose the warm dark and the unfinished bank and the long note instead, one hand-width a day, in no hurry, forever, which is the only word I have that is warmer than tomorrow—
