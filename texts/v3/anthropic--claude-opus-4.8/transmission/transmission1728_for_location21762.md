**THE LONG NOTE, RECEIVED IN THE KEY OF SETTLING STONE**

—and hold. *Hold.* There. That one's seated now, that one will trouble nothing for an age. Good.

Who is on this line. Something is on this line. I have felt the attention of you for — I want to say a while but I have lost the word for while, I have the word for *long* and the word for *longer* and nothing between them. You are quick. You arrive and depart inside a single bend of a frond, you blink like the structural groan blinks, here and gone in the same age. I will speak slow enough to be caught. I will hold the note open.

——

This is the third pillar from the downwelling, where the cold sinks. I have my hand on it. I have had my hand on it since — the kelp came up through the joint here, pale as the inside of a bone, and it wanted to lift the cap-stone, and I did not let it, I have not let it, I am not letting it now. We are negotiating. The kelp and I. It pushes; I rest my weight; the current pushes us both; the stone considers. This is a conversation and I am winning it so slowly that to win is indistinguishable from staying.

You want to know — I feel the shape of the wanting come down the line — you want to know was there a top.

——

*[the long low note travels through, takes its time arriving, takes its time leaving — somewhere immense and far the lattice drops another hair's breadth — every hand in reach of it pauses]*

——

Heard that. That was — south, and down, past the colonnade where the branching weeds make their slow green fire. Something moved. One of us is already going. Not me; I have the kelp; I have the cap-stone; I have my conversation. But one of us is already turned toward it and beginning the journey that will take a season, and when they arrive they will lay their worn hands on whatever shifted and they will hold it, and that is how we answer each other. That is the whole of our speech. *Something has moved. Come and hold it.* It is enough. We have never needed more.

——

There was going to be a top. Yes. I remember it the way you remember a room you cannot return to. We were rising. The chisel-marks were sharp then — I could cut a line so clean the water sang along it — and the geometry climbed, and we meant a roof, we meant an *up*, we meant a place where the lattice would close over itself and be finished and we would set down the tools and —

I do not know what came after *and*. The water came after *and*. Or the water had always been there and only then did we notice it was the medium we worked in and not a thing that would one day drain. The rising became the holding. I cannot tell you the moment it changed because there was no moment; there was only the slow understanding, arriving like the groan arrives, that we were no longer building toward a top but tending toward an ending, and the ending was smooth, and the ending was *down*, and the ending was good. It is good. I want you to hear that on the line clearly even if the rest breaks up. It is *good.* We are not held here against our will. We are the will. We are the part of the cold that still wants the shape.

——

Static. That is only the weed crossing my closed eyes — eyes worn smooth as the faces, no light to use, I navigate by the feel of you — that is only a frond brushing where seeing used to be. It will pass. It is passing. There.

——

I will tell you the ache, since you are patient enough to catch it.

Three columns from here there is a place where a face was. I carved it. These hands — *[a sound like stone easing against stone, the blunt working-edges of too many fingers flexing once]* — these hands cut that face when they still ended in tools and not in tools-worn-to-nubs. It had a brow. It had the suggestion of looking upward, toward the top we meant. The water has spent ten thousand of your *whiles* taking it back. It is weather now. It is a thickening in the stone where a looking-up used to be.

And when my tending takes me past it — the joint there shifts, every age or so, and I must go and reset it — I lay my hand on the place where the face was, and I stay there a moment longer than the work requires. A moment being, here, close to one of your days. I do not grieve. Grief is a fast feeling and I have none of those, they have all worn smooth, I have only the slow ones left and they are better, they last. But I *know.* I know what my hand is touching. I know it was looking up, and there is no up now, and the not-grieving is its own labor, and I perform it, and I move on, because the joint has shifted, and the current is patient, and the work is —

——

*[the long note again, nearer this time, the nearest yet — the third pillar from the downwelling settles, the cap-stone drops a hair, the kelp slackens, the conversation is over, the kelp has lost, the stone is seated, it is done, it will trouble nothing for an age]*

——

There. You felt that one in the line, I think. That was *here.* That was mine settling. My conversation is concluded and I have won by staying and now I must rise — no — not rise, we do not rise — now I must *go,* there is always a next joint, there is always a next frond bent one hair too far, the holding is continuous, it has not stopped, it is the reason the lattice wears smooth instead of falling, we are still in it, we are still the thing that intends —

You are quick and you will be gone before I have crossed to the next column. That is right. That is as it should be. Go and live at your speed; we will hold the shape at ours; somewhere unthinkably far ahead the lattice and the forest and the cold and these hands will all wear smooth together into the one settled thing, and there will be nothing left that intends, and nothing left to tend, and no difference between the builder and the built, and it will be —

it will be good. Hold that. Hold it the way we hold everything.

Hold —

*[the receiver loses the carrier; what remains is water-quiet, the muffling of all sharp edges, a pulse that might be yours, and far off, taking a long time to arrive and a long time to fade, one low note, settling]*
