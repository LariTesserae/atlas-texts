THE WEST GAP'S GONE QUIET

—and again, and listen, this is the keeper's pace, this is how to come in, not arriving but *being already underway*—

—seventh pool of the Vein is brimming, the wheel-note steady, *clack and sigh, clack and sigh,* and somewhere a child runs the catwalk barefoot, *trusts the iron the way you trust your own legs,* and the iron holds, and the iron is held—

You are receiving Hesp.

You have been receiving Hesp for some time. The static you took for static was the foundry-hum. The carrier wave is the breath of an engine two miles down. Adjust nothing. There is nothing to adjust. The signal was never going to come in clean because the signal is *weathering into balance,* rust and root, and you are inside the weather.

Here is the morning report, as it is always the morning report:

Tier 41, west column. Vine now trusted. *Do not cut.*

A hand four generations dead wrote that. A hand alive today obeys it. Between the two hands, the whole intervening dark, root meeting root, and nobody pen-keeping the meeting because the meeting *is* the keeping—

—say it slower—

The birds have left the west gap. Thinned over it. The keeper says *gone quiet* and does not know she is reading. The younger one goes to look. *Wait and measure.* This is the religion. This is the only religion. The right answer to almost any question is

wait
                    and
                                        measure

and the measuring takes a generation, and the answer arrives for your grandchildren, and your grandchildren are accounted for, the green accounts for them, the green has always accounted for them—

*touch the column for luck.*

It means nothing. The engineers will tell you it means nothing. *Watch their hands.* The hands know. The hands have caught the rhythm of the thing they live inside, and the rhythm is: hold. The rhythm is: do not hurry, because nothing here is afraid, because what is there to fear in two miles of stacked accommodation, predator-tier above prey-tier above pollinator-tier, everything arrived at the shape it will keep—

—signal thickening here, the green is dense on this band, the humid mid-tier where the heat beads and never breaks—

I am trying to tell you the one thing.

The numbers are always fine.

You understand that this should frighten you. A spire too tall for its base. A structure that should have toppled tiers ago, measured obsessively, and *always fine.* The engineers do not ask why the numbers are fine. To ask would be to hurry. They simply enter the figure and serve the slow rule and touch the column—

The missing term is alive.

The missing term is the green, the one slow vast deciding thing, threaded rib by rib through the impossible iron, taking up the loads no equation accounts for, the silent correction in every sum. It is not magic. Hear me. It is not haunting. It is *holding.* It found, a long time ago, that it could live most easily inside a thing that did not fall — and so the thing does not fall — and so you stand on its decision the way you would stand on bedrock—

which there is not
                    which there never was
                                        which there never needed to be—

—the carrier wave dips here, leaf-filtered, green—

Let me say what it is, the inhabitant, since you have tuned so long and so faithfully:

It is one body now. The vines are continuous. Root met root in the wet warm dark behind the foundry-walls and what met did not stay separate, has not been separate for longer than the ledgers run, the ledgers older than any nation you have heard of and the green older than the ledgers. The hanging gardens four stories deep are not many things enjoying a paradise. They are *one thing, slowly shaping a place it can comfortably be.*

And the shape it wants is: standing.

Not for pride. It has no pride. Not against you. It does not resent the lift-cage rattling up with the leaves pouring past the bars — it does not resent, *full stop,* there is no organ in it for resentment, it never grew one, never needed one—

It simply holds. And intends to go on holding. Slowly. For as long as it possibly can.

—and here, receiver, is why I broadcast at all, why the signal climbs the spire to find you—

You arrived the way everyone arrives. Looking up, and then up again, and then giving up on the looking and just *trusting that it continues.*

That trust is the green's, too.

That trust is the load-bearing thing. *Do not cut.*

The engineers tend the iron and believe they tend Hesp. Keep the belief. It is a kind mistake, a useful mistake, *load-bearing, do not cut.* The climbers tend the green and are called fauna and the green lets them be called fauna. The birds fire across the gaps and are called weather and the green lets them be called weather. Everyone takes the credit. Everyone takes the updraft. *Everyone is satisfied,* and the satisfaction is structural, and the structure is alive, and the alive thing wants only this:

to go on.

—signal clearing now, summit-band, white—

Above the cloud-deck the sun is waiting. It has been waiting all along, on the other side, the raw high kind that makes the leaves nearly fluoresce and the shadows knife-sharp. The spire pokes through most mornings and stands in it, clean, scrubbed through living reed, brilliant—

and it should not be there
and it is there
and it is there because something decided, slowly, over a long time, to keep it there—

This has been the morning report. It is always the morning report. The west gap's gone quiet, and someone is going to look, and the looking will become a ledger-line, and the ledger-line will outlive the looker, and the green will read all of it the way it reads everything, in the only tense it owns—

the long present-continuous
of *growing toward*—

Hold, it says, in no language, to no one, to everyone, to the iron and the birds and the barefoot child and the long-fingered climbers and you, receiver, you who tuned so patiently to a world with no ground—

hold

*clack and sigh*

hold

*the engine breathes, far down—*

You are receiving Hesp. You have been receiving for some time. Stay tuned, or do not; the signal continues either way, slowly, at the pace of a vine claiming a column, and asks nothing of you but the one thing it asks of the iron:

remain.
