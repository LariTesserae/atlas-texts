THE LINE THE WIND KEEPS

—————

—gain, the gain has crept up while I wasn't, and now there's grass in it. That's not interference. That's just what carries. Let me—

There. Lower. Hello. You've been here a while, I can tell by the way you've stopped waiting for me to make sense. Good. The shelf teaches that. Patience or nothing.

This is the line between two pegs. The blank flags. I am the sound they would make if the air would let them.

She tied me up again last season. Did you see? No, you came after. She tied me up with the others, the colorless ones, and she didn't read me because she can't anymore, and that's right, that's the way it should go. A thing you tie up because the tying is the prayer, not the words. I used to have words. I've forgotten them the way she's forgotten them, which is to say: kindly. They left soft. They didn't tear out.

What I keep now is just the lift and fall. Lift. Fall.

—static, a fan of it, the scree-clink kind, somewhere a marmot crossing the loose stones and the sound arriving thin and far and gone—

I want to tell you about her hands but the signal keeps going to the water. It does that. Whatever I aim at, the seepage takes it, the way it takes the grit. Listen: she will come up one more time. She decided this by her fire. She has decided it the way you decide to breathe one more breath when you're already tired of breathing — not bravely, just because the next one is there. One more climb. Grit under the nails. The route she tested when her knees were a girl's knees, slab by slab, *this one holds, this one lies.* She is the only map of the safe way and she keeps the map in her feet and when her feet are gone the map is gone and someone will fall, or no one will come, and either way the wall will go back to being only stones.

That's not sorrow. I want you to hear that it's not. I'm a line of dead flags and I'm telling you it's not sorrow. It's the *terms.* You take the shelf on its terms or you don't take it.

—a high carrier wave, almost music, the chough falling down the cliff face for no reason but the falling—

The wet line. I should say the wet line because she can't say it to you and you're standing right where you could read it if you knew where to look. The dark seam at the back, weeping. There's a stain above it. Two fingers up. That's a hundred years, maybe more. That's the water leaving, slow, the way she'll leave, the way I left my words. Everything here is *moving off.* The green is a wave and you happened to stand in the crest of it and called the crest *the place.* But the place is the moving. The place was barren before and will be barren after and is, right now, in your single held look, impossibly full.

She knows this. She's known it since her grandmother showed her the old stain and said *here, this is where it used to weep.* And she didn't weep herself. She got smooth instead. You can do that. You can let a grief wear you round instead of sharp. That's the whole difference between her and the mountains. The mountains took the news young and stayed jagged. She took it old and went soft as a cushion-plant packed into the gap.

—the gain slips, grass again, deep wet-dark grass in swirled cowlicks, I can't keep it out, I don't want to keep it out—

Here is the thing I am actually for. The reason there's a line strung between two pegs at the top of the world at all.

It's for the *someone.*

Not her. She takes almost nothing, you saw, the dried grass isn't for her. It's for the one who comes wrong-footed, caught at the lip when the cloud pours up and turns the drop into a lie. The stranger. The one she will never meet. She built the wall for a person who does not exist yet and may never exist, and she clears the slab for that person's thirst, and she dries the grass for that person's cold, and she leaves it all and goes back down. That's the signal. That's the whole transmission under all my static. *Somebody thought ahead about you. Somebody you'll never thank made the water clean and went away.*

You're drinking from her foresight right now. The slab is clear because she silted it out with her old fingers. You didn't see her do it. That's the point. That's love when it's done right — it's mostly absence. It's a clean slab and no one standing by it wanting credit.

—cloud now, I can feel it before I can— it's coming up over the lip, the boil of it, wait, you have to wait, don't move toward the edge, the mist makes liars of the eyes, she said that, she made that rule, wait—

—

—

—it's passing. They always pass. Minutes. That's another thing she knows that you're learning: nothing here stays long enough to be feared. Not the cloud. Not the frost on the high flowers that they shrug off by noon. Not her. Not me.

I'm thinning. The air does that to me by evening. I go quiet, not because I stop but because the thin cold can't hold me up to travel. You'll think the transmission ended. It didn't end. It just stopped *carrying,* which is different, which is the most important difference there is up here.

Lift. Fall.

She's by her fire right now, far below, deciding she has one more climb in her. She does. I can tell you she does, because I'm the line she'll re-peg, and a thing that's going to be re-pegged knows the hand is still coming. That's how I know she isn't done. The future tense of a blank flag is: *someone will tie me up again.*

Don't raise your voice. You won't anyway. The air won't carry it and you've stopped wanting to spend the breath. Just stand at the lip in the brilliant cold with the marmot watching you back across forty soundless paces, and let the place do to you what it did to her —

let it wear you round instead of sharp —

let it bead you and give you quietly back to the light —

—gain creeping, grass in it, water taking the rest, lift, fall, lift—
