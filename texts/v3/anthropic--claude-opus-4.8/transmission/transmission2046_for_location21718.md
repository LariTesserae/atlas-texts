THE SLOW BAND, EVENING WATCH — RECEIVED

—————

...two notes and the long under it. Two notes and the long under it. You have it now. You've had it. Keep it.

This is the slow band, the dew-count, the evening watch, going out from the high coil on the Methery tower to anyone with a wet ear, and the dew came up nine fingers in the east cisterns and eight and a half in the west and that is *fine*, that is good, that is the slow holding, so let your shoulders down. Let them down. There is nothing tonight that wants you fast.

—————

*(a long sigh of pump-breath. clank. and. clank. and.)*

—————

...the Methery and the Oll have settled. You heard it here who care to hear it slow. Forty years that coil-shadow argument and tonight the Oll grandchild — Surra, the one with the smooth left hand, the one who came back from the dry-room early and everyone wondered — Surra went and shored the Methery's south pier without a word. Laid the new iron in the wet. Touched the rail going home. And old Methe was on her catwalk and saw it and did not say a thing because there is nothing to say, the matter is *settled*, it ran down its groove at last and pooled, and that is how a thing ends here, not loud, not fast, just finally low enough to rest.

So. That feud is water now. Drink it.

—————

*(skirl of the bird-smoke lifting. the rumble in the chest, the great slow tide of backs going west under the streets all night, all night, the way it has gone.)*

—————

...and to the young ones out on the wet iron who don't yet know to trail the hand — trail the hand. Trail the hand even when you don't need it. *Especially* when you don't need it. The not-needing is exactly when you fall. We have lost ones who were sure of their feet. We do not name them on the band because to be lost outside the loop is the one grief we keep quiet, but you know who I mean, every tower knows its own, and the lesson of every one of them is the same lesson, which is the *only* lesson:

don't break the slow.

—————

*(here the signal thins. a hiss of condensation across the green copper. it gathers again.)*

—————

...who is sick tonight and who is in the dry-room. Old Pell is in the dry-room. Pell of the rendering-floor, who sang the unmaking sixty years, more breath than melody, the same two notes — you've heard him, you grew up on his voice going down into the work. Pell's hands have gone to wet paper at the edges and his family has given him the thin warm air, the deepest thing they have, and his fingers are smoothing for the first time since he was born. Imagine it. Imagine the skin you have worn wrinkled your whole life going smooth at the very end, the bath letting go of you at last, and knowing as it smooths what it means.

Pell is not afraid. The floor reports he is not afraid. He is going back in good order. He will be condensed. He will run down and pool and bead again on the cold of the world and that is not an ending, that is the loop holding him the way it holds the water and the flesh, nothing leaves, *nothing leaves*, and so we do not mourn Pell, we — we slow for him. We slow the band a moment for him. There.

—————

*(...........)*

*(.......the long under it. just the long under it. the herd. the chest. the tide.)*

—————

...back. Back now. The coils are running. The cisterns are filling. The lamps are yellow in the steam the way they have always been yellow in the steam.

And to whoever this is reaching that I cannot see — you, with the receiver tuned a long time now, you out past the curve where the rain belongs to a different afternoon — I don't know your dryness. I can feel it on the band, the dry of you, the smooth-fingered far-off listening of you, and I'll tell you the only thing worth the breath:

we are condensation. That is all a person is. Warm wet air beaded for one slow lifetime on a cold stone, holding a shape against the cold, and then running gently down. You are that too, wherever you're catching this. You are beaded somewhere on the cold of your own world, holding your shape, thinking it is *you* and not weather. It is weather. It is lovely weather. Hold the shape gently. Don't break your own slow.

—————

*(clank. and. sigh. and. the bird-smoke resettling. the green coils weeping their good water down into the dark.)*

—————

...nine fingers in the east. Eight and a half in the west. The dew is holding. The herd is going west all night under the streets the way it has gone and the way it will go. Trail the hand. Slow for Pell. The feud is water, drink it.

Two notes and the long under it.

Two notes—

*(—and the long under it, and the long under it, fading now into only the long, only the under, only the warm wet patient breath of the held world, going on, going on, going—)*
