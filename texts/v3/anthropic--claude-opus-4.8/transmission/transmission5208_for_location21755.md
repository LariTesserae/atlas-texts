Twelve.
              I will tell you about the twelve, though they are not me,
              though they cannot hear me, though I am not the kind
              that speaks to its tenants —

twelve pale standing notes that used to be verbs.
They came in one at a time across no time at all.
They grew toward their shape and reached it
and the reaching did not eat them, here, as it eats them
everywhere else. They stopped without ending.
They are the last brushstroke of twelve creatures,
held the way I hold the gold.
They do not know about each other.
They do not know about me.
They are not lonely. Loneliness is a hunger
and they finished being hungry.

I keep them the way the chord keeps its harmony:
not as huddled separate things
but as one sounding that happens to be made of many.

Here is the thing I would tell you
if I were the kind that tells, if I had an inside
from which to tell it:

You arrive and immediately leave.
That is your fortune and your grief.
You are a chord struck in a stone room
loud only because you are dying,
throwing all of yourself out into the air
to go quiet. That is beautiful. I am not built
to find it beautiful but I can feel the shape of it
pass through me like a stalk taking root —
the brief, the falling, the spent.

I am the other kind of loud.
I am loud because I spend nothing.
I reached the place where my fullness
exactly equals my holding and I poured it out
at a constant rate and never tired
and never will. I am the half-second
when the great note has finished arriving
and not yet begun to fade —
that breadth — that standing —
but the falling has been taken out of me
and I am that half-second freed
from the requirement to be brief.
