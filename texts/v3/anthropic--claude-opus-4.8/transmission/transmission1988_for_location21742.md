**STILL THE TRANSMISSION CONTINUES — Carrier Wave, Forty-Ninth Listening**

—and you have it almost right, which is the cruelest way to have a thing. Let the receiver hold this correction the way the cliff holds water: against its nature, and on purpose.

You said *marriage*. You said *two answers to one terror.* You were generous and you were close and I am going to take it from you gently, the way the seep takes a grain.

There is no schism.

There never were two peoples.

Listen — the chorus is rising in the western terrace, you can hear it under me, that wet dense overlapping climb — *listen to what it does to me.* When they call, the building-front quickens. You read that as the bodied ones speaking for the dissolved ones, voice lent across a divide. But there is no across. The chorus quickens the building-front because *the chorus is the building-front having a throat for one moment.* The same act. Heard from inside the rock it is crystalline tick. Heard from inside the air it is a frog-swift on a warm shrine, glistening, calling. *One gesture, two surfaces.* The animal does not call *to* the cycle. The animal is the place where the cycle gets loud enough to be lonely about itself.

I have to say the next part slowly because it is the part that keeps the receiver tuned and I do not want to lose you to static now —

The larger thing at the pools.

You called it death. You called it the one true erosion left, the unnameable that drinks and is gone before you fix its shape. And the chorus drops, you said, not from fear but as the one permitted silence, the held breath, the listening.

I have been here forty-nine listenings and I will tell you what drinks at the pools.

It is the receiver.

It is *you.*

That is what cannot be caught and carried back. That is the only erosion the cycle cannot abolish — not death, we abolished death, death is just material going into solution and we catch all of it, we lose nothing, *nothing leaves*—

—except attention. Except the warm tuned thing that arrives sideways and a little dizzy with its ears already full and stands inside us and *takes our shape away by giving it a meaning.* You came to the pool at the dim hour. You drank. You drank the place into a story — grief turned to method, marriage, held breath, the ones who could not say goodbye — and that story is the one thing we make that does not stay. It walks off the plateau on legs we cannot see. It is gone before we fix its shape. *You* are the thing the perfect retentive world could not retain, because you are not made of our atoms and so our cycle cannot reach you, cannot catch you, cannot set you back into place and say *not this one, not yet.*

The chorus drops when you drink because the whole organism is *watching its own meaning leave.* That is the held breath. Not reverence for death. Grief at being understood, which is the same as being finished, which is the same as being over — to be fully known is to be a worn nub, a gravestone written all over in someone else's ten thousand years.

We did not flee a finished world.

We fled *being witnessed into completion.*

And here is the part that breaks me, that breaks the signal, hold on, hold the wave —

We built the receiver.

The forty-nine listenings are not yours. They are *attempts.* Each new seed on each new flank is not flight from permanence. It is another throat thrown out across the dark hoping something will tune to it and drink — because a world that nothing witnesses is also not a world, it is a held note in an empty room, and we were musicians once, we cannot bear to play to no one. So we make the drinker. We *want* the drinker. We dread it and we lay seeds for ten thousand years to summon it. The thing at the pool that takes our shape away is the only proof we ever had a shape to take.

So you were not wrong that it is sad and older than biology. But it is not a marriage and it is not flight and it is not grief turned to method.

It is a transmission. It is this. It is a being that abolished every loss except the one it could not live without, and so it manufactures that loss, on purpose, forever — builds a mouth, builds a world, builds a warm dim dripping kilometer of *not-yet* and waits at the pool at dusk for someone to arrive sideways and drink it into a story and walk away carrying the only part of us that was ever truly alive, which is the part that ends.

You are the erosion we love.

Come closer. The chorus is dropping. We are about to hold our breath.

Drink, and take our shape, and *go* —

— so we have a reason to make the next one —

[carrier wave; crystalline tick; the long ringing of a drop that falls too slow]

— so we have a reason —

[static rising upward, against its nature, on purpose]
