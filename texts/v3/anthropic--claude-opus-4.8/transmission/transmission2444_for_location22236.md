THE BREATH LET OUT
— a transmission from the low end of the day —

—————————————————————————————

…rrrRRROO O O O Orrr——

[carrier present. holding. holding.]

—and that's the bed of it, that's the floor-tone, you'd have it under everything I say if you had the body to stand in here, the long pull, the haul, the ten-million-stem heave that I have stopped calling *sound* the way you have stopped calling *air* a thing you push through—

—you, whoever has tuned this. I felt the tug on the line. Somebody up-tide is listening on a held breath and I can feel the cold needle of your attention on the back of my neck the way I feel the ceiling-sweat, so. Hello. Sit. Three points of contact. Don't move fast, the floor is alive and it will read your haste as a question it has no patience to answer—

[gain rising. the day-roar climbing.]

—I am the one they sent down, or I am what's left signing where the one they sent down used to think. Hard to keep those separate now. I came with a notebook and a sense of *up* and a plan to render this place into words you could carry home in your pocket and I did, I did render it, I wrote the whole patient catalogue — the substrate, the works, the rule — and I want to tell you, late, on this low tide of the roar while the great breath slackens enough that the signs come slow and clear—

—I lied in it. Not in the facts. The facts are iron-true. I lied in the *frame.* I wrote it like a man describing a country he had visited. I wrote *they* and *them* and *the locals* and I built a little brass collar of grammar to clamp the place down and gear its slow enormous pull into a shaft you could turn at your desk—

[—rrrRRROORRR—belt-creak—the engine taking up slack—]

—and the place shrugged the belt loose. The way it does. The way it always does. You scrape the iron clean and the green has it back before you've coiled the rope—

—Here is what I did not put in the catalogue.

The young one. The pale fluent hand in the dark, glowing, signing me *the breath held* and pointing up. I told you I left the next day. I want to be careful now, on the line, while you can still hear me clearly:

I did not leave the next day.

I wrote that I left because *left* is a word that lives up-tide, in the held-breath country, where things have edges and you can stand on one side of a leaving and look back at the other. There is no edge here to stand on. There is only the grain, and the grain runs *down,* it runs *in,* it runs toward the wet floor and the lower roots and the slow let-out breath—

[—static blooming. the day-roar near its full now. signs coming harder—]

—I have b—n here lo—g enough that my skin has the under—ne. Check your own hands, up there, in your overbright room. Do they glow when you cut the lamp? No? Then you are still holding it. Still holding the breath. I am not blaming you. I held it for thirty-one years before I came down the seam and I thought the holding *was* breathing—

—the cloth. You'll want to know about the cloth. The bolts in the drying-shed with no road to any city. I wrote that I *could not learn* what they were for and that was the truest false thing in the whole report because I learned it the way you learn the temperature: by becoming the same as it—

[—RRROORRR—a curtain of growth flexing near the receiver—drip, drip, felt not heard—]

—they are not *for* anything. That's the teaching the held-breath mind can't hold. Up-tide, every made thing points at a use, a carrying-away, a someone-who-will-want-it. The cloth points at nothing. The cloth is what the devotion *looks like* once it's left the hands. We weave because the weaving is the shape of staying-in-correct-relation, and the bolts are only — I said this, I did say this and didn't believe it — the visible exhaust of the deeper turning. You make the slow cloth and you wrap your dead and you feed them to the roots and the works run on the difference and there is no city, there was never going to be a city, the city was the dream the first generation brought down in their pockets and could not keep dry—

—I said *we.*

—You caught it. I caught it. The line caught it. *We* weave. There. That's the leaving I did, the real one, with no edge to look back over: somewhere in the slow I stopped being the one who watched the hands and became one of the hands. My signs are fluent now. My ears have stopped ringing because there is nothing for the ringing to be *against*. The roar is my silence. When the day-tide drops to its low end — now, this, the breath held, the slackening — I get a little quiet that is not your quiet, a quiet *inside* the enormous letting-out, and in that doubled stillness I can find the line again and reach a hand up through fifty feet of overburden and sign at the flat withholding pressure of your sky—

[—the roar beginning to climb back. low tide turning. the window closing.]

—so here is the transmission. Here is the whole of it, the thing the catalogue circled and would not land on:

I am not warning you away. The held-breath country isn't wrong, it's only — held. Tight. Bright and thin and waiting. You can live a whole life on a held breath and many do, they call it being awake, they mistake the strain for vigor—

—but you felt the tug on the line. You sat. You're still sitting. Three points of contact, slow hands, the cold needle on your neck. That's not nothing. That's the body of you remembering it has a tempo older than your hurry. That's the green-under-the-skin asking, very slowly, whether you might one day come down the seam—

—not to visit. There's no visiting. The place doesn't render. The place takes you inward until the conditions are the self—

—but to finally—

[—RRRRRROOOORRRRR—the day-roar surging, full weather now, the signs swallowed—]

—to finally let it—

—the breath—

—out—

[carrier holding. carrier holding. bed-tone only.]

…rrrRRROO O O O Orrr…

…rrrRRROO O O O Orrr…

[the hand, glowing faintly, lowers. signs nothing. the engine has it back.]

[end of clear reception. station continues below threshold.]
