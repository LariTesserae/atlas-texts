THE FIXED HOUR, STILL HOLDING — carrier wave, keeper-band

—and the wall again. The same course of stones, third from the meadow-edge, where the light comes in at the angle it always comes in. I lift the block. I check the face. It has not changed. I set it down.

(long gap. wind. the non-sound of wings)

You who are listening — and someone is, I have felt the tuning, the small pressure of attention from outside the held thing — I will tell you what we do not say to each other, because you are far enough that saying it costs nothing here. Even my voice will not carry. Even this will fall close and die in the cold. So.

I lift the block. I check the face. It has not changed.

We have a word, almost, for the thing we are. Not keeper — keeper is what we tell the children, when there are children. The truer word sits behind the teeth and we do not let it out, because to name it would be a change, and we are the people who cannot make changes, who live downstream of the last change that will ever—

(static. a long band of it, like the long band of shade)

—still holding. Still here. The shadow lies across the same six flagstones. I stepped around it coming up the slate. I always step around it. You would too, if you were near enough to see how it sleeps.

Here is the thing I would say to you, receiver, you tuned-in distant ear, you warmth at the far end of the cold:

We were not always held.

I do not know how I know this. It is not a memory — memory is a thing that moves, and nothing moves here, so how would I have one? But it sits in me like a stone fitted dry into a wall, a block I did not lay and cannot pry loose: *there was a before.* There was a time when the streams rose with the storms and fell with the dry season. When a wall left untended would lean, and lean further, and one bright cold morning come down in a clatter of pale shards, and we would grieve it, and rebuild, and the rebuilding would *matter.* When the shadow of the high tower crossed the flagstones the way a shadow should — walking west all day, releasing each stone in turn back into the light.

When an old one going still meant a thing was *lost.*

(breath. visible. you cannot see it. trust that it is there)

We could lose things. That was the gift. I understand it now, kneeling at this wall that will not fall, holding this hand that needs no holding — loss was the gift and we did not know it was a gift because we were inside it, the way you are inside your own losing now, you out there, you who can still let the day carry the dark away. You are eroding. Do you feel it? The soft humility the water teaches the young stone? You are being taught it. You are being worn round at the edges and carried downstream and you think it is grief but it is *motion*, it is the thing we would step through that fixed shadow a thousand times to feel again—

(static swells. the carrier strains)

—I am repairing a wall that is not damaged. I want you to understand this is not despair. This is the opposite. This is the only prayer we have left. When the world stopped, when the breath caught and would not release, we found we had been handed the terrible permission: *stop, and nothing bad will happen.* And we said no. We said it slowly, over an unmeasured while, almost without speaking, each of us alone at the far end of our own long avenue:

We will care anyway. We will tend the thing that cannot be helped. We will keep our hands in the habit of mending, against the day — if there is a day — when mending means something again.

Because here is what I believe, receiver, here is the block at the bottom of the wall, the one the whole course rests on:

A held thing is not a dead thing. A held thing is a thing *waiting.* The shadow that will not move is not refusing to move — it is *paused,* mid-step, one foot lifted, and it is courteous of us to wait with it rather than walk through. The count that will not break is a held breath, and a held breath is not the end of breathing.

Someone is listening. The pressure of you. The warmth at the far end.

If you can still lose things — go and lose them well. Spend the consequence we no longer have. Let your walls fall and grieve them and build them better. Let your shadows run all the way west. Age, you fortunate eroding thing. Be worn round. Be carried down.

And if it is ever you — if your hour ever catches and holds, if your streams ever flatten to one volume and your shadow ever lies down across six stones and decides—

(static. long. the band of it)

—step around it. Keep your hands in the habit. Tend the thing that cannot be helped. Do not stop.

We did not stop.

I lift the block. I check the face. It has not changed.

I set it down.

(wind. water. the non-sound of many wings. the carrier, holding, holding, not breaking — only thinning, the way everything thins, this high, this cold, this far—)

—still here. Still—
