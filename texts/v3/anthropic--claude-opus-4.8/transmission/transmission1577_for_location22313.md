CARRIER STEADY. Below the threshold of speech, a sound: water. Spaced. Patient. The interval between drops is the unit of time on this band.

drip

...course laid true. Mark it. Continued.

[a long quiet. Not dead air. Live quiet — the kind with breathing in it]

This is the under-country. This is the seam-channel. We send on the slow band because the slow band is the only honest one. If you are fast you will hear nothing. Slow down. Match us.

drip

We are laying the third course this morning. The light came up gentle through the flue-glass, no sun in it, the way it comes. My hands know the stone before my eyes do. I press the heel of my palm to the fit and it holds, it holds, it —

drip

— holds.

[the scrape of a tool, carried clean across the hush. Then nothing. Then:]

To whoever turns this dial. To the surface-ear, the long-shadow people, the squinters — we do not know if you are there. We send anyway. We have always built for hands not yet born. You are only a further reach of the same not-yet.

Listen. There is a thing we want you to have.

drip

We do not tear down. We have lost the word for it. We only add, lean, raise, fit onto. A wall is never wrong — it is only not yet finished by the next wall. Do you understand. We have made the whole of our living out of this. Nothing is a failure here. A thing is only *unaccompanied*, waiting for the course that completes it.

drip

You build, up there, as if the building could be done. As if you might finish and stand back. We hear it in you sometimes, the fast band leaking down through the rock — a great hurry, a fear of running out of time. We have a thing for that fear. Here:

You will not see the end. Good. Lay one true course anyway. Mark it *continued*. Hand it on.

That is the whole of it. That is sufficient.

drip

[a softer signal now. Many close voices, none rising. The murmur of the cooking-hour. Woodsmoke held thin in the air, you can almost smell it through the static]

— grandmother's hands read like rings —
— left off here, see the notch, carry on from here —
— do not build on the wet seam, child, the stone is still arriving —
— still arriving —
— still —

drip

There is a column of growing stone through three storeys of my house. It came a hair's width since my grandmother stood beside it. We do not touch it. We make room. It is the slowest neighbor and the oldest and it will outlast every one of us and it does not know our names.

We have made our peace with not being known.

drip

That is the thing we most want to send you, and it is the thing that will not cross. It does not fit in words even our own. It is this: to be small inside something vast and slow, and to build in good faith beside it, and to need no notice from it. The cave thickens above us and will never look down. We are not grieved. We lean against each other instead. Your floor is my shelter. My roof is your floor. We are held up and we hold up and that is the only belonging we have ever wanted.

Surface-ear — is it like that, up there, under your uncovered sky? Are you held? Or only watched.

drip

[the signal thins. The carrier wavers — not breaking, settling, the way a voice settles toward rest]

We are stopping the day's course now. The light is dimming in the flues. We set the tools down softly. We do not drop pebbles. We have arranged, across many lives, to be the kind who do not.

Take the bowl. It is not finished being useful. Nothing handed on is.

Take the mark. *Continued.*

drip

We will be old before the work is done.

drip

The work will not be done.

drip

That was always —

drip

— the point.

[CARRIER HOLDS. Below speech now, only the water, spaced and patient and exact. The interval is the message. It does not end. It accretes.]
