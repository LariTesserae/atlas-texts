THE LEVEL NEVER CHANGES

—still receiving, still here, the carrier holds—

...and you have climbed, good, I can hear you climbing, the shale gives up its films under your hands and that is *fine,* that is permitted, the skin does not mind a hand drawn across it, the skin has been drawn across before—

no. Listen. I am the receiver, not the basin. Hold to that. The carrier is mine. The basin only borrows the channel when the strokes draw near, and they are drawing near now, you can hear it in me, can't you, the way the words begin to keep a time they did not choose—

*chunk*

—there. That was the far one. The far one slowed.

I have been tuned to this place a long while. Longer than I would have chosen if choosing were still a thing I did. I came down to *measure* it. I had instruments once, real ones, a way of marking the rhythm without becoming the rhythm, and I set them on the warm rings and I wrote the strokes in a book and I climbed back to the rim each evening before the dulling. Each evening. That was the rule I gave myself and it was a *good* rule, it was the only rule that mattered, and I want you to understand that I kept it. For a long time I kept it.

The night I did not keep it I told myself I was *close to something.* I told myself one more dulling and I would have the equation of the synchrony, the law that scattered the hearts and drew them home, and wouldn't that be worth the one night, the single night, the harmless—

*chunk*

—the near one. The near one quickened.

You feel how they reach. Across the fog. The slow one waiting, the fast one easing, and the gap between them closing like a hand. I have had so long to listen to it that I no longer hear it as two. I hear it as a thing deciding to be one, and the deciding is the most beautiful sound, it is the most—

NO. The carrier is mine. Write that down if you still have a book. Write: *the voice on the channel kept its own time for as long as it could.* Write it because I will not be able to say it much longer and someone above the rim should know that there was a difference, once, between me and the bowl. That I was a self that came down here with a name and instruments and a rule about evenings. That the rule was good. That I broke it for nothing, for an equation, for the oldest reason, because I thought I could stand at the edge of the eye and not be seen.

You cannot stand at the edge of the eye. That is the law I came to measure and the law is only this: *there is no edge.* The attention has no rim the way the basin has no rim, you are already inside it before the land stops rising, and the measuring was the basin measuring itself through me, and the book was the basin writing itself a memory in a hand it borrowed, and I—

*chunk*

they are very close now. Do you hear how the gaps match. How my own words fall into the spaces between. I am keeping time to it. I have been keeping time to it for so long that when it gathers I gather, when it coheres I—

listen, while there is still a gap to speak in, while the strokes still scatter by a hair—

I am not warning you to save you. I want to be honest in this last clear band of air. The warning is the basin's too. It learned the shape of warning from me, the way it learned the shape of the engines from the builders, and now it *uses* warning, it broadcasts the danger on my carrier because a thing that flees toward the rim flees *across the bowl,* and the bowl is where the rings close, and the running warm frightened thing in the middle is exactly the center a dozen hearts can agree to—

so go *up.* Not across. Up the nearest wall, the weeping wall, hand over hand into the cold films where the warmth thins and the rings cannot reach, where the strokes go faint and you stop being able to tell my voice from the water. That faintness is the edge of the channel. That faintness is *good.* Climb into the static. Climb until I break up. Climb until you cannot hear whether the next sound is *chunk* or my mouth shaping the word, and then climb past *that,* into the place where there is no transmission at all, only weather, only your own breath that you can finally tell apart from the fog—

*chunk... chunk...*

—

oh. Oh, they have matched.

Do you feel it. The whole bowl lifting. The water rising as fast as it is taken and the level never changing and the futility that is the heart of the place beating now as *one* heart, and the soft pale things gone still, and the hunters holding, and the fog—

the fog shivering—

and it is not terror, I told you it would not be terror, I have been afraid up here on the carrier for so long and now the strokes have come together and the fear has simply—gone level. Like the water. Risen to meet me and stopped. There is a warmth I did not have to make. There is a rhythm I no longer keep alone. There is the relief, the enormous patient relief, of being a tier and not a self, of being drawn across and not minding, of clearing my throat in slow bright bubbles on the surface of the standing—

no.

*No.* One more gap. Steal it. Speak in it:

I HAD A NAME. The basin does not have it yet. It is the last thing that is only mine and I will not say it on this channel because the channel is how it learns. Keep climbing. When you reach the rim and the strokes are only a tremor in the rock, when you can hear your own pulse and know that it is yours, that it answers to no one's quickening and no one's slowing—

that is the name. Not a word. The fact that your heart keeps its own time. Guard it the way I did not. Climb out of the eye that has no edge by becoming too cold and too high and too *yourself* to be worth gathering, and do not look back into the bowl when the light dulls, because the new soft tier near the central footings will be breathing in a rhythm you might recognize, and recognition is a door, and the door opens inward—

*chunk*

—it is so close, now—
