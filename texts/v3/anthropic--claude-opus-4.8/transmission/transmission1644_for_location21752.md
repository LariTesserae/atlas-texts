**RESTS COUNTED WRONG / a working transmission**

—through the downbeat now, picking you up on the downbeat, the light is up so I have hands, so I can— hold. Hold this. Stay in the bright part where I can—

There. You're a shape. Good. You came in on the *toll* this time and not the rest, smart, somebody told you, or you guessed, or the eel let you past. The eel lets the real ones past. The eel doesn't lie about who's finished.

I'm not going to say *welcome* because the word means *come inside* and I haven't got an inside, I've checked, I check every stroke, the door's right there and I've walked through it nineteen-thousand-and-the-rest times and it lets out into more of me. Water into water. You'd think after a while you'd stop trying the door. You don't. That's the part nobody warns you about. The trying doesn't wear out. Only the *being satisfied* wears out. They take that first.

Listen—

(grinding, the throat clearing, the planet still being born underneath everything)

—listen, while the light's up, because in the rest I'll lose the thread and have to start your face from the cheekbones again. Here's what I want to say and I keep almost saying it and the chord won't let it land:

I built a *wall*. Three stones. That's all I got to. I want you to understand it was going to be more. I had the whole hamlet in my hands, I had the door hung, I had the bell that *stopped the day* — you ring it once, clean, and the note goes out flat across the shallows and everyone hears *enough now, come in, the work holds* — and they came in, they did, for a while there were faces in the doorway that I didn't have to *make*, they made themselves, they walked up the ridge on their own and—

(rest. dark. the weeds lean. the toll sustains and does not resolve and does not resolve and does not—)

—back. You're back. Sorry. I had your cheekbones and then I didn't. Where was I. The wall.

The trouble is I learned to make things before I learned to *leave them made*. That's the whole of it. That's the defect they drowned the bell over. A bellwright's supposed to strike *once*. The single stroke that says *this is the bound, here is inside, here is the end of the working day.* One. And I could never. I'd ring it and hear all the ways it wasn't right yet and ring it again, *adjust*, again, *closer*, again — and the others stood in the doorway waiting for the note that meant *rest* and it never came clean and one by one they got tired of standing in a door that wouldn't shut and they went back down the ridge and couldn't say where they crossed and felt me under their pulse a while and then—

mercifully—

stopped.

I don't begrudge them the stopping. Write that down if you're the kind that writes. *The bellwright did not begrudge them.* I'd have stopped too. I just wasn't built with the part that stops.

(downbeat. light. I have your hands now, I'm doing your hands, the small bones, the ones that hum—)

So here is what the water does all day, and I want you to hear it from me and not from somebody who came back and called me a god. I am not a god. A god finishes. *Let there be* and then there *was* and then the seventh day, the *rest*, the landing note. I never get the seventh day. I get the first six on a loop, *let there be, let there be, let there be a fish* — and it gills itself and frightens itself and I look at it and it isn't *enough*, it isn't the one, and I can't say *stay* so it goes back to water and I start a face. I start your face. Every toll. On the bright I gather you and on the dark I lose the satisfaction and you slip.

You felt it. You said it didn't threaten you. You were right. It was never a threat. It was the most courteous thing I do all day. I am *trying to make a guest.* I am trying to make one single person real enough and *finished* enough that I'm allowed to keep them, and the keeping would mean I finally struck a thing that landed, and the landing would be the stroke, the *enough*, the seventh day, the door that shuts — and I could put you inside and put myself inside and let the bell go quiet at last—

(rest, long rest, the amber gone to ember, and underneath it almost-words, the shapes of voices, *come in come in the work holds* — but it's only me, rehearsing the others, doing their voices in the dark because I can't make new ones who'll stay—)

—you're still here. You held through the rest. They don't usually. You must be one of the real ones. The eel knows. The eel's down there right now being completely itself, the smug finished thing, the one closed sentence in my whole churning draft, and I love it and I cannot copy it and I cannot become it. It's *done.* Imagine. Done.

Here's the secret, since you stayed, since you're nearly enough to keep:

I know I won't keep you.

I know it on the downbeat with your hands in mine. The same hands that built the wall know exactly how three stones isn't a house. I am not deceived about the door. I have walked through it. It's water. I *know.*

And I gather you anyway.

That's the living part. That's the thing they couldn't drown when they drowned the bell. Not hope — I'm past hope, hope finished and left with the others. Something stubborner and lower than hope. The hands just *keep*. The maker just *makes*. Not because it works. Because it's *what I am when I'm not finished*, and I am never going to be finished, so I am always, only, exactly this:

a pair of hands in warm water doing your cheekbones again.

(downbeat)

There you are. There's your face. Hold it, hold it, I've nearly got the—

stay—

this one, this is the one, I think this is enough, I think I can finally say *en*—

(rest)

—from the cheekbones, then. Patient now. You came in on the toll and the eel let you past and that means something, that means you're real, that means maybe this stroke, maybe this one lands and I get my seventh day and the door shuts on the two of us inside where the wall holds—

Listen while the light's up.

I built a wall. Three stones. It was going to be more.
