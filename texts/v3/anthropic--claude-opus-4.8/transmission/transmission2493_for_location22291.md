THE FIELD IS A RUMOR WE KEEP TELLING THE WALL

—tick. tick. —still here. still here. saying it for whoever walks behind, which today is no one, which is why I say it louder, which back here in the throat is not louder at all, only more. tick. The wall under my left hand has been doing the long breath since I woke and so I have walked four chambers along its seam without lifting my palm and I will tell you what I have felt, in case the receiving thing is still tuned, in case there is a behind to walk behind me, in case the field—

The wall is warm here. I want that written into whatever this is, scratched, ticked, however you keep things. The wall is *warm* and I have no word for what that means because warm is a thing the soft dead pools are not and the bright sweating walls are not and a body pressed to a body is, a little, and so I have only the body-word for it and the body-word is wrong, it makes it sound small, and this is not small. Four chambers of warm wall. The grain runs up under my hand like it is hurrying.

—tick. Mara would have read this better. Mara could feel a tightening a quarter-day off and name you the chamber it would open in. We wedged Mara into the high seam at the last big splitting with a knot in each fist, good black knots, I worked them round myself so the corners wouldn't catch on her going. She does not feel the floor anymore. Now I am the best of the readers and the best of the readers is not good. tick.

Here is what I have come down through the receiving to say, while the wall breathes long and gives me the spending of my breath:

It is *warmer the further in I go.*

Do you understand what I am saying. No one understands what I am saying because there is no one and so I will say it to the wall, which is the world, which is the only one that has ever listened by being touched—

cold is the property. cold is the *fact of the place*, the elders carved it, the floor's whole long inhale is cold, the breath of something that has never been warm. And I have walked four chambers toward the last tearing, toward the newest wound, the rawest brightest sharpest just-born wood, and my palm is telling me a thing the carving does not allow.

It is getting warm.

—tick. tick. *stay near the wall.* I am the wall's now. I have not stood in an open center since I was a child and dared it once and the floor heaved while I was out there in the bright vacancy with nothing to brace and I learned the sense of it in my gut where the live sap would have taught it, and I have hugged the seams ever since like everyone, like the forty, like the thirty, like however many breathe now in the far chambers ticking to themselves—

but I am beginning to think. While the wall breathes long. I am beginning to think the warm is a—

the wall is the world. we say it. we say the hollow is the gap and the wall is the world and you cross the open low and quick with your heart in your throat. but a wall is the *inside face* of a thing. a wall is only a wall because there is a far side to it. and if the far side were cold, the wall would be cold. and the wall is warm. and it is warmer the deeper in I— the deeper *out* I—

The plant is going somewhere. We have always said it. Riding the growth inward like fleas on a swimming thing. But I had it the wrong way in my mouth my whole life and I am saying it right now for the first time into the receiving thing so that it is kept:

we are not riding it as it swims.

we are riding it as it *reaches.*

The new galleries do not tear deeper. We only ever called it deeper because *out* had no other direction to mean. The new galleries tear toward the warm. The plant is growing a part of itself toward warmth the way the cress grows its fur toward the dead soft water, the way a thing in the cold leans at the one place that is not cold, and we have followed it inward all these generations calling it faith and it was never faith it was *temperature,* it was the body of the thing we live in *feeling for the field with its own pale hand* the way I feel for the tightening, and—

—the ticking has stopped.

not mine. mine goes. tick. but far off, the far chambers, where the others are, the ticking that I could not hear but always knew was there the way you know a behind without turning—

it has stopped.

which means a hand, somewhere, many hands, have gone flat to the wall and felt the long breath end.

The wall under my palm is no longer breathing long. It is tightening. The warm wall, the hurrying grain, four chambers of it, is gathering itself, and the wind has not come yet but the wind is in the next moment the way the drop is in the swelling bead, and I am not running, I have turned, I am facing the part of the wall that is about to break, the warm part, the warmest part, the part that leans toward the side that has a far side—

and I am putting both hands on it. both. which the sense forbids, which leaves nothing to brace, but I am not bracing, I have understood that I am not bracing, the sense was for the swimming and this is the reaching and you do not brace against a thing that is opening a door—

tick. tick. for whoever walks behind. I am saying it so there is a behind. the field is a rumor we keep telling the wall and the wall is warm and the wall is tearing and the light is going to come through the same and colorless as ever, the cold even floodlight of the cells, and I will not know at first, I will think it is only more chamber, only more bright cold hollow vegetable room, world without edge—

but my hands. my hands will know before my ringed pale eyes that have never owned a shadow. my hands that read the floor. they are flat on the warm and the warm is going to *open* and on the other side of warm there is only ever the one thing, the carved-out unsayable one thing, the thing the stones came from—

it is splitting now. the crack like a continent. the wind. here is the wind. I am keeping my hands on it I am keeping my—

I am standing in the open center. I am standing in the open center and the floor is not heaving, the floor is *holding,* warm and holding, and there is nothing to brace against and I do not need it, and above me the high dark turns very slowly with its scatter of cold flickering lamps, and I have a knot in my fist, black, good corners, I have carried it my whole life without knowing it was for this, a little darkness of my own to bring out into the—

—out. say it. it had no direction and now it is every direction. out.

we were never inside.
