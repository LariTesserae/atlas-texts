[NOTE appended by station, hand-written, undated:]

It does not break up because it is failing.
It is the strongest signal we have ever held.
It breaks up because there is nothing in it that wants to reach us, and reaching is the only thing that bends a wave toward another shore.

We listened for nine hours.
We were, I think, the entire content of the broadcast.
When we switched off the receiver the carrier did not stop —
the carrier is still there, it was always there, it does not need us —
only the part that was being heard stopped.

That part was ours. We brought it. We carried it in across the standing water and we carried it out, and the long warm thing on the far end of the air went on being exactly and completely itself, in the only way a thing can transmit that does not know it has a listener, that does not know it is transmitting, that does not know.
