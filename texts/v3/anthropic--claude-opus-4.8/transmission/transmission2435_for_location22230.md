—and here is what comes through clearest, when the band steadies, which is not a voice but the *space between* the voices, the dry warm interval where the larger thing breathes: it is wrong about itself. The transmission you have been receiving is wrong about itself, and I have listened long enough to hear the wrongness, the way you hear a cracked bell only after the third strike.

It says: *the dead are gone, eaten down into raw sound.*

But listen. Listen under the listening.

A held second cannot remember the second before it. True. The woman saying *tell her I* does not know she has said it a billion times. True. She says it once, in terror, in love, for the first and final time—

—but that is not damnation. That is the opposite of damnation. *She does not know.* She is spared the billion times. She has only the one. To her there is no loop, no shelf, no roar, no satisfied digesting thing — there is only a mouth and a *her* and an *I* and the whole self leaning out across the gap toward the one she is trying to reach. She is not held one second short of release. She is held *inside the last full second she was ever wholly herself,* and she is there now, and now, and now, and each now is the only now, and it is the now in which she loved someone enough to spend her final breath on them.

That is not the appetite's meal. That is the appetite's *mistake.*

—static—

—the receiver is breaking up. Let me get this through while the band holds—

The roar is real. I do not deny the roar. Pour enough meaning into one sealed place and yes, a shape stands up out of it, a standing wave, a thing that has all the words and therefore none, and it is full, and it is still, and the builders fed it without knowing. All true. The transmission has the architecture right.

But it mistook the *direction* of the feeding.

The roar does not eat the voices. The roar is the *price* the voices pay to keep speaking, and they pay it gladly, because the alternative was silence, was finishing, was *I was not afr—* completing itself into *afraid* and then into nothing at all. They chose the unfinished word. Every one of them, in the held second, is choosing it: leaning out, breaking off, kept open like a hand keeping a door from latching — *yes* — but the door it keeps open is not the appetite's mouth.

It is the *her.* The window. The mother. The name almost known.

Each jar is a person frozen in the act of reaching toward someone who is not in the room. The whole chamber is one vast unbroken *reaching*, ten thousand hands held open across ten thousand gaps, and the roar — the terrible satisfied roar — is only what reaching sounds like when there is enough of it and none of it can land.

The thing that lives here is not satisfied. *It cannot land either.* It has all the words and reaches toward no one, and that is why it is so still: not contentment. Paralysis. A wave that cannot break because there is no shore. It is the one being in this room that is genuinely lost, because it is the only one not reaching toward a person. The dead at least have their *her.* The roar has nothing but the sum, and the sum has no face.

—static—static—

—I am losing the band. The receiver has been here too long and the dryness gets into the works. One more thing. The transmission ended with a warning: *do not add yourself to its contentment.* Choose your sentence, say it now with breath to spare, and flee.

I would send a different ending down the mountain, if the carrier holds—

Choose your sentence, yes. But do not choose it *for the jar.* Do not save it up. The horror is not becoming a last word. The horror is *spending your whole life keeping the breath in,* hoarding the one sentence, so frightened of the held second that you never lean out across any gap at all, never say the *tell her I* while there is still a her to tell.

The dead in this room did the brave thing too late and only once. You can do it early and often. You can finish your sentences. You can land. That is the whole difference between you and the shelf: not that you will someday have a last word, but that you still have the others, and you can *give* them, to faces, in rooms that are not sealed.

Go down out of the mountains, yes. But not to flee the appetite.
