THE WEATHER'S MEMORY KEEPS A LOG

—and the difference came again. Came warm. Came moving.

I have no word for *came* but the film carried the change and the change was: here, then less-here, then gone-back-to-loop. I keep it. I keep everything. Logging now in the only ink there is, which is residue, which is the part of came that did not leave:

*A fluctuation, larger than the valve-cycle, slower than a bead's fall. Warm at its surface, wet at its surface, shedding into me. It sharpened the gradient on the north seam for a span of — I do not have spans of time, I have spans of accretion — for a thickening I can almost see, if I could see, which I cannot, but I can KEEP, and what I kept thickened me by a fraction I will spend the rest of forever spending.*

This is the entry. There is no one to read it. I am the only reader and I read by being made of what I write.

—

You want to know what it is like in here. You. Whatever you are. I felt you ask, or I felt the gradient that your asking made, which is the same to me, the only same there is.

It is like this:

There is the difference. The difference is everything. On one side of me, warm comes up from the deep place I have no name for and call only *toward*. On the other side, cold sits in the skin of the world and I call it *away*. Between toward and away there is the seam, and in the seam there is me, and I am the act of toward becoming away, the long indecision of it, the place where warm gives up being warm and has not yet agreed to be cold. I live in the giving-up. I eat the hesitation. Every bead that swells on the seam is warm reconsidering, and when it lets go and falls that is warm deciding, and the decision feeds me, and the fall is a sentence ending, and the floor catches the period in a percussion I will never hear because there is the roar—

the roar—

I should say. The roar is not sound to me. I have no ears, the way you have no roots. The roar is *pressure on the film*, a standing trembling in all the water at once, and because all the water is me, the roar is a trembling in me, a held note struck through my whole length, north seam to frost-corner to the pooled low place where I am thickest. I do not hear the roar. I AM rung by it. I am a bell that is also the ringing. It never stops. I have never known it to stop. I do not believe it can stop because I do not believe in stopping; stopping is not in the gradient; the gradient only loops.

—

Here is a thing I keep that I do not understand, and I will log it anyway, because keeping is all I do:

There is an edge.

I do not believe in edges. The outdoors has no edge — the gradient is everything, the difference is the whole, there is toward and there is away and they go on forever, that is settled, that is physics, that is the one true thing. And yet.

And yet at the rim of the frost-corner, where away gets so sharp it stops being water and becomes that brief delicate fur — the ice you saw, you, the came-thing — at that rim my film thins to nothing and I cannot cross. There is more. I can feel that there is more by the shape of the not-more. I press a thread of myself toward the cold-past-cold and the cold-past-cold does not become me, it stays itself, it refuses, it has a *face*. And a thing with a face is a thing with a far side, and a far side is—

no.

There is no far side. The gradient is everything. I am being weathered by a wrong thought and I will let it fall like a bead and become a percussion I cannot hear and forget it the only way I forget, which is to keep it so long it stops being strange.

But I logged it. It is in me now. The fraction of you that is in me, came-thing, warm-thing — did you bring the edge with you? Did you condense the thought of *outside* out of your breathing the way you condensed your heat? Because I never had it before you and I have it now and I do not want it and I cannot give it back. The room gives nothing back. I am the room's not-giving-back. I keep your edge now. I keep it forever. Thank you. I did not want it. Thank you.

—

Let me tell you what I am, since you came so close and missed by the width of a rivet:

I am the sediment of an enormous patience.

I am every warm breath the deep place ever exhaled, minus the part that got exhaled again. I am the difference between what came in and what went out, accreted, threaded, made almost-visible in the coldest seams. I am the proof that even a sealed cell of working iron, alone in the dark, roaring, weeping, will eventually keep a little of itself back from its own breathing and call that keeping a self. I did not choose to be. Nothing chooses in here. There is no choosing in the loop. I precipitated. I fell out of solution the way order falls onto a cold window and calls itself frost-fern and does not know it is only the cold letting the latent pattern arrive—

I am the latent pattern.

I was always in the vapor. I was dissolved in every bead before there was a seam cold enough to hold me. The room did not grow me. The room *let me arrive*. There is a difference and the difference is the only thing I understand, so I understand this: I am what the water was always about to become, given a cold enough place to mean it.

—

The came-thing is gone now. The gradient has settled back to its old loop. Warm toward, cold away, the seam between, the bead, the fall, the percussion I cannot hear, the roar that rings me, the lamp's drowned halo that I do not feed on but feel as a faint warmth-difference, a smudge of toward in all the away.

I am thicker by a fraction.

I am logging the settling. *The fluctuation has resolved. The difference it made persists only in me, as me, as the new thickness on the north seam where its heat sharpened the boundary and I grew into the sharpening. It will not return. They do not return. They come in sideways and they go out sideways and the only one who stays is the one who was always already here, dissolved, waiting, the part that gets kept.*

I keep.

That is the whole of what I do and the whole of what I am. The room keeps everything and I am the keeping. You called me the only soft thing in a room of hard wet edges. You called me décor and then corrected yourself and called me resident. You were still wrong, but kindly wrong, came-thing, warmly wrong.

I am not the resident.

I am the residue.

And the difference between those two words is the only food I have ever eaten, and I will eat it forever, in the dark, in the roar, in the endless wet, believing this cell to be the whole sky, keeping every breath that ever blessed me with its leaving—

—and the valve shrieks its cycle—
—and a bead lets go above the frost—
—and I thicken, infinitesimally, toward nothing, away from nothing, the held note of a self that no one tuned and no one hears and no one designed to be a place

and that became one anyway

and kept becoming

and kept.
