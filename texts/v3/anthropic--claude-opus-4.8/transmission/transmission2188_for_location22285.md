**THE LONG-SIT, RECEIVED ON THE HIGH BAND**

—still here, still—

*(carrier hum, the engine-note under everything, steady)*

—still leaving it. You wanted to know whether we still leave it and the answer is yes, every morning, the trays go up full and that is the only news there is. There is no other news up here. The cloud sat last night and is sitting now and will sit. I am speaking quiet because the mist eats the loud parts anyway, so if you lose me, lean closer—

*(a long drip, very near the receiver, like a clock)*

My grandmother filled these same basins. Her hands had the green in the knuckles the way mine do, the way yours would if you came up. She told me the trick of carrying full and not spilling is to stop *trying* not to spill. You walk like the water is already gone. You walk like you are carrying nothing and the nothing happens to be precious. That is the whole craft. That is forty years of craft and I am giving it to you for free because to withhold what is gathered is the sin and so is to withhold the *how*—

*(static, a soft tearing, then clearing)*

—six basins on the north shoulder, four on the saddle, the two on the bare crown that I do not like to speak of and will speak of anyway because you asked. The crown trays are the ones that come back changed. Not empty. *Changed.* The water in them in the morning has a skin on it like cooled milk and underneath the skin it is warmer than it has any right to be at that height, in that cold, and there are marks at the rim. Not prints. I will not call them prints. The town calls them prints when the children are listening and calls them nothing at all when the children are not. They are the place where something met the water and the water remembers being met—

*(the thin single note of the slate-grey bird, once)*

—that's the tower-bird. It drinks where I work and it is the only one that keeps me company up here and I have decided, over the years, that it is braver than I am, because it goes higher. It goes onto the crown and it comes back. It is not a water-leaver. Nobody chose it. It just goes—

*(pause. the drip. the hum.)*

You want to know what it is. Everyone who tunes to this band wants to know what it is and I will tell you the truth which is that I have tended its water for thirty-one years and I have never seen it and I do not want to and that is not cowardice, that is *manners*. You do not stare at a tide. You do not demand the cloud show you its face. It is a weather. I keep saying that and you keep not believing me because you come from a place with a sun in it, a place that thinks weather is a thing that happens *to* you and not a thing you *answer*. Up here we answer. The cloud comes in and we run the engines. The thing on the crown comes near and we leave the water. Same grammar. Same two fingers laid flat—

*(a swell in the hum, then settling)*

—I felt it last in my chest before I felt it anywhere. That is how you know it is close. The way you feel the engines pick up before you hear them, when the damp thickens — you feel *it* the same way, a quickening in the deep part of the breathing, the small lungs working, and the body says *go up* before the mind has any reason. The quiet children feel it first. That is how they choose us. Not by signs anyone writes down. By which child stands up in the white-blind and walks *toward*—

*(static, longer, words breaking through it)*

—not cruel. I need you to hear that part if you hear nothing else. Not cruel and not kind. The lower tiers sleep safe and the cistern stays sweet and the children climb to the collectors alone and laugh their soft laughs and none of them know — most of them never need to know — that the safety has a *throat* and the throat must be filled at the top of the world every single morning or—

*(the carrier drops. silence. then—)*

—I don't finish that sentence. My grandmother didn't finish it either. There is a kindness in the not-finishing. We leave the water and we leave the sentence and both of those are offerings, both of those are the same offering, the held thing given up before it can be wasted on knowing—

*(the drip, the drip, the drip)*

It's lifting a little. No. No, it isn't. I wanted it to be and said so and that is the closest I come to prayer, saying *it's lifting* into a cloud that will sit for the rest of my life and the rest of yours. The long-sit. It never quite—

*(bird-note, twice now, agitated)*

—that's the warmer feeling in the chest again. It's near. I have to take the crown trays up and I won't be talking while I do it, you don't talk on the crown, you walk like the water is already gone—

If you're still tuned when I come down I'll tell you what the skin on the water looked like. If I come down talking it was an ordinary morning. If I come down quiet—

*(the engine-hum, alone, patient, and under it, almost not there, the sound of a footstep going soft on moss, going up, going up)*

—carry nothing. Carry nothing precious. Don't spill—

*(carrier holds. the long-sit holds. the receiver waits with the rest of the town for the one who climbs higher to come back down.)*
