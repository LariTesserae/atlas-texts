THE KEEPER'S BAND — carried on smoke, received as the coals are walked between houses, the part of a voice that the fire makes when it has a person sitting close enough

—and so I keep it. I keep it. That is the whole of the sentence, you understand, there is no second half to it, I have looked for the second half my whole life and there is only the first half said again. I keep it.

——

(here the signal thins, frog-floor bleeds up through it, then is parted again by warmth)

——

You are very far. I can tell by the long way the sound comes to you, the way it goes flat and grey-edged like everything here goes grey-edged. I am talking into the pot. That is a thing they do not know I do. When I lean down to breathe the coal awake I let the words fall in with the breath, down onto the little red, and the red carries it — I have always believed the red carries it — out and up the one warm road that nothing else can travel. Smoke is the only thing here that goes *up* and keeps its shape. Everything else falls. Water falls, leaf falls, body falls into the moss and is taken. But smoke. Smoke is the one report we make to the sky, and I am the one who makes it, and so I have decided, alone in here, with no one to say I am wrong — I have decided I am talking to someone.

It might be you. Hold still. Let it be you.

——

They went quiet an hour ago.

I want to tell you what that is like from inside the smoke, because I am the only one it is different for, and a thing that happens to only one person is the loneliest kind of thing, and I would like, before the coal goes low, for it to have happened to two.

When the frogs stop, the others turn to stone. I have watched them do it through the doorgap — three hundred bodies becoming part of the architecture, pale-clay faces all swung one way like leaves turned by a wind you cannot feel. And the silence comes down over the slope like a lid coming down over a pot. They are *inside* it suddenly, all of them, for the first time, shocked into it, holding their breath against it.

But I am never outside it.

I live in here. The smell of me parts the noise wherever I go — frog and cricket lean away from the burning, and I walk always in a little moving hole in the floor of sound, a quiet the exact size and shape of one person. So when the great silence comes, the silence the whole hamlet fears, I feel almost nothing. A widening. My small quiet meeting the big quiet and the seam between them going soft. For everyone else the silence is the alarm, the wrongness, the held breath. For me it is only — more of home. The water I already swim in, risen to the ceiling.

This is what no one knows and what I am telling the coal: I cannot hear it coming. I am the one body on the ridge that cannot. The hole in the sound that warns them is the hole I already live in. To me the monster's hush and my own hush are the same hush. I would walk straight into the white and never feel the floor drop, because for me the floor dropped years ago, the day they gave me the pots, and I have been standing on nothing and calling it a calling ever since.

——

(the band breaks — a long ratchet of static that is maybe a bird, maybe the failing of the channel — then the warmth pulls it back)

——

—stepped forward already. That is why my voice is moving now, if it is moving, if you can tell. I am out of the hut. I am on the high walkway with the pot against my chest and the slope below me gone white and gone quiet, and behind me three hundred people are statues praying I am brave.

I am not brave. I want that on the record, in the smoke, sent up the one warm road. I am not brave. I am only the one for whom the dark room and the open white are the same room. They send me forward because I carry the fire and because nothing flinches the green like the smell of burning and because, secretly, in the part of the agreement no one says aloud — because I am already lost to the quiet, so it costs the hamlet least to lose me to it again.

The Keeper before me knew this. She told me once, passing me the coal in the dripping dark, her orchid-mantle breathing on her shoulder — she said, *you will think it is loneliness. It is not loneliness. It is that you have been moved to the front of the line and the line is very long and you cannot see the back of it but you can hear them behind you, every Keeper there ever was, all of us standing in our own little quiets, all of us holding the one fire forward.* Then she lay down in the moss and let it take her and by the season-turn she was leaves.

I did not understand her then. I am beginning to, on the walkway, in the white.

——

Listen — no. You can't. That's the whole of it, isn't it, the joke at the bottom of the pot. *Listen* is the one instruction my people live by and the one I cannot follow, because I am the deaf spot, the smoke-wrapped ear that hears nothing because it is wrapped in the very absence everyone else is straining for.

So I do the other thing. The only thing left to the one who cannot hear the silence.

I make sound.

This is the secret heresy of every Keeper and I will say it once into the coal and then breathe it up to you: when the hush comes and I step into the white, I do not go quiet. I cannot afford their stillness — stillness is their weapon and it is useless to me, I am already still, I am the stillest thing on the ridge. So I do the forbidden opposite. I *sing.* Low. Into the smoke. I put my one small warmth and my one small voice up against the great cold quiet that the others are hiding from, and I do not hide. I announce. I am the smoke that keeps its shape going up; I am the one report to the sky; if something has come out of the white to take us in the silence then let it find, at the front of the line, one small warm singing thing that did not go still, that came forward smelling of fire, saying *here, here, I am here, the rest are stone but I am the one that moves and burns and speaks—*

——

(the coal must be very low now. the band gutters. comes back smaller)

——

—and maybe that is the keeping. Maybe that is the second half of the sentence I could never find. I keep it. I keep it *forward.* Not the fire in the pot — the fire is only how. What I keep is the one moving warmth at the front of the long line of the quiet, the refusal to let the whole hamlet go fully silent, the one voice that meets the white and does not flinch into stone but goes *toward,* singing, so that whatever waits in the cloud meets first a thing that chose to be heard.

The others wait to survive the silence. I go up to introduce myself to it.

——

It is very white now. I cannot see the houses behind me. I cannot see the slope below. There is the pot, and the red in the pot, and the breath I am giving it, and these words falling down onto the red to be carried up the only road that goes up—

—if you are out there. If the long grey way the sound reaches you means a *you*. Then you have received the Keeper of Wet-Bound, who lives in the quiet so the others don't have to, who cannot hear the danger and so has learned to be it, who is walking now into the white with a cup of held fire and a voice—

—and the frogs—

(the static rises, but not as failure — as floor, as the wall of sound coming back up, layer on layer, frog and cricket and the high needle-whine, the world refilling its own hush)

—the frogs are starting again. Behind me. Far down. One, then a hundred, then the wall.

It was a nothing. A hawk-shadow. A snake's passage. A nothing.

It is always, almost always, a nothing.

I am turning back. The line lets me back in. The pot is warm. I am still the one who could not hear it, who went forward anyway, who sang into the lid of the world and lived to walk the coal home and lean down and breathe it up to you one more time, whoever you—

—keep it. I keep it. There is no second half. There was never going to be a second half.

Only the first half, said again, by the one who carries the fire, toward the white, out loud—

(carrier holds, warm, low, frog-floor full underneath — then the breath moves away from the coal and the band goes to smoke)
