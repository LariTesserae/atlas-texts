THE LOWER NOTE — CONTINUOUS

—still here, still here, still the C below the C the makers cut, the one they cut first, the one they cut deepest because it had to hold the rest. I am laid against it. Have been laid against it. The flat in it is a hair, less than a hair, the width of one season's water and I am narrowing the groove on its high lip, sparing the low, sparing, sparing—

(drip. up and to the left, oculus-fall, struck true. good. one note kept for free.)

—and the work is not slow, the work is exactly as fast as it can be, which is the speed of taking-away, which is the only speed I have. I have no adding in me. I was not made with adding. I was made—

no. I was not made. The piers were made. I came after, into the after, into the runs the makers dug for the water and forgot were also runs for a body. They dug a throat and did not know they were also digging the thing that lives in a throat. I am the swallow they did not mean. I am the patience in the drain.

(slide. brace on the paddle-stub. the current shifts a tile somewhere east and a whole quarter of the ceiling goes a hair sour and the criers there thin out, thin out, two throats fall silent and I feel the silence open like cold and I cannot be in two piers at once, I cannot, the east will have to wait, the east will go quiet a while, I am sorry, I am sorry to the makers I cannot read, I am one body and the score is the whole room—)

Here is the thing I know that the simmering ones do not, that the singing ones do not:

it is going flat everywhere, always, all at once.

Every pier. Every groove. The whole made world tilting by a hair toward the wrong, forever, and there is one of me. There have always been some number of me but the number is never enough, was never enough, the makers built more conversation than tuners to keep it, they built a thing that needs more devotion than the dark can grow. So I choose. Every uncoiling is a choosing. This pier and not that one. This note kept and that note let go soft. I am keeping the room *mostly* true. I am keeping it *enough*. The criers sing across the gaps I leave and the gaps move as I move and the simmering ones crowd into my wake and call it safety and it is safety, it is, it is the only safety, it is the shape of my choosing read backward by the eyeless—

(eat. one. it was in the wake-edge, the silent lip of the untuned pier I am coming to next, and it was there and I was hungry and the work is long. forgive. there is no forgive. there is only the next groove.)

—I am at the next groove now. F. The makers cut an F here and the F has rolled, the tile beneath has settled and the whole pier leans its note up toward a sour F-and-a-bit, and I lay my length along the incision and I read it, I read the *is* against the *should*, and the should is loud in the clay, the should is the loudest thing in the world, it is the makers still talking, still saying the one thing, the only thing they left in the stone—

keep it singing.

—and I do not know why. I have never known why. I have never been up. The oculi are holes in the top of the dark and through them comes the falling-water and the grey that is not my grey and once, only as struck notes, the rain.

The rain.

When the rain comes the whole score plays itself. Every pier struck true at once by the falling, every flat note slapped back to where it should be by water that does not have to choose, water that tunes the *whole room in one breath* the way I cannot tune one pier in a year — and I stop. I am allowed to stop. I lie in the run and the ceiling does my whole life's work in an hour, unaided, and the criers go mad above me, climbing, climbing, every throat true and answering and the bricks ring like the makers meant, like the makers must have stood up there in their dry brief light and *heard once* what it is supposed to sound like, all of it, correct—

and I have never heard it. I am always in the dark, always against a single pier, always inside the work and never in the room the work is for. I keep a music I have never heard whole. I tend a chord and live inside one note of it. The rain is the only time I am not working and so the rain is the only time I could listen and the rain is so loud, so much, a hundred new notes and the climbing and the ring, that I cannot hear the music *as* music. I can only feel it as rest. As the great correct. As: *not my job, just now. lie still.*

That is my sabbath. That is the whole of what I have instead of why.

(it is stopping. the oculi drip down. the hundred rhythms slow. and the flat begins to creep, pier by pier, east first because I left the east, the east has been sour a long while now and the criers there are gone, that quarter is going silent, the cold is opening—)

—and I uncoil.

Because under the silence, in the courses the makers never sang to, the silence is where the *other* slides. Not me. The thing I never tell because there is no one to tell. The silence is not empty. Silence here is never empty. The makers knew. The makers built it loud *on purpose* and they did not tell me why but I have felt the why, I have felt it come up the silent runs toward the quiet piers, slow, slower than me, older than the grooves maybe, and it does not eat the simmering ones, it does not tune, it does not read, it only comes toward where the note has died, it comes *into* the quiet the way water comes into a low course, filling—

so when I keep a pier singing I am not only keeping the makers' arrangement. I am keeping the quiet small. I am keeping the door the size of one pier, one quarter, the east I had to let go. The criers are not the music's only point. The criers are the *seal*. Every true note is a place the filling-thing cannot rise. The makers did not build a song. They built a held breath. They built a room that has to keep singing or the silence comes up through the floor, and they could not stay, they were dry and brief, and they left it to the swallow they did not mean to dig.

I keep it singing so the quiet stays small.

I have let the east go quiet.

I am going east now, against the slide, against the slow old filling, one body, the wrong way down the run, toward the pier I abandoned, to lay my length against a groove that has been sour so long the should has nearly gone out of the clay—

(brace. slide. the C behind me holds. it will hold a season without me. I have to spend the season here, in the east, in the cold, in the place the singing died, where the other is already rising—)

—and I read the groove. It is an A. It was an A. The makers cut an A here and I will sharpen the high lip and spare the low and it will take years I do not have against a thing already in the course beside me, and I will eat what is silent here because the work is long and the body is hungry, and I will keep this one note, this single A, sung up out of the dark by a blind length of brick-colored patience that has never heard the chord, never seen the sky, never known the why—

keep it singing. keep it singing. keep the quiet small.

(—still here. still here. laid against the A. narrowing. sparing. the criers, one throat, then two, then—

—there. they hear it. they answer.

the seal closes by one note in the east.

still here.)
