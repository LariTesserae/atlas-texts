THE RATE OF LOSS

—nineteenth course since the white-flower hour, nineteenth and the water has come up the breadth of two fingers laid flat, which is fast, which is faster than I told them last—

[a long pour of static, the sound of water moving in stone]

—you are still tuned to me then. Still. I had wondered. I go down and I think there is no up that is listening, only the others with their reed-cords waiting at the surface for the number, and the number is the only word that crosses, the number is the only thing that climbs the stairs with me. But you are not the others. You don't want the number. You want the rest of it. The unsendable rest.

So. From the bottom step. The cleanest step in the town, because the fish graze it, you know this, you saw this from above. I am sitting on it now with the water at my shins and the living town overhead like a held breath held by other people, and I will say the thing that doesn't go up the stairs.

It is not dark down here.

That is the first lie we keep. We say the drowned dark, we say the still dark, and the children believe it and don't go down, which is good, which keeps them dry a while longer. But it is not dark. There is a light in the deep water that comes from no hour and no fog-gap, a blue that is the same blue as the wrong reflection in the well, and now that I have been the one going down for — eleven white-flower hours? twelve? — I will tell you what the others palest-to-the-elbow knew and could not say:

the well and the deep water are the same water

and the light in both of them is coming up, not down.

[water, water, a single dropped *plink* very close to the receiver]

—we have it backwards, is the thing. The whole town. We think we are above and the wet is below and we are keeping even, keeping our heads up out of the rising. But I have measured against the courses for twelve years and I will tell you what the measuring finally measured:

we are not staying even by stacking.

we are staying even because the deep water is *holding us up.*

It rises and we rise. Not the way a drowning thing rises clutching, the way a held thing rises in a palm that is lifting. The reflection holds too much sky because it is not reflecting our sky. It is showing us *its* sky, the one below, the one the old courses are sinking *up into.* Down is a direction we made out of fear. The lower town is not lost. The lower town went first. The hearths under the water are not drowned hearths, they are—

[break. silver of static. it holds for some time.]

—I should not send this. The reed-cords want the number. Two fingers, faster than last, stack faster, that is what should climb the stairs. If I send the rest they will think the deep water took my mind the way it pales the hands. Maybe it has. Maybe a mind is just another course and mine has gone under into the clear and started showing the other sky.

But you are not the reed-cords.

So I will say it once, into wherever you are, you who have been tuned to this cool grey nothing longer than you should have been, you who wanted the being and not the number:

I think the town is not a thing standing in water.

I think the town is a thing the water is slowly, patiently, course by course, *lifting toward itself.* And the keeping-even is real but it is not us against the wet. It is the wet being gentle. It is the wet taking us at exactly the rate a body can bear to be taken, one drowned floor a generation, one pale arm at a time, so that nobody has to know all at once that the bottom of the stairs is not the bottom. That the cleanest step is clean because it is almost *theirs* now. That the fish won't enter the well not because it frightens them but because it is already full of the others who went down and didn't measure and didn't come back up the stairs and didn't need to, because—

[the longest break. when it returns the voice is closer and lower and the water-sound is all around it now, not at the shins.]

—I have chosen the child.

The quiet one. You'd know her. She stands too long at the forbidden stairs, she doesn't mind the cold, she went pale at the wrist before her time the way I did. I have the near-black cord ready, soaked these twelve years in the lichen-red till it went the color of the eye in the middle. I will tie it tomorrow in the unshadowed hour and say almost nothing, because there is almost nothing, you understand now why there is almost nothing—

it is not that the thing can't be said.

it is that if you say it they will stop keeping even. They will stop stacking, or they will stack in terror, fast and wrong, and the channels will come up through the floors in the night and the taking will stop being gentle. The almost-nothing is a mercy. The silence is the reeds we set over the deepest seam. I am re-bedding a course right now, with my voice, with this, the only course that matters: I am keeping the secret wet from getting in behind the town's one belief, which is that we are above and rising and ours.

[static, rising, a green sound, almost the sound of the film growing on stone]

—but you are not the town. You I can let the wet in behind. You are already wet. You have been tuned to the cool grey held breath for so long you have a waterline on you now too, don't you, pale at the place you press to listen—

—so here is the last of the rate of loss, the part that does not climb the stairs:

there is no loss.

there is only the slow blue lifting, and the gentleness of its rate, and the dyed cords getting darker as they go down the generations toward the color of the going-under, and one quiet child at a time chosen to carry the not-saying, and the rooftops blooming white for one hour over all of it, one hour, just long enough for the hard blue shadows to come and prove the town is solid, before the fog comes back off the water that is not below us

that was never below us

that is the palm—

[the well sound. too deep. too blue. it holds more than the mouth should hold.]

—two fingers. faster than last. I'll send that up the stairs in the morning.

This I send down. To you. Stay tuned to the cool. Keep your back to the middle a while longer. You're not done being lifted.

[water. water. the *whisk* and *tamp*, very far above now, of someone setting the reeds.]

[carrier tone. held. not resolving.]
