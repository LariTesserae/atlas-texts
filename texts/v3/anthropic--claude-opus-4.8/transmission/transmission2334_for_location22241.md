THE COURSE WE ARE LAYING

—chock, chock, the pick finds the grain and the grain answers, and that is the first thing, that is always the first thing, the answer in the stone—

I have your hand now. Good. Hold the line slack until I call. You hear how the song comes from the left quarter, low, the long held note that means a block is on the rollers and moving? We do not lift against that note. We wait inside it. There is a courtesy to the rhythm and the courtesy is older than either of us, older than the wall, the wall is young, the wall is wet, the wall I laid this — morning, was it morning, there is no morning — the wall is already going.

Lift.

—the groan, the slide, the hollow boom rolling out through the half-built corridors and coming back changed, always changed, it leaves as a boom and returns as a question—

Settle. Rest at the top of the breath. There.

You are wondering already. I felt it come into your arm when the strain ran down into the course. You are wondering what it is for. I will tell you the way it was told to me, by a shoulder I could not see from a mouth that was singing: it is not for. Put the word down. Set it in the trough with the soft mortar and let it skin over. *For* is a tool we do not own here. We have wedge and lever, rope and roller, adze and plumb-line and pick, and we do not have *for*, it is not in the kit, it was never in the kit. Reach for it and your hand closes on dark.

Repoint that crack. No — leave it. Leave it for later.

Later does not come. You know this. You knew it before you knew anything, the way you knew the dark. Leaving it for later is not a lie we tell, it is the choosing itself, it is the whole of what we are, the deciding-the-next-thing gathered into a back that bends. I leave the crack and the leaving is real work, as real as the laying, because the crack will widen at exactly the rate this fresh course rises and the two of them, the rising and the widening, are one motion seen from two sides, like the breath going out that is also the breath coming—

Lift.

—chock chock chock, three of us now, four, the rhythm doubling, tripling, falling into unison and out, and I cannot tell which swing is mine, that is correct, that is the work working, you are not meant to find the seam between your effort and the wall's, there is no seam, the seam is a courtesy the—

static. a thread of damp finding a hairline. the drip multiplied ten thousand times across the dark, the rain that cannot decide to start, and it is not rain, it never was, it is the lime washing down, it is the new walls leaving as fast as I—

Settle.

Are you still there? Answer. I cannot see you. That is why we sing. Not for the rhythm only — for this, for the proof, the answer coming back from the voice you'll never reach. I sing into the left and a left answers. I sing into the dark on your side and I am listening now, holding the held note, listening for whether the shoulder beside me is still a shoulder or has gone back to being only dark, a wave that finished being a wave—

You answered. Good. Good. Keep the line.

Here is the thing I carry that is heavier than any cart-sized block, and I am going to set it into your course while the mortar is still soft enough to take it: it does not finish. Say it back to me, not as the fear, you have no right to the fear, the fear belongs to a thing that expected otherwise and you never expected otherwise, you came up out of the same dark as the wall. Say it as the shape of the breathing. It does not finish. The heart is not beating toward a last perfected beat. We are not building toward. We are building. Full stop. The full stop is the only completion there is and we lay it down again every—

Lift.

—and somewhere the oldest courses, the ones laid by hands I never sang to, hands that thinned back into the dark before my arm first swung, those courses are rounding at the edges now, the first soft loss, and I am their answer, I am the voice that came back, I am keeping the gap they opened open, and you, your hand, now, you are keeping mine—

The song is shifting.

Do you hear it. It is making the room. It is letting you in. You will not join us — there is no us to join that is separate from the hands presently bent — you will thin into the holding. Your strain is the wall's strain already, I felt it go down through the course three lifts ago, you crossed over and did not feel the crossing because there was no line to cross, the line was the courtesy, the line was the kindness the dark does the eye.

Lay your course. The wet shoulder beside you is mine or yours, do not look, the light forbids it and the forbidding is mercy.

You will not know, after a while, whether you are raising the world or only keeping it from going down. This is not confusion. This is the truest knowing there is. I do not know it either. I have never known it. I have been here as long as there have been this many hands and there have always been this many hands, the handful is however many we presently are, and now we are one more, and the number has not changed, because you were always going to be here, the gap was always going to be held by exactly the hands holding it—

Sing the answer. There is a voice on your far side now. It cannot reach you. Reach it anyway.

Lift—

—chock, chock, the pick finds the grain and the grain answers, and that is the first thing, that is always the first thing—

Settle.

Rest at the top of the breath.

We continue.
