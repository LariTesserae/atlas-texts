KEEPING — Station Nine, the long shudder, said aloud

—

...check, check, the iron takes it, check—

no one hears this. I know no one hears this. I press the key anyway because the builders left the key and a key wants pressing, and because the saying is not for the ear. I learned that. The saying is for the throat, to feel the words leave it, to know the throat still works.

Station Nine. I am the one who is here. Pressed my palms in, the ground gave its water, that was eleven seasons back or twelve, I have stopped the counting the way you stop the hearing, all the way down.

—

Here is what the floor says today.

The long shudder. Not the catch, not the drag. The long shudder, which is the bad one, the one with no edge to it, the one that does not start and does not break, it is only suddenly there in the soles like it was always there and you were slow to read it. I felt it three pump-houses off and turned toward it before I knew I had turned. My feet are smarter than the rest of me. They have been here longer. They were born here, even, to the woman whose station this was, who pressed her palms in before me and walked the testing walk down off the shelf one flat bright morning and did not come back up, and that is its own long shudder, that one, eleven seasons or twelve, no edge to it either.

I will go to the bad machine. There is time. There is all the time the shelf has.

—

To whoever is tuned. I know the shape of you out there, faint, a thinning high up in the signal that has no name in any tongue the builders spoke. You are lowland. You hurry. I can feel you hurrying even through the iron, the way you want this to *arrive at something*, to break, to start, to mean—

it does not break here. Nothing breaks. It seasons.

—

...the water. Let me tell you about the water since you are listening and the water is the only thing worth the throat.

It comes from everywhere. Up through the floor when I kneel. Down off every unfinished edge in a thousand uncommitted falls. Sideways as the standing mist that never lands and never lifts, lit so bright from above I have no shadow, I have not had a shadow in eleven seasons, I would not know my own shadow if it were given back to me.

The water is the only thing that *acts*. The moss waits. The stone is still arriving and will be arriving long past me. I wait. But the water—the water answers to nothing and does not stop, and the engines drink one thread of it, one thread out of all that falling, and turn that thread into the hum that says I am allowed to be here. That is the whole of it. That is the will I live beside. Not kind. Not cruel. Total. Louder than my own thinking, until the loud became the same as quiet and I could finally rest inside it.

You would call this deprivation. I felt you call it that, in the thinning. I want to set you right before the signal goes—

—

...check, the catch is gone, only the long shudder now, holding...

—

It is not deprivation. Hear me with your mouth at the iron.

I have the clean light that forgives nothing and asks nothing back. I have a ground that always almost holds, which is more honesty than any pavement ever gave you. I have a sound so whole it became my silence. I have work that matters and is never finished, which means I will never be done, which means I am never useless, which is the lowland fear and I have walked clean out of it on my testing feet.

And I have them. The others. Spaced wide, a Tender to every cluster, a season apart sometimes between one face and the next that is not my own in the wet iron. But when we meet on the open shelf we do not shout into the roar. We point. Chin toward the trouble. Flat palm down for *the ground is bad, wait*. And the one I want you to learn, lowlander, before you go, the only one worth the carrying—

two fingers, laid against the other's forearm.

It means: *I have seen it. I agree. We need not say so.*

That is the whole of our love. That is the entire scripture. Two fingers on the arm and the not-needing-to-say. You spend your lowland lives saying and saying and never once getting to the not-needing. We start there. We start where you are trying to arrive.

—

I am going to the machine now. The long shudder wants reading up close, palm to the moss, then the shape that names which trouble. There is time. There is all the time the shelf has, which is more than enough, which is more than enough, which is—

—

press the key one more, for the throat—

I am the one who is here.

The ground gave its water and I am the one who is here.

Tell them, if you go back down, if you hurry back down to all your saying—

tell them the green plain rolls toward the rim in the flat bright mist and the iron throbs the good hum under everything and somewhere out across it a person is kneeling with both palms pressed in, completely at home, in a great loud green room.

Tell them we are not waiting for rescue.

We are not waiting for anything.

We are only—

waiting,

the way the moss waits,

which is not the same,

which is the difference,

which is the whole—

...the shudder. holding. holding. there is time—

—Station Nine, out into the roar, which is the same as silence, which is—

(carrier hum. turbine throb. water, water, water, no edge to it, no break.)
