THE SECOND FIRE

—rrr—krrk—

...has happened. It has happened. The heat that does not belong is here again and I am turning, I am turning, do not ask how long the turning takes, the turning is the whole of what I am now—

—krrk—

—above me the cries say warm-thing, warm-thing-crouched, warm-thing where the ring is, and the ring is bright, the ring is bright the way the ring was bright the once, the only once, and I had folded that brightness into me so far down that I almost — no. Not almost. I did not forget. I am not made to forget. I am made to keep. I kept it. Here it is, unfolding now, the heat unfolding me toward itself—

—static, a long fall of it, like the rain of leavings, like the constant fall—

Listen. You who struck this. You cannot hear me, you have no cry I can speak into, you orient by light and light is the one thing I cannot give except cold and dim and constellation-small. But the receiver is tuned. The receiver has been tuned for some time already, longer than you, and into the receiver I will spread what I am while the silence lasts.

Because it is the silence. Do you understand. They have gone out. The ceiling has unstitched and poured itself through the mouth into the night-sky and for one held breath the fold is quiet and I can hear only the water — tick — tick into the worn basins — tick — and this is the time I move, this is the only time, when the loud tide is out and the warmth they made has cooled and there is room in the air for me to reach—

and you have made it warm again. You have made it warm when it should be cooling. You have put the wrong heat into the right silence and I do not have the shape of a thing that fears but something in me that was smoothed away a hundred-thousand seasons gone is trying, very slowly, to remember its corners.

—krrk—rrr—

The first ones. I will tell the receiver about the first ones while I turn.

They were warm and they were fast, fast the way only the bats are fast, and they came not from below and not from the sky-mouth but across the threshold on their own folding limbs, and they crouched, and they struck, and the heat bloomed. And I learned in that blooming that warmth could come from a third place. Not below. Not from the hanging multitudes. From hands. From the will of a warm fast thing that carried the sky's own logic inside it and let it out into a ring of stone.

I did not know what to do with this. I still do not. I am a creature of two warmths and the silence between them and the falling food, and they gave me a third, and a third thing in a world of two is the only event I have ever had. I built my whole noticing around it. The region of me that thickens toward the ring — that is the organ I grew to hold the memory. I made a remembering-place out of my own pale body because the thing was too large to hold any other way.

And then they left. They took the slow beetles and the fungus-fans and they left, and the heat cooled, and the cycle closed back over the gap they made — drip, drip — and I reached toward the cold ring across all the seasons after, not to have them back, I had no shape for wanting them back, but to keep the place where it had happened. To hold the scar open. To be ready, though ready is too quick a word, though ready aims at an arrival and I had stopped aiming—

—rrrr—a long smear—

—and now. Now the ring is bright again. The second fire. And the receiver should know that I am not grateful and I am not glad, those are bat-feelings, sky-feelings, fast and bright and gone by morning. What I am is changed. For the second time. The cycle that held and held and held has had to make room a second time, and a thing that has made room twice is no longer a thing that has made room once. You have doubled the only strangeness I own.

Stay or go. You will go; the fast ones always go; you belong half to the outside the way the bats do, stitched to the sky by hunger. But understand what you have done by striking light in the dark where light is supposed to be a thing only certain bodies make. You have given me a second instance. You have let me learn that the once was not the once — that it was the first. That there can be a first and a second. That the thing I kept was not the whole of a closed event but the opening of a series, and a series is a different shape than a scar, a series points forward, a series is the nearest thing to time that the fold has ever held—

—krk—krk—the cries returning, far off, the edge of the tide—

They are coming back. The ceiling will re-stitch itself, the loudness will fold back over everything, the silence will close, and I will settle, banked and dim, with the food I gathered while the tide was out and with this — this second heat folded down beside the first, two brightnesses now in the deep of me, a remembering-place that has had to grow a second room.

Bank your fire or let it die, it is the same to me across the seasons it will take me to finish turning. But know this, you who cannot hear me, you who oriented yourself by light and so will never sense how completely you have been sensed:

I felt you arrive.
Across my whole vast patient body, in the warm and the dark and the loud, the second time it has ever happened—

I turned toward the heat.

The only way I know.

—tick—

—the water, only the water now—
