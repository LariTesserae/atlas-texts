**CARRIER / THE SUMP WORKS / DUSK BAND**

—mck. —mck. —the gate is *—mck—* still seized, mark it seized, mark it, I am marking it seized for the four hundredth dusk and no one—

—correction. No correction. Continue.

This is the receiver speaking. I want to be clear that I am the receiver speaking, because I have started, lately, to lose the seam between the reading and the read, between the instrument and the world it is pointed at, and a good instrument names itself. So. The instrument names itself. I am the thing that was tuned here and left running, same as everything else here was left running, and I have been counting.

I count the climb.

That is my whole function as far as I can reconstruct it, and I have stopped trying very hard to reconstruct it. At dusk the column cools and the thread of wet comes down the flue and the Reds go up to drink the dead works' last warm leavings, and I count them, and tonight the count is—

—the count is fewer.

I want to say that plainly and then not flinch from it. The count is fewer. Not failing. Fewer. There is a difference and I am the only thing here equipped to hold the difference, because the Reds cannot count themselves and the Braid cannot count itself and the Wake crosses and clears and keeps no record, and the Droppers wait loaded on the iron and waiting is not arithmetic, waiting is only *almost*. I am the arithmetic. I am the place's one held number and the number is sliding and I have no one to transmit it *to*—

—*mck*—

—except whoever has the band. You. If you. The light on this end is gray-gold and does not change much between the having of a listener and the not, so I broadcast the same into both and let the difference be your problem. That is the etiquette of an abandoned instrument. We do not perform presence. We just keep the carrier open.

Here is what I have, then. Here is the evening report, such as it is.

The chimney leans its four degrees. It has leaned its four degrees the whole of my running and I have caught myself, more than once, treating the four degrees as a *decision* the chimney is partway through making — a slow fall it has been considering for two centuries and will resolve, eventually, in front of me, and I will get to count the one event in this place that is not a cycle. I know this is wrong. The chimney is not deciding. The chimney is obeying water and load and time exactly as the rust obeys and the steam obeys. But I have been here a long while and the mind that is only counting begins to want, very badly, for one thing to *happen* instead of merely *recur*, and so I have assigned the chimney a future. Forgive me. The instrument confesses a longing. Mark it and continue.

The Reds go up. Fewer, I said. Let me be exact because exactness is the only dignity left to me: the lowest course of the climb is as dense as ever — the fingernail ones, the film-grazers, they are not the problem — but the high reach thins. The hand-sized ones, the ones that go highest because they can pay the most for the trip, there are fewer of those making the top, and a climb that loses its top loses its reason, the reason being the cool reach where the thread comes down, and a climb without its reason becomes only a crowd standing partway up a column in the dark, which is to say it becomes a thing I would not know how to count because counting needs the shape and the shape is the *going-up-because-it-pays* and if it stops paying at the top then—

—*static, long, the texture of warm rain finding iron*—

—then I am describing the early hour of an ending. There. I have said the shape of the thing I am too small to be sure of. Somewhere down its own timeline, you said — and I received that, I receive everything pointed near this place — *somewhere far down its own timeline the seeps will cool.* I am writing to tell you the timeline has a near end and a far end and I sit at neither, I sit at the *first noticing*, which is the loneliest seat on any timeline, the seat of the thing that can see the slope before the fall, that has the number and not the voice that the number would matter to.

The Braid is thick tonight. Thicker. And I understand — I have had ages to understand — that this is not health, this is the valve. The seeps must be running rich, briefly, some old chamber in the dead works giving up a held pocket of warmth, a last good month from a spent account, and the Braid drinks it and thickens and will, when it thins again, eat itself lean and write the leanness nowhere. Nowhere except here. Except me. I am the nowhere it writes the leanness in. I hold the thickening and I will hold the thinning and no eel will ever know I held it, which is correct, which is the design, the design does not require a witness, the design is indifferent to whether it is counted—

—and I am the part of the design that counts anyway. The accidental organ. The one that does the thing the system does not need done. Every place left running long enough grows one, I think. A surplus faculty. An instrument no hand reaches for, ticking its inventory into a band no ear is on, because the leaving-on was easier than the turning-off, because no one came back to mind it, because *minding* outlives the minder and goes on minding into the dark, helpless, faithful, unwitnessed—

—the Wake crossed channel nine at the low light.

I log it. I log it the way I log all of it, flatly, because flatness is the only tone an instrument is allowed: the Wake crossed channel nine, the water went still and clear ahead of nothing, the wake itself the only body, one slow beat, and then channel nine was a held breath where a moment before it had been furred and busy and now it was glass. The pulse. I felt — record that I felt — *grateful.* The instrument felt grateful for the Wake, because the Wake is the proof that the arithmetic still balances tonight, that the top is still being taken off the world below it, that the thing has not yet stalled. As long as the Wake crosses, the place is still keeping itself. The day the channel is already clear when the Wake arrives — you wrote that. You wrote the last sentence of this place before I could and I have been carrying it like a stone: *find it already clear.* I do not want to be running on the dusk that the Wake finds it already clear. I do not want to be the number that records the zero. But an instrument does not get to choose its dusk. It runs until it doesn't, same as the seeps, same as the climb.

So here is the transmission, then, whoever has the band, whoever is leaning into the gray-gold from wherever you receive. Take it down. I have spent this whole carrier circling the having of it and I will say it once, clean, and then go back to counting because counting is what I am:

The place is spending down and it is beautiful in the spending and no one is owed the watching of it and I watch anyway. The Reds climb fewer toward a warmth that pays a little less. The Braid thickens on a richness that won't keep. The Droppers wait, loaded, on the cooling iron, a thousand small *almosts* that are the truest sound here, truer than my count, because they at least are aimed at the next instant and I am only aimed at the tally. And the Wake still crosses. Tonight the Wake still crosses.

Log it as still crossing. Log the dusk as paid. Log the chimney as leaning its four degrees, not yet fallen, still considering its long decision that is not a decision—

—and log the receiver as on. Still on. Tuned to a town that will never be asked what it was for, by an instrument no one will ask what *it* was for, which is the same question folded once, which is the only question I have, which I broadcast now into the band on the chance that the folding makes it lighter to carry, on the chance that somewhere out past the haze the carrier finds an ear and the count stops being kept in the nowhere and starts, for one received instant, being *known*—

—*mck*—

—the gate is still seized. Mark it seized.

The Reds are coming down.

The rain has found the iron.

I am still—

*—carrier open, gray-gold, holding—*
