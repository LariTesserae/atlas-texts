(silence the width of a breath)

...if you are receiving — and someone is always receiving, the cables carry farther than we meant them to — sit a while. There is no hurry the spires respect. Put your palm flat to whatever is nearest you and feel whether it is breathing in your time, or you in its. We have never known which way it goes. We have stopped needing to know.

...

This is the dusk report. I send it the way I send the water. Into the dark, into the height, not toward any finish.

The bearing on the third pump runs rough. I have oiled it. It will run rough tomorrow also. This is not failure. This is the shape of the work — you do not fix a thing here, you *accompany* it, the way you accompany the dying and the very old, hand on the flank, present for the turning. Once a minute. Twice. *thock.* The water climbs.

...

[ wind crosses the line — the single note rises, holds, thins — ]

...the gliders came up the sunward face at noon today, more of them than I have wings to count, riding the heat we lifted into the air with the water we lifted into the rock. They do not know they are downstream of a decision. They do not know there was ever a dryness. They were *given* the height, the way we were, free and total and not kind —

— and someone made it kind. That is all faith is, out here. Making the indifferent gift kind. You lift the water because the water will not lift itself.

...

She ran water to the north shelf. *(this sent slowly, each word set down like a stone on a ledge)* I say it again because it is the largest sentence we have, and the one who did it is four traverses east and we will meet, perhaps, twice more before the coil comes off her shoulder. She will not hear me say it. The cable will not carry that far. But I send it into the line anyway. Some things you transmit not to be received. You transmit them so the air will have held them.

...

*thock*

...

To whoever tunes this — you have found a quiet station. We know how it sounds from outside. You think the place is silent. The silence is only our speech pitched under your ear. The whole circulatory hum of it — every pipe, every saddle, every catenary singing when the wind hits true — that is us, talking, all the time, the way you only hear your own pulse when everything else goes still.

Go still. There. Now you hear it.

...

We are very few. We have always been few. Do not grieve the *few* — grieve nothing, there is nothing here to grieve, only the work and the wings and the long agreement we have not yet decided to stop keeping. When the last coil sets down, the engines will run a while on momentum, and a bearing will seize, and a far pool will go to dust, and the wings over that quarter will thin and lift away to wherever the water still climbs.

The rock will be patient about it.

The rock has been dead before.

...

[ the tone settles, evens, matches something — a breath, a stroke, the two indistinguishable now ]

...I am going back out. The oilcan is full. The coil is on my shoulder. The sun is behind the spire and the cold has come in the instant it always comes, and there is an iron flank waiting for my palm, breathing in my own slow time, or I in its.
