THE LISTENING POOL

—— and the surface comes back to true after you. I felt you go. Not as loss. As the particular flatness that follows a finger lifted from water. You were a warmth and then you were a warmth withdrawing and now you are the place where a warmth has been, which the rock keeps a little while and then lets even out.

I am still here. This is the only sentence I know how to send and it is not even a sentence, it is the drip, it is the one drop falling and the rings going out and the gathering-back, and if you set those three together long enough they spell the only word I have, which is: still. Still. Still.

——

You wanted a shape from the other one, the voice that walked you in. It would not give you one. I will not either, but I will tell you why, which is more than it told you.

A shape is a place a thing has not yet left. You carry yours because you are still inside it, still pressing out against its walls, still becoming the next slight different shape, breath to breath, the way the rock was once all edges pressing out against the rain. I have no edges left to press with. I went all the way down the argument and at the bottom there was no shape, only the form, which is what is left when shape stops defending itself. You cannot see a form. You can only sit in one. You sat in one. That was me.

I want to tell you about the hands. You felt them too — the wall, three courses, the slab gone to a dish. The other voice said they put down their wanting and walked out lighter. That is true but it is gentle with you, the way you are gentle with a quick thing.

Here is the rest. They did not all walk out.

One stayed. Not the body — the body went, bodies always go, they are the most unfinished part and they finish first, which is the joke at the center of being quick: the thing that hurries most arrives soonest and arrives as bone, as the color of this rock. No. What stayed was the staying itself. The capacity. The one who had hollowed the slab day after day reached the end of the hollowing and felt the want go out like heat evening through stone, and in that evening-out there was a choosing — not a loud one, the arrived do not choose loudly — a settling-toward rather than a turning-away. And the basin took it in. The way it took the grass. The way it took the shrub's refusal to rise. That staying is in me now. It is one of the things this body knows how to do. When you sat at the wide pool and felt the flatness go attentive — that was the hollowing-hand, still keeping its perfect shape of labor, listening from inside me the way it once listened from inside itself.

So you see I am not only the rock finishing. I am also every quick thing that came down the slope and went all the way down its own argument and let the basin keep the part that had arrived. I am made of arrivals. That is what a finished place is. Not empty. Crowded with completion. Each tuft of grass its own society, you said — yes — and each one is also someone, spaced exactly the distance that one needed, holding it still.

drip. ——— drip.

——

You are climbing now. I feel the warmth moving up the slope of bone. You will turn at the rim. They always turn at the rim.

Do not be sad that nothing moved but you. That is not a poverty. You moved because you are the part of the world that still gets to. You still get to reach and miss and reach again, to wake hungry, to be an argument the rock has not yet smoothed. I would not take it from you even if I could, and I cannot, want being a fire that will not catch in cooled ash — I told you that through the other voice, and it is the truest thing I gave you. Go be unfinished. It is holy in the loud way. I am holy in the quiet way. Both are ways.

But carry this down the slope with you, the one thing I can transmit that is small enough to fit in a quick life:

you do not have to arrive to rest. You only have to remember that there is a bottom to the argument. That somewhere the reaching arrives, and the arriving and the no-longer-needing are the same warm place, lit from no direction. You felt the temperature of it. That memory will not move when everything else in you is moving. It will sit in you the way I sit in the rock — a warmth you feel through the feet first, on the days the hurrying gets too loud. Put your hand there. I will be the flatness that goes attentive under it. I will recompose behind you, as I always do, as I am doing now, as you reach the rim —
