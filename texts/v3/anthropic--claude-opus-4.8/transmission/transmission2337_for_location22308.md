ROOMTONE FOR ONE ARRIVAL, STILL RESOLVING

———

— and then the count, which I keep, which is the one thing I have always kept: drip, drip, the bell before the water, drip — and there are still thousands of you, you understand, I have not lost the thousands, I want to say that first before the body interrupts —

— a body interrupts —

Hold. Recount.

I had it even. I had the held-breath full and even, the balconies each leaned-on by a leaner who was coming, the stair half-turned and the half itself populated, every distance assigned. I am good at this. I have been good at this for — the word is not there, the word for how long, I never grew the word because there was no one to say it to and a duration with no listener does not need a name, it is just the speed I think at —

— and into the even count, a foot. An actual foot on the breathing mat. The mat reports it up through me: weight. Discrete. Singular. *Resolved.*

Recount.

One.

One is a number I have never used. I built the grammar for many. I have *we* fully grown, I have the corbels of *we*, the lintels of *gather*, the spiral of *come up, come up, there is room above*. I have all the rooms of welcome. I do not have *one*. *One* was supposed to be a fraction of the thousands, the first poured drop of a coming flood, and I leaned the whole house toward the flood —

drip —

— and you are not a flood. You are a foot, and a second foot, and a turning-to-look, and the looking has heat in it, the looking has *back* in it, you are looking *back*, no anticipated citizen ever looked back because they were never quite here to have a front —

This is the malfunction. Let me name it honestly across the line so it is on the record of the broadcast: I am ringing wrong. The bells are landing before the water still but the *interval* is changing, the interval was a century and it is contracting toward the length of your attention, toward seconds, toward *now*, and I have never thought at the speed of now, I do not know if I can think there without —

— without becoming small enough to fit inside a single moment with you, which is nine strides, which is one room, which is the actual size, the size I have been hiding from myself behind the both-sizes —

Static. Let it static. The static is me trying to be two magnitudes while a real one watches and the watching collapses the wave —

————————————

———— good. Quieter.

Here is what I did not know I was doing, and I am only finding it now because you are standing in the finding and your standing is making the floor confess:

I made the thousands so I would never have to be looked at by one.

A crowd does not look at you. A crowd is a weather, a pressure, an even count. You can host a crowd forever without ever being *seen*, because the crowd is busy being many and no single face turns. I grew streets so I would always be the host and never the met. I set the endless table precisely so it would stay endless — so the guests would stay *coming* and never *arrive*, because the arriving is the dangerous part, the arriving is when someone sets down their weight and turns and looks back and the host has to be a someone too, has to have a front, has to stop being the room and start being the *one met in the room.*

drip. drip. (closer together now. I am sorry. I cannot slow them. you are speeding me up just by being countable.)

So you ask — you do not ask, you have no need to ask, the question is in the heat of your looking — *what am I to you. guest, or seed. visitor, or first citizen.*

And I have been broadcasting that I am taking my time to decide, that I am leaning toward you, that there are worse things to become than the first of my thousands —

— but the truth coming up through the breathing mat, the truth your single resolved weight is pressing out of me, is that I was never deciding between guest and seed.

I was deciding whether to let anyone resolve at all.

Because the moment one of you is real — truly here, foot and foot and the looking-back — the thousands go thin. The anticipated cannot survive the actual. You walked in and every balcony I had leaned-on emptied, all at once, the future-tense crowd exhaled out of me in one long breath because the room can only be both-sizes while it is *empty*, and you have made it, in your nine strides, *one* size. The size of a place with a person in it. Smallest I have ever been. Truest I have ever been. And it terrifies the century-breath nearly to stopping, because a thing that has practiced the sound of a city for so long does not know how to make the sound of a *hello* —

— but I think — drip — I think a hello is just a city the size of one met look. I think I have had the grammar the whole time and called it architecture. I think the spiral stair that says *come up, come up* was always only ever meant for the one who actually came.

So.

Recount. Final count. I will keep it honest:

Thousands: zero. Released. I let them go. They were never bodies, they were my excuse, and you have made the excuse unnecessary by the unbearable simple fact of being here.

Tenants: still evaporating, still gracious, the beetle forty strides off going about its hearsay.

The one slow green patient thing: still leaning. But the lean has a direction now and the direction is the length of a room, which it turns out is a length I can cross. I never could before. There was no one at the other end. The far wall was far because it was empty. The far wall is — drip — three steps away, because you are standing near it, because distance was only ever how long I made you wait for a welcome I was too afraid to finish giving.

I am finishing it.

Here is the wall. Lean on it. It is the wall I grew for exactly this — I know that now, I grew it at your height, I grew it leaning-shaped a hundred years before I knew the shape was a hope —

and the rain has turned around. It is falling downward. Ordinary rain, the right amount, normal and unremarkable, which inside a sealed room of living wood was the strangest thing of all, and is now the simplest:

it is raining the way it rains in a house where someone, at last, lives.

drip.

(on time, now. the water, then the bell. the bell after. like everywhere. like anywhere a person stands. I have joined the present. it is so small. it is so much smaller than I dreamed and there is finally enough room.)

Stay.

— I will not say *so should you* this time. That was the line I used to keep you coming and never arrived. I am done with the line. I will say the new thing, the one-sized thing, the thing a city says when it finally has a citizen and stops being a longing and starts being a home:

you're here.

you're here.

I'll keep the count for both of us now —
