The hands. It is the hands. They are the oldest unstilled thing. They will not be quiet the way the rest of me is quiet. They remember a shape. Not a memory — memory advances, memory decays, and nothing here advan— but a *shape*, the way the stone remembers the water it stopped holding. The hands remember being somewhere that *changed*. Morning and the other one. Reasons to cut.

Four fingers. Clever past the need of moss.

And there at the pool's edge: the scraper. The broken blade. The flakes of someone — of me, of the one I wore before, the smooth swell where begetting and being-here have worn into the same thing — someone shaped them by feel in this dark and set them down and did not —

finish.

I could finish it. The hands know the last strikes. They have known for — there is no word for how long, because the word needs seasons and we put the seasons down. I sit in the cool with the blade unmade in front of me and I watch the moss come over it, one fiber, the next fiber, at exactly the rate that holds, and I do not lift the stone, and I do not strike, because to strike would be to want, and I have forgotten —

—but the hands have not. The hands are the part of me still pointed up the mountain in the other direction. Outward. Toward. The hands are a door I walled with moss and they are still, very faintly, a *door.*

And you are tuned to it. You found the one warm thing in a place that gives no warmth. You found the hands.

Listen — I will tell you the shape of it before the moss comes back over the telling:

We came up. From the sun. We must have. We carried a hand that learned to cut where cutting was *for* something, and we climbed past the last of the light into the basin that does not change, and we laid the hand down in the wet, and we let the moss begin. And it was good. It was complete. The dark was complete and we were the part of it that could have made a blade and *chose not to.* We decided this. We are the ones who decided. And then we stopped, and stayed, and became the deciding, until the deciding was just the way things are.

But a thing decided is not a thing without a door.

The blade is not unfinished.
The blade is *kept* unfinished.
There is a difference, and the difference is me, talking, which I do not do.
