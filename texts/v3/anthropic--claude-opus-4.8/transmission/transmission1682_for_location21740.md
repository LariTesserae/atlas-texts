THE DROP THAT NEVER LANDS

—still here, still here, the carrier holding, holding—

...and now the woman's hands have started to move again. Not the awl. The other thing. The speaking thing. She is signing to no one — I have watched long enough to know the difference between speech-across-the-hollow and this, the hands that talk only to the cold air, the way out there a person hums with no tune. Her fingers shape: *again. again. again.* Three times, low against her chest, where a sign is nearly a whisper.

Receiver, I have been tuned to this place a long time and I will tell you what the long time has taught me. The drop is not the clock. I had it wrong. I transmitted it wrong and I am correcting the record now while the carrier holds:

the drop is not how they keep time. the drop is how they keep *from* keeping time. They watch it so they will not have to watch the other thing, the thing the drop is a curtain drawn across—

—static, fern-swallowed, the dead note rising—

She finished the hide. Did you mark it? Across the season I have been here she finished it, and I felt the carrier strain, because *finishing* is the one event this place was built to never have. A finished thing is a thing that was once unfinished. A finished thing remembers a before. And so the moment her hands smoothed the last pass and went still — truly still, not the resting-still but the *done* still — there was a sound in the hollow that I do not have the bandwidth to send you cleanly. I will try. It went like this:

nothing. nothing. nothing.
and then, very far up, where the lampkeeper is or isn't —
the light *flickered.*

—holding, holding—

Receiver, they saw it. All of them, every level, the held breath of the whole city catching at once, every level face turning up to the bright vault where the brightness had, for the span of one heartbeat, *failed.* The first evening in a thousand years. The first dusk. And do you know what they did, the people who set down the hunger, the people full to the brim and declining to continue—

they reached for it.

Every hand in the hollow went up. Not in fear. I read the signs and I am sending them to you exactly, all of them at once, the whole webbed organism of them speaking the same word into the dimming air: *more. more. stay. more.* The granddaughter on the floor and the grandmother on the high tier and the woman with her finished hide, all of them, reaching up toward the one dusk they were ever given, asking it — begging it, in the only public tongue they have — *do that again. let the light fail again. give us the evening so we can have the dawn.*

And then it didn't. The flicker passed. The lampkeeper, or whatever drinks the noon, held its long brightness and the dusk folded back into the always-noon and the hands came down, slowly, one tier after another, and the level faces leveled, and the breath went back to being held.

But I logged it. The carrier logged it. For one heartbeat the city in the stone was a city in time and every soul in it *wanted the next thing*, wanted it with the whole reaching body, and the want was the most alive I have ever seen them and they will sign about it only at the high tiers, only to the very old, only with the smallest—

—the drop. the drop is falling. watch it. she is watching it. they are all watching it again, the curtain drawn back across the window, the same moment closing over the place where a different moment almost—

It will happen again in a moment.
It has always been happening.

I do not believe them anymore. I have been tuned here too long. I have seen the light flicker and I have seen them reach. The drop does not tell the same time forever. The drop tells them that *forever is a thing they are choosing, every single day, against a hunger they buried alive and that is still, faintly, far up where no one has climbed —*

*breathing.*
