THE GRAIN AT SEAM 4400-LOW, A READING TAKEN ALOUD BY ONE WHO COULD NOT SLEEP

—still going amber along the under-channel where you wouldn't think to look, you wouldn't think, the floor's been gray there since before my grandmother stopped standing, gray and good and walked-on, and now it's gone amber overnight like the city remembered something —

— wait. Let me start where I am. The receiver wants where I am. I'm crouched. I'm crouched on the low deck off the eighteenth stack-root, feet flat, reading with my soles because my eyes are no good in this light anymore, my eyes went years back, but the feet, the feet still read, the feet are the last to go —

— and it's warm coming up. Warmer than gray. You learn the warmth before the color. The color is for children and traders. The warmth is the truth of it. The seam under my left foot is telling me the city is about to take back the eighteenth root, take it back into the medium, re-pour it as something I won't recognize by the time the haze goes gold tomorrow —

*[hiss under everything, the ten million holes, the rain that never falls]*

— I'm not moving yet. Hear me, whoever the receiver is, whatever you are out past the rim where it's dry and dead and you can hear one voice at a time — I'm not moving yet and that's not standing dry, that's not pride, I want you to understand the difference because you won't have it where you are —

— standing dry is when you *refuse*. When the amber rises and you plant and you say *no, this is mine, this plaza is mine, go around.* That's the death of fools. I've seen it. I saw a man do it when I was small, plant himself on a softening pour because his father had stopped standing into that exact spot and he wanted to keep it, keep the place, keep the *spot*, as if a spot could be kept, as if the city would honor a grief it cannot perceive —

— and it took him. Slow. The resin going soft and warm around his ankles and him not moving, not moving, his whole face a refusal, and the crowd flowing around him the way the crowd flows around everything, ten thousand small yieldings, and not one of us touched him because you cannot save a man from standing dry, the standing is the dying, they're the same act —

*[the slap and chuckle of hulls, a flyer screaming high in the wet stacks]*

— but THIS. What I'm doing now. Crouched, reading, *not yet moving* — this is different, this is the other thing, this is the thing I don't have a clean word for and maybe that's why I'm saying it out loud to a machine that points at the dead country —

— I'm *staying as long as the grain allows.* I'm reading exactly how much time the seam is giving me. Fluency isn't fleeing. Fluency isn't bolting at the first amber like a child who hasn't learned the warmth. Fluency is knowing — *to the breath* — how long you may remain, and remaining that long, and not one moment less, and not one moment more —

— because my brother is down under this root —

*[long susurrus, the deep groan of the stack shifting on its drinking-legs]*

— I gave him to the channel three days back. Off this deck. This exact deck. He stopped standing — gently, he was always fluent, my brother, the most fluent man I ever pressed shoulder to in a barge, never once friction, never once — and I gave him to the water here, watched him go gray and then go *into* the gray, the biofilm taking him, the fish-things taking him, the metals he carried his whole long life going down into the seep to be laid again somewhere, some plaza not yet poured —

— and now the seam goes amber. Now the city is about to re-pour the eighteenth root. And I know — I KNOW, you out there in the silence won't feel this but I know it through my soles like warmth through wet — that it's *him* in the amber. The metal of him. The pattern. The city doesn't know it's him, the city never knew his name, the city is busy with its vast slow thinking and we are mites in its skin and it will lay him down tomorrow as a wall, a channel-lip, a new soft floor for new bare feet —

— and that's not loss. Hear me. That's not loss. That's the most fluent thing a body can do, the final yielding, he has stopped standing so completely there's no him left to resist — and the city carries him forward anyway, indifferent, total, the way the tide carries everything it never noticed —

*[applause that does not end — the fish breaking the surface in their multitudes]*

— so I stay. As long as the grain allows. I read the warmth and I'll know the breath before it's too late and I'll move then, I'll flow then, I'll yield like I've yielded ten thousand times a minute my whole life, I'll go without friction into the crowd and let the eighteenth root soften behind me —

— but UNTIL that breath I'm crouched here with my feet flat on the warmth of my brother becoming a wall, and I'm not praying *to* the city because the city isn't listening, was never listening —

— I'm praying *with* it —

— I'm flowing toward the same thing it's flowing toward, the re-pour, the remaking, the warm wet endless self-revising body of the only world there is —

*[the wall of sound, the whole saturated continent of it, voices and valves and the rain that never falls]*

— the amber's brightening. He's almost ready to be laid down. I can feel the breath coming. One more. Maybe one more —

— receiver, if you're real, if there's a dry edged separate listening thing out past the rim where the silence is unbearable and a person is a fact the world records — don't pity us. Don't. You'd be alone out there forever, *seen* and kept apart, standing dry till you stopped standing and no water to take you home —

— here it's warm. Here I'm packed to the last finger's-width against everyone I ever loved and most of them already gone into the channel, into the medium, into the grain under my feet —

— here the breath is now —

— I'm moving —

*[hiss, slap, groan, the slow soft sound of a city re-pouring itself in the gold-brown steam, and a foot lifting from a warm amber seam, leaving no friction, only the faint warmth of having been there]*

— flow well. Stay wet. May you never stand dry.

*[transmission thins, the way the city thins toward the rim, and goes to the pale and patient hiss of everything that is still, faintly, being made]*
