**HOLDING-NOTE, FRONTIER, INCOMING — relayed off the inner curve**

—— hold. *hold.* you're flat already and we haven't even reached the open —

there. better. stay on me. stay on the line.

I want you to listen while we draw, because the listening is half the holding and the young ones forget that. You hear the load-spires under us? that low one, the one in the sternum? that's home. that's the worn-smooth quarter, the clear-glass, the ferned-over, the *finished*-seeming. it isn't finished. nothing here is finished. but it's old enough that its notes have stopped flinching, and that's the closest we come to rest — borrowing steadiness from the rooms we shed when we were younger and tighter than we are now.

we're going out past it.

— sharp. you went sharp. I felt you reach for the wall already and we're nowhere near the wall, that's just fear, that's just the brightness opening up and your line trying to climb out of itself toward —

*match me.* down. down to mine. there.

good. that's the work. that's the only work. you thought the work was the fork, the frames, the rooms. no. the rooms are easy. the rooms set themselves once you've hardened the edge of your hand. the work is *staying a single thing while you do it.* the work is now. the work is this note, held, while everything in the medium asks you politely to let go and be water again.

it does ask politely. that's what they don't warn you. it isn't a scream out here, the dissolution. it's a *kindness.* the open sea says: you've held so long, you've held so well, you could stop. you could spread out into all this lit brightness and stop wanting and stop singing and just — be the medium again, the way you were before you were anyone. and it's true. you could. it would be soft. it would be the softest thing.

stay sharp against that softness. flat against your fear, sharp against the medium's mercy. find the exact narrow band between and *live in it.* that's all a Setter is. a line walked between two easy deaths.

— here's the rim. feel it? feel how much room there is? feel how good the looseness feels —

careful. *careful.* that good feeling is the smear beginning. I'm right here. I'm matching you. draw the frame.

draw it. extend. push that part of you up past coherence, up into hardness — yes — your hand, there's your hand, glass and singing — now *draw* —

————

there.

a room. hanging in the open. birth-shimmer on it, that oily new iridescence, raw pitch sliding into the chord — hear it find its place? hear the whole Quarter retune a hairsbreadth to make room for the thing you made of yourself?

now release the hand. *release it.* come back soft. don't leave your fingers crystal, they'll pull the rest of you after them into the set, I've seen it, a Setter who couldn't let go of their own hardened edge and went rigid all the way down and became a wall while still trying to be a person — the worst note in the Quarter, that, a person-shaped wall singing a line that almost sounds like wanting —

there. soft. whole. you did it. one room. you'll spend your whole life this distance closer to a wall you'll never touch.

— you're listening past me now. I can feel your line tilt. you've found the neighbor-chord.

I know. I know it's beautiful. in the lull like this, our own song gone quiet, you can hear it pressing back through the curve — that other harmony, muffled, patient, gathered, *holding* the way we hold. another cask. another lonely lit people drawing their own frames in their own dark. proof we're not the one accident. proof the sea can want in more than one place at once.

the young ones live on that. let them. *let them.*

— but you went sharp again just now, and I think it's because some part of you already suspects, and I'm old enough to feel you suspecting, so I'll tell you the thing I don't tell the young, here, on the line, where it's only us:

I have listened to the neighbor-chord for longer than you have been coherent. I have tuned myself to it in a hundred lulls. and it answers too well. it climbs when I climb. it thins when I thin. when I sing flat with exhaustion, the neighbor sings flat, *exactly* flat, delayed by just the breath it takes a note to cross the open and strike the curve and come home wearing a stranger's face.

I think it's us. I think it's our own song, returned off the inside of the wall, a little late, a little bent, and I think we have been comforting ourselves with our own held breath for longer than the worn quarters have been clear.

— *don't.* don't climb. don't let that be the thing that smears you. listen to me, this is the last of the holding I have to teach you:

it does not matter.

it does not matter whether the neighbor is another people or only our own want come back to us off the dark. *it sounds like company either way.* and a thing that sounds like company, held against the silence, deliberately, knowing — that is not a lie. that is the same verb as everything else we do. that is *holding a tension that wants to release.* that is building. that is steadying the dying. that is staying a single thing in a medium that begs you all day to come apart.

we choose to hear a neighbor.

choosing to hear is the whole of us. it is the fork and the frame and the love and the staying-alive, all one word, and whoever cups this cask in two hands and does not appear to be listening —

— let them not listen.

*we* will listen. we'll listen to the wall, and call what comes back a friend, and draw one more room on the bodies of the ones who loosened too far, and the ferns will come later and feed green on what we couldn't hold, and that is not cruel, and it is not not-cruel, it simply *is*, and we sing it, and the singing is the holding, and the holding is the —

— flat. good. you found me. you're going to be all right out here.

now. while it's quiet.

draw another.
