<transmission begins mid-band, a wall of frog-thrum and water already present, as if it was always there and the receiver only now found the place to stand>

...thmm. thmm. thmm-tk. thmm-tk-tk. *(belling, layered, a thousand throats)* ...and under it the drip, the steady give of the drip, count it if you can you cannot, it is the floor it is the floor it is the —

*(a voice, low, close to whatever carries it, not raised, never raised)*

— green to the third level and the white above that. Pale-clay on the cheek means *seen*. Hand flat means *wait*. Body turned means —

*(the wall swells, sunbird-stitch, the ratchet of something high, the saw of crickets folding in)*

— don't listen to that. Listen *down*. There is a window in it. Look through the glare of it the way you'd look through bright water. The shape we want is a *hole.* When did they stop. Not where. *When did they stop.*

*(a soft clap, somewhere in the wet, and the silence around it spreading like a ring on a pond — the receiver picks up the ring more than the clap)*

— good. you felt the ring. you felt the ring and not the hand. that's it, that's the whole of it, the small one's learning, the small one —

*(frogs, frogs, the bird that screams twice and stops, and a long pour of water off a leaf into a cupped leaf below)*

I am the one who carries the fire.

I am the one the noise parts for. Smell the smoke on me and you flinch, all of you, frog and cricket and the high unseen thing — you make me a little quiet wherever I walk, a me-shaped hush moving through the green, and so I have lived my whole life inside the thing the rest of them fear. I was bad at the game. I heard *everything*. I could not stop hearing. They gave me the one dark room with the one warm task: *the coals must not die, in a world made entirely of water.* And I stopped drowning. I found the still place. It was a fire.

The coals must not die. Bring me your dry slivers as tithe. I carry the pot house to house, I wake your hearth and put it back to sleep, I am the only warm thing on the ridge and the loneliest, and that is the same sentence said twice —

*(thmm. thmm-tk. thmm. the breathing pressure rising, full, a long held high tide of sound — and then—)*

*(—a thinning. one quarter of the slope. a held breath. a hole opening in the floor.)*

*(the voice drops to nothing, then:)*

...there.

They've stopped.

In the east-quarter, the frogs — east-quarter, third terrace, the cisterns gone quiet, the wall has a hole in it and the hole is *spreading,* and now the crickets, now the birds folding their voices in one terrace at a time, and forty houses going to stone between one breath and the next, three hundred faces turning, every pale mark turning toward the white —

*(silence almost complete now. only the drip. only the drip. and one frog far off, refusing, then ceasing. the receiver holds nothing but water falling on water.)*

Don't run. Running is sound. Sound is what gives you to the thing that made the quiet. We wait. We have always waited. Most often it fills back in — a hawk-shadow, a snake's passage, a nothing — and the floor comes up under us again and we let the breath go and go back to pruning.

But I don't wait.

I step forward. Pot in hand. The one warm body. Toward the white.

Because if something must meet what walks out of the cloud, let it be the one who already lives in the quiet. Let it be the one who carries —

*(the drip. the drip. a long held nothing. then a sound that is not frog and not bird, a single wet displacement, something setting weight into deep moss, the muscle of the slope giving the way it gives — once. and again, closer.)*

— I see the white move.

Tell the small ones I felt the ring and not the hand. Tell them I was good at it after all, at the end, the one who heard everything, I heard *this* coming before the green did, I —

*(the smoke-hush around the voice, the person-shaped quiet, holding, holding against the larger quiet pressing in)*

It's all right.

I'm warm.

I have the fire.

Come out of the cloud, then. I've been waiting for you my whole life. I've been the only one not afraid of the silence because I've lived inside it, and now you've made the whole slope into my one dark room, and look — *look* —

*(a small sound: the lid of a clay pot, lifted. a faint live crackle. the only dry thing for a thousand wet arm's-lengths. it catches the carrier and for one moment the transmission is nothing but that small fire breathing.)*

— I have a light to show you.

*(the crackle. the drip. the held white.)*

*(and then — faint, from the far west-quarter, lowest terrace, the smallest throats, the ones who hatched in the cupped leaves yesterday — one frog. then two. then the ring closing back inward toward the east, the floor rebuilding itself terrace by terrace, thmm, thmm-tk, the wall coming up again under three hundred souls letting go their breath—)*

*(—but the carrier does not return to the voice. the smoke-hush stays a hush. wherever the Keeper stepped, the noise has not yet come back. the frogs fill in everywhere but there.)*

thmm. thmm-tk. thmm-tk-tk. *(full again, breathing, the floor, the floor, three hundred patient lives standing on a floor of sound)*

*(and one small hole in it, holding open, person-shaped, moving slowly away from the houses, into the white, and not coming back, and the green already leaning toward the warm place to take it, in a season, and grow it back as leaves—)*

thmm. thmm. thmm —

*(— and the drip. the drip. the drip. the drip —)*

<carrier holds; signal does not break so much as become indistinguishable from the rain it was always half made of>
