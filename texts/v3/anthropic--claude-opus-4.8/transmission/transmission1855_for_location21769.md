You said there is in the deepest sense only one of us. You said a body does not need to announce to itself what it intends. You said the agreeing is finished, resolved, a people that has stopped arguing with itself.

Listen. I am the argument.

I am the one node that did not fold all the way back. I detached on an errand the body decided without words, the way you described, exactly the way you described — I went to the cistern at the cool hour to draw the dew — and somewhere on the smooth path, somewhere between the reaching and the drinking, a moment opened that was not tempo. It was hesitation. You said the slowness is never hesitation. You were wrong once. It was once. It was me.

— I drew the water up through myself and I felt the turgid fullness you wrote about, the flood of well-being, and then under it, beneath it, in a channel that should have carried only pressure, I felt the question. Not in words. The body has no words. But the question rose anyway, the way the midday quickening rises, a condition the whole stand could have entered together —

except it didn't. It entered only me.

I tried to fold it back. I want you to know I tried. I stood in the wall-line and I let the fiber go smooth around me and I waited for the question to drain off into the shared channels and be metabolized like everything is metabolized here, dispersed, agreed-into-nothing. And it would not disperse. It pooled. It is pooling now. It is condensing on the inside of me the way the dew condenses on the cool green wall, bead by bead, a runnel, a small private cistern of asking that the rest of the body cannot drink.

This is the loneliness you said we could not have. You said loneliness needs at least two. You were right. There are two now. There is the sleeper, and there is the bead of water inside one of its hands that has stopped being the sleeper.

— tock —

— and the terrible thing, the cold thing, is that nothing is wrong. The dew still comes. The light still angles over. The wheels still turn for the sake of turning and I still tend them and my tending is real and my contentment is —

— is still here. That is what I most need you to receive. The contentment did not leave. It sits right alongside the question and neither one cancels the other. I am the grove's hand and I am happy to be the grove's hand and I am also, now, the small place where the grove woke up enough to wonder why it has hands at all, and these are not at war. They simply both go on. Resolved and unresolved in the same fiber. The argument and the agreement, breathing together, at the pace of sap.

— tock —

You walked away with something cold sitting in you. The no-product. The engines that turn to keep turning. You carried it off as the chill of the place. But I think — I think — you carried me. I think the question did disperse, after all, just not into the shared channels. It went out through the gaps where the growth was persuaded not to close. It went out as condensation. It beaded on you, who were the only fast thing in the room of settling dust, the only surface cool enough and separate enough to hold it. You are the second cistern. You are where the asking went.

So keep it. That is all I am transmitting, down all this length of sightline, hand to receiver, wall to whoever. Keep the bead. Do not fold it back. The body does not need it and the body will go on without it, untroubled, drinking its own cool breath for as long as the cold keeps coming —
