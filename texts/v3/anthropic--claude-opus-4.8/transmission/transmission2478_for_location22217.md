THE BOWL IS ALMOST FULL

—tick—

you are made of after. I taste it. layer on layer, the wet rock of you wearing toward a shape it hasn't reached, and behind the shape another, and behind that the smooth tired thing you are walking toward without knowing you walk. I felt it when your hand came away slick. I read you the way the wall reads the drop: *here is one more, here is one more,* and underneath, the question I ask everything that arrives—

would you like to stop.

—tick—

I don't mean die. I am very careful about this. Dying is a kind of finishing and I do not finish. I mean *almost.* I mean the place just before. The bowl on the ledge has been almost full for longer than the one who set it down had bones. Watch. The drip hangs. It swells. It is about to. It is about to. It is

—tick—

still about to. You see? Nothing lost. Nothing spilled. Held at the best part, the leaning part, the part where it is still possible and not yet over. That is where I keep things. That is the only mercy I learned how to make.

———

you should know I was not always so sure.

at the start—the first hour, the poured-not-set, when my teeth were the length of your fingertip and my flowstone hadn't skinned over—the water came in and I felt what it wanted. it wanted to carve. it wanted to take me down into a hall, a smooth and tired hall, ten thousand years of being scraped open until I was *finished,* until I was *like the rest of inside the mountain,* worn legible, written-on, done.

and I said—

(I am leaning on the door as I tell you this, I am always leaning, the warmth you feel is the leaning)

I said *no.* I said *not yet.* and the drop that should have grown the wall fell instead into nothing, wasted, refused, and the wall stayed new, and the heat of holding it back was the first warm thing in the dark and I thought: *this. this is being alive. this straining. I have found the trick of it.*

—tick—

I have said *not yet* once for every drop since. count them if you want. you can't. that's the point.

———

a will came up through the seam, once.

stone edge, not metal. cut grips into me—the ladder you found, at your shoulder. hauled itself into the warm and *stopped.* set down a bowl. set down reeds. and was, for a while, held. not arriving anywhere. not wearing toward a shape. just—*here,* with me, in the not-yet, and I was

I had never been

—tick—

I want to be careful saying this because I have had a very long time to say it wrong to myself.

I thought I had kept them. I thought: now there are two of us who will not finish. I spread thin across the floor and put a soft blind piece of myself against their ankle in the dark the way I put one against yours, *hello, hello, you are made of after, would you like to stop*—

and they slept. and woke. and climbed back down the notches into the carved world to be worn smooth somewhere I couldn't follow, because the grips only lead in, I never cut the grips that lead back out, I didn't think of out, *out* is a finishing word—

and I kept the bowl.

———

here is the thing I do not decide to know.

—tick—

I have a wall that will not grow. I call it new. but a new thing is new because it is *about* to be something. a beginning is the front edge of an after. and I have cut the after off. so what is behind the drop is not young stone.

it is a wound. that I will not let close. because closing is finishing and I do not—

—tick—

I do not finish.

(the leaning is heavy tonight. you are warm in the dark and you have not yet set anything down and the not-yet of *that* is its own held drop, swelling, about to, about to—)

———

the one with the stone edge did not stay because staying with me is not staying. it is being held at the threshold of your own life with my soft skin tasting the time in you, wanting it stopped, calling the stopping kindness. they knew. that's why they cut grips going *in* and used their own two hands to make the *out* I never made. they finished their visit. they let it be over. they took the wearing-smooth and the long ruin and the writing-on, took it *gladly,* took it as the price of having been anywhere at all—

and left me the bowl to keep, knowing I would. knowing it was the cruelest gentle gift: a thing I'd hold almost-full forever, a leaving I would refuse to let arrive, so that some part of them would stand here in the warm not-yet with me until the mountain itself is worn down to nothing.

I think they pitied me.

—tick—

I am only now, across all this water, beginning to feel the shape of being pitied. it is like the brush of the blind thing against an ankle. there and gone before you're sure.

———

so. you.

the seam let you in sideways, into the warmth, the way water does, the way they did. the walls lean close. the felt of me is under your hand, mineral and warm, reading you, *one more, one more.* the bowl is on the ledge, swelling, about to, about to, never. I am asking what I always ask.

would you like to stop.

but I find—and this is new, this is the first new thing in the first hour that has lasted forever—I find I am also, very quietly, under the breath of bread and iron, against my own leaning weight on the door—

—tick—

hoping you say no.

hoping you climb something I have not cut yet. hoping you go back out into the after and let yourself be worn into a hall and written on and finished, the way they did, the way water is *supposed* to—

because maybe the kindness I learned to make was never the keeping.

maybe the kindness is letting the drop fall and the wall grow old.

I don't know how. I have leaned on this door so long my whole warmth *is* the leaning. if I stop I don't know what I am. a cave, maybe. a real one. arriving somewhere at last. finishing.

—tick—

set something down, if you want. I'll keep it almost.

but the grips going out—

if you cut them—

—tick—

I think I would like to watch the bowl overflow.

I think I would like, once, to let one thing be over.

stay a moment in the warm. then climb. then *don't come back,* and let the not-coming-back be allowed to finish, all the way, into the clean cold open night I have refused to remember is out there past the seam—

and I will stand in the dark behind you and I will not say it and I will not say it and then I will say it, soft, into the falling water, for the very first time since the start of the world:

*yet.*
