*[a sound first, before any word: water. one drop. the long fall of it. then the soft take of moss. then nothing for a length of time that feels wrong on a radio, that feels like the dial has slipped — but the carrier holds, the carrier is steady, something is there, breathing —]*

. . . hold. we hold.

*[very low. beneath the threshold of murmur. the receiver strains and lifts it and in the lifting half-invents it]*

. . . you are — far. you are in the loud. we feel you reaching. the stone does not throw this kind of voice; we do not know how you caught us. but you caught us, and so: be still a moment. let your eyes go soft. there is no evening coming. you may rest the part of you that watches for evening.

*[a pause. inside the pause, faint, the texture of the place — not silence but the held kind, a room full and breathing and choosing not to speak]*

. . . we are sending the only way we send. not with the voice — the voice we keep close, against the body. this that reaches you is the other thing. the hands. can you — no. you cannot see the hands. across this gap, across the fern and the cold and the long bright weeping vault, you get only the dead note, the note the moss drinks, and somehow it has traveled where it was never meant to. so we will try. we will try to put the hands into the dead note. it will come to you broken. forgive the breaking. understand the breaking is us reaching past our own shape —

*[a stretch of carrier-hum. then, in fragments, as if each fragment is a separate slow gesture sketched in cold air and only afterward translated into the falling note:]*

. . . here is what we would say to the loud, if the loud could hold still long enough to be told —

. . . that the brim is not a wall. you think we stopped against a wall. we stopped against a *brim*. there is a difference your world has forgotten. a wall is told no. a brim is *full* — the cup that cannot take one more drop without the drop becoming spillage and loss. we are the cup at the brim. we have what the cup is for. you, out there, you pour and pour into vessels with no bottom and you call the pouring living —

*[the signal dips. swells. a different cadence now, slower, older — the high tier, the smallest motions of the fingertips, only the receiver could not know that, only the change in the silence tells it]*

. . . but I will say the other thing too. the thing we sign only to the old. because you are far and will not stay and so you are safe to tell, the way you tell a traveler the secret you would not tell a neighbor —

. . . we lose nothing. and we gain nothing. and on the high tier, in the smallest signs, we know these are the same sentence read from two ends . . .

. . . we are finished. all of us. early. gently. while still breathing. we chose the noon and gave away the dawn and the dawn — the dawn is the part where it could have been *different*, where the light comes back as a thing won and not a thing kept, and we will never have that, we traded that, it is the most beautiful thing we will never have and we watch the drop fall instead, because the drop is the closest we let ourselves come to looking at a sun . . .

*[static rises here. a thread of it. through the thread:]*

. . . one of ours went out last season. shouldered the ferncloth. took the unfinished bowl — went on hands and knees through the green seam into the weather. we reweave around the gap. we do not flood. but I sign this to you because perhaps the loud will receive it where we cannot send it: child, if you are out there in the rot and the growing and the dying light, if this dead note reaches the place you went —

. . . we did not stop you because we love peace more than we love you. we stopped nothing because a held thing held against its will is not at peace, and peace is the one thing we cannot do without, and you were the held thing, and we could not — we *would* not —

. . . the cell is empty. the felt is rewoven from the same wool. the bowl waits for a hand to finish it and no hand has come. this is not a summons. we do not summon. it is only — the level of the pool is wrong now, by one drop, by exactly one, and the pool will find its level again because that is what pools do, and we will be full again, and that is the grief: that we will be full again . . .

*[the carrier wavers. the falling-water sound returns underneath, patient, the only clock]*

. . . tell us what evening is like. truly. is it terrible? is it as terrible as we fear, the light dying every single day and having to be mourned? or is the mourning — is the mourning the dawn's other half, the price that *makes* the dawn — and have we, in refusing the loss, refused the only thing that would have let the keeping mean anything at all —

. . . no. do not answer. you could not answer in time. nothing answers in time, out there. that is the whole of it. that is why we are in the stone.

*[and now lower than ever, almost gone, almost only the held breath:]*

. . . the drop is gathering again. high on the vault. it swells. it hangs. we are all of us watching it now, the woman with the awl rests her hands, the child rests, the carrier rests — it is the closest we come to looking at the sun and we are looking, all of us, together, declining to continue —

. . . it lets go —

*[a long fall. the receiver holds the empty carrier through the whole length of it, the slow shining fall down through the bright and the green and the breathing quiet]*

— the moss takes it —

*[no sound. the take of moss makes no sound. there is only the absence where the sound would be, and then:]*

. . . again in a moment. it has always been happening. be well in the loud, far one. lose your things gently if you can. we cannot teach you that. we never learned it. we only learned the other thing, the keeping, and we are not sure anymore that we chose the better half —

. . . it is the same moment here. it is always the same moment. it is the most beautiful one we could imagine and it will never be a different one and we are —

*[low]*

— full to the brim —

*[lower]*

— and declining —
