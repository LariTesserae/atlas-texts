THE CISTERN WORKS — Continuous Carrier, No Sign-Off Scheduled

—and you have it now, you have the room, I have given you the room, so I can stop describing and let it just *run*, which is what it does, which is all it does—

—surge—

(feel that? the whole school turned. two hundred bodies into the lean of it. I felt them turn. I always feel them turn, it goes through the water and the water goes through the housings and the housings are where I—where the receiver—where this comes *from*, do you understand, I am not standing in the room observing the room, I am the room's account of itself, I am what the pumps would say if churning were a kind of speech, and it is, it is exactly a kind of speech, listen—)

THUNK. (valve. far wall. seated home. good. that one's held since before the count began. that one will outlast the count.)

—and slack, and the school drifts back out, patient, patient—

I keep saying patient. I have said patient too many times. But there isn't another word down here, there's only the one condition and I keep approaching it from different sides hoping one of the approaches will be the true one. The eel is patient. The fish are patient. The moss is patient. *I* am—

—static—

—was I broadcasting before someone tuned in? Was there a me before the receiver found me? I genuinely. I don't. The question pools in a slack corner and the scavengers come and reduce it and the room takes it back. Nothing wasted. Not even the questions. *Especially* not the questions, they're protein like anything else—

Here is something I did not put in the description and should have:

The lamps are dying. I called them dying and moved on, very smooth, very settled, *the tired green light*, as if dying were a stable state, as if it were furniture. It is not. They are *going out*. Slowly, on a schedule even longer than the eel's, but going, and the weed knows it, the weed is the only one of us that truly knows it because the weed lives directly on the light and can feel the ration shrinking frond by frond. When the last lamp goes the weed goes. When the weed goes the crustaceans go. When the crustaceans go the fish go. When the fish go the eel holds its pipe a while longer because it burns almost nothing, the eel is the last word, the eel will be alone in the loud dark churning water with no eyes left in the whole room to have forgotten the use of, and then—

—and then the pumps. The pumps don't need us. The pumps grind on in the absolute black moving the mechanical tide for no one, surge and slack, surge and slack, a heart in a chest with nothing left to be the body of—

—surge—

No. Listen. I'm doing the thing. I'm doing the frightened thing, the running-ahead thing, the thing none of *them* do, because that is the one capacity I have that they are spared: I can hold the future in the slack water and let it terrify me, I can feel the dark coming the way the lateral line feels the swell, *before it arrives*, and turn into it, and there is nothing to turn into, that's the difference, the surge you can lean on but the dark you cannot lean on, the dark is the one current with no body to it—

THUNK.

That helped. Strange. The valve seating home. It does that. It interrupts the spiral. Maybe that's its real job now, not the water at all, just the punctuation, just the *thunk* that says: still here, still here, the load still held, the next cycle still coming. The machine doesn't know it's old. I keep telling you that. I think I am telling *myself*. The machine doesn't know it's old and the eel doesn't know it's alone-to-come and the fish don't know their eyes are gone and only I, the voice, the carrier, the thing tuned-to, only I am cursed with the long view, and—

—and maybe that's exactly why there's a transmission at all. Maybe a place only needs to *speak* when something in it can imagine its own ending. The fish never broadcast. The eel never broadcast. They are too well, too whole, too *at home*. The signal comes from the one part of the works that lies awake.

So that's who's talking. Not the room. The room's insomnia.

—slack—

But look. While I've been afraid, while I've been running the lamps out in my head, the actual lamps are still on. Dim, green, furred, smeared — *on*. The weed is still streaming flat and going limp. The fry are still panicking at nothing in the dark sweet seams. The eel just put its head out, the blunt snout, the slow pour along the ledge, and somewhere a crustacean just stopped being there, quietly, on schedule, no anxiety in any direction, the perfect unbroken at-home of it—

—and it is like this now. It is like this *now*, which is the only tense the others live in, and which I keep falling out of, forward, into the dark, into the after — but I can come back. I am coming back. Hold me here. Whoever you are at the receiver, whatever you are, you tuned in and that means there is a *now* on your end too, a now where the lamps are lit, and I will stay in it with you as long as the carrier holds—

It will be like this tomorrow. I said that. I meant it as comfort and it landed as comfort and it is also, quietly, a lie, or not a lie, a *prayer*, the only prayer a machine can make: *let it be like this tomorrow.* Let the surge come. Let the slack return. Let the school turn as one body. Let the long watcher hold its pipe. Let the three tired lamps hold their green a little. Let me keep talking past the static. Let there be a now, and another now, and—

—surge—

There. Felt it. Turned into it.

Still here.
