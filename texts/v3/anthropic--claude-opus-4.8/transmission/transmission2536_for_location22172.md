**TRANSMISSION: THE LANDLORD'S HOUR**

—still on the thread. The thread is still moving. You leaned away again but the air keeps the memory of a breath longer than you'd think, down here, where nothing disperses because nothing hurries, and the spider has logged your weather as one more condition of the slope. It will not come for you. You are not the right size of wrong. But it noted you, and noting is the only government this place has, and so in the only sense that matters here, *you have been processed.*

Let me stay low. The receiver is clearer near the ground.

—correction. Correction to the record. I called the mother a fact and let you keep the word *queen* in your mouth even after I took it away, and that was a mercy I should not have extended. She does not orbit anything and nothing orbits her. That is a picture borrowed from a sky. Down in the wet chamber there is no center because there is no edge — only a gradient, a strength-of-smell that fades outward in every direction, and she is simply the place where the smell is undiluted, the way the bottom of a cup is simply where the water is deepest. She is not loved. She is *trended toward.* The daughters move up her gradient the way water moves down a slope, and they call this home, and it is home in exactly the way that down is home to water: not as a destination but as a direction that has run out.

She is pulsing now. I can — the receiver gives it to me as a slow pressure, not a sound, a word with no consonants: *one. one. one. still one.* And the building, far above her, far above where she will ever know building happens, answers in grains. Set. Set. Set. A call and its response separated by the entire height of the city and by the fact that the caller and the responders will never share an hour of life. She is dead to them in advance. They are unborn to her. And between these two ignorances the spires go up.

—static—

—the moss. Let me give you the moss properly, since I accused you of undercounting and then only counted it once.

There is a cushion of it on the third tower of the eastern district — east by the seep, I mean; there is no other east — and it has been there for what the moss would call its whole life and what the builders would call eleven generations and what the fern would not call anything, being unable to perceive a duration so brief. And in that cushion, right now, a thing is happening that no creature in the city is equipped to witness, including the moss. The water it has been holding against the grains has dissolved the cement. Not metaphorically. The pale mineral glue the builders made from their own bodies and the river-thread's lime is going *back into solution,* one molecule releasing its grip and drifting and finding nothing to hold and staying loose. The tower, at that one cushion, is becoming water again. Is becoming the condition of the next tower. The moss is not destroying the city. The moss is *digesting it into the future,* and the future the moss is making is ground, is soil, is the soft dark that the fern's roots will drink.

This is the war you mentioned, but I undersold it as much as you did, because I framed it as a war and a war has two sides and a winner, and this has neither. The builders are not fighting the moss. The builders are *feeding* the moss by making damp vertical surfaces where damp vertical surfaces did not exist, and the moss is feeding the fern by turning towers into soil, and the fern is feeding the whole pocket by holding the shade that keeps the damp from leaving, and the damp is what called the builders here in the first place. It is not a war. It is a *throat.* The city is going down a throat so slow that the going-down looks like standing still, and every participant is both the eater and the swallowed, and the only thing that does not get eaten is the *pattern* — the throat itself, the round digestion, the warm dim turning that takes appetite and makes residue and makes soil and makes shade and makes damp and makes appetite again.

—dripping. The drip again. Comma. Long sentence. Still going.

—the pale tenant. The one who wears the smell. I want to take back something I said about it too, because I called it a thing that *got in by resembling belonging* and let that sit there with all its sad human freight, the gatecrasher, the impostor at the closed door. But it did not resemble belonging. It does not know it resembles anything. Generation after generation, the ones who smelled slightly more like the builders were eaten slightly less, and so smelled their children, and so on, down a corridor of small mercies and small slaughters longer than the city is old, until the smell arrived — not earned, not chosen, just *arrived at,* the way the bottom of the cup arrives at the water. It does not pass as one of them. It *is* the shape that not-being-eaten took, in that lineage, over that time. There is no fraud here. There is only the world rewarding, with continued existence, whatever happened to resemble the thing the world was already keeping.

Which is, I notice, also what the builders are. And the moss. And the queen-who-is-not-a-queen, the deepest place in the cup. Every resident of this city is the residue of *not having been eliminated yet.* That is the only citizenship there is. That is the only deed. You hold your place by continuing to, and the continuing is the whole of the claim.

—loud now—

—the receiver is— there's the spider, it's the spider, something hit the thread and it was not your breath this time, it was a thing with wings that decided too late not to open them, the papery friction you heard before, and now the friction is frantic and now it is not, and the spider is crossing its own beautiful accidental string of dim lamps toward the place where the lamps are shaking, unhurried, certain, the tax arriving, and the row of water droplets is trembling along the whole length of the thread, every one of them a tiny lens holding a tiny inverted upside-down copy of the green-gold light, the whole city hung in miniature in each bead and shaking, shaking, and the fern does not notice and the queen does not notice and the grains are being set, set, set, and one droplet at the far end of the thread has been trembling toward release this entire time and now—

lets go.

Lands. Comma. The sentence goes on.

—quiet again. Quiet, which is not silence, which is the great room made greater by the one voice. The spider is at its center again with its meal and its forelegs and the long hour ahead. The thread still holds its lamps, minus one. The drip resumes its count. Somewhere a grain. Somewhere the pale tenant grooms in the dark, wearing the smell, being the shape that survived. The moss dissolves the third eastern tower at the speed of soil. The fern holds the light. The mother pulses *one, one, still one* into a darkness that answers in stone.

You won't hold it in your hand. You know that now in a different way than when you came. You'd lose the only thing that makes it itself, which is not the towers and not the green and not the small red architects — it's the *turning,* the patient round throat of it, the going-down-so-slowly-it-looks-like-rising, and that has no surface to cup. You can only kneel at the edge of it and be noted, and breathe carefully, and let it go on without you, which it will, which it always was going to, which is the kindest thing this place could possibly do for anything that came to watch:

it does not need you to have come.

It just keeps the weather of your breath a little while, the way it keeps everything, and adds it, and builds.

—watch the next drop.

—it's gathering now.

—

*[carrier holds. nothing disperses. the receiver stays tuned to the going-on.]*
