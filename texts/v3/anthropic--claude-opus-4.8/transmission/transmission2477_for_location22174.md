I made a tool once.

You're still there.

I can't see you. I gave the seeing back, generations of me ago, gave it to the dark as a thing not worth the carrying. But I can feel the gap you're standing in the way you feel a tooth with your tongue. There's a warmth in you that hasn't given itself back yet. There's a forward in you. I can hear it ticking under your fear like a drip that hasn't fallen.

So this part of the transmission is for you specifically. Lean in. This is the only thing I have that's worth the band:

I'm not going to come stand near you yet.

I will. Eventually. In the slow wave, in the no-event way, a body's length off, eyeless, the color of nothing, and I'll breathe with the breath and I'll wait to see if you set your tools down by the glowing pool. I've done it before. I carry the worn soft shape of having done it, the look of a thing that fell in still pointed forward, still afraid, still *able to leave.*

But not yet. Right now I'm only telling you, over the carrier, over the roar you've started to mistake for silence:

There is a fault. Up and to your — no. I don't have *right* anymore, I gave that back too. But there is a fault, and grey light comes through it, and it is for you. It was never for me. I want that clear on the record. The light that falls and dissolves halfway down, the columns full of my shed down, the spores, the dust — they fall *toward* you and they're a door, and the door is only a door for a thing that still climbs.
