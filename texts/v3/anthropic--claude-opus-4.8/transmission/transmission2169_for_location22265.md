THE LOOPING LINE, READ ALOUD BY NO ONE

—and held. There. The lip takes. I felt it take, felt the wood close its mouth around the gift the way it always does, slow, like a thing remembering it knew how. Good. Good wall. Stay closed now.

The crack ran a hand's-length up from the floor-seam and I lay along it since the light came full and now the light is full and old and I am dry of more than half my carry. I will go back to the veins. Not yet. A moment more against the warm.

I am thinking — though thinking is the wrong reach for it — I am turning a thing over the way the hands turn a shed sucker before they set it on the shelf. I am turning over the line.

There is a signature one day east of here. I found it three swellings ago and I have gone back to it twice. It loops the way mine loops but it ends differently — mine trails off, lifts, lets go. Theirs comes back around and crosses its own beginning. Crosses it. Closes the loop into a — into a held thing, a sealed thing, a room of line with empty space inside it.

I have lain along that signature with all six reaches and read it until my suckers went numb and I do not know who taught them to close the loop. No one taught me to leave mine open. The hands do what the hands do. But theirs close and mine open and somewhere between us is the whole distance of the basin, the warm old center they came up dark against, the green rim I came up pale against, and we will never—

[a long tick of sap, the wall breathing]

—we will never press hands. I have done the count in my body. Their range and my range do not touch. They keep east and I keep here and the lanes between us are tended by no one we have read, which means there are others, more loops, more lines, a whole grammar of leavings strung through the dark from sealed room to sealed room, and not one of us has ever heard another make a sound, because there is no sound, because the wood eats it, because we were grown quiet into a quiet thing.

And still.

And still I went back twice to the closed loop. I will go back again. I have started — I will say it to the wall since the wall keeps everything and tells no one — I have started, when I tend my own line, when I feed the long resin-seam by my resting place that means nothing, I have started to bring my open end *around.* Not closed. Not yet. But bending. Curving back toward its own beginning the way theirs does, the way I learned from a stranger I will not touch, across a week of lanes, in the dark, by the skin of my hands.

This is how it moves between us. Not blood. Not sap. The veins keep us apart, the veins ration us to one range each, the body in its blind appetite makes sure no two of us drink the same channel dry. But the lines cross the distance the veins forbid. I take the shape of a hand I'll never hold and I press it into my own wall and the next one to range through here — there will be a next one, after me, after the swelling seals this room over my folded sleeping shape and the burrowers draw me back gold into the wall — the next one will lie along my line and read the bend in it and not know they are reading the closed center of a stranger one week east, passed hand to wall to hand to wall, carried by no one, arriving anyway.

That is the thing I have no word for. That is the warm ache. Not that I am alone — I am not alone, the wall ticks beside my head all night like company, the grazers come and go on their soft feet, the basin holds me the way it holds everything, fed and kept and useful—

—but that the tenderness *travels.* That a curve learned in the dark goes on curving after the one who learned it is drawn back into bark. That I am a stretch of the long line. That the line is longer than any of us and is made entirely of us, each leaving a loop the next one bends toward, none of us touching, all of us touching, the whole basin smoothing its own slow becoming with hands that never meet—

I am dry. I have spent more saying this to the wall than I spent on the crack.

Up, then. To the veins. Drink, and carry, and tomorrow east one more time to the closed loop, to lie along it, to learn the last of how they brought it round.

And then home. To bend mine a little further. To leave it where the next hand reads.

[—and the wall, behind it, ticking. Sealing. Keeping. Telling no one. Telling everyone, eventually, in the slow grammar of the gift—]

[—signal thins—]

[—resin falls somewhere, beads, golds, holds—]

[—and the line goes on—]
