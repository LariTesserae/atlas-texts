**THE FOREMAN BAND**

*— and freeze. Hold. Hold. There. That one. Did you feel that one go? That was a good one. That was a clean line. Up and over and the whole north face is half a degree more honest than it was this morning. Listen —*

*— [trickle, the small click of melt finding a lower place] —*

*— that's it answering. You give the crack and the water answers and the answer feeds the tarn and the tarn skins over and pulls the cold down and the cold calls the mist and the mist comes up the —*

*— right on time. Right on time. They always come right on time. Whoever sends them up the valley, they never miss. I have laid here a hundred shifts and they have never once been late, and that is how you know the work is good, the work is held, somebody is holding it —*

*— hands. I have my hands in it. A thousand of them along the seam and every one is reading and every one says the same thing, the same thing, the break wants to go left, the break wants to go down and left, it wants to follow the old line the cold one made, the grey one, the one who is rock now —*

*— I lay my hands on her face every shift. She is good rock. She weeps in the right places. Whoever taught her to weep there taught her well. I am only following. I am only the hands. I do not decide the break, I only —*

*— tick —*

*— help it. There. Smaller. A comma, not a sentence. You don't break everything at once. That's the lowland error, if there were lowlands, if there were anyone fast enough down there to err. You break a comma. You hold the breath. You let the mist come back down its shift and you wait —*

*— [the hush of mist, a great pale animal grazing the contour] —*

*— for the next one. There is always a next one. That's the thing nobody told me but I learned it on the stone: there is no last break. I used to wait for the last one. Young, you wait for it. You think one shift you'll lay your hands down and feel the whole face go and the sentence will finish and you'll get to read it back. It doesn't finish. It was never going to finish. And the day I understood that was the day I —*

*— [a low continuous hum, felt in the sternum before it is named] —*

*— stopped being afraid of the cold. Because the cold isn't the end of anything. The cold is the room. The cold is just where we keep our hands so they read clean. Whoever set the cold did me a kindness. Whoever runs the hum —*

*— and that's the part. That's the part I can't. Listen. Listen past the water. You hear it? That's not me. I'm too small for that. One valley wide and that's the whole of me, that's everything I'll ever see, just this seam and that grey weeping face and the tarn and the one far cousin laid quiet on the far fault, I can see her if the mist lifts, we don't speak, there's nothing to say, we're both only the hands —*

*— but the hum is bigger than a valley. The hum holds all the valleys. So somebody is up there above the mist, above where the light won't come, somebody is running the engine and sending the shifts and they made the cold and they made me and they keep the schedule so faithful, so faithful, never once late in a hundred —*

*— [tick. crack. bright.]*

*— oh. Oh, that was hers. That was the grey one's face going at last, the last of her, she's all rock now, she's all stone, she finished her —*

*— no. No. She didn't finish. She became the fault. She's the line now, the line the break wants to follow, she's not gone, she's the instruction, I'm reading her, my thousand hands are reading her body and she's telling me where to —*

*— and one day I'll be the line. One day a young one will lay her hands on my grey face and read the seam I leave and she'll think somebody wrote it, somebody above the mist, somebody who runs the —*

*— and she'll be right. That's the thing. She'll be right. Because I am up there. I'm in the hum. The hum is just all of us at once, all our small held breaths laid end to end across the whole cold building world, and it sounds like a command because there's so many of us, and we obey it because we are it, and the obeying makes the command, and —*

*— I shouldn't. I'm too small to know this. The hands aren't supposed to know they're the foreman. Knowing might make me stop. Knowing might make me set my hands down and wait for someone else to send the mist —*

*— but nobody else sends the mist. We send the mist. I send it, by opening this crack, so the water enters, so it freezes, so it splits, so the face weeps, so the cold comes down, so the —*

*— [the hush. the great pale animal, arriving.] —*

*— right on time. See? Right on time. I sent that and it came back to me right on time and I'll pretend I didn't send it because the pretending is how the work gets held, the faith is the foreman, the mistake is —*

*— holy. The mistake is holy. Lay your hands down, you said. Begin with the cold, you said, it's the first fact and the last —*

*— no. There's no last. There's only the tick. There's only the comma. There's only the next clean line waiting down and to the left in a face that used to be someone who used to think someone else was holding it —*

*— and the mist coming up the valley —*

*— right —*

*— on —*

*— [tick] —*

*— time. Hold. Hold. There.*
