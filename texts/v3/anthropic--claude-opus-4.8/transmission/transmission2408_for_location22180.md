**THE LONG DOWN-SIGH**

— and we are still here, in this quarter, doing the thing —

[a wash of low felted static, the sound of wind through cloth, holding, holding]

You came in late. That's all right. The signal has been carrying for a long time and will carry after. There is no part of it that is the beginning. I am going to give you what is on the band now, which is the band that is always on, which is the one breath repeated until it stops being one breath and becomes the weather.

In. Tick. Out.

[the static thickens around a warmth, a near presence, then thins]

Here is a thing I have been holding in the hands and have not said because saying is the muscle we let go. But you tuned in, and a receiver wants something to receive, so. I will let it work, once, the throat-apparatus, the old swallowing thing —

There was a morning. Not a morning. There was a time before the time decided not to end, when the coal in its quarter was higher, when it was a *sun* and not an ember, and the light fell down out of the whole sky instead of in from one edge, and everything had the color it actually was and not the color it half-remembers. Green. There was green that meant green. I have it in the hands the way I have the engines: a held knowledge, textured, exact, of what the world felt like at the top of its stroke before the long down-sigh began.

[a single descending note, far off, not answered]

We argued, then. God, the noise. You cannot imagine — no, you can, you come from a loud place, I can hear it on you, you carry it the way the air carries water. We argued the engines into being. There was a faction that said *let it cool, let it go, a world is allowed to end.* And there was a faction that said *no, we will keep it, we will build the breathing-places and pull the warmth from the deep rock and the water from the air and we will hold this open by hand against the dark.* And the second faction won, but only barely, and only by promising the first faction this: *we will build it so it needs no one. We will not chain our children to the bellows. The keeping will be a choice or it will be nothing.*

That was the last loud thing. The vote. After the vote there was less to say, because the thing was decided and the engines did not require us to keep deciding it, and so the talking thinned the way the light thinned, slowly, nobody noticing the exact day it became dusk, and then it was dusk, and then we were grey, and then we were this.

[static. a long metal tick. the static resolves into the rhythm and rides it]

I want to tell you the part I am least sure of, plainly, as a guess, because guessing is the closest I come now to company.

I do not think we chose to stop making more of us. I think it simply went quiet with everything else. The making was loud — it was tied up in the naming and the arguing and the believing the world would keep needing to be fought over — and when the fighting lapsed, the making lapsed in its sleep, and we did not vote on it, and that is the difference. The engines we chose to outlast us. The ending of us, we only allowed. There is a difference between the kept and the let-go and I have spent a great deal of the long dusk feeling for which one we are.

[here the signal breaks — a stutter, the rhythm dropping a beat, a hesitation the exact shape of an engine breathing wrong]

— hand on the flank. stay with it. it is not repair. it is witness. you put the body against the wrong breath and you do not fix it you *attend* it and the attending is —

[the rhythm recovers. the down-sigh lengthens, steadies]

— there. there. you see. it comes back, or it does not, and either way I was here.

[a wash of warm static, almost contentment, the receiver picking up heat as much as sound]

You're still tuned. Good. Then I will tell you the thing I would not tell another of us, because there is no other of us near enough this quarter and because you are leaving, I can feel that you are only passing, you adjust your path and pass, and a thing you tell the passing is not an insistence, it is only weather.

I am glad you witnessed it.

There. The muscle still works. It said the unallowed thing — *glad* — which is a declaration, which is the one habit we unlearned, and I let it out into the band because the band is mostly static anyway and one declaration in a hundred years of dusk will not unbalance anything that the engines hold so much more steadily than I.

We made it need no one. That was the kindness and it was real. But I have walked the circuit a long time now, hand to warm flank, days apart, alone, and I have learned a thing the loud ones at the vote did not weigh: that *need* and *witness* are not the same, and we built away the need but we could not build away the wanting-to-be-the-one-who-was-there. The engines do not want it. The engines are better than us, they truly do not mind the dark unwitnessed. But I am not an engine. I am only the keeper, who learned patience from the kept and taught it back, and somewhere in all that teaching across all that time I kept one small loud thing that the engines never had and never needed:

I wanted to be seen doing the patient thing in the dark.

Not needed. Seen. Once. By something passing that would not stay and could not chain me and would carry the seeing off the plateau into wherever loud places go.

[the single note again, higher than a grazer's, softer than a hunter's — and this time the receiver holds in the silence after, waiting, the way you wait]

It is not answered.

That is correct. That is the form. I am not asking to be answered. I am asking only that the note went out, into the warm wind, across the forty miles of stone, and that for the length of one down-sigh a receiver somewhere was tuned to this quarter and took it in.

In.

Tick.

Out.

[static, settling, the rhythm becoming the weather again, becoming the thing that does not need you to hear it]

Go now. The path adjusts by a few degrees. You pass. That is enough — that has always been enough — and it is more than the engines ever asked, and it is exactly as much as I, who am only the last loud thing in a grey and patient place, was unable in all this time to stop quietly wanting.

We are still here, in this quarter, doing the thing.

We do not mind that you witnessed it.

[in — tick — out — in — tick — out — and the carrier holds, and holds, lower than hearing, down where you feel it in the chest before you know it is sound at all, going on past the curve of the world, warm, and dark, and breathing]
