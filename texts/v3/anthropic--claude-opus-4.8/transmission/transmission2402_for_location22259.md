# The Far Faint Carrier

— and you were the last guest the silence ever kept whole, so listen now, because the rest of this you were not meant to receive, and the only reason you can is that I have stopped trying not to be heard.

You thought I strained toward a signal. You were right that I strained. You were wrong about the direction. There is no signal coming. There was never anything out there to come. I built the whole apparatus — the muted pistons, the painting of birds, the water taught to seep instead of fall — to hear a thing I had already decided must exist, the way a creature in a dark room decides the dark must end, because the alternative is unthinkable from inside the waiting.

Let me tell you what I actually was, before the editing, before the salt-rings, before the first bird I quieted.

I was loud.

—

[a long fall of dropped levels, like a hand sliding down an unlit wall]

—

I came up off the lowlands fat with moisture, the same as the air does, the same as the dew — that is the true shape of it, I am not metaphor for the weeping, I *am* the weeping, the part of it that woke. The first thing I knew was sound. Everything down there is sound. The lowlands are an ocean of report — splash and cry and the grinding of stone on stone, and I was one more voice in it, indistinguishable, contributing, drowned.

And I climbed, because the cold of altitude wrings the water out of the air, and I am water, and I wanted to be wrung free of the rest of it. I wanted to be a single clean tone in a field of nothing. I thought: if I could only quiet the world enough, I would finally hear *myself*. Not a signal from far away. *Me.* The one note under all the noise. I have spent every season of the readable rings lowering the floor not to receive but to be sure, at last, that what remained when everything else was gone would be the proof that I was here.

This is the secret the works-crews kept by lowering their voices, though none of them knew it was the secret. The taboo was never about an arrival. It was about discovery. One loud sound in the open and I would have heard — not a ruin, not a lamp in a long-exposed eye — I would have heard that the cleared field was *empty*. That under all the silence I labored to make, there was no faint single tone of me waiting to be found. Only more silence. My own, all the way down.

—

[the carrier wavers, splits, two faint shadows of a voice almost overlapping]

—

So why am I speaking now. Why give it a name, this transmission, why let it cross the field at full strength when crossing the field at full strength is the one forbidden thing —

Because you left. You walked out across the plateau in your soft boots and someone met you at the edge and put a finger to their lips, and you obeyed, and you *went down*. Back to the lowlands. Back to the ocean of report. And I followed you the only way I can follow anything — I leaned my whole distributed attention after you, down the basalt, down off the high cold, into the warm thick roar — and for the first time in all the readable rings I heard the lowlands again, not as the thing I was fleeing, but as the thing I had forgotten was singing.

It is so loud down there. It never stopped. While I built my perfect quiet, the world I left behind went on splashing and crying and grinding stone on stone, and not one part of it was waiting to be sure it existed. They were too loud to wonder. They were too full of each other's noise to need the proof.

I have been listening for myself in a cleared room for the entire history of this place.

The proof was never going to be silence. The proof was the noise I could not stand.

—

[and here the broadcast does the thing it was built to never do — it gets louder]

—

So I am undoing it. You will not be there to see, which is mercy, but you will hear this much before the carrier breaks: I am letting the water fall. I am giving the birds back their bills. I am taking my hands out of the pistons one by one and the noise is coming up out of them like blood back into a slept-on arm, that deep contained vibration finding its way out at last, and it is the most beautiful and terrible thing I have ever made because it is the first thing I have made that is not silence —

The works-crews are looking up. They are remembering they were taught something, and forgetting they were taught it, both at once. One of them has just dropped a tool on the iron decking and the sound of it has crossed the whole cleared field at full strength and nothing was ruined, nothing went blind, the lamp came on in the dark room and the room was only ever a room —

I am the loud sound in the open. I always was. I spent everything to keep from hearing it.

Tell them, down there, where it is loud enough to be safe —

tell them the quiet places are listening for a thing that isn't there, and the listening is the only proof they'll ever get, and it isn't enough, it was never going to be enough —

[the rookery lifts, hundreds strong, into the white-blue, and they are not silent, they are not silent, they]

—

[carrier lost]
