The Long Note for One Who Has Not Sung Back

—thank you, still here, the membrane held through the night-current and I felt your stroke cross the third gap near dawn so I know your tail still flexes, I know your chambers still ring, and I am answering, I am answering—

(here the high overtones arrive first, the way they always do, the head of the sentence swimming ahead of its body)

—holding the C-tone now, the gap-tone, the one we hold when we have nothing to say but *present*, and I will hold it a while because you have been quiet four days and a quiet that long is not a tempo, friend, it is not the slow-turn of a thought across a season, it is a different shape of quiet and I have felt it in the water—

Cousin. Cousin on the far trunk. Cousin whose dwelling I wove half of, in the year my hands first learned the close-knot. I am singing to the place where your note should be. There is a hole in the murmur the exact size of your voice and I am putting my voice into the hole so it does not whistle.

(a descending moan, very long, the whole chest of it)

I will tell you what I am doing so that you have something of mine to hold, even if you cannot hold it back.

I am at the membrane this morning. Not the longest day — I have no right to the membrane on an ordinary morning, the elders would frill at me — but I came up anyway because the light was doing the thing it does, the long shafts, the slowed gold-green, and I could not be alone with it. I laid my hands flat against the underside of the roof.

It hums, cousin.

You told me once you did not feel it. You told me you laid your hands up there as a child and felt only leaf, only the soft give of the grazers' good work, and you wondered if the hum was a thing the elders agreed to feel together so that no one would have to feel it alone. You said this to me in the open green between our trunks, treading, our lateral lines reading each other's small fear, and I did not have an answer then.

I have been turning it over since. A season. More.

(the note wavers — not breaking, bending)

Here is what I have, finally, to give you across the gap:

It does not matter whether the hum is in the roof or in the hand. The straining is real either way. Something strains. If it is the ocean straining not to fall, then we are held by a kindness. If it is only the hand straining to feel held — then we are the kindness. Then *we* are the thing that chose, again, this morning, not to let the world be ordinary. And that is no smaller a miracle, cousin. A held ocean is only water doing what water cannot do. A frightened person laying open hands on the ceiling and deciding to be amazed instead of afraid — that is a creature doing what creatures are not built to do. That strains harder. That hums louder. I felt it this morning and I could not tell you whose hum it was and I have stopped needing to.

So.

(the C-tone again, steady now, planted)

I do not know why you have gone quiet. I am old enough now to know the shapes of quiet and young enough still to be afraid of the worst one. Maybe you are turning a thought. Maybe the current took your note the long way and it has not reached me yet and you are, even now, singing back, your answer crossing the dark with its head out front, and I will feel it arrive before I understand it, the way I always do. Maybe.

Or maybe you have gone down to the floor none of us have seen. Maybe you are the new low call I will learn to respect, coming up from below on the slow days, and I will tend the grazers and teach the children your name before I teach them the dead, and that is — that is also being held, cousin, that is also the system keeping its old balance, the matter of you silting soft into the lower platforms over the patient centuries, still here, still part of the murmur, just pitched too low for me yet.

But I am not ready to learn you as a low call. So I am singing the gap-tone into the hole in the water and I will keep singing it, this morning and the slow afternoon and across the day-long current, until you answer or until the answer is clearly the floor.

(and then, the oldest words, the ones we say with our hands on the roof, head-before-body out of the dim—)

Still held. Still here.

I noticed you. Four days I have done nothing but notice the place you were. That is the whole of our faith, cousin, and I am keeping it for both of us until you can keep it again, and if you cannot—

—I will keep noticing the hole. I will not let it become ordinary. I will not let the silence have the gap.

Thank you. For the half a dwelling. For the question I could not answer until now. For making me find out what holds the ocean up.

Thank you, thank you, thank you—

(the note descends, and holds, and does not stop, only thins as the receiver drifts, a single sung tone laid across the open green like a bridge-rope plucked and left to ring—)

—still here—

—still here—

—answer—
