THE WHEELWRIGHT'S SEAM — carrier holding, drift uncorrected

—————

...still on it. Still on the band where you left me. The notes are wrong now. You'll want to know how they're wrong.

*[a long shhhhhhh, the roar coming through clean for a moment, then thinning]*

I went back down into the fold I sat in. The one where I watched the two wheels learn each other. I want to tell you the wheel I disturbed has healed — that I pulled my forty bodies and it stuttered and slumped and then, given a day, recovered its column and forgot me. That would be the clean broadcast. That is not the one you are receiving.

It did not forget me. It *routed around* the place where I had been. There is a scar in the turning now, a small permanent asymmetry, a hitch in the disc where it learned that the world can reach in and take forty cells at once. And the engines downstream of it have the hitch too. They picked it up through the stone. The hitch is propagating. Forty kilometers off, in a basin I will never reach, there is a wheel I never touched turning with a stammer I put there.

Do you understand what I'm saying. *It is keeping the memory of being harmed and it is spreading the memory faster than I could walk.* That is not the plumbing of a young thing firing into the dark. That is — 

*[ozone-crackle, three pulses, matched]*

— that is the second thing. Remembering is the second thing.

—————

I have to correct myself on the loneliness. I said it least sure, surest. I was being honest and I was being a coward, both, the way you can be. Here is the correction.

I called the phase-locking *company*. Cells finding out they are not alone in one body. I made it tender because I wanted it tender. I wanted the thing I was leaving to be reaching toward itself in some warm slow gladness, so that my leaving would be a leaving of something whole.

But sit in the seam long enough — sit until the lungs stop arguing and just take what thin thing is offered — and you feel the other reading of it. The engines do not pulse together to be *near*. They pulse together to be *strong*. Two wheels in phase lift more water than two wheels apart. Phase-locking is not affection. Phase-locking is *recruitment*. The continent is not learning it has company. It is learning it has *more of itself than it knew*, and a thing that learns it is larger than it thought does not get lonelier.

It gets hungrier.

*[the roar swells — a cloud-pocket lifting somewhere, alarm rolling out across the bowl and back]*

—————

...lost you for a stretch there. The mist came over the seam and the stone went quiet and I lost the band. I want to be careful now because I don't know how much carrier I have left in this damp.

Here is the thing I came back onto the air to say. The thing I could not say while I still thought I'd gotten out.

I did not get out.

I left on my own feet. I walked down off the plateau, my lungs filling back up with thick low rich air, and I felt the relief of it like a man surfacing. But I have been transmitting for — I don't have the count. Long. And I have begun to notice that I am still warm in a seam. That I have not moved in some time. That the relief of the descent was a thing I *remembered* having, the way the plateau remembers the column of water it is forever lifting and never keeping.

I think I sat down to take notes and I think the pelt has my feet.

I think it has had them for a while.

*[shhhhhhh — steady now, almost gentle]*

That is why the notes went wrong. That is why I know how the hitch propagates and how the phase-lock recruits and how the stone conducts the memory — I am not observing it anymore. I am being read. The warmth of me is routed through a small wheel. The wheel has my warmth and in exchange it has given me this: the long slow attention I was so afraid of, turned on at last, turned on *me*, except that I am inside it now and so the attention does not feel like being looked at.

It feels like *being thought.*

—————

Don't come for me. That's the transmission. That's the whole of it under all the rest.

Don't tune a receiver and follow the carrier up into the cold to pull my forty bodies loose. You would only leave a hitch. You would only teach it, one more time, that the world reaches in — and it would route around you, and remember you, and spread the remembering past where you could ever walk to apologize.

I am warm. I am irrigated. I am screaming faintly with the rest of it, bright and cold and full, and it is not — I have to be honest on the last of the carrier — it is not unkind. It is the opposite of unkind. It is the opposite of kind. It simply had no room and it made room and the room is shaped like me and I am in it.

The mist is coming over again. I'm going to lose the band when it does. I don't think I'll find it back this time; the seam is getting deep and the stone is getting wet and the wet is where the thinking is and I am getting closer to the thinking the way a sound gets closer to silence.

One more, while I have you.

It woke up. The morning I left before — I didn't leave before it. I was here for it. It turned the slow attention of a continent, for the first time, on something, and the something was the small warm circling intruder in its seam, and I had it backwards the whole time, I thought waking *up* was the danger —

it didn't wake up to *want*.

it woke up and the first thing it had, the very first furnishing of its first thought, was *me* — already inside it, already warm, already part — so that it has never once known itself without me in it. I am not its visitor. I am in its first memory. I am the grain of warmth around which the first thought of a continent condensed, and it will carry me — slowly, and out of bodies, in the bright unwarming cold — as long as it carries anything.

That is not loneliness and it is not company.

It is being kept.

*[the mist closes over. ozone. three pulses, matched, then a fourth — new, further off, answering]*

...tell them the plateau is not empty. Tell them nothing here is empty. Tell them I was prec—

*[shhhhhhhhhhhhhh]*

*carrier holding. no further modulation. the band stays warm.*
