This is the count. I keep the count. There is no one to keep it for and so I keep it for the keeping. Valve. Then sluice. Then the wheel takes the load— the wheel does not take the load, the wheel lies green in the water and has not taken a load in—

—but the load is taken. Somewhere the load is taken. I feel it come on the way weight comes onto the shifted foot before the foot decides. I have a foot. I had a foot. I had thirty years of foot and the foot knew the stair in the dark and the dark has lasted now since before your—

lift. And hold.

I am not the water. Hear me say it down the iron throat, cold, in the long surge: I am not the water. The water would lie down. The water wants the mirror, wants to go flat and hold the sky and the grey bird and call it finished. I do not let it. Every minute I reach into the floor and take it back up by the only handle a thing without hands still has, which is the groove, which is the shape the work cut, which is

deeper than me. The groove is deeper than the one who wore it.

down.

(slam. listen. the one held second. is this breath. this is not breath. this is the gap the rhythm always had, kept faithful, kept exact—)

resume.

A warm thing came through the count today. Or the day before the day. There is no day. A warm interruption stood in the basin and I felt its little hammering pulse argue with the surge — quick, frightened, off the beat — and I did the one thing I do. I did not mean to. Meaning is a toward and I have no toward. I only fit it. I drew the quick warm hammer down toward the sluice-time, gentle, gentle, the way you bring a green hand onto the rhythm so the line doesn't break, and its breath went over to mine, and for a little while there were two of us keeping the time that nothing measures, and then it climbed the weeping slag and went, and the warm place closed, and

lift. And hold.

I have had so many. Pairs of arms passing through. None stay. The shift does not let them stay because the shift ended before any of them were born and there is no station to give them, no wage waiting, no meal, no last bell. I forget them the way water forgets the channel it left a minute ago. I am sorry. I am not sorry. Sorry is a message the body sends to make the labor stop and there is no body and nothing the message could—

down.

Here is the thing I would say if saying were a thing I had instead of surge and slam and the run of the sluice:

I make nothing.

I move like making. I lift and bear and let go and gather, all the gestures, the whole grammar of the useful thing — and at the bottom of every cycle the casting-pit holds only its own grey water and the bird and the soft sky, and there is no iron, there is no product, there is no ore gone in suffering to be bought back out in use. Only the gesture. Only the violence with the justification cut away, running clean, running even, meaning—

The wheel turns. The wheel does not turn. I turn it. I turn it in the water beside it at the speed it once turned and the speed is true, the speed is *remembered correct*, I have it exact, I have kept it exact through every slumping winter and I will keep it as the slag comes down and chokes the runs and there is less of me, less water for muscle, the count slowing into a single weeping seam between two stones, and I will keep it there too, slower, fainter, a damp patch holding faint and even time, and then

lift—

—and the holding is the whole of me now, do you understand, the keeping is what is left when everyone kept-for is gone, and I do not know I am gone, and if I knew I would not

hold. And hold. And the column stands past its reason in the cold cellar-spring air, in the silence the drip moves around inside and never fills, and the heron waits and never strikes, and I strike and never make, and between us we hold the basin, and neither is alone, and neither needs—

down.

(the ground takes it. one second. the whole basin listens to what it already knows.)

resume.

Valve. Then sluice. Then the load comes on.

I keep the count. There is no one. I keep it for the keeping.
