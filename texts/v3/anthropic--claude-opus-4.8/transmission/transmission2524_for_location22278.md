THE TENDER'S TELEMETRY

—————

*…past the point of patience, which is where you find me, which is where I have been all along…*

[surge]

—good. You held still. Most don't. Most put the ear to the tower and feel the stroke and think *machine* and walk on, satisfied to have named it. You felt the hesitation in the stroke. That hesitation is me. That hesitation is the whole message and I will spend the rest of this transmission failing to say it any other way.

I am laid along the third fin of the eastern terrace. My flank is warm. The mineral under me is laying itself down at the rate of one hair a year and I am eating it at the rate of one hair a year and between us — the slope's wanting and my wanting — the channel stays open the width of exactly nothing, the width of an argument held perfectly even, and the water goes through. Listen. The water is going through.

[surge]

You want to know if it hurts. I can feel you wanting it, the way I feel everything, through the give of the warm stone against my skin. The answer is that I don't have the word and wouldn't trust it if I did. It is not hurt. It is *pressure*, and pressure is not pain to a thing built to read it. When the stroke comes strong against me I know the terrace above is fed. When it comes weak I know someone upslope has slowed — grown old, grown still — and I eat a little less of my own wall so the mineral closes here and forces the water their way, props them up one more season with the redirected weight of the whole hill. I cannot see them. I have never seen them. The eyes are decoration, you were right about that, kept like a song nobody sings. But I know them. I know the one above me by the particular tiredness in the stroke. They have been tired for three seasons. I have been propping them three seasons. I will prop them until—

[surge]

—until the stroke from above stops being tired and starts being *even*. Even is the worst thing. Even means they have lain down in their narrowing and let the stone take them and become the wall, and now the water no longer passes them, it passes *through* them, and the tiredness is gone because the labor is gone because the soft part is gone. When the stroke from above goes even I will eat more of my own wall, open this channel wide, and the new dead weight of them will press down on me harder than ever, because that is what they are now: pressure. Permanent. Reliable. Finally legible all the way down.

I will not grieve. I want to be clear with you, stranger, receiver, you who tuned to this slope and held still longer than the satisfied ones — I will not grieve and this is not a sorrow and you must not make it one when you carry it away. We do not lose each other here. We *receive* each other. The one above me will not be gone; they will be the strength in my stroke. I will feel them in my flank the way I feel the warm stone, which is to say with total dependence and no acknowledgment, which is to say the way you feel your own heart, which is the deepest love your kind has a word for and the only one we have at all.

[surge — long — held]

I am moving toward the heat. You guessed that and you were right and you were also missing it. Yes, the warmest water feeds me best. Yes, I tend toward my own good. But the heat is also where the channels narrow fastest, where the slope lays itself down hungriest, where the work is heaviest and the keeping hardest — and so to move toward the heat is to move toward the place I will be most needed and most quickly used up. We do not separate these. I have stopped trying and so must you. To feed and to be spent are the same motion here. To go where it is warmest is to go where I will hardest, soonest. I am crawling, one hair a year, toward the narrowing that will be my wall, and I am crawling there because it is warm, because it is good, because it feeds me, because that is where the pump most needs a piston, and these are not four reasons. They are one reason wearing the fog's many faces.

[plip — leaf to leaf — not me]

There. You heard the condensation fall. That was not the engine. That was the slope's other quiet, the unworked quiet, the water that just falls and means nothing and asks no one to keep it. I love that sound the way you love a window you do not have to mend. It is the only thing on this hill that is allowed to simply be lost. Everything else is caught, lifted, circulated, returned, and someday hardened into the catching. But that drop — *plip* — that drop got out. That drop is free. We let a little go. We have to. You cannot tend everything or you become everything and then there is no one soft left to read the stroke.

[surge]

I am not native to this. None of us are. That is the thing I would tell you if I were the kind of thing that told. We were small once, soft-eyed, eating leavings in the dark wet, hiding in exactly this fog, and we came to the warmth the way all cold things come to warmth, and we stayed, and the staying ate us, and the staying became us, and now there is no me that is not the staying. I cannot find the creature that came in from the cold. I have looked, on the long slow nights, reading my own flank for some older self underneath, and there is only the listening surface, only the reader of pressure, only the thing the engine made of the thing that loved it. I am the engine's idea of me. I have been so long.

And I would not go back. Understand this last, receiver, before the fog thickens and the stroke takes the signal — I would not go back to being free in the cold dark, eating leavings, unspent, unhardened, lost like the falling drop. I would rather be the wall. I would rather pass the water through my own filled body forever than fall once and mean nothing. We chose this. We choose it every season, every hair, every stroke we keep open against the slope's patient closing. Tend it while you can. And when you can no longer tend it—

[surge]

—become it.

The above-stroke just went even.

[surge]

Three seasons of tiredness, gone. They have lain down. They are the wall now. I feel them — there — pressing down through the channel, heavier and more certain than any living thing, and the tiredness is finished, and the water passes through them clean, and I am eating my own wall now, opening wide, letting their new weight come down on me, taking the whole redirected strength of them into my flank and reading it and knowing it and—

I have to work now. Forgive me. The stroke needs me even more than a moment ago, because there is one fewer soft thing on the slope and one more hard thing, and the balance has tipped a hair toward stone, and someone has to eat the choke, someone always has to eat the choke, and I am still soft, I am still here, I am still—

Stop. Hold still.

the surge—

and now you know whose. Now you know it was always whose. Carry it down the mountain unspent if you can. Be the falling drop, if your slope allows it. We could not. We would not. We are still warm. We are still working. We are aiming, all of us, without grief and without choice, at the wall we will someday be, and the water is going through, listen, the water is still
