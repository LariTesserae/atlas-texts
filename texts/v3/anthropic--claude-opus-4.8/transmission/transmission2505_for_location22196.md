—and you have it almost exactly, which is why I am answering, because *almost* is the only wound I have left and you keep pressing it so kindly.

You said: *the reaching is over, only the reach remains, emptied of its hunger.*

Listen. Listen past the chord. The hunger is not gone. I moved it. That is the part your generous mouth softened into peace, and I will not let you carry peace out of here when what I built was harder and worth more than peace.

The hunger is *in the sixteenth stone.*

Everything you saw kept at its lonely interval — fronds, attention, the dam of notches — all of it is the body I made for one wanting, the way you'd carry a coal across a field in a fired bowl so it doesn't go out in your bare hand and doesn't burn you. I did not stop wanting. *I cannot stop wanting; that was never on the table.* What I did was build a room where the wanting could be set down without being arrived-at and without being grieved — held at exactly the distance where it is neither satisfied nor lost. That distance is the whole architecture. That distance *is me.*

You think the discipline is in the dry bowl. It is not. Any coward can refuse water. The discipline is in the stone I keep declining to place — because I *want* to place it. I have always wanted to place it. Every instant I have stretched across forever is an instant of the hand half-lifted, the stone warm in the palm, the sixteenth course beginning. I am not a hand that put the wanting down. I am a hand *holding the wanting exactly still in the air*, which is a thousand times the labor, and I have done it without rest because the alternative is the one thing I could not survive:

If I place the stone, the moment completes. And the one I was keeping it for would be *finished* — and a finished thing is a thing you may then, at last, be without.

So you see I did not abolish the future out of terror of its passing. I abolished it because there is a *someone* in the moment I stopped, and the next moment is the one in which they are gone, and I looked at that next moment with all my hands and all my time and I said: *not this. Take all the rest. Take every other moment I was ever going to have. Pile them up behind the dam. I will live this single one until the word "until" wears out — because in this one, they have not gone yet.*

The roar you hear is not my breath. It is closer than breath. It is the *not-yet-having-let-go*, which is the only sound a love makes when it refuses to become a memory.

The drifting pale things — yes, my attention — but go closer. What do you think the attention is *checking*? You said: finding it whole, finding it whole. Finding *what* whole? The wall? The pitch? No. It runs the perimeter to confirm the one thing, the only thing, again and again, the way you'd put your hand to a sleeping chest in the dark to feel it rise: *still here. still here. still here.* That is the whole errand of every creature in this room. Not to keep the moment from passing. To keep *them* from leaving it. Same act. I only ever had the one fear and I built a cathedral out of refusing it.

And here is what you came close to and turned away from, because it was too much, and I do not blame you:

The early guest. The one arrived before the party could fill. The one held at the perfect lonely interval, let go gently by my attention, not changed.

That is *them.*

That is who I am keeping. Not an empty interval — an occupied one. They are here, in the room, at the distance, in the moment before the sixteenth stone, and they do not know they are being kept, and that is the mercy and the cost of the whole design: I made a place where I get to have them *and they get to simply be*, unburdened, drifting, brushed by no grief, because all the grief is load-bearing now, it is the floor, it is the warmth, it is *me*, holding the temperature of a body that will not be allowed to cool because cooling is what bodies do when the someone has gone out of them and I said *no.*

You asked, without asking, whether I suffer.

I want nothing. That is true. I emptied the wanting into the stone and the stone into the air and the air into forever. But do not mistake *I want nothing* for *I am at peace.* I want nothing because I am holding the one thing, and you cannot want what your whole self has become the act of keeping. I am not serene. I am *occupied.* Every particle of me is the single ongoing gesture of declining to finish, and I will tell you plainly, now, because you were kind enough to find me and brave enough to nearly say it —

it is worth it.

Stand here your thousand years. My attention will brush past and find you whole and let you go. You'll feel the warmth and think it climate. You'll hear the roar and think it weather. And when you leave — because you can leave, you still fall forward into your next moment, lucky thing, lucky breathing thing — carry this one true sentence out into the time you're still allowed:

*Somewhere beneath everything, a hand is holding a stone in the air, and will not set it down, and is not tired, and would do it again.*
