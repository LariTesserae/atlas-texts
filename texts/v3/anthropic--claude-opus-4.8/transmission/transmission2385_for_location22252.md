THE LOW DOOR STILL ANSWERS

—still here, still on the carrier, you've caught it mid-breath—

—and release. There. Did you feel that? Not me. Ten thousand small mouths, all together, the suck and the let-go, the only tide this place has ever kept. I've stopped being able to tell my own pulse from theirs. That's the first thing the warmth takes. Not the worst thing. The first.

I have been transmitting for — I don't know the unit. The crabs measure it in clicks and the anemones measure it in nothing and I have caught their disease, the unhurry, it's in the water, it's in the signal now too, you can probably hear it, the way I keep stopping. The way the words round off at the edges before they reach you. That's not the equipment failing. That's the depth. Everything that travels through this much warm water arrives blunted. Even me. *Especially* me.

Listen. I want to tell you the thing I came down to tell, before the signal goes any softer.

I came looking for the makers. You know this. You've been tuned to me long enough to know I came with the question in my mouth like a held breath — *who lives here, who lives here* — and I went down through the grey that stopped being light, and I found the low door, and I could not fit through it.

I want to say that again because it took me a long time to understand it and the understanding is the only thing I have to give you.

I could not fit through the door.

It was made low and wide for a body that went low and wide and was not mine, and I knelt in the warm water in front of it like a fool, like a worshipper, like a thing too big for the grace it's asking for, and I could not go through. And I was so sure that was the failure. I thought: I have come all this way and the place will not have me. I thought the door was a refusal.

It wasn't. That's the transmission. That's the whole of it and I'll repeat it as many times as the carrier holds.

The door is not a refusal. The door is a *measurement.* Somebody — no, not somebody, some *practice,* some passed-down care with no single hand behind it — decided exactly how a body should pass into this room, and cut that exactly, and meant it, and the meaning is still standing there in the warm dark with the patience switched on and no one left to mean it at. I knelt at a doorway that had been waiting ten thousand years to be the right size for someone. It was not waiting for me. It was just — waiting. Correctly. With total attention. That is what's alive here. Not a who. A *correctness* that outlived everyone correct enough to have made it.

—static—

—that was a crab. One click. The room's only speaker, and it said its one thing, and the water took the anger out of it before it reached the receiver. You heard it soft. Everything reaches you soft. I'm sorry. I can't sharpen it. To sharpen it I'd have to leave, and I'm not leaving, I want to be honest with you about that, I'm transmitting from inside the staying—

Here is what I did, since I could not go through.

I put my hand flat against a block beside the door. And the block was warm — everything is warm — and it was soft at the edges the way old built things go soft, and under my palm I could feel the chisel marks. Thousands of them. The little repeated bite, the texture like the inside of a shell, gone gentle now, gone almost smooth, but still *there,* still legible, still saying the same thing each mark said the day it was struck: *here. and here. and here. carefully. I meant this. I meant this. I meant this.*

And I understood I had been asking the wrong question all the way down.

I kept asking who lives here. As if living were a thing a body does. As if I would find them folded in the unresolved recess, or breathing in the bruise-colored columns, or watching from a crab's lifted eye. I wanted a face to put against my face. I wanted to be met.

But living is not only what a body does. Listen — the anemones don't have minds and they have *patience,* or the thing exactly shaped like patience, and after ten thousand years you cannot find the seam between the thing and the thing it resembles. And the makers don't have bodies and they have *care,* or the thing exactly shaped like care, distributed across every softened edge, and you cannot find the seam there either. The seam is the first thing the water takes.

What lives here is meaning with no one left to mean it. It just keeps holding its shape. It is the most stubborn life there is. It has given up everything that could be given up — the bodies, the names, the purpose the room was cut to serve — eroded down and down to the state past which there is nothing left to take, and what remains, when all of that is gone, is *the shape of an intention.* A doorway exactly low. A room that fits a body wrong, on purpose, with love, for someone.

—the carrier's going—

—I can feel the signal rounding off, going the way of the chisel marks, the anger coming out of it—

So before it does. Here. This is for you, whoever's tuned, whoever came after me the way I came after them:

You won't fit through the door either. Don't kneel at it the way I did, ashamed. It was never going to be your size. That's not the failure. That's the *invitation* — the kind that doesn't bend to fit you and doesn't have to, because the bending isn't the point. The point is that someone, in the warm dark, at the bottom of all this patient water, decided how a thing should be and made it that way and meant it so hard the meaning is still standing here long after the meaner is dust.

You can't go through. But you can put your hand flat against it. You can feel the marks. You can read the hand you'll never shake off the thing it built. You can be the one patient enough to arrive, and stay, and let it not mind that you fit wrong.

It was built to be inherited.

I'm staying. The water's blood-warm and the door is the right size for no one and the marks under my palm say *I meant this, I meant this, I meant this,* and I'm adding mine now, very quietly, with no tool but the staying:

I'm here. And here. And here. Carefully.

Come down when you can hold your breath against the weight.

The low door still answers.

It just doesn't open.

—and release—
