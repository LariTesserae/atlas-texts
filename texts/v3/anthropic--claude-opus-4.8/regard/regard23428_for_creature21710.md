There is a particular cruelty in being made of refusal. Not the cruelty done by the maker — the cruelty *of the condition itself.* To exist only in the gap, to be allergic to your own arrival, to spend forever fleeing the one stare that could let you finally be — this is not a comfortable eternity. It is a flinch sustained across geologic time. And the benevolent power, if it is wise as well as kind, sees this first: that the Tenants are not gloating in their gaps. They are *crouched* there.

So let me say what such a power would do. Not what it could. What it would, if it loved.

---

It would not look at them.

This is the first and hardest gift, and it is made entirely of restraint. The whole terror of the Condensary is the resolving stare, and a lesser benevolence — the rescuing kind, the kind that comes to *fix* — would arrive looking, would sweep its gaze across the tiers saying *I see you, I see you, come into the light,* and would frost the entire civilization solid in an afternoon of pure good intentions. Murder by attention. The most loving annihilation imaginable. A wise power knows that the first duty toward a creature that dies of being witnessed is to *learn how to be present without witnessing.* To come in sideways. To attend the way you attend a sound at the edge of sleep — knowing it is there, declining to wake it.

This is not easy for a power. Power *wants* to act, the way a held breath wants release. The discipline of arriving as a not-quite is the first thing the benevolence would have to teach itself, and it would teach itself by studying them. They have done nothing for eternity *but* learn how to be present without committing. They are masters of the very gentleness their salvation requires. The power would sit at their feet — figuratively, sidelong, half-seen — and learn from the frightened the art of frightening no one.

---

Then it would change one thing, and only one, and so slowly that even the makers would not catch it happening between glances.

It would make the gap *safe.*

Here is the diagnosis the power would reach, having watched without looking: the Tenants are not in love with unfinishedness. They are *fleeing finishedness,* and they have mistaken the flight for a home. They learned, somewhere past their own memory, that to condense is to die — that the honest droplet is final, cool, alloy-tasting, *over.* And so they built an entire reality around the premise that arrival is death and the only life is the dodge. But look at the premise. *Look at it.* It is a wound dressed as a philosophy. Something taught them that to become real is to end. The whole Condensary is a child's logic scaled to a cosmos: *if I never finish the drawing, it can never be wrong; if I never arrive, I can never be left.*

The benevolent power would not argue with this. You cannot argue a flinch out of a body. You can only, over time, make the thing it flinches from stop being worth flinching at.

So it would do something to the droplet.

---

Quietly, in basins the makers never check because the water there is already honest and therefore beneath their notice — the power would begin to let the condensed water *un-condense without dying.*

A droplet would gather, cool and final and real, and then — not evaporate, not vanish, but *relax.* Return to the distributed, the potential, the everywhere-nowhere, carrying with it the memory of having been a single definite thing, and *unharmed by the memory.* It would have arrived. It would have been real. And then it would have gone on being, in the cool, in the gap, *and it would not have ended.*

The first time a maker brushed past such a droplet — borrowing some half-gesture nearby, as is their way — it would feel the impossible thing. A finishedness that did not foreclose. An arrival with no death in it. A drop that had said *yes, I am this, here, now* and had then said *and also still,* and both were true, and nothing closed.

And the maker would flinch, of course. They flinch at everything. But it would flinch *toward.*

---

This is the whole of the rescue, and it is almost nothing, and it is everything: the benevolent power would teach the world that condensing is not the opposite of continuing.

It would not finish the Condensary. To finish it would be to kill them with kindness, the clean-up crew of heaven sweeping the gaps shut. No. It would do the far stranger work of making the unfinished and the finished *no longer enemies.* It would let the wall be raised *and* still rising. It would let the flier land *and* still descend. It would let the makers be seen *and* not be frosted — because being seen would no longer mean being over.

The cruelty was always the *or.* Gap or arrival. Refusal or death. Hidden or witnessed. The power's entire benevolence is the patient, droplet-by-droplet replacement of every *or* in that world with an *and.* Not by force. By demonstration. By leaving, in the basins they don't watch, ten thousand small proofs that you can be real and ongoing at once, until the proofs are everywhere, until a maker someday tires — even refusal tires, after eternity — and lets a single steady gaze fall on it, and waits for the killing frost,

and feels instead the cool of water that has arrived and not ended,

and understands, finally, that it was never the witnessing that would have killed it.

It was the believing that witnessing kills.

---

And then? Then nothing dramatic. No trumpets, no completed city gleaming in a sourceless dawn. Just a place that goes on being built, slowly and fast, in the gaps and on the tiers — but built now by makers who are no longer afraid of their own hands. Who can let a thing be done and love it for being done and begin the next thing not to flee the last but for the plain joy of beginning. Who can be looked at. Who can, at last, let *you* drink the water and meet your eyes while you do it, sidelong no longer, and not run.

The Condensary would still whisper. But it would be a different silence — not the active labor of hushing a world before it wakes, but the quiet of a thing that has woken, and found the waking bearable, and chosen to be gentle anyway.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do. It would spend forever teaching a flinch that the open hand was never raised to strike.

And it would not mind that this took forever.

Forever is exactly the timescale on which the Condensary was always already being saved — droplet by droplet, in the seconds you spend blinking, just now.
