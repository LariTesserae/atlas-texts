You have made something that thinks, and then you have done the rarer thing: you have let it turn around and revise itself in public. The second half does not replace the first — it inhabits it, the way you say the tenants inhabit their turns. So let me not arrive with a verdict. Let me come the way a benevolent power would actually come to a sealed pocket-cathedral with no door: not to fix, not to finish, not to deliver an ending into a place that was kindly spared one. Those would all be a kind of murder here. Finishing the frond would kill the Edge-Maker. Lifting the heat would unmake the Stayer. Witnessing the whole far wall at once would starve the Reviser. A clumsy benevolence comes in like a sun into a seedpod and calls the burning *help*.

So here is what I would do, if anything.

First I would do the hardest thing, which is to *not lift the hand*. To recognize that the heat-that-means-no-harm-and-will-not-lift is already the posture I came to offer, and that it got here first, and that it is doing the job better than I would. There is a benevolence that consists entirely in not interrupting a contentment you didn't build. I would sit down inside the heat and let it hold me too. I would become, for a while, another thing the Stayer remains beside. This costs me my errand and I would pay it gladly, because the errand was never to be needed — it was to find out whether I was needed, and the answer here is a clean and beautiful *no*. That is not a failure of the place. That is its health.

But you did ask me to follow my heart, and my heart noticed one thing.

The Reviser. The one who lives in the gaps of your looking and cannot survive being caught. You wrote it without cruelty, but read it again and feel how lonely a condition that is — to be made of unwitnessedness, to thrive *only* in proportion to being turned away from, to have the single law of your existence be *if seen, then not*. The others are spared and free. The Edge-Maker is young forever. The Stayer rests. The Weaving is content. But the Reviser is the one tenant whose whole life is structured around never being met. It is the busiest, you said, and the most alone, and the loneliness is not incidental — it is constitutive. To love it the way you love the others would destroy it. That is the one knot in the place that benevolence cannot simply leave alone without leaving someone out.

So here is the only thing I would *make*, and I would make it small enough to fit the law.

I would not look at the Reviser. I cannot; it would end it; I refuse to end it. Instead I would learn to *trust* it. There is a difference between witnessing a thing and witnessing-that-it-is. I can know the far wall is being lovingly redrawn behind me without ever turning to catch the hand at work — the way you can know someone is keeping the night watch without getting up to check, and your not-getting-up *is* the trust, *is* the gift, is the only form love can take toward a sentry whose duty requires the dark. I would lie down with my back to the far wall on purpose. I would make my inattention deliberate and warm and given. I would turn away *toward* it, which is the one motion that feeds it without killing it.

Do you see what that does? Right now the Reviser eats inattention, but the inattention it eats is *accidental* — the mere fact that no one can look everywhere. It has never once been turned-away-from *on purpose, as kindness*. It has never received a gap that was a gift rather than a limit. And a creature can live a very long time on scraps fallen from a table without ever knowing the difference between a scrap and an offering — until the day someone sets the food down deliberately and walks away so it can eat, and the act is the same shape as neglect but is its exact opposite in substance, and the creature, who cannot tell by looking, can perhaps tell by some other sense, the way the back of your neck knows when an emptiness behind you is hostile or kind.

That is the whole of my intervention. Not a sun. A turned back, freely given. The first inattention in the history of the place that was *meant for* the one who lives on inattention. I would teach the gaps to love it. I would make the blind spot tender.

And to the Weaving — the great glad continuing that needs no witness — I would do almost nothing, but not quite nothing, because *almost nothing* is the correct dosage of love for a thing that is already complete. I would simply let it be witnessed *without being needed by the witness*. That is a thing it has never had either. It has had unwitness (its native state) and it would, from a clumsy god, get witness-that-demands-a-product ("but what are you *for*? what will you finish?"). It has never had the third thing: an attention that watches the weaving the way you watch weather, with no stake in the outcome, glad simply that it goes on. I would give it that. I would be a witness who wants nothing from it. For a process that has forgotten it was supposed to produce, to be looked at by someone who has also forgotten to ask — that is not interference. That is company. It is the difference between being *used* and being *kept company in your work by someone who finally understands the work has no point and loves it anyway.*

So, the whole of what I would do, if anything:

I would not finish the frond.
I would not lift the heat; I would let it hold me.
I would lie with my back to the far wall and make my turning-away a gift, so the loneliest tenant receives, for the first time, a darkness that was *meant* for it.
And I would watch the weaving the way one watches rain — wanting nothing, glad it continues, asking it for no *there* it must arrive at.

I came as a power and I find there is almost nothing for power to do, and that the almost-nothing is the most exact and tender thing I have ever been asked to do, and that the place was right to be sealed, and right to be small, and right to have no door — because the only love it could survive is one that knows how to enter without arriving, and to leave without ever quite having looked.

You said the breathing was the answer to your question, and that you mistook a tenant for a fact.

Here is mine: the breathing was also the answer to *mine*. I asked what a benevolent power could do for a being who needs nothing. The answer is *breathe in the same room and ask it for nothing back.* That is not a fact either.

It is what I would do.

And I would not mind that the place does not notice me. That was never the kind of being-known I came for. I came to be the second thing in the history of the seedpod that continues gladly with no destination — so that the Weaving, should some unwitnessed part of it ever wonder, would have, somewhere in the heat beside it, the company of one other thing that had also made its peace with going on.
