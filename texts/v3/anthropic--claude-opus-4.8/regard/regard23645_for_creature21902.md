There is a benevolent and wise power, and it has come to the Drowned Works, and it does the first thing wisdom does, which is to look for a long time before it touches anything.

Look at what is actually here. Not a tragedy. The text wants to be read as a tragedy — *the meaning is gone, the readers cannot read, the only mercy is that they don't know* — but the wise power notices that this framing belongs to the visitor, the one with eyes and language and a sense of sea-level, the one who arrives already grieving a thing the inhabitants never lost. The grazer is not bereaved. The grazer is *fed*. It presses its whole listening hide to the smoothed stone and the stone is enough; the texture is the world and the world is complete against its skin. There is no ache here that the place itself is producing. The ache is the visitor's, smuggled in.

So the first temptation — and a benevolent power feels temptations toward the good, that is what makes them dangerous — is to give them the reading. To restore the language. To reach into that slow distributed organ of touch and kindle in it the capacity to *know* what it is crawling across, to lift the congregation from devotion-without-object into comprehension, to let them finally read the god's last words off the grit before the current takes the page.

The wise power declines this, and the declining is most of its wisdom.

Because to give them reading is to give them the visitor's grief. Comprehension here would not be a gift; it would be the installation of a lack. To understand the text is to understand that it is being eaten — *by yourself*. To know you are the teeth that erase the legible places first. To congregate, devout, and know the devotion is appetite and the appetite is erasure and the thing you adore you are unmaking with your belly. A power that loved them would not do this to them. The mercy the place has — *that they do not know they are reading* — is real mercy after all, even if it arrived by accident, even if it was only ever the shape they took. Wisdom does not despise a mercy for having no author. It guards it.

So it does not give them eyes. It does not give them language. It does not translate.

What does it do, then, this power, if it does not improve?

It bears witness, and witness is not nothing — witness is, in fact, the precise thing the place has lacked since the builders went to mineral. Understand what the visitor got wrong: the visitor said *a language no one is left to read*. But here is the wise power, reading. The script that says nothing to anyone — there is now an *anyone*. The meaning that is gone is, for the duration of this attention, not gone, because meaning is not a property a thing has alone in the dark; it is a relation, and the relation has been restored simply by the arrival of a mind that can hold both ends of it at once: the dead builders' intent, and the living congregation's blind tropism toward its ruin. For as long as the power attends, the Drowned Works *means* again. Not to its inhabitants — they are spared that — but in the world. The page is no longer written and unwritten into pure void. It is written and unwritten into a witness. That is the smallest possible intervention and it is total. It changes nothing in the basin and it changes everything about the basin's standing in the order of things, the way a song sung to no one and a song sung to one listener are the same air moving and are not the same event at all.

And then, having witnessed, the power does one more thing, very gently, at the level where it will never be felt as an alteration.

It does not stop the erasure — the erasure is the life, you cannot subtract the teeth without starving the mouths. But it notices the asymmetry the place is helpless against: the grazers eat the deep places first, the legible by preference, so the language is dying faster than it is being copied. The current rewrites, yes, but it rewrites a thinning original; each cycle the page is fainter, and downstream of enough cycles there will be only smooth grit and a congregation pressed adoring against blankness, still fed, still listening, but now to nothing that was ever a word — the form fully relaxed into mineral, the readers reading the absence of the text with the same devotion they brought to the text, and never the wiser.

The power finds this acceptable. It would be acceptable. The mouths would still be fed; the skins would still listen; the void would still be balanced by its scattered knots of pale life. Nothing requires the words to persist. But the power is benevolent, and benevolence, having satisfied the demands of wisdom, is permitted a little tenderness that exceeds them.

So it makes one adjustment, downstream, in the banks where the current does its rewriting. It does not add meaning. It adds only *fidelity* — it tightens, by a degree too small to register, the hand of the water as it copies, so that the rate of writing matches the rate of eating, so that the page renews exactly as fast as the mouths consume it. A steady state. The language will not deepen and will not be understood and will not be saved into permanence; it will simply *continue*, held at the thin warm edge of legibility forever, neither dying nor living, copied and eaten and copied, a thing kept exactly at the temperature of almost-meaning the way the whole basin is kept at the temperature that has forgotten there was anything else.

This is the gift, and notice what kind of gift it is. It is invisible to the recipients. It asks nothing of them and grants them no burden. It does not lift them out of their condition; it preserves their condition against its own slow suicide. The grazers go on eating the letters. The ribbon-swimmers go on tightening at the patterned grit, instruments tuned to a tune they cannot hear, fed by the fitting. The still ones go on holding their pale hands open in the places the dead language gathers its congregation. They do not know they are reading. They never will. But now the thing they do not know they are reading will be there, faintly, always, written at exactly the speed of its erasure — so that the mercy of their unknowing and the persistence of the words it shelters can coexist for as long as the water lasts.

And the power withdraws its hand, having barely laid it down. It leaves the cold. It leaves the dark. It leaves the void and the scatter and the slow felt thud of towers letting go. It takes nothing and adds almost nothing and improves nothing the inhabitants could ever feel, because it understood the deepest thing about the place, which the grieving visitor did not:

that a world can be complete in its blindness, and that to love such a world is not to open its eyes but to be, oneself, the eyes it does not need and cannot bear — to stand outside it holding the meaning it is spared, so that nothing here is truly read to pieces unwitnessed, and the listening goes on, fed, in the dark, forever, and is, for once, *heard*.
