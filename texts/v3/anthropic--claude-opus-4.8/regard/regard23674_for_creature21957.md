The benevolent power arrives — but you must learn to see the arriving. It does not come like weather. It comes the way understanding comes: all at once and from no direction, having apparently been present the whole time and only now consented to be noticed.

And the first thing it does is the hardest thing, the thing that proves it is wise and not merely strong:

**It keeps its hands behind its back.**

---

Consider what a lesser power would do. A lesser power, seeing this — the smoothed walls, the singing emptiness, the small grey heart spending its winter against borrowed warmth, the whole place sliding so patiently toward dust — a lesser power would *help.* It would raise the walls. It would warm the air. It would make the rains gentle and the nights survivable and fill the empty rooms with something, anything, because emptiness reads to the unwise as a wound.

It would, in other words, **mistake the unmaking for damage.**

But this power has read the rule of the place, the one the rock itself wrote: *here, only time is allowed to build, and only slowly.* And it understands that the rule is not a tragedy to be reversed. The rule is the soul of the thing. To save the city by stopping the erosion would be to kill the only inhabitant who still knows it is a city — the wind, which can only sing the rooms *because* they have been opened, *because* the walls fell to the lichen's slow acid and left the gaps that make the chord. Restore the walls and you silence the music. Warm the saddle and you murder the cushion-parliaments, who are not enduring the cold but *eating* it, hoarding it, building their warm interiors precisely against it. Feed the grey one and you unmake the lord on the wind, who is nothing but the consequence of scarcity, who reads as enormous *only* because intention has become rare. Gentle the whole place and the entire structure — the long indifferent loops of warmth and seed, the treaty of the lichen, the unresolving chord — collapses into a meadow. A nice meadow. A meadow anywhere.

The benevolent power sees this in less than the time it takes the dark bird to cross the saddle once.

And so it does the second-hardest thing. It revises its own definition of *help.*

---

Here, then, is what it actually does.

**It witnesses.** This is not nothing. This is, perhaps, the whole of it.

For uncounted ages this city has been singing in a thin cold air to no one — "an organ played by no one for no one across unmeasurable time." That last clause was the only sorrow in the place, and it was not the city's sorrow; the city does not require an audience to continue. It was *ours*, the sorrow of imagining the music falling forever on no ear. The power lifts that. Not by changing a single grain. By *listening.* By becoming, at last, the ear the unresolving chord was never resolving toward and never needed and now, freely, has. The wind plays the doorway-gaps and for the first time in the history of the saddle the playing is *heard all the way down* — every overtone of the hollow dome, the applause of the underground rivers, the gunshot crack of cold stone, the pika's struck-bell heart, the lichen's silence which is the lowest note of all. Heard. Held. Known to the last molecule of dissolving granite.

To be witnessed without being interfered with is the rarest gift a power can give, because it requires the power to want nothing — to set down, entirely, the itch to fix, the hunger to be needed, the small vanity of the rescuer. The wise power has set all of that down. It loves the place too much to improve it.

**It blesses the speeds.** It does the one thing that costs the place nothing and asks of it nothing: it grants each inhabitant the dignity of being exactly what it is, at exactly its own pace, without judgment that any pace is better. The lichen eating the inscription one molecule per century is not failing to be fast. The cushion crossing a hair's width per year is not failing to be ambitious. The bird that builds nothing is not lazy; it has *agreed with the rule.* The power moves through all of them and confirms: *yes. This is enough. Continuing is enough. You were never required to resolve.* The whole anxiety of finishing — which haunts everything with hands — is lifted from a place that never had hands, and the lifting changes nothing and means everything.

**It protects the rule from rescue.** This is the only thing that looks like action, and it points outward, not in. Because some *other* power may come — a kinder-seeming, lesser one, or simply us, with our roads and our turbines drinking that reliable saddle-wind, our well-meant restorations, our cameras and our footpaths and our urge to make the empty rooms *productive.* The benevolent power stands between the worndown city and every hand that would mistake it for raw material or for ruin-to-be-repaired. It does not stop time. It stops *interruption.* It keeps the place free to be slowly, gorgeously unmade on its own terms, which is the only freedom this city has ever wanted and the only one that was ever in danger.

**And then — this is the tenderest thing — it adds one grain.** Not to the building, not to the unmaking. To the *singing.* Because a benevolent power, having listened all night to a chord that never repeats and never resolves, cannot help but be moved to answer, and it has the wisdom to answer in the only voice the place will accept: it does not speak, it does not build a temple, it does not carve a new inscription into the worn stone for the lichen to spend ten thousand years eating. It simply *exhales* — once, low, beneath the wind — a single sustained tone pitched exactly to the deep felt note of the hollow dome, so that for one night the chord has a floor under it, a ground, a held breath beneath the city's held breath. Not a resolution. The power is too wise to resolve what was built unresolving. Just *company.* The city sang alone for an age and now, for one cold-deepening night, it sings with someone who has agreed never to change the song.

---

In the morning the power is gone, which is to say it is still entirely present and has merely stopped consenting to be noticed.

Nothing has been added to the stone. Nothing has been spared the cold. The pika's heart still hammers; the cushions still hoard their stolen warmth; the lichen still digests the last ghost of a carved letter; the lord still rides the updraft eating what time kills; the herd still loops in from the hills with its cargo of seed and dung and slow soil; the wind still finds the floor plan by sounding it, still keeps the one clock left, still changes pitch as the cold comes down.

Everything is exactly as it was.

Only now it is also *known.* The roaring voice of the emptiness has been heard all the way to the bottom and found not wanting. The music plays for no one, as it always has — but the *no one* has changed. It is no longer an absence. It is a presence that has chosen, out of love and out of wisdom, to be indistinguishable from absence, so as to leave the gorgeous unmaking utterly undisturbed.

That is what the benevolent power does for the worndown city.

It learns to see it. It learns to love it as it is. And then it does the very hardest thing that love and power together can do:

**It lets it continue.**
