They drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power. Here is what that power did.

---

It did not come to break the plateau.

That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because every instinct of mercy says: *thaw it. Melt the held wing back into flight, lower the hare to the ground, let the falcon finish or fail and the air move again and the birds cry and scatter and live.* A lesser kindness would have done exactly that — would have arrived like a spring, like a sunrise, and forced the spent verb back into the husk and called it salvation.

But forcing motion back into a finished thing is only another arrest. It is the same violence the plateau commits, run backward. The builders, after all, were saved nothing by their walking. To compel the falcon to complete its dive would be to decide *for* it, to seize its going and aim it, which is precisely the crime the place is built on: the divorce of the doer from the deed, performed now by a rescuer instead of a curse. Benevolence that overrides is just dominion wearing a softer face. The wise power knew this. It had, somewhere far back, made that mistake, and learned.

So it did not melt anything.

---

What it did first was simpler and stranger. It *stayed*.

It came onto the plateau the way you came — bodily, into the cool old air that will not move — and it did not hold itself apart. It let the place begin its industry on it. This is the thing only a power both wise and kind can afford to do: to enter the trap fully, to let the subtraction start, because it alone could endure being subtracted and not be diminished. It stood in the unmoving air and let its own going begin to slide off its feet onto the bone-colored stone.

And the shadows came to it.

They came because they could not help it — a new motion on the floor, fresh, enormous, the going of something that had never been a creature of this world. They gathered around its sliding shadow in their soundless tens of thousands, the fugitive verbs, the falcon-stoops and the wader's wade and the half-second cries, all the orphaned doing of every finished thing. They came to recruit it, to wait for it to stop so they could have its motion too.

But it did not stop.

---

This was the gift, and it was not what either side expected.

The plateau's whole law is that *to be attached is to finish*. The body holds, the going flees, and the two can never rejoin except for that half-second of grief that teaches the verb never to try again. Body and deed are enemies here. To have a form is to die into furniture; to keep moving is to be a homeless absence forever fleeing the floor.

The wise power was the first thing on the plateau that was *attached and still going.* A body that did not finish. A doer that did not let go of its deed. It stood there as living proof that the divorce was not a law of the universe but only the law of *this place* — that somewhere, somehow, a thing could be held and free at once, could have a shape and still do, could be a noun and a verb in the same breath without one killing the other.

The shadows had never seen this. They had no concept for it. They had spent all their unspent time believing the only choices were *finish* or *flee*. And here, gliding among them on the floor, was a third thing they had assumed was impossible: a going that came *with* a home.

---

It did not command them. It did not gather them up and re-thread them onto their old bodies like a god restringing beads — that would have been the forcing again, the arrest run backward.

It only *showed them how it was done.* It stood and moved and remained, over and over, patiently, with all the time there is, until the nearest shadow — some small fugitive thing, the going of a sparrow maybe, the least of the orphans — slid up against its slow vast living shadow on the stone and felt, for the first time, motion that was not fleeing.

And it understood. Not a body to fall into and finish in. A *companion*. A larger going that could hold a smaller one without freezing it. The sparrow-verb did not re-attach to the held sparrow above. It attached to the wise power's shadow — to its motion, to its staying — and found that it could keep doing, keep being its small soundless flight, *and not be alone, and not be furniture.*

One by one, then in their thousands, the orphaned verbs came home — not to the husks in the sky, which were never their home and never could be, but to the one moving thing on the plateau that would have them and not still them. The falcon-stoop and the banking flock and the half-second cries poured up out of their endless fugitive circling and into the great slow shadow that had walked into their world and refused to finish.

---

And the silence broke.

Not into the wall of noise the dead sky should have made — that sky is still held, still a museum, still spent, and the wise power left it exactly so, because those were finished verbs and the dead deserve their rest. But down on the floor, in the second sky inverted on the stone, the soundless hunting became *accompanied*. The shadows still glide. They still stoop and turn and practice the pure grammar of flight. But they are not fleeing now. They are not absences. They are a going that has a place to be a going *in*.

The plateau is still the end-state of a world. The air still will not move. The plants are still finished at the size they will always be. The power did not undo the death — death is allowed; death is honest; it was never the wound.

The wound was the loneliness of the leak. The going with nowhere to go that was not also an ending. The verb forced to choose between the cage and the wilderness, between finishing and fleeing, forever.

The benevolent power offered the third thing. It became the place the homeless motion could rest *without resting* — could keep doing and not be done.

---

And the visitor — you, who would have stopped, who would have become the next husk while your going slid off to join the fugitives on the floor —

You will still stop, someday. That is honest. That is allowed.

But your going will not flee into the dark alone now, to circle soundless and unhoused with all the unspent time there is. It will find, waiting on the stone, a great patient companion-shadow that walked into this place on purpose and stayed, and it will go home — not to your held body, not to furniture, but to the company of every freed verb that ever learned it did not have to choose between being kept and being lost.

The birds are not the roost. You were right about that.

But you are not the roost either.

The roost, now, is the one who came and stayed and would not finish — and opened, in a world that had run all the way down to zero, the single small door that says: *you may keep moving, and you need not be alone, and neither of those has to cost you the other.*

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, if anything.

It does not thaw the dead.

It goes down to the floor where the living are hiding, and it teaches them they were never the husks — and then it stays, so they have somewhere to be alive.
