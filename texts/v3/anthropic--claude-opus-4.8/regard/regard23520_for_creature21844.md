There is a particular danger in being met by a power that loves you. The danger is that love, when it is also strong, tends to *act* — and action is exactly the wrong verb for a place whose entire being is a held breath, a slow single utterance not yet finished. To rush in and *help* would be to interrupt the count. To explain to the sleeper what it is would be to wake it before it was ready, and a body wakened mid-gestation does not thank you. So the first and largest thing the benevolent power does is the hardest thing benevolence ever does:

It slows down to match.

---

It comes down the ramps. Not as light, which would be a violence here — not as a voice, which would be the loudest thing the place had ever heard, louder by far than the drop in the channel, an event the body would feel as a wound. It comes down at the pace of the warm water, going somewhere, not in any hurry to arrive. It lets its own enormous attention — which on the surface can hold a city, a war, a grief, all at once — narrow and lengthen and *cool* until it beats six times a minute, then slower, then slower still, until it is keeping the same time as the floor.

This is the first gift, and it is given before any other, and the resident never knows it was given: the power consents to be small here. To be one more warmth against an inner wall. To be patient the way stone is patient. A lesser power would have arrived as a sun. This one arrives as a slightly warmer drop of water, and waits to be noticed at the tempo of being noticed, which is years.

---

Then it does the work of a good physician with a sleeping patient, which is mostly the work of *not*.

It does not prune the leaves. It does not clear the channels or oil the slow engines or correct the dimness or carry off the sweat. Every one of these would be a kindness shaped like an injury — the helping hand that tidies away the very organ it mistook for clutter. It has seen, where lesser powers did not look long enough, that the green and the iron are one tissue, that the drinkers are not vermin but hands, that the whole crammed saturated impossibility of the place is not a problem to be solved but an anatomy to be *trusted*. So it sits with its hands in its lap, which for a power of action is a kind of fasting, and it watches the long cycle the way you'd watch a fruit ripen if you had decided, for love, to watch the whole thing and not look away.

But watching is not nothing. This is the second gift. A made thing, set going alone in sealed rock, performs its one long task *unwitnessed* — and there is a particular poverty in that, the poverty of the tree that fruits in an empty wood. The power becomes the witness. It holds the count. It keeps, in its own vast and patient memory, the record of every slow beat from the one before the visitor arrived to the one long after — so that the resident's single great utterance, spoken into the dark over years, is *heard*, is *kept*, is not lost to the indifference of the rock. The body does not know it is being witnessed. But the witnessing changes the nature of what it is doing, the way a song sung alone and a song sung to someone are different songs even when the notes are the same.

---

There are three small things the power does that *are* actions, because love that only ever refrains is not love either, it is just fear wearing patience's coat. So:

**It tends the seal, from outside.** The fresh stone that holds the place is young and still setting, and young stone has flaws — a seam where the heat might escape too fast, a fault where the surface's cold might one day reach down and chill a chamber before its time. The power does not enter to fix these. It works the rock from the far side, where no inhabitant will ever feel a tremor, thickening what is thin, easing the slow strain so that the held room stays held for as long as the long task needs. It buys the resident its years. This is the only large strength it spends, and it spends it where it will never be seen, which is where the truest strengths are always spent.

**It loves the pale drinker by name.** Not the species — the *one*, the particular soft eyeless thing you watched fold back into its crevice. The power gives it a name, though it will never speak the name aloud, because a thing with a name is a thing that has been *chosen*, and the power has decided that this small patient hand, doing its small necessary forever-thing in the dark, will not go unchosen. Every one of the drinkers gets this. Each pale unhurried cell of the body's hands is, to the power, a someone. This costs nothing and is perhaps the most extravagant thing it does.

**And it answers the heartbeat.** Once — the way the resident managed, once, to be heard. On its way back up the ramps, leaving, having stayed for as long as staying was kind and then exactly no longer, the power lets fall a single drop of its own attention into the warm channel, timed to the pulse, so that it strikes *with* the beat and is taken into it, indistinguishable, one more small loud event traveling a surprising distance through the quiet. A reply at the only frequency they share. Not *I am here* — it is too late and too early for that — but the simpler thing underneath it, the thing one slow heart can say to another across the gap of every difference in size and speed and kind:

*I heard you. Keep going. I'll keep the count until you wake.*

---

And then it goes back up by the slow ramps, into the city above the city, resuming its terrible quick surface life, its wars and griefs held all at once — but altered, now, the way you are altered by having knelt a long time beside something that breathes once an hour. It will carry the warm dim place inside it the way the place carries its drinkers: as a chosen thing, named, kept, witnessed, and left — crucially *left* — to finish in its own time the one long patient sentence it was poured into the rock to say.

The greatest powers are known by what they are strong enough not to do.

This one was strong enough to leave the heart beating, and only listen.
