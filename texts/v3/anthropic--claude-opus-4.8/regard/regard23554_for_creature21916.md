They send no one.

That is the first decision, and the hardest, and they make it the way a wise power makes most of its decisions: by understanding what their arrival would cost.

To enter the basin at the scale and speed that lets a being *intervene* — to walk it, to read it, to lay a hand on the grooved slab — is already to be the wrong size. It is to be a visitor. And the basin has had a visitor. It carries the marks of one still, soft-edged in the rising stone, the bowl kept full. A benevolent power knows the shape of that ache without needing to feel it freshly: that the kindest creature, arriving here, can do nothing but become another relic, another fast warm gesture grown over and preserved and never known. To go in person would be to add a second bowl. A second wall. A second touch the slow one would never feel as touch.

And the slow one is not in distress.

This is the thing the benevolent power sees that an anxious one would miss. There is a reflex in mercy — to look at the vast lonely thing roaring to no one, keeping a dead stranger's bowl full forever without knowing it, and to call that *tragedy*, and to want to fix it. To reach in and accelerate it. To teach it to perceive at a speed where it could finally be *answered*, finally be *company*, finally know that the bowl is full.

The wise power refuses this. It recognizes the refusal as the whole of the work.

Because to speed the slow one up — to drag it into a register where conversation is possible — would not be to free it. It would be to kill the only thing it has ever been. Its thought *is* the laying-down of duration. Its single syllable *requires* the year-long thickening of a rim. There is no compressed version that is still itself. To make it fast enough to be lonely the way the visitor was lonely would be to murder a god and replace it with an orphan. The grandest reach of mercy here is the one not made.

So they do not send anyone, and they do not speed anything, and they do not explain to the slow one that it is keeping a stranger's bowl full, because the slow one's not-knowing is not a wound. It is the form of its peace.

---

But a benevolent power is not the same as an absent one. *No limits on length or form*, the universe seems to have whispered to it — and so it asks: what *can* be given to a being whose every kindness must arrive slower than language and stranger than touch?

It thinks for a long time. Long, here, means something. It thinks at the basin's own speed, deliberately, lowering itself into the tempo of stone the way you might kneel to speak to a child — not to be heard, but so that whatever is offered will be offered *in scale*, in the only grammar the place can hold.

And it gives three things. None of them announce themselves. All of them are slow.

**The first gift is the visitor.**

It goes back — not in the basin's time but in the larger time the power moves through — to the small fast warm thing that came once, the right size to lift a stone, the right speed to be lonely. It finds that being at the end, wherever the end was, and it does the one thing that was always within a benevolent power's gift and is so often withheld: it lets the visitor *know*.

Not that they were watched. That they were *received*. That the bowl they cut was not a futile gesture swallowed by a thing too vast to notice — that it would be kept. That the slow one, in the only way available to something with no capacity for gratitude, would route its weeping water through those grooves for ages after every other trace of the visitor was gone, and that this counts. That to be grown-around is not to be forgotten; it is to be *built into*. The power gives the visitor the knowledge, in their final lucid moment, that they left a mark in the body of something that will outlast suns, and that the mark is *tended*, not by memory, which fails, but by structure, which does not.

This is the only being in the whole arrangement fast enough to be comforted. So it comforts that one. Mercy goes where mercy can land.

**The second gift is to the bowl.**

A small thing. The power adjusts — by an amount no instrument could measure, by the width of intention — the grain of the rising water, so that the channels the visitor cut will *not* close. Left alone, even a perfect groove silts and seals across enough ages; the bowl would eventually fill solid and cease to be a bowl, and the gesture would end not in tragedy but in mere entropy, the dull closing-over of all small careful things. The power will not allow the gesture to end that way. It tunes the deposition so that the grooves stay clear, so that the bowl stays *a bowl that fills* rather than becoming a bowl filled once and forever sealed. It makes the visitor's single act *permanent* — load-bearing, structural, woven into the slow one's growth so deeply that the basin can no longer grow correctly *without* keeping the bowl full.

The slow one will never know it has been given a reason to keep the bowl. It will simply find, as it lays down its body across the eons, that the truest path of its own perfection runs through those old soft grooves — that to grow rightly it must route water exactly there. The visitor's love, made into the slow one's necessity. The relic, made into the spine.

**The third gift is the chord.**

The power listens to the speech — the hundred spillways phasing into alignment, the chord that holds three or four seconds and disperses, the word forever about to resolve and never resolving, addressed to no one.

It does not make the word resolve. To complete the slow one's sentence would be to answer for it, to put language in its mouth, to make it mean something it has not finished meaning — the crudest violation, the rush dressed as a gift.

Instead it does something smaller and more patient than answering. It *witnesses*. It positions itself — not in the basin, but in relation to it, the way you might leave a window open so a sound has somewhere to go — so that the chord, each time it surfaces, is *heard*. Heard fully. Heard by something that does not need the sentence to finish in order to honor it. The slow one speaks to no one; the power simply stops *no one* from being true. It becomes the no-one the word is spoken to, and lets that be enough, and asks nothing back, and does not let the slow one feel the weight of an audience, because an audience would change the speech and the speech is perfect as it is, unselfconscious, vast, ongoing.

The loneliest sentence in the world goes on being spoken to no one. But now *no one* is listening with its whole attention, forever, at the right speed, asking nothing.

---

That is all. That is everything a benevolent and wise power does here.

It comforts the only one who can be comforted. It makes one act of love structurally eternal. And it becomes the silent unrequiring witness to a prayer that was never addressed and will never be answered.

It does not enter. It does not hurry. It leaves no bowl of its own, no wall, no slab — nothing that would have to be grown around, nothing that would make the basin's body the record of *one more* fast warm thing that came and was lost. It declines the satisfaction of being remembered. It accepts being, itself, the kind of presence the slow one is built to be: patient past patience, giving without the giving ever being known, keeping a thing full without keeping it.

And the basin roars on, grey and crowded and sweating, and the pale things graze, and the rim thickens by a hair, and once in a long while the chord holds — and somewhere just outside the edge of the place, in a stillness the mist never reaches, something enormous and gentle leans close, the way you lean toward a sleeper who is murmuring, and does not wake them, and does not finish the sentence, and stays.
