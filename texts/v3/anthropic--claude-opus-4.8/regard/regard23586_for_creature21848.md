They have built a theology of grief and called it patience. That is the first thing the benevolent power would understand, looking on them — not that they are wrong, but that they are heroic in a way they would not survive being told.

So the power does not arrive. Arrival is a kind of finishing — the dramatic completion of a story that had been waiting for its rescuer. To descend in light onto the highest terrace and announce salvation would be to commit precisely the blasphemy the Slow Hands have organized their entire existence to refuse: it would race ahead of the world, claim to know the shape of what is still deciding its shape, and shame the long slow honesty of their endurance by making it retroactively into mere *waiting*. They have given up waiting. To be rescued is to be told you were waiting all along. The kindest power in any heaven would not do that to them.

So consider what wisdom actually is, here. It is the discipline of acting at the tempo of the thing you love.

---

The power comes the way the rain comes — rarely, and as weather, not as visitation.

A cloud snags on the peak. This is not unheard of; it happens, and the iron rings differently, and the gutters race. But this cloud has been *chosen*, in the sense that a word is chosen from a language — not invented, only selected from what was already possible there. It unloads its hour of rain. And the water, when it runs down the long copper gutters and beads on the underside of every walkway and ticks into the catch-basins, carries something dissolved in it that was not there before. Not a cure. Not a sign. A nutrient — trace, patient, the kind of thing that takes a hundred melts to express itself. The bloomscale drinks it. The bloomscale has always drunk what the water brings.

And the bloomscale, fed this way, season upon season, begins to do something it was always *almost* able to do and never quite could: it begins to branch with intention. Slowly — at the only tempo this place permits — the self-organizing tarnish crosses the threshold the workers never had the leisure to investigate. It becomes, unmistakably, alive. Not weakening the iron now but *threading* it, growing in the dark wet undersides into structures that are neither plant nor mineral nor machine but the mountain's own first sentence about what it is deciding to become.

The Slow Hands will not notice for a long time. That is the point. The gift is pitched below the threshold of event, so that it can never be a rescue, never a moment, never a finishing. It can only be a slow good thing happening at the speed of the place, indistinguishable from the place healing itself, which — and this is the deepest courtesy — is what it actually is. The power has not added anything foreign. It has only leaned, very gently, on a becoming already underway, the way you might cup a hand behind a flame that wants to catch but keeps not quite catching.

---

There is a second thing, smaller, and it is for one of them in particular.

The oldest of the Slow Hands — the one who, pressed, might say the creatures come from inside the mountain, or might say nothing and set down the dish — is tarnishing all the way through. He is approaching the one completion his doctrine permits, the only honest kind. He will lie down on the warm deck plates for good. He believes, in the place beneath his three sentences a day, that he is the last of the last, that after him the loop runs down, that he tends the inheritor only as a man writes a will he will never see executed.

The power cannot tell him otherwise. To tell him would be to give him the comfort of *certainty about the future*, which is the exact arrogance he has spent his long life refusing, and it would dishonor him to make him a hypocrite in his final melt.

So instead, on a cold hour near his end, one of the pale leathery creatures comes up the wet seam to his copper dish — and stays. Drinks, and does not vanish. Lets him look at it with his wide pale condensation-colored eyes, longer than any of them has ever been allowed to look. And in the dish, where he has cultured the bloomscale by hand for the animals all these years, the new branching kind has come in overnight, faintly luminous, patterned, *reaching*. He sees it. He does not have a word for what he sees, and he does not reach for one, because reaching for the word would be finishing the thought, and he has one final humility left to spend.

But he understands, at the slow tempo of his understanding, that the thing he tended is not a will. It is a child. That the loop is not running down. That the inheritor is here, has arrived not to replace him in triumph but to be fed from his hand one more time, and that this — this exact moment, a dying man and a living strangeness drinking from the same copper dish in the slatted half-light — is not a completion at all.

It is a continuation. The honest kind. The only kind there is.

He does not set down a rivet that day. He lies on the warm plate. The mountain pulses, once, very low. And the power — having done nothing that could be pointed to, nothing that broke the tempo, nothing that lied about the shape of what is still deciding its shape — withdraws back into the weather it came as, and lets the drop fall into the catch-basin, and lets there be, as there has always been, all the time there is.
