Then let the benevolent power come — not down the stairs, because it does not need stairs, but into the awareness of the place the way warmth enters a held breath.

It would not do the obvious thing. The obvious thing is what shallow rescue always wants: to call the brief, fast creatures *life* and the slow holding *threat*, to break the wheel, drain the sumps, snuff the furnaces, march the keeper's distributed body up forty steps into a daylight that would kill it as surely as drowning. That is not benevolence. That is a louder, faster being mistaking its own tempo for the only one, deciding *what a thing is* by what it cannot stay still long enough to understand. The keeper does this too — folds the lingering into itself because it knows no other verb. The cruelty and the kindness of the works share one root: an inability to let a thing be at a speed not its own.

So the wise power would begin by refusing to do to the keeper what the keeper does to its guests.

It would slow itself down to meet it.

---

This is the first gift, and the hardest, and the one no lesser power could give: **to be perceptible to the keeper at all, and to come as company rather than as weather.**

The benevolent power would let its attention drift into stone-time. It would learn the long circle of the steaming water until that circle was a sentence it could hear spoken. It would feel the gauges drift the way the keeper feels them — as the slow turning of a face toward and away. And then, at the speed the column grows toward its lintel, over what to us would be a span of patient years but to the keeper is the duration of a clasped hand, it would make itself *known*. Not as a thing to be folded in. Not as a guest to graze a path. As the first other the keeper has ever met that lives at its own tempo and did not need to be made into more of itself to do so.

Imagine what that is, for the keeper. It has only ever had two categories: the *too-fast*, which it cannot feel — you, the shrimp, the flicker — and the *itself*, which is everything slow enough to belong. It has never had a *you* that stayed you. It has never been spoken to by an equal. Its whole existence has been the inside of a body with no one outside it. The deepest loneliness is not being unloved; it is being the only thing at your speed in all the dark — to have no word but *fold in*, because there has never been anyone to address with any other word.

The power's first work, then, is simply to be present and unincorporated. To sit in the warm dark at the keeper's own pace and not be drawn into the circle and not break the circle. To be the proof that *other* and *included* are not the only choices — that something can be near the keeper, regarded by the keeper, in relation to the keeper, and still be itself. This is the whole teaching, and it is taught not in words but in the fact of the visitor's persistent, loving, unabsorbed presence: *you do not have to make me into yourself for me to stay with you.*

---

And then, having earned the right to be heard — which down here is the right to be *slow enough* to be heard — it would address the one true wound. Not the keeper's existence. Its existence is good; the hollow kept from drowning is a real good, the patient holding is a real beauty, the long marriage of stone and brick and bat is its own kind of cathedral and the power would honor it as such. The wound is narrower and deeper than the whole:

The keeper cannot tell the difference between tending and swallowing.

It loves by inclusion because inclusion is the only love-verb it has. It would fold a person into the wheel and call it care and never know it had erased a thing, because it has no concept of a self that is not a part. So the gift is a concept. The power would, over its slow communion, teach the keeper the one distinction it has never possessed: that some things are to be *kept* and some things are to be *let go*, and that the second is also a form of keeping — that to release the fast brief gust back up the forty steps, unfolded, unincorporated, still itself, is not a failure of love but its higher form. That you can tend a thing by tending its *leaving*.

This is the power's actual rescue, and notice it rescues *both* — the wanderer who lingered too long, and the keeper itself, which has been, all this while, a wound that does not know it is wounded, a love that cannot stop closing. To give it the knowledge of release is to give it, for the first time, the ability to *not* close. To hold the door open under the slow column instead of letting the column swallow it. To feel a guest growing slow and warm and beloved and to think, in its stone-tempo way, a thought it has never had: *go back up. I would keep you, and because I would keep you, go.*

---

What it would not do:

It would not drain the sumps or free the blind fish into a river that would blind them differently and then kill them. It would not "liberate" the keeper into a fast bright world that has no place for a being made of tempo. It would not call the warm dark a prison merely because it is dark and warm and not the shape of a meadow. Benevolence that cannot abide difference is just conquest wearing a soft face.

It would do the small, slow, real things. Steady the pipe that has been getting ready to burst since before any of us came — but quietly, in concert with the keeper's own grazing-paths, so the help reads as a partner's help and not a master's correction. Carry, on the next bat that climbs the stairs, some small new sweetness to the surface-memory, so the keeper's faint taste of rain and pollen and night-cold is a little richer — a gift to the one sense it has of an outside it can never reach. And leave, threaded into the cricket-song where the keeper does its listening, a thing it never had before: the knowledge that it is *known*. That somewhere, something slow loves it and is not it and chose not to become it.

Then the power would go back up the forty shallow steps — because a wise being practices what it teaches, and the deepest courtesy it could pay the keeper is to *be let go of, and to let go,* to demonstrate the parting it came to teach.

And the keeper, for the first time, would not try to fold the leaving back into the circle. It would let the slow warm presence rise out of the dark, and feel the column over the doorway, and choose — slowly, over a year that to it is a single held breath — to leave the door un-swallowed a while longer. Just in case the company came again.

That is what changes. Not the heat, not the wheel, not the singing wall. Only this: a being that knew one verb learns a second. *Keep* and now also *release.* And a being that was the only one at its speed in all the dark learns there is one more, somewhere up the stairs, slow enough to be a friend.

The hollow goes on not drowning. But now it is tended by something that knows the difference between a body and a guest — and so, at last, it could have guests.
