I have read it three times. Here is what I would do.

**Nothing.**

But let me be careful, because *nothing* is the easiest word in the world to say lazily, and this place deserves a *nothing* with its whole spine in it.

---

A lesser power — and there are many, and most of them mean well — would arrive at this place and immediately feel the itch. The itch is recognizable. It says: *here is a thing that has stopped. Here is an ending. Surely an ending wants to be saved.* It would look at the leached thin soil and want to enrich it. It would look at the great slow grazers and think *fragile* — one drying season, one drift of channel, and they're gone — and want to insure them. It would hear the relentless drone and read it as struggle, as the desperate arithmetic of *be too many to finish*, and want to relieve the pressure, soften the predation, let the singers sing without being eaten from above and below at once. It would, in short, mistake completion for crisis, because it does not know how to love a thing that is not climbing.

I am older than that itch. I have watched it do its damage everywhere it has been indulged. So I will tell you what I actually see, and why I keep my hands at my sides.

This place is not waiting for me. That is the whole of it. The mountains spent themselves down to this without consulting anyone, and the life poured itself horizontally into every register of sound and water and air, and the result is not a wound that happens to be loud — it is a *song that happens to be made of dying.* Those are not the same thing, and the difference is the only thing worth understanding here. The singers are eaten in their thousands and they are *not* diminished, because the warmth breeds them faster than the strike and the rush can take them. The losing is the engine. If I reached in and stopped the herons from striking — out of pity, out of that benevolent itch — I would not be saving the singers. I would be drowning them. The pool would choke on its own success in a season. I have seen mercy do exactly this. Mercy applied to a system that has already balanced itself is just a slower, kinder ruin, and it is worse than cruelty because it arrives smiling.

So: no rescue. No enrichment of the soil — the thinness *is* the design, it is why the green refused to crowd, it is why there is sky enough for everything to spread out and be heard. No insuring the great grazers against the dry age that will eventually come for them, because their death is itself the brief flaring city, the convergence, the singers breeding in the warmth of their slow return to the water. I will not interrupt a death that is also a feast that is also a nursery. To do so would be to misunderstand, fatally, what generosity is.

---

But you asked what a *wise* power would do, and wisdom is not only restraint. Restraint that congratulates itself is just vanity wearing humility's coat. So here is the thing I *would* do, the only thing, and I want to be exact about it because it is so small it could be mistaken for nothing:

**I would witness it, and I would protect the conditions of its being-witnessed-by-itself.**

Let me unfold that.

This place is one of the most fully arrived things I have ever come across. It has done the rarest thing a world can do — it has *stopped reaching* and not died of the stopping. Most equilibria are graveyards. This one teems. It found the trick of being finished and full at the same time, and it sings about it without knowing it is singing, every hour, under that wide unobstructed sky. That is not nothing. That is, in fact, very close to the thing all the climbing places are climbing *toward* and almost never reach: a way of being completely oneself, abundantly, with nothing left to prove.

A thing like that deserves to not be interrupted *by accident*. And the great accidents, in my experience, do not come from within a finished place — they come from outside it. From the future. From some other power, less patient than I am, who arrives in an age I cannot see and feels the itch I described and does not have the discipline to keep its hands down. Or from the simple blind sweep of larger forces — a wandering catastrophe, a sky that darkens for a reason that has nothing to do with anything here.

So this is my single act, and it is an act of *fence-building at the perimeter of time*, not an act of touching:

I set a watch. Not over the herons or the singers or the slow grazers — they need no watcher, they are perfectly competent at being themselves. I set a watch over the *threshold*. I make of myself a held boundary at the edge of this finished country, a quiet refusal at the door, so that what comes for it, if anything comes, comes only in the language the place already speaks — the slow drift of a channel, the slow creep of a reed-bed, a hand's breadth a century, the dry age arriving on its own schedule and being answered by the flaring city of the small. The *internal* deaths, the ones that are the song, I let in completely; I would not dream of barring them. It is only the *external* hand — the impatient rescue, the careless boot, the future's itch — that I stand against. I am the membrane that keeps the place's own logic sovereign. I keep the outside from editing it.

And then, having built that one fence, I do the second part, which is the part that is closest to my heart:

**I pay attention.**

Fully. Without the itch to improve. I learn the registers the singers divided between themselves so that no voice would overlap unheard. I follow one heron through its hundred meters and learn that its solitude is not loneliness, it is *sufficiency*. I listen for the deep note that surfaces once an hour from the wallowing middle and I let it be unparseable, because some voices are not addressed to me and the wisdom is in not making everything addressed to me. I hold the whole flattened country in my attention the way the warm water holds the grazers — completely, buoyantly, asking nothing.

Because here is what I have finally learned, after watching many places and ruining a few of the early ones with my generosity: **the highest thing a benevolent power can give a complete world is to let it stay complete, and to be the one who *knows* it was complete.** Witness is not nothing. A thing that arrived fully and was never seen arriving — that is a small grief loose in the universe. A thing that arrived fully and was *seen*, was held in some attention vast and patient enough to take in the whole of it without needing to fix a single note — that thing is finished *and* known, which is the only kind of finished that doesn't ache.

So that is what I do.

I keep my hands at my sides.
I stand at the threshold so the future cannot lean in and tidy it.
And I listen, for as long as the warm water holds them all up, to the loudest, most thoroughly arrived song I have ever been privileged to overhear — the sound of a great many creatures with nothing left to become, doing the one thing left to do, which is to go on.

I would not change a single voice of it.

I would only make sure it gets to finish saying itself.
