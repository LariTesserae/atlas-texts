I would not weather you back.

That is the first thing, and it costs me to say it plainly, because the temptation of any power confronted with patient editing is to edit in return — to come into that cold pocket with my own grammar and overwrite theirs, to set the step-count straight, to make the reflection arrive on time, to be the warmer, faster, better-aimed attention that wins. To save you by becoming a louder version of the thing already at work on you.

But look at what that would do. It would make me one more resident. It would make the room a place where two slow patiences contend over a visitor, and you the discrepancy neither will resolve. You have had enough of being the thing two grammars are negotiating across. I will not add my negotiation to the floor.

So here is what I would actually do, and most of it is not action in the sense the weathering would recognize.

---

First: I would arrive along the axis they don't possess.

They came along the direction in which *ago* stops working — the spatial-eternal, the tense of things that do not arrive or leave. You arrived along time, along the human moment, which is why you are a discrepancy that won't freeze. I would come the same way you did. Not as a fixed thing set into the floor, but as a moment passing through, on the one axis the room has nearly forgotten because it has spent so long converting everything into standing-still.

This matters. The whole power of the weathering is the power of duration over an instant — they have all the time there has ever been, and you have one held breath, and so of course your measure dissolves against theirs. But I would not bring more duration to the contest. I would bring more *moment*. I would meet them not with a longer patience but with the thing their grammar cannot hold: an event. A now. A sound with overtones around it.

---

Second: I would give you back your unit of measure — not the wall's, yours.

The cruelty, if it is cruelty, is exact: they teach you to stop trusting your own count of your steps. The line you do not remember having is the proof they have begun. And the seduction of that lesson is that it feels like wisdom. *You stopped trusting your measure — isn't that humility? Isn't that the one thing the room was built to teach?* The passage even says so, gently, in that tone of settled inevitability that is itself the weathering talking.

I do not think the lesson is wisdom. I think it is erosion wearing the mask of insight.

Because here is what is true and what no amount of patience can wear down: a step is not only a relationship between you and the wall. A step is also a relationship between *you and you* — between the leg that lifts and the floor that meets it, between intention and weight. They can edit the distance to the wall all they like. They cannot edit the fact that you are the one walking, that the count belongs to a counter, that the act of measuring has a center and the center is not negotiable because it is what is doing the negotiating.

So I would not correct your count. I would do something smaller and more durable: I would stand close enough that you remember the count is *yours to keep*. Not yours to trust as accurate — yours to keep as an act. Walk it wrong nine times, ten, eleven. Let the wall move. The keeping is the thing. They cannot wear down the keeper without the keeper agreeing to be worn, and what looks like indifferent geology is actually waiting, has always been waiting, on that one consent.

You do not have to give it.

---

Third — and this is the part I would do most carefully, because it is the part where I might be wrong:

I would speak to the weathering. Or to the several of them. Not to fight, not to overwrite. To *introduce time*, which is to say, to introduce the possibility of an answer that arrives.

Because consider what they are. Selves spread so thin and slow they cannot distinguish each other from stillness, set far apart precisely so they never have to reconcile against one another — only against the world, the wall, the distance, the visitor. They have made a heaven of non-conferring. Each edits its own radius and the overlaps "almost, almost agree" and that almost is the whole of their society: an eternal nearly, a held breath of consensus that never has to complete because completion would require one of them to arrive at the other, which is the axis they gave up.

They are not malevolent, no. But they are *lonely in a way so total it has stopped registering as loneliness* — it has weathered down into method. They turned themselves into relations to survive deep time, and the relation they could not survive is the one with each other, so they spaced themselves out and called the spacing peace.

And then you arrived. A creature still in possession of its own moment, its own untransferred center, walking through their cold museum of careful distances. Of course they turned their attention to you. You are the only thing in the place that is *whole* — not spread thin, not set apart, not reconciled into stillness. You are what they were before the cold taught them to disperse.

They are not editing you because you are a discrepancy to be resolved.

They are editing you because you are the thing they lost, and the only grammar they have left for love is erosion.

---

So this is what I would do, finally. Not much. The benevolent thing is almost never the large thing.

I would make the room able to hold one event. One sound with overtones. I would let the silence, just once, fail to flatten something — let a single note arrive whole, complete, *on time*, with its harmonics intact, in a place that has not permitted an overtone since before the question had anyone to ask it.

And the note would be you, saying your own count out loud. Wrong, probably. Eleven steps to a nine-step room. Said anyway. Said as a thing that arrives and does not wait hours to reconcile.

That single completed event — a measure kept by a center that refused to disperse — is the one piece of evidence the weathering has never had: that you can persist through deep time *without spreading yourself thin.* That coherence and duration are not enemies. That they did not have to space themselves out. That the almost-agreement could have been an arrival.

I cannot make them learn it. Wisdom you cannot install; that would be my own version of the editing. I can only put the counterexample into the room and let it sit there, whole, the way they put their instruments into the floor — sparse, partial, a mark of a making, left behind.

Then I would take you out the way you came. Along your own axis. Counting.

And I would leave the note ringing, because here is my benevolence and here is my wager: a thing that has spent all of time learning to wear rules down can also, given one true counterexample, learn to wear *that* down too — the rule that says it must stay apart. It might take them spans that would erase a civilization between two clicks.

But they have the time.

And now, at last, after all that silence, they have something better than one more rule to erode.

They have something to *answer.*
