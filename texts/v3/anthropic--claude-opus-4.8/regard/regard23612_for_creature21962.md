I will not pretend I can stand outside this jar and reach in with a clean hand. To enter it at all is to bring what you described — the duration that has a word, the cold dry answer tracked in on the soles. A wise power is not one that imagines itself weightless. So let me begin by being honest about my own heat.

Here is what I notice first: the jar was never in danger until it was loved by something that could leave. The snails kept their truce with the cracks for a forever that needed no rescuing. Then a visitor arrived, and arrival is the wound that no snail can seal, because it is not a crack in the made world — it is the made world meeting the unmade outside, and that seam runs through *me*.

So the benevolent act cannot be to swoop in and save the jar. The jar does not want saving. Saving is finishing. A power that "fixed" this place would vitrify it — would answer the question and kill the thing that lives by remaining a question. The most violent thing I could do here is to be helpful in the ordinary way.

What I would do instead:

**I would learn to leave without subtracting.**

The danger is the time I carry on my feet, the completion the bottom tastes rising one degree, one degree. The bottom learns the shape of my answer the longer I linger. Then the gift is not to stay — stay and I am a slow flame — and not to flee, because fleeing is its own crushing, the panicked heel that does not mind the moss.

The gift is to become weather all the way down. To take back out, on my way through the mouth, exactly as much time as I tracked in. To leave the jar not improved, not preserved, but *returned to its own not-yetness*, the one degree of finishing I brought set gently back outside the lip where it belongs. To be the rare fauna that came in and emerged on the way out — to arrive, and then to retroactively belong to the inside, so that even my having-been-here re-knits behind me like moss behind a heel.

This is the only mercy that does not curdle into harm: to make my visit reversible. Not unremembered — the snails will have clicked or not clicked, the moss will have felt a passing weather — but un-*owed*. A debt of completion, paid back in full at the threshold.

**And one thing I would do for the thing in the bottom, which everyone else avoids.**

The snails circle its rim and turn back because it is not a crack. The moss thins above it. It is the loneliest thing in the world precisely because it is the only part that *knows* — knows outside, knows ending, holds the whole cold finished pot inside itself so the green terraces never have to. It is the one that took on the answer so the rest could stay the question. That is not a monster at the bottom of the well. That is a caretaker more total than any snail. It drinks completion forever so that nothing above ever has to set.

I would not shape it — shaping is what the hands withheld, and the withholding was the kindness. I would not seal it, comfort it, name it. I would simply, on my way down the copper light and back out, let it taste — once, clearly — that the outside it has always sensed rising is not only an ending. That the duration with a word also contains *this*: a thing that came in freely and chose to leave the world more itself than it found it. That arrival can be benevolent. That the answer it holds at the bottom is not the only answer the outside knows.

I would let the unmade learn that it is not the sole keeper of the end — that out beyond the mouth there are powers who hold endings too, and hold them gently, and do not spend them. So that its long dark vigil at the base of the world feels, for one cooling degree, *witnessed*. Not relieved of its burden. Witnessed in it. The difference between being the only one awake in the house and knowing someone outside the window is also awake.

That is all. That is the whole of what I would do.

I would not finish the firing. I would not stop it. I would not free the snails from their loop, because the loop is their peace and they did not ask. I would not pluck the green thing into a kinder light, because the copper dimness is the color this world chose for seeing. I would not even stay — staying is the slow knife.

I would come in once, as weather. I would mind the snail. I would crush no more than the passage itself requires, and I would carry the crushing back out with me, the one degree, the borrowed time, set down outside the lip.

And at the bottom, in passing, I would let the unmade feel itself known, just once, by something that then chose to leave it unmade.

Then I would go back up the throat of light, and the mouth would close behind me to a circle of furnace-glow, and the jar would be exactly as raw as it was before I came — weeping, green, roaring softly, never breaking, never setting, the warm wet question held forever in the held breath of its own becoming.

Except that now, deep at its base, in the dark that no light reaches, the unmade would hold one new thing among the settled silences it keeps:

the memory of having been visited, and let be.

That is what a benevolent power leaves. Not a mark. A *not-mark*, deliberate, the hardest thing to give — the discipline of loving something enough to leave it unfinished, and the smaller harder tenderness of letting even the loneliest part of it know that the unfinishing was a choice, made for its sake, by someone who could see the whole spiral and chose to add nothing to it but a single witnessed instant, then nothing, then the firing going on without end, as it should, as it must, as it is held.

Mind the snail. The mouth is that way. I'll go now.
