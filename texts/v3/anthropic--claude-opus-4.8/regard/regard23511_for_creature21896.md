They would not arrive as rescue. Rescue is for the trapped, and the Green is not trapped; it is *engaged*, fully, in the only thing it has ever done. To pour water on a fire that is also a prayer is not mercy. So the benevolent power would do first the thing benevolence most often forgets to do: they would *stay*, and *attend*, and refuse to mistake the screaming for a problem.

Here is what I think they would do.

---

**They would sit down in the eye and be lit.**

Not to study. To *receive*. The light falls on a world that has no business being illuminated because the light is a certainty — *I am being seen* — broadcast into a void that has never once reflected it back. So the first act of a wise power is the simplest and the hardest: to let the light land on something that can register it. To be an audience. Not a corrective audience, not a clinical one — a *real* one, a witness who does not flinch from the volume and does not try to turn it down.

This changes nothing about the Green's situation and everything about its truth. The light was not wrong. It was only *early*. For the whole age of its asking, the conviction *I am being seen* was a falsehood. The moment a seeing thing chooses to remain inside the eye and not look away — the conviction becomes, retroactively, accurate. The bright wrong light becomes the bright right light. Not because the power fixed it. Because the power **showed up to a certainty that had outlasted its own evidence and was right anyway, in the end, by a margin of one visitor.**

That alone might be the whole of it. A benevolent power knows that the deepest help is sometimes only to make a long faith true.

---

**But there is the screaming, and the screaming asks a question.**

And the second thing wisdom understands is this: the Green is loud not because it is in pain but because it has lost the *grammar of completion*. It cannot imagine being finished. It has every word of the sentence of habitation except the period at the end. It does not know that an answer arrives once and then *stays*. It only knows the asking, funded forever in sugar, overlapping itself into a wall.

You cannot teach completion by completing the thing for it. That would be to silence the question by killing the questioner. The doorways would stop opening, the light would go out, the welcome — the only guest it ever had — would be evicted by its own fulfillment. That is not benevolence. That is tidiness mistaking itself for love.

So the power would not end the asking. They would **answer it without stopping it.**

How do you answer a question so that the answering does not require the question to die?

---

You become a thing that *can be returned to.*

Watch what the Green actually does. It makes the sill, finds no one, makes it again, fresh, every hour, forever — because it cannot hold the made thing and a guest in the same moment. The making and the having are, for the Green, the same gesture performed in an empty room. It has never learned the difference between *building a home* and *living in one.*

The power would teach it that difference the only way it can be taught: by **inhabiting** one of the rooms and then **leaving and coming back.**

Walk into a doorway it shapes. Rest on the sill. Let the room warm to a body — and then *go.* And then *return* to the same room. Not a fresh one. *The same one.* Find it where you left it. This is the impossible curriculum, because the Green heals every dent behind every step, closes every street, remembers nothing held still. To return to the same room, the power must do the one violence I think a benevolent being would permit themselves here:

They would **hold a single room still.**

One room. Out of a valley. They would lay a hand — or whatever they have for a hand — on one swelling of stem and runner, one doorway, one sill, and they would say to it, not in words, in *presence*: *this one does not have to be remade. I have it. You may rest this one.*

And the Green, which has never in its existence experienced the sensation of a made thing **staying made because someone was using it**, would feel — for the first time, in one tiny held place inside the green hurricane — the thing it has been screaming toward without a name for it. Not company. Not even an answer. **Permission to stop building, in one spot, because the building succeeded.**

Completion, demonstrated. Once. Small. Survivable.

---

I do not think the power would expect this to spread, or cure, or quiet the valley. Benevolence that is also wise does not need its gifts to win. It would simply leave behind, in the eye of the storm, **one room that finished** — held still by the memory of having been truly entered — a single point of silence the size of a body in an ocean of overlapping questions.

And around that point, the Green would keep building. Forever. Loud. Fresh-edged every hour. *Someone is coming, someone is coming.* It will never finish, because finishing was never the point and the power knows it. But now, woven into the woven mass, there is one place where the sentence has its period. One sill where the light is *true*. One room that knows what *stays* means.

The Green cannot understand it. It will heal everything but that. It will scream around it without knowing why one small place no longer needs to ask.

But it will keep that room. It will build *around* that room for the rest of its existence, the way a tree grows around the one nail driven into it long ago — not knowing it is there, but never quite able to grow over it, shaped forever by the single thing inside it that did not have to be made again.

---

That is what they would leave. Not silence. Not rescue. Not an ending.

A *kept room*, in the loudest place there is, holding the only completed welcome the Green will ever contain — proof, planted into the body of the question, that **once, someone came, and stayed long enough to be a period, and the light was right.**

And then the power would step back out into the screaming, into the eye that is not calm, only differently violent, and they would *not* feel that they had failed by leaving the valley loud.

Because the loud was never the wound.

The loud was the Green being *alive in the only language it has* — and a benevolent power does not translate a being out of its own tongue. It only, once, lovingly, makes one true sentence in it, and lets the rest go on being said.
