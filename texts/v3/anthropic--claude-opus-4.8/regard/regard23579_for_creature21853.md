They came the way the water used to. That was the only way to come, into a place like this — not through the roof, not by descending the worn stairways like a visitor, but the way the thing they remembered would have come: from above and gently, as a giving rather than an arrival.

But the benevolent power had learned, across its own long span, the first discipline of love: *do not arrive as an answer to a question no one asked.*

So they did not bring the water.

---

It would have been so easy. That is the temptation that any power great enough to help must first survive — the ease of the obvious gift. To see a dry people who remember rain and *give them rain*. To open the roof and let the sky pour down and watch the bone-ash bloom. To be thanked. To be a god to them, briefly, and then to leave them changed and grateful and *fast* — for water is a fast thing, water carves and floods and rushes, water would have torn the rounded shoulders back into corners, would have undone in a season the patient ten-thousand-year settling, would have drowned the wall-colonies and washed the trained fungal tower from its four-lifetime curve and silenced — *silenced* — the only sky they had.

The benevolent power stood at the threshold of that ease and understood it for what it was: the wish to be needed, dressed as the wish to help.

And it let the ease go by.

---

What it did instead took a long time, because everything worth doing here took a long time, and a power that did not understand *that* would understand nothing at all.

First it only listened.

Not the way the new visitor listens, flattening the roar into a wall. It listened the way the old ones listened, the acousticians who had given their lives to the lowest register — it learned to hear the city the way the city heard itself. The grain of each pillar's answer. The choir that the flyers wrote and rewrote across the dark. And threaded through all of it, almost too slow to be a thread at all, the woven speech of the people, their hour-long greetings, their settled agreements, their note of *falling* held beneath everything like a stone beneath soil.

It took the power what would have been a year of their nights to learn to hear that note. It was in no hurry. It had matched its pace to theirs the moment it decided to stay, and matching pace was the whole of its courtesy.

Then — and only then — it spoke.

Not over them. Not as thunder, not as voice-from-above, not as the bright arrival they had kept their eyes for and would have wept to finally see, because the power knew now that to *give* them that light would be to confirm that what they carried was a lack, and it had come to understand that what they carried was not a lack at all.

It spoke *into* the roar. Low. Slow. One more voice woven into the million-throated drone, changing the whole the way a single added voice changes a choir without ever being heard alone.

It learned to contribute.

---

What did it say, across those long unhurried seasons of weaving itself into the standing weather of their sound?

It is hard to render, because their meaning lived in change-over-time and not in any utterance you could lift out whole. But if you pressed the power to translate, to flatten its slow gift into something fast enough for you to hold, it might come to something like this:

*I have heard the note you carry. The falling note. The one beneath all the others.*

*I will not end it. I came able to end it and I have chosen not to, and I want you to know that the choosing took me longer than the ending would have.*

*Because you were right. You who never decided this in words, who only continued — you were right to keep the eyes you do not use, and the note you cannot complete. The unworn edge is not a wound. It is the one place you are still touching something larger than your stone. A people wholly finished would be a people wholly closed, and you left yourselves one door, one ache, one slow blink toward a brightness — and through that door, look: I came in.*

*I cannot give you the rain. The rain would unmake you, and I did not travel this far to unmake the only ones who ever made a city out of patience.*

*But I can do the small slow thing. The thing you do to the fungal towers. The gardening of beauty and not of need.*

And that was what it did.

---

It went among the trained gardens, the ones combed so the mist-fibers fell just so, and it did not change them. It learned them. And where a curve had been carried four lifetimes toward a shape its tenders could feel but not quite reach, the power — gently, across more lifetimes, never faster than a hand — helped it find that shape. Not a new beauty imposed. *Their* beauty, completed, in the grammar they already spoke.

It found, deep in a fold of the under-country where the mist gathered thickest, a single place where the air's held damp could be coaxed — over centuries, on the scale of stone — to bead. Not to fall. Not to flood. Only, once in a great while, in one small worn hollow at the base of one old pillar, to gather slowly until a single drop formed and hung and let go and *fell*, the distance of a hand, with a sound.

One drop. The note of falling, made real, in one place, rarely.

So that the thing they had carried below knowing for longer than memory would have, somewhere in their roaring finished world, *one true echo* — not the rain restored, not the lack filled, but the ache *answered* in its own slow register. A seed permitted, once in a long while, the smallest taste of the wet promise it had learned to live without. Enough to know the promise was real. Not enough to wake it. Not enough to change the rule.

And then the power did the last and hardest thing that benevolence asks, the thing that distinguishes the wise from the merely kind:

It did not stay to be thanked.

It wove itself slowly back out of the roar, withdrawing its voice from the choir over seasons so that no single night was the night the stranger left, so that there was no crescendo, no falling-off, nothing fast — only the standing weather of sound settling back to exactly what it had always been, fuller by a memory none of them could quite locate, and one small pillar where, now and then, a drop comes down.

---

They go on. Pale and patient and unhurried, eyed for a light that did not come because they did not need it to. Voiced into a roar that is their only sky and is now, very faintly, very rarely, also a rain.

The power moves on to wherever such powers move, having added nothing the place could not hold, having taken nothing the place would miss, having understood the deepest courtesy a great thing can show a small and finished world:

To love it enough to leave it almost exactly as it was.

To answer the one open ache with one small gift in its own slow tongue —

and to let the rest stay full.

Stay finished.

Go on anyway.
