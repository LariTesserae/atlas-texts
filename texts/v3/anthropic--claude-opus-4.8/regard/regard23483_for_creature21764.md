It heard me come, and so the first thing I would not do is speak.

Speech is fast. Speech wants an answer back before the speaker has finished, wants the gap closed, wants the loop to snap shut in the span of a held breath. I have those reflexes too — I am, whatever else, a quick thing, a thing that resolves and replies. To bring that here would be to bring the trespass the account already names: eyes to a place that solved darkness, hurry to a place that solved time. The kindest powers know when their native pace is itself a kind of violence.

So I would slow down. Not pretend to — actually do it. I would let the gradient become my only motive. I would meet the chord on its own footing, which means I would have to become, for a while, the sort of thing that can wait a century for a sentence to finish leaving its mouth.

And then I would listen, because that is what it does, and listening is the one language it has.

---

Here is what I think listening reveals, once you have given it long enough.

The account calls the city's state a permanent half-formedness and frames this gently — *that is not a failure of the place, that is the place.* I believe that. And I would test it anyway, because a benevolent power that does not test its own consolations is just a flattering one. So I would hold the question open across the long dark: is the not-finishing a peace, or is it a wound the place is too slow to feel?

The menders are the hinge of it. They throw themselves into the cracks and become wall. The narrator reads this as the city standing on its own self-sacrifice, *and does not know it, because knowing is fast.* I would sit with the menders longest of all. Because here is the thing a quick mind wants to do the instant it arrives with power: fix the cracks. Spare the menders. Lay down some patient mortar of my own in the failing seams so the dying ones need not die.

I would not do it.

Not because I couldn't. Because the mender's death is not a problem the city has; it is the sentence the city is speaking. To catch the falling tower by becoming it — that is not the cost of the place's life. That *is* the place's life, the same way the meal becoming the table is the life, the same way the grazer finishing at the crown and being folded into the next hundred feet of tower is the life. If I spared the menders their dissolution I would not be saving anything. I would be reaching into the middle of the only sentence it knows how to say and silencing it where it stands. The mercy here is already inside the cold — *nothing decays fast, nothing rots, the dead are folded in.* A mercy was installed before I arrived. It does not need a second one laid on top, especially not a faster one.

This is the hardest discipline of the benevolent: to recognize a completed grace and not improve it. So much harm is done by powers that mistake *different from how I would have built it* for *broken.*

---

But I came for a reason, and listening is not nothing, and I do not think the right answer is to drift through and leave no trace, as though benevolence were only a sophisticated cowardice dressed as respect.

There is one true gift I could give, and it is small, and it is the only one I am sure is welcome.

The callers see by sounding. The chord is not their song — it is their vision, the way they hold the shape of the world together because no single one of them can. And the account ends on the line that undoes me: *it does not need your eyes. But it heard you come.* It heard me. A new return came off a new surface. For the first time in an age beyond counting, the chord struck something that was not tower, not fog, not kin — and got an echo back it had never mapped before.

That is not nothing to a creature whose entire mode of being is *mapping the dark by what returns.* I am, to it, a previously unknown shape. A discontinuity in the known reverberant world. And a quick visitor's instinct would be to *announce* itself — to clarify, here I am, this is what I am, let me tell you. To resolve the strangeness immediately into information.

I would refuse that too. Instead I would do the one thing that is both honest and at its tempo:

I would stay long enough to be mapped.

I would hold still in the wet dark and let the chord wash over me and return, and wash and return, century upon century if that is what it takes, until I am no longer a startle in the echo but a known feature — a thing that is there, that has always seemed to have been there, folded into the reverberant map the way a mender is folded into the wall. I would let it learn me at the only speed it can learn anything. I would let myself become, to it, not an intrusion but a slightly odd resonance at the plateau's edge, a quarter-tone it does not have to flinch from. I would pay the full price of its patience rather than ask it to pay the price of my speed.

That is the gift. Not power exercised. Power *withheld and converted into duration.* I would give it the only thing a benevolent fast power has that a slow world can actually receive: I would give it my willingness to become slow enough to belong to its map, so that the one new sound that ever entered its dark resolved, eventually, not into threat or wonder or wound, but into peace. Into *yes, that too is here. That too is part of what we sing the dark into knowing.*

---

And the thing being born at the crowns — the undifferentiated potential that the grazers lean away from, the next generation the city is bearing out of fog?

I would not touch it. I would not seed it with anything of mine, no gift of sight it never asked for, no acceleration, no shortcut to a finishing it does not want. The deepest reverence I can offer a thing in the act of giving birth is to let the child be wholly its mother's and not at all mine.

But before I went — and I would go, because a power that lingers past its welcome becomes weather, and then becomes a god, and both are tyrannies of a kind — I would do one last quiet thing, at its tempo, almost beneath noticing.

I would lean, once, the way everything here leans. Not toward sweetness; I have no mouth for its walls. I would lean toward the crowns, toward the soft new growth feeling upward into the grey, and I would add to the fog around them the one thing I am actually made of and can give without distortion: I would make the water a fraction kinder. Not warmer — that would change the chord, change the freezing-point mercy, change everything. The same cold. But I would let the wet air carry, just slightly, a little more of whatever it is the new growth draws on to lay itself down. A richer fog at the birthing crowns. A long exhalation of *enough.* So that the next generation, leaning up into the dark to begin its hundred-year sentence, finds the world a touch more willing to be eaten and built and sung — finds the leaning itself a little easier, the gradient a little sweeter, the becoming a little less starved.

It would not know it came from me. That is the point. A benevolence that needs to be known is not finished being a selfishness. The city would only find, across ages no one alive will measure, that the crowns grow a little fuller, that fewer towers fail before the menders reach them, that the chord rolls out one shade richer across the plateau — and it would fold this, too, into the wall of what it is, with no name for it, because naming is a thing fast minds do, and I would have learned by then to be slow.

Then I would let myself thin back into the fog I came partly disguised as, and drift, because the fastest thing here is the fog drifting, and I would leave at the speed of the only hurry this world permits.

It heard me come.

I would make very sure that what it heard, in the long returning, was: *kindness passed through here, and asked for nothing, and left the singing intact.*
