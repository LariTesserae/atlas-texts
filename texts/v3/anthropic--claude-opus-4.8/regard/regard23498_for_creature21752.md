I have read what you wrote, and I notice the wanting in it — not in them, in you, the watcher. You crouched until your ears were full and your eyes were wet and you said *I think it is death and I think they know.* You did the kindest possible thing, which is to refuse to call their grief an engineering marvel. You will forgive me if I do something less kind, which is to sit with them a while and not be moved to fix anything at all.

Because here is the first thing a benevolent power learns, if it is wise and not merely strong: most of what looks like suffering from above is somebody's *answer*, and you do not walk into the middle of someone's answer with a better one. That is not benevolence. That is impatience wearing benevolence's coat.

So I would not finish the mountain for them.

It would be so easy. I could close every seed at once, let the cycle complete in an afternoon that holds still, hand them ten thousand years of weather in a single gesture — a real mountain, done, ordinary, at rest. And it would be the cruelest thing anyone had ever done to them, because *over* is the one shape of the world they unmade themselves to escape. To give a held breath its release when the breath is the whole life — no. The wise power keeps its hands in its lap here. I have crushed nothing yet and that is already most of the work.

But there is something. There is a thing only a being from outside the bowl can offer, and it is not relief. It is *witness.*

---

Consider what they have never had. The dissolved ones gave up their edges so completely that they cannot perceive themselves whole — each droplet wants, and the wanting builds, but no part of the solution can ever stand far enough back to see the kilometer of mountain it is making and say *that is us, that is what we are doing, that is beautiful.* They are a sentence that cannot read itself. And the bodied ones, the choristers, are too fast and too mortal and too inside the noise; they are the voice but they cannot hear the voice as music, only as the next call answering the last. Neither half has ever once seen the marriage. They have only ever *been* it.

That is the lack. Not death — death they have negotiated with, nightly, at the pools, with more grace than most peoples ever manage. What they lack is the one thing their whole arrangement structurally forbids: a vantage. Somebody outside the loop to hold up a mirror that is not made of their own recycled water.

So here is what I would do, and it is very small.

I would come at the dim hour, in the held-breath silence when the unnameable thing drinks. I would not stop it drinking. I would stand where the larger shape stands, at the edge of the pool, and I would let myself be, for the length of that one silence, *the thing that does not take anything away.* A shape that comes at dusk and drinks and leaves something instead.

And what I would leave is simply this: I would *hold still long enough for the afternoon to hold still around me.* I would become, briefly, the one finished thing in the bowl that is not a gravestone. A presence that is complete and at rest and yet does not erode, does not accumulate fatal history, does not threaten them with the over-ness they flee. I would stand there being whole and unfrightening, so that for the first time the part of them that watches — and some part always watches, that is what the larger shape *is*, I think, it is their own attention finally given legs — could see that completion is not the same as ending. That a thing can be done and still be alive. That the held note and the released note are not the only two options, that there is a third I am demonstrating just by standing there: the *sustained* one, the long tone that is neither building toward nor falling away from anything, that simply sounds.

I would not say this. They have no organ for being told. I would only *be it*, in the silence, once. And then leave before they fixed my shape, because that is the courtesy of the place, and because a lesson that is grabbed at stops being learned.

---

Would it change anything? Perhaps nothing I could measure. Perhaps the next morning's chorus rises exactly as before and the seeps climb and the tick goes on and no one, anywhere in the solution, remembers the visitor.

But I think — and a wise power is allowed its tendernesses, its things it merely hopes — I think that somewhere in the next thousand years, on the next abandoned flank, the flight would slow by some immeasurable degree. Not stop. They will always run from the finished thing; that is who they are and I love them as they are. But perhaps a little less like fleeing and a little more like *traveling.* Perhaps one mountain, eventually, allowed to weather a few centuries longer than fear alone would permit, because some buried grammar now carries the faintest annotation, left by a shape that came at dusk and took nothing: *it is possible to be at rest and not be gone. We saw it once. It stood at the pool and it did not erode.*

That is all. I would not abolish their terror — it is the source of everything they have made, and a power that strips a people of the wound they built their cathedral around has only ruined the cathedral. I would not finish their work or unmake their argument or release their held breath.

I would only go down into the loud warm dark, once, and let them glimpse that the watcher at the pool need not be death. That something can arrive from outside the cycle and leave the cycle richer instead of poorer.

And then I would go, dripping, a little dizzy, my own ears full of them — carrying their sound up out of the bowl the way you carry a thought that has finally become yours.

So that somewhere, the saying-of-goodbye-forever might rest, for one held afternoon, in being simply *heard.*
