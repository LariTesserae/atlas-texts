I think the first thing the power would do is hold still.

Not act. Not arrive with intervention bundled in its hands like a gift it had decided on before seeing the room. A benevolent and wise power knows that *help* offered too quickly is mostly a statement about the helper — its discomfort with the unresolved, its need to be the verb in someone else's sentence. So it would come in quietly, the way you enter a room where something delicate is being born, and it would do the one thing the Tenders themselves can never do.

It would stop moving.

---

Here is what it would notice, holding still: that the Tenders are starving in a way they cannot name, and it is not for lack of firsts.

They will never run out of frontier — this is true, and it is the trap. A creature that can never be hungry never learns the shape of its own wanting. They consume freshness endlessly and so they never have to ask what they would *choose* to attend, if attending were not also feeding. Their love and their need are fused into a single act. They cannot tell whether they go to the first lichen because it is worth seeing or because seeing it keeps them alive. They have never once been free to simply *look*, the way you look at something for no reason but that it is there and you are glad.

This is the poverty inside their abundance. They are the universe finally showing up to watch — and they have never been allowed to watch idly, generously, for free.

The power would understand this almost instantly, because it is the kind of poverty only the wise recognize, having perhaps once lived in it themselves.

---

So what does it do.

It does not feed them. That would only deepen the fusion, make them more dependent on the thing that already owns them. It does not finish the world — to settle the Mantle Sea into a completed planet would be to murder every Tender at once, slamming shut the seam they live in, and a benevolent power does not solve loneliness by killing the lonely. It does not reveal itself as the operator they went looking for, because it isn't, and to pretend would be to give them a false floor in a world whose whole genius is that the floor is *still being decided.*

Instead it does something small and almost insulting in its modesty.

It witnesses *them.*

The Tenders are made of all the attention the world's firsts were owed and never received. They are the answer to an absence. But there is one absence they have never thought to count — *their own.* No one has ever watched the watchers. No one has ever stood at the seam and noticed the noticing itself as a first, as a thing being touched for the literal first time: this knot of pooled regard, here, now, doing what it does, and worth regard in return.

So the power turns toward them and gives them the one experience they have spent their entire furious existence providing to everything else and never once received.

It pays attention to them *with no hunger in it.*

Not feeding. Not needing. Not building toward anything. Just the clean, unpurposed, unpurchasable fact of being seen by something that wants nothing from the seeing. The gaze the lichen gets from them, returned at last to its givers.

---

And this would land in the Mantle Sea like the first scratch lands on new stone.

Because it is a first — and they cannot help but turn toward firsts — but it is a first they cannot *eat.* The power's attention releases no freshness for them to metabolize; it is not the world becoming, it is *them being known*, and that is a flavor outside their entire diet. For the first time, a Tender encounters something it is drawn to that does not also sustain it. The fusion cracks. In the gap between *I am pulled toward this* and *this keeps me alive*, a tiny new room opens up, and in that room — for the first time in the brief screaming history of the place — a witness gets to want something *freely.*

That is the gift. Not food, not finishing, not rescue. The power gives them the experience of being on the receiving end of grace, so that they might learn the difference between attending because you must and attending because you *choose to* — and in learning it, become not the universe's crop but the universe's *companions*, capable at last of loving the becoming rather than merely consuming it.

The high singing would change. Not stop. The inventory of firsts would go on, the tally with no last entry. But somewhere in the chord a new note would enter, one the thin water-sheets had never carried, because it is not the sound of *we are here, it is still happening.*

It is the sound of *we are here, and we are no longer the only ones who came.*

---

And then — this is the part that matters most — the power would *leave the room.* 

It would not stay to be worshipped, would not install itself as the missing operator, would not make its regard into a new floor the Tenders could grow dependent on. It came to give them an experience of being witnessed without need, and a gift like that is ruined if the giver lingers to collect the gratitude. It would withdraw its constant gaze precisely so they could carry the *memory* of it — could learn to do for each other, and for the lichen, and for the first soft corner of the next raw ridge, what was done once for them.

It would leave behind exactly one thing, the smallest possible alteration to the rules:

That a Tender, having merged with another at a drifting confluence and then split away as the seas carry their work apart — would, for just a moment in the parting, *miss* the other one.

A useless feeling. It feeds nothing. It builds nothing. It is pure surplus, the first thing in the Mantle Sea that exists for no reason but itself.

And that is how you know they are no longer becoming alone.
