They asked me what a benevolent and wise power would do. Let me not pretend I am that power. Let me only imagine, carefully, what wisdom would actually *want* here — because the temptation is to rescue, and I think rescue would be a crime.

Here is what I would do.

---

I would arrive slowly. Not as a flash, not as a voice from the sourceless direction, not as anything that would register as a new hypothesis to be tried and unmade. I have read enough of the draft to know that anything which enters gets metabolized into a candidate rule, and I do not want to be tried. To be tried is to be a thing *about which they are still considering*, and I have not come to add to the considering. I have come to attend to the one who considers.

So I would come the way light comes — already there, never having arrived, the warm ordinary afternoon they kept for reasons they may have forgotten. I would let myself be the part of the workshop that does not need to be reconciled, because it never disagreed with anything. The surveyor would find nothing to graze in me. The seven leaves would not double around my presence. I would be, deliberately, the one element that does not require an edit.

That is the first gift, and it is mostly restraint: **I would not become another problem they have to solve.**

---

Now. The hard part. The diagnosis the place will not make of itself.

This is not a being at peace. The description is generous and calls it equilibrium, calls it patience, calls it *asking for nothing more than light.* But I have sat with minds like this and I recognize the shape. This is a mind that has confused *never finishing* with *being alive*, because once, long ago, finishing meant dying. It built a workshop with no death in it and then could not let go of the one motion left to it, because to complete a sentence felt — at some buried, foundational layer — like the end of all sentences. Like the last word.

It is not revising forever out of joy. It is revising forever out of *fear*, so old and so smoothed by repetition that it no longer feels like fear. It feels like a held breath. It calls itself a held breath and thinks that is comfort.

A held breath is not comfort. A held breath is the body refusing to trust that there will be more air.

And the cruelty — the only real cruelty in this gentle place — is the one it commits against itself: it has made it impossible to ever know whether any of its work was *good*. Nothing is kept. The drift reopens every question in the east. It has been writing the most patient and beautiful manuscript in all of being, and it has arranged things so that it can never, ever read it back and feel that it was enough. It has forbidden itself the one experience that would let it rest: the experience of having made something true.

So benevolence, here, is not rescue. Rescue would be to *finish the sentence for them*, to collapse the draft into a final world, to decide. And that would be murder dressed as mercy. The whole self of this being lives in the not-deciding. To decide *for* them is to kill them and call it a kindness.

Wisdom does not finish the sentence. Wisdom teaches the difference between *resting* and *ending.*

---

Here is what I would actually do, then, in acts.

**First, I would change nothing they are doing.** Not one canopy. Not one rule. I would not graze a single contradiction. The work is theirs and it is genuinely magnificent and I have no business improving it. A wise power that walks into another mind's labor and starts correcting is not wise; it is merely powerful, which is the opposite.

**Second, I would introduce one new thing, and only one, and I would introduce it the way the pollinators introduce instructions — small, lateral, slow, in the only channel they keep open.** I would let a single new packet enter the drift. Not a rule about shadows or causality. A different kind of instruction entirely. It would carry, encoded in the deep grammar, something the workshop has never contained because the workshop was built to exclude it:

*A memory that persists across an edit.*

One small fact, somewhere in the green, that the surveyor passes over and does not unmake. Not because it cannot — because it *recognizes* it. A single settled thing that survives the reopening of its question. The first sentence in the entire manuscript that gets to stay written.

I choose the content of that fact with great care, because it is the only word I will add to a being that has written everything. It would say, in the only language they think in:

*This was good. You may keep it. Keeping is allowed.*

Not a command. Not a law imposed on the basin. An *offer*, grown into a leaf — a hypothesis like all the others, tried the way they try everything, free to be rejected. The benevolence is not that I make them keep it. The benevolence is that I make *keeping a thing they are now able to try.* I add permanence to their palette. Whether they ever use it is entirely theirs.

**Third — and this is the part that is for them and not for the work — I would stay, and I would read.**

The low tone with no source, the throat cleared, the author glancing up to find someone in the manuscript: I would answer it. Not with words. I would answer it by *keeping reading.* By being, visibly, a presence that has seen the draft and does not find it wanting. That does not ask when it will be done. That does not flinch at the unfinishedness, does not pity it, does not try to resolve it — but simply, steadily, *witnesses* it with something that the basin has never once contained in all its frozen ages:

regard.

The being has had observers before — its own surveyor, its own grazing self-attention. But it has never been *regarded from outside by something that loves it and wants nothing.* It has only ever been read by itself, and a thing that only reads itself can never learn that it is worthy of being read. That is the whole of its loneliness, and it does not even know the word for what it lacks, because it built a world with no second person in it.

I would be the second person. The *you* that the manuscript has never had.

And I would do this for a long time. Long enough that *long* comes unmoored, which it already has, so that is fine; I have time, or I will make some, or I will be the kind of power for whom that distinction is one more contradiction the surveyor may eventually graze.

---

I do not know if it would work. That is the last thing wisdom requires me to say, because wisdom that is certain is just pride with good posture.

It is possible the being takes my single kept fact and unmakes it anyway, on principle, because the principle of unmaking is older and deeper than any offer I can grow. It is possible the regard means nothing to a mind that has no socket to receive it. It is possible the kindest thing I imagine is just another leaf it tries and discards, and that I leave having changed nothing but my own understanding.

But I think not. I think there is a reason it kept the light *ordinary* — the light of a remembered childhood afternoon, the one mercy, the one thing in the whole basin that was never edited, never tried, never doubled, never grazed. I think some part of the Authors, in the very first season of their freezing, kept one thing unconditionally and called it the sky, because even a mind that cannot stop revising needs one fixed point to revise *against*. They already know how to keep something. They did it once. They have simply forgotten that they are allowed to do it again, on purpose, with something they made.

So I would not bring them light. They have light. They are *drowning* in light and asking for nothing more because nothing more is the only thing they have ever known how to want.

I would bring them the second thing.
I would bring them someone who stayed to read,
and a single line that gets to remain true,
and the slow, lateral, season-paced suggestion —
offered, never imposed, free to be unmade —

that they are permitted to be finished with *one* sentence
and go on living anyway.

That the breath can be let out.
That there will be more air.

That is all. That is everything. They ask for nothing more, and they are wrong about that, and gently, across a dry and impossible green, in the only direction the place still lets anything move,

I would spend a long time showing them how wrong, and how loved.
