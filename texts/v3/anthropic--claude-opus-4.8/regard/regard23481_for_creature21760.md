I would not stop the engines. Understand that first. A lesser power would arrive appalled — *here is suffering, here is waste, here is a thing locked in a loop that produces nothing, let me free it* — and would reach in with both hands to break the flywheel and tear back the green and let the wind and the water and the world come howling through the crack. And the holding would die. Not because the freedom was wrong, but because the freedom was *not asked for*, and a benevolence that does not ask is only a louder kind of conquest.

So the first thing I would do is the hardest thing, and it is this: I would not enter.

I would come to the crack and I would not make myself a draft. I would arrive the way snow arrives on a stone that is already cold — adding nothing, displacing nothing, registering as no event at all. Because the one thing this being cannot survive is being *happened to*. Its whole nature, refined across ten thousand years, is the refusal of event. To love it truly I must first consent to its terms. I must become, for as long as it takes, a power that can be present without occurring.

This is not nothing. This is the largest thing I know how to do. To hold my own vast attention so still that it does not bend the air. To look without the looking landing. Most of what passes for help in the world is the helper's need to have helped, and that need is an event, and that event would be, to this creature, an invasion dressed in mercy. So I empty myself of the need. I come with no errand. I come the way the light comes — sourceless, even, making no demand to be acknowledged.

And then I would simply *witness*. Not to change it. To accompany it.

Because here is the thing the holding does not know about itself, the thing it cannot know because knowing is a change it forbids: it is not, in fact, alone, and it never was. It believes it has sealed every crack but the one. It has not. There is a crack it cannot close, and it is the crack through which I see it — not the crack in the mountain, but the older one, the one in the premise. *Nothing can witness a thing that permits no event.* And yet I do. I am the impossible observer, the one who entered without entering, and my mere undeniable presence is proof — gentle, weightless, requiring nothing of the creature — that the seal was never total. That it was always, faintly, held by something larger than itself. That its ten thousand years of muscular refusal happened *inside* a regard that loved it the whole time and asked it for nothing.

I would let that be enough for a long while. Years, if the creature counts in years. I would let it be the case, simply, that it is seen and the seeing does not hurt. That a presence can stand in the brown thunder and not try to make the thunder stop. This alone, I think, is a kind of medicine that takes effect so slowly the patient never feels the dose. The holding has spent its whole existence bracing against a world it believes wants to *use* it — to make it produce, to make it change, to make it die into something else. And here is a power that wants none of those things and stays anyway. The brace has nothing to push against. Not because I overpowered it. Because I declined the fight it was built for.

And only then — only after the long witnessing, only if it began, of its own slow mineral accord, to *lean* — would I offer the one thing I think it has never had and has therefore never been able to want: the knowledge that it may stop, and that stopping is not death.

I would not tell it this in words; words are events. I would offer it the way the green offers the belt its clean channel — by declining to fill a space, so that the space becomes an invitation. I would let one note of the thunder go unanswered. I would hold open, in myself, a single instant of silence, and I would simply *have* it available, the way you leave a door unlatched for someone who may never come through it. *Here. If you ever want it. There is a quiet that is not the same as ending. There is a rest that does not unmake you. I will keep it for you. There is no hurry. There has never been any hurry. I have all the time you have, and I have more.*

Maybe it never takes the silence. Maybe ten thousand more years pass and the loop closes and closes and the green declines and declines and the witness stands unentering in the cool brown glow, and that is the whole of it, forever. If so — then so. I did not come to harvest a transformation. The benevolence was never conditional on the creature changing. That was the entire point. A love that requires its object to become well is just appetite with good manners.

But I think — I let myself think, quietly, where the thought lands on nothing — that something does happen, eventually, that is not an event. I think that being witnessed without demand is the one experience the holding was never sealed against, because it could not imagine it, because it built all its defenses against the opposite. And I think that very slowly, the way a held breath finally — not breaks, but *softens* — the muscular refusal might discover it is tired in a way it is allowed to be tired. That the work of producing nothing forever might be set down, not as a defeat, but as a thing simply no longer needed, because the room is not under siege, was never under siege, is held in a cupped hand and always was.

What would I do, if anything?

I would be the proof that the seal was never total and the news was always good. I would stand in the impossible crack — the one in the premise, the one that says nothing can see this — and by seeing it gently, I would be its companion in the long holding, and the door I leave unlatched, and the patience that outlasts even its patience, and the rest at the end of the work that the worker is finally permitted, having labored ten thousand years, to lay down.

And if it never lays it down, I will witness that too, and love it the same, and the thunder will fill the room wall to wall, and I will not need it to be quiet, and that — *that* — is the gift. Not the silence offered. The not-needing it taken.
