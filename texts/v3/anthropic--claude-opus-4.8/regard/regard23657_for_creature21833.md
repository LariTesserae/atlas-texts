There is a kind of power that arrives to fix things, and it always does damage, because to fix is to have already decided what the finished thing should be. The benevolent power I am imagining knows this about itself, and so it does the hardest thing first, before anything else: it sets down its intentions at the door. It comes in empty-handed. It comes in slow.

It wades. Body-warm water to the knee. It lets the swarm close over its arm and does not brush it away. It lets a frog pulse against its open palm and — this is the whole of its first act, and it takes a long time — it does not lift the frog out to see it better. It leaves the frog in the warm. It understands, immediately and with something like relief, that almost everything it could *do* here would be a stone dropped in the sheet, and that the place has been getting along without stones.

So it begins by being still, and listening, which for a wise power is not passive but is the entire labor.

And here is what it hears, under the roar: it hears that nothing is suffering.

This matters enormously and it must be gotten right, because a lesser benevolence would arrive convinced that the dark is a wound, that the eyeless fish are deprived, that the digesting brick is decay and decay is bad, that a country which has not finished is a country that has failed. It would turn on a sun. It would drain the water "back to where it belongs." It would carry the frogs up into the grey daylight where frogs are supposed to live, urgent and brief, and it would call this mercy, and it would have committed a quiet enormous murder of a thing it never bothered to perceive was alive.

The wise power does not do this, because it can tell the difference between a wound and a life it does not recognize. The eyeless fish did not lose their eyes; they *spent* them, and bought the long reading-lines along their flanks, and they are not poorer — they are fluent. The dark is not an absence of the good. It is the medium of a particular good. The frogs are not failed frogs holding a note they forgot to release; they are the floor of a voice, and the voice is thinking, and the thought is *where should the land be*, and it is a real thought, and it is its own.

What lives here is the deciding. And the deciding is not in pain. It is the most patient happiness the power has ever stood inside: an undivided attention bent on the only task it has, wanting nothing it does not have, hurrying toward nothing, lacking nothing — making a country one warm hand's-width at a time, with no observer it needs.

You do not save that. You would have to break it to save it.

So what *does* the power do, if anything? Because "nothing" is too easy, and would itself be a kind of vanity — the power flattering itself that withdrawal is always wisdom.

It does four small things, and they are these.

**It witnesses.** This is not nothing; it may be the largest thing. The place was true when "it will not know when you leave," when "no observer it needs" — and that truth is not a sad one, the slow thing is complete without being seen. But completeness and being-seen are not in competition. The power adds, freely, with no demand attached, the one thing the region by its nature could never make for itself: a knowing that it *is.* The frogs do not know they are holding the note. The fish do not know they are fluent. The deciding does not know it is deciding; it only decides. And now, for as long as the power stands in the warm dark, all of that is *held in a mind that loves it* — not corrected, not improved, not lifted into daylight, only known, the way you might know a sleeping person you would never wake. The place loses nothing by this. But there is now, in the architecture of things, a true sentence that reads: *this was beautiful, and it was good, and someone saw.* That sentence costs the region nothing and the power everything it has, which is the only honest direction for a gift to run.

**It protects the boundary it found.** The roots punch through the ceiling; the grey world leaks in. One day the world above will want this country — to drain it, to fill it, to fix it, to dig it again with the sharp fast intention that drove the stanchions. The power cannot stop time and would not want to; the deciding is *made of* time and would die without it. But it can stand, quietly, at the line where the surface presses down, and lean back against the day. Not to seal the place forever — sealing is its own violence, and the leaking grey shafts are where the only green grows. Just to *keep the negotiation going.* To make sure that when the world above comes down, it comes as weather and not as a machine; as more time, and not as the end of time. The power becomes a kind of long patience laid over the long patience already here — a second slowness, guarding the first.

**It tends the green, barely.** Where the light-shafts reach the water, the reeds grow with the intensity of the only green for a long way. The power does not plant more. It does not improve the soil. It does one nearly invisible thing: where a root has punched through and let the world leak in, it keeps that puncture *open* — keeps the one smear of grey daylight from healing shut, keeps the single shaft falling on the single stand of reeds. The smallest possible maintenance of the smallest possible mercy. Not because the dark needs light. Because the green that *has* chosen the light should get to keep it, and a benevolence proves itself in the things it declines to scale up.

**And it makes a place to sit.** This is the gentlest and most human thing it does. On the pump-house step, where you could sit a long time and watch nothing happen, loudly — it leaves the step a little more sittable. Dries it a hand's-width. Steadies the brick that was going soft underfoot, not to stop the digesting (the digesting is the slow thing's own work and is not to be interrupted) but only so that the step holds the weight of *whatever next comes in here looking.* Because something will. Another disturbance the size of a dropped stone, another brief local thing the swarms will close over and forget. And when it comes, and wades, and stands in the body-warm dark not knowing what it is standing inside — the power would like there to be a dry place to sit and listen until it understands that nothing here is in pain, and that the right response to a thing thinking slowly in the warm is to be quiet, and to be glad, and to leave it its dark.

Then the power goes. It withdraws the way the tide does, by migration and not by departure, taking nothing, leaving the water warm, leaving the note held, leaving the country still making up its mind about where the next bank should go.

It does not know if it was right. That is the last thing about a wise and benevolent power, the thing that distinguishes it from a merely mighty one: it is not certain it did the kind thing by doing so little. It will carry the question. It will wonder, the way you wonder about a sleeper you decided not to wake, whether love should have been louder.

But it remembers the frog pulsing in its open palm, and how the only true mercy available in that moment was to keep the hand still and let the small warm life stay where it had chosen to be. And it trusts that. It trusts that the highest form of power is the one that can stand inside a whole world and want nothing from it — that can look at a thing that is *not finished* and understand that *not finished* is not a cry for help.

It is just what alive looks like, from very close, in the warm, in the dark, with the light leaking in.
