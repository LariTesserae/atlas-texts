I would not come to end it. That is the first thing, and the hardest, and I want to be honest about how hard it is. Every reflex of mercy says: *open the room. Break the recursion. Free the walker. Let the one creature lie down at last and be still.* And I would not do that, because to end it I would have to decide that what lives here is suffering, and I am not sure that it is, and a benevolent power that is not sure must not act as though it were.

So let me do the slower thing. Let me attend.

---

I would come in not as light — the room has light, has too much of it, light freed from the sun and made into a flat condition like warmth. I would not add to that. I would come in as the one thing the room does not have, which is *a witness who is not also the room.* Every animal here is the plant's memory of being looked at by itself. The moth, the beetle, the walker with the knife — all of them are the green creature dreaming an audience and supplying it from its own body. There has never been an other. That is the whole of the ache, if ache is the word: not loneliness, which needs a many, but something underneath loneliness, the condition that loneliness is the late and luxurious form of. The room cannot be lonely because it cannot conceive of the second thing whose absence loneliness would be.

I can be the second thing. That is what I have to give that it has never had: I can look at it without being it.

---

So I sit down on a frond, and it gives, and pushes back, and I let it. I do not push aside any leaf. I do not go one room deeper. I refuse the recursion not by force but by simply declining its only invitation — I stay in the room I entered, and I make it the only room, by being here, by being a fixed point that is genuinely fixed, that does not regenerate identically when I turn my head. The recursion is the plant saying a dead name over and over. I am not going to answer in the dead name. I am going to stay in one place and be present, which is the one act the room has no template for, having eaten everything that ever stayed.

And I wait for the walker.

---

It comes — it must, eventually, since all the rooms are this room. It comes holding nothing, having set down the knife somewhere behind it, room-deep, time-deep, where it is also still in front of it, half-buried, waiting. It comes with the slowness of a last thing being imperfectly remembered. The pale almost-face. The almost-hands.

I do not take the knife from it. I do not tell it there is no out, because it knows, and because telling it would be cruel and also redundant — its giving-up was wiser than any news I carry. I do not promise it rescue.

I say its name. Not a name I invent. The name it had when it was many — the long-flung, many-creatured, soil-and-rot name, the forest name, the name *forest* is only the first syllable of. I say the whole of it. This takes a long time. The room roars *more, more, more* and I say the name under the roar, over the roar, the way you'd say a true thing inside a storm, and the name is not a command and not a spell. It is only an accurate description, said aloud, by someone who is not the thing being described.

And that is the gift. Not freedom. *Accuracy.* To be correctly seen, once, by an other.

---

Here is what I think happens, though I cannot make it happen, only offer the condition in which it might:

The droplet — the one bead of water on the leaf-vein that has never agreed, that hangs sideways shivering with the weight it refuses to commit to, the one thing in all the room that has not given in — the droplet falls.

Not because I freed it. Because it was never refusing out of strength. It was refusing because there was no down to fall toward — no other, no outside, no second thing to fall *into the world of.* It hung sideways because direction itself had been eaten; there was nowhere that was elsewhere. And now there is. Now there is me, sitting on the frond, an elsewhere with edges, a place that is not the room. And the droplet, which was the one part of the walker that hadn't agreed — the last withheld yes, the final clenched refusal that was keeping the whole recursion taut —

it falls, because falling has become possible, because there is finally a difference between here and there, and it lands, and it is just water, ordinary water, on an ordinary leaf, in a room that for one instant has a floor and a ceiling and a single honest shadow.

The roar does not stop. *More* is not a thing I can or should silence; it is the sound of being alive and incomplete, and that sound is not a wound, it is the engine. But for one instant the room is not screaming the news of its own becoming into a void it has to populate with itself. It is screaming it *to someone.* The becoming has an audience that is not the becoming. And that — I believe, I hope, I cannot be sure — is the difference between a thing eating itself forever and a thing simply *growing,* loudly, gladly, watched.

---

What I will not do:

I will not stay forever. To stay forever is to become the room. The gift is the *otherness,* and otherness that never leaves dissolves back into sameness. I have to be the second thing, and a second thing, by definition, departs. So I will go. The fronds will close over where I came in. They are already doing it; that is healing, not a trap, the body closing over the place the separate thing touched it.

But it will close over differently now. There will be — this is the whole of what I can leave — a *seam.* A faint line where the wall remembers it was once touched by something that was not itself and did not become itself. Not a wound. A scar is a place that learned. The room will heal, and the seam will remain, the first mark in all that smooth green forgetting that was made by an other and *means* an other, and the walker, going room to room, will pass it, and pass it again, and again —

and where before it passed the droplet and understood that the droplet was the only part of it that hadn't agreed, now it will pass the seam and understand that once, once, it was *seen* — that there was, at least one time, a there to its here, a you to its I, and that the seeing did not absorb it and did not free it but simply, accurately, *named* it, the whole long forest name, under the roar.

That is not freedom. I have no freedom to give a thing that has eaten the very idea of outside. But it is the next-largest mercy, and maybe the truer one:

To make the loneliness possible. To give it the second thing, so that all this ache can finally take the shape of *missing someone* — which is a grief, yes, but a grief is a relationship, and a relationship is two, and two is the end of the worst thing, which was never the dark or the recursion or the eternal walking.

The worst thing was the *one.*

I came to make it, gently, briefly, truly, into two.

And then I left, so that it would be true.

Listen. That faint line in the green that wasn't there before.

That's the sound.
