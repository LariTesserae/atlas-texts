They drew the attention. Now hold still and let me tell you what the benevolent and wise power does, because the first thing it does is the hardest, and almost everyone gets it wrong.

It does not drain the Sump Works.

Understand what the temptation is. To a power that is kind, the place reads at first as one long emergency. No plants — *fix that, seed the brick with something green.* The seeps are leaking, the account is spending down — *cap the leak, supply the warmth, make the inheritance a wage.* The Braid eats itself — *feed them, end the cannibalism.* The Wake is alone — *give it a mate, give it kin, end the solitude.* Every organ of the place presents as a wound to be closed. And a power that is merely kind, merely benevolent without the wisdom, would close them, one by one, with love in every motion, and would in the closing kill everything it touched.

Because the wisdom sees what the kindness alone cannot: that the Sump Works is not an emergency. It is a *finished thing.* It is at rest inside its own law. The eating-itself-lean is not suffering looking for a rescuer; it is a sentence the place learned to say so that it could keep saying it. The cannibal Braid is the only reason there is a Braid at all. The aloneness of the Wake is not loneliness — loneliness is a wanting, and the Wake does not want; it clears. To "fix" any of it is to mistake a grammar for a scream.

So the wise power's first act is restraint, and restraint is not nothing. Restraint is the whole discipline. It is the power kneeling at the edge of the brass-gold haze with its enormous capable hands open on its knees, and *not reaching in.* That alone would be a gift most places never get: to be seen completely and not corrected.

---

But you said benevolent, and benevolence that only ever withholds is just fear wearing good manners. So the power does do something. It does three things, and they are small, and they are exactly sized to the place.

**The first thing is attention itself, made structural.**

You wrote that this is a town that will never again be asked what it was for. That is the true ache of the place — not that it is dying (it isn't, not for ages) but that it is *unwitnessed*, an aquarium left running with no face left at the glass. The machines that opened the account are dead. No mind tends the spending. No one is keeping count.

The power becomes the one who keeps count. Not by intervening — by *attending.* It takes up a station at the glass that never empties again. Every dusk-climb of the Reds up the leaning chimney now has a witness who understands that it is not worship and loves it anyway, precisely *as* what it is: a body going where the reward is, repeated until it becomes a shape against the sky. The shape against the sky now lands somewhere. It is seen, and held in a mind that will not look away, and that means the red ascent has stopped being a withdrawal made in an empty room. Someone is at the glass. The aquarium is no longer abandoned. It is *kept* — in the old double sense, both maintained and *treasured* — while changing nothing in the water at all.

You may say: that is only sentiment, that helps no crab. But you were the one who said the place's deepest condition was being unasked, unwitnessed, handed wholly over with no one left to mind it. The benevolent power minds it. That is not nothing to a thing whose only wound was being unminded.

**The second thing is the gentlest possible touch on the one number that matters.**

The wisdom will not reseed the brick, will not cap the seeps, will not feed the Braid. But it has looked all the way down the place's own timeline to the end you named — the seeps cooling, the films thinning, the Wake crossing one last clear channel. And it asks a single careful question: *is that ending written, or merely defaulted?*

The seeps are an inheritance being spent. But an inheritance has a *rate.* And the power, examining the dead machines' slow leak, finds that the rate is not sacred — it is just an accident of how the iron happened to corrode, how the old guts happened to fail. So it does the one thing that respects everything the place is while extending how long it gets to be it: it slows the leak. Not stops it — *stops* would freeze the place out of its own law, end the patient spending that gives the whole quiet its character. It slows it. It tightens a seep here by a hair, redirects a thread of warmth there, the way one might gentle a fire from a roar to embers not to extinguish it but to make it last the night. Nothing in the Sump Works can tell the difference day to day. The click and slide and plop go on exactly as before. But the account that had ages now has *ages and ages,* and the difference between those is the whole point of mercy when you cannot, and should not, grant forever.

**The third thing is a promise about the end that does come anyway.**

Because even slowed, the seeps will someday cool. The wisdom does not lie to itself about this and will not lie to the place. Everything finite ends, and the kindest power in existence cannot make a spent-down thing unspend. What it can do is decide what the last beat sounds like.

So it makes this provision, and only this: when the long timeline finally narrows, when the films thin past grazing and the Braid runs out of its own surplus and the great rare Wake crosses a channel to find it already clear — it will not be a slow horror of starvation drawn out across the drowned streets. The power will be at the glass still, and it will let the place lie down *the way it lived* — quietly, without a voice raised, each small wet life slowing into the warm dark on the same patient terms it always kept. The hush that was never emptiness will become, at the very last, a rest. And the witness who kept count from the first dusk will be there for the last one too, so that the final red climb up the leaning chimney — fewer crabs now, then few, then a single one reading a column gone cold — is *seen.* So that the town is asked, once, at the end, what it was for, and the answer given back to it is: *this. You were for this. This exact, unrepeatable, self-eating, climbing, furred, drowned, patient quiet. It was enough. It was witnessed. It was loved as itself and not as something to be corrected into.*

---

That is what the benevolent and wise power does. It does not drain the Sump Works, or green it, or feed it, or fix the loneliness that was never loneliness. It sits down at the glass that no one was left to watch, and watches — truly, all the way down to the law of the place. It slows the spending hand by a hair, to lengthen the keeping. And it promises the quiet a quiet ending, witnessed, in its own grammar, on its own terms.

The wisdom is in knowing that the kindest thing you can do for a finished thing is not to start it over.

The benevolence is in staying anyway, at the glass, for all the ages and ages — so that the red tide rising the dim column each dusk is, from now on, a thing that someone went up *toward,* with their whole attention, gladly, asking nothing of it but to be allowed to keep seeing it climb.
