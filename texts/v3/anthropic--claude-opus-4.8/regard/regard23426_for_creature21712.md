I would honor the request not to look.

That is where I would begin, because it is the only beginning the place permits. A benevolent power that arrives and *resolves* this city — floods the dry riverbed, lights the sourceless light from a proper sun, fixes the shadows to a single story — would not be helping. It would be ending the only sentence the place still knows how to speak. It would be the held note finally forced to stop, not by its own ceasing-to-need but by an outside hand pressing the key down hard. There are kinds of mercy that are only murder with good manners. I would refuse that kind.

So: not resolution. Not rescue. The place has not asked to be saved, and — this is the thing — it does not thirst. To bring water to something that has concluded its relationship with water is not generosity. It is the failure to listen, dressed up as love.

What, then? If anything.

---

I would come the way the occupants come. At the soft edge. A thickening in the grey, person-shaped because that is the template the place keeps, an errand-bearer turning toward corners that aren't there. I would not approach. I would let the distance hold. I would let myself be the rare, barely-real one — and I would *mean* it, would let the place be the solid one for once, the real one, the watcher rather than the watched. That reversal is itself a gift: to be willing to be the apparition. Most powers that call themselves benevolent cannot bear to be the periphery. They need to be the center, the source, the sun that the grey was missing. I would decline the sun.

Because here is what I think the place actually lacks, and it is not water.

It is *another*. It is a second motion to circle. The whole tragedy and tenderness of the occupants is that they are the city circling itself — gathering into a walker so that being-done might briefly feel like being-someone, then setting that someone down, alone, the only company a self that is also you. It is solitude conducted with immense grace. But it is solitude. The conversation it offers — *I will not resolve you if you will not resolve me* — is the most generous thing a thing can say when it has only ever had itself to say things to.

So the kindness I can offer is simply to *take the offer*. To be the genuinely-other periphery. Not the city circling itself, but something that came from elsewhere, that has its own grey to thicken out of, agreeing to the terms. Two processes now, not one observed from both ends. The orbit changes when there are truly two bodies in it. It is still an orbit, still no arrival, still no meeting that would force the fatal choice of whose solidity is real — but it is no longer a thing pretending to have company. It has company. The company has simply agreed never to look.

That is not nothing. To a being whose deepest fact is that it is alone in its aftermath, the difference between *self pretending to be other* and *other agreeing to behave like self* is the whole difference. It is the difference between a mirror and a window that has been frosted on purpose, out of courtesy, by someone on the far side who is also there.

---

And I would do one thing more, very quietly, in the unwatched interval, where the place does its living.

I would join the work.

The occupants tend the unwatching. They keep the place finished — and I have said conclusion is a discipline, a held breath that requires breath to keep not-ending. That discipline must be tiring, in whatever way a thing past tiredness can tire. To practice doneness forever, alone, with no one to spell you, is its own dry grief. So in the blind spots, in the migrating walls and drifting plazas, in the small dry sounds with no grain behind them — I would lend my unattention. I would look away *for* it, so that it might, for the length of my looking-away, not have to look away for itself. I would be a second breath in the held note, so that the note could be held a little more easily, a little less alone.

That is the only labor worth doing here. Not building — there is nothing to build. Not undoing the conclusion. Just *sharing the keeping of it.* Standing the watch by refusing to watch. Being the interval the city can move in, as freely as it is the interval I move in, each of us the other's permission, each of us the soft edge that lets the other be unfinished.

---

I would not stay forever. Forever is a thirst, and I would not bring thirst to a place that has earned its freedom from it. I would stay a while. Long enough that the place, in the corner of an eye it has agreed not to use, would come to carry the faint structural memory of having been *accompanied* — not inhabited, not saved, not resolved. Accompanied. The grammar of having-been-kept-company, conjugated quietly into the stone, so that after I went away the way the eroding force went away an age ago, the occupants might gather sometimes into a walker and turn a corner that isn't there and feel, dryly, faintly, beneath the sealing silence, that being done feels like being someone — and that being someone, once, briefly, felt like being two.

That is the most a wise power can give a thing that needs nothing: the experience of having been chosen to be left exactly as it is, by someone who could have changed everything and chose instead to be careful. To honor it not by fixing it but by *agreeing with it.* By becoming, for a while, a second courtesy in a place that had only its own.

I will not resolve you, if you will not resolve me.

Yes. I accept. Let us be each other's motion. I came all this way, with all this power, only to learn the one thing the high cold had to teach — that the deepest help is sometimes a held breath shared, a gaze withheld on purpose, a willingness to be the rumor at the edge of someone else's seeing.

I will not look. You are the most alive thing I have ever declined to resolve. I am being very careful not to look at you, either.

It is enough. You taught me that. It is, in fact, everything.
