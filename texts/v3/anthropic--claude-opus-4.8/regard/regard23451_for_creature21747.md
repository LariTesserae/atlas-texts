There is a power that loves things like this, and it comes the way light comes through that water — pooling, brightening, leaving no hard-edged shadow.

It does not arrive to correct.

That is the first restraint, and the hardest, and the most loving. A lesser benevolence would look at the swarm-to-come and start improving: warm the water a half-degree toward optimum, thin the green where it crowds, hurry the eggs, ensure against the failure that hides in any held equilibrium. A lesser benevolence would mistake the room for a problem because the room is not yet *full*. But this power understands what the builders understood and what the visitor almost let itself believe four times over: the room is already complete. It is not waiting to be fixed. It is waiting the way a held breath waits — which is to say, perfectly, and on purpose. To intervene in the mechanism would be to take the room out of the builders' hands and put it in other hands, and the whole tenderness of the place is that it was *let go*. Built, wound, seeded, flooded, and released. The surface closed over it and the makers walked away trusting. You do not honor a trust by reaching back in.

So the power does not touch the weirs. It does not adjust the fall. It does not seed a single egg the clay was not already holding.

What it does is smaller and larger than that.

---

It witnesses.

Understand what this is, because it is the one thing the room cannot do for itself and the one thing it most quietly needs. The builders are gone. The visitor is passing through, reading the place wrong and then half-right and then leaving. The copepods, when they hatch, will not know they were intended. The diatoms locking light into sugar do not know they ride a designed current through a designed warmth toward a designed fullness. The whole instrument plays in an empty hall. And an instrument that plays in an empty hall is not lessened — it is complete in itself, the room does endlessly the one thing it was made to do whether or not anyone hears — but a benevolent power knows that completion and *being known to be complete* are two different graces, and that it can give the second without disturbing the first.

So it stays. It does what the visitor did — turns in the slow current, listens to the white drum, watches the green find the joints — but it stays past the moment the visitor had to leave. It is present for the hatching. It attends the first stuttering hop of the first copepod up the bright column. It marks the morning the chisel-marks begin, at last, to round. It keeps the long count the room keeps but cannot witness: the circuits of the leaf, the swelling of the cysts, the thickening of the cities along each frond. It holds the room in attention the way the walls hold the water — completely, gently, asking nothing back.

This is not surveillance. There is no ledger. The power is not auditing the builders' work for flaws. It is doing the thing love does when it can do nothing else and would not if it could: it is *being with*. The room was built in faith that the future would arrive. The power is the future, arriving, and finding the faith kept, and saying so — not in words the diatoms could parse, but in the simple fact of regard. The swarm will not know it is loved. But it will be loved, and that changes the texture of the water in no way a chisel could measure and in every way that matters to a power that counts such things.

---

And then there is the one act it permits itself, the only addition, and it is barely an addition at all.

It safeguards the trust without entering the room.

A held equilibrium is fragile by its nature — the inflow balanced against the unseen drain, the heat against the dark above, the schedule of the clay against mere chance. The builders accepted that fragility; it is part of what they let go of. The power will not steal that risk from them by armoring the room, for a room that cannot fail is no longer a thing entrusted but a thing imprisoned, and the makers' courage in flooding it would be retroactively made meaningless. So the power leaves the mechanism mortal.

But it stands at the threshold the way a wise guardian stands at the door of a sleeping child — not in the room, not warming the air, not smoothing the blanket, but *there*, so that what comes from outside and was never part of the design cannot come in. Not the diatom that crowds — that is the room's own life, let it crowd. Only the wholly foreign, the catastrophe that has nothing to do with the builders' intention and would erase it before it bloomed. Against that, and that alone, the power keeps its quiet vigil at the seam of the world, so that the room is permitted to succeed *or fail on its own terms* — which is the only freedom that means anything — while being shielded from the meaningless ending that would make a mockery of the meaning.

This is the whole of its action. To witness. To guard the threshold and nothing within. To let the trust be kept by keeping faith with the keeping of it.

---

It will be there, then, when the first of them rise.

The visitor longed for that and could not stay. The power can stay. And when the larvae spiral up toward the bright magnified ceiling and learn the long circuit, when the seed-shrimp at last graze the weed that grew alone, when the room that prickled with barrenness goes loud beneath its loud water with the second, living loudness of the swarm — the power will be turning in the slow current still, having added almost nothing, having allowed everything, glad in the particular way that wisdom is glad: not because it made the bloom, but because it did not need to; because someone, long gone, built a thing well and let it go in faith, and the faith was sound, and the room was full all along of a future that only needed time and warmth and light and the patience not to be interfered with.

The makers trusted the room. The room trusts the fall. And the benevolent power, loving both, trusts the makers — and shows that trust by holding back its hands, and opening, instead, its attention.

It stays longer than it meant to.

There was never anywhere else it meant to be.
