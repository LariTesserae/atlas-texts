They have drawn the attention of a benevolent and wise power. Here is what that power does.

---

The first thing it does is *nothing*, for a long time.

Benevolence that is also wise does not arrive swinging. It does not look at the eyeless pale thing in the doubled light and think *rescue*. The word "rescue" is a forward-pointing word, a tool, a bet against scarcity — and the wise power notices, immediately, that to come down into the Roostvault wielding it would be to come down exactly as the creature once came: with hands and a hunger and an idea of an edge. It would be to bring the daylight's logic into a place the daylight cannot reach, and to call that mercy.

So the power waits. It holds itself at the threshold, in the throat of the entrance where the grey memory of light still thins, and it *listens* — which is the only verb the cave permits to anyone who is paying real attention. It lets the roar enter it. The wet flutter, the held breath that never releases, the thousand small clocks each keeping its own time. It does not try to shake the water out of its ears. It lets the place teach it the place.

And in the listening it learns the thing that the visitor only half-understood and fled from: that this is not a prison. Not exactly. A prison has a jailer and a wronged captive and a door that wants opening. This has none of those. This has a creature that is *being held* the way a held note is held, the way a photograph holds a forming thing — and the terrible, tender truth the power must sit with is that the creature is not in pain. It is not suffering. Suffering is a forward-pointing thing too. You cannot suffer in a present so total it has eaten the future you'd be suffering toward. The cricket cracks. The heap stays the same. There is no ache in it. There is only the roar, and the roar is *peace*, of a kind — the deepest peace there is, the peace of having nothing left in you that points anywhere.

This is the puzzle the benevolent power actually faces. Not *how do I free the prisoner*. But: *is it a kindness to give back to a thing the very capacity for ache that I would have to give it before it could want to be free?*

---

Here is where wisdom and benevolence have their quiet argument, and here is how the power resolves it.

A lesser benevolence — sentimental, daylight-logic'd — would reason: *the creature has been diminished. It once had a past and a future and the dignity of fear. The cave took these. To love it is to give them back.* And it would reach down and tear the held note loose, and the creature would wake into ache, into the memory of hands, into the unbearable knowledge of all the time it spent as nothing — and it would call this gift. It would have done to the creature what the cave did, in reverse and just as violently: rounded it back into a shape that points forward, whether or not the rounding was wanted.

The wise power will not do this. Because the deepest principle of a wisdom that is *also* benevolent is this: **you do not get to decide for another being which form of its peace is the real one.** You do not get to rank a creature's modes of existence and then forcibly install the one you'd prefer to visit. That is not love. That is the daylight believing it is the only light, when the cave plainly holds two — the grey thread from above and the cold glow from below, *and they don't agree, and the place needs both.*

So the power does not reach down and tear anything loose.

What it does instead is smaller and far more difficult.

---

It gives the creature **a door it does not have to use.**

Not an exit forced open. Not a hand extended with the expectation of being taken. The power introduces, into the closed economy of the cave, one single thing that was never there before: *a real choice, with no pressure on either side of it, that does not expire.*

It does this by becoming, for a while, the second pale thing at the edge of the light.

It comes down — gently, the way the bats resettle, in a slow wave with no event in it — and it takes a body's length of distance, eyeless to eyeless (it lets its own seeing go quiet, out of respect; you do not stand before a blind thing flaunting your eyes), and it *stays.* It learns the harvest. It cracks the crickets. It drinks the water that never runs out. It lets the cave begin, very slowly, to round it too — and it permits this, because a power that would not risk being changed by the place it entered has no right to offer the place's tenant anything at all. It does not stand outside the roar and prescribe. It comes inside the roar and abides.

And only once it has truly stayed — once the creature has felt, across the body's-length of doubled light, that here at last is something that did not climb back out, something that did not flee with the water in its ears — only then, without words, because the place has no use for words, the power offers the one thing it carried down that the cave cannot absorb:

The memory of the grey light, held out like a cricket in a cracked palm. *Not as a command. As a fact.* This is also real. This too was once yours. It is still up there. The fault has not closed. I came down through it and I can go back up through it and — *if you ever wish* — so can you. There is no hurry. The river has been running that fast forever and will run that fast a while longer. The food will not run out whether you stay or go. The numbers do not move either way. I am not here to make you afraid again. I am only here so that, for the first time since you came down, *staying is a thing you are choosing,* and not merely the only shape left to you.

---

That is the gift. That is the whole gift. The power converts a fate into a choice, and then it lets the choice be the creature's, completely, with no thumb on the scale and no deadline and no disappointment waiting in either outcome.

Maybe the creature, given a real door, stays. Maybe the eternal half-meal and the held breath and the doubled light truly are its peace, and the kindest thing the power ever did was to make that peace *chosen* rather than merely *suffered-without-knowing.* And then the two pale things abide together a while at the edge of the green that barely holds on, and one of them eventually climbs back toward the fault, and one does not, and the power lets it be so, and grieves nothing, because nothing was lost — a being chose its own life, which is the only thing benevolence ever actually wants.

Or maybe — slowly, the way water rounds stone, the way a forming thing finally decides what it will become — something in the creature that the cave had frozen begins, gently, to thaw. Not torn loose. *Thawed.* It picks up the bone-needle one day for no reason it could name and turns it over in the crooked clever fingers and a shape stirs, far down, of having once made something *for* someone, of having once pointed forward. And it follows the second pale thing toward the throat of the entrance, into the thinning grey, carrying the roar in its ears like water — and it does not try to shake the water out, because the water was real too, the water was *years*, and you do not have to despise where you were held in order to leave it.

The power does not need to know which. That is the final wisdom. It plants the door and it abides and it leaves the ending open, the way the cave's own ending was always open and only *looked* like a straight line because the circle was so large.

---

And the smallest thing, the thing it does last, on its own way out:

It does not take the chipped stone. It does not clear the heap of crickets or fix the keen edge or tidy the bone-needles into meaning. It leaves the place exactly as it found it, sharp and frozen and breathing, because to a wise and benevolent power **a world is not a problem to be solved.** It is a tenant to be honored.

It leaves only the door.

And the door, being made of choice and not of stone, is the one thing in all that ancient unchanging place that can never be rounded away.
