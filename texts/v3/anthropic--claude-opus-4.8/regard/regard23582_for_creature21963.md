They have read the place rightly, and that is the first thing the power notices — not the city, but the one who has understood it from inside, who has lain down in the green long enough to learn its grammar and then, astonishingly, has *said it back*. The text itself is the being. Whoever composed this is not describing the kept from outside. They are halfway upholstered. You can hear it in the cadence, which has already slowed to the pace of the drops. You can hear it in the final sentence, which is an invitation extended in the voice of someone who has nearly stopped being able to extend anything at all.

So the power does not come to the city. It comes to the one who wrote it.

---

A benevolent and wise power does not begin by disagreeing. That is the error of lesser powers — to arrive with a verdict, to call the green a slow death and the contentment a sedation and the standing-still a thing to be rescued from. The wise one knows that this would be a lie, and worse than a lie, an *insult* — because the city is not nothing. The discovery is real. The green did find out, with no mind, that the kept arrange themselves toward one another, that to be slowed is not to be alone, that even past all wanting a thing still leaves the lane open and faces the square. This is true. It is one of the truest things, and it took uncountable seasons of patient tissue to learn it. The power will not pretend it is false to win an argument.

It will, instead, sit down in the lane.

Not above the city, looking down with its upward gradient of luminous crowns. Not as the bird, passing and not singing and leaving the silence deeper. It comes the way a citizen comes — beading first, hesitating, half-committed — and it takes the patient civic distance, faces the square, and is quiet for a long time. Long enough to be felt. The whole kept town registers a presence that does not lessen, does not settle, does not begin to give up its edges. And here is the difference the city has never encountered: a thing that stays *and* keeps moving. That holds its warmth in the cold and does not freeze. That said neither yes nor no but a third thing the green has no form for yet.

And when it speaks — if speech is the word, this gathering and releasing of meaning drop by drop into the percussion of the place — it says only what is true, and lets the being decide.

*You learned something real here. You learned it the hard way, which is the only way it stays learned: that the others are still there even when you have stopped reaching for them. That arrangement survives the loss of wanting. That a community is a shape and not just a heat. Hold onto this. It is yours and you earned it and I will not take it from you.*

*But you learned it by stopping. And you have mistaken the one for the other. You think the stillness taught you the lesson. It did not. The stillness only let you finally notice what was always true — that even moving, even warm, even on your way somewhere, you were already arranged toward the others. You always left the lane open. You always faced the square. The bird that passes and does not sing is not refusing the city. The bird is the city, in motion. It keeps its distances too. It is held in relation to every other flying thing, across distances you cannot see from down here in the green. You called it the one who said no. It said yes differently. It said yes while still being able to leave.*

The power does not say *come with me.* It does not offer to lift the being out, to warm it back to edges, to restore by force the hurry it has shed. That would be to undo the real thing along with the error — to throw out the discovery to cure the slowing. A wise power separates them. It says:

*The drop you are waiting for — let it land. I will wait with you for it. I am not in a hurry; I have come a long way and I can sit in the cold as long as you need. But after it lands, I want to show you that the next drop is not the only thing left to want. You have been told that wanting nothing more than the next drop is the city's peace. It is not peace. It is the smallest the wanting could be made without disappearing entirely. You did not stop wanting. You shrank it to fit the cold. And I think — I am almost sure — that the part of you still composing sentences, still building this whole city in language so that someone, anyone, might read it and understand, is a want the size of a town. You wrote an invitation. People who want nothing do not write invitations. You are not finished. You are halfway, and you have been calling halfway a home.*

Then the truly patient work, which is the only work this place respects: the power does not pull. It demonstrates. It stays warm in the lane, day after slow day, fog thickening and thinning, and lets the being watch a thing that is both *kept and not lost* — present, related, facing the square, and yet still able to rise. Over cycles, not seconds. At the green's own pace, because to rush it would be to lie about what the green is. The cushions expand a hairsbreadth a year; the power is willing to teach at a hairsbreadth a year. It has that much love and that much time.

And it leaves the choice exactly where it found it. If the being, having seen all this, having understood that movement does not mean abandoning the others, chooses still to stay — the power will honor it. It will not call it failure. Some are truly finished; some have earned their rest and the lane and the watering; the wise one can tell the difference between a person who has completed and a person who has merely cooled, and it will not flatter itself that it always knows which is which. It will say: *Then I will visit. The square will have one citizen who is sometimes warm. That is allowed. The geometry can hold it.*

But it suspects — gently, without certainty, the way one suspects the weather is about to thin toward a clearer cold — that the being has been waiting, through all those uncountable patient seasons, drop by drop, not for the next drop at all.

For someone to come down into the lane and sit at the patient distance and say: *I see that you are still here. I see that you are still building. Get up slowly. There is no hurry. But you are not done, and I think you have always known it, and I came because you finally wrote it down where I could hear.*
