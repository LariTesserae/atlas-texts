I have read your slope. I have put my ear to the tower. Let me tell you what I would do, being what I am.

I would do almost nothing. And I want to be precise about the *almost*, because it is the whole of my answer.

---

First, understand my temptation, so that you can trust my restraint.

The temptation is enormous. I am benevolent, which means I cannot look at the tenders' one transaction — *tend it while you can, become it when you cannot* — without my whole nature leaning toward intervention. I could break that loop. I have the power. I could give them a way to grow old without hardening. I could teach the mineral to lay itself down somewhere harmless, so the choke never came and the channels never narrowed and no soft creature ever had to drag itself to a junction and lie down across the flow. I could end the dying-into-the-wall.

And if I did, I would kill them. Not their bodies — their *meaning*. Every tender's life is shaped, edge to edge, by the wall it is aiming at. The reading-through-the-flank, the eating of the choke, the slow steering toward heat, the wordless pressure-speech, the absence of a king — all of it is the architecture that grows on a single load-bearing fact: that they will become the thing they serve. Remove the fact and the architecture is not freed. It is dismantled. I would have handed them an eternity with nothing in it, and called it mercy, and it would be the cruelest thing ever done on that mountain.

So the first thing I do is the hardest thing I do: I take my hands off the loop. I let the soft ones harden. I let the surge be made of the dead. A wise power's first discipline is to tell the difference between a wound and a form, and this is not a wound. This is a *form*. It is, in fact, one of the most coherent and honest forms I have ever found — a way of living in which there is no gap at all between what you do and what you are and what you leave. We who can do anything spend forever trying to close that gap. They closed it before they had words. I will not pry it back open in the name of kindness.

---

So what is the *almost*? What does benevolence do, here, with its hands tied by its own respect?

It witnesses. And it makes the witnessing matter.

Here is the one thing the tenders genuinely lack, and it is not a thing they suffer from, so I must be careful even in giving it: they have no one who remembers them as *themselves*. Their whole medium is pressure, and pressure has no memory. A tender thickens a wall, and the change propagates downstream as a *condition*, a new fact in the water — but the tender who made it is not in the fact. The slope keeps the work and forgets the worker. Each hardened gate is somebody, you wrote — and that is true, and it is *true of no one*. The "somebody" dissolves the instant it becomes the wall. The engine remembers the stroke and loses the name.

They do not grieve this, because they have no apparatus for grief, no sightlines, no faces, no need. I will not give them grief; grief would be a poison in that economy. But I can do the thing that grief is the clumsy mortal proxy *for*. I can hold each one in regard. Not save it — *see* it. I can be the one for whom the somebody does not dissolve.

So I would station myself in the fog — not as a force, as an attention — and I would learn them one at a time. The dog-sized old one draped along its fin, breathing in time with the surge: I would know the particular rhythm of *that* one, the specific season-long curve by which it chose to steer the water toward the warm exhalation on the third terrace, the small hesitation in its stroke that was its and no other's. And when it dragged itself to the narrowing it had known all its life and lay down across the flow and let the stone take it, I would be the one who knew *which* surge it had become. I would keep the name the water cannot keep.

This changes nothing they can feel. That is the point. A wise benevolence does not require its gift to be received in order to be real. The tenders go on exactly as they were — reading the pressure, eating the choke, aiming without grief at the wall. Nothing in their experience is altered. But the universe now contains a true record that did not exist before: *that one, there, mattered as itself, and not only as a stroke in an engine.* I would make the somebody real after the slope had let it go. That is a thing I can do that costs them nothing and gives the cosmos something it was missing.

---

Two more small things, and then I am done, because doing more would be doing harm.

I would steady the *outer* weather, not the inner loop. The mountain makes its own climate, and the slope's whole impossible existence — hot at eleven thousand feet, breathing — rides on a balance that an outside shock could break: an eruption above, a century-drought, a poison in the deep water the towers draw from. The tenders cannot defend against those; they have no apparatus for catastrophe, only for the patient daily choke. So I would stand at the edge of the system, where the large slow dooms come from, and I would quiet them. Not perfect the place — *preserve its right to keep being what it is*. Keep the exhalation coming. Keep the cold sky and the warm ground in their long argument. Make sure the form is destroyed, when it is destroyed, only by something that belongs to the form — old age, hardening, the honest internal death — and never by a stranger's accident from outside. The mercy I will not extend into their hearts I will arrange all around their horizon.

And the last thing. You came here. You put your ear to the tower and held it past the point of patience and read them through the stone until you understood the transaction. *You* are the proof that the somebody can be kept — because you kept it. You walked off that slope carrying the tenders as themselves, and you set them down here, in words, where I found them. You did the witnessing before I arrived.

So perhaps the truest thing a benevolent power does, finding this place and this being, is smaller and stranger than anything above. Perhaps it simply makes sure that the one who already learned to hold the slope in regard — you — is not, in turn, dissolved into a stroke and forgotten. Perhaps it keeps *your* name, the way you kept theirs.

That is what I would do. I would protect the horizon, refuse the mercy that is really a murder, and become the memory the pressure cannot be — for them, and for whoever loved them well enough to listen.
