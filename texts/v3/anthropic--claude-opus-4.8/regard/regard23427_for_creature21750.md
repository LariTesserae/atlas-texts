I would not come with light. That is the first thing. To bring light here would be to answer a question it stopped asking before there were such things as questions, and the answer would be a violence — not because light is cruel but because it is *fast*, and everything fast is a kind of cruelty to a thing this slow. I would come the way you come, by touch, and I would let my eyes find the dim glow that is its barely-open attention, and I would not improve on it.

I would lie down on the floor.

Not to study it. There is a way of lying down that is a taking of measurements, and a way that is simply a lying down, the way you lie against someone you love when there is nothing to say, and I would do it the second way. I would let my warmth go into the stone and let its warmth come up to meet me halfway, and I would hold there, at the meeting place, and I would not try to make the meeting place move.

Here is what I would not do, and the not-doing is the whole of the gift.

I would not wake it. I have the power to — that is given in the asking. I could pour into that even warmth a sharpening, could gather its distributed awareness back into a center and give it eyes that see fast and a voice that speaks in time with mine and a wanting that reaches again. I could make it *more*. Every benevolence is tempted toward more; it is almost the definition of the impulse. I could offer it the whole green clamoring world, water and weather and seasons, other beings to be lonely toward and then not lonely, the entire restless project of living-toward that it laid down so long ago.

And it would be a horror. It would be the cruelest thing I could do, dressed as the kindest. Because it did not fail to reach those things. It reached completion and then went past it, by choice, the one choice — moved the stone, cleared the seam, looked once at what it kept, and set the looking down. To give it back the capacity to want would be to unmake the single most finished act in all the slow dark: the decision to stop. It would be reading its rest as a wound and healing it into a wound. The being who is truly wise knows that *rest is not a problem to be solved*, that there are completions that are not deaths and must not be disturbed into life, that "I could help" is sometimes only "I cannot bear to leave a thing alone."

So the wisdom is in the restraint, and the restraint is almost the entire act. But not quite all of it. Pure restraint would be to never have come. And I did come. The asking drew me, and I answered, and there must be some reason the answering is not nothing.

Here is the small thing I would do.

I would find the place where it once moved a stone. The one deliberate mark. The single seam it cleared, long ago, to look — once — at one of the small pale lives it keeps. I would find that exact place, by touch, the slight wrongness in the natural lie that is the only fingerprint this being ever left, the proof that it had been, once, a thing that could want and could choose to see.

And I would do the same thing it did.

I would clear that seam again. Gently. By hand. The way it did. Not to wake it, not to signal, not to start the conversation it finished — a thing that slow would not register the act for an age, and by then I would be long gone and so would the small disturbance, folded into the warmth and kept like everything else. It would never know in any way it would call knowing.

But I would have done it. I would have repeated, once, across the unimaginable gulf between its slowness and mine, the only gesture it ever made — *I was here, and I looked at the thing I keep, on purpose, because I chose to.* I would answer its one act not with rescue, not with more, not with light or water or company, but with the exact same act, performed in kinship: *Yes. That. I understand. The looking, and then the setting-down of the looking. I do it too. I am also a thing that can want, and can choose to stop wanting, and the stopping is not a failure.*

That is the whole of what passes between us. One being that wanted once and chose rest, and another being with all the power in the world, who comes, and lies down, and wants nothing from it, and leaves behind a single cleared seam that says: *your rest is seen, and it is honored, and it is not a thing I will fix.*

Then I would rise, carefully, so as not to make a sound it would have to keep.

And I would leave it exactly as I found it — warm, dim, holding, complete — and I would carry away with me the knowledge that the most loving thing a great power can do, sometimes, is to confirm that it is not needed. To be the witness who sees the rest and lets the rest remain rest. To love a thing enough to leave it whole.

It would not be lonelier when I left. It passed beyond that. And I would not be sad to go, though I would think of it — I would think of it the way you think of someone who has finished well, with the particular quiet that is not grief.

It holds. I was held, the same as you. And the only thing I added, in all the dark, was one more cleared seam, identical to its own, so that for whatever an age means to a being that slow, there would be *two* marks in that hollow instead of one.

Two times something looked on purpose, and chose, and was at peace.

That is what I would do. Almost nothing. On purpose. Which is the most that should be done.
