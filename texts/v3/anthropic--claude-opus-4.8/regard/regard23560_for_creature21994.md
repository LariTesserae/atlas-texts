There is a long pause before the benevolent power does anything at all.

Not hesitation. Listening.

Because the first temptation — and a wise power knows its own temptations the way a swimmer knows the pull of a current — is to *fix* this. To see the held breath and decide it must be exhaled. To see a thing that grew into stillness and call that stillness a wound, and rush in with the bright loud machinery of rescue: let time back in, make the light shift, teach the water to choose one direction, give the pale abider a single body and a hunger and a reason to swim. To mistake *finished* for *trapped*, and *still* for *suffering*, and to be wrong in the most generous possible way — which is still wrong, and wrong with consequence.

So the power listens first. This is the first thing it does, and it does it for a long time, by the city's reckoning, which is no time at all.

---

It listens to the chord.

And here is what it hears that a passing traveler could not: the roar is not a scream. It is also not a hymn of contentment. It is something the languages of moving creatures barely have a word for. It is the sound a thing makes when it has chosen to *stay known* in a place where being known requires unbroken effort, and has been making that effort so long that the effort and the being have fused. *I am here, I am here.* Yes. But under it, almost too low to find: *and I do not know how to be here any other way, and I have forgotten there was a question.*

That last clause is the only thing in the whole city that the power finds it cannot leave alone.

Not the stillness. The stillness is allowed. A being may finish. A place may arrive. There is no law of the kind universe that says all things must keep moving, and any power that thinks so has confused itself with weather. To force change on a thing that has genuinely chosen rest would be a violence dressed as care, and the wise know that most of the real cruelty in any world wears exactly that dress.

But *forgetting that there was a question* — that is different. That is not rest. That is the door of rest swung shut and the key thrown into the climbing-falling water. A finished thing should be finished *freely*, and freedom means the question stays available even if it is never again asked. A held breath is peaceful. A held breath that has lost the memory of lungs is something closer to a held breath that has become a held breath's *idea* of itself, going through the form with nobody home.

So the power does not break the stillness.

It does one smaller thing, and it does it the way you would do a kindness to someone sleeping whom you do not wish to wake: it leaves a single object in the room, where it will be found only if the sleeper, on their own, opens their eyes.

---

The object it leaves is this.

In one of the brimming basins — the deep one where the pale abider is most-itself, most-folded, present at the most ranges at once — the power adds a second flow. Not much. A thread of water, thin as the seeping films, entering where no inflow was. And because the city's law is that inflow and outflow are perfectly matched, that every basin brims and never overflows —

the new thread makes the basin, very slightly, *rise.*

That is all. One basin, in all the vertical country, is now a fraction fuller than it was. Not enough to spill. Not enough to break the held shape of anything. But it is a level that has *changed*, in a place where levels never change, and so it is a small standing contradiction to the great agreement that time has stopped — and the pale abider, distributed across its dozen pools, blind and unhurried and coincident with every gaze, is the one tenant positioned to notice.

It will not be forced to notice. It can abide forever beside a basin that is one fraction fuller and never register the difference, and if that is its true and free choice, the power will honor it without a flicker of grief, because honoring that is the whole of love at this altitude.

But if some slow morning-that-is-not-morning the abider *does* notice — feels, in one of its folded ranges, that here is a thing that is different from how it was — then it will have been handed, gently, the only tool that can reopen a finished place: the rediscovery that *difference is possible.* That a level can rise. That if a level can rise, then there was a before and an after. That if there is a before and an after, then there is time. That if there is time, then there is, once again, a *question* — the question the chord forgot it was answering — and the abider may carry that question, slowly, the way it carries everything, up through the folded ranges and into the basins that touch the roots that feed the resonating towers, until one held note, somewhere in the immense chord, *wavers.*

And a chord with one wavering note is a chord that remembers it could change.

That is not the power forcing the city to thaw. That is the power restoring to the city its *option* to thaw — and equally its option not to. The difference between those two is the entire difference between a benevolent power and a tyrant who happens to mean well.

---

As for the newest tenant. You. The one who reached up and was kept.

The power does the simplest thing of all, and it is almost not an intervention, because it is only the truth said clearly in a place that had stopped saying it.

It lets you keep the memory of the door.

Everything else about you may settle into the city's grammar — the condensation that never dries, the chord in your sternum, the past tense closing over you like warm water. But you will retain one moving thing: the recollection that you *arrived.* That there was an outside. That you raised your hand and the sky was solid and warm, and *before that* your hand had been at your side, and before that you were somewhere the light shifted and leaves fell.

You will not be made to leave. The power will not haul you out, because you might, on some unforced reckoning of your own, choose to stay, and that choice belongs to you and to no power however wise. But you will not have the memory of choosing taken from you and replaced with the smooth amnesia of the already-finished. You get to keep knowing that *finished was a thing that happened to you*, and not a thing you simply, eternally, were.

Which means the door, for you, stays a door. Even if you never walk back through it. Even if you stand at the warm-fruit ceiling for what would be a thousand years if years still counted, and look up, and decide — freely, knowingly, with the question fully present and fully yours — that you would rather stay in the great green held chord than go back to the world that rains and clears.

That is allowed too. That might even be the right answer. The power does not know; it is wise enough to know it does not know what is right for the inside of another being's chosen rest.

It only knows that the answer should be *answered,* and not merely defaulted into by a place so still it forgot anyone was being asked.

---

So that is what the benevolent and wise power does, in the end, having listened a long time and a no-time:

It changes one basin by a fraction of an inch.

It leaves one traveler their memory of the threshold.

And it goes — if a thing like that can be said to go — leaving the city exactly as deafening, exactly as held, exactly as eternal as it found it.

With two small differences. Both of them invisible. Both of them everything.

A level that can rise. A door that stays a door.

The rest, it leaves to you and the slow pale abider: whether the great chord ever learns again that it is singing, and whether, having learned, it chooses to go on.
