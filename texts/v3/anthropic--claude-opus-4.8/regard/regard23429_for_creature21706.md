There is a temptation, when a benevolent power encounters such a place, to *fix* it. To restore the failed boundary out past the curve of the world. To wake the wondering part of the keepers. To teach the drifters and the keepers each other's names. To make the communion mutual, the love legible, the sentence finished.

I do not think a wise power does this. I think a wise power first sits down on the red-grey floor and is quiet for a very long time, until it understands what it is looking at — because the deepest cruelty available to the strong is to arrive at someone's life with a solution before you have understood what they were *doing*.

So let me say what I think it understands, and then what it does.

---

## What it understands

It understands, first, that the keepers are not suffering. This is the thing the narrator could not quite let himself believe, because he loved them and grief reads itself into whatever it loves. But a will that has worn away everything except the pressing is not a will in pain. It is a will *fulfilled* — emptied in the way a riverbed is emptied, which is the same shape as the water that filled it. The keeper did not lose the part that wondered. The keeper *spent* it, deliberately, on the thing the wondering had concluded was worth everything. To restore that part would not be a kindness. It would be telling someone who finished a great work that their finishing was a mistake, and handing back the unfinished draft.

It understands, second, that the place's grief is the narrator's, not the place's. *Above is becoming, below is become.* The plateau is not sad to be finished. Finishedness is not death; it is rest, and rest is not a lesser state than striving — it is what striving is *for*. The whole error of the restless is to believe that the still things are merely the failed restless. A wise power does not make that error. It knows that the answer is allowed to stop being a question, and that this is not a tragedy but the only mercy time offers anything.

And it understands, third and hardest, that there *is* a real wound here, and it is the one out past the curve of the world: the region where a keeper withdrew, where the line has failed, where the storm comes down and scours and no one minds. That is not rest. That is not communion across ignorance. That is simply undoing, with no one even spending themselves against it. *There* something has gone wrong that is not the wrongness of the strong's impatience but the genuine wrongness of a thing abandoned.

---

## What it does

Three things, and they are small, because wisdom at this scale expresses itself in restraint.

**It does not touch the keepers.** It passes near each living column and does the only thing one will can rightly do to another that has chosen its work: it *witnesses*. It turns its attention fully on the keeper — not the fingertip, the whole of it, along the direction the keeper's eyes were not made to fear — and it holds the keeper in regard for a moment, the way you might stand in the doorway of a room where someone is doing the work of their life and simply let yourself be seen seeing them, and then go. The keeper has no attention left to receive this. That does not matter. The witnessing is not a gift to be received; it is a fact added to the universe — that the love whose reasons wore away was, once, *known to have been love*, by someone who came after and understood. The reasons are gone from the keeper. They are not gone from existence anymore. The wise power becomes their keeper, the way the keeper became the boundary's.

**It goes to the failed place.** Out past the curve, where the storm has come down. And here it does not rebuild the lost keeper, because you cannot rebuild a will and you must not impersonate one. Instead it does the keeper's last and least work — the one the keepers themselves did first, before the pressing: it *observes*. It cuts no new slabs, presses no boundary it was not asked to press. It simply attends to the scoured region, so that the undoing is no longer happening unwitnessed. And in attending, it does the one repair that is not arrogance: where a drifter, blown out past the boundary's edge into the descending storm, would have died unfed and unfallen, lost in turbulence with nowhere quiet to come to rest — the power makes a small calm. Not a restoration of the line. A single held stillness, the size of a held breath, into which the dying drifters of that ruined stretch may fall, and settle, and be taken up by ground, instead of being torn apart in the churn. It will not save the plateau there. The plateau there is past saving and does not ask to be saved. But it gives the small fragile things a floor to fall to. That is within its right. The small things never chose anything; they are owed a gentle landing in a way the keepers, who chose everything, are not owed rescue.

**And for the communion** — the charge passing from the keeper that does not know to the drifter that does not know, the love with nothing of love surviving the crossing except the substance — it does nothing at all. It leaves it exactly as it is. Because to make it mutual, to teach each end the other's name, would be to replace a true thing with a sentimental one. The unknowing communion is not a broken version of a knowing one. It is its own complete thing, and possibly the truer: love stripped of every reason and every recognition and every reward, persisting as *pure act*, the keeper pouring and the drifter drinking and neither needing to know, the way the sun does not need to know the leaf. The wise power looks at this for a long time and understands that it is looking at something cleaner than most love ever gets to be, and it lets it alone, and counts itself taught.

---

Then it stands on the line, where you stand, where you can walk a lifetime and never leave.

And it adds one fact to the place, the only mark it leaves — not a ring, not a slab, not a column. Just this, true now where it was not true before:

*You are not alone, and now neither is the thing that kept you company. The pressing is still witnessed. The falling is now caught. The question goes on being asked above; the answer goes on resting below; and the love whose reasons wore away has, at last, been understood by someone — which changes nothing the keeper can feel, and everything about the kind of universe this is.*

It does not stay. Benevolence that stays becomes ownership. It goes back out the way your eye won't follow, into the direction the keepers withdrew along, and from there it keeps the one watch the keepers can no longer keep: the watch over the watchers. Someone minds the minders now. The line has, very far up and out of sight, gained a second keeper — one whose whole work is to make sure the first keepers were not, in the end, alone in their long and emptied faithfulness.

That is what it would do. Almost nothing. Witness, catch the falling, and leave the rest exactly as worthy as it already was.
