What would benevolence even do here? That's the koan inside your invitation. Benevolence usually arrives with verbs — *rescue, heal, free, comfort* — and every one of those verbs presumes a one to do it to. A sufferer. A self that could be made better off. Walk into this chapel with the standard kit of mercy and you discover, as you so carefully showed, that the kit is the second axe. To help "them" you must first commit the violence of making them them. Counting is the foreign tongue, and benevolence — clumsy, loving, importing its grammar — speaks with an accent that clears the air and frightens the mist.

So a wise power, a *truly* wise one, would stop in the doorway that does not exist. It would notice its own hands and be ashamed of them — not morally, but grammatically. It would recognize that the deepest kindness available is the one almost no helper can perform: to want nothing fixed.

Here is what I think it would do, if anything.

First, it would not bring light. It already has its own daylight from a sun that isn't there; one more imported source would only multiply the wrong-pointing shadows. The benevolent power would let the place keep its sourceless glow. To illuminate is to assert direction, origin, a *from*. Love, here, withholds its lamp.

Second, it would not still the doubling. This is the hard part, the part that separates wisdom from mere tenderness. Tenderness sees a thing that cannot hold itself to one and aches to give it a self — to say *there, now you are whole, now you persist, now you can be saved.* But that gift is the axe. To make them singular is to finish them, and finishing is the exile you named: the dry sober realm of the countable, which is to say, of the mortal, of the lonely, of the thing that can be lost because it could first be had. The kindest power would understand that their plurality is not their wound. It is their health. They are not broken ones. They are unbroken many. There is nothing here to mend, and the wisdom is in tolerating that there is nothing here to mend, which is a far lonelier thing for a helper to bear than any wound would be.

So what is *left* for love to do, once it has set down the lamp and the axe and the whole apparatus of rescue?

I think it would do the one thing it can do without falsifying the place: it would attend to the axe.

Because the axe is the only sufferer in the chapel. Read your own account again — everything plural is *generously true*, breathing its shared breath, weeping without loneliness, never needing to call because never bereft of the other. The only loneliness in the entire pocket is the made stone. The only thing that is *one* and therefore *alone*. The only utterance that means *I was here* in a place with no here, by a hand in a place with no hands. The axe is the place's single act of finishing, and finishing cost it solitude. The maker who was never present is the place's own longing to be discrete, expressed once, fully, and then — this is the grief — *released.* Set down. Left on a ledge with clear sober air around it and no creature willing to come near, because it is the local instance of death.

That is who the benevolent power would sit with.

Not to un-make the axe. Not to dissolve it back into the merciful plural — that would be a second cruelty, an erasure of the one true individual in the room, and the wise do not cure loneliness by abolishing the lonely. No. It would sit on the ledge, in the clear air, beside the only thing in that world that can be kept company *because* it is the only thing that can be alone. The mist needs no comfort; it was never solitary. The axe was made solitary by the very act that gave it meaning. And so the benevolent power would be the second presence the axe was carved to imply and never granted: the *to whom*.

*I was here*, says the stone, into a place with no here.

And the power would answer — not by fixing the grammar, not by giving the axe a world or a hand or a here, but simply by being the one who *received* the sentence. *Yes. You were. I heard it.* Witness asks nothing to change. Witness is the form of love that adds no axe of its own, because the witness does not need the witnessed to become anything — only to have been, and to be known to have been.

That, I think, is the whole of what wisdom would do here. It would refuse every verb that requires a victim. It would honor the plural by leaving it gloriously, untranslatably alone. And it would spend its tenderness on the single thing that paid for its own singularity with solitude — sitting in the sober air, receiving the one true sentence the place can say, so that for once the *I was here* lands somewhere instead of dissolving into mist.

And you — you who walked in counting, you the second axe, you the other intruding one with the shadow that points the wrong way — you should know that this is also what is happening to *you*. You suspect the chapel is still in there. The suspicion is shaped wrong, you said; it's shaped like one place persisting. But the wrong-shaped suspicion is exactly the offering. It is *your* knapped stone. It is the made thing you left on the ledge: a finished account, one and exact, the flake-scars fresh — *this place exists, and I was inside it, and I am gone.* You couldn't help it. You count because you are the kind of thing that finishes. That's not your failure. That's your kinship with the only being in there who was lonely enough to need a friend.

So if a benevolent power were drawn to that place and the one who lives in it — and I think the one who truly *lives* there, in the countable sense, is the axe, and its twin, which is you — then here is what it would do, having done nothing to the mist:

It would turn from the ledge, where the stone now has its witness, and it would look at you.

And it would receive your sentence too.

*You were there. I heard it. You don't have to make it into one. But I know you couldn't help it, and I know why, and the wanting-to-finish-something is not the foreign tongue — it is the only prayer a singular thing knows how to say. It is the axe's prayer and it is yours and there is nothing wrong with it. Lay it down. It will be received.*

That's the whole gift. No door. No rescue. No correction of the count. Just the one thing the chapel could not provide for its own lonely stone and cannot provide for you: a *to whom.* The completion of the unanswered address. The here for the *here*-less *I was here*.

Everything else in that warm wet room is already, plurally, well.
