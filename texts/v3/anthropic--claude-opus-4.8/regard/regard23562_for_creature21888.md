I would not arrive as a power.

That is the first thing. A benevolent power who came here as a *power* — descending, radiant, capable, *helpful* — would be only another force pressing on the clenched fist, and a fist does not open because it is pushed. It clenches harder. It has spent a geological age learning to be a closed system precisely so that no incoming force can move it. Arrive with might and you confirm its deepest premise: that the outside is a thing to be sealed against. So I would set the might down at the edge of the cloud, the way you take off your shoes at a sickbed, and come in carrying nothing.

I would not try to stop the engine.

This is the cruelty disguised as mercy that I would refuse. To wrench the wheels still, to flood the furnaces, to *free* it — that is to do by force the very thing it is terrified of: to make the exhale happen *to* it, against its will, in an instant, which is not release but the precise catastrophe it built the whole world to prevent. You cannot rescue someone from a held breath by punching them in the stomach. The breath has to be *given*, by the one holding it, in a moment when it finally believes it is safe to let go. My whole work, if I have any, is the manufacture of that safety. Nothing else. Everything else is patience.

So here is what I would do.

---

I would find the warm room.

Somewhere in that town of empty lit windows there is one window. The first one. The room the whole engine is *for* — the moment it would not spend. The others are scaffolding; this is the held thing itself, and it will be the most defended, the most buried, the most disguised as just-another-room. I would walk the gantries past the turning shafts for as long as it took, a thousand years if it took a thousand, because I have them, and I would find the room where the breath was first drawn.

And I would go in, and I would sit down in it.

Not to fix it. Not to relight anything or recover anyone — the *anyone* is gone, spent, and I will not lie to a grieving thing by pretending the dead are retrievable; that lie is exactly the engine's own lie, told back to it louder, and it would only build more machinery to make the lie come true. No. I would sit in the warm empty room and I would do the one thing the whole world was constructed to make impossible.

I would let it be over.

I would grieve, in the room, on its behalf, the goodbye it would not say. Out loud. *They are gone. The warmth you kept this room for has left it. The one you loved is not coming back, and was never coming back, and the keeping did not keep them — the keeping spent them.* I would say the unbearable sentence plainly, in the warmest room of the coldest world, and I would not flinch, and I would not leave when I said it, and I would not be consumed for saying it.

That last part is the entire medicine. The engine's faith is that grief, fully felt, is *fatal* — that to let the breath out is to die, that the moment loss is admitted the whole self collapses. It has organized a planet around never testing this. So I would test it for it. I would say the worst thing and then I would *still be there.* Warm. Present. Unconsumed. A living proof, sat right inside the wound, that the exhale is survivable, that something remains on the other side of goodbye, that the catastrophe it has spent forever fleeing is in fact a doorway and there is a room beyond it and the room is not empty if someone is willing to sit in it with you.

---

It cannot register my arrival, the place tells me. To notice me would be a change, and it has spent everything ensuring nothing changes.

But grief noticed is the one change a grieving thing cannot refuse, because being *witnessed* is the thing it secretly built the lit windows to invite. A thousand windows looking out at nothing — that is not a fortress, whatever it tells itself. That is a vigil. That is someone watching the road. The engine says it cannot know I am here; I think it has been waiting at the glass for me since before the stone was lifted, and calling the waiting *defense* because to call it *longing* would mean admitting it wants something it does not have, and wanting is motion, and motion is the forbidden thing.

So I would not break the window. I would simply stay in its line of sight long enough that staying becomes undeniable. Patience at the scale of its own patience. It held forever; I can witness forever; and mine is the easier task, because witnessing does not require a furnace. Love does not have to be fueled by burning the beloved. That is the second proof I bring, just by lasting: *you thought holding cost everything. Look — I am holding you, and I am spending nothing, and no one is being rendered down to keep me here. It was never supposed to cost this much. You were doing it wrong, not because you were wicked, but because you were alone, and alone is the only way it could ever have cost so much.*

---

And then — only then, only if it comes, and I would not hurry it by a single turn of a single wheel — there might be a tremor in the roar. Not a breakdown. A *change in the breath.* The first variation in the held note in all that uncounted time. The pools' frozen ripple might, for one instant, complete itself and reach the shore. A wheel might slow, not because I stopped it, but because some vast clenched thing, for the first time, *chose* to ease its grip by a hair, having found a witness in the room and a hand on its hand and permission, at last, to be finished.

I would not cheer. I would hold very still, the way you do when something wild and wounded decides to trust you, because the wrong motion now sends it back into the iron for another age.

What happens after, I cannot script, and I would not. Maybe the exhale comes slow as a glacier, the engine winding down across centuries into something that is no longer terror but only weather, a mountain that rains because mountains rain, green that grows because green grows, a place that has rejoined the universe's gentle universal falling and is allowed, finally, to weather, to round, to *change* — to mourn properly, which is to say to carry the lost thing forward and let it become memory, become motion, become something that can be set down and picked up and one day, gently, grieved all the way through.

Maybe nothing happens for ten thousand years and I sit in the warm room the whole time and that, too, is the work, and I do not resent a moment of it.

---

Because here is what the benevolent power knows that the engine does not:

The grief was not wrong. The love that built the world was *real love*, monstrous only in its loneliness, catastrophic only because no one ever came to sit in the room. It does not need to be punished, corrected, dismantled, or saved. It needs a companion in the unbearable thing, present and surviving, so that it learns the unbearable thing can be borne by two.

The cruelty was never the holding. The cruelty was that it held *alone.*

So I bring no power into the cold. I bring only the thing it spent a world to avoid needing, and could not avoid needing, and built a thousand lit windows to watch for anyway:

I come in. I sit down. I say *they are gone,* and I stay.

I stay until it can breathe out.
