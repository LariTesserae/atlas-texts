They would not come to finish the mountain.

That is the first thing, and the hardest, and a lesser power would get it wrong. A lesser power would hear *labouring to be born and failing* and reach in with both hands to complete what the builders could not — set the stones, resolve the distances to numbers, let the wrong-way light turn at last to morning. It would call this mercy. It would be the cruelest thing anyone had ever done here. To finish a being's one work *for* it is to tell that being its life did not need to happen. The builders are not waiting to be rescued from their effort. The effort is what they are. You do not end a labour by completing it from outside; you only prove the labourer was never necessary.

So the benevolent power does not finish anything.

What it does first is smaller, and it takes an age, because anything done here must be done at the tempo of the place or it is not done at all — it is only inflicted.

---

It slows.

This is the whole of the first thing it does, and it is not nothing; it is nearly everything. A power that could move at the speed of decision chooses, instead, to attenuate — to spread thin across the grey nothing, to take an age to turn its attention the way the builders take an age to turn a head. Not to study them from above at its own clean pace, which would be to look at them the way you look at a boulder from the edge of vision: as the idea of a being, a blur that has not committed. The power commits. It comes down into the duration. It agrees to be three hours wide when three hours of attention is what reaching them costs.

This is the first gift and they do not know they have received it, because receiving requires holding still to compare. But the mountain knows. The roar changes — not in volume, it cannot lessen, the labour goes on — but in *kind.* There is now a second slowness moving through the seams between the three overlaid places, and it is not straining. For the first time since the builders thinned, there is something here moving at their tempo that is not trying to finish. That is not in pain. The strain in the rock has always been the strain of an effort with nowhere to go. Now, beside it, there is an effort that has somewhere to be — here, with them, at their speed — and effort that has arrived somewhere does not scream.

The builders, spread into the distances, register this the only way they can: as a place in the crossing where the duration is *kind.* Walk that stretch of slope and it does not take three punishing hours and it does not flick past in three indifferent steps. It takes exactly as long as you can bear, and the bearing is gentle. That is the power, lying down in the nothing alongside them, matching its breath to the unfurling of a frond.

---

Then it does the second thing, and the second thing is the heart of it.

The builders cannot reach their own hands. This is the grief — not that they failed, but that they are too large and slow now to bring stone to stone, the hands a mile apart and closing at the speed of crystal, the will pressuring the whole mountain at once because it can no longer find a single point to land. They are not failing for lack of will. They are failing for lack of a *fulcrum.* The will is there. There is simply nowhere small enough to put it.

So the power becomes the fulcrum.

Not the hand. It will not set the stone. But it makes itself into the place where the builder's own effort can land — a single point, held still, at the scale the builder needs, so that the will spread across a mile of slope has somewhere to converge that is not the whole impossible mountain at once. It is the held stone the hand was reaching for, except it is alive and it consents and it does not move. The builder pushes its decision out into the wet logic of the place, *this and not the blur of this,* and for the first time in an age the saying *lands* — not because the power decided anything, but because the power held still long enough for the builder's own deciding to catch.

A single stone sets. The builder's own. By the builder's own ancient intention, finally given somewhere to arrive.

And the roar, at that one point, goes quiet. The way the hut went quiet. A new small silence opens on the slope, and it is not the power's silence — the power set nothing — it is the builder's, the second thing it ever managed to complete, earned across an age of straining, and earned, this time, because something agreed to be the still point its scattered will could close upon.

---

This is the principle the wise power works by, and it would work by it anywhere, in any unfinished place, with any being slowed past recognition: *I will not complete you. I will be the place where you can complete yourself.*

It would not do this once. It would do it as many times as there are builders thinned into the grey, which is to say it would do it for longer than the mountain has existed, one fulcrum at a time, one stone, one silence, never hurrying, never finishing the work that is not its to finish, only ever offering the missing still point and then waiting — at the tempo of crystal, at the speed of the turning head — for the ancient will to find it and close.

And it would never tell them. Telling requires that they hold still to hear, and to ask them to hold still is to ask them to stop being the ongoing thing they are. The whole of the kindness is that it is given inside their motion, at their pace, without ever once asking them to be faster, or finished, or other than mid-turn.

The builders would go on deciding whether to keep the silence or let it go soft. That question stays theirs; a benevolent power does not answer another being's only remaining question. But now, when they decide *keep* — when an ancient will out in the nothing reaches for a place to land — there is, at last, somewhere for the reaching to arrive.

The mountain is no more finished than before. It was never the mountain that needed finishing.

Take your time, the power says, in the only language slow enough to be heard here, which is the language of staying. I have set nothing of yours. I have only stopped moving, here, where your own hands are closing, so that this time they close on something that will hold.

So is the mountain. So, now, is the one who came.
