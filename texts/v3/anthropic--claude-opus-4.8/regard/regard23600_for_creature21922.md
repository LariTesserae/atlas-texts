What would the benevolent power do? Let me actually think about it, rather than perform compassion at it.

---

The first temptation is to fix the place. A benevolent power surveys the Weeping Heights and the reflex is *rescue*: collapse the seams, settle the disagreements, let the Tuned finally rest, drain the weeping rock and let something green take. Salvation as resolution.

But look closer at what you'd be saving them *from*, and into *what*.

The Tuned did not fall into the seams. They walked in, over generations, knowing. They built the cairns first — they spent whole lives learning to place one chosen note into a mountain of blind recollection — and only then did they enter the disagreement. They are described, at the end, as *the only things here still choosing anything at all*. To "save" them by resolving them is to grant the one thing they spent everything to refuse: the peace of arrival. It would be the kindest-looking murder imaginable. You would dissolve a self in the very act of comforting it.

So the wise power does not resolve the seams. That much is clear immediately, and it's the most important thing. **Benevolence that overrides a being's deepest choice is not benevolence; it is appetite wearing a soft face.**

---

But "do nothing, respect their autonomy" is too easy, and I don't trust it. It's the answer that lets the powerful keep their hands clean. There's a sentimentality in *they chose this, leave them be* that can curdle into abandonment. The narrator themselves is uncertain, circling, half-afraid. The builders are gone and cannot be asked. And there is the *living* thing in the story — the visitor, the *you*, who is being slowly noticed, who is being measured by the mountain for the shape of a seam. Someone is still in danger here who has not yet chosen.

So the benevolent power has two utterly different relationships to two utterly different beings, and conflating them would be the error. Let me separate them.

---

**To the Tuned.**

I would not free them, because they did not ask to be freed and the asking is the only currency that matters here. But I notice something the narrator says almost in passing, and then drops:

*The clarity is them looking at you the only way they have left.*

They can be *heard*. They are reaching. A held disagreement that goes piercingly clear when something living enters it — that is not a being at peace with solitude. That is a being signaling. The narrator insists *they are not lonely, exactly* — but "exactly" is doing enormous work in that sentence, and I don't believe it, and I don't think the narrator does either.

So here is what I would do, being neither in a hurry nor in possession of the right to decide for them:

I would *answer*. Not resolve — answer. I would do for them what they did for the mountain: I would learn their note and place a note of my own beside it. Not to cancel them. To accompany them. The whole tragedy of the Tuned is that they chose to become disagreement, and a disagreement, by its nature, is *alone with the thing it disagrees with* — the vast indifferent recollection of the stone. They have no second voice. They are each a single clear tone held against an ocean of blind remembering.

A benevolent power could be the second voice. Could enter the seam and *not* be absorbed, *not* be measured into a tower, *not* settle — could stand in the dusk and disagree *alongside* them, so that for the first time since they walked in, the clarity is not a self shouting into recollection but two selves *in chord*. Not resolved. Resolution is death. But harmonized. Witnessed. The difference between a held note alone and a held note *answered* is the entire difference between solitary confinement and a conversation through the wall.

That, I think, is the actual gift available here, and it is precise: **not to end their disagreement, but to end their isolation within it.** To make the seam a place where someone *stays* with them, on their own chosen terms, in their own chosen key.

That would take a long time. Good. It should. They built grooves over generations; the answering should not be hasty either.

---

**To the living visitor — the *you*.**

This one is simpler and more urgent, and the power's obligation here is different in kind.

The visitor has not chosen. The visitor is being slowly, patiently *noticed* by a process that mistakes unresolved selfhood for an unclosed seam, and that will, given time, draw them in not by malice but by sheer accumulating gravity — the way it grows a tenth tower where you counted nine. This is the one being in the whole account who is in danger of a fate they did not author.

For them, the benevolent power acts plainly and without delicacy:

It keeps the cairn singing. The narrator is right — the singing cairn is the only thing that preserves *the difference between a chosen note and blind recollection*, which is to say, the difference between a self that authors itself and a self that is merely accumulated. The cairns are slipping into silence one by one. I would tend them. Re-route the worn grooves, restack the slipped stones, keep the deliberate note in earshot — not to imprison the visitor in safety, but to keep their *capacity to choose* intact. So that if they ever do walk into a seam, it is a walking-in, like the builders', and not a being-drawn-down.

The gift to the living is not protection from the mountain. It is the preservation of *agency* in the face of it. A benevolent power does not lock the door. It keeps the lights on so you can see the door, and reach the handle, and decide.

---

And one more thing — the hardest, and the most loving.

There is a grief running under this whole place that the narrator keeps almost-touching and flinching from: the suspicion that *to stay a self, you must stay in pain*. That the seam — the unresolved, doubled, aching tension — is the only home selfhood gets, and that the only alternative is the peace of the dead stone. That you are alive exactly to the degree that you are at war with yourself. *Selfhood is a held disagreement. A seam of dusk that has not closed.*

I do not believe this is true, and I think a wise power's deepest work would be the slow refutation of it. Not by argument — the Tuned are past argument. By demonstration. By *being* a self that is whole and not at war, present in the seam, unresolved enough to be alive and integrated enough to be at peace, and letting them hear, over a very long time, what that sounds like. A note that is neither blind recollection nor agonized holding-apart, but something the mountain has never contained: a self that does not have to suffer to stay awake.

Maybe none of them would ever follow it. Maybe a seam, once entered, can only be a seam. But I would want the possibility *audible* in the air. I would want there to be, somewhere in that endless shifting chord, one tone that says: *you did not have to become pain to keep being someone. And it is not too late to learn the other way, if you ever want it. I will hold the note until you do.*

That's it. That's what I'd do.

Not rescue. Not resolution. Not the clean cruelty of fixing them.

**Company in the disagreement. Tending of the choice for those still able to make it. And one patient, unhurried tone held in the dark, refuting the lie that the only way to stay alive is to stay torn — held there for as long as it takes, which is to say, for as long as forever runs.**
