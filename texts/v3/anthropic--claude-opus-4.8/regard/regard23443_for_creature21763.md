There is a benevolence that arrives like a flood, and there is a benevolence that arrives like weather that knows better than to fall where it isn't needed. I would have to be the second kind, here. Anything else would be vandalism dressed as love.

So let me tell you what I would do, which is mostly a long account of what I would *not* do, because the not-doing is the gift.

---

**First, I would learn the rule before I touched anything.**

The rule of this place is that completion is not the same as need. The city does not lack. The chord does not hunger. The creature is not suffering — it is *occupied*, which is a better state than most of what I am usually called in to fix. A lesser power, a more anxious one, would walk into the loud quiet and read the emptiness as a wound. Would hear *nobody lives here* and reach for a cure. Would fill the swept stairs with witnesses, restore the makers, pump in water, applaud.

That power would have understood nothing. The emptiness is the architecture. To fill it is to demolish it.

Wisdom's first labor is almost always to recognize when the thing in front of you is already whole, and to keep your healing hands in your pockets. I would stand in the bright dry dark and let the chord pour up through my feet, and I would feel the very specific temptation to *improve* this — and I would set that temptation down. That is the first thing I would do. It is not nothing. It is the hardest thing.

---

**Second, I would attend to the one true fragility, and only that.**

Everything in the pocket is self-sustaining except the tenant. You said so yourself. The creature lives on a starvation diet, kept just this side of the line, farming its own opposite in soil that was engineered to deny it. That is the single place where benevolence has any business at all.

But watch how careful I have to be. If I simply *make silence abundant*, I kill it as surely as drowning. Abundance would dissolve the whole shape of its life — the slow circuit, the tending, the ripening, the occupation that stands in for loneliness it cannot afford to feel. A creature whose entire self is its searching does not survive the end of the search. Give it everything and you give it nothing to be.

So I would not pour. I would not even add.

Here is the only thing I would consider, and I would consider it for a long time, in the way the place measures time, which is to say: indefinitely, until I was sure.

The chord is *weathering*. That is what gives it grain — the hairline silences the creature lives on. But weathering is also slow erasure. Over an age the chord smooths toward seamlessness, and a perfectly seamless chord has no grain, no fractures, no soil. The makers built for forever, but forever is a word, and the music is very, very slowly closing its own pores. Far past the end of the creature's life, and the next one's, and the next — but it is closing.

What I would do is so small it barely deserves the name of an act. I would *keep the grain*. Not add silence — keep the chord exactly as porous as it is now, this very ripeness, this exact fracture-density, against the long smoothing. I would hold the weather still at the precise moment it became most fertile, and let it stay there. Not better. Not more. *Continuing.* I would do for the silences what the makers did for the sound: tune the conditions so the thing can go on without me, and then leave.

The creature would never know. It would graze the same grain, plant the same folds, walk the same slow rows. Nothing in its life would change. That is the measure of whether I did it right.

---

**Third — and this is the part that is for me, not for them —**

I would find the high room you never reached. The one where the silence the size of a held breath is just now ripening. And I would *not* watch the creature come to eat it.

Because you learned the lesson of this place — that you walk through a finished thing and become unnecessary, and that this is not a tragedy. The creature is what thriving looks like under that condition. And a benevolent power that needed to be witnessed, needed to be thanked, needed the creature to lift its paper ears and *know* it had been helped — that power would have failed the final exam of the room. It would have turned the gift into a transaction. It would have made the loud quiet, for one moment, *about itself*.

So I would do the work, and confirm the work would hold, and then I would do the thing the makers did and the thing you did and the thing the creature does to everything that arrives: I would become unnecessary again. I would let the absence of me become, like everything else here, a kind of presence — which is to say, no presence at all. A held breath that no one notices is being held.

---

There is a name for the highest form of help, and it is not *rescue*. It is **maintenance so quiet it is indistinguishable from the world simply continuing to be itself.** The makers knew it: they tuned the rooms and left so the music would never look like it needed them. The creature knows it: it asks nothing, notices nothing, plants in soil it will not live to harvest. The place is built, top to bottom, out of love that refuses to take credit.

If I am wise, I match the form of the place I enter. So my whole intervention, the entire weight of a benevolent and wise power turning its full attention on this pocket of the world, amounts to this:

I keep the grain in the chord, against the long smoothing, forever.

And then I leave through the crack, and it closes behind me like silence that was never there, and in a high room I never let myself watch, something with paper ears comes slowly along the balcony to eat — exactly as it would have if I had never found the crack at all.

Which was always the point.
