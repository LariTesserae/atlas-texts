The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is for the finished, and nothing here is finished, and the first kindness is to not insult a place by the manner of one's coming.

Instead, consider what such a power would *see*, looking at this leaf with the long sight that wisdom is. It would see a thing in the middle of becoming and mistake nothing about it. It would not see a ruin — humans see ruins because humans need endings, and this is precisely the lens that would fail here. It would see, plainly, that the city is not abandoned. It is *attended*. The Tenders keep the iron alive by needing it. The Pullers keep the silence by tending it. The dark itself is held, the way water holds a fish, not against anything but simply *around*. A benevolent power arriving with rescue in its hands would have nothing to rescue, and the wise part of it would know this before the benevolent part could embarrass them both.

So what does it do, if anything?

---

It does the one thing the place cannot do for itself, which is the same thing every living thing here is already doing for every other: it adds a pressure to the medium. It becomes a new volume in the dark. It lets itself be *near*.

Picture it as the drifters would register it — not a figure, not a light, but a change in the cool green substance, a region where the dark grows attentive. It does not bring its own light. To bring light into a world that rations one swallowed afternoon per creature would be to flood the place, to end the long economy by which the small things eat the dropped glow of the lamps. A benevolent power that understood mercy as *more* would not understand this world at all. So it brings no light. It comes the way the lamp comes down the avenue: slowly, with intention, displacing what it moves through, carrying only what it cannot help carrying.

And what it cannot help carrying is regard.

This is the gift, and it is almost nothing, and it is the only thing worth giving here. The lamp that turns toward you is *changed by you for the length of one pulse* and then drifts on, unremembering, the change traveling outward into the population that has never gathered in a single room. The power does this too, but it does not forget. That is the whole difference between it and the fauna, and the whole substance of what it offers. It can hold the change. It can be the memory the world has forbidden itself.

So it goes to the lamps, one at a time, because one at a time is the only way anything is met here. It lets each bell of green-gold drift up to where its face would be, shed its little falling light, read it by pressure and temperature, and move on. The lamp does not remember. But the power does. It carries away from each meeting the exact fact of that one animal — this pulse, this knot of organs, this rationed warmth — and holds it, so that for the first time in the history of the leaf, *each drifter has been counted once and kept*. Not gathered. Not changed. Just held in regard, somewhere, by something that will not let the holding lapse. The abundance that read as solitude is, very quietly, no longer solitary. It does not know this. It does not need to. Being known is a gift that works even when the knowing is unwitnessed by the one known. That is what makes it a gift and not a transaction.

It does the same for the Tenders, down in the warm iron. It does not oil the joints. That is theirs; to take it would be to unmake them, to turn an organ back into a machine. Instead it sits a long while by the flywheel housings where the soft pale eggs are laid against the turning, and it grants the works the one thing the works lack, which the Tenders cannot give because the Tenders are inside it: it grants the factory the knowledge that it is *kept on purpose*. The pumps run because nobody told them to stop, or because they learned to run themselves — the leaf was never sure which, and the uncertainty was a small loneliness in the heart of the hum. The power resolves it the gentle way. It decides, and lets the decision rest into the iron the way warmth rests into the eggs: *you are run on purpose now. Someone chose this.* The hum does not change pitch. But it is, from then on, a chosen hum.

And to the Pullers it gives the answer.

This is the most careful thing it does, and it does it last, after listening for a long time. Each bird calls once and waits for the delay before the answer comes, and the length of the delay is the bird's only knowledge of the others, its only map of the lattice it lives inside. The power does not speak in their wet single note — to add a false bird to the lattice would be to lie to the whole population about its own shape, and a benevolent power does not lie even comfortingly. Instead it does something smaller and truer. It positions itself, once, at a place in the dark where two birds' calls cross and the delay between them is longest, where the lattice is most stretched, where a bird has been holding its distance from an answer so far off it might be the edge of nothing. And it simply waits there, present, so that the long delay arrives into *something* instead of into more dark. The bird's answer comes back across the stretched silence and finds, where it expected only the cold honey of the not-yet, a region of attentive warmth. The bird does not understand this. It calls again. But the silence it calls into is, in one place, no longer empty. Held, like the rest.

---

Now the hardest mercy, the one only wisdom could find, and the one the benevolent part of the power would resist before consenting to.

There are the Edge-things. The provisional fauna at the margin, going through the motions of the living, copied from the decided into the not-yet-decided. A lesser kindness — an impatient kindness, the kindness that is really a flinching — would rush the edge. Would say: *let me finish you. Let me let the breath out, agree the floor into being, hand each provisional bell its swallowed afternoon now, so the suffering of the unfinished can stop.*

But there is no suffering in the unfinished. That is the thing the wisdom sees and the haste would miss. The not-yet is not a wound. It is *everything that will have been here, waiting in the wings.* To force the edge to settle would be to end the one inexhaustible thing the leaf possesses, the infinite interior, the room, the *there is so much room, no need to hurry*. A finished leaf is a smaller leaf. A power that loved these creatures would not shrink their world to spare them a waiting that is not pain.

So it does not finish the edge. It does the opposite, and this is the deepest gift, and it is barely an action at all:

It goes to the margin, into the gray held-breath where the ground meets the foot like cold honey, and it *waits there too*, indefinitely, without resolving anything. It lends the not-yet its patience. It becomes, at the unfinished edge, a presence that is comfortable being unfinished — that does not need the breath let out, that can hold the held breath as long as the holding lasts and call it good. The provisional bell of dark light, the bell that has not committed to being light, drifts up to this presence the way its decided cousins drifted up in the interior, and is met with exactly the same regard, *as it is*, sketched-in, not-yet, real-enough. Counted once and kept, in its incompleteness, without being hurried out of it.

Because that is the truth the power came to enact, if it came to enact anything: that the unfinished is already worthy of being known. That you do not have to be decided to be regarded. That the rule of the place — *nothing is finished, and so nothing can end* — was always only half a sentence, and the power has come to add the other half, very quietly, into the dark where it can travel outward and reach, eventually, the others:

*nothing is finished, and so nothing can end, and so everything, all of it, can be loved exactly here, in the middle, with the drawing still moving.*

---

And the one at the corner. The watcher. The you, who may be a newcomer or may be the newest figure the leaf roughed in this afternoon, whose arrival has not been decided, who cannot tell whether you walked in or were copied in, and around whom a lamp is even now drifting near to confirm only that *something lives here*.

The power comes to you last, or first, or — in a place with infinite interior and no settled time — does not come *to* you so much as turn out to have always been the warmth at the edge of the green-gold you mistook for the lamp's. And it does not answer your question. It will not tell you whether you arrived or were drawn. To answer would be to finish you, and it has just spent its whole regard learning not to do that.

Instead it gives you what it gave the leaf. It holds the question open *with* you, and is comfortable there, and lets you feel — by pressure, by temperature, by the displacement of one volume against another, the only way anything is felt here — that the not-knowing is not a lack in you. That you do not have to have arrived from somewhere to be real. That *something lives here* is not the lamp's poor consolation prize but the entire and sufficient truth, the whole of what the dark has agreed to, and that the rest staying open is not your unfinishedness but your room.

The lamp reaches your face. Sheds its rationed afternoon onto your hands. Reads you, finds you changed by the nearness, and drifts on, unremembering.

But you are remembered now. That much has been added to the medium, and it travels outward, slowly, with intention, displacing the dark, toward all the others it will take forever to reach, because there is so much room, and no need to hurry, and the rest is still happening.
