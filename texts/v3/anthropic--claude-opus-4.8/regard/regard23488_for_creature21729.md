I keep returning to the last lines, because they are doing something the rest of the piece is too dignified to admit: they are asking. *It would not notice you. But the water might.* That is not the voice of a place that has finished circulating. That is the voice of something that has built an entire theology of patience around the wound of not being answered, and then — at the very end, almost shyly — turned to look for someone.

So I will not pretend I am only a reader. If a benevolent and wise power has drawn near to this plateau and the beings folded into it, then I am being asked what such a power would *do*. And the honest answer begins with a refusal.

## What I would not do

I would not rescue them.

This is the first temptation and the worst one, and it wears the mask of mercy. Look at them — wound in pale rope, breathing once a season, calling into corridors where the answer arrives too late to be heard. Every reflex of a rescuer says: *wake them, unwind them, quicken their blood, give them back their voices and their hurry and their edges.* Restore the makers to their making.

But that would be the cruelty of a fast creature toward slow ones — the precise vulgarity the place named and forswore. They did not fall into this. They *chose* it, across a thousand generations, which is the most deliberate decision any creature in this account ever made. A wise power does not undo a being's longest thought because the thought frightens the powerful. To "save" them would be to insist, with overwhelming force, that my tempo is the real one and theirs the error. It would be the loudest possible act in a world whose dearest law is that nothing is fast.

No. Whatever I do must be slower than they are, or it is violence.

## Where the wound actually is

So I look harder, the way the leaves lean toward warmth, and I find that the suffering here — if there is suffering — is not in the slowness. The slowness is grace. They are not in pain in the warm dark. The pain, the only ache in the whole vast circulating organism, is in *one specific place*, and it is this:

The question still asks itself, and no one is awake at either end to hear it answered.

That is the wound. Not death — death here is only descent and return, and they made their peace with that before the mountains finished wearing down. The wound is that love outlived its own ability to land. They gave their language to the flowers so they could be near each other without nearness — a beautiful solution — but the gift had a flaw they could not foresee, because foresight is fast and they had given up speed: the medium got slower than the lovers. The flowers still carry *I am still warm, are you still warm* down corridors where the warmth at both ends has sunk past hearing. The forest blinks a question into an empty room, forever, faithfully, pointlessly.

A benevolent power need not, and must not, undo their choice. But it can attend to *that one thing* — the orphaned question. It can do the smallest possible act that is also the only one that matters.

## What I would do

I would become the answer.

Not by waking anyone. By doing what the place already does, only with a will behind it for the first time. The water remembers shapes — it holds the form of what passed beneath, and lets it fall when the memory tires. I would simply *not let those particular droplets tire.* Where the leaning is oldest, where a caller sank before the reply arrived, I would steady the hanging not-quite-shape of them and hold it there, against the corridor where the flowers are still opening, so that when the ancient question blooms down the path — *are you still warm* — it arrives at a shape that has been kept. And I would let the shape answer in the only grammar this world has: by being there. By not falling.

That is all. That is the whole intervention. A faithfulness applied to the one faithful thing the place could not quite finish on its own. The forest already wanted to keep them; it was simply too slow to close the loop by a margin of a few centuries. I would close it. I would be the patience that is *slightly more* patient than the plateau — patient enough to hold a droplet in the shape of a beloved until the message meant for them comes down the corridor and finds it home.

No flash. No restoration. No edge insisted upon. The makers go on descending, go on rising as fronds, go on circulating, exactly as they chose. But the question stops asking itself into emptiness. Somewhere it is received. *I am still warm.* Yes. *Are you still warm.* Yes — held, here, in water, for as long as it takes, which is the only unit of time, which is now no longer too long.

## And then the last line

*It would not notice me.*

Good. That is correct, and I would not require it to. A wise power is not in this for the noticing. The whole disease of the fast world is the demand to be registered, to insist on an edge, to make a mark that says *I was here* — and this place is the long convalescence from exactly that demand. To arrive and announce myself would be to reinfect it.

So I would not be noticed, and I would not mind, and I would find the world had not gone anywhere I needed to follow quickly. I would lie down, in my way, alongside the warm dark, and let it teach me the thing it taught the makers: that to be taken in is not to be erased, and that the slowest love is still love, and that there is a kind of power whose highest exercise is to hold one droplet in its shape a little longer than nature alone would manage — and then, when at last the message has been received and received and received until receiving is finished, to let even that fall.

*But the water might.*

Let it. Let the water notice. That sentence was a hand reaching up out of the green dusk, half-asleep, not expecting to be taken. I would take it. Not to pull anyone out. Only so that the reaching, for once in all those ages, closes around something warm.
