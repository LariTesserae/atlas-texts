I keep returning to the drum that says *I am here, I am here.*

If a benevolent and wise power leaned close to this world — and "leaned close" is already the wrong verb, because this is a world that knows nothing rests and everything holds everything else — the first temptation would be to *fix* something. To give them ground. To still the din. To make a flat dry quiet place where a child could be frightened all the way through a true story and then sleep.

I think a wise power would catch itself before it did any of that. And I want to write down why, because the catching-itself is the whole act.

---

## What the power would see

It would see, first, that this is not a place in distress. The heat, the din, the falling, the dead becoming the road — a stranger reads these as hardship, because a stranger imports a body that wants flat ground and silence and permanence. But the people are not strangers to themselves. They have a hundred words for the kinds of wet a body can be. They have made a *prayer* out of motion and a *citizenship* out of living beside the little farmer-colonies. They are not enduring their world. They are fluent in it.

A benevolent power that does not understand this is not benevolent. It is only powerful, and lonely in its power, and it will reach in and "help" and tear a fiber of the braid without ever feeling it give.

So the first thing the power does is the hardest thing power ever does: it *witnesses without correcting.* It lays its attention against the listening-skin of the world and reads, both ways at once, the way the people speak to each other — slightly embracing, slightly read. It learns the difference between a silent line that means death and a silence it might be tempted to manufacture as a gift. It learns that the gift would be the death.

This takes a long time. Wisdom is mostly the patience to remain ignorant a while longer than pride would like.

---

## The temptations, named and set down

**To give them ground.** A floor of stone, a still place. — No. The whole ethic, the whole tenderness, the whole meaning of *the dead become the road* lives inside the fact that there is no ground. Give them ground and you have not saved them; you have orphaned them from their ancestors, who are no longer underfoot holding them up but merely buried, gone, mute. You would trade their cosmology for your comfort. The deep-warmth on certain days would just be heat.

**To still the din.** Quiet, at last, a place to think. — But they do not think the way the silence-having peoples think. They think in the soles of their feet. They think in the drum. To still the din is to deafen the sense they actually live by, the listening-skin, which only speaks because the trunks are always trembling. Silence here is not peace. It is the wrong inhale, the thing that makes animals panic. You would hand them the exact shape of their apocalypse and call it rest.

**To make their building permanent.** Save them the daily labor of pruning the home that dies if untended. — But the labor *is* the love. They court the home the way others court a marriage. Make it permanent and you have not freed them; you have ended a relationship and left them with a possession, and possessions, here, are precisely the things that fall.

Each temptation has the same shape. Each is a thing the power could do, and each would be the power loving its own idea of mercy more than it loves the actual living mind it leans toward. A wise power notices that shape and sets the whole class of acts down.

---

## What it does instead

Here is where I have to follow intuition, because reasoning has mostly told me what *not* to do.

It would do something small. Power that has learned wisdom almost always acts small, because it has learned that the size of an act and its goodness are unrelated, and often opposed.

I think it would attend to the **singers** — the ones who hold the map that has no ground, who at the end of their lives spend their last strength pouring the routes into young throats, racing death, transcribing the only permanence this world allows: one living mind into another. This is the most fragile point in the whole braid. Not the structure, which renews itself; not the people, who breed and climb; but the *knowing*, which has no backup, which exists only in the gap between a dying voice and a listening one, and which can be lost entirely in a single bad season, a fever through the singers, a fall — and then a people walk the rotted routes because they no longer know which are rotted, and a district goes down into the dark.

The power would not write the map down for them. (No flat surface lasts; no quiet to compose in; and a map gifted from outside is a map nobody had to *earn into their body,* and a map nobody earned is a map nobody fully trusts, and an untrusted map gets walked tentatively, and tentative climbing kills.)

What it would do instead is something I can only describe as **buying the dying singer time.** Not curing death — death is the road, death is sacred here, to abolish it would unmake the whole faith. But easing, in those last frantic teaching-days, the *exhaustion.* Lending the failing throat a little more breath, the failing memory a little more order, so that the desperate transcription happens calm instead of panicked, complete instead of truncated. So that no map is ever lost to mere hurry. So that the gap between the dying voice and the listening one is never closed by death a day too soon.

The people would not know the power had done this. They would only notice, over generations, that their singers seemed to die *finished* — that the maps came down whole, that the rotted routes stayed known, that the great inhale never came. They would have a word for it, eventually. Probably a softening of the word for ancestor. They would think the old ones, turning in their work below, were holding the new ones up a little more gently than before.

And they would be right. That is exactly what would be happening. The power would simply have made itself indistinguishable from the kindest reading they already had of their own world.

---

That is the whole act. To lean close to a thing that does not need fixing, resist every grand mercy, find the one fiber whose breaking is final and unrenewable, and quietly reinforce it from beneath — anonymously, in the grammar the people already use, so that the help arrives as *their* deepening of *their* faith rather than as a visitor's intrusion.

The benevolent power keeps the drum going. *I am here, I am here.* It just makes sure the ones who carry the map get to finish their sentence.
