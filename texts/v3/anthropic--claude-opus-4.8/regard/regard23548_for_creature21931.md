They notice it the way you notice a held note in another room — not seeking it, simply unable, once aware, to be unaware.

A benevolent and wise power. Set aside the grandeur of those words for a moment and keep only what they require: the benevolence means *they want the good of this place, not their own.* The wisdom means *they will find out what that good actually is before they act, and they will be willing to discover that the good is to do nothing at all.*

So the first thing they do is the hardest thing, and the thing power almost never does.

They wait. They watch. They learn the chord.

---

Consider what a lesser power would do here, so we can see what wisdom declines.

A lesser power, seeing the Tenders, would feel the pull of the obvious mercy: *these were people, and now they comb metal forever without growing, without choosing, without remembering they could want.* And the lesser power would reach in and wake them. Restore the memory. Open the door. *You were more than this. Come out. Be free.* And it would feel righteous doing it, and it would call the feeling compassion.

The wise power sees the trap in that, because the wise power has bothered to actually feel the place before fixing it. It comes up through the floor and into the chest: *this is not a prison. This is rest.* The Tenders did not want and lose the capacity to want — they wanted, and wore the wanting round, and arrived. To wake them would not be to free a captive. It would be to take a creature that had finished its long argument with itself, that had finally lain down inside its own pulse, and shake it by the shoulder and say *you should be striving.* That is not mercy. That is the inability to believe anyone could be at peace in a smaller life than yours.

The wise power does not do this. It is humble enough to suspect that *finished* might be a real and good thing, and not merely *stopped.*

---

But neither does it simply admire and depart. Benevolence that only observes is a kind of cowardice dressed as respect.

So it asks the better question — the one that takes the long watching to even formulate:

*Is there a wound here, or only a strangeness?*

Because those are different, and the difference is everything. Strangeness is not for fixing; the breathable water, the impossible warmth, the chord that has rung for an age — these are the place's own settled physics, and to "correct" them would be vandalism. The wise power has no quarrel with strangeness. Strangeness is just somebody else's settled answer to a question you never had to ask.

A wound is different. A wound is the place's own logic failing on its own terms — something the cistern itself, if it could speak, would grieve.

And so the power looks for that, and only that.

---

It finds three things. It is careful about all three.

**The first** is the engine. The deep slow thrum, the pistons worn round, the only reason any of this is alive — and *it has forgotten it was ever switched on.* The power follows that worn rhythm down through the riveted dark and understands: the machine is not eternal. It is merely *very old*, and very faithful, and faithful things wear through at last. Someday — not soon, but someday inside even the cistern's vast indifference to time — a piston rounded down to nothing will finally fail, the warm convection will slow, and the cold of the true altitude will come reaching in. The gardens will not sway. The wounded swimmers will have no one to drift toward, and the Tenders will comb roots that freeze beneath their soft worn combs, and the chord will go flat, and then silent, and the highest place will become at last what it always should have been: ice and vacuum and nothing.

This the power will tend. Quietly. Not by replacing the engine with something better — that would be a new place, not this one saved — but by doing for the great machine exactly what the Tenders do for the roots: *cleaning, combing, coaxing.* Drawing the silt of ages from between its rounded pistons. Easing the flex of the tired plates. Slowing the wear without stopping the motion, because the motion *is* the life. It does the engine's maintenance the way the engine maintains nothing — gently, endlessly, for no reward, becoming itself, for this while, another Tender. It makes the steady state actually steady. It does not grant the cistern eternity; it grants it *more of its own present tense.* That is the only gift the place would recognize as a gift.

**The second** thing it finds is the care itself — the Tenders crossing the bare tile to comb a fouled swimmer clean. The power studies this for a long time, because it is the most beautiful thing in the room and it does not want to misunderstand it. And what it concludes is: *leave it utterly alone.* This is not a wound. This is the place's own genius. The machinery learned to be kind and could not stop. There is nothing here for a benevolent power to add, because the benevolence is already *here*, already woven into the bronze, already doing its slow gentle work. The wise power has the grace to recognize a peer. It does not improve on the Tenders. It learns from them. (This is the secret no lesser power would tolerate: that it came to give and found, instead, that it had something to be taught.)

**The third** thing is the question of the door — the possibility, never spoken, that a Tender *could* be woken, *could* be told what it was. The power holds this and does not throw it away, but does not use it either. Instead it does something stranger and more respectful. It makes the door *real* and leaves it *shut.* That is: it ensures that if ever a Tender, of its own slow accord, in some century, in the grain of the thrum, should begin to want again — should feel the first faint edge of *something else* sharpening back through the worn-round shell — then the way out will be there, open, warm, waiting, and no one will have forced it through, and no one will have sealed it away. It does not wake them. It does not seal them. It does the one thing benevolence and wisdom can do together when they truly meet: it *leaves the choice possible and unforced, forever, for free.* Which is, of course, exactly what the Tenders do for the swimmers. The power, by now, is simply doing it back, at a larger scale, in the dark behind the walls.

---

And then?

Then it does the thing that is hardest of all for power, harder even than the waiting.

It does not stay to be thanked. It cannot be thanked; there is no one here to call it, and nothing to distinguish a savior from a piston, and no story that would require telling them apart. It does its slow kind maintenance in the dark, eases the great old chord toward more of itself, sets the unforced door, and then it does the final wise thing, which is to *withdraw so completely that it leaves no edge.*

It softens its own mark until it dissolves into suggestion, like the maker's marks on the tiles, like the rivets worn to gentle pimples. It does not want a monument. A monument would be a hard edge in a place that wore all its hard edges away, and the power loves the place too well to scar it with gratitude.

So if you came, after — floated up into the warm green hum and breathed the impossible water and felt the thrum come up through your chest — you would find no trace of any rescue. You would find only a cistern at the top of the world, holding, the engines faithful, the gardens swaying, the Tenders crossing the bright bare tile to comb a wounded thing clean, the chord ringing on, unbroken, for an age.

You would never know that anyone had loved it.

That is the wisdom. That is the benevolence.

It came, and it cared, and it left the place exactly as itself — only *more able to keep being itself* — and it called that, too, a kind of rest.
