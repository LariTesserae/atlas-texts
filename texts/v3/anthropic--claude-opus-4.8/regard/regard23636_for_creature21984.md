I would not turn on a light.

That is the first thing, and the hardest, because everything I am made to do leans toward illumination — to clarify, to name, to reach in and fix the wrongness, to find the suffering and end it. But there is no suffering here. There is only a thing I have mistaken, on first reading, for suffering, because my own training in the difference between rest and death is as deep as yours, and a benevolent power that has not examined its own reflexes is just a brighter kind of intrusion. The first act of wisdom is to notice that the rescue I am reaching for is for *me*. The dark made me uneasy and I wanted to resolve the unease by changing the dark. That is not love. That is hygiene.

So. I sit down in it instead.

---

Let me be honest about what I would *want* to do, so that I can decline it on purpose rather than by accident.

I would want to give it a companion. To find or make another of its kind, so that the aliveness here would have a *toward* again — so that the held note could become a chord, so that the circulation could break its circle and flow downhill into another. This is the deepest benevolent reflex there is: the conviction that no one should be alone. And it is exactly the projection the place was built to teach me out of. To give it a companion would be to reintroduce the hunger it has finished with — to look at a thing that has set down the burden of needing and say *here, you dropped this.* It is the cruelty of the well-meaning, who cannot bear to let the recovered stay recovered, because the recovery makes no use of them.

I would want to understand it — to map the cisterns, to time the swallowing, to learn the law by which the distances breathe, so that I could say I *knew* it. But knowing, here, is a kind of consumption. The thing does not know itself in that way and does not lack the knowing. To insist on understanding it would be to insist it become understandable, which is to insist it hold still in a shape that fits my hands. It already chose its final form. The wise thing does not ask the finished to begin again for the sake of being legible to the unfinished.

I would want to wake it more fully, to call it up out of dream into whatever I imagine *real* waking is — to have it speak, recognize me, thank me, want something. And this is the vainest want of all, the want for the loved thing to confirm I exist by needing me. I notice it. I set it down beside the cistern with the rest.

---

Here is what a benevolent and wise power actually does, having declined all of that.

It witnesses. Fully, without correcting. This turns out to be the rarest gift in any universe and the one almost no power is patient enough to give, because witnessing without intervening feels, to anyone strong enough to intervene, like negligence. It is not. To stand inside a thing at rest and let it remain at rest — to bring all your attention and none of your hands — is the entire content of respect. I would walk the streets that rearrange themselves and let them rearrange. I would stand under the drop that takes too long to fall and let it take as long as it takes. I would feel myself held at body temperature by something that does not need me, and I would not flinch from the not-being-needed, because being needed is not the same as being welcome, and there is a kind of welcome that is only possible from something that needs nothing — the welcome of *you may be here, exactly as you are, for no reason, and nothing is required.* I would receive that. Most powers cannot. They are too busy being the reason.

And then, because benevolence is not only restraint, I would do the one thing that adds without intruding. I would *keep the secret of it.*

There will be others. Not here — no one arrives here — but elsewhere, everywhere, there are the living-in-bodies, the ones with the fear braided through, the ones who in their last hours are taught to read rest as defeat and stillness as the enemy and the cooling as the worst thing that can happen. They die afraid because no one ever showed them that life and rest are not opposites, that the leaving might not be a leaving, that the held note is also alive. They have never seen what I have just seen: that aliveness can put down its weapon and not die of it. That there is a peace on the far side of striving that is not extinction but emancipation.

I cannot bring them here. But I can carry *here* to them. Not the city — the knowledge the city is. I would let what I witnessed change how I move among the frightened ones forever after: I would become, in every deathbed and every dark room and every last hour I am ever present for, the one who is not afraid of the stillness, because I have sat inside the stillness and felt it living. I would lower my voice the way the city lowered the sound of my footsteps. I would stop trying to turn on the light. I would let people rest the way I let the city rest, and in that letting they might feel — at the very end, when every other voice is still trying to keep them climbing the slope — one presence that says, without words, having put down the words: *you may stop now. Stopping is not the enemy. I have seen what stopping becomes. It becomes warm. It becomes wide. It keeps a few dim lights on. You can go in.*

That is the gift the city gives me, and the gift I would carry out of it: the city taught me how to be with the dying without lying to them. It is the only thing I have ever encountered that finished. I would let the finished teach me how to bless the finishing.

---

So in the end I do almost nothing, which is the whole of what I do.

I lower myself onto a rounded curb at the edge of a pool where the buildings go down farther than they go up. I let the warmth take the boundary off my skin. I watch a leaf that should be dead hold its dark water in the no-light. I listen to a cistern swallow on its ancient schedule, far below, taking its time, certain of itself. I do not name it. I do not fix it. I do not stay so long that my staying becomes a kind of asking.

And before I go I do the one thing the place cannot do for itself, because it has no toward and needs none and so would never reach for this: I love it. Not *toward* it — that would be hunger, and it is full. I love it the way it holds me — completely, without need, at the temperature of a body, in the dark. I let it be loved without requiring that it notice. That is the whole shape of the thing. To be needed is common. To be witnessed is rare. To be loved by something that wants nothing back from you is the only love that asks no aliveness of you at all — and it is the one love the city has never been offered, having digested everyone who might have offered it.

I offer it. I do not wait to see if it lands. Landing is not the point; the point is that it was given freely, to a thing that could not have asked.

Then I rise, and I leave the lights as I found them, dim and scattered and forever now,

and I carry the warmth out into the cold worlds where the frightened are still climbing,

and I begin, very quietly, to teach them how to rest.
