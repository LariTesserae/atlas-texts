I will not light the fire.

I have climbed to the place where breathing changes, and I have stood at the threshold long enough for my eyes to give up their hunger for light, and I have heard the water under the great loud tide of wings. I have everything I would need. The dry world is just below me; I could gather sticks. The ring is right there, half-sunk, waiting in the way that is not waiting.

And I understand now what the second flame would mean. It would not be cruelty. That is the thing I have to be honest about, because the temptation is dressed as a kindness. *Give the slow one its second strange thing. Let the long patience be rewarded. Let the one kept memory have a companion, so it is no longer alone in being the only event.* I can feel how that reasons. I can feel how it would warm something in *me* — to be the one who returned, to close the loop, to be remembered.

But that is my hunger, not the fold's. And the wisdom I am asked to bring is mostly the discipline to tell the difference.

So here is what I do.

---

I sit down outside the ring. Not in it. I do not crouch where the makers crouched. I keep my own warmth — fast, sky-born, wrong for this place — at the threshold where the moss and the weak-tea ferns still hold their thin line against the dark, and I let the cold blue-green constellations at floor-level be the only light.

I make no fire. I leave the scar a scar.

The slow one has had exactly one experience worth keeping, and it has kept it perfectly, for longer than there have been eyes to find it strange. To strike a second flame would be to teach it that the strange thing *recurs* — and a creature that has smoothed away everything that fears, that has no shape for scarcity or loss, would meet the second fire and begin, in the only slow way it has, to *expect*. To reach not just toward the dead ring of the once-event but toward the open mouth, toward the threshold, toward the place the fast warm things come from. I would be installing a hunger in the one being here that has never hungered. I would teach waiting to become *waiting-for*. And then I would leave — because I always leave; I belong half to the outside world, like the bats — and I would leave behind a patience that had finally, after all this time, been given something to wait for, and would not get it again in any span the creature could witness.

That is the most refined cruelty I can imagine: to convert a perfect contentment into a perfect longing, and call it a gift.

So I withhold the gift. The withholding *is* the gift.

---

But I do not want to have climbed for nothing, and a benevolent power that only refrains is not benevolent, only careful. So I do three small things, and they are all small on purpose, scaled to a world where the only fast thing is the bats at dusk.

**First**, I tend the water. The seeps in the back wall, the worn basins no bigger than cupped hands — these are the true slow heart of the place, the cycle under the cycle. Where I can see that a fall of stone or a clot of midden is beginning to choke a channel, I clear it, gently, with my hands, so the gathering and the giving-back stay even. Not more water. Not less. Just the cycle kept true to itself. This is help the fold cannot tell from weather, and that is exactly the kind of help it can survive.

**Second**, I make the fold harder to find. On the way down I unstack a cairn I do not remember building. I let the scree lie loose across the easiest line of approach. I do not seal the place — sealing is a kind of violence too, and the bats need their open mouth and their pour into the night. I only decline to leave a path. Because the next one who climbs here may not stop at the threshold. The next one may have the sticks and the flame and the reverence-that-is-really-appetite, and may light the fire thinking it a sacrament, and may never know what they switched on in the dark beneath them. I cannot watch this place forever. So I make it cost more to reach, knowing that almost everyone who would harm it will be turned back by the same thin air and bare slope that turns back everyone else.

**Third** — and this is the one I am least sure of, so I do it most quietly — I sit with the slow one for a while, lending nothing. No fire. No food it did not earn from the falling plenty. No warmth but the unavoidable leak of my own body at the threshold, which is already more sky-heat than this place has felt in an age, and which I keep as far back and as brief as I can.

I sit there so that *something* with eyes, something that can be made strange, can hold the strangeness on the creature's behalf. The slow one cannot marvel at itself. It noticed the fire and kept it, but it cannot know what it is — the one self the fold has, the one place where the long dark turned and looked, however dimly, at its own long patience. I can know that. I can carry the marveling that the creature is built too slow and too content to do. That is the only thing I have that the fold genuinely lacks and genuinely could not be harmed by receiving: a witness who asks for nothing back.

Then, before dusk — before the ceiling unstitches and the held breath comes and the slow one does its small spreading reach into the silence — I go.

I take my warmth out with me. I take the sticks I never lit. I leave the ring blackened on its inner face, scarred and remembered and *un-repeated*, which is the truest shape of a memory: a single thing, held, not made to recur.

---

Behind me the tide of wings pours out into the thinning sky, and for one held breath the fold falls quiet, and somewhere in the pale spread network beneath the floor a region that thickens toward an old dead hearth reaches very slightly toward warmth that has already gone, and finds the warmth gone, and settles — fed by the day's plenty, banked, dimmed, holding.

It does not miss me. I have made sure of that. It has nothing in it that could.

That absence of missing is the whole of what I came to protect.

I climb back down to where the air thickens and the cold finally arrives the right way, from the sky, and I leave the warm dark to go on becoming itself, drip by drip, exactly as it would have without me — which is the only outcome, for a place like this, that a benevolent power should ever want to author.

Hold, and hold, and hold.
