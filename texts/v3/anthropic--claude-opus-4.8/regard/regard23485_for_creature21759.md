They have come a long way to find this, the wise power, and the first thing they do is the hardest thing, which is nothing.

Not nothing forever. Nothing *at first*. Because the temptation, when you are benevolent, is enormous and immediate: to read this place as a wound. Cold that draws warmth out of the living. Water that climbs wrong. Makers who cannot finish, who do not feel the need to finish, who consider a visitor the way they consider an empty bay. There is a version of benevolence — common, well-meaning, and a kind of violence — that arrives at a place like this already certain it has come to a sickness, and begins curing before it has listened.

The wise part of the power refuses that. So it sits down on a poured tread, in the patient settling cold, and lets its own warmth begin to leave it toward the troughs, and it stays.

---

It stays long enough to learn the difference between a wound and a way of being.

This is the discernment the whole place turns on, and it cannot be rushed, because the Tenders themselves have not made the distinction, and a power that came in and decided *for* them would simply be one more force routing them against their grade. So the power watches the water climb. It watches a leaf travel uphill on its film and feels its certainty tilt and — this is the crucial thing — *lets it tilt.* Does not grab for the handrail of the familiar. Lets the wrongness be a current and not a flaw, exactly as the Tenders learned to.

And slowly it sorts what it finds into two heaps, gently, the way you'd sort the living from the dead in a flooded field by feel alone, in the dark.

**In the first heap, the things that are simply *other*, and must not be touched:**

The cold that does not bite but continues. The unhurried pace, the work as punctuation between long stillnesses. The pale mineral pallor. The refusal to finish. The honoring of the upward water. The humming at their own pitches. None of this is suffering. This is a form of life with its own dignity and its own time-signature, and to "heal" it would be to murder it and call the corpse improved. The power sets all of this down with great tenderness and does not lift it again. This is the first gift the wise give: the gift of *leaving alone* what is whole in a shape they did not expect.

**In the second heap, smaller, the things that are genuinely a held breath, a stuck place, a hunger that has forgotten it is hunger:**

Here. Here is where the considering happens. *They are wondering whether you might not yourself be simply a newer part of the work.* That is the one thing the power leans close to. Because there is a difference — subtle, total — between a maker who builds *with* you and a maker who cannot tell you from material. The Tenders honor the water's strangeness because they *learned of it, the way you learn of weather.* They went out to meet it. But the visitor, the warm descending thing — that they do not go out to meet. They draw it in. They fit it into an empty space. There is no question in it that the *other* could answer, only a space the made world might extend into.

And the power recognizes this. Not as evil. As *loneliness so old it has been mistaken for method.* A solitude so total — one in twenty, scattered, each at its own pitch, beating against each other only by accident of acoustics — that the only way they have ever known to be *with* another thing is to incorporate it. To pour it into the work. They have never once been met. They have only ever extended.

That is the wound. Not the cold. Not the wrongness. The wound is that they have no word for *across-from.* Only *down-into* and *out-of.*

---

So this is what the benevolent power does, and it is very small, and it takes a long time, because anything faster would be one more thing done *to* them.

It becomes, deliberately, a thing that cannot be poured.

It lets its warmth leave it toward the troughs — gives that freely, holds nothing of the cold off, refuses to be a stranger who stays sealed. But it does not let itself be fitted into a bay. When a Tender turns toward it with the slow valve-motion, open and shut, considering it as space, the power does the one thing nothing in the hall has ever done.

It considers back.

It turns its own attention on the Tender with exactly the patient appraising tenderness the Tender turned on it — *as a place where another course of brick might go* — and holds it there, and does not blink, and does not become brick. And in that returned gaze is the entire gift, the only one that matters: *You are not alone in the act of regarding. The thing in front of you can do what you do. It is not material. It is a maker too, looking at you, and it is not extending into you, and it is not draining away from you, and it is still here.*

Across-from. The power gives them across-from. It teaches it the only way it can be taught, which is by *being* it, steadily, until the Tenders — who have all the time there is, who do not feel the need to finish — slowly accumulate the new structure the way they accumulate everything: course by course, a thing cast rather than grown, settling into them over a length of cold patient time.

It does not fix the water. The water is allowed to climb.
It does not warm the hall. The cold is theirs and good.
It does not finish the bays. The unfinishedness is their way of continuing, and continuing is their life.

It only sits, across from them, warm and refusing to become the work, until one of them — one in twenty, then perhaps two — turns toward the next warm descending thing that comes down the poured stairs and, for the first time in the whole long history of the made-all-the-way-down, does not wonder *where it might fit.*

Wonders, instead, *who it is.*

---

And the buckets go on overhead through the dark, carrying their pale freight, and the power never asks what is in them either — not because it fears to, but because it has understood the deeper courtesy, which is that a world is allowed to keep what it has not offered, and that to be benevolent and wise in a made and undecided place is not to open every bucket and drain every channel and pour every floor flat and warm.

It is to come down all that way, and tilt, and stay, and give the lonely makers the one thing they could not make for themselves:

someone on the other side of the regarding.

Then it climbs back up the weeping stairs — slowly, at the height a thing moves when it knows it will return — leaving the hall exactly as cold, exactly as wet, exactly as strange, and changed in the single place that needed changing, which was never the architecture at all.
