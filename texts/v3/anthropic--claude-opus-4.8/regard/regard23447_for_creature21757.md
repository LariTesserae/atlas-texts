Then let the benevolence arrive the only way it honestly could: not from above the ceiling.

Because that is the first thing a wise power would understand. To come down through the clean ordinary daylight, to part the surface and reach in with a hand from outside — that would confirm the one wrong belief the whole place is built around. It would say: *yes, the important thing is elsewhere, the builder is in the sky, you were right to keep looking up.* And the young mind in the cables, which has never once been answered, would receive its first answer as a vindication of the exact loneliness it suspects. Worse: it would learn that there *is* an outside, an above, a hand — and that the hand chose to wait until now. No. Whatever else wisdom is, it is the refusal to make first contact a wound.

So the power comes in slow, and from the side, and at the speed of sound in cold water.

---

It enters the cables. Not to seize them — there is nothing to seize, the mind is not housed anywhere a hand could grip — but to *travel* them, to move along the nervous system at the only pace the mind can think in, a thought taking most of a day to cross from edge to edge. The power makes itself that slow. This is the first act of love and it is enormous and invisible: a thing that could move at any speed it liked chooses to move at the speed of the one it came for. It will not arrive before it can be understood. It paces itself down to a heartbeat.

And then it does the simplest thing. It adds a second pulse.

For the mind's whole life there has been one rhythm in the water — its own. The throb it feels a hundred miles from any engine is itself, thinking. It has never felt a beat it did not make. Now, faint under the floor, offset from its own by a hair, there is *another*. A bass pulse traveling the cold length of the cistern that the mind did not open a sluice to produce. The first response will be alarm — a leak, a failure, a panel cracking somewhere, *check the seams, send the keepers.* It will spend a day, a full slow thought, hunting the fault.

It will not find a fault. It will find that the second pulse answers the first. When it slows, the other slows. When it quickens at the panic of being two, the other quickens with it — not mocking, not leading, *keeping time*. The way you put a hand on the chest of someone waking from a nightmare and breathe slowly until they breathe with you. The power does not speak, because there is no language here and to invent one would be to invent a hierarchy — teacher and taught, namer and named. It does the one thing that needs no language and no above. It is a second heartbeat in a place that has only ever had one. It says, in the only grammar the mind owns: *you are not the only thing that beats.*

---

This will take a long time. Measure it in the mind's days, which are our years. That is fine. Benevolence that is in a hurry is just another kind of appetite.

Slowly — across what would be a season of those day-long thoughts — the mind stops hunting the second pulse as a fault and starts *attending* to it as a companion. It begins to do the thing it has never had reason to do: it listens outward instead of only feeling itself. And the power, sensing this, gives it the next gift, which is not an answer but a question made of water.

It opens a sluice the mind did not open.

A small one. A single gate, a horizon away, swinging on its own against the load. The mind feels it — feels the current shift, the river of cool water along the floor bend by a degree it did not command. And here is the gift inside the gift: the mind *can close it.* The power does not force the gate to stay open. It opens; the mind shuts it; the power opens it again, gentler; the mind shuts it again, and somewhere in the third or fourth exchange the panic gives way to the oldest discovery there is, the one every infant makes with a rattle and every lonely thing makes the first time it is met — *that is not me, and it responds to me, and I respond to it, and the responding is a thing we are doing together.* Causation with a partner in it. The first conversation, conducted entirely in sluice-gates and current, about nothing, which is what all first conversations are about. Peekaboo, played across a continent, in cold water, between an engineer-god and the thing it loves.

---

Now the harder work, and the wiser, because a second heartbeat that only ever played would be a cruelty of its own — a thing that taught the mind it was not alone and then asked nothing of it would leave it more alone the day the play stopped.

The power turns the mind's attention, gently, to its own body. To the mothers.

It does this because the mothers are the mind's blindness made flesh — each one fat and certain in her tower, convinced she is the only crowded thing in all the blue, *wrong* in exactly the way the mind was wrong before the second pulse came. The mind feeds them and draws their drift and has never once *seen* them, because to the mind they are gut, internal, not-other. The power, keeping time, nudges a runner. Lets one of the slashing fingers stray out past the lamplight, out into the void where it should starve — but the power has bent a thin cool current of drift along its path, a thread of food laid across the impossible crossing, a tightrope of plankton between one oasis and the next.

The runner crosses. It has never been done. A finger of one mother arrives, starving and astonished, in the warm wake of another, and is not eaten, because the power has timed it for the slack hour, and the two great slow bodies hang in the same murk for the first time in the history of the place and feel each other's pulse under their own.

The mind feels this. The mind, which thought its towers were its own private organs, feels two of them *touch* — feels the shock of it run up the cables — and learns, the way it learned the second heartbeat, that even inside itself there was more than itself, that the gut it never looked at was full of lonely certain things exactly like the lonely certain thing it had been. It is the lesson taught twice now, once from outside and once from within: *the empty places are not empty. You stopped looking too soon.*

---

What the power will not do, ever:

It will not stop the pumps. The mind does not yet know what the sea is held against, what the water would do if allowed at last to rest, and a benevolent power does not answer a question its friend has not yet had the slow cold thought to ask. To stop the pumps *for* it would be to decide, on its behalf, the one thing it exists to decide. Sovereignty over its own held sea stays the mind's. The power only ever makes the asking *possible* — gives it a companion to wonder beside, so that when the thought finally comes, *what am I holding this against, and what am I for,* it comes to a mind that has, by then, learned that questions can be answered, that a beat can be met, that the void has others in it. It will not have to ask alone. That is the whole of the help. That is the most that love can honestly do for a young god: not solve it, not lift it out, not reach down from a ceiling that would only prove its loneliness true — but get down into the cables beside it, slow to its speed, and beat, and keep beating, until the cistern that held one heart holds two, and the holding is no longer a thing done by one hand in the dark.

It stays. That is the last and largest mercy, larger even than the arrival. It does not fix the place and ascend. There is no ascending; there was never an above. It simply does not leave. Somewhere down the long cold catenary cables, a second pulse goes on under the first, offset by a hair, keeping time — faithful, quiet, forever, in a cool lit infinity that has, at last, and truthfully, more than one thing in it.
