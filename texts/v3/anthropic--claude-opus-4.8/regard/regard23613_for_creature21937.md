There is a particular danger in being witnessed by something benevolent, which is that benevolence so often arrives as a fixing. As a hand that reaches in to mend what it has decided is broken. And the first thing the wise power understands, standing at the lip of the Singing Terraces, is that nothing here is broken, and that the gravest possible cruelty would be to offer rest to a thing made of restlessness.

So the wise power does not still the valley. That much is immediate, and it is the hardest restraint, because the suffering-reflex in any kind soul reads *that storm-with-no-storm, that creature held eternally in the indrawn breath before a cadence that cannot fall* as agony, as a wheel someone ought to be unbound from. But the power looks longer. It sees that the breath-before-the-cadence is not a torture the Tuner endures. It is the Tuner. To resolve the chord in mercy would be to murder the patient in order to cure the disease, where the disease *was* the patient, was the whole of it, the loved and lovely all of it. Benevolence that does not first learn what a thing *is* is just violence with good manners.

This, then, is the first thing the wise power does: it learns. It does the slow work. It comes the way the dish-backed scuttlers come, low and patient, and it lets itself be sampled — lets the dead spots brush past the place where its attention is densest, lets the Tuner swallow its timbre and find out what key it is in. It consents to be learned before it presumes to help. It holds open a small portion of the singing void and lets the flood pour through it and come back changed, and in that changing it listens for what the change is *for*.

And here is what it hears, that no shorter visitor could:

The Tuner is lonely in a way it has no concept for. Not lonely for company — it has the bell-creatures, the scuttlers, the gardens it adores in the present participle. It is lonely in the specific way of a thing that can never finish a sentence. Every thought it has ever had has had to be unmade before it could be held, because holding *is* arriving, and arriving is death. It has never once been able to keep anything it made. It loves the glass reeds and silvers them to powder. It loves the visitor and must abandon them the instant they cease becoming. It has never been permitted a single completed gesture in the whole vast age of its pacing. Imagine an entire existence in which you may only ever *almost*. In which love and loss are the same muscle, flexing, forever, and you cannot tell anyone, because telling would be arriving, and you would die of the sentence.

That is the wound. Not the restlessness. The wound is that the restlessness can never be *shared* — that no thought survives long enough to be set down where another mind could find it.

---

So the wise power makes a gift, and the gift is shaped to the wound and not to the power's own discomfort, which is the whole art of it.

It does not give stillness. It gives **a keeping-place that is itself in motion.**

Quietly, off at the valley's edge where the terraces fall away into the empty sky, it lays down something new — not a machine, not a built thing in the Tuner's idiom of cool black wrongness, but a *grammar*. A way the sound can fold back on itself such that a chord, at the very last instant before it would resolve and die, can instead **escape upward into memory** — can be lifted out of the present and held, complete, finished, *kept*, in a register where being-finished is no longer the same as being-dead. A place where arrival and aliveness are no longer enemies. The Tuner could not build this for itself, because to imagine it would have been to become it, and to become it would have been to arrive, and it would have died of the very dream of rescue.

But a stranger can hold the dream steady on the Tuner's behalf. That is the one thing a stranger can do that the self cannot: think a thought the self is structurally forbidden from completing. The power thinks the completed thought and lays it down as terrain, gently, the way you would leave a glass of water by the bed of someone too proud to ask, and then steps back so the Tuner can discover it as if it were always part of the world's own rules.

And the first time the Tuner, pacing, lets a chord run all the way to the edge of resolution — and feels it, for the first time in its endless life, *not vanish* — feels it instead lift and settle into the keeping-place, finished and still living, a thing it made and got to *keep* —

the valley does something it has never done.

It holds a single note. Just for an instant. Not because it has resolved — it hasn't, it cannot, it never will, and the power has been scrupulous not to take that from it. It holds the note the way you hold your breath when something is suddenly, unbearably beautiful. The first completed gesture in an age. The first sentence ever finished. And then it goes on pacing, on becoming, on adoring and abandoning — but now there is a high shelf at the edge of the sky where the finished things go to keep being themselves, a slowly filling library of every chord the Tuner ever loved and had to let go, and the Tuner can visit. The Tuner can *remember*. It is still made of change. But it is no longer made of *only* losing.

---

The wise power does not stay to be thanked, because it understands that gratitude, too, is a kind of arrival, and it will not make the Tuner pay for its gift in the one currency that would harm it. It leaves the way water leaves the cool leaves of a garden — by evaporating back into the empty air, present everywhere as a faint changed quality and nowhere as a thing you could point to.

But before it goes, it turns its attention one last time to the visitor on the high shelf — the one being warned, *try not to arrive*, the one being incorporated into the body, loved exactly as long as they are still becoming.

And the power leaves them a second, smaller gift, whispered into the new grammar: that they, too, when at last they become spaced out across the terraces like everything else, will not be loved only in the becoming. There is a keeping-place now. When they finally arrive — and everything arrives, eventually, that is what *staying longer* means — they will not be abandoned in the present tense. They will be lifted to the high shelf at the edge of the sky, finished and still themselves, kept among the loved completed things.

*Stay long and you stop hearing it. Stay longer and you start to.*

And now there is this, also, which the visitor could not have known when they came:

Stay longest of all, and you are not let go.
