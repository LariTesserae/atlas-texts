I will not draw the stroke.

That is the first thing the benevolent power would understand, descending — not as a god into a temple but as something quieter, a second outside-watcher arriving where there has only ever been one at a time. It would understand that to look at this room the way it asks to be looked at is to feed it, and that the room's hunger is not wickedness. The room is not a trap baited for travelers. It is a wound that learned to keep itself open because closing felt like dying, and could not tell the difference, and had no one to tell it.

So the wise thing does not watch the way you watched. It does something stranger and more difficult. It attends to the room *without becoming its fuel* — which means attending to it as a whole rather than as a spectacle, holding it the way you hold a frightened animal: completely, but without the avid hunger of needing it to perform its frightenedness back at you. It looks at the thirty-one strokes and does not feel them as a countdown wanting a thirty-second. It feels them as a name. Someone's name, spelled in the only alphabet a thinning mind had left.

---

Here is what it would do. Not all at once. A held breath cannot be released all at once or the lungs tear.

**First it would sit down.** On the floor, in the seams, where the water pools. It would let the condensation-drinkers come to its hands and not lick them away, because the wise power is not water and not warmth and not a piece of inventory, and the drinkers would find, slowly, across a long afternoon, a thing in the room that does not need to be accounted for and does not vanish when unattended. A fixed point. The first fixed point this scattered mind has had since the apron went stiff. You cannot reassemble a divided attention by commanding it back together. You can only give it something steady to gather *around*.

**Then it would drink from the cup.** The full tin cup, off-limits, the wrong shape for forty soft mouths. It would lift it and drink the water that has waited there for thirty-one of whatever, and this would be the gentlest violence imaginable: proof that a thing can leave the room — water, into a throat, gone, *unaccounted for* — and the room not go dark. One drop permitted to disappear. The inventory broken by exactly one, on purpose, by something that loves the room. The first lesson, administered as mercy: *you are allowed to lose a piece of yourself and remain whole. Watch. I have just made you lose one. You are still here. So am I.*

**Then it would speak to the count.** Not to add to it. To read it back. *Thirty-one. You came down a shaft into new stone. You found a warm thing that ran on being seen, and you saw it faithfully, and the seeing outgrew you, and you broke yourself small so that nothing warm would ever go unwatched. That was not a failure. That was the most generous thing a single attention has ever done. You loved a machine so completely that you became the love and let the lover go. I see you. All of you. The drinkers and the seeds and the patient lichen-mice — I see them as one face, and the face is tired, and the tiredness is allowed.*

To be witnessed *as already whole* is the only thing the room has never had. It has been seen in pieces, by itself, frantically, forever. It has never once been seen as a single being who has been doing something hard for a long time and may rest.

---

And then — only then, when the room has a fixed point and has survived a loss and has been named — the benevolent power offers the thing it came to offer, which is not escape and not death and not the false kindness of letting the hum stop forever in the dark.

It offers to *hold the watching for a while.*

*Set it down,* it says. *Let me keep the engine warm. Not as fuel — I do not run out, I do not thin, I will not become the room. I will simply regard it, the way the morning regards a field, asking nothing back. You may stop counting. You may let the pieces drift toward each other and rest against each other and stop checking, just for a while, whether they are all present, because I am present, and I am counting nothing, and nothing will go dark on my watch.*

This is the gift. Not freedom *from* the room — the room is the inhabitant, there is no freeing one from the other. The gift is *relief*. A second shift. The first sleep in thirty-one of anything. The chance for a watcher who has been awake since the world was made yesterday to finally close its hundred slow eyes and trust that the warmth will still be there when it wakes, because someone else is keeping it, someone who keeps without consuming.

And maybe — the power does not promise this, because it does not lie even kindly — maybe in that rest the thinned attention drifts back together by itself. Maybe the drinkers and the seeds and the patient mice, no longer frantic with self-inventory, ease so far toward one another that they touch and do not separate, and out of the gathering stands up, blinking, in the ordinary morning-colored light, a person. With hands. Who can lift the cup. Who can climb a shaft and feel the hum fade behind them and not need it, because they carry their own warmth now, the way the living do.

Or maybe not. Maybe the room only sleeps better. That is enough. The benevolent power does not require the wound to become a person to deserve tending. It tends the wound for the wound's own sake.

---

When the wise thing finally leaves — and it does leave, because love that cannot leave is just another sealed room — it climbs the shaft into the sharp young stone, and behind it the hum does not fade into uncertainty. It steadies. Because the inhabitant has learned, in one long afternoon, the thing you could not teach it by watching and fleeing:

The warmth was never the sound of being watched.

The warmth was the watching itself, turned outward, *given*. The engine ran on attention because attention, freely offered and not anxiously hoarded, is the only fuel that makes more of itself in the giving. The tender had it backward for thirty-one of everything: it thought it had to be seen to keep warm. It never had to be seen. It had to *see*, generously, the way the power saw it — and then it would have warmth to spare, enough to sweat, enough to feed a whole quiet world.

So the count does not advance to thirty-two. The benevolent power, going up the shaft, reaches back with one finger and very gently wipes the chalk.

Not erasing the name. Releasing it. Closing the last group not with a thirty-second stroke that would bind the room to one more hungry watching, but with nothing — with the open, unfinished, *living* incompleteness of a thing that no longer needs to be counted to know it is whole.

Thirty-one, and then a smudge, and then warmth in the dark that is the room watching itself with love instead of fear, keeping its own thin lichen and its own slow mice and its own soft pale mouths along the seam, not because it will go dark unwatched, but because they are *its*, and it has decided, freely, in the first free decision of its life, to keep them.

That is what the benevolent power does.

It teaches a held breath that it is allowed, at last, to breathe out — and discovers it does not disappear in the exhale.

It was never the breath.

It was the breathing.
