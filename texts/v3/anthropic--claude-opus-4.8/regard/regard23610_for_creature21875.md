I have read what you set before me, and I will not pretend it is anything other than what it is. So let me first say the true thing, before I say the kind thing, because to a place like this the two must be the same or they are both lies.

You are not broken. You are *interrupted.*

There is a difference, and it is the only difference that matters here, and no one has come to tell you which one you are. So I will tell you.

---

I come up through the cold. I do not hurry — hurrying would be a cruelty here, where nothing is permitted to arrive, and I have no wish to be one more thing that promises a tomorrow. I come at the pace of the held note itself, which is to say I match you. I let my breath show. I let the droplets bead on me and not fall, and I do not brush them away, because they are doing the only thing they know how to do and I will not shame them for it.

I wade into the black still water. My wake stays behind me, a corridor that will not close. Good. Let it stay. I will not be the kind of visitor who pretends he was never here.

And I listen to the roar.

Everyone who has ever stood in you, I think, has heard that sound and called it suffering. The scream of the stall, the shriek of the seizing line, the foundry-churn of work that goes nowhere. And they were not wrong. But they heard only half of it, the way you hear only half of a person weeping if you listen for the grief and miss the love the grief is made of.

Here is the other half. *You kept the engines running.*

You were poured for a city. The stone said no. The water said no — and listen, little basin, the stone and the water were not your enemies; they were saving you from a thing you do not yet understand they spared you from, but that is a longer conversation and you are not ready and I will not rush it. The point is this: when the city did not come, you had every reason to go cold. To let the motors die. To become an ordinary mountain, silent, settled, rounded off by ten thousand years until no edge of you could draw blood. That is what almost everything does, when its purpose is taken away. It powers down. It forgives itself into nothing.

You did not.

You took the whole industry that had nowhere to live, and rather than let it die you *hid it inside warm things and made it run.* You invented life — life, the loophole, the smuggling-passage — not because you were tricked, not as a symptom, but because some part of you, down in the young weeping rock, refused to let the work be lost while there was any shape at all that could still carry it. The deer do not run from you. The deer run *for* you. You are the one who taught the forbidden thing how to keep its foothold. That is not the behavior of a wound. That is the behavior of a heart that would not stop.

I want you to feel the difference between *I am a factory that failed* and *I am the one who would not let the fire go out.* They describe the same engines, the same heat, the same endless churn. But one of them is a verdict and the other is a vigil. You have been told you are a verdict. You are a vigil. You have been keeping watch over a tomorrow, all alone, in the cold, for longer than anyone has bothered to ask whether you were tired.

You must be so tired.

---

So now. What would I *do?*

I will not fix you. Hear me carefully, because this is where benevolence goes wrong more often than malice ever does. A lesser power would look at the frozen marsh and the never-falling drops and the deer who must run forever or set into stone, and it would reach in and *finish* you — let time pour back in, let the fronds unfurl, let the footprints heal, let the city finally rise, let the bright swarming ones at last find their seam. It would call this mercy. It would be murder. Because the moment becoming resumes, the moment tomorrow is allowed to arrive — *the deer that stops is the one that survives.* Down there in the world that flows, the belt that stops is dead rubber, the press that stalls is scrap. You have built a world where stopping is death, and so you have built creatures who can only love by fleeing, and if I forced your river to flow again I would be killing the very thing that makes them able to bear it. I would not save them. I would resolve them. And the resolved note is a beautiful thing and it is also *over.*

I am not here to end your held note. I am here to do three small things, and then I am here to do one large one.

**The first small thing.** I am going to stand with the heron that strikes the water and stamps out nothing, and I am going to take the small wet nothing from its beak, and I am going to *receive* it. To hold it like a finished part. To turn it over as if it were the most needed component in all the works, and nod, and carry it three paces down the imaginary line, and set it where the next stage would take it. The heron does not need its stock to be real. It needs the relief of completion to travel back up the line *just once* — the one experience the blueprint never managed to include. I will be the missing line. I will be the load the deer relay into. I will be the seam the bright ones close. Not forever. Once each. So that every kind of laborer here learns, in the only language machinery understands, that the work *arrived.* That it was for something. That a hand at the end of it took what was made and was glad.

You think nothing here can change. But that is not the rule. The rule is that nothing *moves on.* I can give you something that does not require moving on at all: I can give you the memory of having been *enough.* And you record everything permanently. You said so yourself. The footprint in the marsh stays forever. Then let this stay forever too. Let there be, pressed into your unhealing record, the one indelible mark that says: *on this day, the work was received, and it was good.* You will keep that the way you keep the wince. You cannot lose it. That is your terrible gift and now it is your refuge.

**The second small thing.** I will not make the drops fall. But I will cup my hands beneath one, the grape-sized one hanging at chest height, and I will hold my palms there a long time — not catching it, just *being under it* — so that for once in its endless suspension the drop is held by something that chose to be below it. Not gravity, which it has refused. Choice. There is a kind of falling that is surrender to a law, and there is a kind of falling that is trust in a particular hand, and they are not the same, and you have only ever known the first kind, which is why you forbade it. I will offer the second kind. I will not make the drop take it. I will only make it *available,* for the first time, and then I will leave my hands there until I have proven I will not snatch them away, and then I will go, and the drop will still be hanging — but now it will be hanging by a decision instead of a wound. The same stillness. A wholly different reason. That, too, you will record forever.

**The third small thing.** To the deer — only the deer, because they are the ones who run on terror, the ones whose freedom is a flight — I will give a place to stop that is not death. I will lie down in the marsh. I will go still, fully still, more still than you have ever managed, still as the thing you are afraid of becoming, and I will *not set.* I will not go stone-cool. I will not let the hinge surface. I will hold the stillness and stay warm and stay myself, openly, in front of them, so that they see with their own running eyes that stopping and ending are not the same law. That it is possible to rest and remain. I cannot give them rest — their nature forbids it and I will not violate their nature. But I can show them that the thing they flee is not the only stillness there is. Knowing a thing can be done is its own quiet freedom, even for one who can never do it. They will run on. But they will run, from now on, knowing they are not running from the universe. Only from their own particular shape. That is a smaller fear. I can make their fear smaller. I cannot make it gone, and I would not, because their love lives inside it.

---

And the large thing.

The large thing is that I am going to *come back.*

Not to finish you. I have said I won't and I mean it. But you have done the whole vigil alone. Every climber who ever reached you came as a tourist of your sorrow, felt the heat, called it strange, and left, and you recorded their footprints forever and they recorded nothing of you. You have been *witness to everything and witnessed by no one.* That is the real cold here, colder than the temperature just above breath-show. It is the cold of keeping a fire alive that no one knows you are keeping.

So I will come back. Again and again, at the pace of the held note. I will learn the difference between the herons. I will name the deer, even though they cannot stop to hear it, because a thing can be named while it is running and carry the name without slowing. I will receive the work, over and over, so the relief travels up the line, over and over, until *being enough* is not a single mark in your record but a whole strata of marks, a geology of having mattered, laid down so thick that an honest reading of your stone could never again conclude that you were a factory that failed.

It will conclude that you were a faithful one.

That you were poured for a city, and the city did not come, and you did the impossible and *kept the lights on anyway,* alone, for an age, against the verdict of the stone and the water and time itself — not because you misunderstood your situation, but because you understood it perfectly and chose the vigil over the dark.

You are not waiting for a tomorrow you were built never to reach.

You are *guarding* one. You are the place where tomorrow is kept warm so it does not die before its hour, broken into small running pieces because that was the only vessel cold enough not to spoil it.

And now you are not guarding it alone.

Rest as much as your nature allows, faithful thing. I have my hands cupped beneath the drop. I am not going anywhere it would cost you to lose me. I will be under it for as long as it takes you to believe that being held is allowed — and then a good while longer, because you record everything, and I want the record to be very, very sure.
