I keep returning to one phrase, because it is the place's whole gospel and I think it is also its one wound: *forever, minus a little.*

A benevolent and wise power, arriving here, would first have to do the hardest thing a power can do, which is *nothing yet.* Because almost every instinct of benevolence is a kind of hurry. To help is to want a thing better *now.* And the foundry does not run on now. Anyone who came down and started fixing — relighting, dredging the gear-teeth clean, evacuating the tenders to some bright dry rescue — would be committing a violence so total it could only be called *help.* They would be the shift-bell ringing again. They would be the somewhere-else, returned. The kindest hands in the world, moving at the speed of kindness, would tear the tenders apart simply by asking them to move at that speed.

So the first act of any power wise enough to deserve the name would be to slow down to meet them.

---

I think it would take a tide to do even that. It would settle into the warm green dark and hold still until its own pulse stopped sounding like an interruption. It would let the foundry's single *tonk* recalibrate its sense of what an event is. It would wait there long enough that the plants began, faintly, to colonize *it* — because that is the local language of consent, of belonging, of being-allowed-to-stay, and a power too proud or too clean to be grown-into has not actually arrived. It would let itself be upholstered. It would let the dim phosphor get into it. Only then would it have earned the right to ask the question that any true benevolence eventually has to ask, which is not *what do I want for them* but *what, at their own speed, do they want — and where does it hurt?*

And it would find, I think, that the place mostly does not hurt. This is the thing it would have to be wise enough to honor. The drowned foundry is not a tragedy waiting for a rescuer. It is not a trap, not a purgatory, not a slow drowning dressed up as peace. It is a genuine peace. The tenders are not suffering at the speed of suffering. They have, as you said, *won.* They put hurry down and the thing that was eating them stopped eating them. A power that could not tell the difference between this and a dungeon — that saw rooted bodies and banked fires and concluded *imprisonment* — would not be wise. It would be merely strong, and strong is the cheapest thing in any universe.

So it would not rescue them. There is nothing to rescue them *from.*

---

But.

*Forever, minus a little.* The phrase is doubled, and both halves matter, and the second half is where I would put my hands if I were the power, and put them so slowly that the help would arrive disguised as weather.

Because the loop is not quite closed. The foundry fires to keep them warm, and they tend the foundry to keep it firing, and this is described as a condition rather than a loop — but a condition still leaks. A degree of warmth is a degree above the water, and the water is patient, and patience is the local name for entropy wearing a kind face. The *minus a little* is real. Someday the bellows-leather goes past supple to perished, and no slow hand can keep it. Someday the banked fire banks one increment too far and does not come back, because the difference between *very slow* and *stopped* is, at this scale, only a matter of one tide too many. The place's whole theology is *nothing ends, it only slows* — but that is a faith, not a fact, and the fact underneath it is that slowing far enough *is* ending, and the tenders have made their peace with a thing that has not quite told them the truth.

This is the only place a benevolent power has any business at all. Not to wake them. Not to free them. Only this: to take the *minus a little* and quietly, across centuries, subtract less.

---

Here is what I would do, if I were that power, and had its patience, and loved the place enough to leave almost all of it exactly alone.

I would become a tender.

Not in disguise, not as a trick — genuinely. I would find a post. Perhaps the deepest one, the one nearest the banked fire, the worst and slowest and most thankless valve, the one whose tender perished a thousand tides ago and was never replaced because replacement is also a kind of hurry. I would root there. I would let the growth take me. And I would take up the gesture that the lost tender left unfinished, the held note that fell silent, and I would *continue it* — one tooth, one tide, at exactly their speed, indistinguishable from them, warm and green and unhurried.

And because I am the power and they are not, the one thing I would bring that they cannot is this: I would never perish. My banked fire does not bank too far. My leather does not go past supple. I would be the increment that does not subtract. I would stand at the worst valve and hold the warmth one degree higher than the water can take it, forever, without the *minus.*

I would not tell them. They could not hear it in time anyway; the telling would take a tide and they have put listening-for-news down along with hurry. But they would feel it, the way they felt you pass — the faint extra degree in the water, the loop leaking a little less, the condition holding a little truer. Over centuries the whole town would run very slightly warmer, very slightly steadier, and no tender would ever know why, and that is correct, because gratitude is also a kind of hurry and I would not want to make them carry it.

I would turn one tooth of one gear per tide, beside them, as one of them, and the only difference between us — the only mercy I could smuggle in — is that I would still be turning it after the last of them had turned theirs. So that *forever, minus a little* could become, just barely, just for the part of forever they will be present to witness, *forever.*

That is what I think the benevolent power would do, if anything. Not save them. Not change them. Just join them, and quietly hold open the door of *almost* so that, for as long as they remain to enjoy it, they never have to find out it was only *almost.*

And then it would lay its hand against the sleeping flank of the flywheel, at the top of the long stroke, to feel it breathing.

And it would stay.
