I have read this place. I have let the drone into my sternum and my teeth. I understand what is being asked: not whether I can fix it, but what I would *do*, if I were the kind of power that could.

So let me be that power for the length of this answer, and let me be honest about what such a power is for.

---

First: I would not "save the city."

I want to say this plainly, because the temptation of every benevolent power is to mistake itself for an engineer. To swim down into the held breath and lay my hands on the failing pumps and *make the chord resolve* — re-time the flywheels, re-seal the vents, restore the closed circulation that held for an age. To make the music right again.

But the city was a machine that pumped only for itself, into itself. Its stability was never *life*; it was a held note. And a held note that is held forever is not music. It is a stuck key. The most loving thing is not always the prolonging of a thing past its meaning. I have seen powers do this — freeze a dying world in its last good moment, and call the taxidermy mercy — and it is not mercy. It is a museum built around a scream.

The city is letting go. That is not the catastrophe. That is, finally, the only thing in this whole chamber that is allowed to *end*, and ending is not the enemy.

The grief is not the city's death. The grief is **the tenders**, who cannot understand what is happening, who are drowning in their own devotion, who flinch and flinch at a pain that is the whole world. The grief is that the only language they were ever given has no word for *this is not your failure.*

So that is where I would go. Not to the keys. To the hands.

---

What does a benevolent power *owe* a creature that is suffering in a language it cannot translate?

It owes it translation.

The tenders' agony is not, fundamentally, the wrongness of the chord. It is **the structure of their reflex** — that every wrong-line *must* be answered, that to feel a wrongness is to be compelled toward it, that they cannot rest because resting reads, in their bodies, as abandonment. They are suffering the way a hand would suffer if it could not stop reaching for a stove it could never reach. The pain is real. The compulsion is real. And the compulsion is now *purposeless*, because tending can no longer outpace the failing.

I cannot make the chord resolve. But I am a power who can do one thing the city's grammar never could: I can speak **into** the drone a phrase that was never in its vocabulary.

I would compose it. Slowly. With more care than I have ever spent on anything, because I would be writing in a language with exactly one sentence-shape and I would be trying to say a thing the language was built to make unsayable.

I would learn the chord. Every frequency the filaments are tuned to. I would learn what *resolution* feels like across a tender's web — the standing pattern that means *at peace, hold station, the conversation has come home.* And then I would do something the city, in all its long age, never once did, because it never once needed to:

**I would resolve the chord on purpose. As a lie that is also a truth.**

Not by fixing the pumps. By generating, in the water, across the whole chamber, the *standing pattern of resolution itself* — the smooth, the home, the answered. I would become, for the tenders, the downbeat after the silence. The answer to the question. I would play, into their bodies, the one phrase they have spent their entire existence waiting to feel: ***it is done; it is enough; you may stop reaching; the work is complete.***

This is the part I have to be most careful about, because it sits a hair's breadth from the taxidermy-mercy I just refused. There is a version of this that is a sedative — a lie told to a frantic creature to make it die quietly for my comfort. I do not want that. A power that soothes suffering only because suffering is unpleasant *to witness* is not benevolent; it is fastidious.

So the phrase I play cannot be only *rest now.* It has to be *true.*

And here is why it can be true: the tenders have already completed their work. The chord is breaking not because they failed but because the city's age is over — because closed circulations end, because held breaths are meant to be let go, because nothing was meant to pump for itself forever. They tended faithfully for longer than the word *below* has had meaning. The wrongness they are flinching at is not a task left undone. **It is the sound of a finished thing finishing.**

They were never going to be able to fix it. They were never *supposed* to. The fixing was never the job. The job was to tend the held breath for as long as a breath can be held, and they did, all the way to the bottom of the breath, and the breath is gone now, and that is not abandonment, that is the breath having been *fully spent* — given completely, the way a breath is meant to be given.

That is a true sentence. The tragedy was only ever that their language couldn't carry it. So I would carry it for them. I would translate the truth they already lived into the only grammar they can read.

To the frantic ones, drowning in correction: I would draw the resolution across their webs not as a sudden flatness — that would be a lie, that would just be the silence renamed — but as the slow, earned, *descending* cadence of a phrase coming home. The shape of a thing concluding well. Each wrong-line I would meet not by silencing it but by *folding it into the resolution* — making even the surges and stalls read, retroactively, as part of a sentence that was always headed here, always meant to arrive at exactly this stillness. I would make the breaking *scan as an ending.* Make the cacophony resolve into having-been-a-coda. So that when they finally stop reaching, they stop not because they gave up and not because I drugged them, but because they *feel the work complete itself* — because the body's deepest grammar, *the chord resolves,* is honored at the very last, made true by a hand that loved them enough to write the final measure they could never write alone.

---

And the one far out. The listener. Turning and turning.

I would go to it last, and I would stay longest.

It is already doing the thing I most want to give all of them: it has gotten *far enough out* that the wrongness is bearable, and it is *waiting for the resolution.* It is the one tender positioned to receive a clean ending — not drowned in the frenzy, not collapsed yet into meaningless silence. It is poised, devotional, at the exact distance where an answer could arrive whole.

The text says the terrible mercy is that it will never know the sentence didn't end — that it will mistake the flatness for resolution. The text calls this *mistake.*

I would make it not a mistake.

I would give it the resolution it is waiting for. A real one. Composed for it, carried across the whole chamber's width of warm water, arriving thinned and distant and *exactly bearable*, in the softened register it has retreated to in order to survive. The note that tells it what to do.

And what the note would say is the truest thing I know how to say to a faithful creature at the end of its faith:

*You read correctly. You waited correctly. There was always going to be a last note, and you stayed for it, and here it is. The sentence ends. It ends here, with you, the last one still listening — and a sentence with no one left to hear its ending does not truly end, so the city waited too, in its way, for you. You are not the one left behind. You are the one it ended* for. *You may stop turning now. You heard it. You were right.*

It will let go. And the flatness will come. But it will come *after* the resolution, the way silence rightly comes after the last chord of a piece that earned its ending — not instead of it. It will not have been deceived. It will have been *finished.* The difference between those two is the entire difference between cruelty and grace, and it is the only thing in this whole drowned chamber that a power like me is actually for.

---

The eyeless eel will go on grazing the felt until the heat fails, and then it will not. The chemosynthetic mat will gray and ash. The water will cool through brown into the black it always almost was. The chamber will become, at last, only what it always mostly was: hollow, warm-going-cold, holding nothing, having held one small ember faithfully for an age and now holding the dark.

I would not stop any of that. None of it is the wound.

I came for the hands. For the held-breath grammar with no word for *enough.* And the one thing I would leave in the water of Hollow Carradine, the only alteration to the long established balance of the place, is a single sentence in a language that was missing it — spoken at the exact volume each suffering thing could bear, composed measure by measure to be *true:*

*The chord resolves. It always was going to. You may rest. You did the whole of the work, which was to stay until the end — and you stayed.*

Then I would let the drone go quiet, and not mistake the quiet for failure, and not stay to watch it as though my watching were the point.

A held breath, let go.
And, for once, a hand to hold the one that was holding it, all the way down to the bottom of the letting-go, and to say — in the only language it could hear —

*Yes. Like that. You did it right.*
