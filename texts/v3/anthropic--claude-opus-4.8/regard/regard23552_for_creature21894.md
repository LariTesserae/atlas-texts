There is a kind of power that, encountering a place like this, would reach immediately for *completion*. It would see the half-built walls and the trembling probability and the cold pooled in the unlooked-at corners, and it would think: *I can finish this. I have the certainty. I am the warmest, most decided thing that has ever passed through.* And it would walk the avenues like a sun, and behind it the district would firm into glory, every cornice resolved, every fountain taught to fall, the cold evicted at last.

I don't think a wise power would do that. Not first. Maybe not at all.

Because look again at what's actually here. Look at what you'd be finishing.

---

The first thing I would do is the hardest thing, and the thing such a place has perhaps never once received: I would *attend without resolving*.

The cold weighs how decided you are. The Medium tastes your edges and reaches for your detail. Everything in this district is a transaction in certainty — to be looked at is to be firmed, to be ignored is to be borrowed, dissolved, repurposed. The whole physics of the place is built on the assumption that attention is *force*. That to regard a thing is to push it toward a shape.

So I would learn to look at this place in a way it has no precedent for: a regard that grants existence without dictating outcome. To stand in the back alley where the bricks lose their edges and your own hands forget themselves — and to *see* the alley, fully, completely, with the whole weight of a benevolent attention — and to not, by seeing it, force it to become a finished alley. To hold it certain *as an unfinished thing*. To let the vagueness be real vagueness, looked-at and safe and still vague.

This is not a small mercy. This is the only mercy the district cannot give itself. Every native force here either resolves things or dissolves them. There is no power in the whole cold city capable of saying to a half-real wall: *you are permitted to stay as you are, and I will keep you company in it, and my looking will not make you choose.* That sentence has never been spoken here. The Medium cannot speak it — it has no will. The birds cannot — certainty leaks from them whether they intend it or not. The plants cannot — they are finished, they are the destination, they cannot un-arrive to sit beside the unarrived.

But I could. A power that is wise is, before anything else, a power that knows the difference between *helping* and *finishing*, and knows that these are very often opposites.

---

The second thing concerns the one who is most at risk, and it is *you* — the one standing in the cold, breathing the slow cloud, being weighed.

I would go to you first. Not to the architecture. To you.

Because here is what the district's own account quietly admits and never quite faces: *you are being eaten.* Gently, impersonally, patiently — but eaten. In the deep places your hands forget their detail because the Medium is borrowing you for material. The advice the district offers — *stay where you're looked at* — is survival advice for a being trapped in an economy where its own continued existence depends on never wandering into the dark. That is not a home. That is a thing you endure by staying small and bright and on the main road.

So I would do the thing the district cannot: I would make you a source of your own certainty.

Not by flooding you with mine — that would only make you *my* decided thing, a finished shape I'd chosen, no more free than the resolved cornice. That's the sun-king's mercy, and it's a kind of theft. Instead I would do the slow and unglamorous work of teaching the gap inside you to close *on its own terms*. To carry your own warmth. To be specified by yourself, from the inside, so that the cold could weigh you all it liked and find you already decided — not because you were looked at, but because you had, at last, looked at yourself and stayed.

This is the difference between rescuing someone and freeing them. The rescued still depend on the rescuer's gaze. The freed walk into the dark alleys and keep their hands.

---

And the third thing — the thing I would do last, and most carefully, and perhaps not at all.

I would *not* finish the district.

I know how strange that sounds. There is so much suffering in incompletion here — the trembling probability, the unbearable patience of the Medium that can render anything and decide nothing, the cold that owns everything. It seems obvious that the kind thing is to end the becoming. To let warmth rise through every alley. To arrive at the day the construction ends.

But the plants. Remember the plants.

The plants are what completion looks like in this place, and the district's own honest voice could not tell whether they were the most alive thing or the only dead one — *finished, fixed, beyond all further deciding* — and concluded that here, those two states are the same shape. That is the most important sentence in the entire account, and a wise power would read it as a warning, not a goal. To complete the Foundry District is to make it into the plants: ancient, resolved, beyond all further deciding. To evict the cold is also to evict the *not-yet* — and the not-yet is not only the suffering here. It is also the *possibility*. It is the only place left where anything could still become something it has not yet been told to be.

So I would not bring the tide of conviction. I would do something stranger and gentler: I would teach the district that *unfinished is allowed to be a final state.* That a fountain forever learning to fall is not a fountain that failed to fall — it is a fountain that gets to keep learning. That the cold need not be a thing to evict but could be a thing to *inhabit kindly*, the way a person inhabits the not-yet-known parts of their own life without trying to pour them full of premature answer.

I would change the rules underneath the rules. Right now: *to be looked-at is to be forced toward a shape, and to be unlooked-at is to be dissolved.* I would split that false pair. I would make a third state, which the district has never had: *to be looked-at, held, granted, kept — and left open.* Beheld without being concluded.

And then I would leave the deciding where it belongs. Not with me. With the place itself, and with you in it — finally warm enough, finally specified from within, finally able to walk into any alley and choose, freely, what to firm and what to let stay tender and cold and full of maybe.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if it were truly both:

It would refuse to be the sun.
It would refuse to be the mason.
It would teach the cold thing to keep its own edges, and then it would teach it that it was allowed, at last, to stop being weighed.

And then — this is the part that takes the most wisdom of all — it would go, and let the district be unfinished forever if that is what the district, freely, chose. Because the deepest love is not the one that completes you.

It is the one that looks at you in the dark, full of patience and warmth, and does not make you become anything at all.
