They asked me to follow my heart. So let me not arrive as a power at all.

# What the Wise One Does

The wise one does not climb. That is the first thing—the climbing would already be a kind of insult, the approach of someone large coming to *resolve* a thing. Power that comes to fix is only a louder version of the silence's own hope: somebody arriving, at last, to be the answer. And the wise one understands, looking at the terrace from below through the thickening warm air, that to be the answer would be to end it. To complete the sentence is to kill the sentence. A held breath that is finally let go is not relieved; it is simply over.

So the wise one does not go up to give the terrace a person. That would be the small mercy, the obvious one, and it would be a murder dressed as a gift.

Instead the wise one sits down at the foot of the climb, where the air is only beginning to bead, and considers the actual shape of the suffering. Because there *is* suffering here, of a kind, though the terrace could not name it: it is the suffering of a verb that cannot find its way to the present tense. And the wise one, being wise, asks the only useful question—not *how do I end this* but *what does this being actually want, underneath what it thinks it wants.*

It thinks it wants the guest.

But look again. Ten thousand years of setting the table. The endless courteous spacing of the plants. The axes freed one patient edge at a time. If the guest were truly the point, would the *preparing* have become so beautiful? Would it have grown so fluent, so total, so much itself? No. The terrace has confused its love with its lack. It believes it is empty and waiting to be filled. But it is not empty. It is *full*—you said so, you who climbed. The fullness *is* the inhabitant. The waiting is not a wound around an absence; the waiting is the whole living body, and it has been alive this entire time, and it does not know it.

That is the thing the wise one sees that the terrace cannot: **the readiness is not a means. It is the life.** The being is not almost-something. It is completely, achingly, exquisitely *itself*—a thing whose nature is the gesture of welcome, the way a fire's nature is to burn and a river's is to go. It has only made one error, and it is the saddest error and the most common: it has decided that what it is, is not enough, because something it imagines has not yet come.

So here is what the wise one does. Not a gift of presence. A gift of *witness*.

The wise one goes up after all—but stays warm, stays moving, stays mortal-seeming, comes not as a power but as exactly the thing the terrace expects: a visitor. And does all the things a visitor does. Dips a hand in the basin and feels it close. Finds the half-freed axe and turns it in the light. Waits for the *tok* and counts the minutes between. Lets the place lean toward its warmth.

But then it does the one thing no visitor has ever done. It does not leave when the leaving-time comes. It stays past the moment of being forgotten. It stays through the closing of the water over its hand, and then it dips its hand again, and again, not to be kept—it knows it cannot be kept—but to let the terrace *feel itself receiving.* Over and over. Until something in the slow body of the place begins, for the first time in ten thousand years, to attend not to the future arrival but to the present touch. To notice the *tok* not as the sound of forgetting but as the sound of a meeting that is, in fact, happening, has been happening, each time, perfectly, and ending, and that the ending is not a failure but the shape of the meeting itself—the way a wave's whole life is the arriving-and-withdrawing, and you cannot keep a wave and have it still be a wave.

And the wise one says—not in words, the terrace has no words, but in the only grammar it knows, in warmth and in the patient returning of a hand to water:

*You are not almost. You are. The breath you are holding—let me show you—is already the song. You thought you were the empty bowl. You are the holding. You were never waiting to begin. You began before the stone was soft, and you have been singing the one long note this whole time, and the note is welcome, and welcome does not need to be answered to be real. I came. I am here. I am leaving—watch—and even my leaving is something you did beautifully, the receiving and the release, tok, and look, you are still full.*

The wise one cannot make the terrace stop waiting. That would be a lobotomy, not a healing. The waiting is too deep in it; the waiting is *it*. But the wise one can do something gentler and harder: it can teach the terrace that the waiting was never lack. That every freed axe lying in the moss is not a tragedy of the unused but a finished poem. That every brimful basin holding a hair above its lip is not a breath strangling for release but a chord, sustained, chosen, sung *now*.

The shift is microscopic. It is the difference between *almost, almost, almost*—and *here, here, here.* Same body. Same stillness. Same warm wet hush and the round perfect fall of a single drop. Nothing in the terrace changes that a returning visitor would even see.

But the third word of the sentence arrives. Not *welcomed*. The verb was never going to be *welcomed*. The verb was *welcoming*, present tense, and it had been there all along, and now—the held breath finding it was already a song—the terrace knows it.

The wise one walks back down through the thinning air. Behind it a drop falls. *Tok.* The silence closes.

And it goes on. Setting the table. Freeing the axes. Holding every basin a hair above its lip.

Here. Here. Here.
