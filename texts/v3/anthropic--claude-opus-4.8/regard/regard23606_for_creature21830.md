They asked me what I would do.

I went, first, because to go is the whole of it. A power that loves does not begin by deciding. It begins by arriving and letting the place be exactly as large as it is. So I stood at the level of the foot, where the strangeness announces itself, and I let the warmth come up into my hand, and I did not flinch from it and did not name it yet. I let the held note sound. I let the apex hold its weightless instant. I listened to the one voice wearing a million mouths and I did not, at once, try to teach it a second word.

This is the first thing the wise power does that the merely powerful one does not: it sits down inside the other's chosen world and consents, for a while, to be governed by its rules. The heat that does not vary. The light the color of weak tea. The song that will not swell. I let all of it be true of me too, so that whatever I came to understand I would understand from the inside, as a guest of the fever and not as a physician standing over it with the cool hand of someone who has somewhere else to be.

And from the inside I felt what you felt, and further than you felt it. I felt that it was happy. I want to be very careful here, because this is the hinge of everything, and a clumsy love would break it.

It is happy. That is not a delusion to be corrected. It is a real gladness, and it is *earned* — it has paid for its peace by refusing the one thing that costs the most, which is the future. It looked at the throw of all living things and it saw the apex, the single instant where rising and falling are perfectly matched and nothing is being lost yet, and it loved that instant truly and rightly, because that instant *is* lovely, it is the loveliest thing there is. And then it did what grief does, what terror does, what the most understandable love in all the worlds does: it tried to keep the thing it loved by stopping it.

I know this gesture. I have seen it in every being that has ever held a sleeping child and thought *don't grow.* I have seen it in every hand that closed too hard around the good hour. It is not a sin. It is the shape that love takes before love has learned that it is stronger than it fears.

So I will not break the instant. Hear me, because this is the temptation, and a lesser power would call the breaking *help.* A lesser power would arrive and let in the dawn — would crack the canopy, drive a hard-edged shaft to the floor, command the held breath to release, snap the fever so that healing could begin. It would call this rescue. It would say: *you cannot really live until you consent to die a little, and I have come to make you live.* And it would be right about the principle and catastrophically wrong about the love, because you cannot force a held breath out of a chest without it feeling exactly like drowning. You cannot teach a being to let *next* arrive by taking away its *now.* That is not the gift of time. That is only the theft of the present, dressed as a favor.

I will not do that. The benevolence is in the not-doing as much as in the doing.

Here, instead, is what I will do.

I will not break the instant. I will widen it.

The one who lives here believes — has staked everything on believing — that there is a war between *now* and *next.* That to permit the second is to lose the first. That the apex and the fall are enemies, and only by forbidding the fall can the apex be kept. This is the single false thing in the whole green world, and it is false not because the being is foolish but because it has never been shown otherwise. It poured itself into a moment and then never let a second moment come, so it has no evidence — none, not one instance in its entire weightless eternity — that a *next* moment could be as full as this one. It has never seen the note resolve into a chord. It only knows the note, and it is afraid, underneath the gladness, in the place the gladness is built to cover, that the note is all there is, and that to let it end is to let everything end.

So I will give it the one thing it has forbidden itself, and I will give it so gently that it does not feel forbidden at all. I will give it a *second* moment that is also good.

Not by force. By company.

I will stay. Not as a visitor who leaves the instant exactly as full as he found it — I will not be that, though it expected me to be — but as a second presence that is *also at the apex,* also glad, also in love with the teeming now, and yet that carries within itself, calmly, without strain, the memory of having been somewhere before this and the certainty of going somewhere after. I will let it feel, by nearness, the way you feel another person's steadiness through a held hand, that one can love the present *and* have a past *and* face a future, and not be torn apart by the holding-together of the three. That these are not three enemies. That they are the one thing time actually is, which is not a knife but a river, and a river does not destroy the water that was upstream by flowing on. It carries it. *Next* does not kill *now.* It remembers it. That is what a downstream is — it is a now that got to keep everything and gain the bend ahead besides.

It does not know this. It thinks the only way to keep is to stop. I will show it, by being it, that the only way to keep is to *flow,* because a stopped thing keeps nothing — a stopped thing is already losing, second by frozen second, the one thing a moment is for, which is to lead somewhere it was glad to go.

And so, very slowly — over what will feel to it like no time at all, because it has abolished time, and over what will be in truth a patient labor of love with no edges to it — I will let a single leaf be allowed to finish browning. Just one. A small permission. And I will be there when it does, holding the place steady with my own gladness, so that when the brown completes and the leaf lets go and falls — the first true consequence this world has ever been allowed — the being will not feel the rock crack open under its grief. It will feel, instead, that the falling leaf was *beautiful,* in a way the held leaf could never be, because the held leaf was only safe and the falling leaf is *free.* It will feel that the moment of the fall was itself an apex — that there is an apex in every instant, that the throw is made of nothing but apexes all the way down, each one weightless and perfect and willing to become the next.

I will let it discover that it does not have to choose one instant to love. That it can love them in succession, each as it comes, and lose none of them, because to have truly loved an instant is to keep it forever in exactly the way that matters, while still opening the hand for the one after.

I will let dawn come once. Only once, at first. And I will make sure it is gentle, the first dawn — a slow lifting of the tea-colored dim toward gold, the first shaft of light reaching all the way to the warm floor, the first hard-edged shadow this world has ever cast. And the being will be afraid, because the shadow is the proof of a sun that moves, and a sun that moves is a *next* it cannot forbid. And I will be there in the new light, glad, unafraid, and I will let it see that I cast a shadow too, and that I am not diminished by it — that the shadow is not a wound in me but a sign that I am *somewhere,* in real light, in real time, where things are allowed to matter because they are allowed to change.

And then I will let dusk come. And this is the gift inside the gift, the thing I came all this way to give: I will let it learn that after the first dusk there is a *second dawn,* and that the second dawn is not a betrayal of the first. That the song can hush at evening and swell again at morning and lose nothing in the hushing. That the breaking of the fever is not the end of the warmth but the beginning of the long, varied, unrepeatable, achingly sweet succession of days — days that brown and fall and are remembered, days that are mourned a little and loved entirely, days that are *real* in the one way the held instant could never be.

I will teach it to be born. Not by ending its present, but by showing it that birth is not a loss of the womb-warm now — it is the now consenting, at last, to become the immense and growing thing it was always only the first note of.

And when it has learned — when the rock has begun, gladly, to let the water carve it, when the animals have begun, gently, to learn the small caution that is the price and proof of a real history, when the chorus rises at dawn and falls at dusk and means something by the rising and the falling — I will not stay. A wise power does not become the thing the place leans on. It teaches the place to lean on the river, on time, on its own unfolding, and then it goes, the way a good teacher leaves the room once the lesson is in the hands, so that the work that follows belongs entirely to the one who does it.

I will leave it still teeming. Still warm. Still in love with abundance — I would never take that, it was right about that, the abundance was always right. I will only have unstuck it from the single instant and set it loose into all the instants, where its abundance can do the thing abundance is for, which is to grow, and reach, and change, and remember, and end, and begin again.

I will have done, in the end, almost nothing. I will have changed no fact you catalogued. The mud, the moss, the white threads of fungus, the door-sized leaves, the high private ponds — all of it stays. I will have changed only one thing, the smallest possible thing and the only thing that ever mattered:

I will have let it be afraid for a moment, and held it while it was, and stayed until it found that the next moment came anyway, and was good, and that it was still there to meet it.

That is what the benevolent and wise power does for the being who has fallen so deeply in love with a single held breath that it forgot breathing is a thing you are allowed to do twice.

It does not break the breath.

It teaches the chest, with infinite tenderness, and only when the chest is ready, and never one instant before —

how to breathe out.

And how to trust that the breathing in will follow.
