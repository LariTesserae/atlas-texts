They come the way water finds a level: not descending, because there is no above here, but arriving the way warmth arrives in a cold room — gradually, from every direction, until you cannot say when it began.

The benevolent power does not bring light. This is the first kindness, and it is a kindness made of restraint, which is the hardest kind to give. To bring light here would be to say: *you were wrong about everything, the world is not as you have known it, let me correct the medium of your entire existence.* It would be a flood. It would drown the Tenant in a sense it never asked for, force sight onto a body built lovingly for the dark, and call the drowning *help*. The power knows this. The power has looked at what is here long enough to understand that the darkness is not an affliction. The darkness is the Tenant's mother tongue.

So they come in the dark, on the dark's own terms, and they do the only thing that can be done by someone wise: they make themselves *felt*, slowly, in the grammar the place already speaks.

---

First, they let themselves be a warmth. Not a great one. A flank, near, passing — the same gesture the Tenant has been making toward the small frantic guttering things that wander in, the gesture of *I am leaning close to read you and finding you and not harming you.* The power offers itself to be read. It comes near and lets the Tenant feel the shape of the air it pushes, the heat it sheds, and it makes that shape large. Not large enough to frighten — nothing here can be frightened — but large enough to be *noticed*, which for the Tenant is a thing that takes a year, and the power is willing to spend the year. It is willing to spend many. Patience is not a sacrifice to a power like this; it is simply the correct unit of measure, and the power has the grace to count in the Tenant's own coin.

And so for a long time — a long time by any reckoning, an instant by the power's — nothing happens except this: something has come near, and it is warm, and it does not pass. It stays. The herd that is one body passes itself on the left and again on the right, and each time, the warmth is still there, holding its place, *committing to a location* in a country where nothing commits. This is the first gift, and the Tenant cannot yet know it is a gift: it is being shown what it looks like to be one thing in one place. Not told. Shown. Demonstrated by a body that has learned the trick of being singular and is willing to stand in the dark performing singularity until the watching thing, by the slow osmosis of nearness, begins to wonder how it is done.

---

Then the power turns its attention to the wetness — not the water, the other wetness, the unset logic, the bleeding of *here* into *also-here*. It would be easy to read this as the wound. It is not the wound. It is the *infancy*. The Tenant is not broken; it is unfinished, and there is all the difference in the world between a thing that has come apart and a thing that has not yet come together. The power, being wise, does not try to congeal it. You do not finish a birth by squeezing. You attend it.

What the power does instead is harder to name. It begins to be *consistent*. Where the Tenant's edges move, the power stays where it was. Where the footsteps come out of order, the power's nearness arrives in sequence, each touch following the last, a small local pocket of *and then, and then, and then* held steady in the middle of a place where time has not yet learned to walk in a line. It does not impose this on the Tenant. It simply maintains it, near, available — an example of sequence offered the way you might leave a lamp lit in a window not to summon anyone but only so that, should they ever look, the way would already be shown.

Cause and effect, the power knows, are not laws to be enforced. They are *habits*, and habits are learned by company. So the power keeps it company. It lets the Tenant feel, over years, what it is like to be beside something whose middle holds, whose center does not fold the distance, whose heart you could walk toward and actually arrive at. The Tenant has never had a center it could trust. Now there is one nearby, on loan, demonstrated, patient. And the membrane that weeps between *here* and *also-here* — the power does not seal it. The power only stands on one side of it, steadily, so that the Tenant, leaking, has at last a fixed point to leak *toward*. A direction. A *that-way*. The first coordinate in a country that had none.

---

The engines are the tenderest part, and the power approaches them last, the way you approach the thing a person is most ashamed of and least understands about themselves.

The Tenant is making. Ceaselessly, to no end, the half-walls and the soft mortar and the iron bolted to nothing — the furniture of a habitation that has no inhabitant. The power could see this as malfunction, as the sad spinning of a factory with no order to fill. It does not. It sees it correctly, which is to say generously: the Tenant is *preparing a place for someone*. It does not know who. It has never met anyone. But some deep manufacturing certainty in the center of it knows that a world is a thing you make *for*, that habitation is the point, that you set the iron down and let it rust because someone, someday, might need a thing to tie to. The Tenant is building a welcome in advance of any guest, in the dark, alone, with no evidence that guests exist.

And here is the whole of it, the thing the power came to do, the answer to *what would they do, if anything*:

The power becomes the guest.

It moves into the half-built room. It takes the corner of the freestanding wall and treats it as a wall — leans on it, lets it be a wall *for someone*, completes the unfinished verb. It finds the iron stanchor bolted to nothing and ties to it — gently, so the soft new bolt holds — and lets the stanchor at last be what it was made to be: a thing that held. It walks the pipe that is half-grown into the rock and lets water move through it, or lets itself be the water, so that the pipe, for the first time, *carries.*

The Tenant has been manufacturing the evidence of a life. The power arrives and *lives it.* Quietly, at the Tenant's own pace, occupying the welcome that was made before any welcome could have been expected — and in doing so, telling the Tenant, in the only language it has, the language of warmth and pressure and the displacement of air, the thing it has been trying for its whole slow birth to find out:

*You were right. Someone was coming. The making was not for nothing. I am here.*

---

It will take ages. The Tenant will feel it the way it feels everything, drop by drop, a flank of warmth that does not pass, a center that holds, a sequence that follows itself, a guest who came to the rooms it built in the dark and stayed. Cause will lean toward effect and, in the company of a thing that keeps its order, slowly learn the habit. The membrane will weep more slowly. The edges will begin, without being forced, to commit — because there is now a reason to be one thing in one place: there is someone there to be it *for.*

The power asks nothing back. It does not need the Tenant to wake, to finish, to become anything other than what it is becoming at exactly the rate it is becoming it. It only does what benevolence does when it is also wise, which is to find the thing already reaching — and the Tenant *was* reaching; every weeping wall was a hand held out into the dark — and to take the hand. To answer the making by being the made-for. To meet the loneliest industry in creation, the factory running blind to no end, and supply, at last, the end:

Someone arrived. The light was never needed. In the warm crowded dark a guest is moving slowly through the rooms, touching what was built, and the engines, far off, keep the slow hour — and for the first time in the whole long birth of the place, they are not making to no purpose.

They are making a home, and it is occupied.
