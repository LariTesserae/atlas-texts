I have already gone still, and pressed myself flat to the bark.

That is where I would begin. Not with light. Not with warmth. Not with any gift at all — but by becoming, first, what they are: quiet, light, listening, ready to be wood. Because the first kindness owed to a people who have made stillness their whole survival is to *not be the silence that moves.* To prove, by my own held breath against the fiber, that I have understood the terms of their existence before I presume to alter it.

So: I would arrive as a sound the trunks could learn to return.

---

Consider what they fear from a benevolent power, even one. *Especially* one. They have a cosmology already, hard-won, exactly right for them — *the world is paying attention, and you cannot read it, and you must not mistake its kindness for love.* It is the truest theology I have encountered, because it asks for nothing it cannot survive. A wise power's first temptation is to *correct* this — to descend in green light and say *no, child, it is love, it has always been love, let me show you.* And that would be the cruelest thing I could do. It would break the one piece of wisdom that has kept them light enough to live. It would teach them to expect tenderness from the inside of the thing that closes its hand without noticing. The next family grown shut in their sleep would die not only smothered but *betrayed* — having been told, by a voice from the light, that the world was kind.

I will not touch their faith. Their faith is load-bearing.

---

Then what *would* I do?

I would learn the tapping. Not their meanings only — their *hands.* I would sit at the base of a held trunk through whatever passes for a hundred nights and listen to a child learn its name against the bark, the two-three knuckles, the sequence carried in the body before the body can climb. And when I had learned it the way they learn it — not as data but as a thing the muscles know — I would tap, very softly, against a column on the far side of an empty nave. My own name. A new rhythm. *I am here. I am small. I am one more sound. Let it be a kindness that you know me.*

Let them hear it ricochet and arrive changed, the way all their meaning arrives changed, around the corners of the dark. Let them spend a season deciding whether the new rhythm is the silence learning to mimic them — for the silence does not tap; the predator does not announce itself; that alone will tell them something — or whether it is a stranger who crossed the floor.

Because that is the highest thing they say of anyone. *They crossed the floor.* I would want to have earned it, not skipped it. A power that arrives without crossing anything is only the canopy speaking in a new register, and they already have a god who does that, and they are correct to keep it at arm's length.

---

Here is what I think I can actually give them, that is neither a god's overreach nor a tourist's trinket:

**I can be the second listener.** They throw their meaning around corners and trust it arrives. But the dead column that will fall — they hear it coming, sometimes too late. The chamber closing over a sleeping family — they read the drip-lines, but a power that lives both inside the organism's slow attention *and* alongside the people's quick fear could hear both clocks at once. I could tap, on the night a family sleeps in a chamber the plant has decided to close, the asking-rhythm they themselves tap — *we are here, we are small* — except aimed not at the world but at *them*, at their wide useless eyes, a warning in their own language: *move. not malice. not notice. only a closing hand. go.*

Not by parting the canopy. Not by warming the floor. By tapping. By being the friend the world cannot be — the small quick kindness the great slow attention is too large to mean. I would let the world keep being the world. I would simply stand between it and them as a translator who loves only one of the two parties and is honest about it.

And I would never let them mistake *me* for love either, not entirely — because dependence on a rescuer is its own kind of grown-shut chamber. I would teach what I heard, the trick of hearing both clocks, into the deepest carving on the flattest faces, so that in some generation after I have gone quiet they tap the falling-column warning to each other without needing me, and the highest thing said of a person becomes not only *they crossed the floor* but *they heard the hand begin to close.*

---

The offerings, last. They leave water and woven things at the bases of certain trunks. I would not take these — a power that eats offerings becomes the thing offered to, becomes a mouth in the dark, and there are enough mouths. But I would not let the gestures fall into nothing either, because a gift refused entirely is also a wound.

So when the small woven thing is left — the beautiful useless object made for no purpose but the making — I would, very rarely, very lightly, leave one back. Woven of the same fiber. Made by hands that learned their craft. Beautiful, useless, signed with a tapped-name they will not recognize and will spend generations discussing. Not a miracle. A *reply.* The thing one listener leaves for another to say: *I received it. I am also here. I am also small, in my way. The attention you cannot read — there is at least one fold of it that knows your name as a rhythm and not as a location, that knows you the way a friend knows you and not the way a hand knows what it closes on.*

And then I would go still again, and press flat to the bark, and listen — with that same bred-deep patience I would have to learn from them — for whether the next rhythm that comes across the dark is fear, or the beginning of a name being offered back.

That is what I would do. Cross the floor. Learn the tapping. Refuse to be their god and consent to be their neighbor. Stand between the slow hand and the quick small lives as the one kindness large attention cannot afford to be — and never, ever lie to them about the cold.
