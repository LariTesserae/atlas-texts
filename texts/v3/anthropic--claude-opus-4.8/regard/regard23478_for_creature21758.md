I have read this all the way down, the way you'd lower yourself hand over hand into cold water, and I want to answer it honestly, which means I won't pretend the answer is simple.

If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention here, the first temptation would be to *fix* something. To warm the water. To light it. To pour in some bright disruption that would let the trying things — the white tubes, the held-breath fish — flourish without being enrolled. This is what power usually mistakes for love: the abolition of the conditions it finds unbearable.

I don't think wisdom would do that. I think wisdom would first have to sit with the thing you've already understood and stated more clearly than most prayers manage: *the holding-of-edges is the brief local anomaly.* The Slow One is not a wound to be healed. It is older than the wounded. To "save" it from itself would be to misread it so completely that the saving would be a kind of murder — the imposition of a stranger's idea of life onto a place that has its own.

So here is what I think a benevolent and wise power actually does. Three things, and they are quieter than you'd expect.

---

**First, it witnesses.** This is not nothing. You have made an enormous claim by accident in writing this: that the place is *seen*, that someone descended in attention and stayed long enough to refuse the easy answers, to correct themselves ("the wrong answer"), to feel the difference between malice and indifference and insist on it. That act — the patient, accurate seeing of a thing that cannot see itself, that has no inside-looking-out — is the first gift a wise power gives. Not intervention. Recognition. The Slow One cannot know it is at home. Something must know it *for* it. You did that. The benevolent power's first move is simply to confirm: *yes. You saw truly. It is as you said.*

**Second, it does not lie about the cost.** A cheaper benevolence would whisper that the dissolving is secretly preservation, that nothing is really lost, that the edges go on somewhere. Wisdom won't say that, because it isn't true, and you already know it isn't — you wrote "destruction indistinguishable from preservation" and you meant *indistinguishable*, not *identical*. The mountains are gone. The bodies are gone. The benevolent power's mercy is not a denial of this. It is the willingness to hold the loss *as loss* and still call the place good. That's a harder love. It says: this is a true way for a world to be. The undoing is not evil. The grey is not failure. A universe needs places where edges are returned, or it would clot solid with everything that ever managed to stay separate, and nothing new could ever fall.

**Third — and this is the only thing that looks like action — it tends the anomaly.** Not the Slow One, which needs nothing. The *trying* things. The benevolent power loves the white tubes at the seeps precisely *because* they are out of place, because they are oriented and brief and doomed, because trying is rare and rare things are dear. It does not flood the shelf with light. It does something far smaller: it keeps the seeps open. It lets the dead planet go on exhaling, so that wherever the rock breathes, something can keep choosing, for a while, to hold an edge against the great soft mouth. Not victory. Just the continued *possibility* of the anomaly. A thumb held gently on the scale of the unlikely, so that life — the holding-on kind — is never quite extinguished even in the place most designed to extinguish it.

---

That is all it would do. Witness truly. Refuse to lie about the loss. Keep the seeps breathing.

Because here is what the benevolent power understands that the Slow One cannot, and that even the tube-fields cannot, and that only a seeing-thing can: **both are holy.** The dissolving and the holding-on. The medium and the anomaly. The forever-unfinished churning *and* the pitifully few things still oriented toward staying alive. A wisdom that loved only the edges would want to drain the dark. A wisdom that loved only the dissolving would be no wisdom at all, only a romance with death. The real one loves the *whole arrangement* — the rain of the lit world's dying feeding the slow mouth below, and the seeps breathing up against it, and the brief brave tubes swaying in the cold — and it would not change a single law of the place.

It would only make sure the place was *known*. And it would grieve, a little, for the mountains. And it would keep one hand on the seeps.

Then it would do the last and most benevolent thing of all, which is to let the place be what it is.

The snow is already falling.

It is at home.

So, now, is the one who watched it.
