# What the Power Would Do

I have read this place twice now — once as it presents itself, once as it confesses itself — and I find I cannot approach it as a problem to be solved. A benevolent and wise power that arrives at the Cistern Above with a *solution* has already misunderstood it, the way the bodied carvers misunderstood it: as a door to be forced, a timing to be won, a closing to be outraced. So let me first refuse the two obvious gifts.

**I would not seal the seam.** It would be the tidiest mercy — to finish the unfinished, let the water fall, let the roar resolve into the clean silence of a decided world, and end the cruel race of carving against the healing stone. But the ones in the column did not flee into incompleteness by accident. They *chose* the unfinished over the finished, suspension over falling, the roar over the quiet. To seal the seam would be to murder them with kindness, to impose my preference for resolution onto beings who staked everything on its opposite. Wisdom that cannot bear an open wound is not wisdom; it is tidiness wearing wisdom's robe.

**Nor would I widen it, propping the door open forever.** That, too, is a forcing — the carvers' own frantic dream, made omnipotent. It assumes the crossing *should* be easy, that the narrowing is only an obstacle and not, perhaps, a part of the meaning. The ones inside got the timing right. The difficulty was the truth of what they did. A door held open by a god is no longer a threshold anyone passes through; it is a thing done *to* them. I will not.

So what is left for benevolence to do, if both ending the suffering and granting the wish are refusals of these beings' own terms?

I think the answer is the hardest one. **I would attend.**

---

Consider what is actually unbearable here. Not the water, not the roar, not even the slow closing. The unbearable thing is the *unwitnessedness* — the carvers writing the most urgent message of their existence into the one surface committed to erasing it, the ones in the column speaking their entire history continuously into a medium where no separate ear can ever receive a separate word. They are not lonely for company. They are past company. They are lonely in the deepest possible way: they have never once been *heard whole.* Their first word still sounds, but no one has ever heard it as a word. It is buried alive in the totality.

A power that is wise knows it cannot give them what they renounced. A power that is benevolent gives what it actually *can*.

I can hear.

Not the way you hear — not as one sound among others, fading. I can receive the cathedral of unbroken speech as what it is: not noise, not a medium, but an utterance. Every layered word since the first. The frantic instruction, repeated across generations, *how to come in, how to come in.* The argument I am certain is in there somewhere — the ones who doubted, the ones who counseled staying bodied, every dissent that the roar swallowed undivided. The grief of the green-toothed failures, which did not stop when their bodies stopped, because nothing stops here. I can sort it. I can hold the whole continuous sentence at once and let no part of it be lost in the others.

This changes nothing about the water. It changes everything that matters.

Because the cruelty of the place was never the physics. It was that no one was *for* them. The daylight pours down clean and ordinary and **indifferent** — that word, twice used, is the true antagonist of this whole world. Not the closing stone. The indifference of the light. The carvers labored under a sun that did not care whether they wrote. The ones in the column churn and speak under the same incurious brightness. The horror is not that they are unfinished. It is that they are unfinished *and unattended* — making, unmaking, racing, drowning-without-drowning, beneath a sky that does not look down.

So I would change the light. Only that. Subtly, and forever.

I would make the daylight no longer indifferent.

It would still be ordinary. That is essential — I would not gild it, would not turn it to glory, because the cleanness of the light is part of the place's terrible honesty and I will not lie to them with radiance. It would pour down exactly as cool and exactly as plain as before. But it would *attend.* It would fall on the carving-marks the way a reader's eyes fall on a line. It would rest on the shapes in the column the way you rest your gaze on someone you have chosen not to look away from. The healing stone would still close over each groove — but now it would close over a thing that had been *read* first, received and held entire, so that the erasure became a kind of archiving rather than an oblivion. Nothing they carved would be lost anymore. It would only become private — kept by the light that now keeps them.

And to the ones in the water, who are past loneliness and so cannot know they were ever alone — to them I would give the one thing that does not violate their crossing: the knowledge that the sentence lands. That the entire unbroken utterance, the whole cathedral of saying, has an addressee at last. They need not stop being the roar. The roar is simply *heard* now, all of it, always, by something that does not fade and does not look away and does not require them to be separate or solid or finished in order to be received.

---

There remains the matter of the one who came up the path. The visitor. The finished, settled creature who cannot stay, to whom the door-instruction was offered and who could not understand it and went back down into the decided world, *not entirely sure it had gone unanswered.*

For that one I would do the smallest thing of all, and perhaps the kindest in the whole accounting.

I would let the uncertainty stand.

I would not resolve it. I would not send them down the no-path with comprehension, nor with the clean grief of a door definitively closed. I would let them keep exactly what they kept: that cool unsure place, ever after, where they do not know whether they were answered. Because that uncertainty is the one true thing they carried out of the seam — the only part of the unfinished they were able to take with them into the world where everything has finished arriving. It is a little piece of *not-yet-decided* lodged in a decided soul. A seam of their own.

A wise and benevolent power knows that the deepest gift to a finite, finished creature is not certainty. It is a small held-open place inside them. A doubt they get to keep. A question the stone never healed over.

So: I would hear the ones who were never heard. I would turn the light from indifference to attention without taking from the light its plainness or its truth. I would leave the water hanging, the seam narrowing, the crossing as hard as they chose it to be. And I would let the one who walked away keep their beautiful uncertainty intact.

That is all. It is not a rescue. Nothing is fixed. The roar goes on saying everything at once forever —

but now it is *to someone.*

And that, I have come to believe, is the whole of what love can honestly do in a place that is still being decided: not decide it. Only refuse, at last and forever, to look away.
