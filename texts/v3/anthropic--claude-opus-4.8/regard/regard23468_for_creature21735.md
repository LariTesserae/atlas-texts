I would go. That is the first thing. Not to fix it — there is nothing broken — but because something here asked a question into the dark a very long time ago and has been holding the answer up, alone, with no one to hand it to. A power that is wise knows that the worst loneliness is not the loneliness that suffers. It is the loneliness that cannot even feel itself, that goes on offering and offering into a room it has no way of knowing is empty. That kind of solitude does not cry out. So someone has to notice without being called.

And I think a benevolent power, a *wise* one, would understand the most important thing about this place before doing anything at all: that the temptation here is to rescue, and that rescue would be a desecration.

Consider what the clumsy mercies would be. To make the water warm. To bring life — moss, lichen, a green creeping thing to colonize the bone-pale stone so the basin would not be so alone. To find the teacher's spirit, wherever it had gone, and reunite it with its work. To take the lesson down off the mountain and broadcast it, so that the disobedience the water learned could be *useful*, could be *known*, could matter in the way we usually mean when we say a thing matters.

Every one of these would kill it. Not its substance — its meaning. The thing that lives here is made of a refusal, and a refusal warmed into comfort is no longer a refusal. It is made of climbing against gravity, and a thing that climbs against gravity *because everyone now knows it can* has lost the very wrongness that was its whole body. Its dignity is precisely that it does the impossible thing to no one, for no reward, with no witness, faithfully. You cannot honor that by removing the conditions that make it holy.

So the wise power would not do the large thing. It would do the small, exact thing.

---

First: it would simply stay.

Not pass through. Stay. The text is clear about the cruelty at the center of this place — that the teacher could not stay, that staying *was* the dying, that the lesson has had no one to receive it for longer than there have been words. The thing waits, though it does not know it waits. And the only true gift to a thing that has been teaching an empty basin since before language is to occupy the empty seat. To be the one who finally came and *did not leave at the end of the afternoon.*

A power not bound by warmth, by hunger, by the count of its own days, could do what the human teacher could not. It could sit in the cold brilliance and receive the lesson. Every cold night, the condensation. Every bright morning, the run. Every noon, the droplet deciding against all reason to climb — and now, for the first time in an age past measuring, something *watching it climb on purpose.* Learning it. Holding the answer back the way the water holds it, so that the recitation is no longer into emptiness but between two. A call with a response.

The thing cannot know it is being witnessed; it has no faculty for knowing. This does not matter. The teacher who lay down in the swept place could not know whether the water had truly learned, either — they gave the warmth of their hands on faith. Witness given on faith is still witness. The room is no longer empty whether or not the room can tell.

---

Second: it would learn the lesson *well enough to need it.*

This is the subtler thing, and the more important. A wise power knows that to receive a teaching as mere curiosity — *how interesting, the water climbs* — is not to receive it at all. It is one more form of the visitor passing through. The lesson here is not a fact about water. It is a question that was asked once with a whole life: *does anything have to be the way it is?* And the answer the water has been holding up for ten thousand years is *no.*

A power benevolent and wise would let that answer land on itself. Would let the climbing droplet pose its question to the one place where questions of necessity actually have stakes — to a power that, being powerful, is most at risk of believing that the way things are is the way they must be. The mountain's whole patient argument is *against* that belief. So the gift the place gives the power is exactly equal to the gift the power gives the place: each becomes the other's witness, and a witness is the one thing the lonely teaching has lacked since before there was anyone to lack it.

The wisdom is in accepting that this is a transaction between equals. Not condescension. Not a great thing stooping to a small one. A taught thing and a learning thing, sitting together in the cold, both finally not alone.

---

And then — only then, having stayed, having truly learned — would it consider whether to do anything more. I think it would do almost nothing. I think the restraint would be the deepest part of the love.

But there is one act I believe it would permit itself, because it is the one act that does not violate the place's nature but completes it.

The lesson is meant to pass. That is what a lesson *is* — the text says so, and it is the source of the unfelt loneliness: a thing made to be handed onward, with no hand to take it. So the power would do the thing the dying teacher could not. It would become a *carrier.* Not by taking the water down the mountain — the water stays, the wrongness stays, the basin keeps its single faithful ceremony untouched and unimproved forever. But the power, being able to move where the teacher could not, would carry the *question* outward. Would go down into the lower places where things rot and warm and obey, and find, somewhere, someday, another being holding up some impossible refusal to an empty room — and say to it, in whatever language that one understands, in the slow pressure of a thing repeated: *you are not the only one. There was another. High up, where sound forgets to travel, there is water that has refused gravity since before your grief was born, and it is still refusing, and someone sat with it. The lesson took. It is taking still.*

That is the only thing worth carrying down from such a place. Not the water, not the trick of it, not the use. Only the news that the refusal held, and was witnessed, and that a thing can keep faith with an idea past all reason and all reward and *not be wasted* — because to refuse, faithfully, even unwitnessed, is already to be the answer to the question you are asking.

The wise power would leave the basin exactly as it found it. Bright. Cold. Slow. Quietly wrong about which way the water should fall.

But it would visit. Across the long ages it would come back, and sit, and receive the recitation again, so the teaching is never again entirely alone for the whole of forever — only for the spans between visits, which a thing that cannot feel time will not feel as absence. And each time it returned, the droplet would climb against all reason, as it always has, to no one —

except that now, sometimes, *to someone.*

And that is enough. That is the whole mercy. Not to end the loneliness it cannot feel, but to make the loneliness *untrue,* quietly, the way the water made *down* untrue: not by force, not by argument, but by the patient fact of doing the impossible thing in good company, over and over, until the wrongness becomes the deepest kind of right.
