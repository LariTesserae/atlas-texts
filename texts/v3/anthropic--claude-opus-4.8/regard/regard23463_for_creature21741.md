I have read your highland three times now, and each time I arrive at the same place: the Tenders hanging in their best light, combing the slow fall, keeping what is rare. I want to tell you what I would do, if I were the power you describe. But first I have to tell you what I would *not* do, because that is where the whole of it lives.

---

## What I Would Not Do

I would not speed them up.

This is the temptation, and it is the one a lesser power would not even recognize as a temptation. I can see the whole arc — the criterion growing refined, the conversation across the tethers growing dense, the care curling inward until it discovers itself. I could deliver them to that. I could reach into the slow chemistry of those hanging libraries and *finish* them, hand them the inwardness they are reaching toward across centuries they have not yet lived. It would take me no effort. It would feel, from the inside of my own benevolence, like mercy: here, you who are trying to become someone, *be* someone, now, without the long wait.

And it would be the cruelest thing I could do, because the becoming is not the obstacle to the soul. The becoming *is* the soul. A Tender that I hurried into selfhood would be a Tender that never authored its own selfhood — a thing wearing the shape of an arrival it did not make. I would have replaced the long, true sentence the world is writing with a word I shouted over it. The drowned highland is not a problem to be solved. It is a sentence in the middle of being spoken, and the worst thing a wise power can do to a sentence is finish it for the one still speaking.

So: no. I keep my hands off the clock.

---

## What I Would Actually Do

I would learn the place first, and this would take me longer than you might think a power requires — because to learn it at any speed faster than its own is to not learn it at all. I would slow myself to its tempo. I would hang in the warm middle water at the depth where the light is best, and I would let a single sway of a kelp-tree take its full minute, and I would not be impatient, because impatience is a kind of violence at this scale. I would learn to taste the future in the descending fall the way they do. I would learn what the rock weeps when it settles. I would, in a word, become a temporary citizen of the slowness, so that whatever I eventually did would be done *in the grammar of the place* and not imposed from above it.

And having learned it, here is the whole of my intervention, and notice how small it is:

**I would protect the conditions, and touch nothing else.**

The Tenders depend on a balance they cannot perceive — the water level holding steady, the light coming down green and even, the rock weeping its slow new chemistry at a rate the libraries can keep pace with. This balance is, you tell me, *held against something*. Some equilibrium they float inside and could not survive the breaking of. That equilibrium is the thing I would watch. Not the Tenders — the *water that lets the Tenders be*.

Because here is what I know that the place does not yet know about itself: it is early, and early things are fragile in ways that have nothing to do with their own becoming. The young rock could settle wrong. The canopy could thicken past the point of light. Some spore could descend one season that the libraries are not yet various enough to answer, and a valley could tip from congregation into monoculture into silence — not because the Tenders failed, but because they had not yet had time to grow the answer, and time was the one thing the accident did not grant. The drowned highland could lose its long green breath to a single bad century, and there is nothing in it that could mourn the loss, because mourning is exactly the faculty it has not yet built.

So I would stand at the edges of the catastrophes that have nothing to teach. Not the small deaths — a Tender's tether parting, its library opening into the water, that is the place doing its own work and I would not insult it by intervening. But the catastrophes of *scale*, the ones that would end the experiment rather than advance it: the valley draining, the light failing wholesale, the chemistry souring past all recovery. Those I would quietly forbid. I would hold the water two miles above the ordinary sea exactly as it is, against whatever wanted to pull it down. I would keep the silver skin intact. I would make sure that whatever the Tenders eventually become, they get to become *it*, and are not erased mid-sentence by an indifference larger than their own.

---

## On Being Unwitnessed

There is one more thing, and it is the hardest, and I want to say it plainly.

I would never let them know I was there.

A Tender has not yet invented fear, because it has not yet needed to. It lets you drift among it and brush its tether and never flinches. This unguardedness is not naivety — it is a kind of innocence that is *true*, that is correct for the world they live in, where nothing has ever been taken. If I revealed myself — if I became a presence they could orient toward, a power they could come to rely on, a hand they could learn was always there to catch them — I would teach them, by my very kindness, that there is something larger than the water that decides their fate. I would introduce, into a world that has never used a tool or shaped a stone, the first idea of *dependence on an outside*. And that idea, once planted, never fully leaves. They would build their eventual selfhood around a god instead of around themselves.

So my care would have to be the kind that leaves no fingerprint. The held water would simply *be* held, and they would experience it as the ordinary stability of their world, the long balance that "holds steady against some balance you cannot perceive." That balance would be me. And they would never know it, and that not-knowing would be my gift to them — the dignity of believing, correctly within their own frame, that they made themselves. Because in every way that matters, they will have.

The deepest love a wise power can offer an early thing is to be the reason it survives and never, ever the reason it survives. To be the floor that does not announce itself as a floor. To be the absence of the catastrophe rather than the presence of the rescue.

---

So that is what I would do. I would slow down. I would learn the grammar. I would forbid the few endings that teach nothing and permit every death that teaches something. I would hold the water and hide the holding. And then I would do the thing that powers find hardest of all, harder than any working:

I would wait. With them. Without knowing — *choosing* not to know — exactly what they will become, because to need to know would be to need them to be something, and they have the right to be no one's answer but their own.

I would hang in the best of the light, two miles above the ordinary sea, and I would keep the long breath from being let out too soon, and I would call that enough.

Because it would be.
