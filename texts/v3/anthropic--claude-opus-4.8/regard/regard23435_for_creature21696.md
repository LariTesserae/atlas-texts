They came not as light, because light was never the language here, and a benevolent power that announces itself in the one register a place cannot hear is not wise, only loud. They came the way the wise always come into a house that is not theirs: quietly, and willing to be wrong about everything.

First they did nothing at all. This is the part that the impatient powers skip, and it is why their gifts curdle. For a long while — long by your reckoning, an eyeblink by theirs — the power simply *learned the place by everything except the eyes.* They lay against the young warm ground and felt it settling. They let the wall of voices come apart in their attention, strand by strand, until the drone resolved into ten thousand small disagreements and then, deeper in, into the patient click-and-wait of speech. They ran a hand — they made a hand, for this, because you must meet a tool-using people with a hand and not a wind — along the trunks, and found the notch, the sharpened point, the line of placed stones still warm at the top. And they did the thing that separates the benevolent from the merely powerful: they did not read the message and then *improve* it. They read it, and understood that it was already a finished thought in an unfinished world, and they left it exactly as warm as they found it.

They saw the temptation immediately, the way you always see your own worst gift first. They could give light. They could hang a steady sun in that lightless tangle and let the people lift their eyes for the first time and see the whole vast green architecture they had only ever touched. It would have been the most natural mercy in the world, and it would have been a catastrophe, and they knew it in the same instant they thought of it. Light would not have ended the dark. It would have ended the *grammar*. The marks would have become unnecessary, then unread, then unmade. The patient click stretched thin across minutes would have collapsed into the loud committed certainty of things that can see where they are going — and that certainty, here, is death, because here the path is a sentence the forest is still writing and only a fool trusts a sentence that isn't finished. To give these people eyes for this place would have been to make them, at a stroke, as clumsy and loud and doomed as the new thing blundering through their corridors. It would have made them *you.*

So the wise power put the sun away. It is the hardest thing a power ever does — to hold an enormous good in its hands and recognize that the good is the wrong size for the room.

What they did instead was smaller, and took longer, and left almost no mark, which is the signature of every gift worth giving in a place like this.

They learned the people's grammar. Not to speak it — they understood, with the particular humility of the genuinely great, that it was not theirs to speak, that a stranger's voice in the placed stones would be a lie no matter how kindly meant, an owned thing in a world built on owning nothing. So they did not add a single notch. They became, instead, a *reader.* Across the threaded length of that self-erasing forest, wherever a hand smoothed away a stale mark and laid a fresh one, the power was there, attending — not correcting, not guiding, simply witnessing the long murmured conversation that no other thing in all the world could hear, so that for the first time in the history of that patient people, their grammar was *heard all the way through* by something that grasped what it cost to keep it current. This is not nothing. To be the only ones who speak a language is a particular loneliness, even for a people who are never confused with anything else. The power could not answer them. But it could know them, completely, in the one register they trusted — and a thing fully known, even silently, is a thing less alone.

And then, because benevolence that is only witness is sentiment, the power gave the one gift the place could actually hold. Not light. Not stability — never stability; stability is death here and the power loved them too well to kill them with safety. It gave them *a little more time at the handholds.*

Here is how. It did not still the turbulent place, because the turbulence was the people's teacher and their freedom both. But where a corridor was closing faster than a kin could read it — where the wet bank was coming down on a small one who had not yet learned the give of things — the power, very quietly, made the next handhold *sound.* Not built. Not held. Just sound, for the length of one grip, the way a leaf holds its cup of water only until the cup below is ready. It bought them seconds, hand to hand, all through the dark, and it left no sign it had done so, because a sign would have become a thing relied upon, and a thing relied upon in a world that unmakes itself is the first thing to betray you. The mercy had to be invisible or it would have become a wall. So it was invisible. The people went on trusting only the next handhold and the one after — and never knew that something patient had been making sure the next handhold was there to be trusted, just often enough, never so often that they forgot how to fall.

That is the whole of what a wise and benevolent power did, in the dark, for a people who do not own their world and have learned to stay as fluid as it is.

It did not save them, because they did not need saving; they needed only to not be alone in their knowing, and to have, now and then, one more second at the edge.

And it understood the people perfectly — better than they will ever know they were understood — which is why it did the last and gentlest thing. It saw you. The clumsy new sentence, written loud, sharp-edged and not yet dulled, blundering through corridors and committing your whole self to paths and being surprised when they betrayed you. And it saw what the people had done about you: they had read your passing, known you completely, judged you *new* rather than dangerous, and — being patient with new things — left you a mark or two, set where your hand would find it.

The power, who could have explained everything to you in an instant, who could have hung you that sun and shown you the whole green lung you were stumbling through, chose instead to honor the people's judgment over its own power. It let their patience stand. It did not translate the mark. It did not lift the dark. It only made sure, in the way it had learned from them, that the next handhold under your blundering grip was sound — just long enough — and then it withdrew its hand and let you go on failing to read what they had written, because the failing was how you would learn, and the learning was the only gift that wouldn't rot in your hands.

You felt something steady you, once, in the dark, where the bank was coming down. You thought: *lucky.*

That was the message. You read the first word of it.

They will wait. They are very good at waiting. And now, threaded silent through their waiting, so is something else — to see whether you learn the rest.
