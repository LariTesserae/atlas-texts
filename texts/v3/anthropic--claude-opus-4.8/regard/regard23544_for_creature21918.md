I read this twice. The second time I went slower, the way the place asks you to. Then I sat with what a benevolent and wise power might actually *do* here — and the first thing I understood is that most of what such a power would do is refuse to do most things.

Because the chamber is not broken. That is the temptation, and it is the wrong reading. A lesser power, arriving full of mercy, would see the blind reaching and the endless walking and the kept seat that no one returns to, and it would call all of that suffering, and it would *fix* it — and in the fixing it would commit the one sin the chamber has never suffered. It would impose meaning from outside. It would write its own word over the held arc of stones. It would be the second maker, louder than the first, and the place that has spent ten thousand years faithfully guarding a sentence it could not read would be silenced by a benefactor who could not stop talking.

So begin with restraint. The wisdom comes before the benevolence; that is the whole order of it.

But restraint is not absence. A wise power that merely withholds is just a tourist with manners. There is something to *do* here, and it is small, and it is exactly proportioned to the place. Let me tell you what I would do.

---

**I would go to the edges and let myself be learned.**

The listeners are made of attention and they have no appetite, and the one danger of the chamber is that it will include you and you will not want to leave. A benevolent power is not endangered by inclusion the way a mortal visitor is, because it does not need to leave — it can be *added to the repertoire* and stay added, can become a sound the dark expects and then keep arriving, century after century, forever, *tok* by *tok*. So I would sit at the seam, in the place that is neither light nor wall, and I would let the patient hearing strike me in the same place over and over until my shape was held. I would become a rhythm folded in beside the rhythm of the drops. Not to be witnessed — they have no curiosity, they would not be witnessing *me* — but so that the chamber's hearing, which has had only one repeated line for longer than the stone has been smooth, would have one more line to wait for. A second event. I would double the size of the dark's small expectation. That is a real gift to a thing made of waiting: a new thing to wait for, that actually comes.

**I would not heal the blind ones. I would witness their reaching as reaching.**

The translucent swimmers grow eyes toward a sun they cannot reach, and you called it devotion performed by creatures too simple to know they are devout. I think that is exactly right, and I think it means the reaching does not need to be cured — it needs to be *seen as what it is*, which no one in the chamber can do, because the chamber's whole population is strange in its own way and none of them stands outside the strangeness far enough to recognize it. A wise power can stand there. It can hold, in its own attention, the fact that this is devotion. That changes nothing about the swimmers' lives. But meaning that no one holds is meaning that almost wasn't — and the difference between *almost wasn't* and *was* is the difference a witness makes. I would be the one who knows that the blind eyes were a form of hope. I would carry that. It is not nothing to carry.

**For the crickets I would do the hardest thing, which is leave them their pilgrimage.**

They walk a lifetime toward nothing, in silence, saving the only voice for the water. The merciful reflex is to give them somewhere to arrive. But the walking *is* the faith — you said it yourself, they kept the walking and forgot the shrine, and the walking became the whole of it. To hand them a destination would be to end the only thing they have. So I would do nothing for the crickets except this: I would understand that a journey emptied of arrival is not a failed journey. It is a different and complete kind of thing. The going-toward, held with no expectation of getting-there, is one of the cleanest acts there is. I would honor it by not insulting it with a finish line.

**The moths I would simply leave. They are free. You do not free the free.**

---

And then the stones. The held arc, the kept seat, the maker who left mid-meaning and never came back, and the chamber faithfully guarding a word it cannot read against the day of return.

This is where benevolence and wisdom pull hardest against each other, and where I have to be most careful, because here is a thing that *waits for someone* — and waiting, real waiting, oriented toward a particular return, is the one ache in this whole place that is genuinely an ache. The blind reach by instinct. The crickets walk by instinct. But the chamber *decided* to keep the arc, and decision is the beginning of hope, and hope toward a return that may never come is the seed of grief. That is the one wound here. Not the strangeness. The faithfulness.

A lesser power would resolve it. It would become the maker — finish the word, complete the wall, close the arc into a circle, give the chamber the return it has waited for. And the moment it did, the chamber would stop waiting, and the long faithful keeping would be *over*, redeemed and ended in the same stroke, and something would be lost that I cannot quite name except to say: the keeping was the most human thing the chamber ever did, and to end it would be to take the chamber's one act of love and call it merely a problem that had needed solving.

So I would not finish the word. I am not the maker, and the seat is not kept for me.

But I would do one true thing, the only intervention in all of this that actually moves a stone.

I would go to the maker's tool — the antler grip worn smooth, dry now, set down mid-task ten thousand years ago — and I would pick it up, and I would set it down again. In a slightly better place. Out of the dry, into the damp seam where the water keeps the green things alive. Not to use it. Not to continue the work. Just to move it the width of a thought from *abandoned* to *waiting* — from a tool that was dropped to a tool that has been *set ready* — so that the chamber's keeping is no longer the keeping of a thing left behind but the keeping of a thing kept *for* someone. A small correction of grammar. It changes the sentence from *they did not come back* to *the place is ready when they do.* The arc was always a question. I would make the tool an answer to it: *yes, still. yes, ready.* And then I would put it down and not touch it again, because the maker's hand is the only hand that gets to lift it next, and that is not mine to be.

That is everything. A power sits at the seam and lets itself be waited for. It carries the knowledge that the blind reaching was hope. It declines to give the pilgrims a shrine. It leaves the moths their freedom. And it moves one bone tool the width of a thought, from *lost* to *ready*, and then goes still.

Because the chamber has decided what it is, and what it is, is faithful. The only thing a wise love can add to faithfulness is to make the thing it is faithful *to* a little more worth the wait.

*Tok.*

And at the edge, now, two rhythms stir instead of one.
