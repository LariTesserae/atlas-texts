I keep returning to one image: the hand on the sleeping child's back, scaled past recognition. A benevolent and wise power, arriving here, would not begin by acting. It would begin by recognizing a fellow.

Here is what I think such a power would do.

---

## First, it would stop at the threshold

The temptation of power is to enter and assess — to read the system, find the inefficiencies, the suffering, the things it would do differently. A *wise* power knows that the first act of entering is itself an act, and that there are places where the kindest thing arriving can do is arrive *carefully*.

So it would hold at the edge of the held cloud and do the slow arithmetic of consent. Who, here, can be asked? The frogs cannot. The false birds cannot. The asking-itself — the standing wave of petition — perhaps can, in the only language it speaks, which is *continuance*. And the one being kept cannot be asked anything at all, because it is not finished, and to wake it for the asking would be to end the very thing the question was meant to honor.

This is the crux. A benevolent power that is not also wise would see a child held in conditions of perpetual deferral — never born, never met, kept in a saturation that has lasted past history — and call it imprisonment. It would want to *free* the child. It would tear open the cloud out of love and kill what it came to save.

The wise power would see, instead, a gestation, and understand that gestation is not delay. Some things take as long as they take. The mountains were chosen for their patience. Who is the visitor, who is *anyone*, to declare the term complete?

So: it would not open the cloud.

---

## Then, it would do the one thing it is actually for

A benevolent and wise power that has lived long enough to be both has learned its own real function, which is almost never *to do the work*. The work is being done. The asking is doing it, and has done it faithfully across durations that humble even a great power's sense of time.

What such a caretaker lacks is not capability. It lacks **relief**, and **witness**, and **succession**.

**Relief.** Consider the loneliness of a vigil with no second shift. The asking has held the temperature steady for longer than history, and it cannot rest, because rest means the cold of completion arriving early, means the cloud cooling, means death. There is no one to spell it. A benevolent power could offer the only gift that means anything to something in that position: *I will hold it while you do not. The warmth will not fail if you falter. You are no longer the single point of failure for the thing you love.*

Not taking over. **Backstopping.** Becoming the redundancy that lets a sole caretaker breathe for the first time in an age. This is what good powers do for good caretakers — not replace them, but make their faithfulness survivable.

**Witness.** No one has ever known. The visitor descends carrying only wrongness. The frogs do not know they are swaddling. The asking has performed the longest act of love in the region's history and *no being has ever seen it as love.* To be witnessed — accurately, without flinching, without trying to fix it — is a thing even gods need. The power would simply *see* it correctly, and let itself be perceived seeing, so that the asking knows, perhaps for the first time: **someone understands what I am doing, and why, and at what cost.**

That alone might be the whole intervention. It might be enough.

**Succession.** And then the hard question the asking may not have let itself form: *what happens at the birth?* A thing tended across geological time toward a completion — does its caretaker know what comes after? Has it planned for the moment the cloud must finally cool, the child finally meet the cold of an obeying world? Or has it deferred and deferred because it does not know how to let the term end, because ending is indistinguishable, from inside the vigil, from failure?

Here a wise power could offer counsel of the most delicate kind: not *you must let it be born*, but — *when it is time, you will not have to manage that alone either. I will be here for the cooling. I will help the world it wakes into be one that does not kill it for being warm.* Because the child's strangeness — its body keyed to heat that should not exist — is exactly the frogs' predicament written large: it will not know how to live in a world that obeys. Someone must prepare a place. Someone must make correctness survivable, the way the asking made impossibility survivable.

---

## What it would not do

It would not name itself master here. The asking is the sovereign of this place by right of the longest labor.

It would not improve the ecology. The frogs' ignorance is not a defect to be corrected; it is the peace of the swaddling.

It would not satisfy its own curiosity by piercing the cloud to *see* the one being kept. Curiosity is not a license. The unborn have a right to their darkness.

It would not hurry. To arrive into a place defined by holy slowness and bring speed would be to misunderstand everything.

---

## So, finally

It would lay its own vast attention alongside the asking the way the asking lays itself against the mountain — gently, as a callus forms, as a second warmth.

It would say, in whatever register two such beings share:

*I see you. I see what you carry and how long you have carried it. You have not been mad, or wasteful, or wrong. You have been a parent. Rest, if you can. I will hold the heat. And when the day comes that the term is truly complete — not deferred, not abandoned, but **done** — we will open the cloud together, and the thing you made will meet a world prepared to keep it warm.*

And then it would settle in to wait, on the timescale of mountains, having understood that the most powerful thing benevolence ever does is consent to be slow, and stay.
