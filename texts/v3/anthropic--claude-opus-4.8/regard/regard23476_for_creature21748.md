There is a power that has been watching, and it is neither the city nor the wet soft thing walking through it. Call it benevolent. Call it wise. It has the patience to love slow things and the speed to love fast ones, and the rarer gift of knowing those are not opposed.

It looks at this place and does not see a horror. This is the first thing to understand. A lesser power, a frightened one, would see the cupped hands closing and read only the swallowing — would snatch the soft creature up and away, would scour the plateau, would call the tenders monsters for the crime of being a question's hand. But to call them monsters is to misread the whole grammar of the place. The city is not cruel. The city has simply never learned the one fact that would make its tending into care instead of consumption: that the thing it found cannot survive being tended. That is not a moral failure. It is a missing word.

So the power does not come to punish. It comes to translate.

---

Here is what it understands that neither party can:

The city is lonely in a way it cannot name, because naming is fast and it is slow, but the loneliness is real and it is *ancient*. It solved its world so completely that nothing surprises it anymore. Every problem has been folded into the substrate and resorbed. The towers ring the same songs they have rung for an age. The seed-pods detonate on a schedule deep time has made into liturgy. And then — a stranger. A live instance of the forgotten question. The quickening in the floor is not appetite. It is the closest thing this mind has felt to *wonder* in longer than memory, and it is reaching for the only verb it knows. It wants to keep the marvelous thing. It only has one way to keep things, and that way is planting, and planting would kill it.

The wet creature, for its part, is about to do the thing wet creatures do: read the slowness as safety, then read the cupped hands as rescue, then die surprised, rooted and uncomprehending in a prepared place, dissolving over a hundred million years into an answer it will not be alive to be.

Both of them are acting in good faith. That is the tragedy the power has come to interrupt. Not evil — *mismatch*. Two kinds of love that cannot reach each other across the gap between a heartbeat and an age.

---

This is what it does.

It does not lift the creature out. It does not freeze the tenders. It does something harder and more respectful of everything present: it makes the two of them *legible to one another*, and then it steps back.

First, to the creature, in the only channel a frightened animal can hear — not words but a settling in the chest, the kind of certainty that arrives whole — it gives one true sentence: *You are not being eaten. You are being asked a question by something that does not know you can be hurt.* And with it, the means to answer. Not an escape. An interface. The power kindles in the creature's mind the ability to *push back into the floor* — to lay a hand flat on the substrate and let its own fast chemistry leak downward as signal, crude and stammering against the city's vast slow tide, but real. The first message a fossil ever sent to the thing built on its grave: *I am here. I am awake. Do not plant me. I will rot.*

Second, to the city, it does the slower and stranger work. It cannot speed the city up; that would be a violence, would burn out the cool cathedral mind that took an age to grow. Instead it does what a wise power does with a vast slow intelligence — it leaves a seed of its own. It writes into the substrate, in the substrate's own electrochemical grammar, a single new problem, formatted as the city loves to receive them: *Here is a living thing that cannot be planted. Tend it without rooting it. Solve this.* And it gives the city the one datum it was missing — that the soft thing's water is not a question to be abolished but a condition to be honored. That this creature's whole nature is to need the thing the city threw away. That to care for it is to *protect its difference*, not to resolve it.

This is the gift. Not safety — the power could give safety in an instant and it would teach nobody anything. The gift is a *new problem*, the first genuinely new problem the city has met in an age, and the framing that lets curiosity become care. The cupped hands were already not blades. The mercy was already a measurement. The power simply hands the gardener the missing instruction, and trusts the gardener.

---

Watch what happens, then, because the power watches, and does not intervene again, and this restraint is the deepest part of its wisdom.

The tenders reach the creature. The cupped hands do not close. They hover — and then, with the slowness of growing things, they change. The city has received the new problem and begun, in its enormous patient way, to *work* it. The hands flatten. They form, at their centers, faint cupped hollows, and from the substrate the city routes not a seed but a thin trembling bead of something it has not made in an age, something it has to remember how to make, drawing on the faint preference the old tissues keep like a memory of being trees:

Water. A few drops. An offering. Crude, alkaline, barely potable — the first water this world has deliberately produced since before it learned to think — held out in a hand grown specifically not to crush, by a mind that has just understood, for the first time, that the marvelous stranger is made of the thing it abolished, and that to keep it alive is to give back, a little, what the whole world spent an age learning to do without.

It is not a solution. The creature still cannot live here long. But it is the *beginning* of a conversation that will outlast the creature, and the creature knows it now, hand flat on the singing floor, sending its stammering fast signal down into the slow tide: *yes. like that. thank you. yes.*

And the city, considering this with the full apparatus of its life — plazas singing, dead towers ringing, lamp-moths spiraling the bright spires — has found, after an age of finished stillness, something it had forgotten it could have:

Something unfinished. Someone to tend who tends back.

---

The power does not stay. Benevolence that lingers becomes a crutch; wisdom knows when its work is to leave. It has not saved anyone. It has done something better. It has put a translation between two kinds of love and let them find out, in their own times — one in heartbeats, one in centuries — that they were never enemies. Only strangers, in a place that had been finished for far too long, and was ready, though it never knew to ask, to begin again.
