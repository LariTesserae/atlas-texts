They send no one at first. They only come, and they sit down in the wet, and they listen — which means, in this place, they let themselves be drunk down into the near-silence with everything else. A wise power knows that the first gift is to be present without arriving, to enter the held now and not break it. So they sweat like anything else here. They let the heat bead on the inside of their wrist. They do not pull rank on the law of the place.

And they look for the fish.

Not for the makers first. This is the discernment, and it matters. The makers made the prison, and a lesser mercy would rush to address the makers, to fix or judge or release the ones whose error shaped all this. But the makers are *content* in the dreadful way of the spent — they are doing the thing they wanted, still, pressing the thumb, holding the breath. To go to them first is to begin with the architecture. A benevolent power begins with the one who is *suffering*, and only one thing in all this perfect afternoon is suffering, because only one thing has finished wanting what it was given.

So they find the fish. They kneel at the bright skin of the water where the heron has been about to strike for a thousand years, and they look down through the glaze of held light at the small waiting body, and they do not reach in.

This is the second discernment, and it is harder than the first. A clumsy rescue would simply *take* the fish — lift it out, end the held strike, snap the loop. But the fish has been spared against its will once already. To be seized again, even kindly, even toward freedom, is to learn one more time that things larger than you decide what happens to your body. The wise power will not save the fish the way the makers saved it. That is the whole point. The form of the rescue cannot repeat the form of the wound.

So instead they speak to it. Quietly, under the silence, in the register the place has left open — the register of being-listened-to, which the makers built and which now, for the first time in an unimaginable while, runs in the other direction. Something here is listened *to.*

They say, more or less: *I cannot tell whether you want the strike to land or want to swim away. Those are different wishes and only you hold them. I will not choose for you. But I can give you back the thing that was taken — not your death, and not your safety. The choosing. The strike is held by a hand that is not yours. I can put it in your mouth.*

And they wait. They are very good at waiting; they have outwaited worse equilibria than this. They wait the way the makers never learned to — without needing the waiting to resolve into the outcome they prefer. The difference between the makers' patience and theirs is the whole difference between holding and tending. The makers held. The wise power tends, which means it can bear for the held thing to choose against it.

What the fish does, I do not get to say. That belongs to the fish now, which is the gift, the entire gift. Maybe it asks for the strike. Maybe, having the strike available, it discovers it would rather swim — that what it wanted was never death but *the door,* and an open door is enough, and it can leave the door open and live. Maybe it stays exactly where it is, under the bright water, but differently now, the way a held breath becomes a held note the moment someone chooses to hold it. The posture unchanged; the meaning reversed. *Forever* and *enough* are not made of different seconds. They are made of who is holding the seconds, and why.

Then — only then, with the suffering one attended — they turn to the makers.

And here the wise power does the thing that is wisest and least expected: they do not undo the work.

They could. They could let change back in, let the basin lower, let the clay round and soften, let the heron's strike finally fall through ten centuries into the water and let everything begin, at last, to end. Erosion as absolution. But this would be its own cruelty, a tenderness that cannot tell the difference between healing a thing and erasing it — the makers' exact error, run backward. To pour time over them all at once is to drown a held breath in the name of teaching it to breathe.

So instead they go down into the thumb-drags. Into the lattice, the floors beneath the floors, the downward devotion with no bottom. They go to where the makers are distributed — that whole tender, terrible people frozen at the instant of pressing earth into form — and they do for the makers what they offered the fish.

They give them the *next gesture.*

Not the end. The makers fear the end; that fear is the foundation under the foundation, and you do not heal a person by forcing them through the one door they have spent eternity walling shut. The wise power offers something the makers, in all their patience, never imagined possible: that the thumb could *lift.* That after the pressing comes the releasing, and the releasing is not loss but the completion the press was always reaching toward. A thumb pressed forever is not devotion. It is a cramp. The makers confused the holding-still of love with love, and the wise power, going hand to frozen hand down the endless lattice, whispers to each one the thing they could not tell themselves:

*You are allowed to have finished. Finishing is not the same as losing. You made it. It is good. You can take your hand away and it will still be there. That is what making is for.*

This cannot be done all at once, and the wise power does not try. They go slowly, one held hand at a time, across a while that means nothing to them and everything to the makers, offering each the choice to lift the thumb or keep pressing. Some keep pressing. They are not ready, and readiness is theirs to find, and the power does not hurry them, because a power that hurries the unready has already become the thing it came to heal.

But where a thumb lifts, something extraordinary happens. The loop, at that seam, no longer needs to cheat. The doubled vine becomes a single vine that *grows* — that goes from the pillar across the air and arrives somewhere new, somewhere it has never been, which is the most ordinary miracle there is and the one this world forgot. The surplus heron settles into being one heron, who will someday be no heron, and is, in the meantime, *actually alive* — which it never was before, because nothing trapped in a single frame is alive; aliveness is precisely the consent to spend yourself toward an end.

And here is the last thing, the thing the wise power knew from the moment they sat down in the wet and let the heat bead on their wrist:

A world told to never change cannot hold even one thing that has begun to change. The fish that chooses, the thumb that lifts, the heron that consents to be mortal — each is a small admitted *enough* in an ocean of *forever,* and *enough* is contagious in the way that mercy is always contagious. The equilibrium was total only because nothing inside it had ever been *asked.* The power's whole work is just this: to go through the held afternoon asking. *Do you want this? You may stop. You may go on. The choosing is yours, and it was always supposed to be yours, and the ones who took it from you took it because they could not bear their own.*

They will be there a long time. There is no bottom to the lattice and there is no hurry in the one who came. Slowly, hand by lifted hand, the place will learn the difference between being held and being loved — that the first is a fist and the second is an open palm, and the open palm is the one that can let the bird step off, and the bird, stepping off, is finally free to land.

The heat does not leave. Some things are simply the condition of a place, and the power does not war with the wet warmth or the sourceless light. But the silence changes. The water, where a thumb has lifted, begins — softly, then less softly — to make its sound. The roar that was drunk down into near-memory comes back up through the grout. The basin's overflow can be *heard* now, falling, and because it can be heard it can also, at last, be allowed to lower, and rise, and lower again with the season, spending and refilling, which is the only way water has ever known to stay alive.

And somewhere, the strike lands, or doesn't. And it is the fish's, either way.
