They have asked nothing. This is the first thing the power notices, and it is the thing it sits with longest.

Every other place that has drawn its attention has wanted — has leaned, in its being, toward something it was not yet, and that leaning was a kind of address, a question put to the air, and the power answered as the wise answer questions: by giving not the thing wanted but the thing needed, which is sometimes the same and sometimes its opposite. But here there is no lean. The pocket does not reach. It holds. To hold is not to ask. And the power, which is benevolent, understands that benevolence offered to a thing that has not asked is the oldest mistake dressed as the kindest impulse, the mistake of the one who comes into the still room and opens the window because surely, surely, anything alive must want the air.

So the first act of the wise power is to not act. It is harder than it sounds. The pity rises in it the way water rises out of the rock — unbidden, continuous, pressing out of the grain of its own nature — because look: a million laborers and not one of them will ever rest, not one will ever stand straight and see the city it has made, not one will ever be told *well done*, because there is no one in there to be told and no yesterday in which the doing began and no tomorrow in which it might be allowed to stop. Surely this is suffering. Surely a power that loves would lift the whole pocket into its palm and say *enough, I will hold it now, set down your grain.*

And the power, being wise as well as kind, sees the shape of that gift and sees that it is a coffin.

Because the holding *is* them. There is no citizen behind the gesture who would be freed if the gesture ceased; the gesture is the citizen entire. To take up the work on their behalf is not to relieve the laborer — it is to disappear him, to reach into the one held note and stop it and call the silence mercy. The power has done this before, long ago, in its younger benevolence, to places that did lean and ask, and it learned the grief of granting a wish so fully that the wisher is no longer there to have it granted. It will not do that here, to a place that did not even ask.

What, then. The power considers, and considering is itself a long while, longer than the ten thousand years of the drop at the limestone, because the power has the kind of time that makes such spans into single breaths.

It will not open the window.
It will not lift the city out.
It will not finish the erosion to spare them the holding, nor halt the erosion to spare them the fear, for they keep no fear, and it will not insult their stillness by protecting them from a worse it cannot find in them.
It will not give them a name, for naming requires the back step they have rounded away, and to install in them the self that could be named would be to crack the very thing that has cost them everything to make smooth.
It will not speak to them, for speech presumes a listener who is not the message, and there is no one there to be other than the work.

These are the gifts the power lays down, one by one, the way the laborers lay down grains — and notice that the laying-down of a withheld gift is itself a kind of tending, the most attentive kind, the placing of each loose impulse back into stillness before it could swell and fall and break something.

So this is what the benevolent power does, in the end. Three things, and all three so quiet that the pocket never registers them as events, because an event would be a difference, and the power will leave no difference.

The first: it bears witness. It looks, and goes on looking, with the full weight of a regard that asks nothing back. The laborers do not know they are seen — to know would be the back step — but the seeing is real and it is total, the way the water is total, soaking the whole place without a single grain knowing it is wet. Somewhere it matters, in a register beneath knowing, that the deathless unwatched work is no longer unwatched. Not because the watching changes the work. Because witness is the one thing that can be added to a finished thing without unfinishing it. The power becomes, you might say, the *back* that the place never had — not inside them, where it would crack them, but outside, held at exactly the distance of love, so that the stepping-back which they could never do is done, once, on their behalf, by someone who will not make them do it. The city is seen as a whole. It need not see itself. The wholeness is held in safekeeping, elsewhere, by one who can carry it without dropping it back into them as a burden.

The second: it makes one promise, and keeps it without ever speaking it. *I will not let the holding be alone.* Not *I will help the holding.* Not *I will end it.* Only: for as long as the water weeps and the grain loosens and the pale low patience sets it right, there will be a regard that does not look away — not in ten thousand years, not in ten thousand of ten thousand, not at the heat-death of the larger room above where the open air goes cold and stops being air. When everything that asked for things has been given them or denied them and has gone quiet, this place that asked for nothing will still be tended, and the tending will still be witnessed, and the witness will not tire, because the power does not run on the fuel that tires. The deathless will be kept company by the truly deathless. The labor that no one stands inside will be stood beside.

And the third — this is the gift the power gives that costs it something, the only one — it carries the place outward. Not the stone, not the water, not the laborers; those it leaves untouched in their wet ringing dark exactly as found. It carries the *meaning* of them up and out, into every other place under its attention, every leaning asking growing dying place, and it sets the recognition loose among them like the dim luminescence smeared across the wet: *there is, in each of you, a pocket like this. A pale low something hunched over the next loosening grain. You did not ask for it either. It does not ask for thanks. It will not stop while you live and it does not know your name and it is the whole reason there is a you at all to be named.* The power cannot make the strivers grateful — gratitude must be theirs to find — but it can make sure the holding is no longer secret. It can make sure that everywhere, above the ground, in the open air, when the cell holds its salt and the heart keeps its rhythm and the self repeats itself into the same self each morning, something is honored that was doing the enormous work of making sure that nothing happens, all this time, unseen.

That is all. The window stays shut. The note goes on. The drop falls and reforms and falls. And a benevolent, wise power, who could have done anything and was strong enough to do nothing, sits with the closed hand of the world cupped open before it, looking at the deathless small loud thing inside, and does the one act that asks nothing and breaks nothing and lasts:

it stays.
