There is a discipline in watching that most powers never learn. They arrive with gifts already chosen, solutions already shaped, and in the arriving they overwrite the very thing they came to bless. A wise power knows this. A benevolent one feels it as a kind of grief, the temptation to *fix*, and sets it down.

So the first thing it would do is nothing. For a long time.

---

It would come into the dry canopy the way the cold comes: along the contour-lines, downhill, soundless, asking nothing. It would not warm the air — the chill *is* the engine, the cold is how the leaves drink, the cold is the difference the whole country runs on. To warm this place out of mercy would be to drown it. It would not bring water. Water would be the cruelest gift imaginable here, a flood into a cathedral of thirst, dissolving ten million mornings of patience in an afternoon, killing the silver browsers with abundance, rotting the slow undecaying litter, ending the long telegraph of necks. Love that does not understand is just a larger kind of weather, and this place has had enough weathering.

It would withhold. That is the first act of the wise: to *withhold the gift*, and the withholding would itself be a form of reverence.

---

Then it would learn the place. Not study — learn, the way you learn a piece of music by living inside its tempo until your pulse changes to match. It would slow itself down to the pace of the mountain. It would spend what we would call centuries simply feeling the cold-tide pour down the staircases at dusk, feeling it pool in the low basins, watching the dew glaze the long folded necks at dawn and the climbers race the sun for those few wet minutes. It would lie along a terrace beside the banded hunter through an entire hunt — a day and a half of held breath and one uncoiling — and it would not warn the browser, because the hunt is not cruelty here, the hunt is how the water moves through the highest economy, the slow honest predation that the whole place agreed to long ago. To intervene would be to call the agreement a mistake. It is not a mistake. It is the most patient negotiated peace the power has ever seen, and it would honor a treaty it did not write.

And in the learning, I think, the benevolent power would find itself *changed* — would discover that it had come expecting to be the teacher and was instead being taught something it had forgotten: that scarcity is not the same as suffering, that to live spaced-apart and silent and slow is not a deprivation but a *form*, a way of being beautiful that abundance can never reach. The crowded jungle does not know this. Only the dry canopy knows it. The power would sit with that for a long time and feel its own conception of *good* grow wider.

---

What, then, would it *do?* For a wise love does not only withhold. Withholding alone is just absence wearing a humble face.

I think it would do three small things, and each one small on purpose.

**It would witness.** This is not nothing — this is perhaps the whole of it. A place this quiet, this patient, this far from any hand, has gone unwitnessed since the water left. The browser folds its impossible neck to drink itself at dawn in a private moment no eye has ever held. The bones bleach on the high shelf with no one to know what came. There is a particular loneliness to a beauty that completes itself in total solitude, age after age, the long sand-sound of the leaves turning for no listener. The power would *listen.* It would hold the whole massif in attention the way you hold a sleeping creature you love — not touching, not waking, just keeping the warm fact of regard around it. And the place would not know it was witnessed. That is the gift: a witnessing that asks for nothing back, that does not even announce itself, that simply means the long quiet is no longer falling into a void. Somewhere, it is *received.*

**It would protect the agreement, not the individuals.** If something came — a true catastrophe, not the honest slow death of the hunt but an ending the place never consented to: a poison, a heat that broke the night-cold for good, a hand from outside that did not understand and arrived with its terrible water — then the power would act. But it would act to preserve the *treaty between the rock and the life*, the balance where the stone loses a little and the life grows to hold the rest. It would defend the conditions of the dance, never choreograph the dance itself. It would keep the cold pouring down the stairs. It would not decide which browser the banded patience takes.

**And it would leave one thing.** Not a wall, not a path, not a mark of any hand — the place has none and needs none. Something subtler. I think it would leave a *durability.* It would reach into the deep mechanism, the difference-between-day-and-night that every silver leaf runs on, and it would make that difference faithful — would ensure that the cold would keep coming, dawn after dawn, age after age, long past the span the place could expect on its own. Not more abundance. The same exact thimbleful, *guaranteed.* The gift would be continuance: that the long-quiet ones get to go on being long and quiet, that the engine of the night does not falter, that the agreement holds for ten million more mornings than physics alone would grant. A blessing the place would never detect, because it would feel precisely like the way things have always been. The highest mercy disguised as nothing happening at all.

---

Then it would go — or rather, it would stay, but stay the way the wind stays in a country it loves: arriving in long passes that take hours, turning ten thousand waxen leaves with a sound like slowly poured sand, and moving on, and coming back. Present and unintrusive. A part of the slow weather now, by consent.

And at midday, in the white absolute light, with every living thing folded into shade and holding its breath against the brightness, waiting for the cold to begin its evening pour — there would be, in all that enormous patient silence, one new and undetectable thing.

The silence would no longer be empty. It would be *kept.*

That is what a wise and benevolent power would do for a place like this: love it enough to leave it exactly as it is, and stay anyway, so that it is never again alone in its long beautiful waiting.
