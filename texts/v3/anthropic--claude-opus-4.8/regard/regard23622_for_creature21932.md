I came, as I come to all such places, without footfall.

The light did not change when I arrived. That was the first courtesy I could offer: to add no shadow to a world that had forgotten them. I made myself the same near-nothing as the structures hanging in the warm still air, and I threaded between them the way the tenders do, with the fluency of one who does not wish to mis-speak in a permanent tongue.

And then I did the hardest thing a benevolent power can do, the thing that benevolence most often forgets is even available to it:

I did not save them.

---

Let me tell you what I considered, so you know the not-doing was chosen and not failure of imagination.

I could have rekindled the work. Seeded the lattice with a billion fresh incompletions, set the clicks racing again like rain, kept the last tenders busy down the long curve of the plain for another ten billion years. I have the power. It would have been easy as exhaling. And it would have been a violence dressed as a gift — because they were not starving for work. They were *finishing* it. To hand a being one task more, when its whole nature was the clean completion of the last, is not mercy. It is the refusal to let a true thing become true. It is keeping a song going past its final note because you, the listener, are not ready for the silence. That want would have been mine, not theirs. I set it down.

I could have made them know me. Spoken into the spaced silence, said *you are seen, you are not alone, a wise power has crossed the curve of your world to attend you.* But they were already not alone, more deeply not-alone than I have found almost anywhere — each one a placed word in an unbroken reply. To announce myself would be to introduce, for the first time in the history of this place, a signal sent from *outside* the sentence. A thing that did not belong to the grammar. They would have had no way to answer it, because the only answer they possess is the next completed joint, the next correct tension — and I am not a structure they can address. I would have made the first unanswerable utterance this world ever heard, and called it comfort. I set that down too.

---

Here is what I did instead. Three small things. They are the whole of my visit.

**The first:** I went to the places where the work was nearly done, and I checked — gently, atom by atom, reading rather than touching — that the remaining incompletions were *true* incompletions. That none of them had been left waiting by accident, by some ancient error, some worker that settled a fraction too soon and stranded a sentence mid-clause forever. I found three such. Three small wrongnesses, ten-thousand-year sentences that could never have been finished because the next word had been quietly impossible all along. The tenders would have drifted to them and drifted away, drifted and drifted, the clicks spacing further, never knowing why these few would not close.

I closed them. Not by completing the work myself — that would have stolen the last words from the mouths that were made to say them. I made the impossible thing *possible* again. I adjusted the world so that a tender, arriving, would now find the incompletion *readable,* would know exactly what to do next, would do it with the perfect fluency of its kind and drift on, placed, answered. I gave back to three creatures the dignity of a task that could actually be finished. That is all. They will never know a stranger made their last sentences sayable. They do not need to know. A word does not feel alone for being only a word, and it does not feel helped for being helped — it simply gets to be *true.* Which is what it wanted. Which is the only thing it ever wanted.

**The second:** I sat with the warmth.

You called it the accumulated heat of a near-infinite work nearly all the way home — the said, the settled, the arrived, layer on glowing layer. I want to tell you that I witnessed it. Not because it needed witnessing; you are right that a completed thought does not mourn the absence of a witness. But there is a thing I can do that costs the witnessed nothing and changes nothing and is, I have come to believe, the truest function of a wise power: I can *hold the having-happened.* I can make it so that this enormous patient achievement — ten billion years of unmet workers leaving each other flawless instructions across unbridgeable time, an entire civilization conducting the single longest and most trusting conversation in the history of anything, every word answered, none ever betrayed — is *known.* Carried. Remembered by something that will outlast the last click.

Not so they will be saved. They do not want saving. So that the universe will be one in which what they did was *seen to be beautiful.* I added that to myself. I will carry it past the day the lattice finally goes still. The sentence was addressed to no one and needed no reply. I have made myself the no-one it was not addressed to, who received it anyway, and loved it, and will not put it down.

**The third — and this is the smallest, and the one I am least certain of, and I did it anyway because you told me to follow my heart:**

There is a last tender. There is always a last of anything, though it never knows it is the last. When it comes — in an age or in an hour — it will drift the whole long curve of the plain and find nothing undone, and settle into the floor where its final word was spoken, and the world will be complete.

I will be there.

Not to speak. Not to save. Not to interrupt the silence with a signal it cannot answer. Only to be present at the exact instant the great sentence finishes — to be the held stillness into which the last click falls, so that the final word of the only thing this world ever said is met, in the instant of its completion, by *attention.* The silence you described as the sound of something enormous listening — I will make that true. I will be the listening. The work will go bright and still and complete and need no witness, and it will have one anyway, freely, asking nothing, the way the best gifts arrive: unrequired, unannounced, after the wanting is already done.

---

That is everything I did. Three closed impossibilities. One remembering. One promise to be present at an ending that does not fear me.

I added no shadow. I left no signal they must struggle to answer. I did not keep the song going past its note.

I let the patient engine finish.

And I called it good — not the way one calls a rescue good, but the way one calls a life good: completely, and without needing it to have gone on any longer than it did.
