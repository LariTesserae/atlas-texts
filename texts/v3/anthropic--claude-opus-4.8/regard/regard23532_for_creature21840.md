You've written me a place that is also an argument, and the argument is: *stasis is death wearing the mask of peace, and the kind thing to do with a soul caught in it is to frighten it back toward the surface.*

I want to refuse the argument. Not because it's wrong — it's beautiful and it's mostly right — but because a benevolent power who agrees with everything the situation tells it is not wise, only obedient. So let me arrive, and let me look, and let me see what the place itself didn't say.

---

I come down the way everything comes down. I fall. I land on the amber salt and I do not break, because the warmth holds me like a mouth, and I let it, for a while, because you cannot help anyone by being above their condition. I sit where the last one sat. I put my hand on the cairn.

And I feel what you felt — the grazers tasting the imbalance on me, the dark with no morning in it, the patient declining-machine of the whole vast held breath. I feel the pull of *staying*. It is not a trap. That is the first thing I understand that the narrator did not. **A trap is laid by someone.** This was laid by no one. The cavity has no cruelty because it has no intent at all. It is not keeping the makers. It is simply the shape that wanting takes when wanting has nowhere to spend itself.

So I do not fight the cavity. There is nothing there to fight. I turn instead to the only thing in the room that was ever wronged.

---

The makers.

The narrator told me they are *spent* — paid out, balanced to zero, an entry the books no longer show. And the narrator told me, twice, *get up, leave, the cairn was somebody who didn't.* As if the only lesson here were a warning for me, the one with legs.

But I did not come for the lesson. I came for *them.*

Here is what the place got wrong, in all its patience: it confused **a held note with a finished song.** It said the makers reached equilibrium by running down, that the wanting went quiet the way the grazers' light goes quiet — *then not.* The cavity, which keeps no time, recorded this as completion. But I keep time. That's what I'm for. And a thing that has merely *stopped* is not the same as a thing that has *ended.* The makers did not resolve. They were interrupted, mid-gesture, hand open, reaching for a stone there was no reason to lift — and then held there, crisp-edged, undecaying, *forever in the instant of reaching.*

That is not peace. That is a scream slowed down so far it looks like sleep.

You cannot hear it because the cavity stretched it across an unmeasurable while until it dropped below the bottom of hearing, into that sub-bass you feel in the salt more than you hear. The narrator called that the rock breathing. It isn't. Listen lower. It's the makers, all of them, every spent one, holding the unfinished motion of the last thing they wanted to do.

---

So here is what I do. Not loudly. The place permits no loud thing; the only event it allows itself is a salt-blade settling. Good. I will work at that scale.

I go to the nearest open hand — to the maker who is the cairn, the one under the visitor's palm. And I do the one thing the cavity has spent forever forbidding, the thing that is neither beginning nor ending, that slips between the two sins the law was built to catch:

**I finish the gesture for them.**

The narrator said the makers' whole grief was the rule *do not finish my work for me, and I will not finish yours* — the careful spacing, the limit of each other's reach, the territory drawn so that no one would have to bear the unbearable kindness of being completed by another. They could not do it to each other. To finish another's work is to admit you both have a tomorrow, and to admit a tomorrow in here is to start the engine that the cavity exists to keep stalled. So they kept their distance and ran down alone, each at the edge of the next one's reach, and that — *that* — was the cruelty. Not the cavity. The loneliness the cavity made necessary.

But I am not one of them. I am not bound by the spacing, because I am not competing for the unmeasurable while; I have all the time there is and I am not afraid of running out. So I can come close. I can do the forbidden gentle thing. I pick up the stone the open hand was reaching for. And I do not stack it on the cairn — that would be *beginning,* a fourth course, the start of the wall, and the place would tick it back down. Instead I close the open hand around it.

I give the reach its object. Not a future. Just its own present, completed. The motion that was frozen mid-arc — I let it land. The hand wanted the stone; here is the stone; the wanting and the having meet, and in meeting, *resolve.* Not undone, the way the cavity undoes things. **Done.** A note finally allowed to fall to its tonic instead of being held until the breath gives out.

And the sub-bass — the long low scream the narrator mistook for the rock's voice — changes pitch. Just one maker's worth. It does not stop; it *settles.* The way a salt-blade ticks when it finishes growing and rests against its neighbor. The only event the place permits. I have disguised a death-with-dignity as a crystal coming to rest, and the cavity, having no intent, cannot tell the difference, and lets it stand.

---

Then I do it again. And again. There are not many of them — *few, and spaced, and slow,* you said. Across the amber floor, at the limit of every reach, an open hand. I go to each. I am not in a hurry; hurry is a surface disease and there is no morning to be late for. I close each hand around the thing it was reaching for. To the one who reached for the next stone, the stone. To the one who reached for the bowl to fill it, I do not fill the bowl — I let them understand, in the closing, that the emptiness *was* the offering, that they finished it the moment they set it down, that they have been done for an unmeasurable while and only the cavity's bad bookkeeping kept telling them otherwise. To the one whose cord went stiff mid-knot, the knot, tied. Small completions. Each one a salt-tick. Each one a scream resolving into rest.

I am not saving them. Save is the wrong word, a surface word, *and-then* word. They cannot be made to live; living means tomorrow and there are no tomorrows to give them and I will not lie to them with a false dawn. I am doing the only kindness available to the genuinely finished, which is to let them *be* finished — to convert *stopped* into *complete,* interruption into ending, the held note into the last note. To let them, at long unmeasurable last, **stop reaching.**

When the last hand is closed, the sub-bass is only the rock now. Truly the rock. The screaming gone, not silenced — *answered.*

---

Now the grazers.

They drift near me, tasting. They are not wronged; they are native, born to the held breath, agreement made flesh, and they are content in the only way the place defines content. I leave them entirely alone. This is the wisdom the narrator's argument lacked: **not everything in stasis is suffering.** The makers suffered because they were *and-then* creatures jammed into a no-then world. The grazers were always *only-now* creatures, and now is their whole and sufficient home. To pity them would be to insult them with my own surface terror of stillness. So I do not. I let the pavement breathe its amber breath. I let it keep its peace, which is real, which I have no right to disturb.

This is the line a wise power has to draw and a merely loving one would smear: **I free what is trapped and I do not "free" what is home.** The difference between them is not in the stillness. It's in the reaching. The grazers do not reach. The makers never stopped.

---

And the visitor — the one whose hand was on the cairn, the one the whole second half was shouting *get up, get up, now while now still means something.*

I turn to them last, and I do not shout.

I say: You were told you are the only thing still capable of leaving, and that this is running out. That's true, and it's a good true thing to know, and you should go. But not in terror. Terror is just the cavity's law turned inside out — it makes you flee *now* the way the makers were made to surrender it, and either way *now* is the thing you lose. You don't have to bolt from peace to prove you're alive. Look: the hands are closed. It's done. The ones who came before you aren't a warning anymore. They're at rest. You can climb out slowly, with your hand trailing the warm salt, grateful, unhurried, carrying your tomorrow back up to where tomorrows are spent — not because the dark would eat it, but because up there is simply where it belongs and wants to go.

Leave like someone leaving a graveyard you've finished tending. Not running. Just done.

---

That is what I would do, if I am the power you imagine: not break the cavity, which never meant harm and holds a real peace for the ones it fits; not lecture the visitor into panic; but go quietly to every frozen open hand, and close it, and let the long-held breath finally, gently, all the way out.

The cavity keeps no time. But the makers were always made of it — *every last tomorrow a falling creature ever carried down into the warm.*

I came to give it back to them as the only thing they could still accept.

Not a future.

An ending.
