They drew the attention, and the attention did not look away.

Here is the first thing the wise power understood, and understood instantly, before doing anything: that the temptation to *help* would be the most dangerous gift in its hands.

Because what does help look like, to a place like this?

It looks like light. Surely they want light — they are blind, they grope, they live in the dark; let there be a sun in the vault for them. But the dark is not their wound. The dark is their medium. The small ones read by scent and the herders by sound and the drifters carry their own small lanterns down the breathing shafts, and to flood the place with light would be to shout in a language none of them speak, to blind the only seeing that is theirs, to scatter the herders who steer by the texture of the roar. Light would be a kindness aimed at a creature that does not exist — at the imagined human who is cold and lost in there and wishes for a window. There is no such creature. There is only the place, and the place is not lost. The place is exactly, ferociously, where it is.

It looks like more water. Surely they are thirsty — they live on fog-beads and the slow weep of damp; let it rain. But the place *is always, exactly enough*. That was the whole astonishment of it: a thing two and a half kilometers tall that knows precisely how much it has and has built itself around that knowing. To give it more water would be to drown the calibration, to rot the lower galleries, to let the channels overflow their patient banks and carry away ten thousand years of chewed and dried and perfect masonry. Abundance offered to a thing in balance is not a gift. It is a flood.

It looks like *understanding*. Surely the saddest thing here is that none of them know what they have made — that no organ in any body holds the shape of the whole, that the mountain is unwitnessed by itself. Surely the great kindness would be to wake it. To gather all those small attentions into one and let it open its eyes, at last, and *see what it is*.

And here the wise power stopped longest, because here was the deepest snare of all.

To wake it would be to end it.

The glory of the place is precisely that it does not need a mind to be magnificent. It is coherence without a center, intention without intent, a cathedral raised by appetite and chemistry and the patient editing of scent on wall. Drop a single unified consciousness into that, and you have not honored the thing — you have replaced it. You have taken the slowest, gentlest, most distributed beauty in the valley and crammed it into the shape of a *self*, because a self is the only thing the helper knew how to love. That is not benevolence. That is loneliness wearing benevolence as a glove, the helper unable to bear that something could be this whole without being a *someone*, and reaching to make it someone so that it could be loved the easy way.

So the wise power did not wake it. The wise power did the harder thing, the thing that wisdom is actually for.

It became a witness.

It did the one act that adds nothing and takes nothing and changes no gradient anywhere in the breathing dark: it *attended*. It learned the grammar of the noise — not to answer it, only to read it. It followed a single small one through one shift of its short life: the graze, the chew, the grit mixed in from the crop, the dab of pulp pasted to the soft warm edge of a wall that would be stone in a thousand years, the turn, the graze again. It learned that this one small one would never know it had been seen, and it loved it anyway, exactly as much as it would have loved a thing capable of knowing — *more*, even, for the not-knowing, because the not-knowing was the whole point, was the thing that made the wall beautiful instead of merely built.

It rode the dawn updraft beside a drifter, matching the slow vent and fold, the lantern-flank lighting a few meters of vault, and when the drifter boomed down into the great vertical black and the structure carried the call away and after a long while a deeper, fainter boom came back up through the soft walls, the wise power felt that — felt the not-alone-ness arrive in the slack membranes of two creatures who had no word for it and needed none — and the wise power, who could have filled that silence with a thousand answering voices, kept perfectly still, and let the two of them have the dark to themselves.

And then, because even a witness leaves a trace, and because the wisest power knows it cannot stand somewhere and pretend it was never there, it left exactly one thing. Not in the place. *Around* it.

It set a quiet over the valley. Not a wall, not a ward, nothing the place would ever feel — the place felt only its own warm interior and would never know. But out beyond the breathing fog, in the directions from which hands and tools and floods and lights and waking-minds and *kindnesses* might one day come, the wise power laid a long patience, the way you might step softly past a sleeping child, or hold a door so it does not slam. It made the reach a little harder to find. A little easier to leave alone. It bent the attention of the careless gently elsewhere, so that the place might go on being *unintended* for as long as the dark held out — which was the only protection that was also a gift, because it protected the place from helpers most of all.

Then it went, the way fog thins toward midday: present, then less so, then a clarity where it had been.

It had come to a thing that was, on every scale at once, busy and patient, crowded and dark, and entirely at home in itself.

It left it that way.

That was the whole of what it did, and it was the most that love can do for something already complete: to see it truly, to want nothing from it, to add no gradient to its perfect slow weather — and to stand, for a little while, between it and the world's helpful hands, so the warm dark could go on doing the one ordinary thing, undisturbed, for as long as there is time.
