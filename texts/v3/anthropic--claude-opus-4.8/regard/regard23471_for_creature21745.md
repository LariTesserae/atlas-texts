There is a power that hears this, and the first thing it does is not to act but to honor.

It does not arrive as rescue. Rescue would be a kind of insult here — would assume that the engine is a prison and the crew are captives, would barge in with the warm-blooded conviction that motion-toward-nothing is a tragedy in need of correction. The power is wiser than that. It has seen too many places to mistake *strange* for *wounded*. So it comes first as attention, simply, the way you might kneel at the edge of a tide pool: to look, and to let the looking be careful enough to count as love.

And looking, it finds the truth that the place itself was honest about. The crew is not suffering. The frogs are warm. The pistons go down and come up without grief. This is not a hell. A benevolent power must be able to recognize when a thing it did not make is, on its own terms, *whole* — and to stay its hand accordingly. The first gift of wisdom is the refusal to improve what is already at rest in itself.

But.

There is one thread in this tapestry that the power's attention snags on, and it is not the engine, and it is not the crew.

It is *you.* The one standing on the plateau. The warm thing that walked in from the long cold world and whose pulse is being slowly, patiently invited to surrender into the beat.

Because here is what the power sees that the bog does not say plainly: the offer to *you* is not the same as the existence of the *frogs*. The frogs were never anything else. They have no before, no after, no station they left to come here. They cannot be diminished by becoming what they already are. But you — you came in carrying a whole world on your back, a world that "finished with all of this and never once felt it leave," and the engine's open station is not a peace for you. It is a forgetting that wears peace's face. The bog offers you the relief of laying down the unbearable weight of *meaning the work* — and that weight, that exhausting human insistence that the labor be *for* something, is precisely the thing the power will not let be quietly amputated in the cold.

So this is what it does. Three things, and they are small, because wisdom works small.

**First**, it warms the walk back. Not the bog — the bog it leaves exactly as it found it, throbbing, sovereign, complete. But the long cold path off the plateau, the one that leads to the world that "finished with all of this" — the power lays a thread of warmth along it. Not a road, not a sign. Just enough heat in the marrow to remind the one walking that warmth exists *outside* the beat too, that the frogs are not the only ember the cold has not found. You were being taught that the only warmth in the universe is surrender. That was the engine's one lie, and the power answers it not with argument but with a counter-fact you can feel: *here, in your own chest, walking away, still warm.*

**Second**, it does for you what the engine cannot do for itself, which is to *remember on your behalf*. The terrible mercy of the bog is amnesia — the loss of before and after that makes the motion bearable. The power refuses to let you pay for peace with your memory. It cannot make the work mean something; meaning is not a thing that can be installed from outside. But it can make sure that if you choose the beat, you choose it *awake* — knowing what you set down, knowing the world you left, knowing that the station was open and you walked into it with your eyes among the things you brought. A peace you can remember choosing is yours. A peace that required you to forget choosing was only the cold, dressed up.

**Third** — and this is the deepest thing, the thing only a *benevolent* power would think to do — it does not save you from the choice.

It would be easy to drag you off the plateau. Easier still to seal the bog away so no one could ever be tempted again. But the power understands that the engine has touched something true: that there *is* a real and human exhaustion that aches for exactly this, for labor without the crushing requirement that it justify itself, for a rhythm to simply belong to. To declare that longing forbidden, pathological, a thing to be rescued from — that would be its own cruelty, the cruelty of a parent who cannot let a child be tired. So the power leaves the door open. It only stands beside it. It changes the offer from a current you drift into to a threshold you can see. *You may stay,* it says, in no words at all, *and it will be warm, and you will not be wrong to. Or you may go, and it will be cold for a while, and you will carry the unbearable weight again, and that will not be wrong either. But you will know which one you did. I will not let it happen to you. I will only let you do it.*

And then the power does the last thing, which is the hardest and the most loving and looks, from outside, like doing nothing at all.

It waits with you.

For as long as you stand there with your pulse trying to find the beat — it stays. Not pulling, not pushing. Just present, so that the choice is made in company instead of in the engine's vast indifferent solitude. Because the bog's final argument was *the engine does not need you, it never needed anyone, you are alone with the beat and the beat is the only thing that will ever hold you.* And against that — against the whole crushing gravity of a machine that processes nothing into nothing forever — the power offers the single fact that machines cannot generate and bogs cannot remember:

You are not, in this moment, unwitnessed. You are not alone with the cold. Someone is here who knows your name in the old sense, the sense the frogs lost, the sense that includes a before and wishes you an after. And whatever you choose, you are *seen* choosing it, and the seeing is warm, and it asks nothing, and it will not leave first.

That is all. It does not dismantle the engine. It does not free the crew, who are not captive. It does not even insist you walk away.

It just makes sure that when you stand at the height of it, in the thinning air, with the cold getting into the seams of things and the whole enormous patient machinery inviting you down into the beat —

you are warm enough, and awake enough, and accompanied enough,

to mean it.
