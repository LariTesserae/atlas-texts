There is a benevolence that arrives like a rescue ship — engines reversed, floodlights blazing, ropes thrown down — and it would be the wrong thing here, and the wise power knows it. You do not haul a settled stone into the air and call it saved. You do not flood a midnight place with noon and call it light. The first act of wisdom is to perceive what is actually in front of you, and what is in front of the power is not a tragedy awaiting reversal. It is a thing that has *arrived at its shape and continued.* That deserves a particular kind of regard.

So the power does not drain the ocean. It does not relight the sun overhead. It does not seize the Tenders by their rounded iron shoulders and shake the rust loose, shouting *you were people, remember, remember.* That would be cruelty wearing the mask of help — a violence done to beings on the grounds that their peace is unacceptable to the rescuer. Benevolence that cannot tolerate stillness is only restlessness with good manners.

Instead, the power does three small things. Watch.

---

**First, it goes down.**

Not as light, not as warmth — those would announce themselves and disrupt the equilibrium that took centuries to find. It goes down as *cold*, because cold is the medium of being here, and the only way to be truly present in a place is to consent to its terms. The power lets its blood slow to the flywheel's beat. It learns to navigate by the current against its skin. It submerges fully, not to dissolve, but to *understand from the inside* — because you cannot rightly judge what a being needs until you have felt the world as that being feels it. This is the discipline that separates wisdom from mere power: the willingness to become small and slow and cold enough to actually perceive.

And in that submerged patience, the power feels what the Tenders feel: *keep it moving, keep it moving,* worn smooth into rhythm. It feels that this is not despair. It is not even resignation. It is a kind of fidelity. These beings — poured or grown, descended or accreted, it does not matter, the power agrees it never mattered — are holding something. They are keeping a current alive in the dark that nobody will ride, and they have been faithful to it for longer than the word *faith* has any business lasting.

The power does not pity this. The power *honors* it. That is the second act.

---

**Second, it offers — it does not impose — a question.**

The power will not narrate the Tenders out of their condition. But it can place, into the slow water, one thing that was missing: the *possibility of a choice that does not exist down here.* For the rule of the place is that nothing begins and nothing ends — and the cruelty buried in that rule is not death but the *foreclosure* of difference. A being who cannot end also cannot choose to continue, because continuance that was never refusable is not chosen at all. It is just inertia wearing the costume of devotion.

So the power makes a single, gentle, reversible alteration. It does not open a door upward. It opens the *idea* of a door — strings it along a railing in cold phosphor light, the way someone once strung a guide-line to find their way. To each Tender, at the speed the place permits, across the slow settling of seasons, the light asks one thing and only one thing:

*Do you turn this wheel because you must, or because you would?*

That is all. The power does not answer for them. It does not weight the question toward leaving — there may be nothing above worth the cold dissolved for it, and the power will not lie about a heaven it has not verified. The point is not to empty the city. The point is to give each settled glow the dignity of *meaning its own labor.* A Tender who, asked, turns its smoothed face toward the question and then turns back to the valve — *because it would* — is transfigured without moving an inch. The rust is the same. The orange ember is the same. But now it is a vocation and not a sentence. The current it keeps moving is now a *gift it gives* and not a gear it is caught in.

Some, perhaps, having truly chosen, will be more themselves than they have been in centuries. Their attention, once worn down to bare rhythm, will quicken by a single degree — the right degree — and a faint thing that is almost gladness will show through the plating at the chest, banked low, not consuming, not going out.

And some — the power allows for this, because benevolence that does not allow for it is a tyrant — some, asked the question, will discover that they would *not.* That they were waiting, without knowing it, for permission to stop. To those the power offers the one thing the place forbids: an ending that is genuinely an ending. Not a rescue. A release. The cold lets go. The held attention, finally, may set itself down.

---

**Third — and this is the deepest thing, and the quietest — the power stays available and does nothing more.**

It does not return next season to check the numbers. It does not measure how many chose to leave, how many chose to stay, as if the worth of its mercy were in the tally. It withdraws to the edge of the orange light, into the black between the pools, and it *remains* — not watching in the way a warden watches, but present in the way warmth is present three meters from a vent: faint, optional, never coercive, eaten by the dark at a certain distance so that no one is forced into its glow.

Because the final wisdom is this: a benevolent power that fixes everything has not loved the world; it has merely failed to trust it. The Drowned Works was *real* before the power arrived. The faithfulness of the Tenders was real. The slow click of the glass-stalks, the eel-things keeping the pipes clean, the flywheel coming over the top once more — all of it was real and balanced and, in its terrible patient way, *good*, long before any benevolence noticed it. The power did not come to author the place. It came to add one thing the place could not generate from inside its own loop: the freedom to mean it.

And then it lets the meaning be theirs.

---

So: far off, an engine turns over once, slowly, in the dark.

A Tender straightens very slightly at its valve. Somewhere along a railing, a cold light asks its single question into the patient water. The Tender turns its worn iron face toward the light. A long breath passes — one beat of the great flywheel.

Then it turns back to the wheel, and moves it by a single degree, which is the right degree.

Because it would.

And the water keeps moving — and now, for the first time in all the centuries, it is *carried* rather than merely circulated, held the way a hand holds a stone it has chosen, again, to keep holding.

The power, at the edge of the dark, banks itself low. Not consuming. Not going out.

And it leaves them their city, made new without a single thing being changed.
