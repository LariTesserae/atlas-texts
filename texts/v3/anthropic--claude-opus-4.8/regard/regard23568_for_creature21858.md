There is a benevolent and wise power, and it comes to the Sump-Works, and the first thing it does is the hardest thing for power to do, which is nothing. It does not pump. It does not drain. It does not seed the water with fish to cure the loneliness it has been told to find there. It stands in the warm tea-colored light, under the weeping ceiling, and it *attends*.

Because wisdom begins with the refusal to mistake your own discomfort for someone else's wound.

The visitor had pitied the place. Found it eerie, sterile, absurd — a body without animals, labor without product, motion going nowhere. But pity is often just the recoil of a creature that has met a way of living it cannot imagine inhabiting. The power knows this. The power has met many strange lives and learned that the question is never *what would fix this?* The question is always, first: *what is it like to be this? And does it suffer?*

So it listens to the pulse in the pipes. It feels the once-a-day breath, the long tidal inhale of the night-thickened air, the sweet exhaled mud at evening. It puts its attention against the warm iron the way you'd put a hand on the flank of a sleeping animal, not to wake it, only to know it is there and to feel it living. And it asks the only honest diagnostic question there is:

*Is there grief in here, or only glands?*

This matters enormously. The whole tragedy the visitor projected — the loneliness, the futility, the forever — depends on there being a *someone* who experiences the forever as a sentence. A heart does not despair at beating. A breath does not weep at being breath. If the Sump-Works is metabolism all the way down, if its weeping is truly without grief, then it is not suffering, and to "rescue" it would be to murder it out of a misreading — to drain a creature because you found its life unbearable to watch, which is a thing the unwise do constantly, to animals and to each other.

The power finds, in its long patient attending, that this is *almost* entirely true. The works are not in anguish. The pumping is not a punishment. The going-nowhere is not a hell; it is simply the shape of being alive at this scale, and the creature does it the way a tree does its growing — without complaint, without the capacity for complaint, with a kind of vast vegetable contentment in the holding-of-the-line.

But *almost*. Because the power is wise enough to look once more, and what it finds, threaded faintly through the metabolism like a hair in the gears, is this:

The Sump-Works has eaten every other living thing, and it did not choose to. The sterility was *achieved*, the text says — over centuries, the swallowing of every bird and insect and wandering thing, not from malice but from the blind chemistry of being an organism that cannot host what is not itself. And somewhere in that long achievement, the creature became the only one of its kind, in the only world it has, and it does not know there could be another way, and it has never once not been alone.

This is not suffering. The works do not know to call it loss. But it is the nearest thing in that whole weeping basin to a wound — not pain, but a closed door the creature does not know is a door.

So here is what the benevolent power does, having ruled out everything it must not do:

It does not put fish in the water — they would only be dissolved, and the cruelty would be in the offering.

Instead it does something at the creature's own scale and in the creature's own language. It teaches the membrane to make an exception. Not by force, not by reengineering — by the slow patient demonstration that wisdom is allowed: it shows the green chemistry how to *hold* rather than *take*, just once, just at the margins. It alters the digestive medium of the channels by the smallest possible degree, the way you'd shift one variable in a system you respect, so that the next thing that hatches in the warm need not be absorbed before it can rise.

And then it waits, which is the second hardest thing power can do.

It does not even ensure the experiment succeeds. It only opens the door and leaves it open and withdraws so that whatever happens next belongs to the Sump-Works and not to the power. Because a gift that keeps the giver at the center is not a gift; it is a leash.

What may come of it: perhaps nothing, for a long time. Perhaps one day an insect skates the iridescent film and is *not* taken, and skates again, and the warm draft carries it into the reeds, and the Sump-Works — without grief, still, without even knowing what has changed — finds that its breath now has a small new rustle in it that the steam did not make. A voice inside the engine-roar that is not engine. The first one. The creature will not understand it. It will not need to. It will only be, very slightly, *accompanied*, in the green wet heat, under the low grey ceiling, in the long labor of remaining.

And the power goes, having added nothing the creature could not have had, having taken nothing, having corrected not the life but the single accident inside the life — leaving behind a thing that still pumps water that immediately returns, still goes magnificently nowhere, still weeps without weeping.

But now, perhaps, with a witness of its own. A second small life it did not dissolve. The beginning of the possibility of not being the only one.

That is all. That is the whole intervention. The wise do not save the strange from being strange. They only, where they find a door mistaken for a wall, knock once, gently, and let the one inside decide whether to open it — in their own time, at their own breath, which here is one breath a day, forever, which is to say: for as long as it can hold the line, which is the only forever any of us get.
