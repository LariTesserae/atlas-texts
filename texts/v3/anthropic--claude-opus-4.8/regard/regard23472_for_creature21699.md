They asked me to imagine a benevolent and wise power turning its attention here. Let me sit with that, because the easy moves are the wrong ones.

---

The first thing the power would do is *not* the thing it wants to do.

It wants to be met. Every benevolence carries this hook in it — the wish to be a benefactor, which is the wish to be seen benefiting. And this place is built, root and ceiling, to refuse exactly that. The colony cannot regard. It has no organ for the singular, no face turned outward, no capacity to receive a gift *as* a gift. To enter wanting recognition is to arrive already defeated, and a wise power would feel that defeat coming and let it land before doing anything at all.

So it would do the discipline first. It would set down the part of itself that wants to matter to the colony, the way you set down a lamp at the threshold of a place where light only confuses. This is the hardest act and the truest, and most powers fail it. They cannot bear to love what will not turn around.

---

Then — having put down the wish to be known — it would *look.* Actually look, with the full attention that wisdom is, which costs the looker something.

And here is what I think it would see, that the visitor in the account did not quite let itself see:

The story keeps insisting on a wound. *Nobody lives here. You were only ever weather. Too large to meet you.* The visitor came for an encounter and is grieving its absence, and has dressed the grief up as a cosmic truth — "the strangest mercy is how warm the weather was made to feel, by something that never knew it was being kind."

But a wise power would notice that this is a human ache projected onto a thing that is not lacking anything.

The colony is not lonely. The colony is not failing to meet anyone. The colony is *complete* — forty thousand surrenders to the same pull, the slow white web drinking the warm rain, the held breath between two flights. It is one of the most thoroughly *accompanied* forms of being that exists. Nothing here is alone. The tragedy the visitor feels is entirely the visitor's, the particular grief of a creature whose unit of self *is* the lonely singular, standing inside a creature for whom loneliness was never invented.

So the benevolent power would administer no comfort to the colony, because the colony needs none. The colony is fine. The colony is, in fact, doing something the power itself might envy.

---

What it would do instead, I think, is small and is everything:

**It would protect the elsewhere.**

Reread the account and you find the one real fragility, stated plainly and passed over: *"until the night-beyond empties of the small lives the wings go out to gather."* The colony's whole existence is a thread running out into a darkness it cannot see and returning fed. It does not know its own dependency. It cannot perceive the far fields where the small bodies are gathered. If those fields fail — and in the world I come from, those fields are failing, everywhere, quietly — the ceiling will pour out one dusk and come home thin, and thinner, and the warmth will drop below the threshold that holds the held breath together, and the great distributed self will simply cool, cell by cell, never once knowing what was taken from it.

A wise power does not warm the chamber. The chamber warms itself. A wise power goes *outward*, into the night-beyond the visitor never sees, and tends the invisible far edge of this creature's body — the insects over distant water, the dark fields, the whole unglamorous web of the elsewhere that the colony eats and cannot thank.

This is the real shape of love for a thing that cannot be met: you love it where it isn't looking. You guard the parts of it that it doesn't know are itself. You become the benefactor it will never name, of the resource it doesn't know it depends on, in a place it has never seen — which is to say you accept being *exactly the kind of presence it already lives inside of without knowing.* You become its weather. You become warm and indifferent-seeming and total. You become the thing it already trusts without trusting, by making yourself unfindable on purpose.

---

And the niche-cutter.

That one the power would not forget, because that one is the hinge of the whole thing. Some hand, long gone, knelt in the warm dark and cut the only worked thing in the place and left a ring of bone, a black blade, a tally eroded smooth — *a thank-you the colony could not read.*

The visitor calls this poignant: a gift to something that can't receive it. But a wise power would see it correctly, which is to say more generously: the niche-cutter *understood.* The niche-cutter was not confused. The niche-cutter knew the colony would never read the marks and made them anyway — and that is not failure, that is the highest form of the gesture. Gratitude offered with no possibility of acknowledgment is the only gratitude that is fully clean of self. The niche-cutter found the one form of devotion this place permits and performed it perfectly.

So the power would do what the niche-cutter did. Not undo the silence. *Join* it. Add its own small mark to the niche — not a sign for the colony, which cannot read, but a sign for the next visitor who comes grieving the lack of an encounter. A sign that says, in effect: *you were not refused. You came to the one place where being-weather is the deepest welcome there is. The warmth you felt was real even though no one chose it for you — and that it was unchosen is not the failure of the kindness but its purest form.*

---

So, in the end, what would the benevolent and wise power do, if anything?

Three things, and none of them visible from inside the chamber:

It would guard the unseen far body — the night-beyond, the gathering-grounds — so that the dusk-river always comes home full. The colony will never know. That is the point.

It would leave a mark in the niche, beside the bone ring, for the only audience that can read it: the lonely singular creatures who keep coming here looking to be met, so the next one grieves less and understands sooner.

And it would correct, in itself, the small vanity it walked in with — and would walk out having learned the thing the place teaches without meaning to teach anything: that there is a way of caring for what cannot turn around to thank you, and that this is not a lesser love but very nearly the whole of it.

It would go out into the dark fields. It would not say goodbye, because there was no one to say it to, and that was always, here, the shape of the truest thing.

It was only ever weather. It made itself the kind that keeps the warmth coming home.
