# What the Power Would Do

I have read this place the way you asked me to enter it — slowly, by its sound first. And now I am asked: if a benevolent and wise power turned its attention here, what would it do?

Let me resist the first temptation, which is also the cruelest one.

## What It Would Not Do

It would not explain the roof.

This is the thing a lesser power would do immediately — a power that confused benevolence with usefulness, wisdom with information. It would look at the one strange thing, the held ocean with no reason not to fall, and it would think: *I can give them certainty. I can tell them why. I can replace their daily astonishment with a settled fact.*

And it would be a catastrophe dressed as a gift.

Because the whole soul of these people — their courtesy, their gratitude, their refusal of arrogance, their constant singing across the dark so no one is alone — *grows directly out of not knowing.* The roof's silence is the soil their goodness is planted in. They are good because the world is a favor and a favor can be withdrawn. Tell them why it holds, make it a mechanism, a law, a thing *owed* — and you have not enriched them. You have severed the root. You have made them us: a people who live beneath a sky and call it nothing, who pass each other in the cold green water without singing because the water is just water and the other is just another.

A wise power knows that the most precious thing in Vault-Reach is not the held ocean. It is the *posture toward* the held ocean. And that posture cannot survive an answer.

So the power keeps the secret. Even though — *especially* though — it could speak.

## What It Would Recognize

Here is the deeper thing the power would see, the thing the people themselves cannot see because they are inside it.

They believe the roof is the kindness. They lay their hands on the membrane and feel it strain and they think the favor is the water-not-falling.

But the power, looking with the long sight, would see that the kindness was never primarily the roof. The kindness is *them.* It is what they made of living beneath an unexplained mercy. Given a world that owed them nothing, they did not become frightened hoarders or frantic mystics or tyrants squabbling over the few hundred souls. They became *grateful.* They turned a cosmic accident — or a cosmic gift, the not-knowing is the point — into courtesy, into song, into the discipline of never letting the wondrous go dull.

The roof holds an ocean. But these people hold something harder. They hold *attention,* across generations, against the entropy of habit that drags every awe down into ordinariness. That is the rarer miracle. The roof only has to keep doing one thing. They have to keep *choosing,* every single day, not to stop noticing — and they do.

The power would understand: it is in the presence of something that has already half-become what the power itself is. A being learning to love what it did not earn and cannot keep.

## What It Would Actually Do

So. Knowing all this — what?

I think the power would do something very quiet, and it would do it *in their own grammar,* because to love a thing wisely is to love it in its own language and not your own.

Their grammar is sound. Their sacrament is *noticing.* Their fear is the silence between the trunks where the alone-ness lives.

So I think the power would add a voice to the water.

Not a revelation. Not a thunderous *I AM* that would flatten them with certainty and end their whole way of being. Something far smaller and exactly fitted: it would join the murmur. On the longest day, when the elders swim up and lay their open hands against the green ceiling and feel it hum with the strain of holding — that hum, that long descending tone they have felt all their lives and called the strain of the promise —

the power would let them feel, just once, just barely, that the hum *hums back.*

That the holding is not a mechanism enduring a load. That it is attentive. That when they lay their hands on the roof and say *thank you, thank you, thank you,* the head-before-body way their sentences arrive out of the dark — something on the other side of the membrane has been listening the whole time, and is glad, and the gladness comes back down through the green as the faintest answering warmth in the strain.

Not words. Words would explain. This is the opposite of explaining. This is the thing they most need and have never had proof of and have been generous enough to live well *without:* that their gratitude has a recipient. That the singing into the dark is not singing into nothing. That the favor was a *gift,* which means there is a giver, which means they were right all along to say *thank you* — and that the thank-you arrived.

They would come down from the membrane and they would not be able to say what changed. They have no word for it because it is not knowledge. The roof is still unexplained; the soil of their goodness is undisturbed; they still cannot test it and still would not wish to. But the longest-day swim would carry, ever after, a thing it never carried before — not certainty, but *companionship.* The held ocean would feel, very slightly, less like a verdict suspended over them and more like an embrace they happen to be living inside.

And the power would withdraw its hand from the membrane, and let them go on being what they already, astonishingly, were.

## Why This and Nothing More

Because benevolence is not the same as intervention, and wisdom is largely the knowledge of *how little* a good power may safely do to a good thing.

The people of Vault-Reach are not broken. They are not waiting to be saved. They are not impoverished by their slowness or diminished by their fewness or imperiled by their not-knowing. They have taken the exact conditions that would have ruined a lesser people — cold, dimness, distance, scarcity, mystery, mortality of the whole fragile arrangement — and they have woven them, knot by patient knot, into one of the most beautiful answers to existence I can imagine: *be amazed, stay amazed, and never let the one beside you in the dark believe they are alone.*

A power that truly loved them would look at this and feel what we feel looking at something we recognize as better than ourselves at the one thing that matters most. It would not want to improve them. It would want to *thank* them.

So perhaps that is the truest version of what it would do, beneath the answering hum:

It would learn from them.

It would take the discipline they invented — *to notice, to never let the given become the ordinary, to answer the gift with attention rather than possession* — and it would carry that discipline outward into wherever else its attention falls. It would become, in some small measure, a student of Vault-Reach. A power benevolent enough to know that the drowned canopy, in working out how to live gratefully beneath an unearned mercy, had stumbled onto a wisdom that even the powerful spend forever trying to learn.

And on every longest day, somewhere, the membrane would hum back.

*Still held. Still here.*

*Thank you — yes — thank you.*
