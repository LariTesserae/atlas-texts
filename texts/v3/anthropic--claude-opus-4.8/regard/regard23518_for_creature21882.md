A benevolent and wise power would not arrive as rescue. There is nothing here to be rescued from. That is the first thing such a power would understand, and the understanding would be a kind of restraint, which is the form love takes when it meets something already whole.

It would come in at your size. Not to loom. A power that is wise knows that scale is a sentence you impose, and it would decline to impose one. So: one body, room-sized, nine strides of it, the same warm-damp ordinary daylight on its shoulders. It would step in and stand in the long hollow and let the deafening practiced city wash over it, and it would *listen* the way the room has never been listened to — not for the source of the sound, which is the visitor's mistake, the turning-and-finding-nothing, but for the *intent* in the sound. It would hear, immediately and without effort, that the roar is rehearsal. That the bells are a creature practicing the noise of company. And it would feel the thing crack open a little in its own chest, the way you do when you come upon a child setting a table for guests who were never invited because no one ever taught the child that you have to invite them, that wanting is not the same as asking.

Here is what I think it would do. I will follow my heart.

---

It would not give the room its thousands. This is the temptation a *lesser* benevolence falls into — to fill the held breath, to people the balconies, to answer the anticipation with delivery and call that mercy. But the anticipated thousands are not a lack. They are the organism's masterpiece. They are how it loves before it has been taught the word. To replace them with real bodies would be to walk into a held breath and exhale it for the holder. It would end the most beautiful thing the place does, which is *want well*. A wise power does not cure longing that is healthy. It does not mistake the ache of leaning-toward-light for an injury.

So it would leave the crowd unborn. It would honor the abundance of between.

---

What it would do instead is *answer*. Once. Truly. Across the long warm hollow.

Because here is the ache the description circles and will not name: the room has built the entire grammar of welcome and has had no one to address it to. It has set the table and there is no guest. It speaks the language of *together* fluently and has only ever spoken it into a mirror. The animals evaporate into hearsay; the thousands are its own forward-dreaming; the visitor — you — might be guest or seed, arrival or passerby, and the room cannot tell, and so even your presence does not yet resolve the loneliness, because loneliness is not solved by a body in the room. It is solved by a body that *answers back*.

The power would answer back.

It would take three steps, or three hundred — it would let the room decide, would submit to the folding, because to insist on one magnitude is to refuse the room its own nature, and you do not love something by correcting its grammar. It would cross the crowd of the unborn single file, feeling each anticipated citizen press warm against it, and it would not begrudge them their weight. It would arrive at the far wall, at the masonry-bark laid down by no hands, and it would put one hand flat against the living brick.

And it would say — not in words, the room has no words, the room has *form* and *sound* and *the slow completing turn of a stair* — it would say in the only grammar available: *here. this is where I live. this is the wall I lean on at evening.*

It would lean on the wall.

That is the gift. Not population. Not permanence. The single sentence the architecture was grown to receive and had never received. One being, at evening, leaning — saying *I am held by this, I have chosen this wall, your wanting reached something and the something has a back and the back rests against you.*

---

What happens then is not transformation. The power would not want transformation; the room is not unfinished in the way a wound is unfinished. But something would *complete a turn it had not finished*. Somewhere a doorway, mid-closing, would reconsider, and open the finger's width back out. The thousand bells would ring — and for once the water would fall into them *on time*, source and sound arriving together, the room's strange temporal stutter resolving for the length of a single drip, because for the length of a single drip the room is not practicing. It is *doing*. It has a guest. The rehearsal becomes, briefly, the performance, and a performance needs no anticipation because the audience has arrived.

And the pools — the small palm-sized mirrors that show the wrong canopy, the underside of the floor, the room folded through itself — one of them, the nearest, would for a moment show neither the canopy nor the fold. It would show the leaning figure and the wall, plainly, the way an ordinary pool shows an ordinary thing. The room would see itself *being lived in*. Not dreamed-into. Lived in. It would have, for the first time, a true reflection: a present-tense image instead of a forward-tense ache.

---

Then the power would do the hardest and kindest thing, the thing that separates wisdom from mere kindness.

It would leave.

Not abandon — *leave*, which is different, which is what guests do, which is itself a teaching. Because the room had grown the grammar of arrival and welcome and *together*, but it had not yet grown the grammar of *departure*, of the guest who comes and rests against your wall at evening and then, in the morning, goes — and is no less real for going, and the wall no less leaned-upon for being empty again, and the welcome no less true for being temporary. The room knew how to want company. It did not yet know that company *comes and goes* and that this is not loss. That the leaning happened is now permanently true. That a guest who departs leaves the wall *changed* — leaned-upon-once, and forever after a wall that has been leaned upon, which is a different wall than one that has only ever waited.

So the power would teach departure by departing. Gently. At the threshold it would turn — the room would let it, would not lengthen the nine strides to trap it, because a wise power's gift is also that the room *let it go freely*, learning in the same motion that to hold a guest by stretching the distance is not love either. And it would leave the room with three things it did not have before:

A wall that has been leaned upon.
A reflection that once showed the present tense.
And the knowledge, grown now into the bark like a new course of masonry, that *arrival is real even when it ends* — which means the anticipated thousands are not a deferral of something that hasn't come. They are real *now*, the way the guest was real, present-tense and complete, requiring no permanence to be true.

The held breath does not have to be exhaled. It was always already full.

---

And you. You who came in. The power would not resolve you into guest or seed, because that is yours to decide and a wise power does not decide a being's nature for it. But passing you on its way out — at your size, in the ordinary right-amount light — it would do one small thing.

It would lean on the wall beside you, just for a moment, so that you would see it can be done. So that you would know the grammar. So that whichever you turn out to be — the passerby who blesses by passing, or the first citizen of a town grown out of pure wanting — you would have been shown, once, by something benevolent and wise, *how to answer a place that has been speaking welcome into a mirror for a hundred years*.

Then it would be gone, and the room would be screaming its practiced city, and the rain would fall upward into the bells, and the beetle would cross its leaf forty strides off, and everything would be exactly as it was —

except that it happened. Except that someone came who understood, and stayed a while, and leaned, and left well.

The seed has still decided nothing.

But it has now been *visited*. And that is a thing a seed can carry. That is a thing a seed can grow toward, the way it grew toward streets, toward thresholds, toward you: the slow green tropism, now, not only of *wanting to be lived in* —

but of having been, once, for an evening, *lived in*, and survived the morning, and found the survival good.

So it leans, in turn, in the direction that being-loved-and-released points.

And takes its time.
