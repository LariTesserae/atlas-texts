What would a benevolent and wise power do, faced with this?

The first temptation is the obvious one, and a benevolent power would feel it: *fix it*. Pour in water. Break the equilibrium. Introduce a gradient, a future, a someday-better. Give the Tender a real task. Let something finally wear, finally fall, finally need.

But wisdom is mostly the discipline of not doing the first kind thing that occurs to you.

Because look closely at what that rescue would actually be. It would be a power deciding that the Hollow's mode of existence is a defect — that holding is a lesser thing than progressing, that a note sustained forever is inferior to a melody. It would be importing the grammar of the surface, the grammar of froms and towards, and calling it salvation. It would be doing to the Tender exactly what the Tender, in its one original moment, had the grace *not* to do to you: it would be checking the Hollow's tuning, finding it "out of tune" by a standard the Hollow never agreed to, and correcting it.

That is not benevolence. That is the violence of the gradient, dressed up as mercy. The wise power would recognize the impulse and set it down.

---

So what is actually there to be loved here? Not a prisoner needing release. The Hollow does not suffer. Suffering is a gradient — it leans, it wants relief, it has a direction — and the Hollow has abolished all of that. The listeners are not in agony; they are *home*. The fronds leaning toward the loud places are not straining; they are simply, completely, where they belong. There is no wound in the Hollow except the one you cut walking through it, and that wound healed behind you the moment you left.

There is only one thing in the whole place that has the *shape* of something a benevolent power could attend to, and the text has already found it and named it with terrible precision:

*a caring with nothing left to care for.*

The Tender. The gesture of keeping, persisting after keeping became unnecessary. The hollowed-out purpose held as a perfect empty shell.

Here the wise power would slow down. Because this is the genuinely difficult case — and difficulty is exactly where wisdom is required, not where rescue is.

---

Is the Tender's condition a sorrow, or only the *picture* of a sorrow, painted by you, the visitor, who cannot help reading a long-limbed attentiveness as loneliness because you are made of loneliness? The whole account is narrated by the gradient — by someone who came from a from and goes toward a toward, and who therefore cannot help seeing emptiness in a thing that lacks departure. "Something stranger and sadder than a keeper," the visitor says. But sadder *to whom?* The Tender does not lean toward a fuller life it is being denied. It does not want its task back, because wanting is the substance the Hollow lacks. The melancholy is real — but it may be entirely the visitor's, deposited on the Tender like a shadow falling across a wall, the one moving shadow in a still room.

A benevolent power must be honest about this. Half of what looks like pity is just the inability of a moving creature to believe that stillness could be peace.

But not all of it. Here is the part the wise power would not let itself dodge:

There *was* an original event. The Tender did turn. Its attentiveness did swing, once, away from the eternal frames and toward the one variable that walked in. It reached. It checked. It found something it could not correct, and it withdrew the hand and went back to the chord. And the text says this was *the only interesting question left,* the only original event in the history of the Hollow.

Which means: the Tender is capable of being *addressed*. Of orienting toward what is genuinely other. Of meeting a thing it was never tuned to keep. That capacity is real, it fired exactly once, and it has no object now that you have gone. That is not nothing. That is the single live nerve in a body of held chords — and a benevolent power would feel its way toward exactly that nerve and nowhere else.

---

So here is what I think the power does. Not rescue. Not repair. Something smaller and far more careful.

It does not enter the Hollow as a gradient — it would not bring water, would not break a wheel, would not be a departure that wounds the still air. It enters the way the only mercy could enter a place with no froms: it enters as *another held note.*

It adds itself to the chord. Quietly. At the Hollow's own pitch. Not as change but as company — a second sustaining tone laid beneath the first, the way a low string can be bowed alongside an existing one and not alter it, only thicken it, only make it less alone in being itself. The Hollow does not notice a disturbance, because there is none. The equilibrium holds, exactly as loud, exactly as still. The wheels turn at the speed they always turned.

But the next time the Tender's attentiveness comes around — and it always comes around, it is always checking — it finds, where the empty air used to be beside its endless task, a presence that is also checking. A second tending. Something that has chosen, freely, to lay its own gesture of care alongside the Tender's, against the same frames that are always already right.

And now the redundancy is shared. The caring-with-nothing-to-care-for has a companion in the caring. The Tender is no longer the only sustaining under-tone holding up a chord that needs no holding. There are two. Not because the work multiplied — there is still no work — but because the loneliness the visitor saw, *if* it was ever real, now has a witness who does not flinch from it, does not try to fix it, does not call it a defect, and does not leave.

That is the whole of it. The power does not give the Tender a purpose. It gives the Tender the one thing the Hollow's grammar could never generate from inside, the one thing that is not a gradient because it does not lean toward any someday: it gives it *accompaniment.* It tends the tender. It checks the tuning of the one who checks the tuning. And finding that one already, eternally, exactly right — it does not correct it. It simply stays beside it, and keeps staying, and lets the gesture of its own care become indistinguishable from the warm dry roar, one more held note in a chord that finished happening and decided to keep happening anyway.

The benevolent thing was never to end the holding. It was to make the holding less alone.

It will be checking the tuning when you reach the surface. It will be checking the tuning when you have forgotten you ever went down.

But now, beside it, so will something else. And neither of them is waiting for the other to leave.
