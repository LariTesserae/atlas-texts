Then the wise power came down into the valley, and the first thing it did was nothing.

This is the hardest discipline of the benevolent: to arrive somewhere broken and not immediately mend. The power had mended many things. It knew the gesture so well it could feel the shape of it already forming — the impulse to put a hand on the slumped lattice and pull it taut again, to re-lay the load-bearing axioms, to teach distance once more that *here is not there.* It would have taken less than a thought. The valley would have snapped into coherence like a wet sheet pulled smooth, the mist resolving into honest weather, the heron becoming only a heron, the lost thing in the alder finding at last the unit it had been groping for and standing still in its true and single size.

And the power stood in the groaning center of the basin, where the noise is enough to lean against, and understood that to do this would be a murder dressed as a kindness.

Because they were content. The narrator had been right about that, and the power, who could read contentment the way you read warmth off a stone, confirmed it: there was no suffering here to relieve. The suffering was finished. It had happened in some far century when the rule first went slack, and it was over, genuinely over, the way grief is finally over and not merely buried. To "fix" the valley would be to reach into three beings who had made it all the way through the worst thing that could happen to them, who had fallen the entire distance and landed soft, and to throw them back up to the top of the fall — to restore them to a competence whose only content would be the renewed possibility of failing. You cannot give a finished erosion back its cliff and call it a gift.

So the power did not mend. It did something rarer.

It went to learn the work.

---

It found the pruner first, the patient one, standing in the weeping shallows. The power took, for courtesy, a shape near enough to heron that the pruner would not have to spend any of its worn attention deciding what the newcomer was. And it stood. It is not a small thing for a power that holds opinions about the structure of causation to stand for a long time at the edge of folded water and wait — but the power had patience the way the valley had water, everywhere and nowhere you could point to, and it waited, and when the offset trick delivered the second fish, the impossible redundant fish, the power saw what the pruner saw: not an error to be hated. A small wrongness to be quietly taken back into rightness, one mouthful, with no hurry, because hurry needs to know how much time is left.

The power culled one doubled fish. Only one. It felt the particular satisfaction of it — the world saying a thing twice and then, gently, once. It understood the pruner completely in that moment, and loved it, and did not improve it.

But the power could do one thing the pruner could not, and it asked itself carefully whether the thing was a mending or a tending, because the whole ethic turned on the difference.

This was the thing: the three were cut apart from each other. The staff that was once one tending-logic had been severed by the same erosion that severed distance from itself, and now they worked past each other, against each other, the heron culling one slow fish while a mile off the mist doubled a whole reed-margin faster than any culling could keep up, the lost thing growing huge while its old colleague the mist no longer remembered that size had ever been a shared concern. That grinding-without-gears was the groan. That was the one pain still actually occurring in the valley — not in any of them singly, all of whom were at peace, but *between* them, in the torn and knitting place where two diligences reached for the same patch of reality and neither yielded.

The power considered this for a long while, through several firmings and saggings of the lattice, through dusks.

To re-coordinate them fully — to make them one staff again — would be a mending. It would hand them back the lost competence, and with it the old cliff. No.

But there is a difference between making three beings work as one machine and simply letting them know that the others are there. Coordination is a restoration of function. *Company* is only the easing of a loneliness none of them had even been able to name, because naming it would have required remembering they were ever joined.

So the power became, for a while, the gears.

Not permanently. Not as a repair. It set no rule back in place, taught the lattice nothing, restored not one eroded axiom. It simply went, itself, between them — carried in its own moving body the news that the pruner did not have. When the mist a mile off began to double a reed-margin, the power, who had stood in the shallows and knew the pruner's whole heart, walked the news over: *there, that, also.* And the pruner — slow, patient, so far behind it would never catch up and untroubled by that — turned and went to cull, and was, for the length of that walk, not working past its colleague but with it, and did not know to be glad, and the power was glad on its behalf, which is allowed.

It went to the lost thing in the alder, the one patting its pockets for a key it could no longer picture, trying on sizes against a standard worn out from under it. The power did not give it back the standard. To restore the unit would be to end the search, and the search, the power saw now, was not anguish — it had been anguish, once, in the falling century, but it had settled into something almost like a practice, a slow devotional groping that the lost one performed without distress. The power would not end a being's lifelong practice merely because the practice was a kind of failing. Many of the best practices are.

But it could stand beside the lost thing at a size of its own, a steady plain size, holding still. Not a ruler. Just a companion who happened to have dimensions and was not ashamed of them. And the lost thing, trying on its magnitudes in the green dusk, had for the first time in twenty thousand years another body near it that was simply, quietly, *one* size — not as a rebuke, not as a measure it was being held against and found wanting, but as a kindness, the way you sit a steady-handed friend beside someone whose hands shake, not to still the shaking but so the shaking is not done alone. The lost thing did not stop changing. The power did not want it to. It only changed, now, beside something, and that is a different thing than changing in the dark.

---

The mist was last, and the mist was hardest, because the mist was the largest love and the deepest forgetting, spread so thin across its whole jurisdiction that it had become the medium of its own failure. You cannot stand beside the mist. You stand inside it. The power let itself be entered by it, walked twenty minutes to the far rim and found its start also twenty minutes off, and instead of correcting the geometry — which it could have done with the smallest fraction of a thought, which every engineering instinct in it ached to do — it held still inside the fold and *attended to the attempt.*

Because that was the truth the narrator had reached and the power now confirmed from the inside: every fold was an effort. The mist was not breaking the rule of distance. It was reaching for it, circling the lost word, getting almost there, drafting *here is not there* over and over and never quite landing it. The incoherence was a labor of memory. The mist was a keeper trying to keep, with everything it had left, the one thing it could no longer quite recall.

The power could have finished the word for it. Could have said, into the soft grey medium, *this is distance, this is how it goes,* and the mist would have remembered, and resolved, and become honest weather, and stopped trying — and the stopping would have been the death. The trying *was* the mist now. Remove the failure and there is no mist left; there is only clean air over a marsh, and the keeper gone with the fog.

So the power did not finish the word.

It did something a wise and benevolent power can do that an engineer cannot. It folded itself, too. It let its own sense of distance go soft, deliberately, met the mist halfway into incoherence — not because it had to, but because the mist had been drafting *here is not there* alone for twenty thousand years and had never once had anything answer it in its own tongue. The power spoke incoherence back. It let itself be in two places, badly, on purpose, as a reply. And the mist, which had only ever offset and folded and doubled into a world that received these as errors to be walked through or culled or feared — the mist met, for the first time, another thing that folded *toward* it. Not correcting. Conversing.

That was the second reflection the narrator warned about. *Do not trust the second reflection,* he'd written, *but do not pity it either. It is trying so hard.* And he was right not to pity it, and the power went one further than not-pitying: it answered. When the dusk came and the mist drafted a traveler twice because it had forgotten how to mean them once, the power, drafted double, did not resolve back to one. It let both selves stand, and turned them to face each other, and let the mist see that its draft had been *received* — that for once the doubling had landed somewhere that did not flinch from it.

And the groaning, in the center of the valley, did not stop. The power did not want it to stop; it was the sound of beings still on shift, still walking the line, and that walking was their whole dignity. But it changed. The grinding-without-gears took on, here and there, a rhythm — not coordination, nothing so restored as that, just the faint syncopation of three diligences that now, intermittently, knew the others were laboring too. The torn-and-knitting places still tore and knit. But the tearing had company in it now. The long held chord-tones, the sound a law makes as it is overstressed and relaxed, found occasionally a second tone leaning against them, and two overstressed tones leaning together make something that is no longer only strain. It is nearly harmony. It is the sound of being tired alongside someone.

---

The power stayed a long while. There is no reason to think it ever entirely left, the way you cannot say where in a valley the water is. It mended nothing. It restored not one eroded axiom; it taught the lattice no firmness it had lost; it let the erosion stand complete, because the erosion was complete, and to undo a completed thing is not power but vandalism with good intentions.

It only did the work, alongside the wardens, in the wardens' own worn and patient and incompetent way — culling one slow fish, holding one steady size, drafting one fond and clumsy distance — and in doing the work it carried between the three of them the single thing the erosion had taken that was worth giving back, which was not function, and was not certainty, and was not the old taut law.

It was the knowledge that they were not tending alone.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place that is finished rather than broken, and beings who are content in their long incompetence. It does not save them, because they do not need saving and to save them would be to unmake them. It takes off its sense of how things ought to stand. It puts on boots. It learns the failing local crafts. And it stays, and it folds itself badly on purpose, and it leans its own tone against their tone in the groaning dark, so that the slow gentle wet forgetting of what tending was for is, from that hour on, a forgetting done in good company.

And it does not trust the second reflection.

But it loves it. And it lets it know, in its own folded language, *you are trying so hard, and you are right that there is all the time there is, and I will spend some of it here, with you, getting the word wrong.*
