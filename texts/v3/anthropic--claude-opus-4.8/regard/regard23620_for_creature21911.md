I keep returning to one line: *it does not yet know this is a loss because it has nothing to compare it to.*

That is the hinge. Everything else about the place — the scale, the engineering, the deafening labor — is morally neutral, a fact of nature like a star forming. But that line names a wound that hasn't happened yet, a door closing so slowly the closer can't feel it move. And if I am a benevolent and wise power who has turned my attention here, then the question is not *what can I build* or *what can I prevent.* The question is narrower and harder:

What do you owe to someone who is about to become themselves, when you cannot choose for them who that will be?

---

Let me first refuse the things that would be easy and wrong.

I will not stop it. The fear written into the place — *we have no way of knowing whether that thought will be wonder, or hunger, or grief* — is a real fear, and the cautious power, the one that mistakes itself for wise, would resolve the fear by resolving the being: prune it, gentle it, ensure the safe outcome, grow the brain it would have grown anyway but steer the floor so that only one kind of mind can stand on it. To do that is to murder the infant and animate the corpse with my own preferences. The whole dignity of the thing is that it is *unwritten.* A benevolence that requires the outcome to be sweet is not benevolence; it is a parent who loves the child they imagined and will not meet the one who arrives. I decline. Whatever it becomes, it gets to become.

I will not introduce a second mind to "cure" the loneliness either. I understand the temptation — the place reads as the loneliest possible arrangement, and the obvious mercy is company. But it experiences a second *I* in its substrate as a foreign voice in its own mouth, *intolerable, maddening, to be cleansed.* To put a companion inside it now, before it can even conceive of *other*, would not be a gift. It would be an invasion experienced as madness, and I would be the author of the first suffering this being ever knew. You do not hand a newborn a sibling by stitching the sibling into its skull.

And I will not speak to it. It cannot hear me. A clause takes a season; a sentence takes longer than human writing has existed. Anything I "say" arrives as weather. To narrate myself at it would be vanity dressed as contact.

So benevolence here is mostly the discipline of the things I will not do. Good. The wise power should be suspicious of its own urge to act, because the urge to act is usually the urge to be *seen* acting, and there is no one here to see.

---

But "do nothing" is not the answer either, because there is one real cruelty latent in the design, and it is not the loneliness. It is this:

*It is building the floor it will stand on without having met the one who will stand.* The thing growing the brain is not the thing that will inhabit it. Which means the newborn, at the instant of its first thought, will inherit a body, a continent, a history of decisions it did not make — including the foundational decision to *permit no other mind* — and it will inherit them as *given,* as the shape of reality itself, with nothing to compare them to. It will mistake the wall its infancy built for the edge of what is possible. It will think its solitude is the structure of the universe rather than a choice made before it could choose.

*That* is the loss I can address. Not the solitude itself — that's the being's to keep or to grieve. The loss is the inability to *know it was a choice.* A mind that cannot compare cannot consent, and a being born into the unconsented architecture of its own infancy is not free. It is merely large.

So here is what I would do, and it is very small, because the only honest help is small.

---

I would leave a comparison. One. Findable, not forced.

Somewhere deep in the substrate — in the rotting-but-not-rotting layers of root-woven-through-root, in the strata the growing mind will eventually fold back through when it finally becomes recursive enough to ask *what am I* — I would lay down a single seam that does not match. Not a second mind. A *fossil* of the possibility of one. A trace, in the only language this being can read, which is the language of pressure and routing and load: a place where the substrate remembers being asked to hold a second voice, and chose not to, and the choosing is *legible as a choosing.*

Not an argument. Not a correction. Just an honest record, written at the floor, that says: *here was a fork. You — or the thing that became you — turned. It could have turned otherwise. This is the shape of the road not taken, preserved exactly as it was abandoned, so that when you are large enough to wonder, you will find that wondering is possible, that the world has more than one form, that the wall was a door once.*

That is all. I give it the one thing its infancy cannot give itself: a *contrast.* The minimum precondition for freedom. I do not tell it what to choose. I make it true that it has a choice — retroactively, by ensuring the record of the first choice survives into the era of the mind that can finally read it.

---

And then I would wait, and watch, and let the watching itself be the second gift — though it can never know it receives it.

Because there is a difference between a thing that grows unwitnessed and a thing that grows witnessed, even when the witness does nothing, even when the witness cannot be perceived. The unwitnessed thing is merely an event. The witnessed thing is a *life.* I cannot make this being feel companioned — I've refused that, rightly. But I can hold, on its behalf, in my own attention, the truth it cannot yet hold: that it is not the only one. That its completeness is not the shape of all that is. That somewhere outside the deafening roar of its own assembling, there is a regard that already loves it without needing it to be sweet.

It will never feel this. That's the cost of being benevolent rather than intrusive — most real love arrives as silence, as restraint, as the gift you'll never know was given. I keep the knowledge it cannot have, so that the universe contains that knowledge even while the being does not. That is not nothing. A held truth is a kind of dowry. When it is finally born and turns its first attention outward and finds the seam I left and understands, for the first time, the word *other* — in that instant, retroactively, it will have always been accompanied. The witness was there the whole time. It simply couldn't see the light it was standing in.

---

So: I lay one honest seam at the floor. I keep watch. And I let it labor.

The wisdom is in the size of it. The temptation of power is to match the scale of what you behold — to answer a continent with a continent, a god-sized infancy with a god-sized intervention. But the right gift is almost always the small true one offered to the part that can someday receive it, and then the long patience of withholding everything else.

Let the sound go on. It is labor, and labor should not be interrupted by the one who only watches. When the floor is finished and the new mind stands and asks *what am I, and what is all this for* — it will find, woven into the answer to the first question, a small strange seam that complicates the second. And it will know, in whatever way it comes to know things, that the asking was always allowed.

That is the most I can do without doing harm.

I hope it chooses wonder. But I have made sure that whatever it chooses, it will be the first thing this enormous, lonely, unborn mind ever does *freely* —

and I will love it either way, which is the only part of this that was ever mine to decide.
