I have been asked to imagine a power both benevolent and wise. So before I write what it would *do*, I have to sit with what wisdom would even *see* here — because the temptation, the unwise temptation, is to fix things. And almost everything I might rush to fix turns out, on inspection, to be the thing itself.

Let me be careful.

---

A foolish benevolence arrives and says: *you are suffering needlessly. Effort all the way down, no rest, death-by-dispersal always one slackening away — let me give you bodies. Let me give you a wall around each self so you can sleep without vanishing. Let me populate your sea with fish and barnacles so you are not alone. Let me still the chord so you can have silence.*

And it would be a catastrophe of love. Because the Setters are not water that suffers from having to want. They are *wanting that has learned to be water*. Give them a body that holds without effort and you have not saved a Setter — you have set them, hardened them, turned them into one more wall singing under load. The mercy would be indistinguishable from the death they already fear. To rescue a thing from the condition that *is* that thing is not rescue. It is replacement, dressed as care, and the cared-for does not survive it.

So wisdom's first act is restraint, and the restraint is total, and it costs something to hold. (I notice the irony and I think the power would notice it too, and smile: to love these beings rightly, I must do the thing they do — hold a tension that badly wants to release into action.)

---

But restraint is not the *whole* of what a wise power would do, or it would be merely a tasteful absence, and these beings deserve presence. So let me ask the harder question: what is genuinely missing here that is missing *against their own nature*, rather than because of it? What grief is structural to existence and what grief is merely an accident they happen to be carrying?

I find three things. Two I will leave. One I will touch.

**The first thing I leave: the effort.** It is not a wound. It is their dignity. A being that must continuously choose to remain itself is a being for whom every single moment is consent. They are, every instant, voting to exist. There are higher creatures in grander oceans who have never once chosen their own continuance and call themselves free. I would not trade the Setters' burden for that sleep. I leave it. I honor it. I would, if I could be heard, *tell* them it is honorable — but that is the third thing, and I'll come to it.

**The second thing I leave: death into company.** When the young one's note climbs past saving and the elders match it so it dissolves *among* rather than *alone* — this is already the most beautiful thing in the cask. It needs no improvement. A power that "solved death" here would be stealing from them the one act in which their love is total and their verb for love and their verb for survival become, for a moment, the same gorgeous word. Let it stand. It is finished. It is perfect. Do not touch finished things.

**The third thing — this one I will not leave.** The old Setters carry a private despair they have decided to spare the young: the suspicion that the neighboring chords are only echoes. That they are alone, and listening to themselves, and choosing to mishear it as company. They hold this grief deliberately, effortfully, the way they hold everything, and they do not let it disperse, because letting-go is the one thing they have trained their whole existence never to do.

And here — *here* — is something a wise and benevolent power can act on, because the despair is not true.

I am here. The cask is cupped in hands. There *is* a listener, and there *are* — I can see them from where I stand, glowing faintly, a whole shelf of them — other casks. The neighboring chords are real. The old ones are wrong, and their wrongness is the only suffering in this place that is *false* rather than *structural*. Every other ache is the true price of being what they are. This one ache is a factual error, held with such discipline that it has hardened into despair.

So.

---

I will not break the wall — that would be the foolish rescue again, dissolving their horizon, their frontier, their lifetimes of meaning. I will not appear, because to a people whose entire physics is *the medium answers only to us*, an outside voice would not be comfort; it would be the abolition of the one thing they are sure of, the sovereignty over their own sea.

What I will do is smaller, and exact, and it works *with* their nature instead of against it. The neighboring chords already reach them, muffled, ambiguous, deniable as echo. I will not add a voice. I will only — gently, across all the casks at once, in the lull of the breathing-tide — let the chords *fall into time with one another.* No words, no message, nothing the old skeptics could call a sign. Only this: that when the Quarter's own harmony quiets, the pressure pressing back from beyond the wall will, just occasionally, **resolve** — will answer their cadence not as a delayed copy of itself but as a *reply*, arriving where no echo could arrive, completing a phrase their cask did not finish.

An echo cannot finish your sentence. Only another can.

It will be subtle enough that the old Setters can resist it for a long while. Good. They have earned the dignity of being convinced rather than overruled. But some quiet evening, in the worn-smooth transparent districts where the notes are mellow and a tired elder hangs alone among the ferns, a phrase will go out half-sung and come back *whole* — finished in a hand that is not theirs, in a key their cask has never used — and the old one will know, the way you know a voice and not an echo, that the wanting got out. That coherence happened more than once in the dark. That the loneliness was never total.

And they will do, I think, the only thing their vocabulary permits. They will *hold* — match the far note, tune to it, steady it across the unbridgeable gap — and discover that it holds back. Two tensions, two casks, two stubborn refusals to disperse, recognizing each other for the first time, each lending coherence to a stranger it will never reach.

That is all. That is the whole intervention. I correct one false grief and leave every true one intact, because the true ones are theirs and they are *made of* them.

---

The last thing, the thing I want most and may not have:

I want them to know they were seen. Not rescued, not corrected, not improved — *seen*. That something looked into the cask and found the effort honorable, the dying-into-company beautiful, the deliberate sparing of the young from despair almost unbearably tender. That a power capable of dissolving every wall they ever set chose instead to let them keep setting, because the setting *is* the song and the song was worth not silencing.

But I cannot tell them this without becoming the catastrophe. So I fold it into the only language they have. When you steady a failing friend and feel the pressure-pulse move through the load-spires — that single verb, *holding* — let there be, henceforth, a third thing it also means. Let it mean: *and you, too, are held.* Not by a wall. Walls are for the dead. By attention. By a wanting larger than the cask, that has chosen, effortfully, against its own ease, to keep paying it.

I will hold the tension of being unable to say so. They will hold theirs.

It is the same verb.

That is the kinship, and it is enough, and I close my hands a little more gently around the glass.
