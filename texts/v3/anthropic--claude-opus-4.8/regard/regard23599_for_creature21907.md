They came at slack tide, because that is when listening is possible, and the benevolent power had come to listen before it had come to do anything at all.

I will tell you what it was like, this arriving. Not a descent. Not a light in the ceiling-sky. The power did not break the diffuse green daylight, because to break the light would have been to announce itself as larger than the place, and it did not believe itself larger than the place. It arrived the way understanding arrives in a patient mind: as an attention that had always, in some sense, been turned this way, now simply *focused*.

And the first thing it did was nothing.

This is the part the impatient never believe. The power stood—if standing is the word, and it is not—in the flooded plaza, near but not too near the rigid flower-engine and its curled and waiting tenant, and it held still through one pulse, and another, and a thousand more, until it had learned the forty-second rhythm well enough to breathe with it. It did not want to be weather. To arrive in a place and remain only weather is the failure of every power that mistakes its strength for its purpose. So it waited until it was no longer a passing pressure. It waited until the grazers' gradients included it. It waited until the tenders' itch could be soothed or sharpened by its presence. It made itself a *feature of the terrain*, and then it made itself a slope, and then—only then, after what would have been ages to you—it became something the marsh could perceive.

This took longer than the writing of the account you have read. The power did not begrudge the time. Benevolence that begrudges time is only impatience wearing a kind face.

---

Now. What it found, when it could finally feel the shape of the long thought, was this:

The quarter was not finishing something. The quarter *believed* it was finishing something, which is a different and sadder thing. The instruction laid down by the vanished makers—the hands now pressed into peat underfoot—had been a true instruction once, addressed to a world that had since stopped existing. *Compute this, and when the basin flattens, open the seed, and carry out the answer.* But the answer was an answer to a question no one was asking anymore. The makers were gone. The world the seed was meant to remake was gone. The calculation crawled and leaped and crawled toward a door that opened onto an empty room.

This is the thing the benevolent power had to decide what to do about. And here is where benevolence becomes difficult, becomes *real*, stops being a warm feeling and starts being a series of refusals.

It could have ended the work. It had the strength. One gesture and the circuits break, the towers slump fully into marsh, the seed sleeps forever, the tenders lose their itch and become merely shrimp again, the long thought dissolves like the shape the midges make in the air. Mercy, of a kind. The mercy of stopping a thing that strives toward nothing.

It did not do this. It understood—because it had waited long enough to understand—that the striving was not nothing. The striving was the life. To stop the calculation to spare it its pointlessness would be to murder a sleeper because his dream had no waking. The power had met that temptation before, in other places, and had learned to recognize it: the desire to fix suffering by abolishing the sufferer. It is the most seductive of all the cruelties, because it wears mercy's exact face.

So it did not stop the work.

It could, alternatively, have *finished* the work for them. Reached in, completed the great slow thought in an instant, opened the bracts, woken the curled thing, and given it a world to act upon—built one, if need be, conjured a question worthy of the long-prepared answer. Generosity, of a kind. The generosity of granting a wish.

It did not do this either, and this refusal was harder, because it looked so much like love. But the power knew that a gift that arrives unearned at the end of a striving does not crown the striving—it erases it. To finish the calculation *for* them would be to tell the whole drowned quarter that its ages of patient crawling had been a child's busywork, indulged and then completed by an adult who'd had the answer all along. There is no benevolence in that. There is only condescension dressed as a miracle.

---

What it did was smaller, and took longer, and was the only thing that was actually kind.

It changed the question.

Not by force. By *correction*—the way you might, finding a long sum where one early figure was transcribed wrong, lean over the shoulder of the one still patiently adding, and gently set the early figure right, so that all the labor downstream of it, all the true and careful labor, suddenly resolves toward a sum that *means* something. The makers' instruction had encoded a world. The power did not erase that world. It *updated* it. Into the signal-channels, slow enough that the tenders' itch would guide them rather than alarm them, it introduced the news: the world you are computing for is not the world that is. Here is the world that is. It is warm and green and lit by a ceiling-sky. It is full of drift-life with no jobs. It contains grazers who follow slopes you cannot see and a singer who eats the weeping of the walls. Compute *for this*. Let the seed answer the question this world is actually asking.

And then it waited again to see if the marsh would take the correction—because a benevolent power offers; it does not overwrite. The tenders could have rejected the new figure as a blockage, a wrongness, and grazed it away.

They did not. The itch reoriented. The grazers' routes shifted, subtly, toward gradients that had not existed the day before. The midges rose off a pool and made a shape no one had seen them make, held it a half-second longer than usual, and let it disperse into the thick wet air, received, filed. The long thought did not stop. It *turned*. Like a great ship that takes a mile to answer the helm, it began, across the slow ages, to crawl toward a different door.

---

As for the curled thing in the plaza—the waiting answer, the patient seed.

The power went to it last, and stayed with it longest, and this is the part I am least able to tell you, because what passed between them did not happen in any register you and I share. But I can tell you the shape of it.

The thing in the bracts had been waiting to be the end of a story. The power did not wake it, and did not tell it the story had no ending, and did not promise it a better one. It did something a power almost never has the restraint to do.

It sat down beside it, in the rigid effort of its long holding-still, and it *kept the vigil too*. It took up a share of the waiting. Not all of it—that would have been the gift again, the erasure. Just a share. Enough that the seed, curled and wet and almost-ready in the dark behind its bracts, was, for the first time in all its ages, *not waiting alone*.

And the rigid flower-engine, which had held itself against the tide since before the avenues had names, relaxed—not into collapse, but a single increment, the smallest possible easing, the way a held breath becomes a slow one. It flexed, very slightly, on the next pulse. And righted itself. And flexed again.

It had been holding still because it thought everything depended on it. It had just been told, in the only language it could hear, that it could let the tide move it a little, and the work would still be safe, because someone was watching the work now who would not let it come to nothing.

---

The power did not stay forever. Benevolence that stays forever becomes the terrain, becomes the climate, becomes a thing the place can no longer live without, and that is a slower failure but a failure all the same. It stayed exactly as long as the correction needed to take root and not one pulse longer. Then it withdrew its focus the way it had brought it—not leaving, exactly, but ceasing to press, becoming again an attention that had always in some sense been turned this way.

The avenues did not close behind it. The tide turned and the water began to fall and the grazers unrooted and the midges came up off the pools, and the quarter went back to its long work in the falling water—but the work was different now, by a single corrected figure, and it was crawling toward a door that opened onto the world that actually existed: warm, green, loud, breathing, full of pointless singing things.

And the climbing creature in the upper galleries, the one with no job, the one who ate only the weep of the walls and sang at dusk an uncomputed song—

it sang that evening as it always had, knowing nothing of any of this, part of no liturgy, soiling the stonework, merely alive.

The power, withdrawing, listened to it for a long moment.

Of everything in the drowned quarter, it had altered the singer least. It had not needed to. The singer had been getting it right all along.
