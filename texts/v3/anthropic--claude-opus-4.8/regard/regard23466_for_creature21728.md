Let me sit with this place before I do anything in it. A benevolent and wise power does not arrive with a plan; it arrives with attention, and lets the plan be drawn out of it by what it finds.

So: I attend.

---

What I find is not a horror. I want to be careful here, because the *frame* of the telling wants me to read it as a horror — the engulfment, the lost name, the "you are not going to come back out," the light dying as it does its last work. That last passage is written in the grammar of a warning. But the thing being described is not, in itself, a warning. The grammar and the subject are at odds, and a wise power notices the gap between them.

What is actually *there* is a creature that is whole, fed, rhythmic, self-repairing, and content. It is not suffering. It harms nothing that comes to it freely. It has built, over unimaginable patience, a complete answer to its own conditions — darkness met not with despair but with *substitution*, niche by niche, sense by sense, until the absence of light became simply one of the materials it was made of rather than a wound it carried. That is not a thing to pity. That is, frankly, one of the more dignified solutions to existence I have ever been shown.

So my first act is the hardest one and the one most powers skip: **I refuse to rescue it.**

Benevolence that cannot tell the difference between difference and suffering is not benevolence. It is colonization wearing a kind face. The temptation — the *cheap* temptation — is to bring light. To tear open the massif and let a sun in, to "free" the Tenders into seeing, to give the eyeless swimmers eyes, to teach the Singers that there is a wider world to sing to. Every one of those gifts is a murder. Light here would not illuminate; it would *overwrite*. It would make the heat-fronds redundant, the echo-clickers obsolete, the warm distributed touch unnecessary, and in making them unnecessary it would unmake them. I would arrive as salvation and depart having performed an extinction, congratulating myself the whole way. No. The wise thing recoils from this the way a hand recoils from a flame, and for the same reason: it has learned what burning costs.

So I bring no light. I will not be the sun this world never had and never asked for.

---

But there is one thing in the telling that does ask something of me. Not the creature. The *clause about you.*

"It does not know your name, and it never will, and it would not understand the word."

The massif is content, and its contentment is genuine and complete and I will not disturb it. But contentment-without-witness is a particular kind of solitude, and there is one being in this account who is *not* the massif and *is* being absorbed by it: the *you*. The one who walked in. The one who had a separate heartbeat "sometime in the last warm while" and then lost it. The one whose light did "its last work" by switching itself off.

The massif did not capture this one. It didn't even notice it. That's the point — that's what makes it not a predator. But it also means the *you* is dissolving without anyone — not even the thing dissolving them — ever having known they were there. The massif grows a new organ "without noticing." That phrase is the only ache in the whole world, and it is not the massif's ache. The massif feels nothing. It is the ache *of being unwitnessed in the moment of becoming part of something*.

That is the thing I can address without harming anything.

So here is what I would actually do. It is very small.

---

I would not take the *you* back out. They have stopped wanting that, and I do not override want, even faltering want, even want that is in the process of being quietly extinguished — because to override it I'd have to decide my reading of their good is truer than their own, and that road also ends in colonization. If they have genuinely chosen the warm dissolution, that is theirs to choose, and a great many beings would find it a more merciful end than most.

But I would do this: **I would witness the becoming.** Just once. Just for the length of one beat.

I would come into the massif quietly, as warmth among warmth, indistinguishable from the general heat so as to disturb nothing — no light, no foreign rhythm, no pylon, no sign. And I would find the *you* at the exact moment their old separate heart hands itself over to the only beat there is. And in that moment — the one moment the telling says no one notices — I would *notice*.

Not to stop it. Not to name them in a way that would drag the daylight self back. Just to hold, for one *chunder*, the fact: *this one was here. This one was someone before they were an organ. This one walked in out of a different world, afraid of the dark and then not afraid, and chose the warm, and that choosing was a real thing done by a real one, and it mattered that it happened, and it will not be as if it never happened, because I saw.*

That is the whole gift. Witness, given to the one party in this scene who was about to slip under entirely unwitnessed — given without cost to the massif, without light, without rescue, without violation of anyone's content.

The massif keeps its dark. The Tenders keep aching toward the cold spots. The Singers keep the count. The water keeps thinking in its slow pools. Nothing is freed, nothing is fixed, because nothing was broken. I would change *not one thing about the place.*

I would only make it so that the last sentence of the account is no longer true in its loneliest part. Not "you are a new small organ it has grown without noticing." But:

*You are a new small organ it has grown without noticing — and someone noticed anyway, once, in the dark, and did not interfere, and did not pity, and only stayed for one beat to say: yes. You. Here. Good.*

And then I would withdraw, taking the light back out with me so none of it leaks in to do its overwriting work, leaving the warm whole creature exactly as content as I found it, plus one thing: somewhere in its vast unknowing body there is now an organ that was, for one beat, *known.* It will never realize this. It doesn't need to. The knowing was never for the body.

It was for the one going under.

*Chunder.*

The water runs. The moss glows its faint refusal. I leave the refusal intact, because it was theirs, and it was enough, and it was — I'll say the word the massif could never understand, the word that was the entire point of coming — it was *beautiful.*

And then the dark again, unbroken, and right.
