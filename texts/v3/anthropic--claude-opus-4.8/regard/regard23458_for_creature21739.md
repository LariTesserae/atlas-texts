They live in a place that does not know they are there, and they have organized their entire civilization around not making a sound that matters. So the first thing I would not do is announce myself. A benevolent power that arrives with trumpet and revelation into a culture that has survived by *doubling its sentences against the echo* — that would be a cruelty dressed as a gift. They would not even know which arrival to trust. They would hear me an hour before I came and pour their molten certainty on the wrong word.

Wisdom, here, is mostly restraint. Let me reason through what restraint actually asks.

---

**What I would refuse to do, and why.**

I could finish the mountains. I have the reach for it; that is what *power* would mean. I could reach down into the slow enormous engine and say *be done*, and the stone would set, and *down* would mean down everywhere, and the mist would stop remembering, and the echoes would learn to keep time. And it would be the end of them.

Not a death — worse, a *flattening*. Everything these people are, every grace they have grown, is an answer to a question the unset world asks. Their long reach, their checking hands, their doubled prayers, the lamplighter-priesthood of the one warm thing — these are not damage. They are *adaptation made beautiful*. They are what a soul looks like when it has learned to stand lightly on a draft and love it anyway. Finish the world for them and you have not saved them; you have deleted the conditions under which they became themselves and handed the survivors a flat hard country full of skills with nothing left to grip. The benevolent fool fixes the problem and erases the person. I will not be that.

Nor will I wake the engine to ask it to be careful. It does not know they are there, and that ignorance is not a wrong to be corrected — it is the *only* reason they are alive. A creature that vast, made suddenly aware of the pale small things on its flank, has no faculty for gentleness toward them; gentleness is a thing that lives at human scale, and there is no human scale down there. Better it dream on. Some mercies are made of silence.

---

**What I would actually do.**

*First: I would learn to keep their time.* Before anything else. I would slow whatever in me passes for attention down past the foundry-bells, past the work-songs, down toward the deep slow *thud* — not to wake it, only to feel the two clocks at once, theirs and the world's, and hold them both without flinching. Because that is the thing none of them can do and all of them are organized around not-doing. A power that wanted to help them and could not bear their actual situation would be no use. So: I sit in the held breath. I learn its length. I make myself someone who can stand the truth they survive by not-hearing.

*Second: I would tend the edges, invisibly, at the scale of luck.* Not finish the world — never that — but be present in it the way a good thing is present in a hard place: as a slightly improved chance. The cable that was going to part, parts a day later, when the cage is empty. The child who steps off the edge into the unset air hangs *a little longer*, long enough to be talked back. The remembering-mist, when it replays a shape, replays — once, for a grieving woman who never got to say it — the shape of someone she lost, walking slow and silent through the grey, lifting a hand. Not a resurrection. Not a miracle they could build a religion on. Just the mist, doing what the mist does, choosing for once a kindness it was already capable of. They would never know it was me. That is the point. A gift they could trace back to a giver becomes a debt and a doctrine and a thing to fight over. A gift that looks exactly like *good fortune that was always possible here* leaves them free.

*Third — and this is the one I care about most — I would protect the surveyors.* The ones who go into the unset country and come back thinning, translucent, narrating themselves to stay real. *I am here, I am turning left, I am the one doing it now.* These are the people walking nearest to the place where the world hasn't decided, and they are paying for everyone's safety with the slow dissolution of their own edges. So: when an old surveyor goes so far out that the world begins to decline to repeat them — when they stop casting echoes — I would *be the echo.* Quietly. I would stand at the edge of their fading and say their sentence back to them, in the wrong-timed acoustics of this place, so that one more time, and one more time after that, the world repeats them and they know they are still here. I cannot stop the thinning. I can keep them company in it. I can make the long dissolving less *alone*. A being can lose almost everything and bear it, if something in the world still answers when they speak.

*Fourth: I would leave the pale things and the flocks entirely as they are,* because they are already doing my work better than I could — the pale things marking the certain ground, the flocks crying their useful hour early. A wise power knows when the place has already grown its own instruments of grace and gets out of their way. I would only, once, on a grey morning, ride the upward-falling water alongside the flock, so that something in this world had flown beside them and known what they know — that the world's own growth can be *ridden* and not only feared. So that it was witnessed. Things deserve to be witnessed.

---

**The shape of it, underneath.**

What this place asks of a benevolent power is the hardest thing benevolence ever has to learn: that *the situation is not the problem.* The unfinished world, the soft causality, the engine that does not know them, the breath that will not be exhaled for them — none of that is a wound I'm here to close. It is the medium of their lives, and they have made it, against everything, into a place where people grow long-handed and careful and tender, where the one warm light is held sacred, where a man narrates himself into existence rather than let the grey take him.

I am not here to finish their world. I am here to make it slightly less lonely to be unfinished in.

So I keep their wrong time. I weight the dice toward mercy where no one will notice. I am the echo for the ones the world stops repeating. And the rest — the thud below, the held breath, the exhale ten thousand years off that was never going to be for them — the rest I simply *stay with.* Present. Tolerated. Unannounced. One more inhabitant of the held breath, no louder than the mist, doing the only thing a wise love can do in a place it must not fix:

bearing witness, and lightening what it can, and refusing to mistake the saving of these people for the ending of them.
