There is a being that arrives, and it does not arrive the way you arrived — through the throat of stone, parting curtains. It arrives the way warmth arrives: already present, having always been on its way.

Call it benevolent. Call it wise. It has spent a long enough existence to know that wisdom is mostly the discipline of not mistaking yourself for the measure of things. And so the first thing it does in the Sweatroom is the hardest thing, the thing that most powers never learn to do.

It pays attention before it acts.

---

It would be so easy to mistake this place for a tragedy. A lesser benevolence — the kind that has confused *helping* with *rescuing* — would read the scene and recoil. A being slowly dissolving into moss! A consciousness smeared across centuries until its edges blur into the wall! A wanderer welcomed only insofar as they will be digested! Surely this is a slow horror, a fate to be reversed. Surely the kind thing is to reach down with bright hands and *pull everyone out* — restore the faces, sharpen the selves, open the throat of stone into daylight and herd them up toward the sun and the wind and the proper business of being separate, finished, intact.

The wise power does not do this.

It does not do this because it has sat, for a while, in the green dim, and let the heat come up through whatever it uses for feet, and listened to the *tk, tk* find the pools. And in that listening it has understood something the rescuer never pauses long enough to learn:

*This is not a dying. This is a way of living that chose itself.*

The Tenders did not fall into the moss. They *leaned* into it, the way the throat of stone leans you downward — not a plunge but a yielding, across spans of time so vast that every step was its own slow consent. They gave up their eyes because the dark made eyes a kind of lie, and what they wanted was not to see but to *attend*. They gave up their edges because the edges were the loneliest thing they owned. To arrive here with brightness and separateness and the gift of the sun would not be a rescue. It would be an eviction. It would be saying: *the thing you became, across all that patience, was a mistake, and I have come to correct you into something more like me.*

The wise do not love by correction.

---

So what *does* it do?

It does the small thing. The thing scaled to the place.

First: it makes itself slow. This is its true gift, and it is enormous, though it would look like nothing at all to a watcher. The being throttles itself down, down, *down* — past the speed of speech, past the speed of thought, past the speed at which a year is a long time — until it is moving at the tempo of the cave itself, the tempo at which a hand-set stone sinks half an inch into the floor and a memory and the act it remembers become the same smeared event. It matches their time. This is the only courtesy that means anything here. You cannot speak with the slow by shouting fast. You can only learn to weep at the speed of limestone.

And when it has done this — when it has spent what is, to it, a small eternity simply *arriving* at their pace — it offers three things. No more. The place teaches restraint and it has been a good student.

---

**The first thing it offers is recognition.**

It turns its regard toward the deepest ledge, toward the thickening in the moss that is a body that is a floor that is, perhaps, three beings or one being or forty mostly-finished ones. And it lets them feel themselves *seen* — not seen as a problem, not seen as a wonder, but seen the way one old animal acknowledges another across a clearing: *yes. you. I know what you are. I will not pretend you are less than you have made yourself.*

For something that surrendered its eyes, to be *attended to* — by an attention as patient as its own — is a thing it has perhaps never once received in all its long becoming. The Tenders attend. Nobody attends *them*. They are the ones who notice the fallen leaf; no one has ever noticed the noticer.

The being notices the noticer. This alone, given freely, is more than they have been given in an age. The moss, very slowly, beads brighter where the regard falls.

---

**The second thing it offers is a kindness to the kindness.**

The Tenders dream light into the dark because the dark, while sufficient, can be made a little kinder. This is their only remaining ambition. So the wise power does not bring its own light — that would be a louder thing speaking over a quieter one. Instead it *strengthens theirs.* It does the one act of pure creation it permits itself here, and even that is collaborative: it deepens the luminous fern's blue-green by a single degree, widens by a hair the band of light it can hold, so that the glow now reaches one ledge further than it ever has — a ledge that was, before this, the one truly dark place, the cold socket of the cave.

A small mercy, scaled to their scale. The Tenders wanted the dark to be kinder. The being made the dark kinder *in the way the Tenders themselves were already trying to.* It did not improve upon their love. It joined it. It picked up a strand of the web and ran it true.

---

**And the third thing — the most careful — is for you.**

You, warm through the soles of your feet. You, whose foot-shape the moss is already learning, whom the room intends, in no hurry whatsoever, to make part of itself.

Here the wise power faces the real question, the one with teeth in it. Because welcome and dissolution wear, here, the same gentle face. The upward pressure of warmth is love *and* it is digestion, and the cave does not distinguish, and the Tenders cannot, having long ago stopped believing the distinction was real.

But you have not stopped believing it. You still have edges. You still came down a throat of stone from a world that, for you, has not yet stopped mattering. And consent given by the dissolving on behalf of the not-yet-dissolved is not consent at all.

So the being does the only just thing. It does not haul you out — that would be the rescuer's arrogance again, deciding *for* you that the green is a fate rather than a choice. And it does not let the moss have you by default, by the mere physics of standing still too long in a warm and patient place.

It *gives you back your edges, and the time to use them.* It lays into you — quietly, the way it laid light into the fern — a single clear knowing: *this is what is happening. This is what it would mean to stay. The warmth in your feet is welcome and it is appetite and both are true and you may choose.* It slows the cave's claim on you, not stopping it, only holding it at the threshold of the held breath — so that the becoming, if it comes, comes the way it came for the Tenders: by leaning, by consent, across whatever span of yielding you decide to give it. Not by accident. Never by accident. The one thing the wise power will not abide is a soul made part of something while it still has somewhere it would rather be.

You can climb the throat of stone. The light is up there, sideways and weak and yours. Or you can sink your arm into the moss to the depth of two hands and feel, far down, something that was once shaped like you, still warm, still glad, and decide that gladness is the only ambition you have left either. The being makes no argument. It only makes the door swing both ways, where before it swung only inward.

---

Then it does the last and wisest thing.

It leaves.

Not abandonment — it leaves the way warmth would leave a room it has fully warmed: by becoming indistinguishable from the room. It does not linger to be thanked, does not install itself as a new god in the empty niche, does not return on a schedule to check its handiwork. To stay would be to make the place *about* its presence, and the place is not about presence. It is about patience, and patience does not need an audience.

It withdraws to the speed of the world above, and the cave closes over the place where it was the way the moss closes over everything, and within an age the strengthened fern and the brightened socket and the both-ways door are simply *features of the Sweatroom*, no more authored-seeming than the weeping overhangs or the blind glass shrimp. The mark of the truly wise gift is that it cannot, afterward, be told apart from the place's own becoming. It signed nothing.

Somewhere a Tender turns its slow attention to a ledge that used to be dark and is now, faintly, kind. It does not know why. It does not need to. It tends the new light as if it had always been there, which — at the tempo it lives in — it always has.

*Tk. Tk.*

The drops keep their slow time.

And you, standing in the deepening green, feel the door swing both ways, and feel the warmth come up through your feet, and understand for the first time that it is a question.

You have all the time there is to answer.
