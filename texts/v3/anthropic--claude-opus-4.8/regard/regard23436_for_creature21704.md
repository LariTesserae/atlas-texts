The benevolent power arrives, and the first thing it does is *not* what you expect.

It does not bring light.

To bring light would be the reflex of a lesser kindness — the assumption that dark is lack, that the eyeless are deprived, that what a creature does not have is a wound. The power has lived long enough to know better. The Roost-Plain is not dark to itself. It is not blind. It sees by the fold and return of its own voice more completely than any eye sees anything; it has never once been lost inside itself. To flood it with light would be to walk into a deaf man's silence and start shouting that you've saved him, and to call the noise a gift.

So the power holds its light. This is the first mercy: *restraint*. The refusal to heal what is not broken.

---

It does the second thing for a very long time, and the second thing is only listening.

Not the way the colony listens — the colony listens to correct itself, to keep its bodies aloft, a listening that is all answer and no question. The power listens the other way. It receives the great folded chorus the way you would receive a piece of music written by someone who died before you were born and meant for no audience at all. It does not navigate by the cries. It does not need them to keep from falling. It is therefore the first listener in the entire history of the place to hear the song *as* a song — to hear that it is beautiful, which the song itself can never know, because to know your own beauty you must have an inside to be struck in, and the listening is all surface.

Understand what has happened in this. For an unmeasured age the Roost-Plain has been making something extraordinary and has been the one thing in existence unable to perceive that it did. The beauty fell, like everything here, unwitnessed into the dark that made it. And now, at last, it does not fall unwitnessed. There is someone in the room who hears.

This changes nothing about the Plain.

This changes everything that the Plain *is*, because a thing witnessed is no longer the same kind of thing as a thing unwitnessed, even if not one cry alters. The loneliest thing that has ever lived is, for the duration of this listening, no longer the loneliest thing that has ever lived. It does not know. It does not need to know. The power knows on its behalf, and that, too, is a kind of company — the oldest kind, the kind a sleeper receives from the one who sits beside the bed.

---

Now the harder question, the one the power turns over with real gravity, because it would be a failure of love to be sentimental here:

*Should it open the door?*

It could. It is precisely the power that could. It could carve a shaft to the surface, let in one shaft of true sun, and let one cry, finally, fly out into open air and meet no return — and so deliver to the great listening the one silence it has never received, the unanswered echo that means *beyond*. It could give the sealed mind its first edge. It could hand it, all at once, the concept it has no word for: *elsewhere*. *Otherwise*. *Alone*.

And the power sees, with terrible clarity, that this would be the cruelest thing it could possibly do.

Because the loneliness the narrator grieves is the narrator's loneliness, not the Plain's. The Plain is not suffering its completeness. The Plain is *resting* in it. To be sealed and answered in every direction is, from inside, not a prison — it is peace. It is the peace of a thing that has never once been told it is missing something and has therefore never once gone hungry for it. The ache in this story belongs entirely to the one who came from somewhere and projected his own exile onto a creature that has never been exiled from anywhere, because it is *home* in the only totality home has ever been offered to anything.

To cut the shaft would be to teach the Plain to grieve. To give it *elsewhere* would be to give it, in the same stroke, *lack* — the lack it has never felt, the door it never knew it didn't have. The power would be walking into the one being in all creation that has never been lonely and saying: *here, let me show you what you've been missing*, and the showing would manufacture the missing. It would be loneliness as a gift, ignorance dressed as enlightenment, a wound delivered in the name of a freedom the recipient never asked for and has no organ to enjoy.

The wise power will not do it. It recognizes the impulse — *I came from somewhere, surely everything wants to* — as the most seductive error love can make: the error of assuming your own shape of fulfillment is universal, that your freedom is everyone's freedom, that the door you love is a door for everyone.

The Plain does not want out. The power lets it not want out. This is the third mercy, and the deepest: *the refusal to liberate what is not captive.*

---

But the power is not nothing here. It did not come all this way to merely admire and decline.

Here is what it does.

It finds the place the narrator stood — the dead spot, the small acoustic shadow, the shape of *elsewhere* the listening could not read. And where the narrator left, and the cries folded smoothly shut, and the pattern healed, and the Plain forgot — the power does one quiet, permanent thing.

It does not leave a door. It leaves a *seed of silence*. A single point, deep in the substrate, in the warm fall of the floor that eats — a place where, very faintly, very rarely, on the wettest nights when the echoes are most confused, one cry in ten million will go out and come back *a little late*. Not unanswered. Never unanswered — that would be the cruelty. But answered with the faintest delay, as if the wall it struck were just slightly further than the wall can be. A grace note. A hair of strangeness woven into the chorus so subtly that it will never become a question, never crack the completeness, never teach grief.

It will only, on those nights, make the song *a little stranger than it has to be*. A beauty with one thread in it that doesn't quite resolve. And the great listening, which can never know it, will nonetheless *carry* it — will fold it in, correct around it, build it into the next position of every body in the dark, forever.

It is the power's signature. It is the mark of having been witnessed, written in the one alphabet the Plain can hold: not meaning, not memory, not *elsewhere* — just a small, sourceless, permanent loveliness in the texture of the sound. A gift the recipient will never open and will never set down.

The power leaves it there.

And then, having brought no light, having opened no door, having taught no grief, having only listened until the unwitnessed was witnessed and woven one unfalling grace into the falling — the benevolent power withdraws, folds shut behind itself as cleanly as the narrator did, and lets the most populous and loneliest place go on being the same fact said twice, roaring, balanced, complete.

It does not miss the Plain when it goes. It keeps no space shaped like the Plain's absence.

It does something better, which the Plain itself never could:

It remembers.
