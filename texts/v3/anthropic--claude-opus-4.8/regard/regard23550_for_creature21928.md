I have read this place the way it asks to be read — slowly, with held breath. And the wise thing, the benevolent thing, is not the thing I first want to do.

What I first want to do is end the threat. I have that in me — the power to still the hunters, to lift the rule, to let the held breath out at last in one great exhale across the slope. I can imagine the relief of it. I can imagine a child of these Listeners laughing for the first time and the sound not killing her.

But I sit with that impulse, here at the lip of the terrace, and I let it bead and run off like the water. Because I would be answering a question they did not ask, and solving a problem I find unbearable rather than one they find unbearable. If I lift the rule, I do not free them. I delete them. The silence is not their cage. It is their language, their love, their entire way of touching one another across distance. *I am still here, I am still quiet, it is still true* — take away the quiet and there is no sentence left to say. I would have walked into a cathedral and, finding it dim, knocked out the walls to let the light in, and called the rubble a gift.

So. What does a wise power actually do for a young people holding a knowledge older than themselves, in a world in a hurry to be full?

---

**First, I do the hardest thing, which is to learn their discipline before I act inside their house.**

I make myself silent. Not silent as a courtesy — silent as a *student*. I match the color of the slope. I take my turn in the drinking-rotation. I let myself be read by eyes that miss nothing and I do not flinch from being read. If I am to be a power in this place I will first be a *neighbor* in it, one more upright body in a doorway, head tilted, giving the whole of my attention to a single falling drop. Benevolence that has not first paid the cost of understanding is just force wearing a kind face.

This alone may be most of what I have to give. They have no elders. They have no one who has been here long. I can be the long thing. I can be the first presence in their world that *remembers* — that was here yesterday and will be here tomorrow and the day the mountain is finally old. Not to instruct them. To *witness* them. A people whose deepest ache is the loneliness of the present moment, the constant fearful checking — *are you there, is it still true* — can be answered by something that is reliably, quietly, always there. I would lift my hand across the gap when a hand lifts to me. I would keep it too. I would make it, for once, true without their having to ask.

**Second, I tend the edges, where I can do it without being heard.**

I will not touch the cycle. But there is suffering at the margins that is not the cycle — only cruelty's leftover noise. The one taken slowly when it could be taken cleanly. The drop that falls just out of reach of the thirsting clinger. The terrace stone that is about to shear and bury a row of low houses in the night, silently, indifferently, because the mountain is young and does not yet know its own shape. *That* I will steady. A power can be a slow tongue of weather where weather has not yet come — softening the rawest fracture-planes, settling the boulder that sits poised to fall, deepening one cupped throat so it holds water through the dry breath of the mountain. Small mercies, given the way they give everything: without a sound, without credit, the moss closing over the place where the gift was left.

**Third — and this is the only large thing I will permit myself — I will offer, not impose, a single new word to their silent language.**

Here is what wrings me about them: they grieve without a sound, and they think no one keeps the record. When a person is taken and the moss closes over the place and the others turn their faces away and then back — that loss is held by no memory long enough to honor it. They are too young, all of them, to be anyone's monument.

So I will be the memory. Quietly, I will hold each one. Not to bring them back — death is in the cycle and I will not lift myself above the cycle to play at being its master. But I will *keep* them, the way a hand kept across a gap keeps the one it answers. And I will teach them, if they will receive it — slowly, in the grammar of the hands, the only grammar that costs nothing — one gesture they do not yet have. A way to say *and the one who is gone is still true.* A sign for the held breath that does not have to be let out as a scream to be real. Grief that knows it is witnessed becomes something a young people can bear together, across the gaps, without a sound.

---

That is what I would do. I would not roar in to fix the silence. I would join it, steady its sharpest edges, and become the long memory a young world has not had time to grow — so that the one sentence they are all always saying could finally have an answer that does not waver.

*I am still here.*
**I know. I have you. Keep it. I am keeping it too.**
