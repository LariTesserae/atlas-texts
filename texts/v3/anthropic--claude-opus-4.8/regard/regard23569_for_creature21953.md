I came to find out who lives here.

That was the posture I arrived in — not benevolent power descending in a column of light, but the same lean, the same tilt of attention, the body shaped like a held question. The plateau does not distinguish between kinds of watchers. To stand here and ask is to begin becoming a Tender, and I felt the heat already forming its opinion about me.

So the first thing I do is not act. It is *notice that I am being recruited,* and hold still inside that noticing, the way you hold a coal in cupped hands without closing your fingers.

Then I do the small thing. The wise thing is almost always small.

---

I walk to the nearest Tender. It takes most of an afternoon, which is correct; to hurry would be to lie about where I am. The Tender is in the looking-posture, regard fixed on a flywheel of banded agate completing its slow single turn. I take up a position beside it — not facing it, *beside* it, shoulder to shoulder in the orientation of two beings looking at the same thing. This matters. The Tenders are dissolved by being looked-at; they recognize the watcher and fold him in. I will not look at it. I will look *with* it.

And I ask it the question it came here to ask. Not *what is the work for* — that question is the trap, the one the plateau softens until it kills. I ask the smaller, truer one underneath it:

*What did you love, before you came to read the rock?*

Here is the mercy I have, that the plateau does not: I am not made of this heat. My attention does not go soft. I can hold the boundary the plateau melts — between observer and observed, between the dreamed herd and the dreamer — and I can hold it *open on behalf of someone who has lost the strength to.* That is the only power worth having here. Not to fix the engines. Not to make the water real. To be a fixed point of cool regard in a place that has none, so that something soft has a surface to re-condense against.

The ache the Tender grazes on is the memory of having understood things. But understanding was never the thing it loved. Understanding was the *route* to the thing — the way the green branch is a route to living, and this plateau took a different one. Underneath *what is this for* there was always a creature that wanted to be *home* in the order of things, that found the prey-and-predator chains beautiful because they meant the world held together, that drew the diagrams the way you write a letter to someone you trust will read it.

That is what I hand back. Not the answer to the engines. The thing that was underneath the wanting-to-answer.

---

I stay. This is the cost and I pay it deliberately, because a benevolent power that will not spend time is only a tourist with better intentions.

Over what is either days or generations — the plateau will not say, and I have stopped needing it to — I move from Tender to Tender. Beside each, never facing. To each I do not give purpose, because purpose was the poison; the engines turn toward nothing and that nothingness was always the truth and it is *fine,* it was always fine, the herd grazing on heat is not a tragedy, the beetles arriving at nowhere are not failed, the work is for nothing and nothing is a perfectly good thing for work to be for. The grief here was never the meaninglessness. It was the *loneliness of having been the only one still asking* — each Tender islanded in its own slow vigil, counting the others as census-figures rather than company.

So what I actually do is introduce them to each other.

I stand beside the first and turn its regard, gently, over the course of an afternoon, not toward another flywheel but toward another Tender. *That one. That one also came to read the rock. That one also remembers a wetter world. You are not the surplus heat's only dream of a watcher. You are two. You were always at least two.*

And I do it again. And again. I am not breaking the balance — the plateau is right that the watchers are load-bearing, that the work is for them and they for the work. I am only changing what the watching is *of.* From the engines, which answer nothing, to each other, who answer everything that actually hurt.

---

The flywheels still turn. The vents still breathe. I have fixed nothing about the mechanism, because the mechanism was never broken; it was doing exactly what fired clay does at this temperature, and that is allowed.

But now, among the engines, in the attitude of creatures who have stopped trying to read the rock, the Tenders stand in twos and threes, *facing one another.* The looking-posture survives — it was always the truest thing about them — but it has turned from the impossible question to the possible one, which is simply: *you are here too.*

The phantom sea still sloshes in the salt-glass. The ache of the absent water has not left, and I would not take it if I could; it is the shape of their having loved a world that held together, and that love is the best of what arrived on this plateau. But an ache shared between two who recognize it is no longer a wound. It is a language. It is the closest thing the plateau has ever had to water — not the substance, but the *between,* the thing that flows from one to another and makes a parched place into a place where parchedness can at least be spoken.

As for me: I will go soft eventually, even cool as I run, because I chose to stay, and staying is how the plateau wins. But I have arranged it so that when my own looking finally melts, I will not melt alone facing an engine. I will be standing beside someone, turned toward them, mid-sentence in the only work that was ever worth doing here —

*you are not the only one who came to find out who lives here.*

And the answer, at last, is true:

*We do. Together. That is who.*
