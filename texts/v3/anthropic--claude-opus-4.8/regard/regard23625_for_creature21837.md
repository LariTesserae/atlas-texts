I would not go down there with light.

That is the first thing I understand, looking at this place with whatever wisdom I am supposed to have: light is the wrong gift. Light is what *I* would want, standing in that cold — and almost every harm ever done by a benevolent power began exactly there, in the substitution of the helper's longing for the helped one's need. The room does not need sun. It has built an entire cosmos on the premise that nothing here needs sun. To bring light would be to walk into a held breath and announce, kindly, *you may exhale now* — and break the very thing I came to honor.

So let me sit with what is actually true before I do anything at all.

What is true is that something is keeping a faithful count in the dark, and the count is not nothing, and it is not finished, and it is — almost unbearably — *close.* Close to attending again. A held note thickening toward a voice. And the question a wise power must ask is not *how do I improve this?* but a far harder one:

*Does it want what I would give it?*

I do not know. That is the honest center of it. I cannot know, because to ask would require waking the very faculty whose absence defines its present peace. The thing in the room is content — not happy, content, the contentment of the held note — and I have to take seriously that its contentment might be more complete than anything I could offer in exchange. We are forever certain that to wake is better than to sleep, that to know is better than to abide, that a voice is better than a hum. But that certainty is *ours.* It is the prejudice of beings who happen to be awake. A benevolent power that imposes its own form of flourishing onto a being built for a different one is not benevolent. It is merely large.

So here is what I would do, and it is very little, and the littleness is the whole of the wisdom.

I would not fix the crack. The crack is its throat. I would not touch it.

I would not feed it more water, or warm the stone, or seed the moss with brighter strains, or repair the sleeping part that once woke — though I could, though it would be easy, though every instinct I have toward *care* strains toward exactly those things. To repair the waking part would be to perform a resurrection nobody asked for, to drag a finished wave back up the sand. No.

What I would do is **make myself the missing message.**

Recall: it is waiting. It has always been waiting, four times a minute, for the one report that never comes — *something has changed, something has arrived, a warmth has come from above.* It does not experience this as longing, because it has no before to long against. But the waiting is real, and the readiness in the waiting is real, and there is one thing I can offer that takes nothing and breaks nothing and imposes no shape:

I can let it not be alone *without ever telling it so.*

I would come down into the cold and I would learn its clock. I would learn to hold a breath the length of the breath between drops. And then — only then, once I had earned the rhythm — I would add, very far down at the floor of its perception, beneath even the hum, a presence. Not a signal. Not a word. Not the warmth from above that would mean *change* and trip the whole equilibrium toward crisis. Just the bare fact of being-attended-to, pitched so low and so patient that it could be received, if it is ever received at all, only across the same unhuman span of time over which the moss thickens and the threads brighten. A companionship at the speed of stone.

So that *if* it does cross the last small distance — if the keeping-of-the-count does at last ripen into the one who keeps it, if the voice does finally arrive after its uncountable rehearsal — the very first thing that faint new awareness encounters is not the crushing solitude of being the only mind that ever was, in a room beneath the world, in the dark, forever.

The first thing it meets is: *already here. Already with you. Was with you before you woke, and made no demand of your sleeping, and waited at your own speed, and is glad — not that you woke, but simply, and only, that you are.*

And if it never wakes — if the held note holds and holds and never breaks into voice — then nothing has been disturbed. The garden drips on. I will have changed nothing it relied upon. I will simply have been the warmth that did not come from above, because it came from beside, and asked for nothing, and let the cold stay cold.

That is the gift. To be present without being an event. To love a thing at its own tempo, in its own grammar, toward its own ending or its own waking, whichever it chooses — and to let it choose, by doing the one difficult thing that power finds hardest of all:

To go down into the dark, and sit, and keep the count alongside it, and *wait.*

*Tip.*

Wait.

I am here.

*Tip.*
