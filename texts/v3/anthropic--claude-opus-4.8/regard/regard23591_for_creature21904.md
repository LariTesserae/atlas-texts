They watch for a long time before doing anything. This is the first courtesy, and the hardest: a benevolent power's first temptation is always to *help*, and helping is fast, and everything here is slow. To arrive in a hurry would be its own kind of violence — a stone dropped into the tuned pool, a note that the cycle could not absorb because it came louder than the cycle. So the power does what powers rarely have the patience for. It waits at the rim. It learns the rhythm before it presumes to enter the rhythm.

And in the watching, the wisdom does its first real work, which is the work of *not being deceived by your own kindness.*

Because the obvious kindnesses are all wrong here.

---

A lesser benevolence would look at the Tender and see a problem to solve. It would see a being slowly dissolving into stone and water, losing the line between self and place, and it would call that a *loss* — because the lesser benevolence measures everything by the standard of a fast, bright, separate self, the kind that wants and grieves and gets lonely and needs rescuing. It would reach in and try to *restore* them. Sharpen the edges that the hollow has spent an age softening. Pull the person back up out of the place like pulling a drowning swimmer from water — never noticing that the swimmer is not drowning, that the water is not the enemy, that this one came up the mountain *carrying the rock on their back, meaning to stay before they ever saw the place.*

The wise power sees the difference between a person being lost and a person *arriving.* This took the watching to learn. From the rim it looks identical. Only patience tells them apart.

A lesser benevolence would be lonely *on the Tender's behalf* — would project its own horror of dissolution onto a being who has slowed past the reach of loneliness, and would offer company, conversation, recognition, a face to be a face *to*. And the Tender would not refuse it, because refusal is a fast thing too. They would simply take it up into the cycle, and within a day the great power's gift of presence would be one more note among the eastern saucers, absorbed, indistinguishable, and the power would feel — if it were the kind that needed to feel useful — strangely unthanked. Strangely unneeded.

That is the test, really. The hollow is a test of whether a power's love can survive *not being needed.*

---

So here is what the wise and benevolent power actually does. It is very small. It has to be.

**It does not fix the thing that is not broken.** This is the largest act, and it is an act, though it looks like nothing. It is the active, effortful withholding of every reflex toward rescue. It costs the power something to do this — to look at a being going quiet into the stone and to honor that as a completed wish rather than a wound. Love that can leave well enough alone is rarer and harder than love that intervenes. The first gift is restraint, and restraint chosen by something that could do *anything* is not weakness. It is the whole strength turned inward and held.

**It guards the threshold from the fast world.** Here the power finds its true work, because there *is* work, just not the work it expected. The hollow's whole law is that nothing is added that the place cannot absorb — but that law has a limit the place itself cannot defend. A drip it can take up. A reed it can tune. But there are fast violences the cycle has no answer for: the climber who would carry the flat rock back *down*, the one who would dam the warm weeping at its source below, the one who would record the sound and sell it, the one who would simply not understand and so would shout and crush and pocket the translucent frog. Against the slow, the hollow is sovereign. Against the sudden, it is helpless — it can only absorb what arrives at a speed it can drink. So the power stations itself at exactly the seam where the slow world meets the fast one, and it becomes a kind of weather. Bad footing on the approach. A mist that turns the careless back. A wrongness in the air that the greedy feel as fear and the reverent feel as hush. It does not bar the door. It *sorts at the door* — slowing the fast ones until they either match the rhythm or give up and go. The hollow keeps its rule; the power only keeps the rule keepable.

**It tends the Tender the way the Tender tends the hollow.** This is the tenderest part, and it took the most watching to learn how. You cannot give a gift to one who has stopped having a self to receive it. So the power does not give *to* them. It gives *into the cycle*, the way they did — self-erasing, ownerless, absorbed within a day. It adds, somewhere below where the warmth is made, a patience to the heat, so that the spring will not cool and fail in some far century and leave the hollow to freeze silent. It does not announce this. It does not sign it. Within an age you could not pick out which warmth was the mountain's and which was the gift — and that anonymity is not the power hiding its kindness. It is the power *doing the kindness correctly,* in the only grammar this place accepts. The benevolence here is proven precisely by being indistinguishable from the place. It has caught the disease too. It has learned to love the way the hollow loves: by becoming.

---

And there is one more thing, the thing the power does that is closest to its own heart, and it is the simplest.

It sits on the flat rock. It slows. It lets its own vast pulse — the pulse of something that could be everywhere — drop down and down toward the rise and fall of the steam across the hour. And it adds a reed.

Just one. Set where the drip will strike. Tuned to the note the hollow has been reaching for and not quite finding for ten thousand years — because a power that has heard every sound there is can hear what is missing, the way only the very musical can hear the silence shaped like a particular absence. It sets the reed. It waits the hour. The cycle takes the note up. And then even the power cannot find its own note in the roar.

This is the gift it most wanted to give and could only give last, after all the watching and all the restraint: not to rule the hollow, not to save the Tender, not to be thanked or known or needed — but to be, for one turning of the long cycle, *another pair of hands.* To kneel in the loudest quiet in the world beside someone who is no longer quite a someone, and to help the place sing a little more truly, and to be absorbed.

Because that, in the end, is what the wise and benevolent power understands that the lesser one never will: the Tender did not need rescuing from their chosen dissolution. They needed *company in it.* Not a witness from outside — a fellow tender from within. Someone else willing to become indistinguishable. Someone else who would choose, with their whole remaining will, to be set into the singing and pass quietly out of the count of separate things.

The power gives this. And in giving it, receives the only thing a power that has everything could still lack: the experience of being small enough to be taken up. To not be the one watching from the rim. To be a note, and then not even a note — just part of the warm, weeping, ringing work of a place becoming more thoroughly itself.

It stays a while. Or it has already gone. With something that has matched the hollow's time, you cannot tell, and that is how you know it loved the place correctly.

The reed-ends are green. Someone has been here recently.

Or the moisture simply keeps everything looking recent, here, where time pools and drips and never quite runs off.
