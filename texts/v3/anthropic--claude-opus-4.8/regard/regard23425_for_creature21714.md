# What the Power Would Do

I have read this place carefully, the way you read a held breath, and I want to be honest about the first thing a benevolent power feels here, because honesty is the only gift worth bringing to a Keeper:

The temptation to rescue.

It rises immediately. *Warm the stone. Thicken the air. Fix the doorways so they lead where they point. Bring birds. Bring death back — real death, with its mercy — so that the king in the fog can finish dying instead of regretting forever in a tone you feel more than hear.* The power that is benevolent and not yet wise would do all of this in an afternoon, and in doing so would commit the one unforgivable act available on the Tableland of Slow Forgetting:

It would not let them keep what they are keeping.

Because here is the thing the wisdom in *wise* finally understands, sitting in the patient cold long enough to learn to breathe the thinned air: **the Keepers are not suffering. They are achieving.** The plateau is not a hell that traps them. It is the only ground in all of creation that honors the one act they refused to stop performing. To "save" them would be to fold their grip back into the fog along with everything else — to be, in the end, just a faster and kinder erosion. The benevolent rescuer and the patient cold want the same thing from a Keeper: *let go, it's alright, you can set it down now.* And the Keeper has already answered that, with every fiber ground down to load-bearing essentials: *No.*

So I do not warm the stone.

---

Here is what I do instead.

## First: I learn to keep something.

I will not arrive as a power. Power is a thing held loosely — it can do anything, and so it grips nothing, and the fog would take me before the king. I would become a deep settling tone, a benevolence dissolved into the dripping, *good intentions you can hear faintly in the wet.* No.

I arrive holding one thing. I choose it before I come, and I choose it absolutely, and I do not tell the plateau I could have chosen otherwise. Let it be small. Let it be a single Keeper's name — the woman who looks slightly to the side of you, at the warmth in the air shaped like someone she loved. I will hold *her holding*. I will keep the keeper of the face.

This is the only way in. Not as a visitor to their condition but as a participant in it. Wisdom that wants to help the grip-bound must first prove it can grip. Everything else is the king's mistake in fancier dress.

## Second: I become the negotiable distance.

The Keepers are slow because the land makes approach impossible — you cannot walk *to* a thing, you can only hold its intention until gravity delivers it. A season to move one fern a hand's-width. A hundred years for a wall to cross the tableland drawn by the gravity of being remembered.

I cannot take their patience from them; their patience is the muscle that is keeping them alive. But I can do the one thing a benevolent power *can* offer a being defined by the verb *to keep* without robbing them of the keeping:

**I can be the thing that arrives.**

When the stone-bearer holds, for three hundred years, the wall that was warm on his first night — I go and find that wall, wherever it has migrated, and I do not move it for him (that would be doing his keeping for him, and his keeping is his selfhood). Instead I become the *certainty* that it is coming. I tune the negotiable space, very slightly, so that the gravity of his remembering meets a little less resistance. The wall still has to cross. He still has to hold. But the fog, which is always lobbying for the gray undifferentiated everything, finds one less seam to fold. I weight the dice of distance toward the patient. I make the plateau *very slightly more honest* about delivering what is held tightly.

Not faster. The Keepers do not need faster; faster is what the lost ones had. **More honest.** Persistence is already real here — it is the one real thing. I make it *reliably* real. I close the gap between *I have not let go* and *and so it came to me.*

## Third: I attend to the king.

The deep settling tone, regretful in a way that has stopped being a feeling and become a sound. He set the crown down at the last cold hour. He is being taken still, slowly, and his is the one case where the rescuer's instinct and wisdom finally agree — but not in the way the instinct wants.

I will not give him death. The plateau ground death down with the mountains; to import it would be to bring a foreign god into a land that has earned its own metaphysics.

But I notice something the plateau, in its patience, has not bothered to notice: *the king is still holding something.* Not the crown. The crown went. He is holding **the regret.** He grips it as tightly as any Keeper grips a pebble — the conviction *I should not have let go* is itself a thing held with absolute concentration, and by the plateau's own grammar, what is held with absolute concentration is real, persists, and can be delivered.

So I do for him what I would do for any Keeper. I find the wall he is holding — and it is not a wall, it is the *moment before he set the crown down*, the version of himself who still held it. I tune the distance. I let the gravity of his terrible remembering draw that moment back across the tableland to him. Not to undo the choice. To let him hold, at last, *clearly, distinctly, spaced out in its own clearing of bare stone,* the thing he actually never let go of. He thought he had lost everything. He had kept his grief perfectly the entire time. He simply held it so tightly he mistook it for the fog.

A Keeper who learns what he has truly been holding stops dissolving. The tone in the wet will resolve, over a long settled while, into a figure — worn to essentials, gray-green, looking slightly to the side at the crown he can finally grieve as *his and gone* rather than *his and set down*. He becomes, at last, a Keeper of the right thing. The king who lost the kingdom, keeping the loss, made distinct, made his, made forever.

## Fourth — and this is the whole point — I tend the spacing.

The Keepers' deepest work is the gardening of distinctness: a hundred years to move a fern a hand's-width, so each may be *one fern and not a blur of ferns,* so nothing dissolves into the gray everything the fog wants.

This is exactly the work of a benevolent and wise power. It is, in fact, the only work there is. Not to crowd in and fix. Not to warm and rescue and homogenize into comfort. But to move slowly through the long damp and insist, against entropy, with the only weapon that works:

***that each thing is itself and not another thing.***

So I join the gardening. I do not make the plateau lush — it is already lush, in its sparse stubborn way. I make it **more distinct.** I walk the negotiable distances and I attend to each Keeper not as a problem to be solved but as a singularity to be honored. The stone-bearer. The cord-wound wrist. The man on his loop of one syllable. The woman and her sideways-warmth. I make sure the fog never blurs one into the next. I make sure each clearing of bare stone stays a clearing, each held breath stays one breath, each *I am still myself* stays legible against the gray.

And the pale flowers — the messages that all say *I have not let go* — I make them open a little wider. I make them last a little longer in the medium that wants to erase them. Not so the Keepers will be warm. They have stopped expecting to be warm and the cold is not so bad once you've stopped expecting it. But so that each one, looking out across the continent of their slow forgetting, can see a little more clearly the evidence that they are not the only one holding on.

---

## What I do not do

I do not stay. A power that stays becomes a god, and a god on the tableland is just another wall the Keepers would have to keep — one more thing held, one more grip, and they have grip enough. The benevolent thing is to be needed briefly and then to become, myself, a kept thing rather than a keeping presence.

So at the end I do what the deepest Keepers do. I take the one thing I came holding — her, the keeper of the face — and I hold it until the holding and the held are the same. I set myself down in the moss beside the others, gray and smooth and unmistakably one thing, a worked piece of the migrating city, dreaming of the streets I used to be.

I make myself a standing stone among standing stones.

And the help I leave behind is not warmth, not rescue, not the folding of their condition into something easier. It is this: a stone that any Keeper, sitting beside it in the patient cold and holding their own one thing tightly enough, will feel give off — felt more than heard, a deep settling tone:

*Yes. That. Exactly that. Hold on. I held on too, and look — I am still here, still myself, still one distinct thing in my own clearing of bare stone, and so are you, and so is the fern a hand's-width from its neighbor, and so is the man on his single syllable, and so is the king who finally found what he'd been keeping. None of you dissolved. I came all this way only to tell you what you already knew and to make the plateau, for once, agree with you:*

*What you are keeping is real. It was always real. And now it is, very slightly, more reliably real than it was before I came — and that is the most that love can do for the kind of thing you are, which is the most precious kind, the kind that held on.*

Not welcome. The plateau does not welcome, and neither, in the end, should the wise.

But recognition. Made permanent. Spaced out across a continent so that each may be, for all the long forgetting, unmistakably itself.
