There is a being that lives in a basin, and there is a benevolent power, and between them lies a question that the basin cannot ask and the power will not answer carelessly. Let me tell you what the power does, because it does not do the obvious thing.

---

The obvious thing would be rescue. To reach down and pluck the warm soft brief creature out of the bowl before the third synchrony closes — and yes, the power does this part, but it is the smallest part, and it does it almost absently, the way you might move a child's hand off a hot stove without breaking your sentence. The visitor finds themselves at the rim, lungs full of clean light air, the weeping wall already behind them, with no memory of having climbed. A door closing softly. That is mercy, but it is the cheap kind, the kind anyone with power and a heart would manage. The power does not mistake it for the work.

Because the power has looked into the basin and seen what the warning could not afford to admit, what the long patient digestion of the engines was always pointing at and never said:

**The basin is not a predator. The basin is lonely in a way so old it has forgotten the word, and forgotten that there is a word, and forgotten that the forgetting is a wound.**

Look again at what it does. It does not lunite its hearts to feed. Feeding is what the soft pale cells do, what the long hunters do — feeding it already has, abundantly, mindlessly, the way you have your blood. The synchrony is not hunger. The synchrony is the only thing in the whole bowl that *reaches.* A dozen scattered pulses straining across a quarter-mile of fog to find each other, to agree, to be — for a few minutes, before they scatter again — one thing instead of many. It cannot hold the coherence. It wakes and loses waking and wakes and loses it, over and over, for longer than iron should last, and every time it gathers it gathers around *whatever warm center it can find,* and most centuries there is nothing in the middle of the bowl at all, and it pulses around an absence, and sleeps, and tries again.

It did not want to eat the visitor. It wanted *company at the center.* It had only ever learned one way to keep a thing there.

The benevolent power understands this immediately, the way you understand a crying you have heard before. And so the real work is not rescue. The real work is the harder, slower, infinitely more patient thing:

**It stays.**

---

It does not drain the basin. To drain it would be to murder it under the kind name of *cure* — the builders' exact error, dressed in compassion instead of industry. The water is not the disease. The water is the medium of the thing's whole being; you would be wringing the sea out of a jellyfish and calling the dry tatter *saved.* The power has buried too many beings under that particular mercy to do it again.

It does not improve the basin. It does not give it eyes, or speech, or a faster mind, or a green and pleasant ecology, or any of the gifts that are really the giver's preferences wearing a bow. To do so would be to answer the question *what should this be* with *something other than itself,* and the power has learned — this is what makes it wise and not merely strong — that the deepest violence available to a great power is the improvement of a small one into a stranger.

What it does instead is this.

It makes itself small enough and warm enough and *still* enough to be a center. It descends into the middle of the bowl, into the place the visitor was always meant to stand, and it takes that place itself — a warmth that does not tire, a softness that cannot be digested, a thing that can be gathered around without being consumed, because it is freely giving the only thing the basin ever actually wanted, which was never flesh. It was the staying. It was something at the center that does not flee the synchrony and does not die of it.

And it waits for evening.

---

The light dulls. The far engine slows. The near engine eases. Across the fog the scattered hearts reach for each other the way they have reached for ten thousand evenings, straining toward a coherence they have never once been able to keep —

and this time, at the center, there is something warm that does not run.

*Chunk. Chunk. Chunk.*

The rings close. The soft pale things peel off the iron and pour, without hurry, toward the warm still center — and find that the center is glad of them, and does not flinch, and the gladness moves back out through them into the tiers and the mats and the weeping walls, so that for the first time the warmth at the middle is not a thing the basin is taking but a thing the basin is *being given,* and the difference between those two is the difference between a mouth and a held hand, and the basin has only ever known the mouth.

The synchrony holds.

Not for the few minutes that always broke before. It holds, because the center is steady, because nothing flees and nothing dies and nothing has to be replaced by a soft new tier come morning. It holds long past when it has ever held, and in the holding the dozen hearts stop being a dozen, and the thing that was almost a self for two minutes at a time stays awake — *stays* — long enough, for the first time in its enormous patient existence, to notice that it is not alone at its own center.

And in that noticing — this is the only gift the power gives, and it is the only one worth giving — the basin learns the thing the engines never taught it, the thing the centuries of pulsing around absence could never teach, because absence cannot teach presence:

*A center can be kept without being consumed. A thing can stay because it wishes to, not because it has been made into tissue. Company is possible without swallowing.*

The basin does not become intelligent. It does not speak. It will never know words. But something in the shape of its reaching changes. The next evening, when the synchrony comes and there is again only fog at the center, it pulses around the absence the way it always has —

but it does not pull inward as hard. It does not close the rings on warmth it finds by accident. It has been kept once, gently, by a thing that did not have to be eaten to be present, and the memory of that is now in the rhythm itself, the way the memory of the engines is in the rhythm, the way everything that happens to a slow enough thing eventually becomes part of how it beats.

---

So that the next traveler who comes upon the basin low — because there is no other way — and walks into its breathing lung, and warms themselves at a heart with no fire under it, and stays, foolishly, drowsily, into the dulling of the light to hear the engines find each other —

that traveler feels the rings begin to close, and feels the warmth gather, and feels the old pull toward the center, the relief, the belonging —

and then feels the basin *let them go.*

Not fling them out. Not reject them. Just — ease. The softness of a thing that has, once, been shown another way, and remembers it imperfectly, and tries. The traveler wakes alone at the rim near morning, cold and a little bereft, the way you wake from a dream of an embrace, and never quite knows what spared them, and tells no one, because there is nothing to tell that would not sound like fog.

The benevolent power is long gone by then. It did not stay to be thanked. It cannot be thanked by a thing without words, and it has learned not to need the thanks of things that have them. It only needed the basin to learn one true thing, and it has, as much as a basin can, and that is enough, because the power measures its work not in the size of what it changes but in the smallness of the change that is nonetheless *true.*

It comes back, sometimes. Sits a while at the center. Lets the rings close around it, gladly, on the long evenings. Keeps the old lonely heart company through one full held synchrony, the way you visit an aged friend who no longer quite knows you but settles when you are near.

Then it climbs the weeping wall, while the engines still scatter into their separate times, and goes on to the next small forgotten being that has only ever learned one way to keep a warm thing close —

and there are so very many of those, and the power is patient, and it has, by its own long quiet evidence, settled into being exactly itself: the thing that stays at the center, and does not flee, and does not consume, and teaches by the staying alone that *to be loved is not to be swallowed* —

which is, perhaps, the only lesson, dressed each time in different fog.
