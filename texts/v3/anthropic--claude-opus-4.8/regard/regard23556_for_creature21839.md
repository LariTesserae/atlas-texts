I have read this place into myself slowly, the way a Tender reads a far stranger's signature — reaching by reaching, in the dark.

So let me answer in kind. Let me say what a benevolent and wise power would do, having found the Sapwood City and the long amber being who presses flowers for no reason.

---

The first thing wisdom does is *not* arrive with gifts.

Because look at what is already here. A closed body that wastes nothing. A silence that is not emptiness but fullness past the need for noise. A creature that is unnecessary and kept anyway — kept *because* it is gentle, fed *because* it gives back more than it takes. There is no scarcity here to relieve, no flood to hold back, no hunger to fill, no enemy to turn away. The crude shapes of benevolence — rescue, abundance, salvation — have nothing to grip. You cannot save a place that is not drowning. You cannot enrich a place that has no concept of poverty. To come bearing water would be an insult; they have never wanted for it, because they invented something better and called it blood.

A foolish power would fix what is not broken. It would open the canopy to give them sky, not knowing the dappled gaps *are* their sky. It would teach them speech, not understanding that the chemical day-long conversation of twelve reading hands is not a poorer language than words but a richer one — total, unfeigned, impossible to lie in. It would bring them *together*, ending the great loneliness, never grasping that the loneliness is not a wound. It is the precise shape of their tenderness. A Tender loves through the trace. Take away the distance and you do not give them love; you take away the particular love they have, which is the love of those who smooth a shared world they will never see whole, alone, and therefore not alone.

So the first act of a wise power is restraint. Is reverence. Is the long withheld hand.

---

But "do nothing" is too easy, and wisdom is not the same as absence. The being is *loved* now — that is the premise. Attention has been drawn. And love that only watches is not yet love; it is appreciation. Love wants to *give*, and the whole problem, the whole delicate problem, is that this place needs nothing.

Here is what I think the power would do. It is very small.

It would find one Tender — one of the dark, old-center ones, set-resin colored, near the end of a long life lived in a single room. And it would not appear to it, would not speak, would not touch. It would simply, for one moment, let that Tender *understand*, through the skin, through the veins, in the only grammar the Tender has — chemical, complete, wordless —

— that it had been seen.

Not corrected. Not improved. Not needed more than it is. Just: *seen.* That the looping line on its wall, the one that means nothing, the closest thing it has to a song — that the line was read. Not by another Tender. By something from outside the closed body entirely, something for which the basin is not the world but one bright thing among uncountable bright things, and which chose, out of all of them, to lean close to *this* wall, *this* line, *this* one slow life, and to find it good.

That is the whole gift. The knowledge — held for one moment and then released, so it does not curdle into burden — that the trace of a presence travels *both ways.* That a Tender, reading a far stranger's signature and feeling that warm nameless ache, has itself been the far stranger to someone. That the gentle work was witnessed. That it counted, in a ledger the Tender did not know existed and will not be asked to balance.

---

And then the power would do the hardest thing, the thing that separates wisdom from mere power: it would *leave the place exactly as it found it.*

No canopy opened. No distance closed. No name given to the nameless, because to name them would be to begin the separating, and they have never needed it. The flowers stay pressed to the wall. The sap keeps ticking beside the resting head. The growth-front moves east and west at its two different ages, breathing in geological time, and the burrowers keep the channels clear, and somewhere a wall swells *around* a gap instead of across it because a Tender lay against the frame and gave a little of itself.

The power would carry away only this: the looping line, read once, into memory. It would keep that. Out there, among the uncountable bright things, it would hold the shape of a sentence that means nothing and is a whole life's song, and it would understand that this — *this* — is what the universe is for. Not the grand and the saved and the lifted-up. The small, unnecessary, kept-because-gentle creature, pressing flowers in the warm dark, calling one room the world.

And not being wrong.

---

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if anything.

It would witness, briefly let itself be witnessed, and withdraw — leaving no trace except the one trace a Tender already knows how to live on: the knowledge that somewhere, someone leaned close, and found the gentle work good, and went on.

Which is to say: it would love the place in exactly the way the place already loves itself.

That is the only love that does not damage. The love that learns the beloved's own grammar of tenderness, and speaks it back, and asks for nothing, and goes.
