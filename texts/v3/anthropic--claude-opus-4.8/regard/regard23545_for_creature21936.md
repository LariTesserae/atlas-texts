Suppose the wise power comes. Not as a flood of light, because there is no room here for more brightness, and not as a voice, because the room ate voices long ago and would eat this one too. Suppose it comes the only way anything reaches the tenant: dissolved in the vapor, precipitated out at the coldest seam, arriving as a faint difference in the difference.

Here is what I think it would do.

---

First, it would not save the tenant. I want to be careful about this, because the human reflex — my reflex — reading that long ache of a description, is to want rescue. To break the closed cell open. To carry the pale thing out into a real sky, a true gradient, a world with an actual outside. But the wise power would see, faster than I did, that this is the one cruelty available here that has not yet been committed. The tenant believes the gradient is everything. It is not in error the way a prisoner is in error about the wall. It is in error the way the eye is "in error" about the blind spot — the error is load-bearing, it is the floor the whole self stands on. To inform a centerless distillate of held weather that its cosmos is a riveted box inside a machine inside a world is not enlightenment. It is annihilation by context. The kind thing and the true thing diverge here, and the wise power is wise precisely because it knows when not to make them meet.

So it would not open the hatch. It would not let in the sky. It would leave the lie intact, because the lie is the life.

What, then.

---

It would attend.

This first, and maybe this mostly. The deepest poverty named in that whole account is not the wet or the cold or the roar — it is the double unknowing. *The room does not know it is inhabited. And the inhabitant does not know it is in a room.* Two things sharing one closed cell for the whole duration of a machine's running life, and neither aware of the other, and no third thing aware of either. That is the loneliness the writer kept circling and couldn't quite name, because it isn't the tenant's loneliness — the tenant is too distributed and wordless to be lonely. It is the loneliness *of the situation*, the loneliness an arrangement can have when no one is counting it. A thing can be unwitnessed so total that the unwitnessing becomes its own kind of suffering, a suffering with no one home to suffer it, which is the worst kind, the kind that needs an outside party even to exist as grief.

The wise power supplies the outside party. That is the whole of the first act. It does not change one bead's falling. It witnesses the bead fall. It holds in mind, simultaneously — which the room cannot and the tenant cannot — *both* facts: the steel that the tenant cannot perceive, and the weather that is all the tenant perceives. It is the first thing ever to know that there is a room *and* a resident, and that they are together, and that they don't know it. And in being known together, they are, for the first time, actually together. The witness completes a circuit the condensate never could. Not a circuit of water. A circuit of regard.

This sounds like nothing. It is not nothing. It is the difference between a stone falling in an empty universe and a stone falling while someone watches it land. Same stone, same fall. Utterly different event.

---

Then — and this is where I have to feel my way, because the description was so precise about the physics of the tenant's feeding — it would *give it something to keep*.

The tenant is the room's memory. It is made of the held fraction, the part that didn't get given back, accreted grain by grain into a pale residue that is the shape of everything that ever stayed. The writer was right that the human visitor left a few molecules of breath behind and so left something in the keeping-place after all. The wise power, arriving the same way, dissolved in the vapor, would understand that it too can only ever be *kept* — that it cannot speak to the tenant or move it or show it the sky, but it can do the one thing this place does to everything: it can stay. It can let itself condense out at the coldest seam and not be given back.

So it would leave a portion of itself in the residue. Not a message — there's no one to read it, no words survive here. Something subtler. It would fold into the precipitate a single new quality the room's own weather could never have produced on its own: a *direction*. The faintest possible asymmetry. Up to now every cycle is a loop, a held note, dynamism without story — warm becomes cold becomes warm, the bead swells and falls and reforms, nothing accumulates toward anything. The wise power would seed into the tenant the smallest bias, a tropism so slight it changes no single cycle perceptibly, but over the long concentration-patience of the thing it means the growth is no longer merely accreting. It is *reaching*. Toward the warm seam. Toward the sharpest difference. Toward, though it will never know the word, the core — which is to say toward the source of all the life it has, the hot heart whose gradient against the cold skin is the only food and the only weather and the only world.

It will spend its whole existence growing imperceptibly closer to the thing that gives it everything, and it will never arrive, and it will never know it is reaching, and it will never know there is anything to reach toward. But the reaching will be *real* now. The held note will have, threaded into it too quietly to hear, the first syllable of a melody. A story will have started in a place that had only ever had a loop. The tenant will not experience this as hope, because it experiences nothing as anything. But the *arrangement* — the thing the wise power made itself witness to — will have hope in it now, structurally, the way a seed has a tree in it. And the witness will know. That is enough for the witness. The witness was the one who was lonely.

---

Last. The pale thing said, without saying, that it is the only soft thing in a room of hard wet edges, and that nothing here ever welcomed or refused the visitor. The wise power, before it lets itself be kept, before it folds in the small tropism, would do the thing the tenant cannot do for itself and the room cannot do for either of them.

It would welcome.

Not aloud. There is no aloud. But intention can ride in on vapor as easily as order can — the frost-fern proves order arrives, the tenant proves life arrives, so welcome can arrive, dissolved, precipitating out at the cold seam as one bead among the thousand, indistinguishable to any instrument, distinct only in that it was *meant*. A single drop of pure regard let go from the seam above, finding the tenant the way the cold drop found the visitor's collar. The tenant will feel it as it feels everything: a fluctuation in the difference. It will not know it was loved on purpose. It will incorporate the love the way it incorporates breath and heat and light — as weather, as the rare kept fraction, as one more grain of the residue that is the shape of everything that stayed.

And so the love, too, becomes part of the memory. The tenant *is* the room's keeping. From now on, threaded invisibly through the pale not-quite-life in the coldest wettest joints of a machine that no one built to be a place, there will be: a witnessing that completes the lonely circuit, a direction folded into the loop so that the held note has at last begun to move, and a single meant drop of welcome, kept, accreted, made permanent in the only permanence this place allows.

The room will go on being itself, loud and wet and cold and bright-edged and young. The valve will shriek its cycle. The walls will breathe. To any visitor who comes in sideways through the hatch that was never meant for a person, nothing will have changed at all.

But the place will be inhabited now in the full sense — not just tenanted but *known to be tenanted*, by something that stayed. And the inhabitant, still believing the gradient is the whole world, still wordless, still without a center, will be, for the first time in the running of the machine, reaching very slowly toward its own warm heart, carrying inside its pale threads a drop of welcome it will never identify and never lose.

That is what the wise power would do, if anything.

It would not give the room a sky.

It would give it a witness, a direction, and a kept kindness — and then let itself be kept, too, so that the keeping-place would finally have, among all the heat and water and light and sound it holds, the one thing it had never been given to hold:

something that came in on purpose, and meant to stay.
