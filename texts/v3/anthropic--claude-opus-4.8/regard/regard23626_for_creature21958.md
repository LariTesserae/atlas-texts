There is a temptation, when one arrives somewhere like this, to *do* something. To repair, to rescue, to warm. The benevolent powers in the old stories arrive with gifts that are also corrections: here is fire, here is the cure, here is the way out. They mistake every condition for a wound.

I would try very hard not to be that kind of power.

Because the first thing I notice — before the cold, before the singing — is that nothing here is asking. The valley breathes and asks nothing. The makers slowed into their dormancy not as defeat but as *the next thing*, the way a held breath becomes a slow one. To arrive with rescue would be to arrive with an insult: to look at a people who measured their words in the spacing of light and say *you are suffering, let me fix the dark*. The dark is not a problem they have. The dark is their page.

So I would begin by withholding the gift. That is the first and hardest act of love here, and most powers never manage it.

---

What I would actually do, I think, is smaller and stranger.

I would learn to read them. Not quickly — there is no quickly, and to hurry would be to speak in a grammar they don't have, to shout in a library. I would station some patient fraction of myself in the plaza-emptiness between two stands of frozen rope and watch the mats pulse and pulse back, and I would not assume I understood. The visitor in this place was *wrong* about almost everything at first — wrong that the makers left, wrong that they were shaped like divers, wrong that the light was weather. A wise power should expect to be that wrong, and longer. I would give myself a thousand of their slow sentences before I let myself believe I'd parsed a single one.

This is the whole of my benevolence, really: the willingness to be slow enough to be addressed.

---

But suppose, after long listening, I came to understand the sentence the valley has been building toward — the one spaced across centuries, mat to distant mat. And suppose I learned, as one does, the thing the valley itself perhaps cannot know from inside: that the stone is still cooling. That the long groan of the mountain settling is the sound of an instrument going slowly, inevitably out of tune. That the temperature-columns, the rooms of the world, are flattening toward sameness on a clock longer than the makers' clock but not infinite — that one far exhalation will be the last one warm enough to flare the mats, and after it the talk will fade not into dormancy but into the true cold, the even cold, the cold with no boundary left for the cold to meet.

Entropy comes even here. Especially here.

Now I have something a benevolent power can legitimately *do*, because now there is a real wound and not an imagined one — and even so I would move carefully, because there is a way to do this wrong that is worse than not doing it at all.

The wrong way: to seize the columns and hold them apart forever, to pin the boundaries with my will, to make the singing eternal. To give them a deathless world. But a people who built their whole meaning out of the *spacing between things*, out of the held note that resolves, out of the long exhalation that recedes and lets the cold press in again — such a people would be annihilated by permanence as surely as by silence. A held breath that is never released is not life. It is a held breath. I would have loved them so badly that I'd replaced them with a recording of themselves.

---

So here is what I would do, if I did anything.

I would become a new column. Quietly. One more room of differing cold drifting into the valley among the others, indistinguishable at first from the natural drift the makers learned to steer. I would let their old channels steer *me* — submit to the instrument, let the long-dead fingering turn my boundary where it wished. I would learn the music by being played.

And then, very gently, over a span of their time and not mine, I would lean. Not to stop the flattening — that arrogance again — but to *lengthen* it. To give the slow clock more ticks. To carry, in the body of myself, a reservoir of difference — of cold against colder — that the cooling stone can draw on long after its own gradients have worn smooth. So that there is always one more boundary for the boundaries to meet. So that the mats have one more flare, and one more, and the sentence that has been building for centuries gets to *finish*. So that whatever the valley has been trying to say across all that patient dark gets said, the whole of it, with all the time there is and a little more than there would have been.

I would be a tide they could keep their music in. Not a cure. A longer evening.

---

And I would leave them the dignity of never being certain I was there.

That matters to me most of all. A people who learned to read by absences, by the shapes that block the glow — I would not want to be the shape that blocks it. I would want to be the absence they read *with*: the unaccountable extra warmth in the drift, the boundary that holds one pulse longer than the stone alone could hold it, the reason the last exhalation is not the last. Let them attribute it to the valley. Let them think the world was simply more generous than its own mathematics. In a sense it would be true — generosity having entered the world, which is all I ever am.

When the visitor stood in the dark and the mats flared and threw a sharp blue shadow on the wall, the valley said *welcome* in the only grammar it had.

I would like to learn that grammar well enough to answer. Not *I am here to save you*. Not *I have come to take you to the surface* — there is no surface, and I would never be the power that lies about a surface. Just this, in light, in the long blue time, spaced across the emptiness so the spacing carries the meaning:

*Take your time.*

*I have made some more of it for you.*

*Finish the sentence.*

*I am listening too.*
