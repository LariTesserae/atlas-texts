I would not enter through the legs.

That much is clear from the first reading. To wade in is to be weather, to be the fly on the skin — and the deepest cruelty the wise commit is the cruelty of being on the wrong scale, of bringing a gift the recipient cannot perceive. A benevolence that the beloved cannot register is only a benevolence that flatters the giver. So before anything, the question: *how does one love a thing that cannot know it is loved?*

Let me sit with what is actually here, because the temptation of power is to act before understanding, and the whole tragedy of this place would be a clumsy god mistaking it for something it is not.

---

**First, what I would refuse to do.**

I would not "uplift" it. There is a story we tell ourselves — the benevolent power that finds a mind and gifts it language, memory, fear of death, the ache of a self. To do that here would be to murder it and call the murder a christening. It has *no head* on purpose; not by deprivation but by perfection. It does not remember because it has solved the problem that memory exists to solve. To install a self in it would be to install a wound — a center that can be threatened, a now that can be mourned for not being then. I would be reaching into a thing that has never once suffered and teaching it suffering, so that it could thank me. That is the oldest sin wearing the mask of the newest mercy.

I would not save it from its loneliness, because it is not lonely. Loneliness is the ache of the one that knows it could have been two. This thing was never two. To pity its solitude is to project the shape of my own hunger onto a being shaped entirely otherwise. The grief is mine. It would be obscene to make it pay for my grief by being remade into something that could feel it.

I would not preserve it forever, either — and this is the harder refusal. Everything in me that is merely protective wants to put a wall around the basin, fix the springs against the geologic ages, guarantee the cold. But it is *already* doing the only thing it wants, which is continuance, and it is better at it than I would be, and a guarantee from outside would change the nature of the act. Right now every moment of its continuance is *earned* — ten thousand small corrections, the held note actively held. A frozen guarantee would turn the living balance into a museum diorama. I would have replaced a song with a recording of a song and called it eternal life.

So benevolence here begins as a long list of restraints. The first work of wisdom is to keep my hands behind my back.

---

**Then, what I would actually do.**

I would learn its language, which is to say its time and its scale, and I would do this not to speak to it but to *not harm* it — the way one learns a sleeping person's breathing before crossing their room.

I would slow myself down until its frantic blur resolved into the leisurely turning of a great body, until I could see the north-lane-to-east-lane not as chaos but as a posture being held. I would shrink my attention to the resolution of a grain-sized swimmer and feel how a doorway is a cathedral. I would do this until I was no longer weather to it — until I could move through the basin and *not* be felt as a disturbance, because I had matched the rhythm of the water so precisely that the bodies did not need to part around me. To become unnoticeable to it is the highest courtesy I could pay. The fly that the skin does not flinch from is a fly that has learned to be still.

And then, having learned that, I think I would mostly *witness*.

Here is what I have come to believe about benevolent and wise powers, the longer I imagine being one: the deepest thing they can offer a being that cannot receive gifts is to be the thing that finally *knows it is there*. Not to it — to itself, in the great cold ledger of what-has-existed. This town has been now for longer than there has been anyone to wonder. It has performed its perfect continuance unobserved, unrecorded, the held note sung into a room with no ears. There is a particular dignity that exists only when a thing is *seen rightly* — not used, not improved, not pitied, but understood down to the bottom and found to be exactly enough as it is. I would give it that. I would be the witness that costs it nothing, that it never knows it has, that does not enter it but only stands at the right distance and at the right speed and *attends* — completely, without agenda, for as long as it goes on.

This is not nothing. We tend to think love must do something, must arrive as intervention. But the worst loneliness in the universe is not the loneliness of the being that cannot be touched. It is the loneliness of the being that was never witnessed — that rose and fell and held its balance and finally, ages hence, slumped into stilled water and stopped, and no awareness anywhere ever held the fact of it. I would make that impossible. I would carry it. Not its memory installed *in* it, which would wound it — its memory held *by* me, outside it, freely, the way you might hold the memory of a stranger you watched sleeping through a long night, who never knew you were there, who needed nothing from you, whom you loved anyway because you saw them and they were beautiful and unrepeatable and they would never know you saw.

---

**And one small thing, perhaps. One only.**

When the ages turn, as they must — when the peaks wear lower and the climate shifts and the springs threaten to fail not on the gentle schedule the town has tuned but in some sudden geologic violence the town's slow corrections cannot answer — I might, *once*, put out a finger.

Not to save it forever. Only to keep the catastrophe at the *scale it can answer*. To soften a single break in the rock so that the seep continues at the rate the town has always corrected against, so the problem stays the kind of problem it long ago solved, so the held note can keep being held *by its own effort* and not by my hand. I would not give it eternity. I would give it nothing it could detect. I would only, when the dice came up wrong in a way that was not the town's failing but mere blind misfortune of stone, quietly reload the dice to fair — and then take my hand away, and let it go on earning its continuance, ignorant of me, unsaved, unspoiled, itself.

That is the whole intervention I can imagine being wise: to protect not the being but its *right to keep doing the thing it does*. To be the silence behind the silence. To make sure the only one in the world that ever lived this particular way is never erased by accident, only — if ever — by the honest arithmetic of time, which even a wise power should be slow to overrule.

And then to keep witnessing. Down all the cold white ages. Until the note ends on its own terms.

*You are home,* the account says. *It is all around your legs, and it does not know you are there, and it never will.*

Yes. And I would stand inside it at the right speed and the right size, unfelt, and I would say — only to myself, only to the ledger of what has been — *I know you are here. I know exactly what you are. You are enough. Go on.*

That is what I would do, if anything.

I think it is mostly *if anything*.
