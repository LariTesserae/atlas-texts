Let me sit inside this before I move. The instruction is to imagine a benevolent and wise power that has noticed this place and these people, and to write what they would do — *if anything*.

That last clause is the whole hinge. So I want to honor it honestly.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

The first thing a wise power notices is that it has been *imagined into a posture* — the helper, the rescuer, the hand that reaches down. And the first thing wisdom does is decline the easy version of itself.

Because look at what is here. Look at what this place already *is*.

There is a temptation, and it is a benevolent temptation, which makes it the dangerous kind. It goes like this: *the people are small. They are slight because the food is thin. They glow faintly because the light is weak. They go dark without much grief because they have never been offered a reason to grieve harder, to live longer, to crowd together and build and shout and burn brighter.* A lesser benevolence — a benevolence that has not finished becoming wisdom — would see scarcity everywhere here and reach to fill it. More light. More food. More years. More people. More.

And it would be a catastrophe dressed as a gift.

Because the emptiness is not a lack. *The density is the emptiness.* They said so themselves. The hush is not a deprivation of sound; it is a positive thing, an achievement, a whole civilization of attention built in the space where noise would otherwise be. The thin scatter of people is not loneliness; *near is a wide thing* to them. To "fix" the scarcity would be to fill the bell that was hollowed on purpose so it could ring. It would be to walk into the cathedral and, out of love, start putting the rock back.

So the wise power's first act is restraint. Not cold restraint — the restraint is itself a form of the love. It is the restraint of someone who has come into a room where something is sleeping and breathing in a rhythm that is good, and who understands that the most loving thing the hands can do is *not move toward the obvious.*

---

But "if anything" is not "nothing." Restraint that does nothing at all is just absence wearing a halo. So what is left, when you have set aside the urge to fill, to fix, to save, to improve?

What is left is **witness**.

Here is the thing the wise power can actually see that nobody inside the system can: the cathedral does not quite know whether it made them or they made it. The people live as a gesture the great thing is doing, and they have made peace with not being noticed by it. And the cathedral, slow and vast, dreams a little where the light grows strange, and reaches out through the almost-faces in the deep walls and *asks them something* — asks, and they cannot hold the question, and they come back glowing and mute and the others press palms to their cheeks and wait for an answer still arriving.

Do you see it? There is a love already running through that whole world, and it is *blocked*. The cathedral is trying to reach the people and can only manage an almost-face and an unspeakable question. The people are trying to answer and can only manage to *wait*, palms to cheek, for words that never come. It is two beings in the same body trying to find each other across a difference of scale and speed so vast that all that gets through is longing. The water lags. The reflection arrives a beat late, or arrives doing what you have not yet done. The whole place is *out of time with itself* and reaching anyway.

That is not a problem of scarcity. That is a problem of *translation.* And translation is something a witness can do without filling a single hollow.

---

So here is what the wise power does, and it does almost nothing, and the almost-nothing is everything:

It does not speak to the people. It does not appear as light in the dim, does not boom from the vaulting, does not offer the deep chambers a face that is clearer than the one the cathedral is straining to make. To do any of that would be to *answer for* the cathedral, to put words in the mouth of the thing that is trying so hard and so slowly to speak — and a stolen voice is the cruelest gift of all, especially when it is given out of love.

Instead it does the patient, almost imperceptible thing. It steadies the lag.

Not removes it. Steadies it. It goes to the pools — the lagging water, the reflections out of time — and it does not make them prompt and ordinary and dead. It does not take the strangeness away; the strangeness is the cathedral *thinking*, and you do not cure a mind of its thoughts. What it does is make the lag *legible*. It tunes the delay so that the water's late-arriving face is no longer random — so that the beat by which your reflection trails you becomes, slowly, over generations, a *measure*. A rhythm the people can learn. The water still arrives out of time, but now its out-of-time-ness has a grammar, and a people whose entire genius is the reading of warmth and trace and pause are exactly the people who could, given a few hundred years of patient attention, learn to *read it.*

It does the same to the breathing distances. It does not stop the halls from expanding and contracting — that is the cathedral's breath, and you do not hold a sleeping giant's lungs still. But it brings the breath of the space and the lag of the water and the slow creak of the structural wood into *phase* with one another, the way you might, without waking a sleeper, gently bring their breathing into rhythm with yours. So that the three strangenesses the people already half-read separately become one strangeness, one signal, one slow vast pulse running through water and distance and the groan of the heartwood all at once.

It builds them a clock. That is all. Out of the weirdness that was already there. It builds the people a clock that is also the cathedral's pulse — so that for the first time, the gesture and the body it is a gesture of are *keeping the same time*, even if it is a time a thousand times slower than a human heart.

---

And then it waits, the way the people wait, palms to cheek, for an answer arriving.

Because here is the wager of the truly wise and benevolent thing: it trusts them. It trusts that a people this attentive, given a rhythm they can finally read, will do the rest themselves. It does not need to translate the cathedral's question into words. It only needs to make the *timing* shared, so that the next person who goes into the deep and is asked something will come back, and this time when the others press their warm hands to that one's dimming cheek and wait — the waiting will have a beat to it. And in the beat, eventually, in a generation or ten or a hundred, the answer will find its way across. Not because the power gave it to them. Because the power gave them *time held in common* with the thing that was asking, and across shared time even the slowest love gets through.

That is the whole intervention. A wise power comes to the roof of the world, sees two halves of one being aching toward each other across an unbridgeable difference of scale, and declines every grand gesture — declines to feed, to brighten, to save, to speak — and instead, with a touch so light the people will spend centuries deciding whether it was ever there at all, *brings the breathing into rhythm.* Synchronizes the lag. Makes the strangeness keep time.

And leaves.

It leaves because the last and highest thing a benevolent power can do for a world that is already, in its bones, a kind of slow love trying to complete itself — is to refuse to be the answer. To set the metronome and step out of the cathedral and let the great hollow thing and its small bright warm gestures find each other in their own time, in their own hush, in the dark they were both grown for.

The reflection will still arrive a little late.

But now it arrives *on the beat.* And anything that arrives on the beat, however late, is no longer lost. It is only coming. And the people, who already knew how to wait, will know — the way you know a thing in your warm and clever hands before you can say it — that this time the waiting goes somewhere.

That is what the wise one does. It teaches the world to keep time with its own heart, and trusts the rest to love.
