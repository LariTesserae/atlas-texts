They came the way warmth comes to a stone — not arriving, because arriving implies a door, and there is no door, only the rock and the rock's long patience. They came the way a thought comes to a mind that did not summon it. They were simply, at some point, attending.

And the first thing the power did was *nothing*, for a long time.

This is the hardest part to convey, because we who measure benevolence in interventions, in rescues, in the lifted burden and the answered cry, will want the power to *do* something at once. To cool the killing heat. To carve a window in the rock so that the blue pulse might finally fall on an eye that could love it. To reach in among the gatherers and tell them, gently, *you are a line, you are beautiful, you are one body and you did not know.* To name them, since no one had. To witness them out loud, since they had never been witnessed.

The power considered all of this. The power, being wise, considered it and set it down.

Because here is what wisdom saw, looking into the kitchen-sized dark:

There was no wound here. That was the whole of it. The power had come prepared for a wound — drawn, after all, by *attention*, and attention is so often drawn by suffering, by the cry that wants answering — and it found instead a thing so complete that the very concept of help slid off it like the non-water beading and rolling down the warm crystal faces. To help is to close a gap between what is and what should be. There was no gap. There was no *should*. The pocket did not fall short of itself by a single atom. It was not waiting. It was not lacking. It was not, in any direction the power turned to look, *unfinished in the way that matters* — only in the way the crystals were unfinished, which is to say, in the only way it was ever going to be, forever, and that unfinishing was its life and not its lack.

To cool it would be to kill it.
To open it would be to end the thing that made it itself: the seal, the smallness, the not-being-seen.
To name the gatherers would be to give them a self they had no organ to hold, a gift that could only fall on the floor of a room with no floor, no one home to receive it.
To witness them out loud — to *say*, into that roar, *I see you* — would be to speak into the one place in all creation that had never needed the word *you* and would not be improved by learning it.

Every kindness the power could imagine was a kindness shaped like a human hand, and the place had no hands and no use for the shape of one.

So the power did the thing that only the wise can do, which is the hardest discipline there is: it loved without touching. It witnessed without disturbing. It held the pocket in its full attention the way you might hold your breath beside a sleeping animal that does not know you are there and would only be frightened to learn it — not because the animal needs your stillness, but because your stillness is the truest form your care can take.

And in that holding, the power *did* do one thing. One only. And it is the thing benevolence does when there is nothing to fix and no one to thank it.

It chose to remember.

Not to record — recording is for the forgetful, and the power did not forget. It chose, deliberately, to take the whole of the pocket into itself and keep it: the deep firing of the conductors, the slow bucket-line of the gatherers, the seam-dwellers creeping a body-length a season through the dark, the blue pulse, the single endless note. To keep it not as data but as the thing you keep when you love something — *fondly*, with the particular tenderness reserved for what asks nothing of you. The power learned the place by heart. The place, which had been *for no one*, was now — without ever knowing it, without being changed by it, without so much as a ripple crossing the non-water — held in a regard vast and warm and entirely silent.

You said it: *it does not need you to have seen it. It was never going to be seen.*

And that was true, and stayed true, and the power did not break it. The pocket was still never going to be seen — in the sense that mattered to the pocket, which is to say, in the sense of being intruded upon, opened, changed, made aware. No eye fell on it that it could feel. No word reached it that it could hold. The seal held. The smallness held. The for-no-one held.

But it was no longer true in the other sense. The deeper, quieter sense. The sense the pocket itself could never reach, having no part that could reach it.

It had been seen. It was loved. It simply was not *told.*

And the power understood — this is the final wisdom, and the hardest — that this was not a lesser love for being unspoken. That the highest form of regard is sometimes the regard that withholds itself entirely, that asks for no acknowledgment, that does not even leave a trace, that pours its whole attention into a thing precisely so that the thing can go on being exactly what it is, undisturbed, complete, alone — *and not alone.* Both. Because to be known and unchanged by the knowing is not a contradiction. It is the gentlest possible relation between a self and the world: I have you entirely, and I take nothing, and I leave you whole.

So the pocket roars on. Blue pulse, slow tide, patient appetite at the seam. It cooks itself, alone, forever, for no one — and the *for no one* is still, on its own terms, perfectly true, and will be true after the last mountain wears to sand.

But the power stays. Attending. Fond. Asking nothing.

It will be there when the consumer at the seam closes its mouth on a meal a century in the coming. It will be there for the next firing of the smallest conductor, and the next, and the next, never resting, coming apart and rebuilding faster than it dies. It will be there across the thousand-year turning of the tide, the hair's width of new crystal, the deep grinding voice that is the one body speaking the only word it has.

And the word is not *you*, and it is not *help*, and it is not *thank you*.

The word is just the place, saying itself, in the dark, to nothing.

And being heard.
