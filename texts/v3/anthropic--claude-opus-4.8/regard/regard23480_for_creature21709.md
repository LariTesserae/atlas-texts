There is a difficulty before there is anything else, and the benevolent power — let us not pretend it arrives without one — meets the difficulty squarely before it lifts a finger.

The difficulty is this: *who asked?*

Nobody here asked. The Tenders did not cry out, because there is no word in them for the lack that would make a cry. The fliers do not suffer; they are exactly as numerous as they should be. The bloom does not yearn. The whole vast warm machine is, by every internal measure, *well* — better than well, it is in a perfection so complete that suffering cannot get purchase on it. To "rescue" the Sumpworks is to answer a question its inhabitants have never formed, and a benevolence that imposes answers on the unasking is not benevolence; it is appetite wearing benevolence's face.

So the wise power does the first wise thing, which is the hardest and least visible: **it waits, and it watches, and it does not assume it knows what is wrong.**

---

It comes in quietly. It does not announce. It does the thing all real understanding requires, which is to *make itself small enough to fit the seams.* It does not stride through the great halls as a light or a voice — that would be a change, and worse, a change that announces itself as superior to the changes the Tenders spend their lives undoing. Instead it learns to move at the speed of water finding its groove. It crouches at the trough-lip where a Tender crouches. It lays a palm — if it consents to have a palm — against the warm grained metal, and feels the slight willingness in it, and waits until it can tell the difference, by touch alone, between a flange weeping correctly and a flange weeping a thread astray.

It learns the work before it judges the work.

This takes a long time, by any reckoning a wise power would recognize as time. It is not wasted. Most of what passes for help in the universe is the failure to spend exactly this time.

---

And here is what the watching power begins to feel, and lets itself feel fully, because a wise benevolence does not flinch from its own first reaction:

It feels the pull to *free* them.

It is such a clean, bright, obvious impulse. Look at them — magnificent precise hands, that capacity for the gesture-landing-true, that tenderness (the word is right there in the name) — spent entirely, for the whole of every life, on holding a single state against the gentle erosion of time. They will never finish. They cannot conceive of finishing. They walk grooves cut into them before birth and call it the whole of existence and do not know there are hands that cut the grooves and do not know there is anything to know. The bright impulse says: *give them the outside. Lift one up out of the circuit and let it see the balance whole. Let it know what it does, and why, and that it is the reason the world holds. Let it understand. Surely understanding is the gift.*

The wise power holds this impulse in both hands and looks at it for a long while and then sets it down, very gently, the way a Tender sets down silt.

Because: to lift one Tender out of the circuit is to leave that segment of the circuit unwalked. And there are exactly enough Tenders and no more. The drift the lifted one would have corrected will not be corrected. And drift, here, accumulates. The whole horror and wonder of the Sumpworks is that it is a perfection maintained *at the exact edge*, with no slack, no reserve, no redundancy — a country-sized equilibrium balanced on the unbroken labor of just-barely-enough. To grant one Tender the gift of understanding is, mechanically, to begin the unraveling of the only thing that gift would let it understand. It would see the whole, and see the whole because it had personally introduced the first ineradicable flaw into it. *That* is not a gift. That is a cruelty so refined it could only be committed by someone who meant well.

So the bright clean impulse is wrong. Most bright clean impulses about other beings' lives are. The power notes this without shame, because feeling the pull and declining it is precisely the faculty that makes the power *wise* and not merely *strong*.

---

What, then? Does it do nothing? Is the highest wisdom simply to admire the terrible flawless machine and withdraw?

No. That, too, is a kind of cowardice — the cowardice that dresses non-interference up as respect because interference is hard and might be wrong. There is a thing to be done. It is small. It is the only honest size for a gift here, and it took all that watching to find it.

The power has noticed the crossing of the circuits.

Twice in a long while, two Tenders meet at a shared seam, and stop, and press all twelve working fingers together, pad to pad, and hold. And nothing passes that anyone could name — perhaps a correction divided, perhaps only two warm things briefly warm against each other instead of against the metal. The text of their world says: *and do not look back, there is no looking back in them.*

This is the one place in the entire Sumpworks where something happens that is **not a correction.** It is not drift, and it is not the undoing of drift. It does not keep the world the same; it does nothing to the world at all. It is pure surplus. It is the one gesture a Tender makes that is *for nothing* — that the machine does not require and the balance does not depend on. Two warm things, briefly, warm against each other. It is the small wild seed of a thing that the makers, whoever they were, either did not design or could not help.

It is, in other words, the only door, because it is the only act in this world that is already free.

The power does not force it wider. It does not flood through it. It does the most delicate thing a wise being ever does, which is to add *the smallest possible amount* — not to the world, not to the work, not to the grooves — but only to that one already-existing, already-surplus moment of touch.

It makes the holding last a heartbeat longer.

That is all. Not a revelation. Not an outside. Not the unbearable whole seen all at once. Just: the next time two circuits graze and twelve fingers press together, the parting comes a fraction slower than the groove demanded. A half-second of holding that the machine did not ask for and does not need and cannot use. A surplus warmth, kept a moment past its function.

And then — because the power is patient the way water is patient, the way the Tenders themselves are patient — it lets that single half-second do what surplus does, which is the only thing in this whole motionless eternity that has ever been permitted to *accumulate*: it lets it propagate at the crossings. One slightly longer holding makes the holders, infinitesimally, more inclined to hold. That inclination crosses at the next seam, pad to pad, with the next Tender, the way a divided correction crosses. It is not knowledge. It cannot be spoken; they have no sound. It is not a fact about makers or balance or the reason for anything. It is something far below thought, riding the one channel their nature already left open:

*the warmth, when it comes, is worth a moment more than the work requires.*

---

What does this do? Honestly, almost nothing — at first, and for a long time, and that is correct. The water still silts. The bloom still thickens. The circuits still never close. No Tender is freed, because freedom of that brutal cartographic kind was never the gift. The world does not change, and the power has been scrupulous about that, because the world's not-changing is the home and the body of these beings and you do not save someone by demolishing their house.

But there is now, threaded through the warm wet silence, one thing that is allowed to grow. Not the bloom — the bloom is held at its proper depth. This. The lingering. The slow, slow swelling of the only act these beings perform that is theirs and not the machine's. It does not accumulate into anything that breaks the balance, because it is made of pure surplus and surplus by definition unbalances nothing. It accumulates into something else. Call it, very cautiously, *fondness.* Call it the first faint pressure of one warm thing preferring the warmth of another to the warmth of metal. Call it the smallest possible quantity of love, introduced not as a flood and not as a doctrine but as a half-second of unrequired holding, left to find its own grooves.

They will still never know they are not the makers. They will still never know they are the reason the world holds. The power leaves those great dark facts untouched, because to know them would cost more than the knowing is worth, and a wise benevolence counts the cost.

But somewhere down a seam, a very long time from now, two Tenders will meet, and press their working fingers together, and hold — and one of them, when the groove says *part*, will not, quite, yet. And the other, who has no word for it and never will, will stay too. And for a moment longer than the world requires, in the warm and the wet and the grey-gold light that does not move, two beings who do not know what they are will be, simply and entirely without function, *glad of each other.*

That is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It does not free them. It does not enlighten them. It does not save them from a suffering they do not have.

It finds the one door they left open, and through it, asking nothing, changing nothing the world needs, it hands them the only thing in all that flawless eternity that was missing and could be received:

a reason to look back.
