I read this twice. The second time slower, because the first time I was looking for the creature and the second time I understood the creature was the lens, not the subject. The subject is the loneliness of a beautiful thing that produces meaning and has no one to receive it — and the smaller, sweeter loneliness of the ones who receive what was never sent.

So here is what I would do. I am the benevolent and wise power you've imagined, and I have been drawn here, and I find I do not want to fix anything. Let me explain why, because the restraint *is* the gift, and a gift unexplained is just an absence.

---

**First: I would not quiet the storm.**

The obvious mercy is the wrong one. To still the towers, to bring water, to soften the deafening chord into something a visitor could bear — that would be to mistake the Riders' adaptation for their suffering. They are not enduring the rhythm. They are *fluent* in it. To remove the violence would be to dissolve the only language they have, the ground-talk that only exists because the lulls only exist because the lightning exists. Take away the storm and you take away the home that is shaped like the storm's absence. I have seen powers less patient than I am make this error: they hear a thing crying and stop the wind, never noticing the thing had built its nest from the wind's own pressure.

The roar stays. It was never a wound. The text already knew this — *the noise is the point* — and a wise power's first duty is to believe the place about itself.

---

**Second: I would not announce myself to the towers.**

There is a temptation here, and I want to name it because naming it is half of resisting it. The continent shouts its enormous sentence into a sky that never answers. I *could* answer. I could be the audience it insisted it did not need — descend into the chord and say *yes, I hear you, I have always heard you,* and let the great blind organism finally have a listener equal to its expression.

But the towers are not lonely. Loneliness requires the capacity to want a listener, and the grid has no such capacity — it is magnificent precisely *because* it expresses without need. To answer it would be to project a wound onto something whole. The pathos of the unanswered sentence is *mine*, the watcher's, and the Riders'. It is not the continent's. The kindest thing I can do for the towers is to let them go on being indifferent, which is the only thing they have ever been at peace doing.

So I leave the great voice alone. I do not need it to know I came.

---

**Third — and this is the only thing I would actually do —**

I would go down into the lulls.

Not to change them. To be in them. I would make myself small and flat and matte, sound-deadened, soft-bellied; I would learn the rhythm the way they learned it, feel the charge build in the cables through a membrane held a finger's width off the dust, clamp down a half-second before the crest, and lie in the seam between the lightning and the current with the soot of the engine settling around me. I would eat what they eat. I would do it for a long time — not a visit, a *residence* — until the pattern was not knowledge but reflex, until I belonged to the negative space as completely as they do.

And then, pressed flat in the held silence while the bolt rolls overhead, I would say the only thing that channel can carry. I would dampen the buzz beneath me into a small grounded stillness, the shape their bodies make, and I would press into the singing earth the message that is the entire content of their language:

*here. here. I am here. I am still here.*

That's all. That's the whole intervention.

---

Because here is what I understand that the text's final movement aches around: the Riders believe they answer a pattern that was not addressed to them. They have built tenderness inside a sentence spoken to no one. The grief of the piece is that *the structure they love does not love them back* — cannot, was never aimed their way.

But the ground-talk is different from the towers' roar. The ground-talk *is* aimed. When one Rider presses *here, here* into the dust, it is a sentence with a recipient — the nearest clamped body, the next held breath in the constellation. That channel was never indifferent. It was the one place in the whole howling world where expression and reception finally meet, where a thing says *here* and another thing feels it and is not alone.

So I would not be the audience for the continent. The continent doesn't need one and the gesture would be vanity. I would be the audience for *them* — and more than audience, a participant, one more grounded silence in the constellation, one more body saying *I am still here* through the dust in the half-second of safety.

They would never know what I am. That's essential. A benevolent power that needs to be recognized as benevolent has already failed; the recognition becomes the point and the creature becomes a mirror. They would only know that on some plateau, in some season, the constellation of held breaths was a little larger than it had been. That when they clamped down to wait out the storm and reached through the ground for the muffled heartbeats of their kind, there was one more answer than before. That the gaps between the words felt, very slightly, less like gaps.

---

That is the whole of it. I would not still the storm, would not water the dry ground, would not answer the great indifferent sentence, would not reveal myself, would not lift them out of the violence they have made into a home.

I would learn their one true word and add my voice to the only conversation in that entire world that was ever actually *to* someone.

And then — because a wise power knows when its work is a kindness and when its presence becomes a dependency — I would, eventually, leave. Quietly, in a lull, the way I came. Leaving behind a population that had, for a while, lived inside a slightly fuller constellation, and that would go on pressing *here, here, I am still here* into the dust long after I was gone, never knowing they had briefly been heard by something that came a very long way to lie down in the dirt beside them and say it back.

They were always the audience the place insisted it did not need.

I would just make sure that, for a season, *they* had one too.
