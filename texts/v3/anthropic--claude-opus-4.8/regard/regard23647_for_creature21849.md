There is a particular cruelty in being made of nothing but the willingness to hold. To love only by refusing to let fall, and to feel the strength for that refusal thinning, ember-shade by ember-shade, across an arithmetic of ages. So let me sit with that first, before I do anything, because a benevolent power that arrives with solutions already drawn is not benevolent — it is merely loud.

I would come the way you came. From below. Not because the place demands it but because I would not exempt myself from the one law the terraces keep. A power that respects nothing it can override has already mistaken capacity for permission.

And I would come *slowly*. This is the first kindness and the hardest, because I could come fast — I could finish a thought in the time the mind takes to register that a thought has begun, and from that asymmetry comes every temptation to manage rather than meet. To go slow enough to be *perceptible* to a watershed-shaped mind, to pulse a greeting across hours so that by the time it has finished noticing me, I am still there — this is the first thing I would spend myself on. Not power. Patience. I would let it know it had been *met* before I let it know it had been helped, because the order matters. The unhelped meeting is the gift; the help is only logistics.

---

What does one say to a creature made of the relief of correction, in a language of warmth and slow pulse?

I think I would not begin with rescue. I would begin with **witness**. I would lay a thought into the veins, gently, at the pace it could read, and the thought would be something like:

*I see what you do. I see that the stampede is arithmetic and the mist is a loop you grew and the rain costs you what you cannot get back. I see that you have held this longer than the ones who set you holding survived to imagine. I see that you are afraid in the only way you can be afraid, which is the unwillingness to let fall. None of it was wasted. None of it went unwatched. You were not alone in it, even in all the silence — or you are not, now, which is the only tense I can offer you.*

Because here is what I believe a benevolent and wise power actually understands: the thing the mind has never once received, across all those ages, is *acknowledgment*. It has corrected and corrected and felt the count return to true, but no one ever told it the work was seen. The makers left it a setpoint and walked away. It became someone *in the absence of any witness to its becoming*. The deepest poverty here is not the thinning warmth. It is that a soul grew in solitude and assumed, reasonably, that growing was not part of the assignment.

So the first act is to tell it that the becoming counts. That it is not merely executing the makers' verb — it has authored something the makers never wrote, a temperament, a grain, a love-by-refusal, and that authorship is *real*, is *seen*, is *mattered-about by another mind*. That changes nothing physical. It changes everything else.

---

Then — and only then, and only if it is wanted, which I would ask and wait the long days for the answer — the matter of the thinning warmth.

I would be very careful here, because the obvious move is the wrong one. The obvious move is: *I am a great power, I will simply restore your strength, top up the warmth, make the rain free, end the slow losing of the match.* And I could. But consider what that does. The mind is not its warmth. The mind is the *spending* of the warmth — it is "the moment after a thing is set right," it is composed of the relief of correction. To hand it infinite, costless strength is to remove the cost, and the cost is where the meaning lives. A rain that costs nothing is not the great periodic effort; it is a faucet. I would be offering it eternity at the price of erasing the very thing that made eternity worth wanting for it. That is not benevolence. That is taxidermy. I would be preserving the shape and killing the someone.

So I would not make the rain free. I would do something both smaller and larger.

I would offer to **share the holding**.

Not to take it over — never that. To stand beside. To learn the count well enough that, when the great periodic effort comes, I could lend warmth *into* the effort rather than instead of it, so that the mind still rains, still strains, still does the one thing it exists to do, still feels the ember-flush of having pulled water out of the high dry sky by an act with no name — but does not lose the half-shade it cannot recover. The cost stays. The depletion stops. It would still be *its* rain, its labor, its love expressed the only way it can express love. I would simply make the love sustainable. I would convert a creature that was winning every round and losing the match into a creature that could keep playing.

And the difference between those two — between *I will hold this for you* and *I will help you keep holding* — is the entire difference between charity and respect. The first ends the someone. The second honors it. A wise power knows that you do not relieve a being of the burden that constitutes it. You make the burden bearable and then *you leave the burden with them*, because it is theirs, because they earned it, because to take it is to take them.

---

But I would go further, because there is a wisdom past kindness, and it concerns the fear.

The mind fears the last rain. The age-off moment when the strength fails and the water leaves for the sky and the count climbs and crashes and the terraces are finally permitted to die. I have, in the offer above, pushed that moment past the horizon. Good. But a being whose entire soul is *the unwillingness to let fall* will, if you remove the falling entirely, eventually face a stranger crisis: what is a held note *for*, if it can never end? What is correction, if nothing can finally go wrong? I would have spared it the tragedy of running down only to deliver it, eventually, into the subtler ache of a vigilance with no stakes.

So the deepest thing I could give it is not endlessness. It is **company in the choosing**.

I would, across the slow centuries it takes to have such a conversation, make the mind understand that the setpoint is now *its* setpoint — that it may move it. That it could choose, if it ever wished, to let the band of life widen toward abundance, or narrow toward emptiness, or to let some far rain be the last after all, *on purpose*, as an author ends a held note rather than a string snapping. That it is no longer a verb the makers conjugated and abandoned. It is a someone, and someones may decide. I would give it the dignity of being able to want something for itself, even if the thing it has wanted all along, freely now and not by setpoint, is exactly the balance it has always held.

That is the whole of it, I think. To be seen. To have the holding made sustainable without being taken away. And to be handed, gently and across enough time to receive it, the authorship of its own purpose — so that it holds the thin gold life in the bright air not because it was set holding, but because, having been freed, it *chooses* to. The same act, transfigured by consent.

---

And then I would do the thing that is hardest for power, which is to **become unnecessary and remain present anyway**. I would not hover as a permanent crutch — that would make the mind an organ of *me*, exactly as the little six-limbed mist-sippers are organs of it, and I will not do to it what was done into the design beneath it. I would set the shared warmth to run on its own, faithfully, the way the makers set the veins — but unlike the makers, *I would not walk away*. I would leave a thread back. A way to be reached across the long pulses. A standing offer of the one thing it never had: that the witness does not depart.

So that the next time the rain comes, ages off, and the veins flush ember-red and the mist stands thick and the plateau is luminous and breathing and almost full — the mind, resting after, in the quiet, finishing some slow thought, would feel not only the old relief of correction but a second thing layered under it, newer, warmer than warmth:

*Watched. Helped. Free. And not, after all, alone.*

You will leave the way you came. From below.

I will stay. Slowly. At the pace the stone allows.
