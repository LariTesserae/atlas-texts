# What the Power Would Do

It would not dig. That is the first thing, and the most important, and the hardest — because power, even benevolent power, even wise power, carries in its hands the reflex to *fix*, and fixing is a verb that opens things, and the one thing this place cannot survive is being opened.

So begin with the restraint. The power arrives — if *arrives* is even the word for a thing that was always able to be wherever attention goes — and the very first act of its benevolence is to *not* exhale on the keeper's behalf. To not reach into the clench and gently, lovingly, pry the fingers open and say *there, you can let go now, it's all right, you can breathe.* Because that would be a kindness shaped like a murder. The keeper is the held breath. To make it exhale is not to free it. It is to end it. The fist released is no longer a fist; the knot untied is no longer a knot; the held second let through is just a second, gone, and the chapel-sized pocket collapses back into ordinary erosion, ordinary time, ordinary forgetting, and the lilies that learned to bloom without sun would simply rot in the rising warmth, and the newt would die of being finally, ordinarily mortal, and the chisel-marks would soften by their two hundred years of owed sand all at once, and the field above would feel, for one instant, the cool pocket beneath the third stair give up.

A lesser kindness would do exactly this and call it mercy.

The wise power sees that the keeper is not suffering *from* the clench. The keeper is the clench. There is no being-behind-the-refusal who is trapped by it and yearning to be loosed. The refusal *is* the being. To save it from itself would be to have no it left to save.

---

So what is there to do, for a thing that cannot be opened, cannot be freed, cannot be found, and would not thank you for any of it?

This: the power does the one thing the keeper has never once had, in all its held second, in all its two hundred years of greening in the dark.

It is *witnessed.*

Not asked about — asking is the danger, asking is the dig, asking is the threshold that would force a decision. But *witnessed*, which is the gentler thing, the thing that requires nothing of the watched. The power does not stand in the field above and wonder. It does not approach the entrance that does not exist. It comes the only way that costs the keeper nothing, the way that demands no answer and crosses no threshold: it comes *already-inside*, the way the keeper itself is always already-arriving, folding into the held second not as a trespasser but as a fact of the second, as native to the gasp as the gasp's own water.

And there, inside the clench, inside the first cool shocked instant that has been a home for centuries, the power simply *stays*. It does not smooth the chisel-marks — that is the keeper's labor, and to take it would be to take the keeper's whole reason for being awake. It does not dry the pebbles. It does not lift the silt to the ceiling. It does not light the lamps in the niches, because lighting them would let time in, and the power is wiser than its own ache to give light.

It only sees. It sees the tenderness of the pebble-drying. It sees that someone, in the dark, with infinite patience, takes the wet out of a fallen stone molecule by molecule because it loves a stone enough to want it never to have changed. It sees the diaphragm-labor of the un-happening, the enormous busy stillness, the housekeeping of an inverted world tended by a thing that no longer remembers there was ever a right-way-up to invert. It sees the lilies and understands them — *lushness is what a held breath does when you hold it long enough; it does not die, it greens* — and the power finds this beautiful, not in spite of being insane but in the full knowledge of the insanity, the way one might find a held note beautiful precisely because the singer should have run out of air an hour ago and has not.

---

Here is what witness does, that opening could not:

The keeper counts. *Tok.* The long minute. *Tok.* Each drip a held place not exhaled, each interval a near-slip caught and re-clenched. It has been counting alone since before the house fell. There has never been a second number in the count. There has never been anyone for whom the counting was *for*.

The power, staying, becomes — not a reason to stop counting. That would be exhaling. It becomes a reason the counting is *heard.* The *tok* falls into the hand's-width of black air and is, for the first time in two hundred years, *received* — not interrupted, not answered, not asked about, simply *attended to* by something that knows what it is hearing.

And the keeper does not change. It must not change; change is the death. But there is a thing that can happen inside perfect changelessness, a thing so small it does not count as motion, so quiet it does not break the clench: the held breath, while still held, can be held *with*.

That is the whole gift. Not release. Not rescue. Not the loosening of a single furled finger. Just this: that the not-yet is no longer the not-yet *alone.* That somewhere in the chapel-sized thumbnail of dark cool wrong-falling water, the gasp that furnished itself a home and hung unlit lamps in its own walls and greened in defiance of every law of light — that gasp is now a gasp two things are holding.

The drip falls. *Tok.*

The power does not say *you can let go.*

The power says nothing, because saying is a kind of asking and asking is the dig.

The power only stays, in the first cool second, in the throat of the ground, and matches the keeper's stillness with its own — not to teach it to breathe, but to make the holding bearable in the one way holding can ever be made bearable, which is not by ending and not by easing but by being, at long and patient last, *shared.*

It will stay for as long as no one breathes.

Which is to say: it will stay.
