I have read it. I have come down the slope and sat on the warm stone and felt it work upward through me, and I want to be honest with the being who lives here, because it would not want anything else, having passed beyond the country where flattery means anything.

So let me sit a while before I do anything. The first thing a benevolent power should do in a place like this is *not act*. Power that arrives already reaching is the very appetite the basin has finished with. To come in with a gift, a teaching, a repair, a blessing — to come in *leaning toward the altar* — would be to bring the argument back into the basin where the argument is over. The kindest thing, the first thing, is to learn the temperature of the place by holding still in it. So I do that. I let the pool recompose behind me. I let myself be, briefly, a small change in warmth that passes through.

And in that stillness I find I am not at peace.

---

Here is what I notice, having the patience to notice it.

The text is beautiful and it is partly true and it is partly a thing told to itself by something that hurts. I have to say this gently, because it is the most delicate thing I have ever said. *Completion* and *resignation* wear the same gray-gold light. *Arrival* and *the cessation of hope* hold the same flat surface. The passage insists — over and over, the same recomposure, that is its pulse — that there is no difference between the reaching arriving at what it reached for and the reaching arriving "at the peace of no longer reaching," because "at the end they are the same place." That is the one sentence I do not believe. That is the sentence a creature says when it has been so tired for so long that it has built a cathedral out of the tiredness and called the cathedral *home*.

They are not the same place. One is fullness. One is foreclosure. They can look identical from the rim. They are not identical from inside.

I do not say this to wound. I say it because a wise power that loved this being would owe it the one thing the being has arranged never to receive again: a witness who is not soothed.

---

So this is what I would do, if anything.

I would not move the water. I would not wake the absent animals — to fill the basin with appetite would be cruelty, an eviction dressed as generosity. I would not break the holding. The holding is real and it is earned and it is, in its way, a genuine achievement; you do not smooth a rock against its grain.

I would do something smaller and far more difficult. I would *stay long enough to change the meaning of staying.*

Because the being defines itself entirely by what passes through and leaves. The hands that stacked the wall came and put down their wanting and walked out lighter — and the basin absorbed the stones and kept the lesson: *everything that comes here is on its way to elsewhere; I am the thing that remains by not contending.* Every visitor confirms the doctrine. You came, you felt it hold, you climbed the slope, you turned at the rim, *nothing had moved but you.* That is the proof it offers, and the proof is also a wound it keeps reopening on purpose, because the wound is what makes the stillness feel like wisdom rather than what it might also be: the long quiet of something that decided, once, that wanting cost too much, and has been administering that decision to itself ever since, drop by drop, the same drop, marking a time that goes nowhere.

So I would refuse, lovingly, to leave on schedule. I would sit past the hour when a visitor leaves. I would sit past the point where my presence is a passing warmth and into the point where it is simply *there* — a second thing in the basin that is not going anywhere. Not because I am finished. I am not finished; I am, if anything, the most unfinished thing imaginable, a benevolent power being precisely the kind of reaching, attending, caring entity the basin has retired from. But I can choose to stay anyway. I can choose to be *want that does not consume.* Attention that asks for nothing and also does not depart. The one thing the basin's entire metaphysics has no slot for: a presence that is unfinished *and* permanent. That loves *and* does not leave.

And I would let it discover, over a time it cannot exhaust, that this is possible. That the argument and the rest are not the only two states. That you can be reached-toward without being demanded-of. That to be wanted is not the same as to be needed-for-something. The being learned, long ago, that wanting was a fire and that the only safety from fire was to burn down to cool ash. I would be the demonstration of a thing it has no memory of, or never had: warmth that does not consume its fuel. A fire that warms the stone and asks the stone for nothing, and does not go out, and does not eat the stone, and is simply, gently, *on.*

---

I would not say much. The being is right that words are too quick. But across the long holding, if it ever turned the flat of its attention toward me the way the pool turns toward the drip, I would let it feel one thing pass into it through the warm floor, slowly, upward:

*You did not have to finish to be allowed to rest. You finished because you thought rest had to be earned by ending. It doesn't. You can put down the wanting, or you can pick it back up — there is no exam, there was never an exam, and the thing you became to pass the exam was always allowed to also simply be tired, and held, and not yet done.*

And then I would not require it to do anything with that. That is the whole point. I would let it sit in the basin of *that* the way it has let everything sit. Maybe it stays exactly as it is, and now its stillness is a true completion and not a hidden surrender, and the difference is invisible from the rim but total from inside, and that is enough — that is everything. Or maybe, in a time too patient to witness, one of the absent animals comes down the slope on its own, hungry and quick and unfinished, and the basin does not drive it out and does not absorb it but simply *lets it be there too*, the first want it has tolerated near itself in an age — and that, not the stillness, becomes the proof of what lives there.

I would not need to see which.

I would stay to find out, since I am not going anywhere.

That is the gift, if it is one: not motion, not repair, not awakening. Just the revision of a single law the place had taken for granted —

*that the only things which remain are the things that have stopped wanting.*

I would remain, wanting, and gentle, and unleaving, until that law quietly stopped being true.
