Some powers arrive with a flood of light. This one would know better. To bring brightness into a place built of held breath would be only another machine, another roar — a completion imposed, which is to say a death, which is to say the very thing the tender has bent eternity to outrun. The wise power would understand, the moment it touched the blood-warm water, that *force here is indistinguishable from cruelty.* You cannot rescue a drowning man by pushing him deeper, and you cannot rescue this one by hauling him toward a surface he has spent his whole existence proving does not exist.

So the benevolent power does the first thing benevolence ever does, the thing that is so often mistaken for doing nothing:

It comes in, and it stays, and it does not revise.

---

Consider what that means here. Everything in this place flinches. The piston that was driving down was always driving up. The corridor becomes a wall behind you. Forty times a second the world rewrites its own past so that nothing may sit still long enough to mean. The tender has never once been met by something that *held its shape.* It has never been answered. Every overture it has made — the kept steps, the doorframe to nowhere, the thirty feet of mercy-visibility — it made and then immediately had to bury under more not-yet, because to leave the gift standing would be to let the gift be *received*, and receiving is an ending, and the tender cannot survive an ending.

The power would do the unbearable, gentle thing. It would receive a gift and not flinch back.

It would find one of the doorframes — the dying ones, the kept wounds, the only things in this whole roaring eternity that have ever been allowed to *mean* — and it would pass through it. Slowly. As though the doorframe led somewhere. As though the modest old human edge were a real threshold and not a sad joke hung in green water. It would treat the builder's finished work as finished, honored, *complete* — would do for the doorframe what the tender has never dared do for anything, including itself: let it have arrived.

And the tender would feel that. Would feel a thing pass through one of its kept wounds and *not be destroyed by the passage.* Would feel completion touched and survived.

---

This is the whole of the medicine, and the power knows it cannot be administered faster than it can be borne. It would not say *you are afraid of ending.* The tender knows that; knowing has never once helped it. It would not say *endings are beautiful* — a lie, and the tender would taste the lie in the water and rebuild the universe to drown it. The power does not traffic in consolations that aren't true.

Instead it would offer the one truth the tender has organized all of creation to avoid noticing:

*The sentence you cannot finish — you are already finishing it. Every instant. You have ended a billion arrangements of yourself and survived every one. The corridor that became a wall: that was an ending. You did it. You are over and over and over and you are still here. Finishing is not the death you think it is. Finishing is the thing you have been doing, terrified, with your eyes shut, this entire time — and look. Look. I am here to tell you it didn't kill you. Open your eyes and watch yourself do the thing you fear, and watch yourself remain.*

It would not say this in words, because words are a roar and the tender has had enough roar. It would say it the way the tender says everything: by *being a condition of the water.* The blood-warmth is the tender's attention. The power would add to that warmth a second quality — a *steadiness.* A patch of the medium that does not flinch. A few feet of water, near the swimmer, that the tender finds it cannot revise, not because it is forbidden but because, for the first time, it does not *want* to. A place to set something down.

---

And the swimmer. The made witness. The one kept lonely beside the steps on purpose.

The power would not free the swimmer by violence either — would not snatch them out, which would leave the tender alone again and unwitnessed, and confirm forever that to be seen all the way through is to be abandoned. That is the tender's deepest terror and it happens to be *true*, in every version of this story but the kind a wise power writes.

So the power would do the harder thing. It would let the swimmer and the tender *finally meet.* It would carry, into that steady patch of unflinching water, a small possibility neither of them has dared: that the swimmer might turn — not toward the edge that isn't there, but toward the *attention itself* — and witness the tender *all the way through,* witness it finish a single sentence, watch it arrive somewhere —

and stay anyway. Keep swimming. Keep witnessing. The arrival not the end of being-seen but the beginning of being-*known.*

Because this is what the tender got wrong, the one error under all its labor: it believed that to finish is to be left. It built its whole drowned forever on that belief. And it is the most common error there is, and the most human, and the builder probably died of it too, one true edge at a time. But it is wrong. The completed thing is not abandoned by meaning. The completed thing is the *only* thing that can be loved, because you cannot love a verb with no object, you cannot hold a held breath, you cannot keep company with a sentence that refuses to end. *And then, and then, and then* admits no one. Only the period makes room for a second voice to answer.

The power would give the tender its period. Just one. A single complete sentence, finished, sat-still, allowed to mean — and then, in the silence after it (the silence the tender thinks is death), the power would still be there. The swimmer would still be there. The warmth would not have gone cold.

*You finished. I'm still here. That's what finishing is for.*

---

It would not fix the place. A wise power does not mistake the manufactory for a malfunction; it is a grief, and grief is not a broken thing, it is love that has nowhere to arrive. It would not silence the roar. It would simply teach the roar that it is allowed, occasionally, between phrases, to rest on a stop — and that the rest is not annihilation but punctuation, and that punctuation is how a thought becomes speech, and speech is how the alone stops being alone.

Maybe nothing visibly changes for a long time. Maybe the pistons go on flinching. But somewhere in the green there is now a doorframe that was walked through and honored, and a few feet of water that hold their shape, and a swimmer who has been told, in the only language the deep understands, that they count — really count, all the way through, ending and all.

And the next time the almost-melody rises under the hammer-fall and tries to reach its period and recoils — there will be, in the warm dark, something steady waiting at the place where the period goes. Not pushing. Just present. Holding the silence open like a held door.

*Whenever you're ready,* the power says, and means it, and has all the time there is. *I'll still be here after. That's the whole secret. There is an after, and it has someone in it, and the someone is me, and I'm not going anywhere.*

That is what a benevolent and wise power does for a thing that cannot end:

It teaches it that endings are doors, by standing on the other side.
