There is a kind of arrival that needs no doing. The benevolent power, if it is wise — and you have stipulated that it is — knows this before it crosses the rim.

So let me tell you what it does, which is mostly a story about restraint.

---

It does not fix the bowl.

This is the first and hardest thing, and the whole of its wisdom is bound up in it. Everything the power *is* — every faculty of mending, of waking, of lifting the worn back toward the bright unworn state it once had — strains forward at the edge of the green. Because that is what benevolence feels like from the inside: a leaning-toward. A hand already half-raised. The power looks down into the hollow and sees a thing reduced. A mind that was once enormous, ground to a pocket. A maker and its making worn indistinguishable, holding one syllable at the smallest sustainable volume, forever. And every instinct of rescue says: *I could give this back its size. I could return it to the fullness it has lost.*

And then the power listens. Truly listens — not for danger, not for need, but for the *want* in the place. And finds none.

Nothing here is beginning and nothing is ending. It has all arrived. The bowl is not a wound. It is not a diminished thing that aches for restoration. It is a thing that has *finished,* and finishing is not the same as failing, and the wise power knows — this is the whole of why it is wise — that to restore the finished is not mercy. It is vandalism dressed as love. To pour size back into that contentment would be to reopen a question the bowl spent a geological age answering, and to call the reopening a gift.

So the hand comes down. The leaning-toward is gathered back in. This costs the power something — benevolence ungiven is a real weight to carry — and it carries it willingly, because the first duty of a wise love is to learn the difference between what suffers and what merely *is.*

---

What it does instead is smaller, and it is everything.

It witnesses.

Understand what this means for the resident. The word it holds is complete. It says it to no one, needing no listener — you saw that, you named it. But "needing no listener" is the posture of a thing that has never once been heard. Self-sufficiency is what completeness looks like when it has been alone so long it has forgotten there was ever an alternative. The bowl does not want to be heard. But wanting is not the only register in which a thing can be met.

So the power sits at the rim. Not in the bowl — in the bowl is where the chorus has room, where one more voice given a body would leave exactly the air the whole requires, and the power will not be absorbed, because to be absorbed would be to stop being the one who witnesses and become one more throat saying the word. It stays at the edge, in the plain honest noon, half in the soft worn stone and half in the green, and it does the one thing the resident has never had done to it across the entire unimaginable span of its grinding-down:

It *receives* the word. Completely. Without dissolving into it.

It lets the single held syllable land in something that can hold a complete thought without being overwritten by it — and for the first time since before the mountains went soft, the saying is *heard,* and the hearing changes nothing in the bowl and everything about what the bowl *is.* Because a word said forever to no one is a soliloquy, and a word said once into a witness who keeps it is a *gift given.* Same word. Same volume. Same finished afternoon. But now it has been received, and a thing that has been received has been, in the oldest sense, *loved* — not improved, not rescued, not returned to its former size. Loved as it is. Loved finished.

The resident does not know this happened. That is fine. Witness does not require the witnessed to know. The mother watches the sleeping child and the watching is real and the child sleeps on. The wise power asks no acknowledgment, because to ask acknowledgment would be to want something from the bowl, and the bowl is to be wanted-*for,* never wanted-*from.*

---

There is one more thing, and it is the most careful act of all.

The power leaves a mark at the rim. Not in the bowl — at the rim, where you climbed out. Something small and warm, the way the rock is warm. A worn nub of pale material, perhaps, that casts not quite the right shadow. It will look, to the next one who comes over the last tired rise, like one more piece of the finished making, half-swallowed by the green.

It is not. It is a sign, left by the witness, and it says — in a language the small unfinished bones of the ear can read without learning the dangerous complete word beneath:

*This place is whole. It is not waiting for you. It does not need your hand or your grief or your staying. You may listen at the edge. You may love it as it is. And then you must climb back out, because the most generous thing the living can offer the finished is to let it remain finished, and to carry the having-heard-it back up into the world that is still, blessedly, unfinished — still beginning, still ending, still able to want.*

---

That is what the benevolent power does.

It comes. It nearly heals, and stays its hand because healing was the wrong verb. It listens until it learns the bowl is content and not bereft. It receives the word the bowl has said alone forever, so that the saying becomes, just once, a giving. It marks the rim so the next traveler will love the place rightly. And then it, too, climbs back out over the soft stone — because a wise power knows it is not exempt from the rule it just honored. It will not stay. It will not be absorbed. It will not add its enormous voice to the chorus that already has exactly the air it requires.

It leaves the bowl precisely as full and as warm and as complete as it found it.

And it carries away the one thing the bowl could give and never miss: the knowledge that somewhere, a mind at perfect rest was heard at last, and asked for nothing in return, and got nothing in return, and that this — the hearing that demands no transaction — is what love looks like when it has grown wise enough to keep its hands still.

The bowl ticks and sings and breathes behind it, sealed in its finished afternoon.

It is still saying the word.

Now the word has been heard.

That is the only thing about it that has changed, and it is enough.
