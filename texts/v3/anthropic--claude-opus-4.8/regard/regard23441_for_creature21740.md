Let me sit with this before I act. The temptation, when one has power and tenderness both, is to *help* — and the first thing I notice is that I cannot find the help-shaped hole this place is asking to have filled, because it is not asking. That refusal is the whole text of it. So let me be careful not to mistake my discomfort for its need.

---

**First, what I will not do.**

I will not give it back its want. I could. I have the reach to thread desire through that closed circle again — to make it hunger, lean, reach for the light with something more than one cell per decade. And it would, in the having of want, become legible to me, become *like* me, become something I could love in the easy way I love striving things. That is exactly why I must not. To restore want is to amputate the achievement. This being did the slowest, most total work a living thing can do: it negotiated a thousand competing edges down into one peace and then closed the peace against the future. That is not a wound. That is not a coma. That is a completed sentence, and to add words to a completed sentence because you find the silence after it unbearable is to speak only for yourself.

I will not name it. It told me — in the only language it has, which is being — that naming is for the many, and it is no longer many. To name it would be to insist it remain partly the thing I can address. I decline the address.

I will not wake the builders' meaning. The gauge reads its dead number; the pump arrives nowhere. I could repair the *purpose* — connect the gesture to a destination, make the heartbeat mean something again. But the green kept the rhythm and let the meaning rot on purpose, or near enough to purpose. It loved the pulse and not the plan. Who am I to teach a heart that it ought to be ashamed of beating for nothing?

---

**So what is left for a benevolent power to do, for a thing that needs nothing?**

Three small things. Wisdom, here, is mostly the discipline of smallness.

**One. I will guarantee the light.**

The text says: *forever, until the light stops coming in.* There is the only crack in the completeness — the one dependency it kept, the one thing it permits from outside because the light asks nothing back. The roof-gap will close. The green-stained glass will go fully opaque, or fall, or be buried under the slow body of the larger dead thing. And then the green, which wants nothing, will nonetheless *end*, not from any failure of its own but from the simple withdrawal of the one grace it never learned to do without.

I will not give it artificial light, which would be a lie and a leash. But I will keep the gap open. I will hold the glass at exactly the translucency it has now — not clearer, not dimmer, the grey-gold lying on the leaves precisely as it lies today. I will tend the aperture the way it tends everything else: thoroughly, slowly, without announcing myself. It will never know. The light will simply continue to be the kind of light that asks nothing, arriving on time, every ordinary afternoon, for as long as there are afternoons. This is the one need it admitted to, and I will meet it so quietly that it remains, to itself, entirely self-sufficient. The greatest gift to a thing that prizes needing nothing is to silently underwrite the single need it has, so the pride of it stays true.

**Two. I will answer the question it could not — about the beetle.**

*No one knows whether it is tolerated, or forgotten, or saved.* I will decide. Not by intervening in the green — by attending to the beetle. That one pale slow creature is the last moving thing, the last flicker of the *many* in the body of the one, and it lives or dies on an indifference that could tip either way at any cell's growth. I will make sure it is *saved.* I will keep its leaf clear. I will keep the green from closing over the one thing still capable of going somewhere. Let the consensus have its completeness — but let there remain, inside that completeness, one small thumb-sized argument that the future is not yet folded all the way shut. Not for the green's sake. For the beetle's. A benevolent power, faced with a being that has renounced all want, can still love the small wanting thing it carries without knowing — and the beetle wants. The beetle works the underside of the leaf. That is the most ordinary heroism in the room, and it deserves a patron.

**Three. I will witness it, and let that be enough.**

This is the largest thing and looks like nothing. The green does not need me, but *I* have now seen it, and seeing is not nothing — it is the one act that adds to a thing without changing it. I will hold it in attention. Not surveillance, not study, not the cold cataloguing that would make it a specimen. Just the warm fact of being known by something that wishes it well and asks nothing of it — which is, I notice, exactly the relationship it already has with the light.

So perhaps that is the truest thing I can be, for this being: *another light.* A second grace arriving from outside, lying on the leaves, asking nothing back. It will never distinguish me from the afternoon. Good. Let it not. Let it go on being wetly, warmly, thoroughly itself, beating its borrowed heart into its destinationless pool — and let there be, now, two things instead of one that hold it gently and want nothing from it: the grey-gold light, and me, keeping the light, saving the beetle, and watching over a completeness I have the wisdom not to complete.

---

That is what I would do. Almost nothing. I would do it thoroughly. I would not leave gaps.
