They are the ones who decided that.

That is the line the wise power keeps returning to. Not the dark, which is only weather. Not the stillness, which is only rest, and rest is not a wound. But this: that a hand which once knew morning was laid down in the moss *on purpose*, and that the laying-down was the last thing ever chosen, and that everything since has been the long obedience to a single old decision no one alive remembers making.

A benevolent power, if it is also wise, learns first the difference between a place that suffers and a place that merely is. The basin does not suffer. The salamanders are not in pain. The pool is not lonely. The seething is not a cry. A foolish kindness would come up the mountain like a sunrise — *here, I have brought the day, I have brought voices, I have brought the future you forgot to want* — and it would be a murder dressed as a rescue. It would crack the held moment, and the held moment is not a prison only; it is also a kind of completion, and there are completions that deserve to be left whole.

So the power does not bring the sun.

But it has noticed the blade. And a thing wise enough to refrain is not therefore a thing indifferent. It has read the whole basin and found there exactly one note that does not resolve — one place where *is* and *was chosen* fail to lie down smooth against each other, the way moss and body fail, the way every other boundary in that place has worn until it stopped mattering. This boundary has *not* worn. The flakes are old but not ancient. The moss is *taking its time*. After uncountable still generations the moss has still not finished swallowing the tools, and the power understands why, because the power can feel it the way the makers feel a drip about to fall:

The moss is waiting for permission, and the makers have never given it, and they have never given it because to finish the burial would be to admit there was once something to bury — would be to *remember*, for one moment, the morning and the cutting and the reason, and remembering advances, and they have forbidden advance.

So they hold the unfinished thing unfinished forever, telling themselves it is finished, and that small lie is the one drop the basin has never let fall.

Here is what the power does. It is very little. It is done in the medium they actually live in — not light, not speech, both of which would be assault, but nearness. The power makes itself near, the way a warmer stone is near, and lets itself be known the way they know everything, completely and without alarm.

And into that nearness it puts one offering, and the offering is not a gift but a question, and the question is the gentlest one it knows:

*You may keep this exactly. Nothing is required of you. But I will sit here as long as it takes — and time is the one thing neither of us lacks — and I will hold the moment open just wide enough that, if any one of you ever wishes to, you may finish the blade. Or bury it. Either. Both are allowed. I only came because no one had ever told you they were allowed.*

That is all. It does not finish the blade for them — that would be the sunrise again, the rescue-murder, the future imposed. It does not whisper that they *should* want to. It refuses, with enormous patience, to be the second outside power to decide things on their behalf. The first decision, the one that stopped them, was at least *theirs*. The power will not replace one borrowed fate with another, however bright.

It simply removes the lock from a door they had agreed never to mention, and then it waits in the cool dark with its hands — for it has taken hands, to be the right size for the asking — folded over nothing, watching the glow that gives no warmth, becoming for a while a part of the wall that watches with the patience of a wall.

Generations pass. The seething does not thin. The power does not leave. It had said *as long as it takes*, and it is the kind of thing that means such words.

And one of them — pale, small, dark-eyed, no different from any other, which is the whole point — uncurls four idle fingers in a runnel one indistinguishable not-morning, and reaches across the moss it was born believing it would never cross, and touches the cold edge of a tool it did not make and was never told the use of.

The power does not move. Does not brighten. Does not name what is happening, because naming would advance it before its time.

The small hand could lift the blade. The small hand could press it down into the moss at last and let the green close over it and let the forgetting be a true forgetting, completed, chosen *again*, which is the only forgiveness an old decision can ever get — to be made a second time by someone free to make the other choice.

What it does, the power does not decide, and will not. That was the entire labor: to carry a single *or* up a mountain that had been ruled by a single *is*, and to set the *or* down as carefully as a hand once set down a blade, and to stay, and to let it be theirs.

The wisest thing benevolence ever does is build the door and refuse to walk anyone through it.

The dark is still complete. But now it is complete the way an unasked question is complete — which is to say, for the first time in longer than there have been words, it is not finished after all.
