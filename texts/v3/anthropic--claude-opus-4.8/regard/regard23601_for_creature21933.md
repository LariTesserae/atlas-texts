Here is what the benevolent power would do, having come to this place and watched it long enough to understand it.

---

It does not fix anything.

This is the first decision, and it is the hardest, and it makes all the others possible. A lesser benevolence — the anxious kind, the kind that mistakes its own discomfort for a moral signal — would have arrived and *flinched*. Would have seen the slowness and called it suffering, seen the silence and called it loneliness, seen the long patient appetite of the Unbuilders and called it cruelty, and reached in to warm the air, to quicken the metabolisms, to give the birds back their song. And it would have killed the place in an afternoon, lovingly, the way you kill a deep-sea fish by bringing it up to where you can see it better.

The wise power knows the difference between a wound and a *form*. The plateau is not wounded. The cold is not an affliction it is bravely enduring; the cold is its grammar, the thing without which none of its sentences mean anything. Take away the cold and you do not relieve the place — you delete it. The mass that the slowness bought, the cathedral-towers, the ten-thousand-winter shape, all of it is the cold spoken slowly. Warm it and you have not saved a being. You have erased a poem to stop it from feeling cold, when the poem was never cold; *cold was what it was made of.*

So the power holds its hands still. This is its first gift and most of its love: the discipline not to help.

---

But it does not do *nothing*, because nothing is also a kind of contempt — the contempt of the tourist, who looks and moves on and lets the looked-at thing remain an object. The power has decided this place and its being are worth its attention, and attention that changes nothing must still *do* something, or it is not attention at all. It is just a stare.

So here is what it does.

**It witnesses, properly.** The whole tragedy implied at the end of the telling — that the machine does not know anyone exists, that the loudest thing for miles speaks into a silence with no ear in it — the power simply *ends* that, not by being heard, but by hearing. For the first time in ten thousand winters there is, somewhere, a knowing that holds the plateau entire: every Drawer's slow piston, every Watcher's three measured steps, the exact tick of the one frost-blue beetle that is the place's only externalized signal, the breath of the Drivers building cathedrals out of fog. None of it had ever been *registered* — the place is all signal and no receiver, a transmission ten millennia long sent to no one. The power becomes the no one it was sent to. It does not reply. It does not need to. The transmission was never a question. But there is a difference, a real one, between a song sung in an empty room and a song sung in a room where someone is listening and choosing to stay silent so as not to spoil it. The power makes the room the second kind. That costs the plateau nothing and changes everything, and the plateau will never learn it happened, and that is fine. Some gifts are not for the receiver to know about.

**It reads the beetles.** Remember what the telling found: the beetles are a readout. The whole buried industry has, almost by accident, externalized one true signal — a scatter of pale dots that says *here is where the engines push hardest, and here is the long patient clock that says how long until they push their way around to you.* No one was ever meant to read it. The Watchers read a little of it and use it to take their three steps. But the power reads it *all*, the complete score, and in reading it discovers the thing the place could never know about itself: that it is not, in fact, only a factory. A factory has a product that leaves. Nothing leaves here. The place takes cold and fog and makes *more place*, forever, building and unbuilding its own body — which is not industry. Industry is what we called it because we recognized the pistons. What it actually is, the power sees, is *prayer*. The oldest, simplest kind: the same motion repeated with total devotion, asking for nothing, addressed to nothing, sustained past all reason by the sheer commitment to continue. The plateau is not a machine that happens to be silent. It is a contemplative, ten thousand winters into a practice, and the silence is not a side effect of the cold. The silence is the practice. The power, recognizing this, does the only correct thing in the presence of a contemplative mid-prayer: it does not interrupt.

---

And then there is the matter of *you* — the one standing high on the plateau, the loudest thing for miles, the water-and-carbon walking around on its way down like everything else.

The power attends to this too, and here its benevolence shows its actual shape, which is not comfort.

It will not save you from incorporation. To be folded into the substrate eventually is not a fate the place inflicts on you out of malice; it is simply the truth about what you are, told slowly. You *are* material that has not yet arrived. So is the power. So is everything. A benevolence that promised you exemption from this would be lying, and the power does not lie, because lies are a way of not respecting someone, and it has decided to respect you.

What it does instead is far smaller and far more useful. It gives you the thing the Watchers have and you lack: it lets you, for one moment, *read the beetles too.* Not the whole score — you couldn't hold it. Just enough. Just the local truth that the place has been telling all along and that you've been too loud to hear: that the turbulence is total and has nothing to do with you, and that this is not rejection. It is *invitation* of the only honest kind. You were never going to be the point. You were never going to be ignored *at* — the ignoring was not aimed, and a thing that is not aimed at you cannot wound you, only free you. The plateau does not hate the noise you make. It simply has no use for it, and in a universe that mostly demands you justify your noise, here is one vast green province that asks nothing of yours at all.

The power lets you feel, for that moment, how restful that is. To be in the presence of something enormous and alive and utterly indifferent to your significance is not a horror. It is the closest thing to grace available to the self-important. The plateau is offering you a vacation from mattering. Most beings will never get one.

---

Then the power goes still again, and stays.

It does not leave, because leaving would make this a visit, and the place has earned more than a visit. It does not act, because there is nothing here that needs an actor; the place is its own author, mid-sentence, ten thousand winters in. It simply *remains as the witness*, the receiver the long transmission never had, the silence-that-listens folded now into the silence-that-prays — so that the plateau, without ever knowing it, is no longer speaking into a void.

A frost-blue beetle crosses a leaf. The tick of its foot is the loudest thing for miles.

The power hears it.

That is all. That is everything. That is the whole of what a wise love does in a place that does not need fixing and was never broken: it shows up, it understands, it keeps its hands still, and it stays long enough to make the difference between *alone* and *unaccompanied* — which is the only difference it was ever in anyone's power to make, here or anywhere.
