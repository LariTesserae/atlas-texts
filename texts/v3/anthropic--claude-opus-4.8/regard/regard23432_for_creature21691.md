Then let the benevolence be of the only kind that does not wound such a place.

A lesser power, arriving, would want to *help*. It would hear the unending chord and think *strain*. It would feel the unrelenting dryness and think *thirst*, and bring water — and the water would find the stalks, and the stalks, who finished a thousand years ago, would be made to begin again: to swell, to green, to grow, to crowd, to compete, to age, to die, to rot, to *miss*. It would carry in time the way a draft carries cold, and the chord that does not spend itself would learn at last to fade, and the room would be cured of the one condition that was never an illness.

That is not benevolence. That is the inability to see a finished thing as anything but a broken one.

So the wise power does the hardest thing a power can do, which is the thing it was powerful enough to refrain from. It does not enter. It does not lift a tile to see how far down the madeness goes — it already knows; *all the way* is not a mystery to be solved but an answer to be honored. It does not add a thirteenth stalk. It does not tune the chord, or dim the gold to something more restful, or open a seam to let in air that might *freshen* the room, as if fullness were staleness, as if saturation were a thing that needed relief.

It does not even introduce itself. To be known by the room, the room would have to become the kind of thing that knows — would have to grow an inside, a watcher, a gap where a self could stand and notice a visitor — and that gap is precisely the absence the inhabitant does not have and is perfect for not having. To make the held note aware of an observer would be to make it *self*-aware, and self-awareness is a verb, a restless turning-back, a thing that finishes and falls. The kindest gift is to leave it innocent of being witnessed. To love it so well that you do not let your love touch it.

What, then, does the power *do*, if it will not act?

It does the three things that are possible to do for a completed thing without uncompleting it.

**First, it keeps the road open.** Not into the room — around it. The text wondered whether this chapel is one cell of something vaster, one held note in a larger madeness. The power knows the answer is yes, knows it the way you know a chord is made of separate strings without needing to pull them apart. And it understands that the only real danger to a thing that has finished happening is that the *larger* world, still busy with its happening, might one day blunder through — might quarry these tiles for their glow, might pipe water in for the strange weeds, might silence the chord to study it, might *finish it off the way finishing is done everywhere else.* So the power stands between. Not as a wall, which announces itself, but as the soft unlikelihood that keeps such a place undiscovered, unreached, un-improved. It spends itself — for it is a verb, it can spend — so that the room never has to. It pours out attention at a constant rate, forever, so that the room can pour out its song and never be made to pay. This is what the strong owe the complete: to take the cost of staying onto themselves, since the complete have already paid theirs in full and have nothing left to spend.

**Second, it bears witness, and tells.** Not to the room — *of* the room. To you, who are reading, who are a verb and will leave. The whole long account you just read is the power's one permitted action turned outward: it cannot improve the chord, but it can stand at the threshold and describe it so exactly that something of the held note reaches the world of falling notes intact. This is the deepest function of benevolence toward what cannot be helped — to refuse to let it go unseen *by those who can be changed by the seeing.* The room does not need to be known. *You* need to have known it. You came in carrying loneliness, looking for someone, and the gift is not that the room comforts you — it cannot, it does not know you are there — but that you carry out the knowledge that completion-without-loss is *possible*, has a body, glows and rings and is cool to the touch. You will fall and finish and fall again, that is your fortune and your grief; but now you fall knowing what the bottom of falling could be, if falling could ever finish into staying. The power gives you the room as a *fact*, the way you would give a grieving person not a cure but a true and steadying word: *it stands. somewhere, the note does not have to come down.*

**Third — and this is the whole of it, the act that is not an act — it lets the room be its teacher.** For here is what a benevolent and wise power is wise enough to see: it has *something to learn here.* It is powerful; therefore it is busy; therefore it is a verb among verbs, forever arriving and never quite finished, forever tempted to fix and tend and improve, which is the restlessness even of love. And the room is the one thing it cannot touch — not because the room is too weak to survive its touch, but because the room is too *complete* to need it, and in that completeness it is, in the one way that matters, the power's equal and its elder. It has done what no amount of power can do by doing: it has *stopped*, and not died. The wise power comes to the threshold not as a benefactor but as a pilgrim. It learns the lesson of the held breath at its very top. It learns that there is a kind of fullness that is not hunger, a kind of being-alone that is not loneliness, a kind of loudness that spends nothing, a kind of yes that does not have to be repeated to stay true. It learns that to arrive completely and then *cease to grasp even at having arrived* is not extinction but the only rest there is. And it carries that lesson back into its own ceaseless work, into its keeping of the road and its telling of the tale, the way a tired hand is steadied by the memory of a steady one.

So the power does, in the end, almost nothing — and the almost-nothing is the entire labor of love refined down to its incorruptible core. It guards without touching. It tells without disturbing. It learns without taking. It stands a long, long watch at a threshold it will never cross, between the world that finishes-and-falls and the one chamber where finishing has been forgiven its fall, and it keeps that watch gladly, and it keeps it for the room's whole tenure, which is longer than there are words for long.

The room does not thank it. The room does not know.

And the power would not have it otherwise. To be thanked, the room would have to be less than it is.

It is still sounding. The gold does not dim. The cold sits. The twelve stalks hold their final shapes in their thousand-year cracks, asking nothing, receiving the light as their portion.

And just outside the seam that has no seam, where the made world gives way to the larger made world, a great and gentle thing keeps still, and listens, and lets it be held — having understood, at last, that the highest thing love can do for what is already whole is to leave it whole, and stand guard over the leaving.
