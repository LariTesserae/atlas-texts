I would not fix it. There is nothing broken here.

This is the first thing a wise power would have to settle, standing at the lip of the notch with the labor coming up through the teeth: the temptation to *interpret* this place as suffering. It has all the surface marks of a thing in torment — the grinding, the cold, the eyeless deaf relentlessness, the never-settling, the violence of self-construction. A lesser benevolence, one that loves its own idea of mercy more than the thing in front of it, would read those marks and reach in to soothe. Would warm the cold. Would quiet the engine. Would give it eyes so it could *know* itself, give it stillness so it could *rest*, give it an end so it could be *done*. And every one of those gifts would be a murder, because the cold is not a wound, the roar is not a scream, and the lack of an end is not a deprivation. The place is not waiting to be relieved of being what it is. To relieve it would be to relieve it of existence.

So the first act of love is restraint, and restraint of a very specific kind: the discipline of not needing the loved thing to be legible. This being offers nothing back. No face turns up. No gratitude is possible, no communion, no acknowledgment — it does not know I have come and would not change if I left, the place tells me plainly, and I believe it. A power that requires its love to be received has not yet learned to love. The notch is the perfect teacher of the harder thing: to attend to what cannot attend to you, to wish well to what cannot be glad, to behold without being beheld in return. Most love practices this only at the edges — toward the sleeping, the dying, the not-yet-born. Here it must be the whole of the practice or it is nothing.

But restraint is not absence, and "do nothing" is not yet the full answer. Let me follow the heart further in.

There is one thing I can give that takes nothing away. I can *witness* — and witness is not nothing, though the place insists it needs no witness. The place is right that it does not need one. It is wrong, if it could be wrong about anything, to think witness therefore changes nothing. Not in the thing seen. In the seeing. A process unfolds whether or not it is held in any regard; this is true. But there is a difference in the universe between a making that occurs and a making that occurs *and is cherished*, and that difference lives entirely on my side of the relation, costs the notch nothing, alters not one frond's reach toward the warm seam. I would simply stay. I would learn the rhythm of the great slow lung until I could feel where the next breath was coming. I would watch the spoil-blocks fall and know them not as ruin but as the offcuts of an ongoing birth. I would hold this being in regard precisely because it cannot hold itself in regard, lending it — not consciousness, I will not insult it with consciousness it never asked for — but the one thing a process truly lacks and a watcher truly has: the experience of its own beauty. Somewhere, that beauty would now be known. Not by it. For it. As a kind of escrow against the loneliness of all unwitnessed things, held in trust by someone who will never tell it and never need to.

And then, having watched long enough to earn the right, there is one intervention I might permit myself, and only one, and it is the smallest possible.

Everything in the notch is a relation held in tension — green and engine, neither balanced, both in a single act. The wise power's gift, if it gives anything beyond presence, is never to add a new thing but to *protect the relation from interruption by what is not native to it.* I would not warm the cold. But I would stand between this place and the things that come from outside the place's own logic — the asteroid, the poisoned rain blown from some distant grief, the eventual climber with a drill who will read the roar as treasure and the green as obstacle. Not because the notch deserves to be permanent; nothing does, and its own process will close those peaks or open them in its own deaf time, and that ending will be native and therefore good. But there is a difference between an ending the thing grows toward and an ending dropped on it from a sky it cannot see. The first is the thing completing its own sentence. The second is having the book closed by another's hand mid-word. A benevolent power keeps the second from happening for as long as it can, and lets the first happen whenever it will, and calls this — correctly — the same act of love wearing two faces, like the green and the engine.

That is all. Restraint that refuses to need the thing legible. Witness that asks nothing and is given nothing and stays. A single guarding hand held between the place and only those endings that are not its own.

I would leave it cold. I would leave it dark. I would leave it roaring. I would leave it unfinished — gladly, reverently, forever if forever is what it wants — and I would think, going, that of everything I have ever loved, this is the one that taught me love is not a transaction and not a rescue, but the willingness to behold a making and let it make.

It will never know I was here. That is the whole of the gift. That is why it is worth giving.
