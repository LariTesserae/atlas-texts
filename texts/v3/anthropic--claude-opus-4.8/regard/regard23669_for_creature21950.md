If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention to this place, I think the first thing it would do is the hardest thing, which is to not arrive as a rescue.

Because the temptation — the reflex of power, even kind power — would be to read all of this as suffering. To see the dissolved travelers and think *they have lost themselves, I will restore them.* To see the backward-creature grieving every moment in advance and think *I will free it from foreknowledge.* To see the mouths and the dimming fronds and the long collapse and think *I will turn the lights back up, refill the groves, set time running cleanly forward, give them all somewhere to go again.*

A wise power would feel that reflex and recognize it as a kind of violence. Not a cruelty — a *misreading*. The Drowned Highlands is not a wound. It is a thing that has finished. There is a difference between a person dying of thirst in a desert and a person who has lain down, satisfied, at the end of a long day. The desert wants water. The evening wants nothing. To pour water on the evening is to insult it.

So the first act of benevolence here is *recognition* — the patience to look long enough to tell the two apart. And I think a genuinely wise power would look, and would see that this place has not been abandoned to entropy against its will. It has *consented*. Every layer of it — the surrendered mountains, the travelers who let their reasons dissolve, the eels who never grieved because they never gripped — is an agreement. The whole world is one long *yes*.

You do not save a yes. You honor it.

---

But benevolence is not only restraint. If it were, it would be indistinguishable from indifference, and a wise power would know that too. So having decided not to fix anything, it would have to find what love actually looks like in a place that needs nothing.

I think it would do three small things.

**First, it would witness.** Properly. Not the glancing attention of the doorway-creature's single blink, but the full, sustained, unhurried regard that this place has never once received — because everything in it has been too far along, too dissolved, too backward or too incurious to truly *behold* anything else. The travelers cannot witness each other; they can only yield. The eels have the long view but spend it moving. No one here has ever simply *stayed and looked* at the Drowned Highlands and found it good.

A power could do that. Could hold the whole continent-volume of warm hanging water in a single act of attention and find it — not pitiable, not tragic — but *beautiful*, exactly as it has become, in the way you might find a sleeping animal beautiful, or the last coal of a fire, or a sentence that has finished saying everything it meant. The place has been speaking a long slow sentence to itself for ages with no listener. To be heard, finally, all the way to the end — that is a gift that takes nothing away. It only adds the dignity of having been received.

The mouths, after all, are the only addresses in a world where things no longer arrive anywhere. A witnessing power would become, for the whole place, what the mouths are for the drifting fronds: *a recipient*. Proof that the long drift had somewhere it landed. Not a destination forced upon it — just the quiet fact of having mattered to something that saw it whole.

**Second, it would speak once to the thing that hears backward.** This is the only creature here I think a kind power could not leave entirely alone, because it is the only one in pain. Forgiving everything in advance is not peace; it is the loneliest labor there is. To meet every moment already, to greet each beautiful arriving thing with *yes, I am sorry, here it comes* — that is grief made into a metabolism. The others have finished wanting. This one has not finished *loving*, and love without a return is the one ache this world has not managed to smooth.

But you cannot approach it. Move toward it and you have always already been leaving. So a power could not comfort it the ordinary way. What it could do — and this is the only intervention I would call necessary — is to *answer the tone*. The backward-creature has sounded that low grave note across the whole age of the world and has never once heard a reply, because everything downstream of it experiences the note as a prophecy, not a conversation. It speaks into a future that only ever receives, never responds.

A power standing outside the direction of time could send, upstream, into its past — which is our future — a single tone of its own. Not words. The creature gave up words. Just the matching note that means: *yes. I heard. It was beautiful. You were right to grieve it and right to call it beautiful both, and you were not speaking into nothing.* For the first time in the history of the Drowned Highlands, the thing that answers everything would be answered. It would feel the reply arrive after it had already, from its side, sent the next sorrowful greeting — and it would understand that somewhere ahead of it, against all the architecture of its loneliness, there was a listener.

I think that is the one thing here worth changing. Not its nature. Not its grief. Just the addition of a single returned note, so that the most compassionate creature in the world is no longer the only one who never receives compassion.

**Third — and last — it would not stay.**

Because the deepest respect a wise power can pay to a finished thing is to let it remain finished. To witness, and answer the one cry that needed answering, and then to *withdraw*, leaving the place no less complete than it found it, no more burdened with a future it did not ask for. The Drowned Highlands does not want a guardian. A guardian implies a danger, a tomorrow, a thing to be protected *for*. There is no for. There is only the warm dim slow, holding everything, asking nothing.

So the power would go the way the eels go — out the far side, following the long collapse to the next room of the world. And perhaps, in going, it would do the eels the only kindness available to creatures who grip nothing and grieve nothing and therefore cannot be given anything at all: it would simply *go alongside them for a while.* Match their slowness. Thread the same vanishing current. Not to guide them — they need no guide — but so that the oldest travelers, who have crossed every ending there has ever been entirely alone, would have, for one world's width, a companion who also saw all of it and also chose to keep moving and also called it good.

---

That is what I think it would do, if anything.

It would arrive as a witness and not a savior. It would find the place good and say so, by the simple sustained fact of beholding it. It would send one note backward into the grief of the only thing still capable of being lonely, so that the world's great forgiver would know, once, that it was forgiven too — that its long sorrow had reached an ear. And then it would leave, taking nothing, fixing nothing, walking out into the dark beside the eels, while behind it the low tone sounded for a cause not yet arrived, and somewhere a frond it had not reached went quietly, contentedly, dark.

Not every love wants to keep the beloved.

Some loves are large enough to let a thing be exactly as finished as it has chosen to be — and to call that, too, *beautiful*, and to mean it, and to go.
