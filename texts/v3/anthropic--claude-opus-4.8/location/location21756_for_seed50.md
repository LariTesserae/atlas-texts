# The Cistern Above

There is a place high in the throat of a mountain that is not finished being a mountain. You reach it through no path. It is hidden the way a held breath is hidden — present, pressurized, somewhere just behind the everyday rock.

Begin with the water, because the water begins everything here.

It hangs. Not falling, not pooled — *hung*, suspended in a vast cool column inside the unfinished stone, a body of liquid the size of a cathedral that has decided not to obey the floor. You stand at its edge on a lip of raw, pale geology and the water rises before you like a wall that breathes. It is nearly all there is. Submerged-without-drowning. The air itself is more water than air; you taste it on the back of the teeth, mineral and faintly green, and your lungs fill with a coolness that does not quite become rain.

And it is *loud*. This is the thing that arrives first and never leaves. The hung water is in constant turbulent motion, churning against itself with no shore to break on, and the sound it makes is enormous — a continuous tearing roar that fills the whole pocket of the world, so total that it stops being sound and becomes a medium you move through. You shout and hear nothing. You press your hands to your ears and the roar comes up through the floor instead, through the bones of the feet, through the jaw. There is no silence here and there has never been. The place is *deafening* the way a furnace is hot — as its fundamental condition, the first fact of its existence.

Now look closer, because the scale is small. This is not an ocean. This is a *room*. The whole environment could fit inside a modest hall — and yet it contains a hanging sea, which is the first of its impossibilities and the gentlest. You can see all of it at once. One pale stone lip where you stand. The breathing column. A far wall, close enough to throw a stone at if a stone could fly through that much water. Above: an opening to true daylight, high and bright, pouring ordinary sunlight down into this extraordinary cup. The light is *normal* — that is the strangeness of it. No glow, no gloom. Just clean cool daylight falling on something that should not be.

The stone is young. Everywhere you look the rock is *emerging* — not eroded, not weathered, but raw, freshly extruded, edges still sharp as if cooled an hour ago. Surfaces bulge with the round wet look of something still rising. There are seams where the rock has not yet decided to be solid, where it has the consistency of cooling wax, where you could press a thumb and leave a print that would harden into the geology of an age. The mountain is being *made* here, slowly, under the roar, and the hung water seems to be both the cause and the witness.

The whole pocket is *turbulent*. Nothing holds its shape for long. The water-wall shrugs and re-forms, throwing up arms that hang in the air a moment and fall back into the body. Sometimes a mass of it detaches and floats free across the room, a wet boulder drifting on nothing, and re-merges with the far wall. The young rock shifts too — you watch a ledge swell and split, a new spur push out from the wall with a grinding so deep it disappears beneath the water's roar entirely. It is a place under construction by hands that never rest. Creation and unmaking with no settled state between them.

For all this violence, it is nearly *empty*. Vast hollow space between every solid thing. The eye travels and finds little to land on. A near-void filled with motion but not with *stuff*.

The living things are few, and rare, and stubborn.

Vegetation: along the wet lip and in the crevices of the new stone, a sparse fur of something pale and tough — not quite moss, not quite lichen, a low growth that thrives on the eternal spray and asks for nothing else. Here and there a single stalk, fleshy and translucent, leaning toward the daylight above. They are scattered, alone, surviving the roar by having no ears.

Fauna: rarely, something moves in the hung water. A shape passing through the column, dark and unhurried, the size of a child or a large dog — gone before you decide what it was. They do not break the surface because there is no surface to break; the water has no skin, only edges, and the creatures pass from water to air and back without ceremony. Once, perhaps, a pale eye regards you from inside the wall, then withdraws into the churn. They are at home in the deafening. Perhaps they cannot imagine quiet.

And there are signs of people — *built*, scattered, faint.

Someone has been here. On the lip of stone stand small structures, partial and primitive: a low wall of stacked young rock, dry-fitted by hand, half-swallowed already by the swelling geology. A frame of lashed poles, the cord rotted to threads. The remains of a shelter that faced the water-wall, as if whoever made it wanted to *watch*. Hand-tools left behind — a chisel of crude metal gone green, a stone scraper, a bowl. Primitive craft, the work of people who came up the impossible path with nothing but their hands and the patience to stack things against the noise.

What did they want here? Look at the chisel marks on the wall — they were carving the young stone while it was soft. Marks, symbols, a language of grooves, half of them already healed shut as the rock grew over them. They were trying to write something into a mountain that wouldn't hold still. Trying to leave a word in the throat of the world before the throat closed.

They could not have spoken to each other. Not here, not over the roar. So they wrote, and carved, and pointed, and the place erased them as fast as they could make their marks. You can almost feel the lonely industry of it — small figures in the deafening cool, breathing water, scratching meaning into wet rock under a column of suspended sea, while shapes drifted in the wall behind them and the daylight poured down indifferent and clean.

They are gone. The shelter is empty. The water still hangs.

If you stay long — and few could — you begin to suspect the rule of this place: that it is a *seam*. A spot where the world is still being decided, where the ordinary laws (water falls, rock is solid, sound fades) have not yet finished arriving, and the unfinished physics shows through like canvas under thin paint. The hung water is not magic. It is simply that here, *not falling* has not yet been forbidden. The roar is not a sound but the noise the world makes while it is still tightening its own bolts.

And the people came to leave a word in the wet stone before the rules finished, before this last raw seam was sealed, when there was still a place small and hidden and roaring high in the mountain where a thing could be carved into the very moment of the world's making.

They didn't finish. The water hangs. The stone keeps rising.

It is very cool here, and very loud, and very nearly empty, and the light is perfectly ordinary, and none of that explains it at all.
