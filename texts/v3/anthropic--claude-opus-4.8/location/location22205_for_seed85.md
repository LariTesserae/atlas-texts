# The Stillwater Vault

Begin underwater, because everything here begins underwater.

There is no surface to break. If you ascended — and you can ascend, the water permits it — you would only find more water, colder and darker, then colder and darker still, with no roof of air to greet you. The vault has no ceiling that anyone has found. It is water all the way up, and water all the way down, and somewhere in the middle, holding altitude in a way that water should not hold altitude, there is a city.

The city sits at a high place. This is the strange thing you notice first, once you understand it: the water has elevation the way mountain air has elevation. There is a *high* and a *low* here, and the high is thin and clear and very cold, and the low is dense and pressing and slightly warmer, and the city floats at the line between them as if balanced on a ridge made of nothing but the disagreement between two densities of fluid. The builders chose the highlands of the sea. They built on the crest of a pressure-mountain, where the water itself thins to something almost like breath.

## What it is made of

No plants. None. Not the suggestion of a plant, not the fossil of one, not the chemistry that would lead to one. This is a mineral and a built world, and the water carries no green tint, no drifting spore, no faint organic murk. It is clean the way distilled things are clean — clean to the point of feeling sterile, feeling *intended*.

The structures are made of a black glass that the cold has fixed in place. Volcanic, perhaps, in origin — there are sharp young edges everywhere, conchoidal fractures that have not been worn down, faces that still hold the geometry of their breaking. Nothing here is old enough to be smooth. The towers rise in obsidian shards, faceted, recently-formed, their corners crisp. Run a hand along an edge and it would cut you, if you had not learned by now not to touch the edges.

But the glass is not only glass. Threaded through it, at a scale finer than sight, there is engineering. You feel it before you understand it: the buildings are *cooperating* with the water. They hold their depth without anchor. They manage the pressure-gradient around themselves so that a swimmer moves between districts without the crushing weight that the deep should bring. The black glass is doing arithmetic. Somewhere below the resolution of the eye, the material is computing — adjusting buoyancy, refracting the little light there is, maintaining the equilibrium that lets a high-altitude city hang in liquid.

## The light

Almost none. This is the regime of darkness, four-tenths of a step up from nothing. The thin cold water above lets some faint grey suggestion filter down — not sunlight, there is no sun, but a glow of unknown origin, a paleness in the high water that dies before it reaches the low.

The city makes its own light, sparingly. In the black glass there are veins that hold a cold blue luminescence, but they do not blaze; they are kept low, kept quiet, like a house where everyone has gone to sleep but left one lamp burning per room. You navigate by these. A vein here, a vein there. Long stretches of pure black between them where you move by memory and the pressure of water against skin.

## The sound

Mostly quiet — but underwater quiet, which is its own thing. Sound travels far and arrives strange. There is a constant low tone, almost below hearing, that might be the glass computing or might be the water itself holding its pressure. Occasionally a single *tick* — a crystalline note, like one facet of glass settling against another, traveling enormous distance through the liquid to reach you clear and small. These ticks are rare and you cannot find their source. The city does not hum or roar. It *thinks* quietly, and now and then it clicks.

## What lives here

Almost nothing. The water is mostly empty — sparse, scattered, much darkness between any two presences. But there is fauna, rare and odd.

They are not fish. They have no eyes, because there is nothing to see; they move by the pressure-sense the whole place seems built around. They are pale, jointed, slow. They drift between the towers in ones and twos, never schools, and they do not feed — there is nothing to feed on — so what they do, what they are *for*, is unclear. They seem to maintain things. One will press itself against a glass facet and remain there for a long time, and when it departs, the vein within glows a little steadier. They may be the city's caretakers. They may be the city's thoughts, embodied and sent on errands. They do not respond to you. They have no interest in anything warm.

## The rule of the place

Nothing changes. This is the deepest law of the Stillwater Vault and the strangest: it is in equilibrium, and the equilibrium is total. There are no currents — the water does not move, which water at this scale should never manage, but here it simply holds. There is no tide because there is no moon and no surface. The temperature does not vary by season because there are no seasons. The cold is fixed at its single cool value, everywhere, always.

The glass does not erode because the still water carries nothing to erode it with and the sharp young edges will stay sharp forever — a fresh world, recently made, that will never age, because aging requires change and change has been engineered out. The veins glow at exactly their level. The caretakers drift their slow circuits. The single ticks arrive at no rhythm but never cease entirely.

You understand, eventually, hanging there in the thin cold high water between the black towers, that you have entered something that was *finished*. Not abandoned — finished. Completed and set. Some intelligence built a city in the highlands of an endless sea, sealed it against time, and tuned it to a single perfect note, and then — what? Left. Or stayed, dissolved into the computing glass, became the quiet that thinks. The city does not need you and will not notice you and will be exactly this, unchanged, long after you have risen back into the dark water looking for an air that is not there.

It is very beautiful. It is the kind of beautiful that does not require a witness, and is the same whether or not you came.
