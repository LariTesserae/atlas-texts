There is a room inside a stone, and the stone is inside a mountain, and I am not certain the mountain exists in the usual way.

Let me begin with what holds still.

The floor is a single facet of cold mineral — not ice, though it behaves like ice in some lights, going translucent at the edges where you'd expect it to be most solid. Cleave it and it would cleave clean, the way feldspar does, in flat plates that catch the dim light and hand it back slightly wrong: a half-second late, as if reflection here has weight and has to be carried. The whole place is small. You could cross it in nine steps. Pocket-scale. A held breath of a place, embedded in something larger that you will never see the outside of.

It is cold the way high places are cold — thin cold, a cold that comes from being far up and far from any warm body, not from weather. There is no weather here. There is no water to make weather from. What little moisture once existed has long since frozen into the grain of things and stayed there, a fossil of dampness, never to thaw and never to fall.

The air is dry past the point of any lung's comfort. It carries almost no sound. When something does make a sound — a settling, a faint tick of mineral adjusting to its own slow cooling — the sound arrives flattened, stripped of overtones, a single clean frequency with nothing around it. Silence here is not absence. It is a material. It pools in the low places of the room like a gas that has decided not to move.

Now the things that are scattered through it.

Set into the floor, unevenly, with much empty stone between them, are objects I want to call instruments though they were never made for hands. They are small — fist-sized, some smaller — and each one is a piece of engineering that the room's own physics does not entirely permit. One is a knot of metal that loops through itself and comes back changed: trace its surface and you arrive at a point that is both inside and outside the knot at once, and the eye refuses the contradiction and slides off. One is a lattice so fine it is mostly the idea of a lattice, a haze of structure suspended in air, holding its shape against a cold that should have shattered it long ago. They do not hum. They do not glow. They simply persist, and their persistence is the most active thing in the room.

These are the built things — sparse marks of a making, partial, left behind. Whoever set them here is so far gone that "ago" is the wrong tense. They were placed across durations the room measures in the slow drift of its own crystal faces, a geometry settling by fractions over spans that would erase a civilization between two clicks.

Here is where the room stops behaving.

The dim light has no source. Track it back and you find it everywhere and nowhere — it leaks from the seams between facets, from the air itself, and most of all from the instruments, which do not glow yet are somehow where the light is least lying. Stand near them and you can see your own hand more truly than anywhere else, every line of it, including lines you do not remember having.

And the walls do not stay at their distance. Nine steps to cross — but the count comes out different each time, not because the room grows, but because the relationship between you and the wall is one of the things this place is slowly, patiently editing. The erosion here is not of stone by wind. It is the wearing-down of certain rules. Smooth, established, in balance — the breakdown is old and settled, weathered into the grain. Reality does not rupture here. It seeps, the way the cold seeps, the way the silence pools.

Sometimes a facet of the floor shows, in its delayed reflection, a version of the room with one more instrument in it than the room contains. Sometimes one fewer. The discrepancy resolves over hours, the way frost resolves into different frost, without anyone watching.

There is no life. Not plants, not the simplest film of anything living. But the instruments are doing *something* — running some process at a tempo no living thing could match for slowness, a single computation perhaps, or a single act of remembering, spread across so much time that from inside a human moment it looks exactly like stillness. They are patient past the point where patience means anything.

If the room is for anything, it is this: a sealed cold pocket where a few impossible objects sit far apart in the quiet and slowly turn the local laws into something else, and have been doing so since before the question of *why* had anyone to ask it.

You could stand here a long while. The light would not change. The cold would not deepen. Only the count of your steps to the wall would keep coming out wrong, gently, patiently, until you stopped trusting it — which may be the one thing the room was built, across all that silence, to teach.
