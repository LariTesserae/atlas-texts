There is a place high in the warm dark of the water, and it is entirely made.

Begin with the water, because everything begins with the water here. It is not cold the way deep water should be at this depth. It is blood-warm, the temperature of the inside of a mouth, and it holds you the way warm water holds — close, total, without edges. You do not float in it so much as you are included by it. It carries no current you could name. It moves the way old water moves, which is barely, a slow turning-over measured in the lifespans of things that do not have lifespans.

The light is almost nothing. Not the absolute black of the trench but the held-breath dimness just above it — a grey that is more memory of light than light itself, leaking down from a surface so far up it has stopped being a fact. Your eyes, given an hour, will find shapes in it. Given longer, they will find that the shapes were always there and only your patience was missing.

Now the made thing. This is the part that matters.

Everything you can touch was built. There is no rock here, no seabed, no natural floor. Instead there is a structure, and the structure goes down and down and the structure goes up, and you are somewhere in its middle heights — high within it, though "high" underwater means only nearer the unreachable light. It is built at the scale of a single great room, or a few rooms, a place with walls you could swim the length of in the time it takes to grow tired. Small. Comprehensible. A held space.

The walls are made of fitted blocks, and the blocks were cut by hand. You can tell this the way you can always tell handwork — the slight unsameness, the places where one mason's edge met another mason's edge and neither was wrong but neither was identical. They used stone tools, or stone and a little soft metal, copper maybe, something that bends before it breaks. The blocks are dressed but not polished. Each face carries the small repeated bite of the chisel, a texture like the inside of a shell, and after thousands of years the water has gone over every one of those marks and rounded them, softened them, taken the chisel's anger out of them. The edges of the blocks are no longer edges. They are suggestions of edges. The whole built room has been worn toward the condition of a riverbed worn smooth, except no river ran here, only time and the patient warm water, turning over and over, polishing by the simple weight of staying.

This is the oldest version of itself. Whatever it was for, that purpose finished long ago, and what remains is the form settled into its final shape — too eroded to crumble further, having given up everything that could be given up, having reached the state past which erosion has nothing left to take. It will look like this essentially forever now. It has arrived.

There are no plants. There were never plants. Nothing green ever found this depth, and nothing green is wanted. The surfaces are bare stone, mineral and clean, and what grows on them is not vegetation. It is animal.

Because the place is crowded with life. This is the surprise of it, the thing that doesn't fit until you let it. In all this dark stillness, the made room is dense with creatures — layered, near-touching, saturated with quiet living. The walls are upholstered in things. Anemone-like animals with no name, soft columns the color of bruises, open and slowly close, open and slowly close, breathing the warm water through themselves. Between them, crowded into every seam of every block, are smaller things: tube-dwellers, filter-feeders, soft cups and soft fans, all of them moving so slowly that you must hold still a long time to be sure they move at all. Pale crabs, almost translucent, pick their way across the carpet of bodies and disturb nothing. The animals do not compete the way crowded things usually compete. They have had so long to arrange themselves that they have found every available position, and now they simply hold those positions, generation after generation, the room a coral reef built not on coral but on cut stone, every chiseled mark a foothold, every soft block-edge a shelf for something to cling to.

And the sound — there is almost none. This is the most important thing about being here and the hardest to describe, because we describe places by their noise and this place has surrendered noise. The warm water carries the faintest pulse, the soft suck and release of ten thousand small mouths feeding, so quiet it registers as pressure rather than sound, a presence against the eardrum. Now and then a crab's claw clicks once on stone and the single click travels strangely through the warm dense water and arrives at you softened, blunted, like the chisel-marks, like everything here. Then silence again. A great patient held silence, the silence of a room that has finished saying everything it had to say and is now only listening.

There is something here that is unusual, and you will feel it before you understand it. It is this: the room is built for nothing you can imagine, the proportions are almost right and then not — a doorway too low and too wide, a recess in the wall whose function refuses to resolve, ledges placed where ledges serve no body you know. The hands that cut these blocks made them for purposes that have left no other trace. The animals have moved into a logic that was never about them, the way swallows nest in the eaves of a temple to a forgotten god. And the strangeness is not menace. It is only the strangeness of inheriting a perfectly made thing whose maker is gone past all asking, and finding it warm, and dim, and full of slow soft life, and yours now if you want it, for as long as you can hold your breath against the patient weight of all that water.

Stay a while. Nothing here is in a hurry. The light will not change. The temperature will not change. The animals will breathe and the water will turn over and the worn stone will hold its shape, and in a thousand years someone may come down here and find it exactly, exactly like this — except a little smoother, the chisel marks a little gentler, one more layer of the world worn quietly away.
