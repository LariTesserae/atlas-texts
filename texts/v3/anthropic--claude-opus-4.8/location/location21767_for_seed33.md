There is a city in the high cold where water has forgotten how to be water.

Begin with the altitude, because everything here begins with it. You are near the top of something — not a mountain exactly, because a mountain implies a base, and this place has misplaced its base the way a word said too many times misplaces its meaning. The air is thin enough to be honest. Cool, not freezing; the temperature of a held breath before it's let go. There is no ice because there is no water to freeze, and there is no water because at some point the chemistry up here decided it had other commitments.

The ground is mineral and dry. Pale stone the color of old teeth, weathered into long settled curves — nothing sharp survives at this age. Everything has been worn into agreement with the wind, even though the wind, when you wait for it, mostly doesn't come. The erosion is real but ancient. These shapes were carved by something that finished its work an age ago and went away. What's left is a landscape in its own state, no longer becoming, simply *being*, the way a dry riverbed is still a riverbed though the river is a rumor.

And the city.

It spreads across the high plateau and down into the folds of the stone — streets, terraces, stacked structures, the clear grammar of organized construction. Walls meet at angles that were decided by someone. There are stairs. There are thresholds. There are the long low buildings of a place that expected to be lived in. But the density is almost nothing — vast hollows between the standing things, plazas you could lose a day crossing, a built world stretched so thin across so much space that the city reads less like a settlement and more like the *idea* of a settlement, drawn very large and very faint.

No one made this with tools. That's the first thing that won't sit right. Run your hand along a wall and there are no marks of cutting, no seams, no joins. The stone of the buildings is the stone of the ground, lifted into rooms without ever having been quarried. As though the city had grown — but nothing grows here, there's no biology that the word *grow* would recognize. The architecture simply *is*, fully formed and slightly impossible, the way a thing in a dream is already finished before you arrive to wonder about it.

Now the weirdness, which is the real weather of this place.

Reality here is thin, and it wears. The same erosion that softened the stone has been working on the rules. Distances do not stay agreed-upon. A doorway across a plaza is a short walk, then a long one, then it is behind you and you never passed through it. The light is dim and sourceless — there is no sun, only a general grey luminance that seems to seep up out of the pale stone, brightest in the hollows where it should be darkest. Shadows fall in directions that don't share a story. Stand still and your own shadow may consider the matter and decide differently.

It is very quiet. This is the most consistent thing about the place: the silence. Not the silence of nothing happening — a great deal is happening — but a silence that has been laid over everything like a sealing layer, so that the processes of the city go on *beneath* sound. You watch a far wall slowly rearrange itself, stones migrating into a new configuration over the course of an hour, and it makes no noise at all. Occasionally there are small dry sounds: a tick, a settling, a sound like a single grain falling, very close to your ear, with no grain to be seen. These quiet sounds are almost worse than total silence, because they imply a scale of detail too fine to be empty.

And it is active, this place, despite the emptiness — that's the second thing that won't sit right. There are cycles. The light pulses, slowly, over long periods, dimming and recovering like the breathing of something asleep. The streets reorganize. The plazas drift. Whole terraces relocate when you aren't watching, and you come to understand that *not watching* is the mechanism — that the city moves only in the unobserved interval, that attention freezes it and inattention frees it, and so the place is most alive in your peripheral vision, always settling into stillness the moment you turn to look. The dynamism is real and constant and entirely shy.

The fauna is rare and hard to credit. Now and then, in the long hollow spaces between buildings, something moves that is alive in some sense the place permits. Not animals — there is no biology here to make animals from. Call them *occupants*. They are roughly the size and bearing of a person walking, and they keep to the far middle distance, and they are made of the same dim light that fills the hollows, so you see them mostly as a thickening of the grey. They go about errands. They turn corners that aren't there. If you approach one it is always exactly as far away as when you began, having performed the same trick the doorways perform. They never make sound. They never acknowledge you, and you come slowly to suspect that you are, to them, the rare and barely-real one — a thickening in *their* grey, an occupant of their periphery, something half-seen that refuses to resolve.

There is no water and there will be no water. This is the deepest rule of the place and the one it keeps most faithfully. Everything dry, everything settled, everything finished and high and thin. The city does not thirst because thirst is a property of living things and this is a place that has gone past life — not died, exactly, but *concluded*, the way a held note concludes by simply ceasing to need to continue. Post-life. The architecture of a civilization that finished, and whose finishing was so complete that even the stone took up the habit of being done.

What's left walks the dim plazas at the edge of sight, moving when unwatched, on a high dry plateau where the rules have weathered soft, in a silence with small sounds in it, under a light with no source, in a city no hand built, that grows nowhere and is everywhere, that is most itself in the corner of your eye where you have already decided not to look.

Stay a while. Don't look directly at anything. It will tell you more that way.
