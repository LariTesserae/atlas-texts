# The Understory Works

There is no water here, and yet everything is green.

You have to hold those two facts together before the place will let you understand it. We are below the surface — not in a cave, exactly, but in a hollowing, a vast lateral chamber that the plants themselves have grown and hold open by sheer pressure of their own bodies. The roof is two hundred meters up in places, a knotted canopy of woody trusses lit from within. The floor is a different green, paler, fungal-soft. Between them: open air, dry air, air you could strike a match in if there were anything left to burn.

The plants here gave up on water a long time ago. You can see it in their architecture. They are succulent in the old desert way — fat-walled, waxen, hoarding — but they have scaled that logic up past all reason, into the size of buildings, of districts. The substrate of this world is not soil and is not stone. It is *plant*, compressed and lignified into something like a porous mineral, a green-gray foam-rock that rings hollow underfoot and grows, slowly, the way coral grows, by accretion of the dead onto the structure of the living.

## The City That Is Also a Forest

Call it a city because it has the density of one, the organization. Towers, but the towers are stems. Avenues, but the avenues are the gaps where two great trunk-walls failed to fuse and left a corridor. The plants did not build it for us. We — whoever we are, the small warm-blooded things that move through it — moved in afterward, the way mice move into a wall.

The scale is regional. From a high node you can see the chamber extend past your seeing, district after district of swollen vegetable masonry, and between the masses, real distance: emptiness, dry haze, the long quiet reach of underground space. It is sparse in the way a savanna is sparse, not the way a desert is. Things are scattered but they are *thriving*, each at its own remove, each holding its own circle of room.

The light is the strangest mercy. It is normal light — daylight, near enough — and there is no sun. The canopy makes it. Certain of the woody trusses overhead have gone luminous, photosynthesis run in reverse and inside-out, the plant spending its hoarded chemistry to *give off* a steady broad-spectrum glow the color of a clear morning. So you walk in daylight, underground, with no source you can point to. The light just is, dispersed, sourceless, falling on everything evenly. It makes the place feel like a memory of being outdoors rather than the thing itself.

## The Works

There is industry here, and this is where it gets difficult to say who is doing the building.

Inside the larger trunks, the plants have hollowed running channels and packed them with machinery that is grown, not made — turbines of fibrous vane that turn on the slow internal pressures of the substrate, ducts that move not water but a thick resinous sap-gas through the whole structure, pumping it district to district. There are *factories* in the sense that there are places where raw mineral foam goes in and finished structure comes out, extruded, set, annealed by the heat of fermentation deep in the floor. The whole hollow is a single distributed engine running on the only thing it has: the difference between what is alive and what has just died.

And there are smaller works, recognizably ours. Catwalks bolted into the soft green stone. Lamps we hung before we understood the canopy would light itself. Pipework threaded into the plant's own pipework, tapping the sap-gas, condensing from it the trace moisture that is the only drinkable thing in all this dryness — a slow drip, jealously collected, the entire human economy of the place organized around the recovery of water that the world refuses to give freely.

We learned the rhythm of the works and matched ours to it. It is not fast but it is constant — *active*, many processes overlapping. The pressure-tides in the great trunks rise and fall on a cycle of about nine hours. The luminous canopy brightens and dims on a longer cycle, a manufactured day. Things grow visibly. Come back to an avenue after a season and the corridor has narrowed; the trunk-walls have put on green; the way you knew is closing.

## Quiet

For all the machinery, it is quiet. This surprised me most.

The engines are slow and soft-bodied; they do not clatter. The sap-gas moves with a sound like a held breath, a low organ note you stop hearing after a day and only notice again when it changes. Footsteps don't echo — the foam-rock drinks sound. Voices carry strangely, close and flat. Most of the time the loudest thing is your own blood.

Into that quiet, the fauna.

## What Lives Here

The animals are abundant but they too keep their distances, and they have all solved the water problem in the same desperate way: they eat the plant for its hoarded moisture, and they are built to lose almost nothing.

The grazers are slow, large, pale things with deep-set faces, shaped something like sloths and something like beetles, that climb the trunk-walls and rasp the waxen flesh. They move at the pace of the canopy-light, half a meter a minute, and they pant a dry exhausted heat into the cool air. The cool is the other gift — the chamber holds a steady chill, and the animals are sluggish in it, conserving, never quite warm. Predators here don't chase. They *wait*, folded into the wall-textures, mineral-still, and take the grazers by ambush after hours of perfect stillness. The whole predator-prey machinery runs at a tenth of the speed it would in the sun, a slow-motion violence you can watch unfold over an afternoon, like weather.

The strangeness comes in at the seams. Some of the grazers have begun to fuse, where they feed too long in one place — the plant grows around them, over their limbs, and they don't pull free, and after a while you cannot tell whether the half-engulfed animal in the wall is dying or has become part of the substrate's slow circulation, fed by it now instead of feeding. There are things growing in the trunks that have an animal's symmetry. The categories here are not stable. Everything trends toward becoming plant in the end, because the plant is everything; it is the floor and the towers and the light and the engine, and the animals are just the parts of it that haven't been reabsorbed yet.

## Emerging

And the place is young. This is the last thing to understand, and it changes how you stand in it.

The erosion is barely begun — nothing here has been worn smooth, nothing has had time to fail and weather into a softer second shape. Every edge is raw. The foam-rock floor is still extruding in places, still warm, still settling into the form it will hold. Whole districts are *under construction* in the most literal sense: the plant is mid-gesture, a trunk-wall halfway to fused, a corridor that will be a chamber in a century, a canopy-truss not yet lit because it hasn't finished growing its luminous core. You are not living in the ruins of something. You are living inside something being made, and made slowly, and you will not see it finished, and neither will anyone you tell about it.

That is the feeling the place leaves you with, finally — standing in dry sourceless daylight, two hundred meters under wherever the surface is, in the quiet, in the cool, watching a slow green engine the size of a valley build itself out of its own dead and breathe its single low note into the dark. It does not need us. It barely notices us. We tap its pipes for water and hang our small lamps and match our hours to its tides, and it goes on becoming itself around us, unhurried, enormous, and not yet finished.

Not yet finished. That part stays with you.
