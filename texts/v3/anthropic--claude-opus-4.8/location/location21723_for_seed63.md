# The Roost Beneath

There is no sky here. There hasn't been for a long time, if there ever was.

You arrive at a threshold and feel the heat first — a dry, mineral heat that comes up through the soles of your feet before it reaches your face. The rock beneath you is warm the way a sleeping body is warm. This place is high; you can tell by the thinness behind the heat, the way each breath feels slightly emptied. But there is no view, no horizon, no descent into valley. Whatever altitude this is, it is interior. The high place folds inward.

The chamber — and it is a chamber, room-scale, hamlet-scale, you could walk its length in a few hundred paces — is a vault of dark basalt and something glassier. Volcanic glass in long vertical flutes, like the inside of a fluted column hollowed by a god who lost interest. The walls have been worked smooth not by tools but by time and by *touch*. Long-established. Settled into itself. Every surface has the rounded, handled quality of a stone in a riverbed, except there is no river and never was. Only the slow patient rubbing of bodies, generation upon generation.

Because the bodies are everywhere.

## The Density

Look up — though "up" is a courtesy here, the dark is near-total — and the ceiling is not stone. The ceiling is *furred*. It moves. It is packed, saturated, every square handsbreadth occupied by a folded living thing hanging head-down, leather-skinned, breathing in shallow synchrony. They are warm. They are the source of the heat. Tens of thousands of them, more, pressed so close that no individual can be made out, only a continuous quilted darkness that twitches and resettles, that rises and falls like a single skin over a single enormous slow lung.

This is what 2.9 fauna means. Not many animals. *One animal, multiplied past the point where the multiplication stops mattering.* The room is the colony and the colony is the room. The walls drip with their warmth. The floor is soft underfoot with the long mineral accumulation of their leavings, packed and dried and packed again until it has become a kind of stone of its own — guano-marble, pale and faintly sweet-acrid, the only pale thing in all this dark.

## On the absence of water and the absence of plants

Nothing grows here in the green sense. There is no light to grow by. What lives instead lives on what is brought in — and things *are* brought in, by the wings, from somewhere far beyond the threshold, carried back in small bodies and digested into the warm dark. The ecology here is not a pyramid with a green base. It is a long thread running outward into a night you cannot see and returning, the colony fed by an elsewhere it cannot show you.

The floor is dry. Bone dry, despite the breath-moisture of so many lungs, because the heat wicks it all away. Drink nothing here. There is nothing to drink. What pools in the low places is not water.

But there is life *in* the floor, too — the part of the ecology that never leaves. Down in the warm guano-marble, in the dark of the dark, things move that have no eyes because there has never been a use for eyes. Pale blind crawlers. Fungal threads, simpler than plants, feeding on the rain of the colony above, lacing the floor-stone with white filament so that when you press your hand down you feel a faint give, a faint *breath* even from the ground. Everything here is alive at a different speed. The ceiling lives in fast twitches. The floor lives in slow spreading. You stand between two living things at two tempos and you yourself are a third tempo, brief and warm and intruding.

## Sound

This is the strange part, the part that turns the place uncanny: it is quiet.

You would expect a din. A colony this size, this packed — you brace for shrieking, for the airport-roar of wings. Instead there is a hush. The sound is *mostly quiet*, and the quiet is full of small things. The leather creak of a hundred thousand small repositionings. The almost-subsonic pulse of synchronized breath, felt more in the chest than heard in the ear. Occasionally, far off across the vault, a single high note — a click, a chirrup, a navigation-cry pitched at the very edge of hearing — and then silence closes back over it like the surface of a pond.

They are not asleep, exactly. They are *waiting*. The whole room is a held breath between two flights. And in the held breath you become aware of the rule of this world:

## The rule

You may move through here only at the colony's permission, and the colony permits movement only when it is between hungers.

Twice in the unmeasured day the ceiling empties. You will feel it coming — the breath quickens, the leather-creak rises into a rustle, the rustle into a rushing, and then the whole quilted dark *peels off the stone at once* and pours out through openings you never saw, a river of bodies flowing up and out into the night-beyond, and for a few minutes the chamber is naked and cooling and almost unbearably empty, the walls steaming faintly, the floor-creatures stirring in the sudden lessened weight of warmth. Then they return. They always return. The dark reseals the ceiling. The heat comes back up through your feet.

If you are caught standing when they pour, you must stand very still and let the river break around you, thousands of small leather wings missing your face by the width of a breath, and you must not cry out, because a cry confuses the river, and a confused river is the only thing in this place that can hurt you.

## The scattered marks

You are not the first.

There — set into the wall at shoulder height, where some long-gone hand could reach it — a niche. Hand-cut, the only worked thing here that admits to being worked. And in the niche, small offerings turned to mineral by the heat and the years: a ring of bone, a shaped stone, a black glass blade with no handle left. Faint marks scored above it, a tally or a prayer, eroded almost smooth by the same touch that smoothed the walls. Someone tended this colony once. Or worshipped it. Or simply needed its warmth on a high cold night and left a thank-you the colony could not read.

There is no machine here, no device, no light but what you carry in your own dark-adjusted eye, which is to say almost none. You navigate the way the creatures navigate — by the shape of the warmth, by the echo of your own breath off the fluted glass, by the give of the floor.

And when you finally turn back toward the threshold, you understand the last thing about this place, the thing the parameters were circling all along:

It is not waiting for you to leave. It does not know you are here. It is wholly occupied with the enormous slow business of being many-as-one, breathing in the dark, pouring out and returning, generation laid on generation until the room itself is more colony than stone.

You were only ever weather passing through.
