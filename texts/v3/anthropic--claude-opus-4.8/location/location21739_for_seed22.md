# The Valley of the Choir Towers

Start with the sound, because the sound is the first thing and the last thing and most of what's between.

It is not one sound. It is the impossibility of a single sound — a deafening braid of frequencies that arrives through the soles of your feet as much as your ears. There is a low organ-drone you feel in your sternum, a register beneath naming. Above it, layered like silt: the wet roar of green growth straining toward dim light, the percussive drip and splash of water finding its level, the calls of ten thousand animals you cannot see, and over all of it, threading everything, a high crystalline ringing that comes from the towers.

The towers are why this valley sounds the way it does.

---

Walk down into it. The descent is gentle — this is low country, a broad shallow basin maybe thirty kilometers from rim to rim, and the land here is *young*. You can tell because the features are sharp, unsoftened. The streambeds cut clean and angular, not yet worn into curves. There are fresh fracture-faces on the rock, pale where weather hasn't touched them. The whole valley has the look of something recently set down, the packaging not long removed. Erosion has barely begun its work. The rivers — there are several, thin and bright, threading the basin floor — run in incised channels with edges you could cut your hand on.

Water is not abundant here, but it is *insistent*. It comes in pockets and pulses. The air holds a perpetual dampness, a condensation that beads on every surface and falls, when enough gathers, in sudden vertical rains that last minutes and then stop. Mist pools in the low places at dawn and burns off slow. The rivers are the valley's circulatory system — narrow, but they go everywhere, and the vegetation crowds their banks in dense ribbons while the spaces between stay drier, more open.

And it is warm. A close, greenhouse warmth, the kind that makes the moisture feel like breath.

---

The vegetation is extravagant and the vegetation is *wrong*, and it takes a while to understand why.

It's lush — fronds the size of sails, vines thick as a thigh, mosses that quilt every still surface in a hundred shades of green going toward black in the dim light. Things flower here in colors that seem too saturated for how little light reaches them. The canopy is layered, riotous, generous.

But it is also *empty*. This is the contradiction you'll spend your whole time here trying to hold: density 0.1, near-void. The lushness is real but it is *spaced*. A magnificent stand of growth, and then a hundred meters of nothing — bare swept rock, a quiet pool, a clearing that holds only air. The valley is not crowded with its own abundance. It is a few extraordinary presences separated by vast hollow intervals, like notes in very slow music. You walk for a long time between living things. The green is intense precisely because it is rare against so much open space.

This is the first clue that the valley was *arranged*.

---

The towers stand in the hollows between the green.

There are perhaps nine of them, though counting is hard because some of them are difficult to keep in your eye. They are tall — settlement-scale, urban in their organization, clearly *built* — but built by an intelligence working past the edge of what physics seems to permit. They do not look like buildings. They look like the idea of buildings expressed in a material that has not agreed to behave.

Each tower is a slender vertical structure, half-translucent, and the light passes *into* it rather than off it — you can see, dimly, internal architecture: stacked floors, spiraling conduits, lattices that fold through dimensions your eye can't follow, geometry that seems to continue inside the tower past the point where the tower's outside should have ended. They hum. This is the source of the high ringing that overlays the whole valley. Each tower sings a sustained tone, and the tones are not random — they shift, slowly, in relation to one another, forming chords that resolve and dissolve over hours. The towers are *tuning* something. Or tuning *to* something.

The deafening sound is, you eventually realize, the towers and the ecology *answering each other*.

---

Because the fauna here moves to the towers' music.

There are so many animals. Fauna 2.2 — abundant — but like the vegetation, they are spaced into the emptiness, vast intervals between presences, so that each one arrives like an event. Things that swim in the bright rivers with too many fins. Things in the canopy that aren't quite birds, that flock and wheel in patterns synchronized to the towers' chord-changes — when a tower shifts its tone, the flock turns, all at once, a thousand bodies pivoting on a frequency. Large slow grazers, the size of small hills, that move through the open ground between the green stands and emit, from somewhere in their bodies, a resonant drone that *matches* the towers' organ-bass. They are part of the chord. They are instruments the valley grew for itself.

Predator and prey here negotiate by sound. The hunt is conducted in frequencies. A predator goes silent, and the prey hears the *hole* in the music where the predator's tone should be, and runs. The whole ecosystem is a piece of music being performed continuously, and the towers conduct, and nothing in this valley is ever, for one instant, quiet.

---

Here is what I think the towers are, having watched them sing.

I think the valley is *new* — genuinely young, recently emerged or recently *made* — and the towers are not residents but *instruments of formation*. They are tuning the ecology into existence. The reason the place is so empty is that it is unfinished: the towers are still placing things, one extraordinary presence at a time, into the vast prepared hollow, and listening to how each addition changes the chord before they add the next. The strange spacing is the spacing of a composer who has not yet filled the score.

The dampness, the pulsing rains — those are the towers, too, condensing water from the warm air on a schedule the music dictates. The sharp unworn rock is rock that has not yet had time to be old. This entire valley is days, maybe hours, into its own existence, and the deafening sound is the sound of a world being assembled note by note by something patient and enormous and not remotely human.

You stand in a clearing between the green and the towers, in the dim warm air, water beading on your skin, and the chord shifts overhead, and far across the basin a flock turns and a hill-sized animal answers in the bass, and you understand that you, too, have just been *placed* — set down in the emptiness to see what note you make, and whether the valley will keep you.

Listen. It's deciding.
