There is a room inside a mountain, and the mountain is inside a machine.

Let me find the edges of it first, because the edges are where this place insists on itself.

You stand on a floor of milled steel — not poured, not cast, but *cut*, the toolmarks still fresh, spiraling outward from a center you cannot locate. The metal is cold the way deep cold is cold: it has never been warm, and it makes a promise to never be. Frost does not form here, because there is no water to give it. Whatever moisture a body might bring is taken almost gently, wicked away into the dry. Your breath does not fog. It simply stops being yours.

The space is high — you sense the height before you see it, the way you sense altitude in your inner ear, a thinning, a leaning-toward-emptiness. When your eyes climb the walls they find girders and gantries rising into a clean, even light that comes from no source you can name. The light is ordinary. That is the strangest thing about it. In a place this made, this loud, this cold, you expect the light to be lurid, to be red emergency or sodium-orange or the blue of dying screens. Instead it is the flat honest daylight of an overcast noon, falling on everything equally, casting soft true shadows, illuminating a world that has no sky.

And the sound.

The sound is the floor and the ceiling and the air. It is not loud the way a thing is loud — it is loud the way the ocean is wet. A vast machine-roar, layered: a bass tone you feel in the sternum, a midrange grinding like continents milling each other to flour, a high shriek of metal-on-metal that never resolves into a single event. There is no silence to compare it to, so after a while the sound becomes the definition of presence — to be here is to be inside this roar. You will find yourself shouting and not hearing it. You will find your thoughts arriving slowly, displaced, having to push through.

Now look closer, because the seed asks for patience.

The roar comes from everywhere and nothing moves.

This is the thing that takes time to understand. The engines are deafening but they do not turn. The gantries thrum but they hold their positions exactly. There is no smoke, no flicker, no piston-stroke, no belt running. The machine is at full violent output and perfectly, eternally still. It is not idling — idling is a kind of waiting. This is *equilibrium at maximum*, a frozen scream, a flywheel spun to such speed it appears not to move at all, except there is no flywheel, only the noise of one, only the heat that should be here and isn't.

Across the milled floor, things have grown.

Sparse. Scattered with much black distance between them — you could walk a hundred paces and find one. They are plants, or were once the idea of plants, and they have made themselves out of what was available: thin grey stalks of some crystalline growth, mineral-pale, branching in fixed fractal angles like frost that decided to keep going after the water left. They are alive in the slow patient way of lichens, drawing — what? There is no soil. There is no water. They drink the vibration. They have learned to feed on the sound, on the relentless deafening output of the still machine, and they grow toward the loudest places, fanning their grey fronds against the steel, trembling at a frequency just slightly different from their neighbors so that, if you could hear it, they would be singing — but you cannot hear anything but the roar.

There is no animal here. Nothing crawls, nothing watches. The closest thing to motion is a single grey seed-thing that drifts and never lands, held aloft on a standing wave of sound, turning slowly in a column of noise, and it has been turning since before you came and will turn after.

The whole place is *young*. This is the contradiction the cold and the stillness almost hide. The toolmarks are fresh. The steel has the bright burr of metal cut yesterday. The crystalline plants are first-generation, none of them grown old, no fallen ancestors, no detritus, no rust, no patina. Whatever made this room made it recently and made it finished. There was no slow weathering, no settling, no accumulation of years. It arrived complete and loud and cold, and then it stopped accumulating time. The freshness is preserved by the stillness — nothing erodes, because nothing happens. The sharp edges will stay sharp forever. The new will stay new forever. It is a young world that will never get older, sealed at the moment of its making like an insect in clear resin, except the resin is sound.

And it is small.

That is the last thing, and the one that changes everything once you know it. For all the height and the gantries and the milled distances, this is a pocket — a single contained feature, a room, embedded in something larger you will never see. The walls do not open onto more world. They are the edge. Beyond the furthest girder the made-ness simply stops, not at a horizon but at a boundary, the way the inside of a closed box stops. You are not in a landscape. You are *inside an object*, standing on the manufactured floor of one cold loud thing that someone built and set spinning and walked away from, leaving it to roar in perfect equilibrium with its grey singing crystals and its one drifting seed, forever, in the flat honest light, where nothing moves and nothing is ever quiet and nothing, ever again, will change.

You could stay. It would not notice. It does not have the capacity to notice. It is too busy being exactly, deafeningly itself.
