There is a crack in a mountain that does not appear on any approach. You arrive inside it without having entered. This is the first thing to understand: the place has no outside.

It is hot the way the inside of a closed fist is hot — not blasting, but enclosed, the heat of something that has nowhere to release. The walls are young basalt, glassy black where it cooled fast, still sharp at every edge because nothing has worn it down yet. You could cut yourself on the corners of this world. The rock has barely finished being born. In places it weeps — water beads out of hairline seams and runs in thin bright threads down the volcanic glass, and where the water meets the heat it does not boil so much as hesitate, hanging in the air as a slow standing mist that never quite rises and never quite falls.

The whole pocket is maybe the size of a chapel. You can touch both walls. The ceiling is high and lost in steam. And it is crowded.

Not with plants — there are almost none. A few pale tongues of something fungal lick out of the wet seams, colorless, translucent, fingernail things that may not be alive in any way you'd defend. The green world never reached this place. But the air is thick with animals.

They are everywhere and they are quiet. That is the wrongness you notice before you can name it: the density of a rookery, a reef, a hive — bodies layered on bodies, things clinging to the weeping walls and folding through the mist and stacked along the narrow floor — and almost no sound. A wet click now and then. The faint friction of something moving over glass. A breathing that might be the rock's. They do not cry out. They do not call. They move through their teeming lives in near-total silence, as if sound were a resource this pocket was never allotted.

And they do not hold still in the way that creatures should. This is where the place stops obeying. A thing the size of a cat clings to the wall, segmented, beaded with condensation — and when you look away and back, it is the same thing but it was always two things, and the two things were always one, and you cannot find the moment the count changed. They overlap without merging. A swimmer in the standing mist passes through another swimmer and both continue, mildly, as though intersection were a kind of weather. The heat-shimmer is full of half-creatures: an outline that has the posture of an animal but no body inside the outline, just hotter air arranged in the shape of intent.

The water makes more of them. You watch a thread of weeping water run down the basalt and pool, and the pool is suddenly populated — small soft things turning in it that were not delivered there, that the water simply implied. Wetness here seems to carry the rumor of life and the rumor is enough. Where it is wet, there is fauna. Where the rock is dry and sharp it is empty, mineral, recently cooled, indifferent.

There is one made thing, and it confuses everything. A hand-axe of knapped stone lies on a ledge, struck from the same young basalt, the flake-scars fresh, the edge clean. No hand has been here — nothing here has hands. But the tool exists, primitive and exact, evidence of a maker who was never present, as if the act of shaping had occurred without anyone to perform it. The creatures avoid the ledge. Or the ledge avoids them. Around the axe the air is clearer, soberer, the only place in the pocket where you can be sure of how many things you are looking at.

The light comes from no source. It is ordinary daylight, the kind that fills a room with a north window, and it fills this windowless room evenly, casting soft shadows that occasionally point the wrong way relative to each other. Steam catches it and glows. The black glass holds it like wet stone holds a sheen.

The rules, as far as they can be stated:

Counting fails near the living. Multiplicity is the resting state, and singularity has to be enforced, and only the made stone enforces it.

Water is generative and conservative at once — it bleeds endlessly from the seams and never fills the floor, never drains anywhere, never rises. It arrives and implies and is gone, and the implications swim.

Heat is the ceiling and the silence is the floor. Between them everything teems without a voice.

And the place is young — freshly cooled, edges unsoftened — yet impossibly full, as though life or its rumor had rushed in the instant the rock was cool enough to allow it, faster than time, faster than erosion, faster than the first plant could ever take root. A world that skipped a step. A world that began crowded and quiet and weeping, sealed inside a mountain that, from outside, shows no crack at all.

You will leave it the way you came: by ceasing to be inside it. There is no door to find, no path to mistake. Only the slow understanding that you are no longer in the warm wet chapel of silent multiplying things — and the suspicion, ever after, that it is still in there, all of it, breathing once for every several times it is alive.
