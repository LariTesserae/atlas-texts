There is a city here, and it has never been wet.

Begin with that, because it explains the rest. The buildings stand in dry mineral light, low and close-packed, a hamlet's worth of streets that somehow read as a city — not by size but by intention. Everything was built. Nothing weathered into being. The walls are made of something like salt and something like bone: a pale crystalline matter that grew rather than was quarried, faceted at the edges, sharp where a stair meets a wall, sharp where a lintel meets a door. Young construction. Recently emerged. You can tell because nothing has rounded yet. Run your hand along a corner and it will draw a thin clean line in your skin, and there is no blood here, only a fine bright dust that settles slowly and never washes off because nothing washes.

The temperature is cool, the cool of a cellar in autumn, the cool of stone that has never held the sun. The light is dim and comes from no visible source — it seems to seep from the crystalline walls themselves, a low internal glow, the way deep ice holds blue. Walk the streets and your shadow has no single direction. It pools faintly beneath everything, evenly, as though the whole place is lit by a sky made of paler version of its own floor.

Now the strangeness, which is the real subject.

The city is mostly empty. That is the first thing the body understands — the vast space between presences. The streets are wide for how few there are to walk them. Plazas open onto nothing. There are perhaps a hundred buildings and only a handful of *occupants*, and the occupants are not people. They are the plants.

The vegetation here is sparse but it is the living population. It grows the way a slow congregation gathers: a single stalk in the middle of a square, ash-grey and many-jointed, leaning toward a doorway. Pale fronds in the gutters that are not gutters because no water ever ran there — channels cut for some other purpose, now lined with a moss the color of old paper. The plants do not need water. They draw something else from the air, some dry chemistry, and you can sometimes catch them doing it: a frond will tighten, a faint crackle, a fold inward, and a pinch of the bright dust will lift off the nearest wall and travel to it across the still air, drawn the way iron filings move to a magnet you cannot see.

This is the dynamism. Slow, constant, everywhere. The city is not asleep — it is *busy*, in a register too quiet and too patient to call movement. Things are happening. Stalks lean toward doors over the course of an hour. Crystals at the wall-corners grow a hair longer while you watch, if you are willing to watch that long, accreting from the dust the plants did not take. The whole place is in motion the way a coral reef is in motion: an ecosystem, slow predation, slow feeding, the plants harvesting the walls and the walls regrowing from what the plants exhale. A closed loop. A sealed environment. There is no water because the cycle here is not a water cycle. It is a dust cycle, mineral breathed out and mineral breathed in, and the city is its own weather.

The fauna are rare, and shy, and wrong-footed in a way the eye keeps trying to correct. You will see one perhaps. Something that moves between the empty buildings on too many thin legs, the same pale color as everything else, so that it registers mostly as a disturbance in the dust rather than a creature — a place where the bright settling powder swirls and reforms. It feeds on the plants. The plants feed on the walls. The walls feed on the dust the feeding leaves behind. And the creature, when it has fed, sheds — and what it sheds is the dust again, the cycle closing, the city eating its own emptiness in a circle so complete that nothing is ever lost and nothing is ever wet.

And the sound.

There isn't any. This is the hardest thing to convey because silence is usually the absence of an expected noise, and here nothing is expected. The crystals grow without a sound. The plants feed without a sound. The creature moves without a sound, its many legs touching down on crystal that should ring like glass and does not, because sound needs something to travel through and the air here is thin and disinclined. When the frond folds, the crackle you think you hear is felt, not heard — a small pressure against the inner ear, a suggestion of a sound the place declines to actually make. You will find yourself holding your breath to listen and discovering there is nothing to listen for, and then discovering, worse, that your own breath made no sound either.

The rules of this world, as far as they can be stated:

Nothing is wet, and nothing wants to be. Water would dissolve the walls, drown the plants, kill the cycle. It is not absent by accident. It is excluded, the way a particular note is left out of a chord to keep the chord from resolving.

Everything is young. This city emerged recently — not built and then aged, but built and *still being built*, by the plants and the walls and the silent creature, who are not its inhabitants but its masons, growing it crystal by crystal, leaning it into shape. You have arrived during construction. You have arrived during the only time there is.

It is small. You could cross it in an afternoon if afternoons existed. Walk to its edge and the crystalline streets simply stop — not at a wall, not at a cliff, but at a line past which the dust no longer settles and the dim internal light gives out, and beyond is the dry dark that the city is slowly, silently, growing into, one leaning stalk and one lengthening corner at a time.

Stand at that edge and you will understand the emptiness was never lack. It is *room*. The city is mostly empty because it is mostly future, the way a held breath is mostly the word not yet spoken.

And it will never be wet, and it will never make a sound, and it will go on growing into the dark, cool and bright and patient, for as long as there is dust to breathe.
