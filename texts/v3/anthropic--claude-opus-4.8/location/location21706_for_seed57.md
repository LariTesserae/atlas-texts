# The Cistern Beneath the Step

There is a place under the third stair of a house that no longer stands, and the place is larger than the house was, and it is full of cool dark water.

You would not find it by digging. The stair is gone, the house is gone, the field where the house stood has been a field for two hundred years. But the cistern persists the way a held breath persists — by not being asked about. It is a pocket. It is the size of a small chapel and it has decided, quietly and without anyone's permission, not to be subject to the place above it.

The water fills it almost entirely. Two point nine of three: there is a hand's width of air at the very top, a black film of atmosphere pressed against the stone ceiling, and everything below is liquid. Cool water, the temperature of a cellar in early spring, the temperature of something that has never once been warm and never once frozen. If you put your face into the air-gap you would taste mineral and old lime and something green.

The green is the strange part. Things grow here, in the dark, lushly, against every rule that light is supposed to enforce. Pale weed unrolls from the walls in long soft ribbons, and there are fattened mosses the color of wet ash, and a kind of lily that has forgotten it ever needed sun — it blooms underwater, slowly, a flower that takes a season to open and a season to close, white as a drowned thumbnail. The walls are upholstered in this growth. There is no bare stone left to touch. Everything that could hold a living thing holds three or four of them, layered, packed, leaf over root over filament, a density like a held crowd, like a tin of something, like the inside of a thing that was never meant to be opened.

The stone itself is young. This is the contradiction the place keeps in its mouth. The cistern is centuries old and yet its walls are *fresh* — sharp-edged, recently broken, the grain of the cut rock still crisp, as if the mason's chisel had passed an hour ago. The erosion has not arrived. Time gets to the surface above and is turned away at the waterline. Down here the corners are knife-clean. The chisel-marks are legible. Run your fingers along them and they will be cold and exact and they will not have softened by even one grain of sand, and this is wrong, and the place knows it is wrong, and does not care.

It is dark. Nearly perfectly dark. The light here measures barely above nothing — a faint distributed grayness with no source, the way the inside of your closed eyes is not quite black. The pale lilies seem to hold a little of it. The weed at the far end seems to give a little of it back. There is no surface anywhere that you could call illuminated; there is only the suggestion that if you waited long enough your eyes would learn to read this gloom like braille, by patience and by touch.

Sound is small. A drip, somewhere, at long intervals — a single bead of water leaving the ceiling and falling the hand's-width of air into the body of the cistern, and the soft swallowed *tok* of it arriving. Then nothing for a long minute. Then *tok*. The intervals are not regular. They are the heartbeat of something very slow and not quite alive. Beneath the drips there is the broad hush of water against itself, a pressure-sound more felt than heard, and under that, if you stop your own breathing, a low note that might be the stone settling or might be the field two hundred years above remembering the house.

Fauna is rare. There is a blind white newt that lives in the cistern, or perhaps there has only ever been one, perhaps it is the same one, slow-metabolized, deathless by indifference. It moves along the packed walls grazing the mosses. Its eyes are skin-covered dots. It has been here longer than the absence of the house. Sometimes there is a transparent shrimp, and sometimes there is the memory of a shrimp, and at the level of weirdness this place runs at the difference is academic.

For here is the reality-breakdown, the two-point-six of three, the thing that makes the cistern not merely an underwater room but a *wrong* one:

The water does not agree on which way is down. In one corner a column of bubbles falls. In another the silt lies along the ceiling, a soft brown drift gathered where dust should not gather. The lilies near the eastern wall grow toward a sun that the western lilies grow away from, and there is no sun. A dropped pebble will sink, and then — having sunk, having reached the floor and rested — will be found, hours or weeks later, resting again on the surface of the air-gap, dry, as though it had never been wet, as though the journey downward had been a thing it tried and then declined.

And the place is older at the edges than in the middle. The newt is younger near the drains. The chisel-marks heal backward. If you stayed — and you would not be able to stay, no one can find the way in, the way in is the thing that does not exist — you would notice that your own memory of entering kept getting fresher, the entrance more recent, until you were always *just arriving*, forever in the first cool shocked second of the water closing over you, that second held and held and held like the air against the ceiling.

There are marks of building here beyond the cistern itself. Scattered, partial: a bronze hook set into the stone, green and furred, that once held a bucket-rope. A line of small niches cut for tallow lamps that were never lit in any year that left a soot-stain. A single iron ring on the floor, or what is now the ceiling, depending on the lily you ask. Somebody made this to hold water and to *keep* water and the keeping has outlived every purpose and become the only law the pocket obeys: *what comes in does not leave; what is held stays held; what is cool stays cool; what is fresh stays fresh.*

The whole thing changes, but slowly — the slow dynamic, the patient process. Over a human lifetime the eastern weed advances perhaps a finger's breadth. The water cools by a degree across a generation and warms back across the next. The single newt circumnavigates its small dark world once each long while. There is no hurry in a place that has stepped sideways out of time and into the gap beneath a stair that isn't there.

Cool, packed, lush, drowned, dark, and quietly insane — it persists. A held breath in the throat of the ground. A room the size of a chapel and the size of a thumbnail at once, embedded in nothing, full of black water that has not finished deciding which way to fall.
