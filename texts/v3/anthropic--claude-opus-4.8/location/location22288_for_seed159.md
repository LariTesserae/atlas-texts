# The Hollowing

You come down into it through a throat of warm stone, and the first thing that reaches you is not sight but pressure — the close animal pressure of breathing things, thousands of them, layered into the dark-but-not-dark of a place that has its own light.

This is below the surface. Not a cave exactly, because a cave is finished and this is not finished. The Hollowing is being made, right now, around you and probably partly by you, by the act of your passing through it.

## The Light

There is light here and it should not be possible. It comes up from the floor in some places and sideways from the walls in others, a steady ordinary daylight-strength glow with no sun behind it. The mineral the chambers are cut from — a pale glassy stuff, half basalt and half something not yet named — holds light the way wet sand holds the print of a hand. It does not generate; it *keeps*. Light that fell here long ago, or light shed by the creatures, gets caught in the stone and returns slowly, and so the place is lit evenly, shadowlessly, like an overcast noon that has been pressed flat and buried.

You can see everything. That turns out to be the difficulty.

## The Terrain

The rock is young. You feel it in the way the walls aren't sure of themselves yet — surfaces still pulling into shape, ridges half-extruded, a faint *give* underfoot as though the floor were a skin over something settling. There are no old edges here. Erosion has barely begun its long work; nothing has been worn, nothing rounded, nothing made smooth by time or water. Every contour is raw, freshly parted, the marks of its forming still on it like the seams left by a mold.

Warm. The stone gives off a body-heat warmth, and the air sits at the temperature of a held breath. Condensation gathers on the cooler overhangs and lets go in slow fat drops that fall a long way and land with sounds you learn to track. There are no rivers but there is always water somewhere — beading, dripping, pooling in the cupped places, sliding in thin sheets down the glassy walls. The damp is in everything. Your skin learns the wet of the air within minutes and you stop being able to tell where you end.

The scale opens past anything you'd expect from a hole in the ground. Walk and the chambers keep arriving — vault after vault, each the size of a valley, linked by passages wide enough to lose a town in. It is regional. It is a country down here. You will not find the far wall today.

## The Living Thing of It

And it is *full*.

Fauna at near-saturation, packed into every volume the chambers offer. This is the heart of the Hollowing and the reason it feels less like a place than like the inside of something alive.

They are not animals you'd name. The closest word is *settlers* but they don't seem to have come from anywhere — they belong to the stone the way condensation belongs to the cold. Soft-bodied things the size of dogs that move on rings of cilia and graze the light off the walls, leaving dim trails where they've fed. Hanging clusters of translucent sacs that pulse in slow shared rhythm from the ceilings, thousands strong, each catching a falling drop and passing it down the colony hand to hand. Things that swim through the warm air on membranes, things that walk the floors on too many legs, things that are mostly mouth and things that seem to be mostly listening.

There is almost no plant life to feed them — a smear of something fungal-or-simpler furs the wetter rock, and that's all the green the world allows. So they feed on the light, and on the water, and on each other, and on the stone as it forms. The density means you are never not near them. They brush you. They part around you and close again. You walk through a held breath of bodies and the warmth of the place is partly *their* warmth, and you understand suddenly that the heat in the stone might be coming from them — that the world is warm because it is so densely, overwhelmingly inhabited.

## The Sound

It is loud here. Not the loud of machinery — the loud of a marsh at dusk multiplied past any marsh, a continuous layered roar of clicks and low hums and the wet percussion of the dripping and the rasp of cilia on rock and a deep tidal *thrum* that you feel in the sternum before you hear it, the colonies pulsing all at once. Underneath everything, the stone itself ticks and groans as it forms, settling and parting and sealing. You will not have silence. The Hollowing is talking to itself constantly and you are inside the conversation.

## The Turbulence

This is the parameter that governs all the others, and it took me until now to feel it properly:

**Nothing here holds still.**

The Hollowing is in a state of continuous creation and unmaking. A chamber you walked through an hour ago has closed — the young stone flowed shut behind you, healed over like a wound. New passages part open with a long tearing sound and a gust of fresh-warm air. The colonies migrate in great slow tides, draining a vault and flooding another. The light shifts as the lit-stone is overgrown and rebared. The whole place churns at the pace of a storm, except the storm is made of geology and bodies and water all at once, and it never resolves into stillness.

You cannot map it. The map would be obsolete before you finished drawing it. You learn instead to read the *direction* of the churn, the way a sailor reads a current — where the stone is opening, where the herds are flowing, where the drip-rhythm is steady enough to mean a chamber will last the night.

## The Marks of Hands

And this is the strange, tender thing. Scattered through all that turbulence, you find *making*.

Small structures. Not many — they're swallowed too quickly by the moving stone to ever become a city. But here: a low wall of fitted glass-fragments, stacked by something with hands and intent, holding back a herd-tide from a sheltered cup of floor. There: marks scratched into a wall in rows, a tally or a script, half-grown-over already. A cluster of simple tools — a scraper, a hooked pry-bar, a vessel of fired clay holding caught water — left in a niche as though their maker meant to come back. Hand-craft. Pre-metal, or barely metal. Someone *lives* here, or lived, in the gaps of the churning, building small and quick and provisional, because anything built large gets eaten by the world before it can mean anything.

You never see them. But the marks are fresh. The water in the vessel is still clear.

## The Rule of the Place

If the Hollowing has a law, it is this: **permanence is not available, and so meaning is made small and made often.** The stone won't let you keep anything. The herds won't let you be alone. The light won't let you hide. Everything is in the middle of becoming, nothing is finished, and the only way to belong here is to build with the understanding that you are building on a tide — a wall for tonight, a mark for the wall to carry a little way before it closes, a vessel of water set down in trust that the world will not have unmade the niche before your thirst returns.

It is warm. It is wet. It is impossibly crowded and impossibly loud and it has never once held still since it began, and it began only just now, and it is beginning still, all around you, as you stand in the breathing pale light and feel the floor settle very slightly under your feet, deciding, even now, what it will become.
