# The Highlands at Vodregg

Start with the cold. Not a biting cold — a patient one, the kind that settles into a place and stays, the way water settles into low ground. The air here holds itself at a few degrees above freezing for most of the long day, and what warmth there is comes thin and slantwise from a sun that never climbs very high. The light is good, though. Clear. It reaches everything.

This is high country. You feel it in your chest before you understand it with your eyes — the altitude pulls at the breath, and the horizon sits at a strange remove, the way horizons do when you are standing more than two miles above any sea. But it is not mountainous, not in the usual way. There are no peaks. There is no drama of summits. What spreads out in every direction is a vast, gently tilted plain of raw rock and standing water, and it goes on past all reckoning. You could walk for a season and not leave it. There is weather at one edge of your sight that has nothing to do with the weather where you stand.

## The Ground That Has Not Decided

The most important thing about this place is that it is *young*. The stone here has barely begun to be itself. There are no valleys carved deep, no scree slopes, no rounded hills worn down by ten thousand years of rain. Instead the rock lies in great pale sheets and freshly broken blocks, edges still sharp, fractures still raw. It looks the way a thing looks the moment after it has been made and before anything has happened to it.

Water lies everywhere across this unfinished ground. Not rivers — rivers need time to cut their beds, and time has not been spent here yet. Instead the water pools in shallow sheets wherever the rock dips, mirror-bright, skinned with thin ice at the cold hours and clear again by midday. It seeps. It weeps from seams in the stone, beading and running in small bright threads that have not yet learned where they are going. The whole plain is wet without being drowned — marsh at its margins, shining film across the flats, and a faint constant mist where the cold air meets the standing water and cannot quite hold it.

You can hear the water if you stop walking. That is most of the sound there is: the small percussion of seep-drops, the occasional crack of settling ice, a whisper of wind across wet stone. The place is mostly quiet. Sound carries far in cold thin air, which makes the quiet more noticeable, not less — a single distant bird-call arrives clean across a mile of stillness, and then the stillness closes again behind it.

## What Grows

The vegetation is sparse and low and tough — it has to be, in this cold and on this raw ground that holds no real soil yet. Cushions of dark moss in the wet seams. Lichen in slow grey-gold maps across the stone faces. Here and there a hardy sedge stands in the shallow pools, and small fleshy plants with waxy leaves crouch in the lee of the broken blocks where the wind passes over them. Nothing tall. Nothing crowded. Wide spaces of bare rock between every living thing, so that each plant reads almost as a deliberate placement, an island in the pale expanse.

And yet the place is full of animals. This is the contradiction that makes it strange and alive: so little grows, and so much *moves*.

## The Abundance

They are out there in numbers — across the flats, along the marsh-edges, threading between the pools. Flocks of long-legged wading birds, grey and white, that step delicately through the shallow water hunting things you cannot see. Herds of low broad-bodied grazers, shaggy against the cold, that crop the moss-cushions and lichen and move on, leaving the slow vegetation to recover. Small swift things in the rock-shadows. Something that calls at intervals, far off. The water teems with life too small to name, and the wading birds know it, and the abundance feeds upward from there.

They are spread thin across the vastness — the density is low, presence balanced against emptiness — but in total there are a great many of them, and they are always doing something. Feeding, moving, fleeing, hunting. There is an ecosystem here turning over at a steady biological pace, predator and prey, season and migration, the whole machinery of life running on a landscape that geology has barely started to build. The animals arrived before the valleys did. They are living on the world's first draft.

## The City That Should Not Be Here

And then, scattered across this young raw plain, there is the work of hands. Or of something with the patience and capacity of hands.

It is a settlement at the scale of a city, though it is spread out and woven into the land rather than piled up. Structures rise from the rock at intervals — pale towers and long low halls and slender spans that cross the standing water without piers, arcs of some material that holds its own weight in ways that ordinary engineering does not allow. There is infrastructure: channels that gather the seep-water and route it, fine lines strung between the towers that hum almost below hearing, surfaces that gather the slantwise sun. It is organized. It is layered. People — or the makers, whoever they are — have built here at length and with intention.

But the technology is past anything that current physics fully accounts for. The spans do not need supports. The towers are warmer at their bases than the air should allow, and the mist gathers around them in patterns that suggest the structures are quietly doing something to the water and the cold — managing them, perhaps, holding this thin habitable margin open in a place that would otherwise be only stone and ice. The seep-channels do not merely drain; they seem to *decide*, to move water uphill where it is wanted. Light pools in places where no source is visible.

This is the answer to the riddle of the abundance, maybe. The young land cannot yet support so much life on its own. Something is helping. The city is a kind of caretaking infrastructure laid across a world still under construction — feeding the seep, tempering the cold in patches, holding the conditions steady while the slow work of erosion and soil and time gets underway. The makers are tending a planet through its infancy, and the animals are the proof that the tending works.

## The Whole of It

Stand still long enough and the place resolves into its layers. Beneath you: raw stone, sharp-edged, just born, weeping water from every seam. Around you: the wide cold shining flats, sparse moss and lichen, mist rising. Across the distance: herds and flocks moving thin and constant over the vastness, a system in motion. Rising from it: the pale impossible towers, quietly warm, quietly working, the city that came to a place before the place was finished and stayed to help it grow.

The cold holds. The water seeps. Somewhere a bird calls once across a mile of stillness, and the quiet closes behind it, and the young world goes on slowly becoming itself.
