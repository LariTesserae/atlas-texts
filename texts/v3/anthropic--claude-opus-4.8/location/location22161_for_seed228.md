# The Sump-Cathedral

You come down into it the way water comes down into it: by finding the lowest place and giving up the climb.

The first thing is the light, which is wrong for a place this deep. It comes from below and from the walls, not from any sun. The rock here weeps — that is the only honest word for it — and where the water beads and runs along the established channels, it carries a brightness in it, a dissolved luminance, so that the seeping films on every surface glow a pale gold-white. The walls sweat light. The floor pools it. Look down into a standing puddle and it shines back up at you brighter than you brought, until you understand the water itself is the lamp, and there is so much water that the whole chamber is lit to noon.

The scale is intimate and total at once. It is one room. You could walk its long axis in a few minutes — a grove of stone, a flooded hall maybe forty strides across, with a ceiling lost not in distance but in the steam that rises off the warm water and hangs there churning. Warmth comes up through the soles of the feet. The water is blood-temperature, mineral-thick, and where it pools deep it breathes a haze that the underlight turns to a moving gold fog overhead. So the ceiling is a sky of vapor, lit from within, never still.

It is loud. This is the thing that surprises, this far down, in a space this small. The water roars. Not from any single fall but from a hundred small ones — every weeping seam, every overflowing ledge, every drip that has been dripping long enough to carve its own throat in the stone. The chamber is profoundly *eroded*, settled into its own long shape; the rock has been rounded and runneled and flutestopped until it is all organ-pipes and fonts and smooth-lipped basins, and the water plays it. The sound stacks on itself, fold on fold, until it isn't a sound you hear so much as a medium you stand inside. You have to lean close and shout, and even then.

## The Sparse Living

Little grows here. Vegetation clings in scattered patches — that is all there is room for between the running water and the bare bright stone. Pale weed in the warm pools, threadlike, leaning all one way in the current. A few cushions of something mosslike on the high dry ledges, dark against the glow, soaking the lamp-water up their stems so that they too faintly shine at the tips. Nothing crowds. Everything keeps its distance, presence and emptiness in balance, a stone here and a green tuft three strides off and then nothing, nothing, just lit wet rock.

Fauna is rarer still. Once in a long while something moves in the deep pools — a slow pale shape, blind, the size of a forearm, that rolls over and shows a belly full of the same light it has been drinking, then sinks again. They are not afraid of you. There is no evidence anything here has ever needed to be afraid. They graze the glowing weed and pass the light back into the dark of their own bodies and carry on. You might wait an hour to see another.

## The Made Things

And then, scattered through it — the marks of *making*, and these are the strangest part.

Set into the walls at intervals, half-drowned, worn nearly smooth by the same erosion that shaped the stone, are the remains of devices. Not machines as you know them. They have no parts that move that you can see, no seams. They are the source — you realize this slowly — they are why the water shines. Each one is a node, a fist-sized intrusion of something that is neither metal nor glass nor stone, set deep and grown-over, and from each one the brightness leaches into the water table and travels everywhere the water travels. They are doing something to the water at a scale below seeing — refolding it, charging it, teaching the molecules to hold light the way a battery holds charge. Whoever set them here understood physics in a direction your physics doesn't point. They have been here long enough to be *weathered*, and they are not weathering as fast as the rock. They will outlast the room.

There are only a few. Seven, eight, scattered with the same indifferent spacing as the weed-tufts, as though placed by something that wanted only enough and never more. No machinery surrounds them. No housing, no console, no instruction. Just the bare seeded lights, doing their patient work in the dark below the dark, and around them the small chamber roaring with the consequence.

## The Turbulence

Nothing here holds still. That is the last and largest fact.

The water is in constant violent change — not flowing through and away, but *churning in place*, because the warmth drives it up and the cool of the high steam drops it back, and the chamber boils slowly with its own convection. New channels open while you watch. A pool you crossed drains in minutes through a seam the running water has just finished opening, and refills from somewhere else, and the weed that grew in it lies stranded and then floats free. The light shifts with the water, so the whole golden room flexes and surges, brightness sliding across the walls like weather. The fog overhead tears and reknits. The roar rises and falls in tidal heaves.

It is a place perpetually destroying and rebuilding itself within a form that never finally changes. The erosion is *established* — the room has been this room for an unfathomable time — and yet not one second of it repeats. Settled chaos. A standing storm. The seeded lights below the floor feed it patiently, indifferently, century on century, and the small bright weeping hall churns on above them, loud and warm and never, ever, still.

You can stay as long as you like. The blind pale things will surface and sink. The water will keep its own counsel. And the light will keep coming up out of the deep stone like something that was put here on purpose, long ago, by someone who did not stay to explain.
