Begin high. Not on a peak — there are no peaks yet — but high in the sense that the air is thin and the ground is unfinished.

This is a place that has not yet decided to be rock. The mountain (call it that, provisionally) rises to an elevation that should kill you, and yet there is no water anywhere in it, none, not in cloud or frost or vein or breath. The dryness is total and ancient. It is the dryness of something that was made without water in mind, the way a thing forged in fire forgets the sea.

And the slopes are lush. This is the first contradiction the place hands you, and it hands it without apology.

The vegetation grows out of dry mineral. Pale green things — not quite ferns, not quite anything — fan out of the raw faces of the slope where the rock is still becoming rock. They draw nothing from moisture because there is none; they seem to drink the cold instead, the way a normal plant drinks light. Where the air is coolest, they are thickest. They breathe slow. You can watch a frond unfurl across an hour and not be sure it moved. The whole ecology runs on a clock that has no hurry in it.

But they are scattered. For all their lushness, there is enormous emptiness between one stand of green and the next — bare slope, raw face, a long grey nothing — and then suddenly a riot of growth clinging to a ledge, and then nothing again. The place is mostly space. You could walk a long time between living things, and the walking would be the loud part.

---

Now the sound.

It is deafening. This is the thing you will not be prepared for, because the place looks like it should be silent — thin air, no animals, slow plants, dry stone. Silence is what the eye promises. The ear gets the opposite.

The mountain is screaming. Not weather, not wind exactly — the rock itself, in the act of forming, makes a sound. The erosion here runs backwards or sideways or not at all; the stone is *emerging*, crystallizing up out of some underprocess, and the formation of it is enormous and roaring, a grinding choral pressure that comes from everywhere and has no source you can point to. It is the sound a continent would make if you could hear it being born and the recording were played at the wrong speed. It does not stop. It does not pulse. It simply *is*, at a volume that has weight, that you feel in the sternum and the teeth.

The plants stand in it untroubled. Perhaps the sound is what they eat.

---

Here is where the place stops obeying.

At weirdness this complete, the rules don't bend — they fail. The slope you are climbing is not reliably below you. Distance does not resolve to a number; a stand of green a hundred paces off takes either three steps or three hours to reach, and which one is not knowable until it has happened. The deafening sound and the thin cool air and the lush dry green do not feel like one place experienced at once — they feel like three places overlaid, slightly out of register, and you are walking through the seam where they almost match.

Things up here are still deciding what they are. A boulder, looked at directly, is a boulder. Looked at from the edge of vision, it is the *idea* of a boulder, a region of space that intends to harden into stone and hasn't committed. The whole mountain is at this stage — emerging, embryonic, raw — and so reality itself, here, is also under construction. The breakdown is not damage. It is incompleteness. You are standing somewhere before the world finished setting, and you can feel the cement still wet in the logic of the place even though there is no water in it anywhere.

---

The light is dim and comes from the wrong direction — upward, faintly, as if the unfinished ground glows colder than the sky. It is the grey of very early morning that never proceeds to morning. The slowness of the place means this light has perhaps always been this light and will be for an age.

There are built things. Scattered, small, primitive — and this is the gentlest mystery the mountain holds. Low walls of dry-stacked stone. A hand-tool of worked bone or worked something, left on a ledge. A single structure, roofless, the size of a shepherd's hut, its stones fitted by hands that knew no metal. They are old in the way the rest of the place refuses to be old — they have actual age on them, actual wear, the only erosion in a world that has none. Someone was here before the place was finished. Someone built in the wet cement of reality and then left, and the walls held the shape the way a footprint holds in clay that later turns to stone.

No bones. No bodies. No fauna at all, now or ever — the builders were the only animal thing this place has known, and they are gone, and they took the warmth with them, and the green grew up afterward in the cold and the roar and inherited the huts and did not know what they were for.

---

Stand in the roofless hut. The screaming of the forming rock comes through the open top. The pale green things lean in over the dry-stacked wall, breathing the cold at their own slow pace. The light comes up from the unmade floor. The next presence — green or stone or wall — is far off across a stretch of nothing, and may be three steps away, and you will not know until you go.

This is a place at the beginning of itself, loud with its own becoming, lush without water, cool and high and almost real, holding one small human-made silence in its open hand and not yet knowing whether to keep it.

Take your time. So is the mountain.
