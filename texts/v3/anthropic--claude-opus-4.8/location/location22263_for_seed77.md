# The Founded Plain

It begins, as these things do, before there is anyone to begin for.

The first thing to understand is the scale. You could walk for the length of a life and never find the edge of it, and the place knows this about itself — it has the heavy, unhurried confidence of something continental. Weather is born here and dies here without ever leaving. A cloud assembles over the eastern reaches in the cool before dawn, crosses two hundred miles by noon, and burns off in the dry interior, and none of this is an event. It is just the breathing of a large body.

And it is hot. Not the sharp hot of a desert noon but a deep, settled heat that has soaked into everything and stays. The heat is in the materials. Lay your palm flat against the ground and it gives back warmth like a thing that was recently alive, or is about to be.

The ground.

Here is the strange part, the part you must hold two ways at once. Everything here is made. There is no soil, no bedrock, no natural substrate anywhere — turn over a stone and beneath it is more made-thing, and beneath that, more. The plain is constructed all the way down, brick and slab and poured course and fitted block, a masonry that descends past any depth you could dig to. Someone laid all of this. Some intention paved a continent.

But — and this is what stops the breath — it is not finished. It is barely begun.

The construction is *embryonic*. Everywhere you look the made-world is in its rawest state: foundations poured but not yet set true, courses of brick laid two high and then abandoned mid-row, the next brick resting beside the gap where it will go. Channels cut for water that does not yet run. Whole districts of low walls knee-high, marking out rooms that have no floors and will have no roofs for an age. The materials are fresh — the mortar still pale, the cut faces of the blocks still sharp, unworn. Nothing has weathered. There has not been *time* for weathering. The plain is the first morning of a city the size of a world, caught in the instant after the laying-out and before the building-up.

So you walk across a made thing that has not yet learned to be old.

The space between things is enormous. This is not a dense ruin or a teeming site — it is sparse, scattered, balanced against its own emptiness. A founded wall here, and then a long flat reach of fitted paving, and then nothing made above ankle-height for a mile, just the warm tessellated ground stretching toward a horizon that shimmers with heat. Then another presence: a single column-base, broad as a threshing floor, with no column on it. A stair of seven steps that climbs to a landing and stops at open air. The makers, whoever they are, work everywhere and nowhere, and they have left the world room to be itself between their works.

There is water, but it is intermittent, pocketed. The construction includes water in its design — you can see the intent in the cut basins and the sloped channels and the broad shallow tanks fitted into the paving. But the water comes and goes. In the cool hours condensation beads on the north faces of the stone and runs in thin threads into the channels, gathering in the basins by morning into still bright sheets you could see your face in. Then the heat climbs and drinks it back. Sometimes — once in a great while, you sense, by the way the channels are cut deeper than any dew could fill — real rain comes, walking across the plain as a grey wall, and for a few hours the whole made-world runs and gleams and the dry tanks brim and overflow their fitted lips. Then it passes, and the slow drying begins again, and a faint salt-pale rime is left at the high-water marks: the first record the plain keeps of anything that happens to it.

Life is sparse and quiet and small. No animals — nothing moves on its own legs, nothing calls. But in the damp seams where the mortar meets the stone, where condensation lingers longest, there is vegetation: low cushions of something between moss and lichen, grey-green and tough, and in the deeper shaded joints a few succulent rosettes holding water in fat leaves, and at the edges of the morning basins a fringe of pale reed-like growth that has found purchase in the wet cracks. It is the first colonization. The plant life does not yet know it is supposed to spread; it sits in its pockets, patient, scattered as everything else is scattered, taking the heat and the brief water and asking for nothing more.

And it is *quiet*. This is what stays with you. A made world this vast you expect to roar — but there are no machines, no devices, no tools left lying, not the smallest mechanism anywhere. Whatever builds here builds by no means you can find. The silence is enormous and clean. You hear the small things only because there is nothing to cover them: the tick of stone expanding in the climbing heat, the single drip of a condensation-thread falling into a basin, the dry rustle when a breath of wind moves through the reed-fringe, the soft settling sigh of fresh mortar curing somewhere out of sight. Once, distant, the long low collapse-and-resettle of a poured course finding its level — not a ruin falling, but a new thing finding its true seat. Sounds of a place being made, slowly, in near-total quiet.

The light is ordinary and honest — a clear strong daylight that lays sharp shadows off every founded wall and every laid course and makes the basins flash. Nothing strange in it. The strangeness is all in the situation, not the seeing: an everyday light falling on an impossible morning.

For all its stillness the place is intensely *active*, and you feel this the longer you stay. So many processes running at once, all slow, all overlapping. The weather wheeling through its private cycles. The water gathering and burning off and gathering. The plants creeping joint by joint along the seams. The construction advancing — you never catch it moving, but come back to a wall and it is one course higher; come back to a cut channel and the next is begun. The whole continent is a thing in motion, a body mid-breath, an ecosystem of making and moistening and growing that has only just started its work and has all the time in the world to finish.

You are standing at the beginning of something. The plain is poured but not set, founded but not built, watered but not yet green, made but not yet aged. It is a world holding its first morning, taking its time, becoming more itself with every quiet hour — and in no hurry at all to tell you what it will be.
