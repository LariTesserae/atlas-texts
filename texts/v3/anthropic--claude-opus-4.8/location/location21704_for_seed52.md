# The Settling Ground

Begin with the air, because the air is where the strangeness lives.

It is warm here, the kind of warm that has no source you can point to — not the sun, which sits low and dim behind a permanent veil of high cloud, more pewter than grey. The warmth comes up through the soles of your feet, out of the ground itself, and it carries moisture with it. Not rain, mostly. Just a damp exhalation, a breathing. Every few hours the cloud-ceiling lowers and lets down a brief, fine rain that beads on every surface and then is gone, leaving the city glittering and the smell of wet metal hanging in the streets.

It is a city, what you've come into. But it is a young city, and I mean that almost geologically.

---

The land it stands on is fresh. You can see it in the rock — basalt-dark, sharp-edged, unsoftened by time, ridges and outcrops standing up at angles that older ground would have long since rounded down. The valley floor is wide, open, running off toward a horizon you can't quite resolve in the dimness, and the city is scattered across it loosely, with great gaps of green between the built things. This is not a place that has filled in. It is a place still arriving.

Because the buildings are not built so much as *grown*, or *settled* — and this is where I have to slow down, because I'm not sure the words exist.

---

Walk a little. The first structure you reach stands alone in a meadow of broad wet leaves. It is perhaps the size of a cathedral, and it has the look of something poured and then frozen mid-pour: smooth flanks, a translucence in its upper reaches where the dim light passes through and comes out the color of weak tea. Put your hand against it. It is faintly warm, like the ground. It hums — so low you feel it in your jaw rather than hear it.

The structure is doing something. There are no engines, no visible mechanisms, but matter is moving inside it, slowly, the way sap moves in a tree. Over the course of an hour, if you stood and watched, you would see one of its buttresses *thicken*. The building is repairing the sharpness of the land around it, or perhaps disagreeing with it — drawing material up from below, depositing it in the precise lattices it prefers, and the freshness of the rock and the patient self-assembly of the architecture are two halves of the same process. The land is young because the city is making it. The making is not finished.

This is the tech, at its rated three-of-three: not machines but *tendencies*, engineering written so deep into the matter that it behaves like a law of nature you've never met. Things here want to organize. Dropped stones drift, over days, into cairns. Spilled water finds channels that were not there before. The city is a wish the ground is granting, very slowly, with no one apparently doing the wishing.

---

Now the sound. Mostly there isn't any.

This is the surprise of the place — that something so active should be so quiet. The structures hum below hearing. The rain whispers and stops. Far off, a sound like a struck bell that has been stretched out over thirty seconds — one of the larger growths reaching some threshold, completing some interior fold. You hear maybe three or four such tones in an hour, from different distances, and between them the silence is total enough that you become aware of your own pulse, the wet click of your own swallowing.

Footsteps on the ground make almost no sound. The surface is faintly resilient, a skin over the warmth.

---

The green is everywhere the buildings aren't, and it's genuinely lush — but it is a strange lushness, sparse in its placement, generous in its individual extravagance. Plants here grow in isolated profusions: a single fern-thing the size of a house, unfurling fronds the deep blue-green of plants that have learned to live in dim light, and then forty meters of bare wet rock, and then another lone explosion of growth. They are not crowded. They have agreed to spacing, as the buildings have. Everything in this valley keeps its distance from everything else, and the distances are filled with mist and warmth and the patient labor of self-assembling stone.

The leaves are broad — built to catch what little light there is — and slick with condensation, and they drip steadily into pools that form in the rock-hollows. These pools are the water of the place: not rivers, not rain so much, but accumulations. Cupfuls and basinfuls caught in young stone. You could drink from them. They taste of mineral and faint electricity.

---

The fauna are rare, and shy, and odd.

I have seen — describing this place, I keep seeing it — a creature like a long pale grazing animal that moves between the plant-profusions on too many legs, browsing slowly, and that seems to be partly made of the same translucent material as the buildings. When it stands still it is almost invisible, just a faint vertical seam in the light. There are others: small darting things in the pools that may be animals or may be the water organizing itself; a high slow shape that crosses the cloud-ceiling now and then and is never closer than the horizon.

The ecology and the architecture are not separate systems here. That is the thing I keep arriving at as I write. The grazing animal eats the lush plants, and what it leaves behind it deposits as the smooth warm material the buildings are made of. It is excreting *city*. The buildings grow from the digested green. And the green grows from the moisture the buildings exhale and the warmth they pump up from below. Predator and prey, weather and stone, growth and decay — all of it folded into one slow loop, turning at the pace of sap and stone, beneath a dim sky, in near silence.

---

Stay until the cloud lowers again. The rain comes, fine and brief. The whole valley beads and shines — every leaf, every flank of every growing tower, every cairn and pool catching what light there is and throwing it back soft. For a moment the place looks finished, complete, a settled city in a settled land.

It isn't. By the time the rain stops, somewhere out in the open green, a new seam of building has begun to rise out of the fresh ground, and a long pale animal is standing over it, patiently, making the world.
