There is a basin near the top of a mountain that was made yesterday — not a figure of speech. The rock is young in the way a new tooth is young, pale and unworn, its edges still sharp enough to draw a line of blood from your palm if you steady yourself against them. Nothing here has had time to round off. The stone remembers being liquid; in places it still seems to wince.

Water is everywhere and nowhere it should be. It does not run downhill so much as hang. The whole basin breathes a cool fog that condenses on every surface and beads there and does not fall — droplets the size of grapes clinging to undersides of leaves, to the sharp lips of rock, to the air itself, hovering at chest height in slow constellations you can walk your hand through. The marsh at the basin's floor is real water, black and clear at once, and ankle-deep, and it does not move. No current. No ripple even when you wade. You leave a wake that stays behind you, a corridor of disturbed water holding its shape for hours like a scar that won't heal because nothing here heals or changes or decays.

That is the first rule of this place: nothing settles, because nothing moves on. It is fixed. Frozen at a temperature just above the point where breath shows. Equilibrium so total it feels less like stillness and more like a held note — the kind that goes on so long you stop believing the singer will ever need air.

And it is *loud.* This is the contradiction that takes longest to accept. A place this still should be silent, and instead it roars. The sound has no visible source. It is the sound of machinery — vast, industrial, the churn of unseen engines, the clang and shudder of presses, the steady electrical hum of something enormous kept running. It comes up through the soles of your feet from the young rock, and it comes down from the fog, and it fills the basin to its brim. There are no factories. There is no built thing here at all — no wall, no road, no rail, not so much as a cairn. Only the noise of industry without industry, production without product, the audio of a world that was promised machines and got mountains instead. The leaves vibrate with it. The hanging droplets shiver and re-form, shiver and re-form, never falling.

The vegetation is lush and cold-loving and sparse — not a jungle, but a scattering. A tree here, then forty paces of open black water, then a stand of something fern-like, then nothing again. The plants are wet-green going almost blue in the dim light. Their growth has stopped mid-gesture: a frond half-unfurled, a flower caught in the act of opening, a vine reaching across a gap it will never finish crossing. They are alive. They simply do not proceed. Press your thumb into the soft new bark and the dent stays, a thumbprint that will outlast you.

The light is dim and even and sourceless, the gray of a long dawn that has decided not to become morning. You cannot find the sun. The fog might be hiding it, or there might not be one. Shadows are weak and indecisive, pointing in slightly wrong directions, as if several suns had argued and compromised.

But the fauna — the fauna is *abundant,* and this is where the place stops merely being strange and starts coming apart. There are animals everywhere, dense in the way the plants are sparse, filling the spaces the vegetation leaves. Things like herons that stand in the motionless marsh on legs too many or too few. Things like deer that are made partly of the engine-sound, their flanks rippling with the rhythm of the unseen presses. Small bright creatures pour over the rocks in numbers, and they are warm — you can feel the heat of them from a distance, the only warm thing in all this cool. They move. They are the only things in the whole basin that move freely, and they move *constantly,* in a place that has otherwise forbidden change, which makes them seem less like residents than like a wound, like the one place where the frozen world is still bleeding time.

Watch one long enough and you'll see what's wrong. The animals don't quite stay themselves. A bird folds its wing and the wing is briefly a hinge, an actual oiled mechanical hinge, before it is a wing again. A creature drinks from the marsh and where its tongue touches the surface the water is, for a moment, mercury, and then water again. They are leaking the missing world. The industry that isn't here, the machines the parameters promised and the stone refused — all of it has nowhere to live except inside the animals, and it surfaces in them like fish under ice, flickering up and dropping away. The roar is them. Each beast is a small factory dreaming it is a beast.

The rules, then, as best I can lay them out:

Distance does not behave. The basin is small — you could cross it in an hour, room-scale, hamlet-scale, a place with edges you can see — and yet it does not get smaller as you cross it. The far rock wall stays exactly as far away no matter how long you walk toward it, until without transition you are simply touching it.

Nothing you do persists in the world's memory except as a mark that will never fade. Footprints in the marsh stay forever. Bent fronds stay bent. The basin records but does not heal, the way young stone records the wince of cooling.

And the animals must keep moving. If one stops — and they almost never do — the engine-sound around it swells to a scream, and the creature begins to set, to take on the stillness of everything else, its warm flank going stone-cool, a hinge surfacing in it and staying surfaced, a thumbprint of machinery pressed permanently into living flesh. Then it bolts, and is a deer again, and the roar drops back to its endless even churn.

This is a place caught at the instant a mountain was supposed to become a city and chose not to. The blueprint is still here, audible, humming, insisting. The animals carry it. The water holds its breath. And the whole cold lush basin hangs in its single unchanging moment, loud as a foundry, still as a held photograph, sharp-edged and weeping and brand new, waiting at the top of the mountain for a tomorrow it has been built, very deliberately, never to reach.
