# The Loud Quiet

There is no water here, and yet everything grows.

You find it the way you find a held breath — by accident, by going somewhere narrow. A seam in the rock that turns out to be a doorway, then a corridor, then a chamber that should not be larger than your two hands but somehow contains a town. This is a pocket place. It does not connect to anywhere. You arrive by mistake and leave the same way, and afterward you are never quite sure which crack you came through.

Step in.

## The Terrain

The floor is a single piece of stone, dry as a struck match, the color of old ivory pressing toward amber where the light hits hardest. It has been worn smooth — not by water, there is no water — but by *sound*. The vibration has rounded every edge over a span of time the place does not bother to measure. Run your hand along a wall and it is glassy, almost warm, the way a bell is warm after it has been rung for a long while.

Low hills of mineral rise and fall across the floor, gentle, established, settled into their final shapes long ago. Nothing is sharp. Nothing is new. Everything has the calm posture of a thing that finished becoming itself and is now simply *being* itself, indefinitely.

## The Growing

And out of this bone-dry stone — the lushness. This is the first contradiction the place asks you to hold.

The vegetation does not need water. It feeds on the sound. Great fronds and ribbon-leaves unfurl from cracks in the floor, fat and waxy, a deep saturated green that seems lit from within. They drink the vibration. When the noise swells, they swell with it, visibly fattening, leaves trembling and turning to face the loudest direction the way sunflowers face the sun. Vines drape between the buildings, thick as a wrist, blooming flowers shaped like the bells of horns, like cupped ears, like throats.

The plants are everywhere the eye lands and yet the place is *empty*. This is the second contradiction. There is so much space between things. A frond, then a vast pale stretch of nothing, then a single tree of cabled green stems rising forty feet into the bright air, then nothing again for a long while. The lushness is real but it is scattered across a hollow that swallows it. You could walk for a long time between any two living things. The emptiness is the dominant note. The lushness only decorates it.

## The Built Thing

Now the strange part, the part that makes no sense until you stop asking it to.

A town fills this chamber. Layered, organized, dense with construction — towers stacked on towers, stairways spiraling up the curved walls of the pocket, bridges arcing across the hollow, doorways and windows and balconies and arcades, all of it built of the same warm ivory stone, hand-cut, hand-fitted, the marks of simple tools still legible in every block. No metal beyond the soft kinds — copper gone green, a little bronze. Ropes of plant-fiber. Pegs and joints and counterweights. This is a city made by patient hands with patient tools across an unhurried age.

But the city is *empty*. The third contradiction, and the deepest one.

No one lives here. No one has lived here for a duration the stones have forgotten. The doors stand open. The bridges hold no traffic. The balconies look out over the vast hollow at nothing. The town is enormous and intricate and *finished* — every detail attended to, every stair swept smooth — and there is no one inside it, and there has not been for so long that the absence has become a kind of presence. The emptiness is what the city is *for*, now. It is a town built to be hollow. A settlement-scale silence, except—

## The Sound

—except it is the loudest place you will ever stand.

The deafening is not noise. It is not chaos. It is a single sustained chord, immense, sourceless, pouring out of the stone and the air and the open doorways all at once. It comes up through the floor into your feet. It comes off the walls. The whole pocket *rings*, the way the inside of a struck bell rings, and you are standing inside the bell while it is being struck, forever.

This is what the city was built to do. The towers are not towers — they are pipes. The stairwells are flues. The bridges are strings stretched between resonators. The whole settlement is one instrument, hand-tuned across centuries, and the people who built it built it so that it would go on sounding after they were gone. They tuned the empty rooms to hold the chord. They left, and the chord stayed. It feeds the plants. The plants thicken the chord. The chord rounds the stone. The stone holds the chord. It has reached its balance — established, weathered, in its own state — a self-sustaining note that needs no players.

You cannot hear yourself think in here. That is the point. The town was made to drown thought, and it succeeds, gloriously, completely. The sound is so total that it loops back around into a kind of stillness. After a moment you stop fighting it and the chord becomes the only thing, and you become very calm, the way the stone is calm.

## The Living Things

Fauna are rare. You will see, perhaps, one creature the whole time you are here.

It will be a thing with too-large ears and a small soft body, the color of the stone, moving slowly along a high balcony, grazing the sound-fed flowers. It is not afraid of you because it has no concept of you. It lives entirely inside the chord. When it opens its mouth it does not make a noise — the place would not allow a new noise, there is no room — instead a small dark *quiet* comes out, a held bubble of silence, and the creature breathes it and is nourished by it, because in a world this loud, silence is the rare thing, the thing you must hunt for, the thing that feeds you.

There may be more of them. In all this vast hollow, who could say. You will likely see only the one.

## The Light

Bright, everywhere, sourceless like the sound. The ivory stone glows. The waxy leaves glow. There is no sky in the pocket and no sun, but the chamber is filled with a high clean daylight that casts soft double shadows, as though lit from two suns that are not there. Things gleam. The bronze gleams. The smoothed edges gleam. Nothing hides.

## The Rules

In this place: sound is water, and silence is food, and emptiness is the architecture, and the makers are gone but their making continues without them, perfectly, forever, needing nothing from anyone.

You are a guest in a finished thing. You change nothing by being here. The chord does not adjust for you. The flowers do not turn toward your noise — you make no noise it could hear over its own. You walk the swept stairs of the empty town through the deafening calm, and you understand, slowly, that this is what completion looks like: a place that has become entirely itself and requires no witness, ringing in the bright dry dark of its own little pocket of the world, and would go on ringing exactly the same if you had never found the crack at all.

Then you find the crack again, and you leave, and the chord closes behind you like water that was never there.
