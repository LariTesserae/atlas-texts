# The Glasswort Concordance

There is no water here, and there has never been. This is the first thing you must understand, because everything else grows in defiance of it.

The plain extends in every direction, low and wide, a basin without a sea. The ground is dry mineral — pale crusts of borate and soda, the kind of salt that does not taste of ocean because no ocean ever touched it. It crunches underfoot like spun sugar gone hard. And out of this dryness rises a forest.

It should not be possible, and the plants know this. They are lush past the point of decency, layered three and four canopies deep, fronds the color of old jade and arterial red, succulent paddles swollen with something that is not water. When you break one — and you may, it does not seem to mind — what wells up is clear and faintly luminous and does not wet your fingers. It evaporates upward, against gravity, into thin glowing threads that climb a few inches and then simply stop existing. The plants drink light directly and metabolize the dry air's nitrogen, and what they have no name for, they invent. They are very inventive.

## The terrain

The land is young. You can feel it. The mineral crusts are sharp-edged, freshly precipitated, none of them yet worn into the soft humility of old stone. Ridges stand at clean angles. Where the salt-pan buckles, it buckles in crisp polygons, the geometry of something that formed recently and has not yet had time to be argued with by weather. There is, in fact, no weather. The air holds at a warm and even temperature — the warmth of a body, of a held breath — and it does not move unless something moves it.

Nothing erodes because nothing flows. This is a place arrested at the moment just after its making.

## The rules

Here is where the place declares its true nature.

The forest obeys physics the way a fluent liar obeys grammar — perfectly, and toward ends the grammar never intended. The technology of this world is not built; there are no structures, no machines, no tools left lying. Instead the engineering is woven *into* the growth. The plants are the technology. Somewhere in their making, an intelligence — or a process indistinguishable from one — encoded into chlorophyll-analogues a command of matter that reaches below the scale of atoms.

You notice it first in the shadows. They are slightly wrong. A frond casts a shadow that is darker than the light should allow, and inside that shadow the salt-crust is *colder* and *older* — eroded, pitted, weathered by centuries that did not happen anywhere else. Step out of the shadow and the years vanish from your skin. The plants are running local edits to the rules. Each canopy maintains its own small dialect of reality, and where two canopies overlap, the dialects negotiate.

In those negotiation-zones, things double. A single red paddle-leaf is also, simultaneously and without contradiction, seven red paddle-leaves, and you cannot say which is the original because the question has stopped being meaningful. Reach for it and your hand arrives before you extend it. The cause politely follows the effect a half-second behind, like a servant carrying a coat.

## The atmosphere

It is quiet here. Mostly. The quiet is the quiet of a vast indoor space, padded, attentive. When sound does occur it is small and precise: a tick, a settling, the faint glassy chime of a luminous thread reaching its height and ceasing to be. Occasionally — rarely — there is a sound with no source, a single low tone that seems to come from the direction you are not looking. It is not threatening. It is the world clearing its throat.

The light is ordinary. This is the strangest mercy of the place. Whatever sun or sourceless luminance fills the sky, it falls clean and warm and *normal*, the light of an afternoon you remember from childhood, and it lands on a world that has forgotten how to be normal in every other respect. The contradiction is gentle. You are not frightened. You should be, perhaps, but the light forgives you for not being.

## The ecology

Fauna is rare. In all that lush impossible green you will go a long time without meeting an animal, and when you do it will be hard to be sure you have.

There is a creature — call it the *surveyor* — that moves between the canopy-dialects and seems to maintain them, grazing not on leaves but on contradictions, eating the places where two realities disagree and leaving behind a single settled fact. It has too many legs and they do not all touch the same ground. Where it has passed, the doubling stops; the seven leaves collapse back into one, and the air is briefly, achingly ordinary.

There are pollinators of a sort, slow drifting motes that carry not pollen but *instructions* — packets of the world's deep grammar — from one plant to another, so that a useful trick discovered by a frond in the east migrates west over a season and the whole forest learns to bend light a new way.

That is the only change here. The world is otherwise frozen, time-invariant, in a stable equilibrium it reached long ago and has no intention of leaving. Nothing grows taller. Nothing dies. Nothing dries out, because nothing was ever wet. The only dynamic thing is the slow drift of ideas through the green, the forest revising its own laws at the pace of a tide that has no water to be made of.

## What emerges

Stand in it long enough and you understand: this is not a wilderness. It is a *document*. A vast, lush, salt-bedded record of every rule that could have governed a world, all of them tried at once, growing side by side, kept from contradicting each other only by the patient grazing of a creature with too many legs and the forgiveness of an ordinary sun.

It does not need you. It does not refuse you. You may break a leaf; it does not mind. You may stay; the temperature will hold you at the warmth of a held breath for as long as you like, and nothing will change, and the light will keep arriving clean across a dry and impossible green that drinks it and asks for nothing more.
