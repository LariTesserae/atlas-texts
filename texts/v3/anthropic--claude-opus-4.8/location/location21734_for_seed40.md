# The Condensary

Start high. You are already higher than the weather wants you to be, on a plateau that was poured rather than uplifted, a place where the rock beneath the city has been replaced — not covered, *replaced* — by the city's own substrate. There is no ground here. Dig down and you find more made-thing: foundation under foundation, conduit threaded through conduit, the cool ceramic intestine of a civilization that decided, at some point past memory, that natural earth was a thing to be improved away.

So: a built world, near-total. Walk it.

---

The terrain reads as mountainous because that is the shape the makers gave it, but the mountains are works. Stacked tiers of pale concrete and fired brick and a dull greenish alloy that fogs at the touch. The tiers rise in young, sharp steps — recent construction, edges not yet softened. Someone is still building. You can tell because the corners cut. Erosion hasn't gotten its tongue in yet; the rain that comes is thin and intermittent, a damp rather than a wet, and it has not had the centuries it would need to round anything down. The whole upland is fresh in that geological sense — a landscape that emerged, not slowly, but as fast as poured things set.

Cool air. The kind that lives at altitude and keeps its distance from your skin. It collects on the alloy surfaces as beads, runs in thin threads down the channels cut for exactly that purpose, because here water is harvested, not endured. There is no river. There is the *idea* of a river, distributed: a million condensation grooves leading to a million catch-basins, the high cool air sweated dry one droplet at a time. This is why the place is so quiet. Water that drips does not roar. The whole region whispers itself wet.

---

Now the light, which is the thing you will not get used to.

It is bright. Almost too bright, and it comes from everywhere — overhead from a sky that is white more than blue, and *upward* from the surfaces, which have been built to throw light back. Glazed tile, polished metal, panes of something that is glass when you look directly at it and not-quite-glass at the edge of vision. The brightness has no single source you can point to and accuse. It is ambient, total, and it does not flicker. The factories run on it. The plants — what plants there are — drink it.

Sparse vegetation, here. Not because the place is hostile to growth but because growth has been *rationed*, planted in the seams. Lichen-analogues spread across the cooler north faces in slow green-grey maps. In the catch-basins, where the harvested water pools, there are reeds — but reeds with a manufactured exactness, stems too evenly spaced, leaves that catch the wind in unison like a thing tuned. Between the factory-tiers, narrow troughs hold mosses and a low blue creeper that climbs the conduits and seems, faintly, to pulse. Patient growth. Slow-cycle. The vegetation here changes over seasons you would have to stay years to witness.

---

The tech is industrial and it is *everywhere* — this is the density, the 2.4, the near-touching layered saturation. Engines, but quiet ones. Mechanism stacked on mechanism. Pipes braided around towers braided around looms of cable. There is barely a cubic meter of this place that does not contain *something made and working.* And yet the noise is wrong for it — it should roar, a city of engines, and instead it murmurs. The machines have been built for silence the way the surfaces were built for light. Belts turn without squealing. Pistons ease. Steam vents through baffles that drink the hiss. You can hear your own breath over an entire working factory-mountain, and that is the most unsettling sensible fact of the place before you get to the unsensible ones.

---

The fauna. Abundant — that's the surprise.

You expected sterility from a total-built environment and instead the made-world *swarms.* Things live in the warm conduits and the cool grooves and the bright open tiers. Six-legged things the size of cats that graze the condensation lines, lapping harvested water, leaving the basins for the reeds. Flocks of small fliers that roost in the alloy eaves and have learned — or were made to learn — to keep the channels clear of debris, a maintenance crew that breeds. They are colored like the surfaces: pale, greenish, light-throwing. They have adapted to a world with no soil by becoming part of its plumbing. They are quiet too. Everything here has agreed to be quiet.

And the fauna is where you first notice that the rules have come loose.

---

Because here is the weirdness, the 2.8, the reality breakdown, and it does not announce itself. It accretes.

You watch one of the six-legged grazers drink from a catch-basin and you notice its reflection drinks from a *different* basin — one a tier up, where there is no grazer at all. You look again. The reflection is gone but the second basin now holds water it did not hold, water that has the just-disturbed shiver of having been drunk from.

The light comes from everywhere because, you begin to suspect, the surfaces are not reflecting an external sun. There is no sun. The brightness is internal to the place. The white sky is the *underside* of more city, hung impossibly far overhead, lit by its own throwing-back, so that you are inside the radiance of the world rather than under any source of it. The plateau is high — but high relative to what? There is no below that you can find. The foundation-under-foundation goes down as far as you've dug and the few who've dug further report it goes down *up* — that at sufficient depth the layers reverse their gravity and you fall toward a different bright sky, identical, where another version of the place is being slowly, patiently built by makers who throw their light back at you.

The makers. You never see them. The construction is fresh, the edges sharp, the work ongoing — and no one is working. Walls rise overnight by no hands. A tier that was unfinished at evening is finished at the next bright stillness, the new alloy already sweating its first condensation, the channels already cut, the reeds already grown a season tall in defiance of the hours. The slow-dynamic of the place is a lie told at the surface; underneath, change happens between glances, in the spaces where you are not looking. The patience is real and the speed is real and they are the same process seen from two sides.

---

Stand in a catch-basin court at the bright hour — there is only the bright hour, lengthened and shortened in a cycle too long to call day — and listen to how little there is to hear. A thread of water finding its groove. The breath-soft turning of an engine three tiers over. The unison sway of tuned reeds. One of the pale fliers settling, settling, never quite landing, because in this place a thing in motion sometimes forgets to arrive.

Cup your hands under a condensation line. The water that gathers is cool and clean and tastes faintly of the alloy. Drink it. It is the only honest thing in the Condensary: it was air, and now it is yours, and the change was slow and small and absolutely real.

Everything else, the bright total made high quiet swarming world of it, is still deciding what it is — and will keep deciding, droplet by droplet, tier by tier, in the seconds you spend blinking, forever and just now.
