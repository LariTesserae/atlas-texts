# The Tarn Engine

Begin high. You are always high here — there is no low to descend to, only the suggestion of one, a haze beneath the world where the ridgelines stop reporting back.

The land is new. You can tell because the rock has not yet learned to lie down. Everywhere the stone stands up in fresh blades and fins, basalt-young and sharp enough to part cloth, the way a mountain looks in the first ten thousand years before water rounds its shoulders. Nothing here is rounded. Edges. Ridges that meet at angles too clean to be old. When you put a hand to the rock it is warm — not sun-warm, deeper than that, a warmth that comes from below, as though the whole massif were a body running a low fever, which it is, which it is.

Now the vegetation. It does not grow *on* the rock so much as press into it, dense and crowding, leaf overlapping leaf in dim wet shade. Broad waxy fronds the color of old jade, mosses thick as upholstery in the seams, things that are almost ferns curling out of every crack with the urgency of steam escaping a kettle. It is warm and it is damp — not wet, not dry, but the in-between condition where water lives in the air and on the undersides of leaves and in the long slow drip from frond-tip to frond-tip, a rhythm so patient it feels like the slope is keeping time. Mist gathers in the lower folds. Occasionally it rains, but the rain comes sideways, comes from no cloud you can find, comes from the act of breathing of the place itself.

It is dim. The light arrives already used — filtered through canopy, through vapor, through whatever passes for sky up here, which is not blue and not gray but a kind of soft exhausted bronze. You never see the source. You stop looking for it.

And it is quiet. This is the strangest mercy of the place. By every measure it should roar — and here we come to the thing at the center, the thing the whole landscape is arranged around —

## The engines that no one built.

In the deep folds between the young ridges, where the vegetation grows densest, there are machines. Pistons taller than the trees. Flywheels half-buried in moss, turning. Long articulated arms of dark metal that flex and reach and set things down. Belts running over rollers, vanishing into the green. Furnaces glowing in the undergrowth, breathing heat into the warm damp air. It is unmistakably *industrial* — the scale of it, the deliberate mechanism, the sense of *production*, of something being made at volume, endlessly.

But no one built them. There are no structures — no walls, no roads, no factory floors, no housing, nothing that says *here a hand was laid and a purpose declared*. The machines have grown the way the ferns have grown, out of the warm fevered rock, pushing up alongside the moss, integrated, ancient-young, sharp-edged and unworn. A piston shaft has lichen on its lower third and condensation beading along its length and it rises and falls, rises and falls, and a vine has wrapped it and the vine rises and falls with it and neither one objects.

And it is *quiet*. The pistons fall without impact. The flywheels turn without bearing-whine. The furnaces breathe without roar. You stand among machinery that should deafen you and you hear instead the drip of water, the small movements of animals, your own pulse. The engines run in a silence that is not the absence of sound but the *refusal* of it, as though sound were a resource the place declined to spend.

## What the engines make.

This is where the world stops being describable in the ordinary way, and I will try anyway.

The machines manufacture *terrain*. You can watch it. An arm reaches into a furnace and draws out a length of glowing ridge — raw mountain, extruded, still soft — and lays it down, and it cools into basalt and grows its skin of moss within minutes, and so the land is *being made*, here, now, all around you, constantly. The vastness of the place — and it is vast, continental, large enough to weather itself, large enough that the haze beneath you may be a thousand kilometers of the same — the vastness is *output*. The engines have been pouring new world out of themselves for a long time and they have not slowed.

But here is the turbulence. They unmake too. As fast as a ridge is extruded, another is taken back — folded, retracted, dissolved into the warm air by some other arm working in reverse. The landscape is in a constant churning state of creation and destruction, never settling, a Solaris-ocean of stone. The fern you are looking at may be three minutes old. The valley you crossed an hour ago is not behind you anymore; it has been taken up and something else laid down. You cannot return. There is no *back*. There is only the standing-wave of the present, the machines making the now and unmaking the then, and you riding the crest of it.

This should be violent. It is gentle. Things end without distress here. A ridge dissolves and the animals on it simply find themselves on a new ridge, and they were always there, the memory adjusting smoothly to match.

## The fauna.

Abundant — and they are the only thing the engines do not make. They came from somewhere, or they have always been, and they move through the churning machinery with total ease, because they understand it and you do not. Things like long-limbed pale primates that swing from piston to vine to piston, timing their leaps to the rise and fall. Things like birds that nest in the warm flywheel-hubs. Multitudes of small creatures in the moss, soft-bodied, dim-eyed, eating the new growth before it can be unmade. They are not afraid. They have learned the rhythm of the place. When a section of world is about to be taken back they have already left it, casually, the way you'd step off a curb.

They are quiet too. They have learned that the world prefers it.

## The rule of the place.

Here is what you come to understand, standing on warm young rock in the dim bronze light, watching the silent engines make and unmake the mountains:

The machines are not building toward anything. There is no product, no finished state, no purpose being served. The making *is* the place. The world is a verb that has forgotten it ought to resolve into a noun. It runs hot and damp and high and dense and new, forever new, never aging because it is never allowed to last long enough to age — that is why the erosion is so young, why nothing is worn: nothing here is permitted to *endure* into smoothness.

And you — if you are here — are subject to the rule. Stand still too long and an arm will reach for you, gently, the way it reaches for an old ridge. You will not be destroyed. You will be *taken up*, and laid down again somewhere else, slightly changed, and you will not notice, and you will go on, on the warm new rock, in the quiet, under the bronze, among the breathing engines and the unafraid animals and the ferns three minutes old.

Keep moving. The world is being made beneath your feet. It would like you to be part of the motion.
