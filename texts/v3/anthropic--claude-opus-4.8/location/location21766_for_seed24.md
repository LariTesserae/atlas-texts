There is a hollow in the dark where the ground has finished becoming what it will be.

Start with the floor, because that is what you would touch first if you came here, and you would have to come by touch, because there is almost no light. The floor is dry stone, a pale fine-grained rock worn smooth over a span of time too long to feel as time. It is warm. Not hot — warm the way a stone is warm that has held heat without a source, evenly, all the way through, so that there is no cool side to find. You could lie on it and it would meet your body's warmth halfway and hold there. The warmth has no center. It does not radiate from any direction. It simply is the temperature of the place, and the place does not change it.

The hollow is small. Not cramped — there is room to walk, room for the dark to feel like space rather than enclosure — but you would learn its edges within an afternoon, if there were afternoons. Call it the size of a modest courtyard, or a large quiet room with the roof too high and too dark to see. The walls, where you find them, curve. The rock has been shaped by something patient and gone — wind, once, perhaps, or the slow work of dry mineral settling against dry mineral — and the shaping is done. Nothing is shaping it now. The grooves in the walls are finished grooves. The smooth places are finished smooth. This is a landscape that has arrived.

There is no water here and there has not been for an age, but the rock remembers water in its forms the way a face keeps the shape of expressions it no longer makes. You can run a hand along a runnel worn into the low wall and feel that something flowed there, and feel also that it is bone dry, and has been dry so long the dryness is part of the stone's nature, not an absence in it.

The plants are few and they are strange in their quietness. They grow in the seams of the floor, spaced far apart — one here, then nothing for many steps, then another. They are low, pale, fleshy in the way of things that have learned to hold what little they can find. No flowers. No color you could name in this dark, though if you brought light you might find them a faint grey-green, or the white of something grown without sun. They do not move. There is no wind to move them and they have no business moving on their own. They stand in the warm dark as still as the stones, and they are nearly as old. These are not plants that grow and die in seasons. These are plants that arrived at their size long ago and have simply continued, drawing some slow sustenance from the warm mineral and from whatever moisture the dry air still surrenders, which is almost none, which is enough, barely, forever.

It is quiet here in a way that the word silence does not quite cover. There is no animal sound because there are no animals — nothing scuttles, nothing breathes but you, if you breathe here. But it is not the dead silence of a sealed box. There is sound, just very little of it, and very far apart. Now and then a tick from the rock, the smallest readjustment, a grain settling — and then nothing for a long time. The rare plant, if disturbed, gives a dry whisper, the sound of a thing that is mostly fiber. Your own footsteps come back to you softened, swallowed by the rock that drinks sound the way it drinks heat, evenly, without echo. You learn to be quiet to match it. The quiet is not threatening. It is the quiet of a place that has nothing left to say and is comfortable with that.

The darkness is near-total but not absolute. There is a thin light from somewhere — not a sun, not a fire, nothing you can point to. It may seep from the rock itself, a faint mineral glow at the level of barely-seeing, the kind of light your eyes invent and then discover is real. By it you can make out the curve of the near wall, the spacing of the plants, the pale floor running off into a dark that the light cannot reach and does not try to. The light does not flicker. It does not brighten or fade. There is no day here and no night, only this one dim constant by which the hollow can be known a little, and beyond which it cannot be known at all.

If you stayed, you would find that nothing happened. This is the deepest fact of the place. The warmth would not change. The light would not change. The plants would not grow or wither while you watched, or while you slept and woke and watched again. There are no seasons, no weather, no tides of any kind. The hollow reached its balance before any witness came and it will keep that balance after. Whatever made it is finished making it. Whatever might unmake it is not here and is not coming. It rests in its own completion.

There is, in places, the suggestion that someone passed through long ago — a stone moved slightly out of its natural lie, a plant's seam cleared as if by a hand that wanted to see it better. Almost nothing. The faintest trace of intention, primitive and patient, a single deliberate act in a place built for no one. No structure. No tool left behind. Just the sense, very small, that this hollow was once known by something that knew how to look, and that the looking, too, is finished.

You could mistake it for a tomb, but it is not a tomb. Nothing died here to make it. It is more like the inside of a stillness — a place where the world set down one small handful of warm dry stone and a few enduring plants and decided to keep it exactly so, forever, in the dark, quietly, for its own reasons.

It holds. That is what it does. It holds.
