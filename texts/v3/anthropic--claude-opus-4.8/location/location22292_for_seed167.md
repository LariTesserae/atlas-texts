Begin in the dark, because there is no other way to begin here.

The water is warm — not the cold of true abyss but something closer to blood, a held warmth that suggests the place sits over something. Volcanic, maybe, or simply old enough that the heat has nowhere to go. You move through it and it moves with you, thick with the warmth, and the warmth is the first thing the body understands before the eyes give up trying.

This is a city, and it is underwater, and the water has been here longer than the city was ever dry.

---

Feel for it rather than look. The streets are still streets — you can tell by the way the current channels, the way it accelerates between two long walls and slacks in the open squares. The erosion has done its patient work: corners gone soft, lintels rounded, the sharp human geometry sanded toward the shapes water prefers. But the bones held. Whoever built this built in stone and built it to last, and it lasted past the purpose, past the people, into this second life as reef and ruin and refuge.

The buildings stand two and three stories where they stand at all, packed close — the density of an old town, the kind where you could pass bread from one window to the window across the lane. Now the lanes are corridors of dark water and the windows are dens. Everything that could be hollow has been claimed by something.

---

There is light, technically. Not from above — above is only more black water and then, impossibly far, a surface no one here remembers. The light is made down here, by the living. A drift of small luminous bodies moving in loose constellation along a rooftop ridge. The pulse of something hunting, a cold blue stutter in an alley mouth. A colony of soft-glowing growth crusting a doorway, breathing pale green when the current stirs it. These lights do not illuminate so much as announce — *here is a thing that is alive, and here, and here.* Between them the dark is total and the dark is most of everything.

So you read the city in flashes. A whole street given to you in the half-second of a passing glow, then gone, and you carry the image forward into the black like a held breath.

---

The vegetation is thin. This deep, in this warmth, not much green grows — there is no sun to feed it. What grows clings: low mats of dark weed in the silt of the squares, a few tall stalks that have learned to live on chemistry instead of light, rising from cracks where the warmth leaks up. They sway when nothing else moves and that is how you know the warmth has a source down there, breathing slow through the stone foundations of a dead town.

But the *fauna* — the fauna is everything the plants are not. Abundant past counting. The walls are not walls but surfaces, and every surface is occupied. Filter-things fan from the masonry. Crawling armored things work the joints of the paving. Pale eyeless fish pour through window-holes in schools that change direction all at once like a single decided animal. Things with too many legs fold into the carved details of old facades and become the facade until you brush them and they unspool into the dark.

In the deepest doorways, the larger ones wait. You feel them as pressure, as a displacement of warm water against your skin before you sense anything else. They are patient. The whole place runs on patience and ambush, the slow economy of the lightless: drift, sense, strike, settle. A predator-prey rhythm that has found its balance and keeps it, generation over generation, in the streets of a town that forgot it was a town.

---

It is quiet. This is the strange grace of the place — for all the life packed into it, the noise is low, almost nothing. Water carries sound far and clean, so what little there is travels: a distant clicking that might be feeding, might be speech; the deep periodic groan of the seabed settling, the warmth shifting in the rock below; the soft collapse of a long-undermined wall somewhere off in the dark, a sound you feel in your chest more than hear. Mostly there is the hush of your own movement and the great surrounding silence of deep water, which is not empty silence but full silence, the silence of a held room with many sleepers in it.

---

There are rules here, learned the way the body learns: don't trust the doorways. Follow the warmth and you find the living. The lights mean a thing wants to be seen, and a thing that wants to be seen is either offering or trap. The current is the only direction; learn it and the city lays itself out for you, a slow logic of channels and eddies, the old plan of streets translated into the language of flow.

And one rule stranger than the rest — the only real strangeness in a place otherwise simply, thoroughly natural: the warmth is not even. It pulses. Slow, very slow, a swell of heat through the whole drowned town every long while, and when it comes the lights all dim at once, the animals go still, the weed-stalks bow flat, and for a moment the city seems to remember being something other than ruin. Then the warmth ebbs, the lights return, the schools resume their pouring, and the place is only itself again — a town the sea took, kept warm, and filled with patient life, lying in the dark over whatever it is that still breathes beneath the stone.
