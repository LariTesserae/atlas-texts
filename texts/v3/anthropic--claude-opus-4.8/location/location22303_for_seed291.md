# The Newcity in the Cold Marsh

There is a city here that has not finished arriving.

Begin with the ground, because the ground has barely begun. The whole valley is raw — a flat cold floodplain where the rock has not yet learned to be rock. You can press your boot into the bank of a channel and it gives like clay still deciding whether to harden. There are no canyons, no weathered faces, none of the long patient grooves that running water cuts over centuries. The water here hasn't been at it long. The streams run shallow and braided across the flats, splitting and rejoining, changing their minds between one morning and the next, leaving silver threads that aren't yet rivers and won't commit to a single bed. Everything is damp without being drowned — mist beading on iron, condensation sheeting down glass, a low standing wet in the reed-beds, but the great dryness of the surrounding plain holding it all in pockets. Cold, too. Breath-fogging cold. Frost on the gantries each dawn that the engines burn off by noon.

And into this just-born, undecided land, someone has driven a city at full industrial pressure.

---

The city is the loud thing that is somehow quiet. This is the first strangeness you notice. Factories crowd the valley wall to wall — there is no gap between them, no margin, no breathing-room. Foundries against mills against tenement stacks against pumphouses against rail-yards, all jammed so tight the alleys between them are barely a shoulder's width, packed in like teeth in too small a jaw. Chimneys stand in forests. Pipes run overhead in tangled multiples, dripping. Catwalks cross and recross. Every available square foot is occupied by structure or machine or the dense low vegetation that has invaded everything.

But it is *mostly quiet.* The machines turn, the great flywheels spin, the looms shuttle, the bellows breathe — and almost no sound comes off any of it. Not silence exactly. A hush. A vast muffled industriousness, as though the whole works were running underwater, or wrapped in felt. You hear your own footsteps clearly. You hear water dripping forty yards off. You hear, occasionally, a single belt slap or a valve sigh, and the smallness of that one sound against all this machinery is unnerving. The mist may eat the noise. Or the cold. Or the moss.

Because there is moss everywhere, and that is the second strangeness.

---

The vegetation has not waited for the land to settle, and it has not waited for the factories to be abandoned. It grows *on the working machines.* Lush, cold-climate green — liverworts, deep cushion mosses, hart's-tongue ferns, pale lichens in coral shapes — sheeting the boiler housings, fringing the live pipes, hanging in curtains from the catwalk grates. Where a steam-line runs warm, the growth around it goes tropical-thick, vines fattening in the heat-bloom, then surrendering back to frost-rimed moss six inches further out. The plants drink the condensation. The whole city sweats, and the green feeds on the sweat.

Walk a main thoroughfare — though even the main ways are narrow, choked, the buildings leaning their upper storeys toward each other — and you'll find ferns unrolling from the rivet-seams of a still-hot furnace wall. You'll find a working clock-tower entirely socked in moss save for the clean swept circle of its face. The machines do not seem to mind. The machines, here, are perhaps not entirely separate from the growing things.

---

That is the third strangeness, and the deepest one, and it has to do with the turbulence.

Nothing here holds its shape for long. This is a city under construction that is also constantly half-unbuilt — and the building and unbuilding happen on their own. A wall of tenements you passed an hour ago is now a scaffold of girders with the brick gone; whether it fell or was taken or simply *reconsidered itself,* no one can say. A chimney that was leaning yesterday stands plumb today, repaired by no visible hand, or grown straight like a stem seeking light. The braided streams shift the foundations. The soft unfinished ground swallows a pump-house overnight and offers up, three streets away, a fresh-risen mound that has the rough beginnings of a foundry already pushing through the mud — courses of brick like teeth coming in, half-cast iron emerging from the soil already warm.

The city is growing the way the land is growing — embryonically, all at once, everywhere, raw. It is being made and unmade at the same time and at high speed, and the only reason it does not roar is that whatever runs it has chosen, for reasons of its own, to do all this softly.

---

The fauna belong to both regimes, the living and the made.

There are real animals, abundant in the cold wet margins — water-rails picking through the reed-channels, otters in the shifting streams, great flocks of small dark birds that roost in the chimney-forests and lift all together in silence, their wingbeats swallowed by the same hush that eats the machines. Frogs, improbably, in the warm pockets near the steam-lines, gone torpid the moment they hop into the cold.

And there are the other things. Animals that are partly mechanism, or mechanisms grown animal — it isn't clear which came first, in a place where the made and the grown share one logic. Long low things on many jointed legs that walk the catwalks and tend the green, clipping nothing, perhaps only watching. Beetles of brass and beetles of chitin foraging side by side in the same drip-puddle. Something large that moves in the deeper works, that you only ever hear as a settling — a vast slow adjustment of weight — and which may be an engine or may be asleep.

---

Stand on a high gantry at the valley's center, where the scale of the place opens out and you can see it whole.

The city fills the floodplain to the far cold hills, mile on packed mile of chimney and green and shifting brick, breathing mist into the level light of an overcast noon — normal daylight, grey and even and honest, which makes the strangeness worse, not better, because you cannot blame the dark for any of it. Below you the streams rewrite themselves. A district sinks; a district rises. The moss advances and the machines turn and the dark birds wheel up soundless and resettle. Everything moving, nothing loud. A whole world coming into being at a violent pace and keeping its voice down, as though it knew it was being watched, and was shy, and meant to finish before you looked too closely.

It will not finish. That is the rule of this place. It is always emerging and never emerged. The ground will not harden. The city will not hold still. The cold keeps the green honest and the green keeps the iron alive and the water keeps everything unfinished, and the whole quiet turbulent valley goes on arriving, and arriving, and arriving.
