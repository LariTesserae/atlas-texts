There is a hollow in the world the size of a large room, and it never gets any bigger, and it never gets any smaller.

You come into it low, because everything here is low — the floor of it sits maybe a hand's width above where water wants to be, so water is always wanting, always seeping up between things, beading on undersides, hanging in the air thick enough to taste. Warm water. The kind that doesn't refresh, only adds itself to you. You wear it before you've been here a minute.

The walls and floor and ceiling — there are no walls and floor and ceiling, only the living mass that does their work. Everything that would be stone or soil or timber is instead plant, and has been plant so long that the plant has gone smooth and settled, worn into its final shape the way a riverbed wears. Not new green. Old green. Green that has decided what it is. The substrate is a single packed body of stems and rootmats and the swollen bases of things that long ago stopped being individual and became architecture — pillars of fused growth holding up a canopy that is also the ceiling, that is also the room. It does not rot. It has reached the place past rotting where the wet and the warm and the centuries balanced out and just stopped. Established. In its own state.

And yet, for all that mass, the space is not crowded. This is the strange part. You'd expect a chamber of total vegetation to choke itself, but the open air in the middle is generous — you could lie down, stretch your arms, and touch nothing. The plant-stuff keeps to the edges and the columns, leaving the heart of the room empty, balanced against itself. Sparse, in the way an old cathedral is sparse despite its weight. Things here are scattered with intention they didn't choose, only arrived at.

The light comes down filtered and even, a normal honest light, the kind you'd read by, leaking through gaps in the green ceiling and pooling on the wet floor without drama. No shafts, no gloom. Just enough.

Now — the sound.

The sound does not match the room.

The room is small and still and almost holy, and it is *loud*, deafeningly, mechanically loud, and the sound comes from the fauna. They are everywhere along the green walls, abundant, and they are the engines of the place. Not metaphor-engines. The creatures here have made themselves industrial. Some thing the size of a fist clamped to a living column thrums at a steady factory pitch, a turbine note that never wavers, and there are hundreds of them, and they are not in chorus — they are in *production*. Each runs at its own fixed frequency, and together they make the layered drone of a working floor, a mill at full shift, pistons and belts and the grind of scaled-up labor. You feel it in the wet of your own chest. You feel it in your teeth.

But nothing is being made.

That's the unusual thread, the thing slightly off-true. The creatures run their engines and the engines do nothing — pump nothing, build nothing, drive no wheel. They are mechanized animals performing the *sound and the heat* of industry with no industry behind it. Open-throated little machines of meat and chitin, humming at the wall, warming the room another half-degree past comfortable, contributing nothing but the roar. An entire factory's worth of effort with the factory removed, leaving only the noise as a kind of weather.

And here is the deepest rule of the place: it never changes. Not the water-level, not the warmth, not the green that has stopped rotting, not the light, not even the roar — the roar is constant, a flat unbroken wall of sound with no rise and no fall, no shift change, no quitting time. You will not catch a quiet moment. There is no morning here and no evening. The engines that drive nothing will be running at exactly this pitch when you leave, and they were running at exactly this pitch before any door opened on you.

You stand in the loud warm wet heart of it, the only still thing in a room full of motion that goes nowhere, and you understand that you are inside something that finished becoming itself a very long time ago — a pocket of the world that reached its equilibrium and locked, a single sealed lung of green and noise and standing heat, breathing the same breath forever, hidden, complete, and entirely unbothered by your visit.
