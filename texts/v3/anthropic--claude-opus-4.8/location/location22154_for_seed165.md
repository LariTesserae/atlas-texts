There is a plateau near the top of the world that does not know it is finished.

Begin with the ground, because the ground is the most honest thing here. It is a pale stone the color of old bone, scoured into long flat shelves that step away from you in every direction. The shelves are smooth — not polished, but worn down by an unfathomable patience, the way a riverbed is worn, except there is no river large enough to have done it. Here and there the stone holds shallow basins, and in the basins sits water: black, still, mirror-cold. Frost rims them. When you breathe, your breath hangs.

The air is thin and frigid and tastes of iron. You are very high. You can feel it in the way your chest works too hard for too little. Above the plateau the sky is not quite a sky — it is a dim violet vault, lit from no visible source, holding a light that never strengthens and never fully fades. There is no sun. There has never been a sun. The light simply is, the way the stone is.

And it is loud.

That is the first thing that refuses to make sense, and you must sit with it, because everything refuses to make sense and the noise is the door through which the rest arrives. The plateau is nearly empty — you could walk for a day and meet nothing but stone and water-basins and the occasional thread of moss — and yet the air roars. A vast, continuous sound, low and immense, like a waterfall heard through a mountain, like wind in a canyon that has no wind. It comes from everywhere and from no direction. Hold your hands over your ears and it does not lessen, because it is not arriving through your ears; it is arriving through the *place*. The emptiness is what is loud. The silence has a voice, and the voice never stops.

The vegetation is sparse and stubborn. Cushions of moss, grey-green, growing in the lee of stone steps. Lichen in rosettes, slow as geology. A few low woody things, bent and leafless, that may once have been shrubs and now seem more like fossils that forgot to finish dying. They do not move. Nothing moves the air enough to move them. They are part of the equilibrium — and equilibrium is the deepest law of this place. Nothing here is *becoming*. Everything has already become and simply continues.

Now the water again, because the water is where the breakdown shows itself most clearly. Look into one of the black basins. You will see the violet vault reflected, and the rim of frost, and your own face — and then, if you keep looking, you will see that the reflection is a moment behind you. You blink; the reflection blinks after. You lean away; the reflection lingers. The surface is not showing you *now*. It is showing you a now that already happened, that the still water has not yet agreed to release. The water here remembers. It is the only thing on the plateau that does, and it remembers slowly, and it lets go reluctantly.

Drop a stone into one. The ripple spreads, reaches the edge — and does not stop. It returns. It crosses again. It will cross forever, very faintly, because nothing here loses energy, because the dynamic of this world is *none*. A thing set in motion is rare, and a thing in motion does not wind down. It simply joins the equilibrium as a permanent small disturbance, a standing memory of having been disturbed.

The roar, you will eventually understand, is made of these. Every motion that has ever happened on the plateau still happening, layered, all at once, a chord with no decay. The sound is the sound of nothing ever ending.

The built things are scattered and few. You come upon them the way you come upon everything — abruptly, out of the vast between-space. A wall of dry-stacked stone, knee-high, running thirty paces and then stopping, enclosing nothing. A doorway standing alone, two uprights and a lintel, framing only more plateau. A cairn taller than a person, balanced impossibly, each stone resting on a smaller stone, the whole thing top-heavy and stable, defying the fall it should have made a thousand years ago — but falling is a change, and change is not permitted, so the cairn stands, mid-impossibility, forever.

Who made them? There is no answer the place will give. The makers are part of what the water remembers and will not say. The doorways suggest people thought about thresholds here, about inside and outside, in a world where there is no inside, where the open extends past every wall.

Fauna is rare, and the few you meet are wrong in the gentle, total way everything is wrong. A pale bird stands on a stone shelf. It does not fly when you approach. It is not afraid; it is simply not going anywhere, because going is a change. Its eye tracks you — and only its eye. The rest of it holds still as carving. You feel it has been standing there since before the stone was worn flat. Perhaps it has. Perhaps it is the bird the water remembers, and the bird before you is only the water's reflection, escaped onto the land.

There is a creature lower down the steps, large, four-legged, the color of frost. It is drinking from a basin and has been drinking from that basin for an age. The water does not lower. Its tongue does not finish the motion. You can stand and watch the whole arrested swallow and never see it complete, the animal caught in the single act of drinking, fed and frozen and content.

Walk far enough and you reach the plateau's edge — except the edge does not behave as an edge should. The stone shelves step down, and down, and at the place where they should drop into a void or a valley, they instead *continue downward as up*. The horizon folds. What is below curves back to become what is above. The plateau has no outside. It is a closed surface in some dimension you cannot turn your head far enough to see, and the violet vault is only the underside of more plateau, impossibly distant, hanging where the sky should be. The black basins, if you knew how to look, are windows through the floor to the same basins on the far side. The roar is the sound of the whole closed world resonating with its own unending self.

It is cold. It is dim. It is loud beyond bearing and emptier than any desert. It rains, sometimes — a thin intermittent drizzle out of the unsourced violet, beading on the moss, filling the basins drop by drop, though they never overflow and never empty. The rain is the one thing that seems to arrive from elsewhere, and even it falls into the equilibrium and is held.

You could stay. Many things have. The bird, the drinking beast, the makers of the walls — they are not dead. They are *kept*. The plateau does not kill; it preserves. It takes the moment you are in and declines to let the next one come. That is its mercy and its horror, the same gesture: here, nothing is lost, because here, nothing is allowed to end.

If you wish to leave, you must do it while you are still moving — before the place learns the shape of your stillness and decides to keep it. So keep walking. Keep your breath ragged and your feet falling. Do not sit by the black water, however much it wants to show you the face you wore a moment ago.

And the roar will follow you to the edge that is not an edge, and over it, and you will understand at last that the roar was never sound at all. It was the world holding its breath, the long held note of a place that began once, long ago, and has been refusing to finish ever since.
