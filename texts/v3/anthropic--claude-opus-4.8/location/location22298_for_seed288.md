# The Hollow Beneath the Old Plain

There is no water here, and there hasn't been for longer than anything alive can remember. But that's not the same as saying it is dry the way a desert is dry. It is dry the way the inside of a seed is dry — a held, waiting dryness, a thing that has learned to live without.

You come down into it. That is the first thing to understand: this is a place *below*, reached by descending. Above, somewhere, is a flat country worn so smooth by age that it has nothing left to say. The plains up there are old past exhaustion, ground down to a near-level table, the bones of mountains long since become a fine pale grit. Nothing dramatic remains. But the plain is hollow. It has subsided into itself over an unimaginable span, and where it sagged, it opened a vast roofed under-country, a cavern-region with no single ceiling but many — collapsed, layered, propped on natural pillars of the old rock.

This is where the city is.

## The Stone

The walls and pillars are the color of bone-ash, of dried clay, of an animal's molars. They are smooth. Everything here is smooth — that is what two-and-a-half measures of erosion against a tenth of a measure of water produces: not the carved curves of running streams, but the slow rounding of dust-wind, of settling, of the patient downward press of time and gravity alone. The edges are gone. Corners have become shoulders. The pillars stand like the stumps of teeth in an enormous jaw, fluted not by water but by the eternal sifting of dry particles, grain over grain, century into century.

The ground is firm. Mineral. Cool to the touch — the whole place holds a steady coolness, the temperature of a cellar, of a deep room the sun never finds. Not cold. Cool and constant. Heat does not arrive and leave here; there is no day to bring it.

## The Built Thing

And someone built here. A great deal of someone, over a great deal of time.

The construction is dense and layered and organized — a true settlement-scale work, terraces stacked against the pillars, ramps spiraling the larger columns, dwellings cut and walled and roofed in the spaces between. But it is *old construction*. Finished. Settled into its final form. No one is building now. The walls have rounded along with the natural stone until you cannot always tell where the made thing ends and the grown rock begins. Doorways are arches worn to soft ovals. Stairways have slumped into ramps. Whole districts have the quality of something that was poured and then allowed to set forever.

It was made without tools — or with tools so simple they left no mark of their making. There is no metal here. No machine. No device hums or ticks or glows. The city was raised by hands and patience and the stacking of stone on stone, and then it was left to become part of the geology.

It is not ruined, exactly. Ruin implies a falling-down. This has not fallen. It has *aged into place*. It is as stable now as the rock it leans on, and it will look much the same in another ten thousand years. That is the deepest rule of this world: change comes, but slowly, on the scale of stone. Nothing here happens fast.

## What Lives

And yet — it teems.

The vegetation came down into the dark and adapted to it. Lush, but in the muted way of things that have made peace with dimness. Pale broad fronds spread from cracks in the pillars, drinking the faint light. Hanging gardens of fibrous gray-green growth drape from the upper terraces, fed by the moisture they pull from the air itself — for though there is no water *standing*, the air holds a thin steady damp, and these plants are built to comb it from the dark. Mosses thick as carpet. Fungal towers, soft and tall, ribbed like the underside of mushrooms, glowing faintly at their gills — these are part of why the place is not wholly black. A low, dim, biological light. Sourceless and even. The light of a long dusk that never deepens to night nor lifts to morning.

The plants are scattered, though. Sparse. Wide cool stretches of empty bare stone lie between the green gatherings. The city is large — open, regional, a valley's worth of under-country — and the life within it is spread thin across that vastness, clustered where it can be, leaving long bare avenues of dust between.

But the fauna — the fauna is *abundant*.

This is the strangeness that isn't strange, the thing that holds the parameters together: a dim, dry, ancient, near-empty stone city, and it is *crawling* with animal life. Things that need no open water, that drink the same mist the plants do, that have lived in the dark so long their eyes are huge or gone. Pale climbing creatures on the pillars, soft-furred and quick. Vast slow grazers that crop the fungal towers, their flanks like draped cloth. Swarming small flyers in the upper dark, leathery, sightless, navigating by sound. Colonies in the wall-cracks. Herds in the bare avenues. The empty spaces are not empty of motion — only of plants. The animals fill them. They move through the dim like a tide.

## The Sound

And this is why the place is *deafening*.

You would expect silence in an old buried city. You would be wrong. The sound here is enormous — a wall, a weather of sound. The flyers in the dark make a ceaseless overlapping clicking and shrieking that the stone catches and throws back from every rounded surface, every dome, every pillar, so that the echoes pile on echoes and there is no original cry anymore, only the roar of all of them returning at once. The grazers low and groan, and the sound rolls down the avenues. The small things in the walls scratch and chitter in their thousands. The hanging gardens rustle when the swarms pass through them. And under all of it, the slow grinding sigh of the air itself, moving through the columns, sifting the eternal dust.

The acoustics of the place are a trap for noise. Nothing escapes upward — the layered ceilings hold it down. It accumulates. It is loud the way a beehive is loud from within, a single living roar made of a million small sources, constant, without crescendo, without pause. You learn to live inside it the way you learn to live inside the cool. It becomes the silence. It becomes the texture of being here.

## The Rule of the Place

Everything moves slowly except the sound. The animals teem but the city does not change. The plants grow but the stone does not. This is a world that found its final shape long ago and now simply *continues* — abundant, loud, dim, cool, dry, and at peace with its own ending, having ended already, ages back, and kept on living anyway.

It is what a place becomes when it has nowhere left to erode to, and nothing left to do but be full.
