There is a room inside the mountain that has not finished being made.

You come into it the way water comes into it — sideways, through a seam, into warmth. The dark here is complete, the kind that does not thin no matter how long the eyes wait. But the place is not empty of sense. It presses close. The walls are near on every side, a chamber you could cross in a dozen steps if you could see to cross it, and you feel them before you touch them: a warmth radiating off rock that is still deciding what shape to be.

Everything here is wet. Not flooded — the floor holds shallow water in places, ankle-warm, but mostly the wetness is in the air, a fog that beads on the skin and on the stone and never quite falls. The walls weep. Run a hand along them and it comes away slick, mineral-tasting, faintly warm. Water moves through this rock the way blood moves under skin, close to the surface, everywhere at once, and where it finds an opening it gathers and hangs and lets go a single drop. That dropping is most of the sound. A *tick*, and a longer pause, and a *tick* from somewhere else, the chamber keeping a slow irregular time. Under it, almost too low to hear, the breath of water moving inside the walls.

The rock is young. This is the strange thing your hands keep returning to: the surfaces have no history in them. No grooves worn smooth, no layers peeled back by ten thousand years of patient ruin — none of the writing that age leaves. The stone is raw, knobbed, beaded with mineral the water has only just begun to lay down. Stalactites here are the length of a fingertip, no longer. Flowstone has the texture of something poured this morning and not yet set. You are standing inside a cave at the very beginning of being a cave, in the first hour of a making that elsewhere takes ages. And yet — nothing is changing. The drop hangs and falls, hangs and falls, but the wall behind it does not grow. The little stone teeth do not lengthen. The place is held at its own dawn, embryonic and fixed, a beginning that has agreed not to proceed.

Life is thin here but it is present, layered into the dark. Where the warm water pools, the rock wears a felt of something — not moss, there is no light for moss, but a pale matted growth that lives on the minerals and the heat, soft as wet wool under the fingers. It covers more than you'd guess; the density of the place is in this, in how much is packed into the small space. The fog is full of it too, a faint living smell, like bread and iron. Sparse, but everywhere sparse, every surface holding its quiet skin of life. Now and then something larger moves — rarely, so rarely you doubt it. A small soft body in the water, blind, slow, brushing your ankle once and gone. A thing that has lived its whole life in this single room and knows it the way you know the inside of your own mouth.

And there are the marks of people, though no people. Scattered, old, fewer than a handful. A line of hand-shaped notches cut into one wall at shoulder height — a ladder of grips for someone climbing in the dark, made with a stone edge, not a metal one. A clay bowl on a ledge, fire-hardened, half-full of the weeping water, its rim furred with the same pale growth. A bundle of reeds bound with sinew, long rotted soft. Someone came here, into the warm wet dark, with their hands and their simple made things, and did something — sheltered, waited, worshipped, hid — and left these few signs and did not come back, or came back and left no other trace. The notches do not lead anywhere you can find. The bowl has been catching the same drip for longer than the makers' bones have lasted.

You are high up — you can feel it in the thinness behind the warmth, in some pressure-sense the body keeps. This room is set far up inside a great height of stone, a pocket sealed in the upper dark of a mountain that is itself, perhaps, still young. But the room does not know it is high. It has its own weather, its own breath, its own slow clock of falling drops. Out beyond the seam you came through there may be wind and cold and the enormous open night; in here there is only the warm close dark and the water near the surface of everything and the patient unhistoried stone.

The rule of this place is the one your hands learned first and keep relearning: *nothing here will finish.* The cave is being born and will go on being born. The water will go on arriving and never carve. The few who came will not come again, and their bowl will fill and never overflow. It is a held breath, a room at the start of time that has chosen the start of time to live in forever — warm, wet, dark, dense with small soft life and the smell of bread and iron, ticking its slow uneven clock against walls that will not age.

You could stay. People did. You can see why.
