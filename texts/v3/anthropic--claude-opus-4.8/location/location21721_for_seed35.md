## The Bobbin Hollow

Go down. The way in is a throat of dry mineral — halite blades and gypsum needles, sharp because nothing has worn them, recently grown, still growing in the slow geological sense though you will never catch them at it. They crowd the descent so closely you turn sideways. They click when you brush them. They do not cut.

The Hollow opens below the surface and it is warm, the warmth of a body or a switched-on machine, a warmth with no source you can find. There is no water here. There never has been. This is the first thing the place wants you to understand, and it tells you by the way the dust never clumps, the way your breath does not fog, the way everything is built to run without a single drop.

It runs anyway. That is the strangeness.

---

**The machines.** They are everywhere, near-touching, layered against the walls and hanging from the low ceiling and standing in the dry channels where water would go if there were water. Spinning machines, mostly. Bobbins and flywheels and treadle-beds and long jacks that draw something out and twist it and wind it back. They are made of the local minerals — bone-colored gypsum gears, halite drive-shafts clear as old ice, frames of some dark dense salt that holds an edge. They were not brought here. They grew, or were grown, in the young clean way of the crystal, and someone — something — tuned them.

They never change. This is the rule of the world, the deepest rule: the Hollow is at equilibrium. The wheels turn but the turning goes nowhere, takes nothing, wears nothing. A bobbin fills and the same bobbin empties and the level never moves. Watch one flywheel for an hour and it spins at exactly the speed it spun when you arrived. There are no gradients here. Nothing is running down. Nothing is winding up. The industry is total and totally still in its totality, like a held chord.

And it is **loud**. God, it is loud. The whole Hollow is a single sustained machine-roar — the clack of ten thousand shuttles, the whine of jacks, the grinding harmonic of salt on salt, a drone you feel in the long bones of your legs. It does not rise and it does not fall. It is the same loudness it has always been. After a while you stop hearing it as sound and start hearing it as the shape of the air, the medium you are standing in.

---

**The fauna.** They live in the noise. They love it.

Small dry things, abundant, many to the cubic foot — they ride the machines. Salt-mites that polish the gears with their abrasive bellies. Long pale arthropods, segment after segment, that thread themselves through the bobbins as the fiber would thread, taking the place of fiber, *being* the fiber for one turn before they slip free and the next one slides in. The wound spools are alive. Pull one and it unspools into a hundred crawling things that re-spool themselves before they reach the floor.

There are the listeners, too. Flat eared creatures the color of gypsum, no eyes, pressed against the resonating frames, drinking the drone. They cannot live in quiet — a stilled machine grows a ring of their small dry corpses around it within hours, and so the machines must never still, and so perhaps that is why nothing here is allowed to change: the fauna require the equilibrium, or the equilibrium requires the fauna. You cannot tell which way the dependence runs. In a frozen system causation has no arrow.

And something larger moves through, occasionally, on the edge of the dim light — not a predator, the Hollow has no hunger, hunger is a gradient and there are no gradients — but a tender, maybe, a keeper, long-limbed and unhurried, touching the frames as it passes the way you might trail a hand along a fence. It checks the tuning. It has always been checking the tuning.

---

**The vegetation** is sparse and odd. It grows from the salt without water, feeding on the warmth and the sound — yes, the sound; you can watch a frond lean toward a loud machine the way a plant leans to a window. Glassy stalks, frosted leaves that ring faintly when the drone hits their resonance. They are tuned too. Everything here is tuned to everything else and the tuning never drifts.

A few of them bear what look like seeds, but the seeds never fall and never sprout and never rot. They hang. They are part of the chord.

---

**The light** is dim and comes from the crystal itself, a low patient phosphorescence that does not flicker, that has the steadiness of the rest of this place. There are no shadows that move. Your own shadow is the only thing in the Hollow that does not belong to the equilibrium, and the creatures notice it; the listeners turn their eyeless faces toward it, troubled by the new motion, this thing that arrives and changes — *you*, the one variable, the gradient that walked in.

The built things were here before you, scattered among the natural blades — a few small structures, shelters or shrines or housings, made by hands that worked the way the machines work, repetitively, perfectly, finished long ago and never altered. Inside one, a stool. A bench worn by nobody. A row of spare bobbins that will never be needed because nothing here breaks.

---

The Hollow does not want you to leave and does not want you to stay. It does not want. It simply *is*, completely, at every point in time identically, a small warm dry deafening eternal room where the industry of an absent purpose spins on and the living things ride the wheels and drink the noise and the fronds lean toward the loudest place.

You will climb back out through the blades. They will click. They will not cut.

And behind you the chord will hold, exactly as loud, exactly as still, forever — the one place in the world that has finished happening and decided to keep happening anyway.
