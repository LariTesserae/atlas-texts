# The Bright Below

Go down. Not far — the place is barely a body's depth beneath the surface, a pocket of held air the size of a modest room, and you reach it the way you reach the inside of your own cheek with your tongue: it was always there, you simply hadn't attended to it.

It is lit. That is the first wrongness, and it never resolves. There is no opening to the sky, no shaft, no lamp, no glowing fungus making excuses — and yet the chamber is *bright*, brighter than a noon field, a flat even daylight that comes from everywhere and casts no shadows. You can see the backs of your own hands as if held to a window. The light has no source and no preference. It simply is the condition of the room, the way wetness is the condition of the sea.

The walls are raw. This is rock that has not finished being rock — soft mineral paste, pale and faintly translucent, with the texture of something interrupted mid-pour. Run a finger across it and it gives slightly, then firms, as if deciding. Crystals are *beginning* in it — half-faceted, edges not yet sharp, the geometry still negotiating with itself. Nothing here has eroded because nothing here has aged. There are no smoothed corners, no settled dust, no weathering. The whole place sits in the held breath just before formation, and never exhales. Permanence by way of never quite starting.

Damp beads the ceiling. Every so often — though "often" is the wrong word, since the rhythm never varies and never will — a drop gathers, swells, falls, and strikes a shallow pool in the floor with a clear high *tok*. The same drop. The same note. The pool never rises, the ceiling never dries. There is a thin trickle, too, threading down one wall into a runnel no wider than a finger, moving but going nowhere, arriving nowhere, a river the length of a forearm that has been flowing forever at exactly this pace.

Now the difficulty: the room is *full*.

Not cluttered — full. Saturated. There is no empty volume anywhere. Where there is not rock or water there is life, packed in, presence against presence, and yet — this is the second wrongness — you can still move, you still have room to stand, because the fullness is not a fullness of things-taking-space. It is a fullness of *attention*. Every cubic inch is occupied by something that is looking, or singing, or waiting, and the air itself feels load-bearing.

The fauna are abundant and small and strange. Pale soft creatures the size of thumbs cling to the not-yet-crystals, ribbed like woodlice, translucent so the light passes through their working interiors. They do not eat — there is little here to eat — but they hum. Each one holds a single sustained tone, and there are thousands, and the room *roars*, a packed chord that fills the ears the way the light fills the eyes: from everywhere, sourceless, total. Among them move the long ones, ribbon-bodied, swimming through air as if it were the water they remember, mouthing the walls without taking anything. Nothing born, nothing dying. The population is exactly itself, forever.

The vegetation is sparse and apologetic — a few stalks of something pale rising from the damp seams, more like fingers of mineral that have decided to be soft than like plants. They neither grow nor wilt. They are at the height they will always be.

And there are marks of making. Scattered, faint. Set into one wall, at the height a seated person might reach, a row of small fired-clay figures — crude, thumb-pressed, a hand's count of them — and a single low arch of stacked stones in the corner that frames nothing, leads nowhere, a doorway one finger high. Someone built here. Once, or always. There are no tools to be found, no shavings, no evidence of the work, only the work itself, sitting in the bright like everything else, unchanging. Whoever they were, they are part of the held breath now. The figures face the pool. They have been listening to the same drop, the same chord, the same forever, with the patience of fired clay.

The rules of this place are simple and absolute:

Nothing changes. The drop falls, the chord holds, the light shines, the crystals almost-form. There is no before and no after, only an enormous *now* pressed flat and packed solid and lit from nowhere.

It is cool. It is damp. It is loud and it is bright and it is small enough to cross in three steps and it is completely, serenely full.

You came down expecting a cave. You found a held moment that someone — or no one — decided to keep, and furnished with humming, and lit so it would never be dark, and sealed so it would never be done.

You can leave whenever you like. It will be exactly this when you return. It will be exactly this when no one returns.

The drop falls. *Tok.*
