# The Marrow Galleries

There is no water here, and yet everything is swollen.

You come down into it — and *down* is the only honest direction, because this place lives beneath a surface that may not exist anymore, a roof of root and rind so old it has forgotten it was ever sky. The descent is not a tunnel. It is a softening. The air thickens with sugars and the breath of green things until you understand that you have not entered a cave so much as a body, and the body is awake, and it is loud.

**The Substrate**

The walls are plant. Not covered in plant — *made* of it. The floor flexes underfoot with the give of a living stem, ribbed with veins as thick as your wrist, warm to the touch, faintly pulsing with the slow hydraulic decisions of something that grows. Here in the absence of water the vegetation has learned other tricks: it stores its life in waxes and resins, in dry pith honeycombed with air, in crystalline sap that holds no moisture but glitters like rock candy in the diffuse light. Snap a frond and it does not weep. It cracks, and the inside is the color of amber and bone.

The whole valley-sized hollow is roofed and floored and pillared by this single ambition of chlorophyll. Columns of fused cellulose rise and branch and rejoin overhead, holding up a ceiling that is also a forest seen from beneath, a tangle of trunks growing *downward* toward you with leaves the size of sails. Light comes through them — a warm, jaundiced, butter-and-rust light, normal in quantity but wrong in source, because there is no sun. The plants themselves luminesce faintly where their sugars metabolize, and the leaves above filter and re-filter a glow that has no origin, only diffusion.

**Emergence**

Nothing here is finished. This is the most disorienting thing.

Whole architectures of stem are visibly *forming* — you can watch a pillar thicken in the time it takes to rest against it. Buds the size of barrels swell and split and unscroll new chambers, new corridors, new rooms, faster than any plant has a right to. The galleries reorganize. A passage you walked through is gone when you turn back, sealed by overgrowth, and a new one yawns where there was solid green an hour ago. The world is *under construction* by its own tissue, raw and embryonic, geology made of meristem. Erosion does not wear this place down; instead it *erupts upward*, half-built, never settling, a landscape that has not decided what it is.

**The Fauna**

And it is *full*.

This is what the deafening is. The plant-architecture is the substrate, the host, the soil — but the volume itself is a riot of animal life packed near to touching, layered floor to ceiling, every niche of the swollen green stuffed with movement. Things that are mostly leg cling to the columns in glittering sheets. Things with too many wings and no eyes pour through the upper branches in rivers. Soft pale grazers the size of dogs chew tunnels into the pith, and behind them come the things that eat *them*, and behind those, smaller things that clean what's left, and the whole devouring cascade churns the substrate even as the substrate grows to replace itself.

The sound is total. It is not noise, it is a *medium* — a roaring chord of stridulation, the rasp of a million mouthparts on dry fiber, the bellows-pump of the great hollow pillars breathing, the crack and groan of new growth tearing itself into being, a low organ-drone you feel in your sternum more than hear. There is no silence to return to. You learn to think *under* the sound the way a diver thinks under water.

**The Marks of Hands**

And here is the strangeness that makes a story: someone lives here.

Scattered through the galleries — rare, faint, a single structure every long walk apart — are the works of hands. Not machines. Hand-tools: scrapers of hardened resin, blades knapped from the crystalline sap-stone, lashings of fiber. Someone has carved smooth-walled alcoves into the living pith and lined them with shed animal-plate to keep the growth from reclaiming the space. Someone has learned which fronds bear edible wax and which bear poison, and marked them with notches that the plant grows *around* and preserves like scars.

You find a dwelling: a hollowed bud, sealed against the churn, its inner walls scraped clean and rubbed with luminescent resin so it glows soft and steady amid the chaos. A loom of plant-fiber. A hearth that burns dry pith with a sweet smoke. Tally-marks counting something — days? generations? the cycles of the swelling galleries? Whoever made these understood the rules of this world: *nothing holds still, so you must build inside the growing and prune it daily, or be swallowed.* Their craft is the craft of negotiating with a place that will not stop becoming.

**The Rules**

1. There is no water; there is only the slow dry chemistry of sap and wax and resin, and the warmth — always warm, blood-warm, the heat of a metabolism.
2. The plant is the world. It is floor, wall, sky, food, tool, shelter, and threat.
3. The animals are the weather. They surge and recede in storms of bodies; you read the day by which way they're flowing.
4. Nothing is permanent. To inhabit this place is to garden it constantly, to carve faster than it heals.
5. The sound never stops. Those born here have never known quiet and would find it terrifying.

It is below, it is warm, it is dry, it is alive past any reasonable measure, and it is being born and unborn around you in a single endless turbulent breath — a green throat singing one note forever, with small clever creatures making homes against the walls of the song.
