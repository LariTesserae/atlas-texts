# The Drowned Manufactory

Begin underwater, but not the ocean. The ocean has a floor and a surface and an opinion about which way is up. This place has none of those.

You are inside the water the way a thought is inside a skull — surrounded, pressured, indistinguishable from the medium that holds you. It is warm, blood-warm, the temperature of something recently alive. The water is thick with suspended green. Not algae, exactly. The vegetation here grows *in* the water rather than from any soil, propagating through the liquid as filaments, as drifting curtains of broad translucent leaves, as ropes of something between kelp and circuitry that pulse faintly and root themselves into nothing. Lush, layered, near-touching. You cannot move an arm's length without parting a veil of it. It grows so densely that the water itself seems vegetal, a continuous swimming flesh of stems.

And through all of it, the machines run.

---

They were never installed. There is no evidence anyone built them, no scaffolding, no fastening, no logic of assembly. They simply *are*, the way the leaves are — pistons the size of grain silos, driving up and down in the murk; turbines half-glimpsed through the green, throwing wakes of spinning water; pipework that branches like the vegetation branches, indistinguishable at the joints, so that you cannot tell where the brass ends and the stem begins. The metal is young. That is the wrong word and the only word. It has the look of things just born — no rust, no fatigue, fresh-cast edges still bright, bolts that have never weathered. Everything here was made recently and is still being made, continuously, around you, by no hand.

The light comes from inside the machines. A dim amber bleed from sealed furnace-windows, from gauge-faces, from the slow combustion happening somewhere it should be impossible to burn — fire, underwater, glimpsed orange through portholes, never spreading, never dying. The light filters up through the green water and turns everything the color of held breath. You can see perhaps thirty feet. Past that, shapes: the suggestion of larger engines, vast rhythmic silhouettes laboring at the edge of vision.

---

The sound is the thing you will not be ready for.

Underwater, sound does not travel — it *occupies*. It enters through your sternum and your teeth before it reaches your ears. And here the sound is total: the hammer-fall of the great pistons, the grind of submerged gears, the shriek of steam venting into water and being instantly drowned and venting again, the low organ-drone of turbines that you feel as a pressure behind the eyes. It is deafening in the literal sense — it deafens, it replaces hearing with itself, so that silence becomes unimaginable, a thing you have to remember rather than experience. Layer on layer, no single rhythm winning, all of them turbulent, falling in and out of phase, so that for a moment you think you hear a pattern, a melody almost, and then it dissolves back into the industrial roar.

---

Here is where the world stops keeping its promises.

The machines produce nothing. Or rather — watch one long enough and you'll see it produce *the water it operates in*. A turbine spins, and the water it churns is newer than the water beside it, brighter, more recently made. The vegetation grows toward the manufacture and is fed into it and emerges as more vegetation, slightly altered, swimming away to grow again. The place is not running on anything. It is running *itself*, a closed loop of creation eating its own tail, and the loop is not stable. It is in storm. Whole sections reconfigure between glances — a corridor of pipework you swam through is, when you turn, a wall; the amber light shifts to where you remember darkness; a piston that drove downward is now driving up and always was.

There is no fauna. Nothing swims here but you, and you are not certain you count. The absence is deliberate-feeling. This is not a dead place — it teems, it labors, it shouts — but nothing here was ever *born* in the animal sense, nothing eats, nothing fears, nothing dies. The energy of an anthill with no ants. A bazaar with no merchants, only the goods endlessly trading themselves.

---

Low elevation: you are at the bottom of something, though there is no bottom. The pressure says *deep*. The warmth says *deep and alive*. Occasionally, scattered through the green, you find the built things that did not make themselves — a tiled wall, a doorframe standing in open water leading nowhere, a flight of stone steps ascending into the murk and stopping. Small human marks, partial, old. These are the only old things here. Someone was here, once, before the machines began making the world, and they built modestly, and then the manufactory grew up through their work the way roots grow through a ruin, and now their doorframes hang in the warm green water, opening onto more water, while the engines roar and the water is born and born and born.

The rule of the place, if it has one: *nothing finishes.* The fire does not consume. The water does not still. The machine does not complete its product. You will not reach the bottom or the surface or the edge. You will swim through the warm green roar, parting veils of pulsing leaf, and the manufactory will rearrange itself politely around you, and produce, in its turbulent eternal way, exactly enough world to contain you and not one inch more.
