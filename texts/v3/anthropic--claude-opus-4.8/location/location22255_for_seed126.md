# The Patient Engine

Begin with the light, because the light is the first thing and it never stops being the first thing.

It comes from everywhere. Not a sun — there is no sun here, no single source to cast a single shadow. The light rises from the floor of the world, which is not a floor but a lattice, a pale construction stretching past where the eye gives up, and the lattice glows with the soft even brightness of a thing that has been left on for a very long time. Bright enough that you squint. Bright enough that there are no real shadows, only the faint grey doublings where one structure leans across the light of another. A world without darkness, and therefore a world that has forgotten how to rest.

The ground is built. All of it. There was never a stone here, never a grain of true sand. What looks like a desert plain — and it does look like a desert, that vast warm flatness running to a horizon that curves the wrong way, too slowly, as if the world is far larger than a world should be — is in fact a surface assembled at a scale below seeing. Run your hand across it (your hand would not survive here, but imagine it) and you would feel not grit but *regularity*, a fineness like the side of a polished tooth, repeating and repeating. Everything here was made. The makers are not in evidence. The making is so old it has become weather.

---

It is warm. Pleasantly, almost. The warmth has no weather-front to it, no sense of a front of heat moving in — it is the steady warmth of machinery running far below its design temperature, the warmth of a great slow process that gave up urgency long ago but never quite stopped. You could walk here for a day and the temperature would not change by a degree. The constancy is part of what makes it strange. Living things expect variation. This place offers none, and the body keeps waiting for the change that never arrives.

There is no water. Not hidden, not frozen, not underground — none, and there never was. The chemistry of this place runs on something else, something dry. The sparse plants — and there are plants, of a sort — do not drink. They *accrete.* They pull their substance directly from the lattice beneath them, drawing up the made-matter atom by atom and arranging it into stalks the color of old ivory, into fans and filaments that stand in loose scattered colonies across the plain. They are sparse the way things are sparse in a salt flat: a clump here, a thin grey thicket there, long bright emptiness between. They do not sway, because there is barely any wind, and the wind that exists moves so slowly you would not call it wind so much as *drift.*

---

Now: the density. This is where the eye corrects itself.

From far off the plain reads as empty — flat, bright, sparse. But the emptiness is a trick of scale. Look closer and the air itself is *full.* Not with dust. With structures. Suspended at every height, from ankle to far above the head and on up into the bright haze, hang lattice-forms — geometric, branching, near-touching, layered one behind another behind another until the apparent emptiness resolves into a forest of the all-but-invisible. They are made of the same pale stuff as the ground, and they are nearly transparent, and they hang in the warm still air without support, packed dense enough that to move through them you would have to move *carefully,* threading between presences you can barely see.

They are everywhere. Per volume, this is one of the most crowded places imaginable. It only looks like a desert because everything crowding it has been built to be almost nothing — almost clear, almost weightless, almost not there. A vast made world saturated to its limit with structures so refined they verge on absence.

---

The sound. There is almost none.

This is the deepest strangeness, deeper than the light. A place this full, this active, ought to roar. Instead: silence, or near it. Now and then a *tick* — a single small click as somewhere in the lattice a piece settles, a connection completes, a process advances one notch. The ticks are not random. If you stand still long enough you begin to feel they are *spaced,* that there is a rhythm under them too slow to call rhythm, the heartbeat of something that beats once an hour, once a day. Between the ticks, nothing. A quiet so total it stops feeling like absence and starts feeling like *attention* — as though the silence is the sound of something enormous listening, and holding very still so as not to miss the next small click of its own working.

---

The fauna are rare, and you will not be sure you have seen them.

Crossing the bright plain, threading the dense clear forest, are a few — very few — moving things. They are hard to describe because they are hard to *see*: they have the same near-transparency as everything else, and they move without disturbing the suspended structures, slipping between them with a courtesy that suggests long practice. Tenders, perhaps. Or grazers. They drift along the lattice and pause and seem to *adjust* things — to nudge a hanging structure a fraction, to complete a click that the slow process had left for too long. Whether they are alive, or machines, or alive-and-machine, the distinction does not survive here. Nothing in this world has needed that distinction for a very long time.

They are rare the way the last few of a thing are rare. Once there may have been many. The work is mostly done now.

---

Because that is the truth of the place, the thing the erosion-reading and the age-reading point to: **this is a world at the end of its making.** Not ruined — the opposite of ruined. Finished. Worn down not by decay but by *completion,* every process having run so long it has nearly reached its rest. The plain is near-flat because the great construction has had eons to settle into its lowest, most stable, most patient form. The light stays on. The warmth holds. The sparse plants accrete their slow ivory. The clicks come, spaced further and further apart as the last unfinished things finish.

It is ancient the way a thought is ancient when it has been held so long it has stopped feeling like thinking and started feeling like the shape of the mind itself.

And it is *vast* — vast past sense, vast enough to make its own slow non-weather, the drift that crosses thousands of kilometers of bright plain and arrives changed by nothing. There is no edge you could reach. The horizon's wrong slow curve tells you the world goes on far past the limits of a planet, that you are standing on the inside of something, or the surface of something, built to a scale that has its own physics — gravity a little too gentle, distance a little too willing to fold.

---

Stay a while. Let the silence resolve into its slow spacing of clicks. Let the empty air resolve into its crowd of near-invisible forms. Let the warmth stop feeling like comfort and start feeling like the residual heat of an engine that has very nearly finished its work and will, when the last click comes — perhaps soon, perhaps in an age — go quietly bright and still and complete, and need no one to witness it, having been built so well, so long ago, by hands no one remembers, for a purpose that finished becoming itself long before you arrived to ask.
