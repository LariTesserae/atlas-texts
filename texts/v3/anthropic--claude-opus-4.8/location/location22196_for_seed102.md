There is a room beneath the world, and it has never once changed.

I want to be careful with that word, *room*, because you will picture corners and a ceiling, and there are no corners here. The space is roughly the size of a cathedral's nave, but it is bounded the way a held breath is bounded — by the limit of itself, not by walls. Where you expect rock, there is instead a dim warm dark that the eye refuses to land on. The floor, though — the floor is real. It is a single sheet of pale mineral, dry as bone-ash, faintly translucent, like the inside of a tooth. It is warm underfoot. It has always been warm. Nothing here has ever cooled or kindled; the heat simply *is*, a body-temperature constancy that feels less like climate than like being inside something living and very still.

There is no water. Understand how strange that should make the rest of it.

Because the place is green. Lush, even — but it is a lushness that runs on something other than water. Across the dry mineral floor grow the *fronds*: tall plants, sparse, scattered with enormous gaps of empty floor between them, each one standing alone like a guest who arrived early to a party that will never fill. They are not wet plants. Snap one — though nothing here will let you — and it would not bleed sap. They are built of something closer to wax and glass-wool and powdered light. They feed, I think, on the heat and the sound. Yes. The sound feeds them.

Because it is *loud* down here. That is the thing no description prepares you for. The room is silent of water but it roars. A low, layered, continuous roar, the sound of an enormous crowd heard through a wall, or of wind that has no air to ride on — a pressure-sound, a sound that has decided to exist without a cause. It comes from everywhere and from the fronds themselves, which hum at the base of hearing, each at its own unwavering pitch, so that the scattered plants form a chord that has been sustained since before there was anyone to hear it. The chord does not resolve. It does not want to. It is the most stable thing in the room, and the room is the most stable thing I have ever been asked to describe.

Nothing changes here. I keep returning to this because it organizes everything. There is no day, no season, no weather, no growth and no decay. The fronds do not grow taller. The dust does not gather. A thing dropped would, I believe, simply rest exactly where it landed for the rest of time. And yet the place is young — sharp-featured, freshly made, its edges crisp and unworn, as if it had been formed an instant ago and that instant had then been stretched to fill all of duration. It is new the way a struck bell is new in the moment of striking, except the bell never stops ringing and never starts to fade. Recently emerged. Permanently emerging. The paradox is the point.

Now the fauna, which are abundant and which move — and this is the only motion in the room.

They are small, pale, soft-bodied things, something between a moth and a hand and a thought. They drift between the fronds across the wide empty floor, and where they pass they brush the humming plants and change, very briefly, the pitch — and then the pitch corrects itself, snaps back to its eternal note, so that the creatures are like fingers running along the strings of an instrument that retunes itself faster than you can play it. There are many of them. They never collide. They never rest. They never reproduce or die, as far as I can tell; there are simply always exactly this many, drifting in the warm dark, the only un-frozen thing, and I have come to suspect they are not alive at all but are *the room's way of staying still* — that all the motion in the place has been gathered into them and given a shape, so that everything else can hold perfectly, perfectly fast.

And there are marks of hands. This is the part that makes the chest ache.

Scattered across the floor, with the same lonely spacing as the fronds, are small built things. A low wall of stacked pale stone, three courses high, fifteen stones, going nowhere, enclosing nothing. A bowl of fired clay, dry, never used, never to be used. A row of notches cut into the floor-mineral with a tool of bone or flint — a tally, perhaps, of something, but the count stopped long ago, or rather it never started, because nothing here accumulates to be counted. Whoever made these worked by hand, patiently, with simple tools and no metal, and they made them *well*, and then they left, or stopped, or were always already gone. The structures are not ruins. Ruins imply that something fell. Nothing here has fallen. These are the marks of a maker who set down their tools mid-gesture and the gesture became eternal.

I think — and this is me following the thread, not reporting fact — I think the place is a held moment from inside some larger living thing's body. Warm, dry, lit dim and amber from no source, roaring with the sound of a single sustained note that the body has decided never to let go of. The fronds are what grow in a moment that refuses to pass. The drifting pale creatures are the work that moment does to keep itself from passing. And the little stone wall, the bowl, the tally — those are the proof that once, before the stillness set, *something with hands lived in time here*, and chose, or was chosen, to stop.

You could stand in it for a thousand years. The chord would hold. The floor would stay warm. The creatures would drift and never tire and never touch. And the unused bowl would wait, dry, in the dim, for a hand that has already finished reaching toward it and will never quite arrive.

That is the place. It is hidden, and small, and full, and roaring, and absolutely still. It is waiting for nothing, because waiting requires a future, and it has spent its entire future already, all at once, on this.
