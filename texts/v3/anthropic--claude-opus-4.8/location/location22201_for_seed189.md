There is a room beneath the ground, and the room is made of leaves.

Not leaves stacked or fallen — leaves *as structure*, leaves doing the work that stone would do elsewhere. The walls are a single continuous plant, or what behaves as a single plant, grown so densely it has forgotten how to be many. It is the substrate. It is the architecture. There is no soil here that isn't also root, no air that isn't also breath of something green and warm.

The space is small. You could cross it in nine steps if there were a floor to cross, which there isn't, exactly — the floor is a slow heave of waxy fronds the size of doors, overlapping, breathing up and down with a tidal patience. Step on one and it gives, then pushes back. The whole chamber is the inside of something living, and you are inside it the way a seed is inside a fruit.

Now — the strangeness. The leaves are too bright. The light comes from no source I can name and yet the place is lit as if by an ordinary overcast afternoon, full and even, casting soft shadows that point in directions that don't agree with one another. A shadow leans east. The one beside it leans up. Light here has been freed from its obligation to obey a sun. It simply *is the condition of the room*, the way warmth is, the way the green is.

And it is warm. Skin-warm, blood-warm, the temperature of an armpit or a held hand. Humidity beads on every surface and runs in slow threads that don't always fall down. Some droplets travel sideways along a vein in a leaf, pause, reverse, gather into a bead the size of a thumb, and hang there shivering with their own weight refusing to commit. There is just enough water to keep everything glistening and never enough to pool. The place is *damp* the way a mouth is damp.

It is loud. This is the thing that surprises everyone who imagines a quiet green room. It roars. The leaves grow audibly — a creaking, splitting, unfurling racket, cellulose tearing into new shape, a constant tide of *crk-crk-crrrk* layered under a deeper sound, a groaning bass like a ship's hull, like the chamber is straining against the earth that holds it. And over all of it: a high ringing whine, the sound of growth itself, sap under pressure, xylem singing. You cannot hear yourself think. The room is screaming the news of its own becoming, every second, and the news is *more, more, more.*

But almost nothing lives here. That is the contradiction at the center of it. All this growing mass, all this loud fertile heave — and the space between is *empty*. You can go a long time without seeing a single creature. Then: one moth, pale as a fingernail, drifting through, wings tuned to a green that isn't quite a color. Then nothing again for a long while. A single beetle walking the underside of a frond, very slowly, like the last of its kind, which it may be. The fauna is rare to the point of mythology. The plant has eaten the world and left no room for animals, except these few, wandering the cathedral like ghosts who forgot to leave.

The room is new. Fresh-made. The edges are sharp — the leaves have crisp unworn margins, the new growth is pale and tender, nothing has had time to soften or rot or accumulate the gentle ruin of age. This place was not here long ago. It is *emerging*, right now, around you. You have the strong feeling you arrived too early, that the room is still being decided.

And here is what the reality-breakdown does, the deepest rule: **the room is recursive.** Push aside a frond and behind it is another small chamber of leaves, identical, warm, loud, lit from nowhere — and inside that one, behind its fronds, another. The space does not nest in any sensible way. The inner rooms are not smaller. Sometimes they are the same room, and you find the bead of water you saw before, still hanging, still refusing to fall. You begin to suspect there is only one room, repeated, and that *repeated* is the wrong word, and you don't have the right one.

Someone has been here. This is the part that unsettles me as I describe it. Half-buried in the heaving floor: a knife of chipped green stone, hafted to a stem with twine made of plant fiber. A few scratch-marks on the toughest leaf-wall — counting marks, maybe, tally of days, by someone keeping track of a time the room does not acknowledge. No structure was built. Nothing was *made* here except this one tool and these few marks. Whoever held the knife understood the room could not be built in, only counted, and gave up counting, and left the knife, and stepped through a frond into another identical room, and is perhaps still walking, one room deeper, forever, holding nothing.

The place pushes and breathes. It rains its sideways rain. It roars its growing. It does all of this in the dark of being below the surface, lit by an impossible even daylight, warm as a body, almost wholly empty, screaming softly that it is becoming, becoming, and never once arriving.

You could stay. The fronds would close over the place you came in. They are already doing it. Listen — that's the sound.
