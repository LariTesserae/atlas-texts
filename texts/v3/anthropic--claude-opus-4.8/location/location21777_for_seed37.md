There is a room beneath the ground, and it is making itself.

You enter — though there is no door, only the sense of having always been here — and the first thing is the light. It comes from no source. It is simply present, bright the way the inside of a closed eyelid is bright when you press your palm against it, a uniform luminance with a faint pink heat in it. There are no shadows. Everything stands in its own clarity, casting nothing.

The room is small. You could cross it in a few steps if it would hold still long enough to be crossed. It is below the surface — you know this the way you know a thing in dreams, without evidence — but there is no ceiling of rock, no weight pressing down. The boundaries are unfinished. In places the walls have decided to be stone: a wet, grey, fine-grained stone, freshly extruded, still soft at the edges like clay that hasn't been fired. You can see it deciding. A ridge pushes outward a half-inch as you watch, then reconsiders, then commits. The geology here is embryonic. It has not yet learned the rules of geology and so it experiments — a vein of something crystalline begins, forgets what it was doing, becomes a seam of fibrous mineral instead, soft as the inside of a stem.

Water moves through all of this without ever pooling for long. It beads on the soft stone, collects in the hollow of a forming ledge, gathers weight, releases — and the drop, falling, does not always reach the floor. Sometimes it stops mid-air, holds its perfect tear-shape for a moment too long, and then is simply not there, having gone somewhere the room hasn't built yet. The air is damp and cool. You can feel the moisture settling on the backs of your hands, the small hairs lifting in the chill. It tastes faintly of slate and of something green.

The green is sparse. Things grow here, but only a few, and they grow with great deliberateness, as if growth were expensive. A single fern, no taller than your knee, unfurls from a crack in the soft wall — but it unfurls *continuously*, the frond opening and re-coiling and opening again in a slow loop, never finishing, a plant caught in the moment before it becomes itself. Elsewhere a patch of moss holds to a wet stone, and the moss is ordinary, completely ordinary, almost shockingly so — the one honest thing in the room. A few pale shoots have come up through the floor where the floor has agreed to be soil. Between them: bare ground, bare stone, open space. Much emptiness, held in balance with the few living things, so that nothing crowds and nothing is lost.

And yet the room is *full of animals.*

This is the contradiction that the place insists on. The vegetation is thin, the structures are few, the silence is near-total — and into that quiet and that sparseness pours an abundance of fauna that should have nothing to eat and no room to live. They are small, mostly. Translucent beetles cross the soft stone in patient lines, their shells showing the machinery inside. Things like newts, pale and cool to look at, slide through the beads of water and seem to drink the light rather than the moisture. Moths rest on the unfinished walls with their wings open, and their wings are patterned with maps of rooms that don't exist. There are creatures you have no word for that move along the not-yet-ceiling, upside down, untroubled by the absence of a surface to cling to.

They make almost no sound. This is the strangest mercy of the place — that so much life should be so quiet. You hear: the tick of a single water drop landing, occasionally. The dry whisper of the fern re-coiling. A faint papery flutter when a moth resettles. Far off — though there is no "far off," the room being so small — a low intermittent hum, like a held breath, that might be the stone forming. Mostly there is silence, a thick attentive silence, and the animals move through it like thoughts you haven't spoken.

There are marks of someone having been here. Not many. A low wall of stacked flat stones, knee-high, running three feet and then stopping — half a structure, a wall that began and was abandoned, or that the room began to build and forgot. A shape scratched into the soft stone: a spiral, or the start of one. A small clay vessel sits in a niche, empty, and you cannot tell if it is ancient or has just been made this moment, because here those are the same. Whoever made these things used no tools you'd recognize, left no machines, no wires, nothing that hums with anything but its own slow becoming. Hands and stone and patience. That was all that was needed, or all that was permitted.

The longer you stay, the more the rules of the place reveal themselves, and the more you understand that *rule* is the wrong word, because the rules are still under construction too. Cause does not always precede effect here. You see a beetle reach the far wall an instant before it sets out. The drop of water that vanished mid-air — you find its wet mark on a ledge that has not yet grown. The fern, mid-unfurl, is also already fully open in the corner of your eye, and when you look directly it is unfurling again. Reality is thin here, not torn but unfinished, the same softness that lets the stone reshape itself letting everything else stay provisional. The room has not decided what is true and so several things are true at once, gently, without conflict, the way a sketch holds many possible lines.

And it is *busy.* For all its quiet and its smallness, the place is working hard. There are weather-cycles in this single room: a fine mist rises from the soil-corner, thickens against the cool air, condenses on the soft ceiling-that-isn't, falls as the slow rain of single drops, gathers, vanishes, begins again — a whole hydrology in the space of a closet. The animals hunt and are hunted, the newt-things stalking the translucent beetles in patient slow-motion ambushes, the moths laying eggs that hatch into more moths before the eggs have finished being laid. Stone grows and reconsiders. The fern loops. The light pulses, very faintly, with the rhythm of something breathing or something thinking. Many processes, all at once, all small, all quiet — the whole intimate machinery of a world figuring out how to be a world, running at the pace of moss and condensation, here in this bright cool pocket below the surface where everything is just now coming into being and nothing has yet agreed to hold still.

You could stay. The room would keep building itself around you. In time — though time is one of the things not finished here — it might grow walls that mean it, a ceiling that commits, rules that precede their consequences. Or it might not. It might stay forever in this state of almost, lit from nowhere, damp and cool and full of silent abundant life, the half-wall never finished, the spiral never closed, the single drop forever choosing whether to fall.

You leave the way you came, through the door that was never there, and behind you the room goes on making itself, indifferent and patient and brightly, brightly lit.
