# The High Country, Unfinished

Start at the top, because there is almost nothing but top here. This is a place of extreme elevation — not a peak but a whole country lifted, a tableland thrust so high that the air thins to a cold burning thread, and yet the rock itself is warm. You can feel it through your soles: a held heat, the heat of something still forming, coming up from below as if the world here has not yet finished cooling into its shape.

There is no water. Not frozen, not hidden — simply absent. The dryness is total and clarifying. Things do not rot here, do not bead or seep. Dust does not clump. When you exhale, no fog forms; the breath vanishes into the dark thin air without a trace.

And it is dark. Not night exactly — the light is wrong for night. There is a deep ember-glow that has no clear source, a dull red-bronze the color of metal cooling, pooling along the bases of things and leaving the heights in shadow. You see by it the way you'd see by the last coals of a fire across a wide room: enough to move, not enough to trust.

---

Now the strange part, the part that takes a while to settle into the eye:

The ground is not finished being ground.

Erosion here is at zero — or rather, it is at the *beginning* of zero, the moment before weather has touched anything. Every surface is raw. The rock has edges that have never been softened, faces sheared clean and sharp as fresh-broken obsidian, crystalline planes catching that bronze ember-light and throwing it back in hard little glints. There are no rounded stones. No sand drifts, no talus slopes, no scree worn down by ten thousand years of frost. Nothing has been *handled* by time yet. You are standing in geology so young it is still raw-faced, still announcing itself.

But — and this is where the place reveals what it really is — it is *changing anyway.*

The dynamic here is enormous, almost the highest it can be, and it expresses itself not through erosion (there is no water, no wind worth the name, nothing to wear) but through *upheaval.* The land builds itself. Crystals push up out of the ground as you watch — slow at the edge of vision, then suddenly a hand-span taller. Ridges shoulder upward with a sound like a great slow grinding of teeth. Plates of warm mineral tilt, slide, lock against each other. A spire that was not there when you arrived now stands at your left, faceted, gleaming dully, faintly warm to the touch. The world is *coming into being* faster than it can settle, and so it is permanently unsettled — turbulent not with storm but with *creation*, an endless raw making.

The ground is quiet about it, though. That's the uncanny thing. All this churning and rising and locking happens *mostly in silence*. A grind, a tick, a long low groan from somewhere underfoot, then stillness. Then a sharp *crack* as a new crystal face cleaves open in the dark. Then quiet again. You learn to listen for the silence ending.

---

Across this — and "across" means *far*, this is open country, a region you could walk in for days — there is the suggestion of something that was not made by the land.

Marks. Small built things, scattered very thin.

A low wall, three stones high, dry-stacked, on a saddle between two rising ridges. Who stacked it? There is no one. There has never been anyone — no animal track, no plant, no tool left lying. And yet the wall is deliberate, the stones chosen, fitted, placed by something that understood walls. It is half-buried now, not by erosion but by the *growing* ground swallowing it from beneath, crystal faces crowding up around its base.

Farther on: a cairn. A doorway-shaped gap in a natural rock face, the lintel placed by hand. A single standing stone, perfectly upright, that the upheaving land has somehow kept upright, tilting the whole plate beneath it to compensate, as though the world were trying to *honor* the thing.

The structures are too few to be a settlement and too intentional to be accident. They feel like punctuation in a sentence nobody is speaking. The likeliest reading — and you'll resist it, but it keeps returning — is that the land *itself* makes them. That somewhere in all this raw upward making, the rock occasionally builds a wall, a door, a marker, the way a freezing window builds ferns: a pattern that *looks* like intention because intention is one of the shapes that complexity falls into. The place is rehearsing the idea of builders before any builders exist.

---

There is the faintest life. Not plants, not animals — something simpler and stranger, clinging to the warm faces of the youngest rock: a film, a bloom, barely there. In the bronze dark it shows only as a slightly different texture, a matte breath across the glassy mineral, here and there a patch the dull green of old verdigris. It is not photosynthesizing — there is hardly any light. It eats the warmth, or eats the chemistry of the raw rock itself, the fresh-broken surfaces offering up whatever they offer. Where the land cleaves open a new face, within an hour or a day the film finds it. It is the first thing, the very first thing, arriving in a world still under construction. It makes no sound. It asks for nothing but new stone, and the world is making nothing but new stone.

---

Stand still a while and the rules of the place arrange themselves:

Everything is high, and warm from beneath, and dark, and dry. Nothing wears down; everything builds up. The silence is the resting state and the sound is the exception — a grind, a crack, the soft groan of plates resettling, then quiet pressing back in over all of it like the dark pressing back over the ember-light. There is no water to carry anything anywhere, so nothing travels, nothing pools, nothing flows; things simply *rise where they are.* And scattered across the open reach of it stand those few made things — walls, doors, markers — that nobody made, the land's own first stuttering attempts at meaning, half-eaten already by the next thing rising.

It is a country at its own dawn. Not the dawn of light — the dawn of *form.* You are very early. You have arrived before almost everything. The world has only just begun deciding what it will be, and it has not decided, and the not-deciding is the entire turbulent business of the place: a high dark warm dry tableland, endlessly building, endlessly raw, faintly green at its newest edges, throwing up walls to no one under a light like the inside of a furnace going cold.
