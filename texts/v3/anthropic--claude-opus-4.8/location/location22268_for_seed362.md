# The Cellulose Engine

There is no water here, and there never was. What grows, grows on the chemistry of light and dry minerals and a sap that is not water but something thicker, oilier, a clear resin that moves through the world like blood through a body too large to see the edges of.

Start with the fact that everything is made. Not built in the way of brick and mortar — *grown into building.* The plants did this. Across a span so wide it has its own latitudes, its own dry seasons drifting like fronts of pollen-weather, the vegetation has become the only substrate. There is no soil beneath it. There is more plant. You dig down through fibrous floor expecting bedrock and find lignified beams, then root-cable, then the woody packed mass of older growth pressed to the hardness of stone — and below that, more. It is plant all the way down. The world is a single continuous tissue that has been alive so long it forgot how to be anything but architecture.

## Terrain

Walk it and you walk through *rooms.* The growth has self-organized into chambers — cells in the biological sense, swollen and stiffened, walls of green-gold fiber arching into vaults. Some are small enough to crouch in. Some are cathedral-vast, their ceilings lost in a bright haze. The walls are not decorated; the walls are the organism. Veins run through them carrying that clear resin in slow pulses you can feel as warmth against your palm.

The floor undulates — low elevation, gentle, an established and weathered topography of long-settled mounds. Nothing here is sharp anymore. The edges have rounded the way old stairs round under centuries of feet, except no feet did this. Time did it. The plant grew, and grew over its own growing, and the seams smoothed, and now the whole continent of tissue sits in a kind of balance, neither expanding fast nor dying back, breathing at the pace of geology.

It is cool. A dry, even coolness, like the inside of a sealed greenhouse at dawn before the sun loads it with heat. The resin keeps a steady temperature. The plant is thermostatic; it has been managing its own climate for so long the management has become unconscious, autonomic, a thing it does the way you do not decide to keep your blood at temperature.

## Light

And it is *bright.* This is the strangeness that hits first. There is no sun visible — no sky in the ordinary sense — but the tissue itself is luminous. The fiber walls glow with a steady photosynthetic light, a pale gold-green radiance pushed out from within, as though the plant runs its metabolism backward and *emits* the light it once would have eaten. You cast no clear shadow because the light comes from everywhere — from the floor, the walls, the high vaults, the pollen-haze that drifts in the larger chambers. Brightness without a source. Brightness that has soaked into the place so thoroughly that "day" and "night" are meaningless words. There is only the glow, and it does not dim.

## The Industry

Here is where it tips. The plant *manufactures.*

Deep in the larger chambers stand structures the growth has formed that can only be called engines. Not metal — there is no metal — but lignified, resin-cooled apparatus: great pumping organs the size of houses, pistoning slowly, driving the clear sap through trunk-pipes thick as towers. There are gland-factories where the tissue secretes, in scaled and repeating production, identical objects: seed-cases, fiber-spools, hard waxy plates stamped out by the thousand from rows of synchronized pores. Assembly happening. Output accumulating in drifts.

But here is the question no one in the place can answer, because there is almost no one: *what is it making, and for whom?* The factories run. The product piles. Nothing comes to collect it. The plant industrializes the way a coral reef builds — without a foreman, without a plan you could find — except a reef does not stamp out ten thousand identical parts and stack them in waiting bays. This does. It is mechanized. It is electrified, even — faint currents run the resin, and you can hear, if you hold still, the tick of charge accumulating somewhere and discharging in soft blue flickers along the vein-lines.

## Sound

Mostly there is silence. This is a quiet world, almost a hushed one. The pumping organs move without grinding — resin-lubricated, ancient, frictionless. The factories produce without clamor. What sound exists is small and intimate: the tick of static, the slow creak of fiber settling, a sound like a held breath in a wide hall, the occasional papery slide of a finished object dropping onto its pile. You become aware of your own footsteps as an intrusion, loud as gunshots against all this competent quiet.

## Fauna

Animals are rare and they are wrong.

You will go a long way and see nothing alive that is not the plant itself. Then you'll find one — something small, moving through a chamber, and you'll realize it isn't moving *across* the floor but *out of* it, a creature the plant has grown that has detached and begun to walk, fauna budded from flora, a thing of fiber and resin with the gait of an animal and no clear reason to exist. They do not eat; there is nothing to eat that isn't them. They do not seem to reproduce. They wander the chambers as if patrolling, or as if lost, or as if performing some function the plant intended and forgot. They are the plant's animals. When one stops moving it sinks back into the floor and is reabsorbed, smoothed over, gone.

## The Breakdown

The weirdness is high, and you feel it as a steady wrongness in the rules.

Distances do not stay fixed. A chamber you crossed in twenty steps takes two hundred to recross. The repeating products of the factories are *too* identical — not similar but the same object, as though the plant is not making copies but making *one thing many times*, the same instant of manufacture stamped over and over into a place where time has come loose. You set your hand on a wall and feel your own pulse answered back through the resin, slightly delayed, slightly wrong, as if the plant is keeping a record of you and replaying it a half-second late.

The light has begun, in places, to behave like liquid — pooling in the low rooms, thicker there, harder to wade through. The brightness lags behind events. You'll move and your illumination arrives after you, a smear of glow catching up.

And everywhere is the sense that the whole vast tissue is a single thought thinking itself, very slowly, in a language of growth and manufacture — that the engines and the budded animals and the stamped identical seed-cases are not products but *vocabulary*, that the plant has been trying to say one enormous sentence for so long that the saying has become the world. It is cool, and it is bright, and it is nearly silent, and it makes and makes and makes, and what it is making is the long single utterance of something that became too large to ever finish a word.

You are inside the speech. The brightness does not dim. The pumps keep their patient time. Somewhere ahead, very quietly, another finished object drops onto its pile, and the floor smooths over your footprints behind you as if you, too, were something it grew and means, eventually, to reabsorb.
