There is a place that has not finished being born, and may never finish, and does not seem to mind.

Begin at the level of the foot, because that is where the strangeness first announces itself. You expect ground. Instead you get a surface that gives — not mud exactly, though there is mud, but a tissue of living things layered so thick that the soil beneath is a rumor. Root and rootlet, runner, the soft red hair of new moss, the white threads of fungus knitting through it all, a litter of leaves still half-green because nothing here gets the time to brown. Press your hand against it and warmth comes up, the steady blood-heat of a body. The whole floor is warm. The whole world is warm, the kind of heat that does not vary between noon and the small hours, a fever held at exactly its peak and never breaking.

The light is poor and even. No shafts, no dappling, because the canopy is too deep for any single beam to survive the descent. What arrives at the ground has been filtered through forty meters of leaf and is the color of weak tea held to a candle — a brown-gold dimness in which everything is legible but nothing is bright. You will not see a shadow with a hard edge here. There are no hard edges. The air itself is half water; it hangs in the spaces between trunks as visible suspension, and when you breathe it you are nearly drinking.

Now look up, slowly, because the verticality is the first real fact of the place. The trees — if trees — go up in tiers, and each tier is its own country. The lowest is a tangle of broad-leaved things, fleshy, dripping, leaves the size of doors held out flat to catch whatever light falls. Above that a story of slimmer columns, and above that another, and the canopy proper somewhere overhead as a ceiling you sense more than see. Lianas rope between the levels. Epiphytes ride the branches — gardens growing on gardens, ferns nested in the crooks of limbs, small pools of standing water cupped in the leaf-rosettes far above your head, each pool its own pond with its own tenants. Density here is not a measurement. It is the medium. There is no volume of air in this place that does not have something alive in it or reaching across it.

But the land is young. This is the thing that takes a while to register, and once it registers you cannot stop seeing it. There are no ravines. No worn channels, no rounded stones, no cliff faces sculpted by patient centuries of water. The rock, where it shows through the living carpet, is raw — sharp-shouldered, freshly extruded, the crystalline faces still unblunted. The few streams run not in beds they have carved but in the accidental low places between roots, finding their way fresh each time, undecided. Water lies everywhere near the surface, seeping up between the toes of the trees, weeping from the unworn rock in beads that gather and fall and gather again. Nothing has had time to be ground down. The place is geologically an infant wearing the full forest of an elder — a contradiction it carries without strain, as though the vegetation arrived all at once, fully grown, onto stone that is still warm from its making.

And the sound. The sound is the second great fact, after the verticality, and it does not stop. It is loud here — not the loudness of storm or machine, but the loudness of multitude. Every layer of the forest is voiced. There is a low continuous drone under everything, the collective hum of more insects than arithmetic can hold, and over it a ringing whine that rises and falls in slow tides, and over that the calls — chirrs, whistles, a wet repeated knocking, long descending cries, a sound like a stone dropped in water repeated a thousand times a minute from a thousand throats. You cannot locate any single source. The forest sings as a body. The strange part is that it never changes. Hour to hour, the chorus holds the same density, the same pitch-band, the same restless steady volume. There is no dawn swell, no dusk hush, because there is no dawn and no dusk that you can detect — only the unvarying dim, the unvarying heat, the unvarying flood of voices. Time passes and nothing in the equilibrium shifts.

The animals overwhelm. This is not metaphor. You feel watched and you are watched, by eyes at every elevation. Things move at the edge of the tea-colored sight — the slither of something pale around a trunk, the sudden close beat of wings, a clustering of small hot bodies in the leaf-litter that parts and reforms as you step. The pools in the high leaf-rosettes are loud with tadpoles of creatures that will never need to come down. The air itself flickers with flight. There are more presences than you could ever name or count, and they are not afraid of you, because nothing here has learned to be — there has been no time, in this young place, for fear to evolve, no history of predation old enough to teach caution. They regard you with the flat curiosity of things that have never been hunted and never hunted you.

Here is the rule of this world, the thing that holds the parameters together: it is a place of enormous life on land too new to have been shaped by it. The forest did not grow here over ages, wearing the rock down as it went, leaving the marks of its long tenancy. It is somehow present in full abundance on ground that bears no record of any past at all. Cause and consequence have come unstuck. The abundance does not age the land; the land does not constrain the abundance; the heat does not break into season; the chorus does not rise or fall. Everything is at maximum and nothing moves toward anything else. It is a still photograph of teeming — a single held instant of riot, extended without end.

You could stay a long while and learn nothing new, because there is nothing new to learn — only the same warm dim, the same dripping green, the same unbroken voice of a million small lives, on stone that has not yet decided what shape it will be, and has stopped trying, and lets the forest decide for it.
