# The Terraces of the Shallow Water

You come up onto it from below, always from below, because there is no other way the land allows. The plateau sits high enough that the air thins to a clean, mineral sharpness, and the light arrives without apology — a hard, generous brightness that lays itself flat across everything and leaves almost no shadow. At this elevation the sun feels close. It is not. But it feels close.

The first thing you notice is the silence. Not absence — *restraint*. The wind moves but does not howl. It slides over stone that has been worn so smooth, over so many ages, that there is nothing left for it to whistle through. You can hear your own pulse up here before you hear anything the world is doing.

## The Stone

The terrain is terraced — wide, shallow steps of pale rock running in long curved tiers, like the contour lines of a map made physical. They were not built. Or rather: they were not built by hands. The erosion here is old and finished, a settled geometry. Water and time argued for an epoch and reached an agreement, and these terraces are the treaty.

The stone is the color of bone left in sun, with veins of a darker, faintly iridescent material threading through it — and this is where the place begins to lie about itself. Look closely and the dark veins are not mineral. They are *structured*. At the scale of a fingertip they resolve into lattices too regular to be crystal, too purposeful, geometries that the eye keeps trying to finish and cannot. The veins run through the whole plateau like a circulatory system that long ago stopped pumping anything you could name. Touch one and it is faintly warm, warmer than the rock around it, the warmth of something idling rather than dead.

## The Water

Water lives here in pockets and pauses. There is no river, no lake — but each terrace holds a thin film of it, a sheen no deeper than a coin, caught in the basins the stone offers. In the bright air this water does an odd thing: it does not simply evaporate. It rises in slow, coherent columns — threads of mist that lift straight up in the windless pockets, hold their shape for a while like standing reeds made of vapor, and then disperse. At dawn the whole plateau wears these mist-reeds, hundreds of them, swaying very slightly, and the silence makes them seem like something breathing.

Where the dark veins cross a water-basin, the moisture beads strangely, refusing to wet the surface, gathering into perfect spheres that roll and rest and catch the light like dropped beads of mercury that are nonetheless only water. The animals know which basins are which. They drink from the ordinary ones.

## The Living Things

And there are many animals — this is the surprise of the place. For all its emptiness of plants, the plateau teems. The vegetation is sparse: low cushion-growths in the lee of each terrace step, gray-green and tough, hoarding their moisture; and a scattering of tall single stalks, widely spaced, each one crowned with a pale seed-head that turns — slowly, deliberately — to track the warmth across the sky. Sparse, but the fauna do not seem to need it. They graze on something else.

There are the small ones first: pale, six-limbed creatures the size of a hand, who move in long quiet lines along the terrace edges and *feed on the standing mist*, sipping the vapor-reeds, leaving little gaps in them like bites taken from a curtain. They make almost no sound. A faint tick of claw on stone, nothing more.

Larger grazers move on the lower tiers — long-legged, thin, their hides the exact pale gold of the rock so that a still herd vanishes entirely and a moving one seems like the ground itself shifting. They crop the cushion-plants and they lick the warm dark veins, and there is reason to think the warmth, the idling energy in those veins, is part of what sustains them. They are, in some sense, plugged in.

And there are hunters. You see one only as a ripple of brightness — something that bends the light around itself, a predator that does not hide in shadow but in *glare*, becoming a smear of excess sun. The grazers scatter from it in total silence, which is the most unnerving thing of all: a stampede with no thunder, just a sudden geometric reorganization of golden shapes, and then stillness again, and one fewer animal.

## The Marks of Makers

Scattered across the terraces, far apart, stand the structures. They are small. A doorway of the dark lattice-material framing nothing, standing alone on an open step. A low dome, half-sunk, its surface continuous with the rock as though grown rather than placed. A single slender spire on the highest tier, perfectly intact, casting in this near-shadowless light only the faintest gray ghost at its foot.

These are the work of whatever ran the veins. The technology here is not machinery in any sense you'd recognize — there are no seams, no panels, no controls. The makers worked at the scale of the stone's own structure, editing the material itself, persuading rock to hold information and warmth and slow patient function across spans of time longer than species last. The terraces themselves may be the largest artifact. The mist that stands up straight, the water that beads where the veins cross, the predator that bends light — these may all be the deep residue of an engineering so thorough it became indistinguishable from ecology. The makers did not build *on* the world. They edited it down to its grammar, and then left.

What they left runs still, quietly, at low power, sustaining a thin teeming life that no longer knows it is living inside a machine.

## The Atmosphere

By midday the brightness is almost a substance you could lean on. The mist-reeds have mostly lifted away; the basins shrink; the seed-stalks have all turned to face the high sun in silent agreement. The grazers find shade in the lee of the terrace steps and go motionless. The whole plateau enters a held breath of stillness and heat and that flat, total light.

Then, in the afternoon, the rain — when it comes, and it comes irregularly, a few times across the cycle — arrives without storm. No wind, no thunder. The sky simply grows a soft ceiling, and water falls straight down, warm, in a hush so complete you hear each drop find its terrace. The dark veins drink first and brighten as they drink, glowing a low ember-red for a while afterward, and during that glow the animals gather close to them, and the mist rises thick, and for an hour the plateau is luminous and breathing and almost — almost — full. Then it dries, and quiets, and empties back toward its settled balance.

It is a place at equilibrium. It has been at equilibrium for a very long time. Its makers built it to last past memory, and it has, and it will, holding its warm and silent and sparsely crowded life in the high clean light, balanced forever between abundance and emptiness, between the living and the made.

You will leave the way you came. From below. The plateau does not notice you go.
