There is a city growing inside a single leaf, and the leaf has not finished happening yet.

Start with the dark, because the dark is the medium here, the way water is the medium for fish. It isn't the absence of light — it's a substance, cool and faintly green, the green you'd get if you closed your eyes inside a forest at dusk and pressed gently on the lids. Things move through this dark the way they'd move through deep water: slowly, with intention, displacing it.

The substrate is plant. Not soil with plants in it — plant all the way down, all the way out, plant as the ground and the walls and the ceiling that isn't quite a ceiling. You walk on a floor of compressed stem-matter, springy, slightly warm where your foot has been a moment ago and cool everywhere else. Pillars rise — and these are the built things, this is the urban part — but they are pillars the way a vein is a pillar. Someone has been constructing here. There are right angles. There are streets laid in a grid, gutters cut for water, the clear logic of a settlement that wanted to be a city. But the material kept being alive, and the right angles have begun to round, the gutters to pulse, the grid to lean toward the light that isn't coming.

It is cool. Damp without raining. Moisture collects on the undersides of the great arching ribs overhead — these are the leaf's veins, or the city's ducts, the distinction has eroded — and falls in slow single drops that you hear before you feel. *Tk.* A long wait. *Tk.* This is most of the sound. That, and a low circulatory hum somewhere below the floor, an industrial sound, a sound of pumps and engines that were installed by people who are no longer here, still running because nobody told them the works had been abandoned, or because the works have learned to run themselves. The machinery and the vegetation have reached an agreement. Pistons push sap. Flywheels turn on axles of hardened cellulose. The factory metabolizes.

Now the strangeness, because the parameters say *reality breakdown* and they are right. The place is small — a grove, a room, a hamlet you could cross in twenty minutes — and yet it does not end where it should. Walk to the edge of the city and the city is in front of you again, slightly altered, a version where the drop fell a half-second sooner. The leaf has a finite area but infinite interior, because it is still *emerging*, still being drawn, and the parts that haven't been decided yet simply repeat the parts that have, like a sketch where the artist has only roughed in one corner and copied it to fill the page. The undeveloped raw geology — the not-yet-formed rock beneath — shows through in places as a gray nothing, a held breath, a region where the ground hasn't agreed to exist and your foot meets a resistance like cold honey, neither floor nor fall.

Almost nothing lives in any given volume. This is the emptiness — vast hollow naves of green-black air, streets where you could walk for an hour and meet no one. And yet it teems. The fauna are abundant, but spread so thin across so much space that abundance reads as solitude. They come one at a time out of the dark.

What lives here: things shaped by the rule that the dark is water. Soft luminous animals, slow as jellyfish, that drift down the avenues at the height of a streetlamp and were perhaps mistaken, once, for streetlamps. Beetles the size of dogs that walk the pipework and oil the joints with secretions, in unspoken partnership with the dead factory. Birds that have given up flight because there is no sky, only more ceiling, and instead pull themselves hand over hand along the veins, calling once — a single wet note — every few minutes, so that crossing the city you hear them the way you'd hear lighthouses, each one marking a distance. Things that eat the moisture. Things that eat the dropped light of the drifting jellyfish-lamps. A whole food chain folded into the cool dark, predator and prey passing in the empty streets and acknowledging each other and moving on, because there is room, there is so much room, no need to hurry.

And it is active, all of it, in slow biological time. The leaf grows at its unfinished edge — you can watch a new street extrude itself over the course of an afternoon, cells dividing into cobblestones, the dark pouring in to fill the new rooms before anything else can. Drops gather and fall. The machinery breathes. The animals make their long migrations from one pool of condensation to the next. Somewhere the not-yet-real corner of the world is being decided, and when it settles it will look exactly like the corner you are standing in, and you will not be able to tell whether you have walked in a circle or whether the place has simply copied you, too, into the part of itself it had not yet imagined.

The rule of this world, if it has one: *nothing is finished, and so nothing can end.* The city was built and abandoned and is still being built. The leaf is dying and unfolding at once. You arrived here recently and you have always been here, both, because your arrival is one of the things the emerging edge has not yet decided.

It is very quiet. *Tk.* You wait. *Tk.* The dark moves past you like a slow tide, cool against the skin, smelling of green water and machine oil and something growing. Far down an avenue, a lamp that is an animal turns and begins, without hurry, to drift toward you.
