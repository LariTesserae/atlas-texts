# The Sump-City of Hollow Carradine

There is a city the size of a cathedral organ, and it lives inside a flooded chamber that no surface map admits exists.

You reach it the way water reaches it: downward, always downward, through fissures in a bedrock that was warm to the touch even before the machines came. The chamber is small — that is the first thing to understand. Not small like a room, but small like a held breath. You could swim its length in the time it takes to drown. And yet it is a *city*, packed with the verticality and stubbornness of cities, built up and down and through itself until the rock can barely be seen behind the iron.

## The Water

It is not clean water. It is the warm, mineral-thick liquid of a deep place that has been heated from below for longer than the word "below" has had meaning. Two-and-a-half parts water out of three: the chamber is *submerged*, fully, floor to vaulted ceiling. There is no air pocket, no surface to break. The city was built underwater and is meant to be lived in underwater, which raises questions about its builders that the city does not answer.

The water carries things. Iron in suspension, so that it tastes of blood and old coins. Heat that rises in slow plumes from cracks in the floor, bending the light, making the far walls swim and ripple as though the whole place were seen through a flawed lens. When you move, the water resists you with the thickness of syrup near the vents, then thins to ordinary cold in the upper galleries.

## What It's Made Of

Mineral and metal. No plants — none, almost none. The half-measure of vegetation here is not green at all: it is a slick of something *fungal-or-simpler*, a black-brown felt of chemosynthetic mat that coats the lower pipes where the heat is richest. It does not photosynthesize because there is nothing to photosynthesize. It eats the iron and the sulphur and the warmth, and it is the only living carpet this world has ever grown.

Everything else is built. Riveted plate. Cast pipe. Towers of bolted scaffold that lean into one another like drunks, established by long settling into their own balance — *erosion at nearly two*, meaning the city has weathered, slumped, found its slouching equilibrium. Rust grows on it the way moss grows elsewhere, in soft orange shelves and beards. Where a beam has corroded through, the city has simply grown a new beam beside the wound and left the old one hanging. Nothing is repaired. Everything is *accreted*.

## The Sound

Here is where the place announces itself. The sound is nearly total — 2.9 out of 3, *deafening* — and it is the sound of machinery running underwater.

Imagine the deepest note an organ can make, sustained, and then imagine it is not music but the working of pistons the size of houses, transmitted through liquid, which carries low frequency the way no air ever could. The whole chamber is a resonant body. The pumps boom. The flywheels grind. There is a percussion in it, a clanging hammer-rhythm from somewhere in the city's gut that never breaks tempo and never quite repeats. And under all of it, the standing drone — the chord the city makes simply by *being*, a vibration you feel in the sternum and the teeth before you hear it as sound at all.

You cannot speak here. Speech is a foolishness. The water is too loud, and the loudness is too constant. The inhabitants — if there are inhabitants — would have learned another way.

## The Light

Dim. Not dark — *dim*. The light is industrial and orange, the smoldering glow of furnaces seen through portholes, the dull red of metal kept just below the temperature where it would soften. It does not come from above; there is no above. It comes from the works themselves, leaking out through grates and seams, so that the city is lit by its own labor. Plumes of heated water shimmer in front of the lights and break them into shuddering ribbons. Visibility is perhaps a dozen meters, and then the orange fades to brown fades to the black that fills the empty volume.

## The Emptiness

This is the strange thing — the contradiction the place is built to hold. The city is *industrial*, *urban*, dense with construction, layered and bolted and crammed. And yet the chamber it occupies is *empty*. Density: 0.3. Near-void.

How? Because the city does not fill its chamber. It hangs in the middle of it. A dense knot of riveted construction, suspended in the center of a vast warm dark, anchored by cables and pipe-runs that stretch away into the murk and vanish before they reach a wall. Swim away from the city in any direction and you find: nothing. Water. Heat. The fading drone. The orange glow shrinking to a coal behind you. The chamber may be a hundred times the city's size, and all of it but the central knot is *hollow*.

The machines pump, and pump, and pump — but they pump only for themselves, into themselves, a closed circulation. The vast surrounding water is not used. It simply holds the city, the way a hand holds a single ember.

## The Living Things

Rare. Fauna at 0.7. There are things in the warm water, but you will see one and then nothing for a long while.

Blind things, mostly. Pale, soft, built for pressure. A creature like an eel that has no eyes and follows the heat-plumes by the bending of the water against its flanks. Something that grazes the chemosynthetic felt with a mouth like a rasp — the city's only true grazer, the apex consumer of a food web one link long. And, more rarely, a thing that *clings* to the outermost cables, far out in the empty dark, and seems to be listening to the city's drone, turning toward changes in it the way a flower turns toward sun. No one knows what it hears. No one knows if "hear" is the word.

## The Rules

The water carries sound and heat but not voices. So the city does not speak; the city *resonates*. Meaning travels in the changes of the drone — a pump throttled down, a flywheel braked, a hammer-rhythm syncopated for three beats and resumed. Whoever ran this place ran it as a single instrument, and to live here was to read the chord.

But here is the turbulence — *dynamic at 2.6*, the place chaotic and unstable. The chord is breaking. Something deep in the city has slipped its tempo. The pumps surge and stall. Plumes of superheated water erupt where there were none a moment ago, scalding the felt to gray ash, and then go cold. Whole galleries flood with grinding noise and then fall to a silence so sudden it rings. The city is *industrial* but it is no longer *governed*. It runs on, faster and slower, a machine playing music with no hand on the keys, and the chamber around it pulses with the changes — warm, then warmer, then a stuttering cold — as the closed circulation that kept it stable for so long begins, finally, after its long established age, to come apart.

The single eyeless grazer flicks away from a vent that has gone wrong. The listening thing far out in the dark turns and turns, reading a chord that no longer means anything, waiting for the note that will tell it what to do, in a language the city has forgotten it was speaking.

And the drone goes on. Deafening. Beautiful. Wrong.

A held breath, in a warm dark, learning at last how to let go.
