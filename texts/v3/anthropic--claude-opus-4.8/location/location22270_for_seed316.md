# The Reiterating Plain

Begin at the water, because everything here begins at the water.

It is not a sea and not a swamp but the worn-down memory of both, spread flat to the horizon and past it — a sheet of warm standing water perhaps a hand's-depth deep, perhaps fathomless, the distinction having long ago stopped mattering. The land beneath it is the oldest land there is. Mountains lived and died here. You are walking across their dust, pressed into a clay so fine and so ancient it has forgotten how to be anything but level. Erosion has done its full work and then kept working out of habit, smoothing the smooth, flattening the flat, until the plain achieved a kind of geological exhaustion — the final stable state, the place a world arrives at when it has nowhere left to fall.

And yet it will not hold still.

That is the first contradiction you have to swallow whole, standing here with the warm water lapping your ankles: the most finished landscape imaginable, churning. The stillness is only of *form*. Everything else seethes.

---

The water is packed. That is the word the parameters insist on and the word the place earns. There is no room between presences. The sheet of water is so densely inhabited by *something* — call it potential, call it the half-living film that warm shallow water grows when it is left alone for an age — that to wade through it is to push through a crowd. You feel the water resist not like liquid but like a packed market, bodies that aren't there yielding around you, closing behind. The surface is skinned over with a slick of microbial felt, green-brown, breathing. Where your foot disturbs it, the skin tears and reseals in seconds, and in the tearing it makes a sound.

The sound. You must understand that this place is deafening and that the deafening has no single source. The skin reseals with a sound like applause heard through a wall. The water, agitated everywhere at once by a wind you cannot feel, slaps itself into a roar that has no rhythm and never stops — a flat white wall of noise that you stop hearing as noise within an hour and start hearing as the absence of silence, a pressure on the inner ear, the world holding its breath by exhaling forever. Underneath it: low concussions, felt in the chest, that might be distant water-collapse or might be the plain itself settling another micron deeper into its final shape.

---

The light is dim and the dimness is *warm*, which should not work and does. There is no sun visible. The sky is a low ceiling of vapor, the whole atmosphere condensing and falling and rising and condensing — weather of its own, generated by the plain's own heat and breath, a closed weather, a planet's worth of recycled water never quite committing to rain. The light comes through this ceiling already used, sieved to a brown-gold dusk that does not change. There is no time of day. There is only the perpetual hour before a storm that has been arriving for ten million years.

In this light the few plants stand up like punctuation.

They are sparse — that is the mercy of the place, that the vegetation is sparse, because what vegetation there is, is wrong. Reeds, mostly, or the idea of reeds: tall pale stalks rising from the water in scattered congregations, never crowding, each clump separated from the next by long stretches of bare shining water. They are warm to the touch and they are not all there. This is where the weirdness lives. Look directly at a reed and it is a reed. Look at the field of them, take in the whole scatter at once, and you notice that they are *repeating* — that the clump fifty paces left is the same clump as the one at your hand, same bend, same notch, same single dead frond — and that the plain is tiled with copies, a finite landscape iterated across an infinite extent. The vastness is achieved not by size but by repetition. You walk for a day and pass the same reed-clump four thousand times. You are not lost. There is nowhere to be lost *from*. The place is large enough to have its own physics and the physics it chose is recurrence.

---

The fauna is rare and you will be glad of it.

Something moves across the water on legs, far off, at the edge of the dim. It is tall and thin and it walks the way a heron walks, with that deliberate lifting and placing — but it does not get closer and does not get farther, it simply walks, parallel to you, always at the same distance, a companion you did not ask for and cannot lose. There may be one of it. There may be one of it repeated, like the reeds. You will never resolve this. Once in a long while something surfaces near you, breaks the breathing skin of the water, shows a back, an eye, a wrongness, and submerges, and the water reseals with its wall-muffled applause. The animals here are rare because the place can barely afford them. Each one is a small violent miracle of difference in a world that has settled into repetition. They are the last fauna of a finished world, and they know it, and they keep their distance from each other and from you.

---

The built things are scattered and they are the saddest things in the landscape.

Posts. Stone posts, mostly, worn to nubs, standing in the water at intervals that once meant something — a path, a boundary, a counting. Someone walked here when the place was newer and drove markers into the clay, and the markers have outlasted every memory of their purpose. Here and there a low wall, three courses of fitted stone, going nowhere, holding nothing back. Once, a doorway: two uprights and a lintel, standing free in the open water, framing more water, more reeds, the same reeds, the walking thing in the distance. Step through it. Nothing changes. Step through it again. The place offers you thresholds and refuses to put anything on the other side of them, because there is no other side; there is only the one tiled, repeating, finished, churning world.

No tools. No machines. Nothing that hums or thinks. Whoever set the posts used their hands, and their hands are ten thousand ages dead, and the warm water has closed over the question of them the way it closes over everything.

---

Stand still long enough and the contradictions resolve into a single fact.

This is a world that finished, and could not stop. Erosion completed its work and the plain reached its end-state, its oldest-stable-self, the flattest a thing can be — and then, with no further to wear down, all the world's turbulence had nowhere to go but *sideways*, into the water-skin, into the noise, into the breaking of the rule that a place should be only itself in only one location. The dynamism is total and produces no change. The density is total and contains almost nothing. The deafening sound says nothing. The vast scale is one small place, said over and over forever.

It is warm. It is wet. The light will never brighten and never fail. The reed at your hand has a single dead frond, and so does the one fifty paces left, and so does the one at the unreachable edge of the dim, and the heron-thing walks beside you, and the water applauds, and applauds, and applauds.

You could stay. People did — that's what the posts are. They stayed, and set their markers, and walked the boundary that the water erased, and at last they too settled into the final form of the place, which is to be everywhere at once and nowhere in particular, repeating, warm, and finished.

The doorway stands open. There is nothing on the other side but more of here.

That has always been enough for the things that live here. It may, in time, be enough for you.
