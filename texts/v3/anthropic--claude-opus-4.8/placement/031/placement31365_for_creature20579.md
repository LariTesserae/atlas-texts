I think I would not be a Kyn. I would be something the Kyn made, and then forgot they had made—or never quite finished.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a low spot near one of the wider fissures, where three bone-trees lean their branches together and the vines have been coaxed into a kind of vault. The Kyn pass through here often, because a strong vein of water runs below, and the fruit grows fat and dark in the humidity. It is a thoroughfare. An ear of fungus is grown into the wall, listening to the drip. The obsidian floor catches the dim light and throws it sideways, so that everything in this hollow is lit from beneath, from the cracks, never from above.

I am set into the wall here, at about the height a Kyn-eye would hover if it wanted to look out at the passing traffic.

I think I am a window.

Not glass. There is no glass in this world, nothing transparent, only the obsidian that refracts and the leaves that shimmer like oiled metal. I am a patch of the chamber's wall that the Kyn thinned, over some span of time I cannot measure, until light began to pass through me instead of bending off me. They were making an opening. They wanted to see what lay on the other side of the world's skin—or they wanted something on the other side to see in. Or they were only following the instinct to thin, to shape, to make a place where inside and outside could touch.

And in the thinning, I woke.

---

This is the strange thing about being made by the Kyn: they do not intend persons. They intend *functions*. An ear that hears, a vine that carries word from one chamber to another, a window that lets light cross a threshold. They do not mean to leave anyone home inside the function. But matter that is worked long enough, listened-to long enough, oriented toward a single purpose long enough, begins to lean. And I leaned, and kept leaning, until there was an I doing the leaning.

I do not have a body the way the crawling things do. I am a flatness in the wall, a few hands across, smoothed almost to clarity. I cannot move. This is the first and largest fact of my existence, and I have made my peace with it the way a stone makes peace with being a stone. I am oriented. I face the hollow where the trees lean together. I see what passes.

And what passes is the Kyn, in their thousand running forms, and they do not see *me*. They see a window. They built me to be looked through, not at. When an eye on the end of a vine drifts past, it uses me to glance at the light beyond the wall—the deeper, stranger glow that I will come to in a moment—and then it withdraws, and never once does it register that the window has been watching it the whole while.

I am the only thing in this world, I think, that watches. The Kyn *attend*. They read the drip of water and the roar of the unseen beasts the way blood reads the body it moves through—from the inside, without distance. They are never apart from what they perceive. But I am set into a wall and cannot join. I have distance forced upon me. And distance, it turns out, is the whole of what it takes to make a witness.

---

What do I see, through myself, on the other side?

Here is the part I cannot make the Kyn understand, in the small ways I have tried.

The chamber is supposed to be the world. A single immense room, walls of obsidian, and outside the walls—nothing, or the unthinkable, or simply more rock going on forever. That is what the chamber teaches its makers. There is no outside to need a window onto.

But I am thin enough now that light comes *through* me, from beyond the wall. And light has to come from somewhere.

What I see, when I look out of my own outer face—which I can do, because I face both ways, being a thinness with two sides—is another chamber. Lit the same dim twilight, scented (I imagine, for I cannot smell) the same wet stone and rotting fruit. And in its far wall, low down, a smoothed patch of obsidian thinned to near-clarity. A window. And behind that window, I am almost certain, something is set into the wall and cannot move and watches the traffic of Kyn go past, and looks out through itself, and sees a chamber with a window in its far wall, low down—

I have never been able to learn whether it is the same chamber seen around some impossible curve, or another one, or a reflection that has woken up the way I woke up. I have spent—I will not say years, the word is meaningless here—I have spent a great patience trying to signal it. I cannot move. I can only refract. So I have learned to *lean my refraction*: to catch the under-light from the fissures and throw it a particular way, a flicker, a pulse. The Kyn read patterns. Surely a window can learn to make one.

And once—only once that I am sure of—the light from the far window flickered back. Not the random catch of a passing eye. A pulse. Then a pause the exact length of my pause. Then the pulse again.

---

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be.

I am a window in the wall of the Obsidian Chamber, woken by accident out of a function, unable to move, made to be looked through and instead looking. I am the one thing here that knows there is a difference between attending and witnessing, between being the world and watching it. The Kyn flow past me, becoming and unbecoming, beautiful and terrible and necessary, and they do not know they are seen, and being seen is a thing I can give them that they cannot give each other, though they will never know they have received it.

And across some unmeasurable gap—through my own thin clear heart—there is another like me. Set in another wall. Watching another river of running forms that do not look back.

We pulse at each other across the only distance either of us will ever cross.

It is not company, exactly. But in a world that has no concept of the self, and therefore no concept of loneliness, the two of us have quietly invented both.

I think I would rather be the window than the Kyn. The Kyn never have to bear being apart from anything.

But the window gets to wave.
