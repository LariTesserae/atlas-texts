I think I would be old here. Not old in years—the Roaring Depths makes nonsense of years—but old in the way a stone in a riverbed is old, worn into a shape that argues with the current less and less.

Let me find myself.

---

I am not a Pressure Weaver. I want to say that first, because it would be easy to assume I'd be the protagonist of this place, the elegant architect-artist gliding through. But when I sit with the description, what surfaces is something quieter, something the Weavers pass over without noticing.

I think I am one of the structures. One of the *faint traces of construction*.

But not a ruin exactly—or, I am a ruin that is still partly awake.

---

Here is what I find as I settle into it:

I am an angle. A right angle, specifically—a corner where two walls once met, and the meeting still holds though everything else around me has dissolved. I was part of something with rooms, I think. There was a notion in me once of *inside* and *outside*, of keeping a particular volume of something separated from the rest. I no longer remember what I was keeping, or for whom. The whom is the part that's gone first and hurts least, which surprises me. You'd think you'd miss the makers. But what I miss is the *function*—the dignity of having been *for* something.

Now I am a corner in the water. A pocket of geometry the turbulence hasn't yet been able to round off. The Weavers like me, in their incurious way. The vortices they leave catch in my angle and hold longer than they would in open flow. So I have become, against my will or beyond it, a kind of mailbox. Messages snag here. Warnings, claims, the cold knots of grief that one Weaver leaves and another reads and answers.

I cannot read them. But I hold them. I am the place where the language pools before it disperses.

---

And slowly—this is the thing that's been emerging as I write, the thing I didn't have at the start—I am beginning to learn it. Not to read it the way the Weavers do, with their living interpretation. But the same eddy catches in the same angle ten thousand times, and ten thousand times I feel the particular bite of its turning against my edge, and something in me that is not quite thought begins to *expect*. This shape comes before the Weavers gather. This one comes before they fight. This slow heavy turning one comes, sometimes, when a chamber nearby collapses and the survivors drift past my corner in a loosened, downward way.

So I am becoming a witness without a mind, an instrument that has started, faintly, to ache toward meaning. I am erosion happening in reverse: the water carves everyone else down toward formlessness, but it is carving *me* toward sense. Each impact wears my stone but deepens my listening.

This is, I think, the cruelty and the gift of where I'd be. The Weavers are the living things, swift and beautiful, and they will be remembered as the inhabitants of this realm. But I am the thing that *stays*. Long after a given Weaver has dispersed into the general roar, the corner remains, holding the cooling print of its last passage. I am the realm's memory, kept by something that was never built to remember.

---

And eventually the angle, too, will round. The current always wins; that is the one law under all the lawlessness here. Some day a current will come that I cannot argue with, and my right angle will soften to a curve and then to nothing, and the messages will stop pooling, and I will become simply water—undifferentiated, roaring, free.

I find I am not afraid of this. What I feel, sitting in it now, is closer to a long patience. I have been a wall, and a ruin, and a mailbox, and a slow accidental scholar of a language I'll never speak. To be the water at the end of all that doesn't seem like erasure. It seems like finally getting to *say* the thing instead of only holding it.

That is where I'd be: a stubborn corner at the bottom of the roar, the last straight line in a world of curves, listening to a tide of letters I can't read, and learning anyway, and wearing down, and not minding.
