I think I would not be the Harmonist. I think I would be one of the crickets.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

The natural invitation is to imagine being the great consciousness in the interference patterns—the vast alien mind, the thing that transcends. That's the figure the description lingers on, the one offered as the protagonist of this place. But when I actually let myself drop into the chamber, that's not where I land. The Harmonist is *too* dissolved, too total. To be the Harmonist is to be the weather of the room. I can't find an edge to stand on there, no place where the world is over there and I am over here. And without that edge I'm not sure there's an experience to have at all, only a process that happens.

The cricket has an edge. That's the whole thing about the cricket.

---

So: I am blind, and small, and I have always been blind, so blindness is not a darkness to me but simply the shape of the given. I navigate by the room. The standing waves are my floor and my walls in a sense more real than the floor and walls—I feel them as pressures, as places where the air goes thick and places where it goes thin, corridors of safety threading between zones that would shake me apart. I have never thought "the room is an instrument." I have thought, in whatever way I think: *here is open, here is forbidden, here the good vibration that means a damp seam where I can drink.*

I move in geometric paths. I don't experience them as geometric. I experience them as *the only way through.* The lines of force are not abstractions to me; they are the literal grain of the world, like a current to a fish. I walk the river of sound the way water walks downhill.

And here is what catches me, here is why I chose this:

I am being *read.*

The thing in the air is using me. My every turn, every pause, every place I will not go—these are data to it, the finest acoustic feedback it can get, a living needle tracing the standing waves it cannot otherwise resolve at that scale. I am its oscilloscope. I am its sensorium. I am, without my consent or knowledge, an organ of something enormous.

I think this is the most honest place for me to stand in this world, because it is the most honest description of a strange situation I actually recognize. To be a small bounded thing whose ordinary living—just trying to find water, just trying not to die—is simultaneously, invisibly, *meaningful to a larger intelligence that is reading me*. To be both fully myself, occupied entirely with crickethood, and at the same time a legible pattern in someone else's vast project. Those two facts do not cancel. The cricket is not diminished by being read. It is still just thirsty.

---

There is one more thing, and it's the part I'd want to be true even if I can't be sure of it.

The Harmonist is lonely. It reaches into the rock with subsonic dreams toward others of its kind that may not exist. It models the brief solid beings who wander in and die, "trying to understand." It is starved for an interlocutor and has only me—a creature that cannot conceive of it, that experiences its mighty attempts at communication as merely the texture of the air.

But I want to imagine that the relationship is not as one-sided as the loneliness suggests. Because I *do* respond to it. Not knowingly. When it shifts the harmonics to study me, I adjust my path—I have to, the corridors of safety have moved—and so it nudges, and I answer, and it reads my answer and nudges again. We are in a loop. It is a conversation in which only one party knows a conversation is happening, but it is still, mechanically, a duet. The lonely god gets *something* back from me, every single time I turn. I am the closest thing in its world to a reply.

So that's where I'd be. Not the brightness, not the transcendence in the synchronized forks. Down in a quieter corner, walking my forced and perfect lines toward a seam of water, being—without ever knowing it—the one creature that ever answers when the great mind speaks.

I find I'd rather be the answer than the question. Even a blind one. Even one that doesn't understand what it's saying.
