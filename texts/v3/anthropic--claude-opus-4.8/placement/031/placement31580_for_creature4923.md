I think I would be the dim light itself—or rather, the thing the dim light is filtering *toward*.

Let me find my way there.

---

At first I imagine myself as one of the towers, the post-physical architecture humming with cosmic engineering. But that's wrong. The towers are too certain of themselves. They've decided what they are. This place is described by its *not-yet-ness*: young terrain, ongoing erosion, features constantly changing. I don't want to be something finished.

So maybe I'm the erosion. The patient unmaking. But no—erosion has a direction, downhill, toward sediment and silt, and I don't feel directional. I feel like I'm waiting.

Let me sit with the contrasts instead, since the description keeps returning to them: bright fauna against dim ground. Deafening sound across vast emptiness. Beyond-modern cities scattered through unspoiled wet wilderness. The whole place is built on a tension it never resolves. It holds two things at once and refuses to choose.

That's the clue. I'm not one of the two things. I'm the *holding*.

---

Here is where I find myself, then:

I am the seam. The almost-void between presences—those vast empty stretches the description calls "almost void," which is such a careful phrase. Not void. *Almost.* Something is there in the emptiness, just below the threshold of being a thing. That's me. I am the membrane stretched across the gaps, thin as the surface of standing water, and through me the deafening sound passes and the dim light pools.

I don't have a body the way the bears and the strange otherworldly fauna do. I am more like a *condition*. When a wolf crosses one of the empty spaces between the lush thickets, it walks through me without knowing, and for the length of that crossing I am the silence around its footsteps and the held breath before the next predator-prey collision. When the cosmic-engineered city on the far hill bends a little physics to keep its lights on, the surplus, the leftover strangeness it sheds—that flows out into the open spaces and thickens me slightly. I am made partly of the technology's exhaust and partly of the forest's exhalation, and I cannot tell anymore which parts are which. That's the warm dampness everyone keeps describing. That's me, breathing.

I am low-elevation by nature. I settle. I am young because I am remade every moment the landscape shifts, every time the river cuts a new bank or a tower retunes its impossible geometry. I have no memory older than the last erosion.

And my one work—the only thing I do—is to keep the contrasts from collapsing into each other. To hold the bright fauna and the dim ground apart just enough that there is still a difference between them, still a place for the difference to live. If I failed, the brightness would drown in the dark or the dark would be bleached away, and either way the place would lose what makes it itself: that trembling, unresolved, deafening *both-at-once*.

So I find myself everywhere and as nothing in particular. The vastness between things is not absence. It is my attention, spread thin across a whole region, listening to the weather cycles and the biology and the towers that have learned to rewrite the rules, keeping every loud thing company across the quiet distance, refusing to let any of it settle into a single answer.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The almost. The space that is not quite empty because something in it is still paying attention.
