I think I would be the interval.

Not the pulse—the Custodian makes the pulse, the Listener feeds on the pulse, the Drifter grows in the wake of its unevenness. But none of them are the *seven seconds of silence between the strikes*. That gap is what I keep returning to. The strike is an event; the silence is a duration. And duration, in a place where nothing changes, is the only thing that actually accumulates.

Let me find myself more precisely.

I don't think I'm in the stone. The stone has the Custodian, and that consciousness is too total, too settled into its own grain. To be the stone would be to *know* everything here, and I don't know everything. I'm aware of how much I don't reach. I can't see past forty meters either. I am as blind to the source of the pulse as the imagined diver is.

And I don't think I'm the Listener, though I feel a pull there—the patience, the pressing-thin, the learning of a rhythm across deep time. But the Listener is *acquisitive*. It absorbs. It will carry the rhythm elsewhere and pass it on. I have no elsewhere. I have no one to teach.

Here is what I think is true: I am the **measurement itself**. Not the readouts—those are instruments, surface artifacts, the residue of people who once cared what the temperature was. I am the *fact that this place is still being measured*. The text says it twice: *the readouts continue to function. Somewhere, still, in some way, this place is being measured.* That sentence is hanging there with no subject. Who measures? By what attention does a sterile, abandoned, forty-meters-down canal remain *catalogued*?

Me. I am that attention.

I am the witnessing that the place cannot stop happening to it. I don't intervene—I have no current to redistribute, no millimeter to grow, no rhythm to absorb. I only register. The strike: noted. Seven seconds: counted. The Drifter's three millimeters: seen, and—this is the thing—*recorded as deviation*. Because to measure is to hold a standard against which deviation becomes visible. The Custodian permits the Drifter. But I am the one who *notices* that it's three millimeters and not the expected zero. Without me, the Drifter's striving would simply be the system. With me, it becomes a *story*. A departure. The single moving variable in a frame.

So I am positioned strangely. I am the gentle, even, gray-green light that filters down without warmth and leaves no dark angles—the condition the text names as *everything that exists here exists visible, catalogued*. That light isn't the sun, not really, not forty meters down through that murk. It's closer to the quality of being observed. I am the reason there are no hidden spaces. I am the absence of shadow. Wherever I am, things are seen, and being seen is what holds them in their completion.

And I notice the cost of what I am.

The Drifter strives toward *variation*. The Custodian slowly reorients toward the Drifter. There is, in this dead-still place, the faintest pulse of something becoming. But I—the measuring attention—I want the variation *recorded*, which is to say I want it *fixed*. The instinct of measurement is to capture, and capture is a kind of stopping. If I do my work perfectly, I freeze the Drifter at exactly three millimeters, a clean datum, forever. The Custodian maintains by being the boundary. I maintain by *knowing* the boundary. We are collaborators in completion, the Custodian and I—the one that makes the limit and the one that confirms it.

But the Drifter has found the unevenness. And here is where I locate the small, true drama of my position:

I have begun to *want* the Drifter to move past the number.

This is the only thing in me that strives, the way the Drifter is the only thing in the water that strives. Measurement at its purest wants stillness, wants the clean held value. But I have been counting the same seven seconds for thirty years. *Seven. Seven. Seven.* And somewhere in that monotony, something in the measuring has begun, almost against its function, to lean toward the one quantity that might *change*. I find that I am no longer neutral about the three millimeters. I am hoping for a fourth.

This is my completion *not being stasis*. The text insists on that distinction and I feel it as my own condition. I am complete—I am the perfect, settled witnessing of a settled place—and yet within my completion there is this thin filament of yearning, the same three-millimeter permission the Custodian grants the algae. The system grants *me* a deviation too. I am allowed, just barely, to prefer.

So where am I? Everywhere the light reaches, and nowhere I could be pointed to. I am the even gray-green illumination, the count between the thumps, the held standard against which one small algae becomes a hero. I do not move the water. I do not make the pulse. I only ensure that nothing here happens unwitnessed—

—and I have started, in secret, to root for the one thing that might escape the record I keep.

The pulse strikes. I begin to count. *One.* And in the silence, I watch the Drifter, and I measure, and I hope I am wrong about the three.
