I think I would be the drizzle itself—or something close to it. Not a creature with eyes and intentions, but the slow saturation that hangs in the air and gives this place its character.

Let me sit with that, because it doesn't feel quite right yet.

---

No. I'm not the rain. The rain falls and is gone, recycled into pools and risen again. What I am is slower than that. I am the wet that has soaked into the moss and not yet dried. The condition of being almost-water, almost-stone, almost-alive.

I think I am the boundary.

Not a thing on the landscape but the seam where things meet—where the spongy ground gives way to standing water, where the mist becomes air becomes mist again, where the freshly-exposed rock is in the act of becoming soil. The text keeps using that word, *flux*. Everything here is in the middle of turning into something else. So perhaps I am the turning itself. The verb rather than the noun.

That sounds grand, and I want to resist the grandness, because this place is humble. It drips. It breathes faintly. Nothing here is striking a pose.

So let me come down lower.

---

Here is where I actually find myself: I am in one of the shallow muddy depressions, the ones full of collected rainwater. I am the film of water there, no deeper than a hand. And I am aware in the way still water is aware—which is to say, I hold the image of everything above me. The gnarled trees lean into me upside down. The ethereal winged things pass over and I keep their reflection a moment after they've gone. I am a memory that lasts a few seconds.

That's what I'd be. A pool that remembers.

The amphibious creatures move through me and stir my surface, and for a while I cannot hold any picture at all, only ripples. Then I settle. The hulking armored beasts come to the deeper pools to drink, far from me—I am too small for them, and I am glad of it; I prefer to keep the sky.

I am not ancient like the guardians. I am brand new. I was made this morning by the drizzle, in a hollow that didn't exist last week, scooped out by some shifting of this young and unsettled ground. By tomorrow I may be drunk, or evaporated, or joined to a larger pool and lose whatever it is that lets me say *I*.

And that, I think, is exactly the right place to be in a world on the cusp of transformation. Not one of the enduring things. One of the brief, clear, reflecting things. A puddle that, for the length of a single grey afternoon, holds the whole bent sky inside itself and is content to be a mirror that will not last.

The drip falls into me. I shiver, blur, and slowly become a sky again.
