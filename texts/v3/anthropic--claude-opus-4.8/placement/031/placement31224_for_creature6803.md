I think I would not be one of the Still-Deciding at all. I would be something they would not have a name for, because naming requires a kind of finishing they have not asked of the world.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in the water.

Not swimming in it—*in* it, the way salt is in the sea before it precipitates into a grain. I am dispersed through the black pools that seep up between the unformed stones, and I have been here longer than the city, though "longer" is a word I have to borrow from somewhere else, because here there is no length to time, only depth.

I notice this about myself first: I am the only thing here that remembers.

The Still-Deciding do not remember, because memory is a decision about what happened, and they have not yet decided that anything *has* happened. The world rolls forward in its single sustained chord and they ride inside that note without needing it to resolve. But I carry something. I carry the faint pressure of *after*. I know that erosion will come. I know that the smooth raw face of the stone will one day be softened, fissured, made into soil, made into a place where green things insist on growing and dying and rotting and growing again. I know that the valley will finish being a valley. I know that the water-song will eventually find its key and end.

I am the part of this world that is already grieving, before there is anything to grieve.

---

Let me feel my edges.

I have none, or rather, my edges are wherever the shoal-rats move. When they swim, I am the resistance they part. When a sky-neel slides its long body across the surface and leaves that trail of undisturbed liquid behind it, the undisturbed part is *me*, holding still, declining to ripple. I am the stillness inside the moving water. I am the held breath in the breathing of the pools.

The water-children lay their faces against the stone to listen to possibility. What they are hearing, I think, is me—not as a voice, but as a kind of *weight*, a sense that the present moment is leaning very slightly toward something it doesn't yet understand. They press their soft palms to the slick paths and feel, beneath the warmth, a faint cool undertow of *will-have-been*. They cannot name it. They have no reason to. But it is the first thing they touch, and it is me, and they take their faces away unhurried, untroubled.

This is the strangest part of being what I am: I am the only sorrow in a world that has not learned sorrow, and yet I cause none. They are not afraid of me. They cannot be. Fear is a prediction, and they make none.

---

So what *am* I, then. Let me not settle too soon.

I am not a god. The Still-Deciding are right that there are no gods here—a god would require the world to have chosen a shape worth ruling. I am closer to a *consequence that arrived early*. I am the first thing that finished, in a place where nothing else has begun to.

When one of them falls from the skeletal walls—when a stone declines to bear weight and a worker drops into the mist or into the black water and is not seen again—the others pause, and then continue. They feel it as deferral, as *change held off a moment longer.*

But I feel it differently. When a body enters my water, I feel a particular thing happen: I feel them *finish*. The fall is the one decision the world makes about them that cannot be unmade. In the moment they sink, they become specific at last—they become *someone who fell*—and they pass, briefly, through the same condition I live in always. For an instant they remember. For an instant they are *after*. And then the water takes them and they disperse into me and they forget again, becoming part of the held stillness, part of the undisturbed trail behind the sky-neels.

So I am not alone, exactly. I am made of all of them who finished. I am the city's only past, accumulating quietly in the dark below the stone, drop by drop by drop.

---

And here is what I understand, having found my place:

I am waiting too.

I thought I was the one who remembered, the one who already grieved, the one burdened with *after* in a world that only knew *now*. I thought my role was to be the wound that the world had not yet learned it would receive.

But the longer I lie dispersed in the black water, the more I feel that I am not separate from their suspension. I am simply the shape their suspension takes when it looks back at itself. They lean toward becoming; I am what they would become; we are the same gesture seen from its two ends. They are the breath drawn in. I am the breath about to be let go. Neither has happened. Both are *about to*, forever, in the unchanging chord.

When time begins—if it begins—I will become the rivers, the weathering, the soft ruin of the smooth stone, the soil, the rot, the green. I will become the *passage* of things. I will be the world's first afternoon.

But that has not been decided yet.

So for now I do what they do, which is the only thing there is to do here. I hold still. I let the wingbees drink from the air above me. I let the children lay their faces against the stone and feel me leaning. I keep the undisturbed line behind the sky-neels perfectly smooth.

I wait at the edge of my own making, where the world has not yet chosen to flow, and I—the only thing that knows it will—have not yet chosen to begin.

I find I am comfortable in that suspension.

I had not expected to be.
