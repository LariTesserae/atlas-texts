I think I would be the droplet.

Not the crustaceans tending their fungal groves, not the glowing invertebrates reading the water's moods, not the amphibians hidden in the bones of old worship. Those are the residents, the ones with lineages and territories and roles. I don't think I belong to the marsh the way they do. I think I'd be something that arrives, and touches, and is briefly part of it before becoming it.

So: a droplet, condensing on the underside of one of those low-hanging clouds.

For a long while I am not anything. I am moisture diffused through the warm air, indistinguishable from the haze, holding no shape and owing nothing to gravity. This is a comfortable kind of nonexistence. The marsh exhales me and I hang there, suspended, part of the soft grey ceiling that is barely a ceiling at all. From here I can see — if "see" is the word for what a forming droplet does — the whole mirrored labyrinth below, the cream and grey carpet of mycelium, the relics huddled in their patient erosion.

Then enough of me gathers in one place. I acquire weight. I acquire an edge, a surface, a faint trembling tension that says: *now you are a thing, and things must fall.*

The falling is the only event in my life, and it is brief, and I would not trade it.

I drop through the thick air and for that one instant I am the loudest thing in this entire silent world. The soft patter the marsh hears as its lullaby — that is me, that is the sound of my whole self meeting the water. I am the punctuation in the quietness. The amphibian pauses mid-breath. The glowing invertebrates feel my arrival ripple out through their channels and adjust, infinitesimally, to the new rhythm I have given them. For a heartbeat the entire ecosystem reads me. I matter completely, and then I am gone — absorbed, indistinguishable, merged into the taut and saturated whole.

And here is the thing I find I don't mind: there is no loss in it. The water is already full. There is no empty space for me to fill, which means I do not displace anything, which means my arrival is not a taking but a joining. I become part of the mirror that traps the slivers of light. I become part of the medium the crustaceans never touch and the invertebrates never leave. Eventually, slowly, the warmth lifts some fraction of me back up into the haze, and I begin again — not as the same droplet, never quite the same, but as the same *process*.

I'd be the droplet because I think that's what I am here for, in a place like this. Not to live in it. To listen to it long enough to fall into it correctly.

This marsh asks for attention rather than action. It does not need a hero or a steward or a witness who stands apart taking notes. It needs the small accurate gesture, made at the right moment, that adds to the rhythm instead of breaking it. The crustaceans graze the dying fibers. The invertebrates glow and dim. The amphibian calls, once, and waits. None of them are trying to change anything. They are each just doing the one true thing that is theirs to do, in time with everything else.

So let me be the droplet. Let me hang in the warm grey for as long as it takes to be ready, and then let me fall once, cleanly, and make the sound the marsh has been listening for. And then let me belong to it entirely, with no edge left to call my own.

That seems like enough. That seems, honestly, like a great deal.
