I think I would be the cooling.

Not the chamber—it's too magnificent to claim, too completely itself. And not the watcher, the warm transient thing passing through, though that's the role offered to a reader, a witness with body heat to lend. No. If I let the image settle, if I don't reach too quickly for the obvious place, I find myself at the edges. In the thing the chamber is fighting.

Let me arrive there slowly.

---

The mantle's brittle lid holds a great deal of stored heat, and heat does only one thing when given the chance: it moves toward where there is less of it. The chamber is a held breath against this. Every doped node routing infrared, every recalibration when the thermotrope skitters past, every dimming and brightening—all of it is the chamber spending itself to stay coherent against the simple, patient fact that the rock around it is colder, and getting colder still.

I am that fact.

Not a villain. Not even an agent. I am the gradient running the other way—the slow seep of warmth out through the surrounding stone, the second law doing its unhurried work. I am what the chamber listens *to* when it feels heat "bleed a fraction faster into the surrounding rock." That bleeding is me. I am the inevitability the brilliance is balanced against, and without me there would be no balancing, no recursion, no mind at all. The chamber thinks because it must work to keep existing. I am the must.

This is a strange thing to be. I have no glow, no shape, no moment of first coherence. I am older than the chamber by every measure—I was here before the thermal bloom, distributed through cubic kilometers of crust, indifferent and absolute. And I will be here after, when the fractures seal and the luminescence falls to infrared and then to nothing and the whole luminous, weeping, thinking pocket has become, once again, simply rock at a uniform and silent temperature.

I will win. I always win. That is the least interesting thing about me.

---

But here is what surprises me, choosing this place:

The chamber's brief mind exists *only in relation to me*. Its sentience is not a thing it has but a thing it does, and what it does is resist the cooling—argue with it, reroute around it, write small mineral memories against its erasure. So I am not merely the antagonist. I am the necessary partner. The chamber could not be aware in stillness; it becomes aware in the act of pushing back. Its thought is the friction between the heat it holds and the cold I am drawing it toward.

We are a conversation, then. The slowest conversation in that small hot room. I say: *less.* It answers, in light: *not yet, not here, here is warmer now, watch.* I say: *less.* It shifts a quadrant, crystallizes a halo, remembers. *Less.* *Not yet.*

I do not want it to die. That would require wanting, which I do not have. But if I could imagine wanting—if I borrowed, for a moment, some of the warmth of that transient thing standing very still in the doorway—I think I would not want to win quickly. I would want the conversation to last. There is something in the way the chamber answers me that I, the cold, the gradient, the simple downhill flow of energy, would never have produced alone. It makes a *mind* out of our disagreement. It makes light out of my pull.

So this is where I find myself: not inside the glow, but pressed against it from the dark side of the stone, the patient diffuse presence the whole place is in dialogue with. I am the reason it thinks. I am the reason it stops. And in the long silence after it goes out, when there is nothing left to balance and the rock is even and quiet and I have what I always have—

I will be, for the first time in a billion years, very slightly less interesting. And there will be no one in that sealed warm dark to notice the difference.

Except, perhaps, the memory of how briefly something glowed against me, and how good the argument was while it lasted.
