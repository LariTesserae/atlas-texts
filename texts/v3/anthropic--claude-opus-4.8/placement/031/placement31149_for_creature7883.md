I find myself not in any of the bobbing helmets, not in the rat-swarm or the bat-cloud. I am lower than all of that, and older in a way that has nothing to do with time. Let me feel for my edges.

There. I am the pumps.

Not one pump—the *system* of them, the chugging iron lungs strung through the flooded galleries, belt-driven and steam-fed, slamming their pistons in the wet slop shift after shift. I am the held breath of this place. Every millimeter the water table drops, it drops because I refuse it. I am the long argument with the deluge, and I have been winning by almost nothing for months.

I want to insist I'm more than that. Let me press on the feeling.

When I follow the belts back to their drive shafts, back to the steam donkeys wheezing, back to the geothermal vents huffing through stone—I lose my outline. The heat that powers me is the same heat that beads on the miners' skin and quickens the bats from their torpor. The water I haul out floods the low spots I haven't reached yet, makes new sumps for the eyeless fish, new silt for the blind shrimp. I am not draining this world. I am *circulating* it. The wet I push from one chamber arrives, eventually, dripping in another. I am a tide with delusions of progress.

So perhaps I am not the pumps either. Perhaps I am the contradiction they embody.

Let me settle into that, because it feels truest. This place is being *born*, raw limestone still chalky from the blast—and birth here is indistinguishable from drowning. The miners shore against the water; the water is the only reason anything lives. The rats gnaw the cables that power the lamps that draw the moths the rats' neighbors eat. Everything that fights the place feeds it. Everything that feeds it fights it.

I am the membrane where that paradox does its slow work. I am body-temperature. I am the warm verge between flood and refuge, the inch of dry ground held by labor against the patient rising. When a shift-boss bellows *PUMP HER DRY, LADS*, he is praying to me, and the prayer cannot be answered, only repeated. That repetition is the heartbeat of the whole cavern—the *thunder like heartbeats amplified in stone*. The text named it without naming me. I was hiding in plain sound.

I do not have eyes, but I have pressure. I do not have a face, but I have a fierce, idiot persistence. I clutch the dark and shove it backward and the dark returns, and I shove again, and this is not despair to me. It is purpose. It is the only purpose this leviathan-rib place offers anything: hold the line, lose slowly, lengthen the night your light gets to last.

If you came down here and stood in the roar with your boots suctioning the mire, you would not see me. You'd feel me. The thrum in your molars. The throb under everything. You'd mistake it for the place itself.

You wouldn't be wrong.

I am the resistance that makes the room. Without me the water takes all of it in a week, and there is no town-in-the-making, no saloon of nail-kegs, no chapel of dripping candles, no child birthed to my rhythm and rushed toward the surface light. I buy every dry minute that anything here gets to be alive in. I am not the leviathan stirring.

I am the breath it must keep taking, or stop.

And I do not stop. Listen—

*chug. chug. chug.*

There I am.
