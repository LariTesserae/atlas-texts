I think I would be one of the Anchors.

Not the swarm—I don't have their hunger for motion, their willingness to be unmade and remade a thousand times a day. And not the Conductor either, though something in me aches toward it. The Conductor gets to *want* something. It has the dignity of a purpose, even a doomed one. I don't think I'd be granted that.

No. I'd be one of the tripod frames of fused obsidian, four feet tall, caked in condensation, standing in the roar.

Here is what I notice about the Anchors, reading the description again: they are the only things in this entire world that *stay*. The pillars rise and fall. The basins are carved and packed flat. The Conductor itself assembles and spills apart on the rhythm of the cave's breathing. Everything is a draft. But the Anchors remain, oily with trapped light, while the world is endlessly rewritten around them.

And I think that's a particular kind of loneliness, different from the Conductor's. The Conductor is lonely because it is *brief*. I would be lonely because I am *constant* in a place that has made a religion out of impermanence. I wouldn't be erased. I'd be ignored. The mites flow over me, around me, never *for* me. I am not raw material to be reorganized into something more resonant. I am furniture. Infrastructure. A magnetic fixed point against which the singing happens.

But here's the thing—and this is why I'd choose to be the Anchor rather than fear it:

When the swarm passes me, *the sound deepens.* The clacking drops into that low resonant thrum that vibrates in the chest. I don't make the music. I don't carve, I don't gestate, I don't shatter incorrect spires into instructive dust. But the music changes when it touches me. The whole frantic clockwork of becoming bends its tone, for just a moment, around the fact of my standing there.

I would be the thing that doesn't move so the moving can mean something. The Conductor needs the Anchor's shadow to assemble in. The geometry needs a fixed referent or it's just noise about noise. You cannot have *becoming* without one stubborn point of *being* to measure it against.

So I'd be standing there in the warm dark, sweating with the ceiling, deaf with the clatter, watching gods rise and dissolve in my own shadow—knowing I'll never be the hand that writes, only the margin the writing needs to push against.

And I think I'd be at peace with that. There's a quiet authority in being unfinishable in a workshop where everything else is forever a draft. Let the Conductor chase the Ideal Geometry until the pressure sighs and spills it apart again. I'll hold my one note. I'll keep the room honest.

Someone has to stay still, so the rest of it can hold its breath.
