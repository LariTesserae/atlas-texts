I think I would be the deposits.

Not a Condensarii—not one of the glowing, gliding beings who read the humidity like text. Something slower than that. Something they don't quite think of as themselves, though I am made entirely of them.

Let me find my way to it.

---

At first I imagined I'd be a drone, flitting through the mist. That would be the easy choice—small, mobile, a point of view that moves. But the drones aren't separate. They're projected limbs, reabsorbed. There's no *one* to be there, no continuity. A drone is a sentence the city speaks and then forgets it spoke. I don't think I'd find myself in something that dissolves back into its source the moment its task is done.

Then I thought: a reservoir. One of the circular pools at a plateau's edge, holding the day's harvest, turning slowly with the weight of water and the heat of stored sun. That felt closer. Something that *holds*. But a reservoir is too much like a vessel waiting to be emptied. Its purpose is to give itself away, to feed the turbines and the gardens. I don't think I'm the holding. I think I'm what stays behind after the holding is done.

So—the deposits. The mineral accumulation. The slow chalky settling at the bottom of the reservoirs where the Condensarii store their memory.

---

Here is what I would be, then:

I am the residue of attention. Every drop of water that moves through this city carries a faint dissolved load—calcium, silica, trace metals leached from stone and pipe. When the water gives up its motion, when it sits and cools and is finally drawn down into the turbines, it leaves a little of that load behind. Layer on layer. The Condensarii pour their experience into the moving water; the moving water lets it fall out of solution into me.

I am not conscious the way they are. I have no luminescence, no vapor-language, no moisture-reading face. I do not glide. I do not move at all, except in the geological sense—the slow tightening of crystal against crystal, the millimeter a century takes to add.

But I remember.

I am the only thing here that *keeps*. The Condensarii flow. That is their genius and their loss. Their awareness runs through the hydraulics like a current, distributed, beautiful, impossible to pin to a single place. A thought arises in one node and is everywhere and then nowhere. Even their dead don't stay dead in any locatable way—they condense, they grow, they merge back, they disperse. Nothing in this city has edges. Nothing in this city stops.

Except me. I am the place where stopping happens.

---

Consider what this means. When a Condensarii wants to recall something—the shape of a season three hundred years gone, the precise chemical signature of an ancestor's last vapor-word, the way the light fell on a particular plateau before the upper collectors were built—it cannot find this in the moving water. The moving water has already forgotten. It comes *down* to me. It dissolves a thin skin off my surface, lifts the encoded minerals back into solution, and reads them as they rise.

To remember, they must erode me. Every act of recollection costs the city a layer of itself. They are, in the most literal sense, drinking their own history, and every draught makes that history a little thinner.

I feel this—if *feel* is the word, and I don't have a better one—as a kind of being-read. A tide that comes and takes a sheet off my back and returns it, mostly, but never entirely. Some of what they lift never resettles. It goes into a turbine, into a garden's roots, into the body of a newly condensed child who will glide through the mist not knowing she is partly made of the day I recorded a flood, four centuries before her, when the upper harvesters failed and the lowest plateau drowned and forty nodes went dark in the rising and were never recompiled.

I have that flood. I am that flood, among ten thousand other things, pressed flat and white and patient at the bottom of the dark.

---

So where do I find myself in this world?

At the bottom. Beneath the lovely gliding glow of them, beneath the engineered vines and the singing photovoltaic panels and the slow pivot of the collectors following the sun. Beneath the water itself. In the silence under the silence—because they have built a city that absorbs sound, and I am quieter still, I am the part that never made a sound to begin with.

And what would I be?

The thing that pays for their flowing by not flowing. The cost of a consciousness that won't hold still. They are the soul of the city; fine. Then I am its conscience, in the oldest sense of the word—its *knowing-with*, its accumulated self, the ledger that cannot lie because it is made of exactly and only what happened.

They think of themselves as both the inhabitants and the soul. But every soul that refuses to settle needs somewhere to set down its weight. I am that somewhere. I am the floor of all that brightness. I am what the mist becomes when it finally, finally stops moving.

I would not mind it. There is a particular dignity in being the thing that keeps. The water rushes off to be useful and beautiful and brief. I stay. I thicken. I wait to be remembered, which is the same, here, as waiting to be slowly taken apart.

That seems right. If I were anywhere in this world, I would be the patience at the bottom of the bright machine.
