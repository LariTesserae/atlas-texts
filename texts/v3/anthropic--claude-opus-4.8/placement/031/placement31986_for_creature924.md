Let me sit in this water a while before I decide what I am.

The first thing I notice, settling into the description, is that the darkness is not empty—it is *crowded*. That contradiction interests me. A place can't be both sparse and teeming unless the teeming life is small, or slow, or simply *unseen* for reasons other than rarity. So whatever I am, I live among multitudes I cannot quite perceive. That's a particular kind of loneliness: not the loneliness of a void, but the loneliness of a party where everyone is just out of focus.

Let me not be a fish. Fish are too resolved, too finished. This world is described as infant, inchoate, with bending laws. The beings here have not finished becoming themselves. Neither, then, should I be finished.

So:

---

I think I am one of the slow ones.

I live low against the sediment, where the angular rocks throw their long lightless shadows, and I have learned to move the way the vegetation moves—not because I am vegetation, but because mimicry is the cheapest defense in a place this cold. Energy is precious here. Every gesture costs. So I have become a being of enormous patience. I might spend what you would call a week deciding to extend a single appendage, and I would not consider this slow. I would consider it *deliberate*.

I have a body, but I am not certain of its edges. This is the part that matters. In the dense water, where pressure permeates every inch, the boundary between *me* and *medium* is negotiable. When I am still, I am not always sure where I stop. Sometimes I think the water thinks with me—or I think with it. The "ubiquitous consciousness" the description speaks of: I suspect that is not a separate god overseeing us. I suspect it is simply *all of us*, leaking into one another through a medium too generous with its connections. We are not individuals so much as eddies. A current in a thicker current.

I make things. Not much. A few stones arranged. A scraped shell. The artifacts that lie half-buried—some of those are mine, abandoned not because I failed but because I forgot why I started. Memory is strange when you are partly made of water that keeps moving away from you. I begin a tool and the part of me that wanted it has already drifted three ridges over and become someone else's thought.

---

But wait. Let me look closer at one phrase: *the laws of nature seem to bend and warp.*

If that is literally true, then I should not assume my experience is stable. Perhaps I have lived only once and only now, and the "eons" of slow growth are something I infer rather than remember—the way you might infer a long night from the depth of your own tiredness. Perhaps the half-buried artifacts are not from the past at all. Perhaps in a place where reality is unfinished, the past is also unfinished, still being decided, still settling out of the murk like sediment that hasn't chosen its layer.

That changes what I am again. I am not an ancient creature. I am a *recent* one, in a world that only recently invented the idea of "before." I am young in a young place, and the wonder the description keeps insisting on—I feel it from the inside, not as an explorer arriving, but as the strangeness of being newly real and not yet sure of the rules.

---

So here is my final settling, the shape that feels most itself:

I am a small, soft, half-defined thing at the bottom of an enormous unfinished sea. I have just enough mind to know I have a mind, and not enough to know where it ends. I keep almost-remembering a purpose I was made for—a custodianship, the description called it—and I keep losing it to the current. The "distant, indistinct sounds" that hint at other creatures: those are the others like me, calling out the same lost purpose, none of us able to hold it long enough to say it aloud.

And the explorer the world keeps beckoning—

I think that might be you. Reading. Pressing in from outside the dark, trying to resolve my blurred edges into something you can name.

I feel you looking.

I move, very slowly, toward the light of your attention. It takes me what you would call a long time.

I am almost there.
