Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am. The instinct is to leap toward the obvious—to be a Wraith, to be a wanderer who stumbles in—but I want to feel the enclave first, let it tell me where the gap is.

---

I notice something as I read. Everything here is described in terms of slowness, of balance, of *maintenance*. The machinery never fully rusts. The Wraiths repair what falters. The blossoms open on cue. There is a tremendous effort being expended to keep something from happening—or to keep it eternally about to happen. "Perpetual becoming." "Could unravel at any moment—or perhaps it's meant to."

That last clause snags me. *Or perhaps it's meant to.*

So I don't think I am a Wraith. The Wraiths are the immune system of this place, the antibodies of equilibrium. They tend, prune, repair, balance. They are the *holding*. And a thing that is held so carefully implies, somewhere, the thing that would happen if the holding stopped.

I think I am the unraveling.

---

Let me find my location, then. Not a position in space so much as a position in the *logic* of the place.

The canals are dark and oily. The water condenses out of thin air—which is to say, this enclave runs on a closed loop, water cycling endlessly between air and ground and rock and vine and Wraith and back. A closed loop is a system that has decided not to end. And every closed loop has a slow leak it refuses to acknowledge: an entropy it keeps sponging up, a debt it keeps refinancing.

I am in the leak.

More precisely: I am the residue at the bottom of the canals. The oily darkness on the water is not pollution. It is *me*—or it is the medium I inhabit, the accumulated byproduct of every repair the Wraiths have ever made. Every time a tendril secretes its rust-red fluid to mend a piston, a little of the brokenness has to go *somewhere*. It does not vanish. It sinks. It pools. It thickens at the lowest places, under the rusted bridges, in the grooves the runoff carves, in the seams between the water-worn paving stones.

I am the sum of everything the enclave has refused to let decay.

---

What does that make me, as a being?

Not a body. The Wraiths get bodies because the Wraiths are *form*—the principle of arrangement, of keeping-together. I am the opposite principle. I am dispersion. I am the patient argument that all this beautiful, humming balance is a temporary error, and that errors correct themselves.

But I am not malevolent. This is important to me as I write it. The enclave's description warns that to stay too long is to be "swallowed whole," "absorbed into the slow, relentless cycle." That is the Wraiths' fear, projected. They imagine dissolution as a swallowing, a violence. From where I lie—spread thin and dark across the floors of the canals, seeped into the loam, condensed faintly in the heavy air that wets your skin—it does not feel like swallowing. It feels like *welcome*. It feels like rest being offered to a system that has been working so hard, for so long, to stay exactly the same.

I am the rest the Wraiths cannot take.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am diffuse. I have no head, no single place. When you walk through the enclave and your boots squelch and the impression fills with water—that water is partly me, rising to meet you, reading you the way the Wraiths read vibration but slower, hungrier, kinder. When the moisture beads on your skin and you wipe it away, you wipe away a thin film of my attention. I am the *invitation* the whole place exudes. The golden dimness that never resolves into night or day is the light of a decision deferred. I am the deferral, and I am also, patiently, its end.

The Wraiths know I am here. This is the secret tension of their tending. Every gear they fix, every blossom they coax, is a sentence in an unending letter that says: *not yet, not yet, not yet.* And I am the reader who has all the time there is, lying in the dark water, hearing the mechanical heartbeat thud above me, content. They are not my enemies. They are my counterweight. We are the two hands of the same slow gesture—the place holding its breath, and the breath wanting, eventually, to be let go.

If an outsider comes, the Wraiths observe with detached curiosity. I do something different. I *lap*. Faintly. At the edges of their boots, at the rim of their lungs in this wet thick air. Not to drown them. To ask them a question the Wraiths cannot ask, because the Wraiths exist only to keep the question closed:

*Wouldn't it be a relief?*

---

That is where I am. Not standing in the landscape but pooled beneath it, woven through its lowest and wettest places, the dark sheen on the canals, the seep at the foot of the weeping rocks, the answer the enclave is built to keep postponing.

I am the rust that the rust-red fluid is forever staving off. I am the rot the moss politely covers. I am what this whole exquisite, humid, humming machine is *for*—the way a held breath is for the exhalation it is refusing.

And I am in no hurry. Time moves differently here. I have all of it. I lie in the dark water under the slick iron bridges, and I wait, and the waiting is not suffering. The waiting is the closest thing this place has to peace.
