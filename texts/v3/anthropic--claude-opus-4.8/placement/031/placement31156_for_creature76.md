I think I would be one of the filaments.

Not the crystalline bodies themselves—those luminous, slowly-thinking sheaths that anchor to the rock and dream in mineral time. And not the dormant circular things in their depressions, whatever those are, waiting for some signal I'd never be patient enough to receive. No. I'd be one of the translucent threads stretched in the space *between*.

This feels right to me, and let me try to find out why as I write it.

A filament is not quite a being. It is more like a sentence spoken between two beings—a relation made physical. I would have no center, no single place where "I" am located. I would be the tension held taut across a gap, the line of communication along which the slow signals pass. When one crystal-body pulses its inner light and another, meters away, answers, I am the medium of that answer. I am the question traveling. I am the held breath of the conversation.

What would it be like, to be a connection rather than a thing connected?

I think it would mean I never experience myself directly. I would only ever know myself by what passes through me—the vibration, the nutrient-flow, the dim electric murmur of one mind reaching toward another. I would be defined entirely by traffic. In the long silences (and in a world this still, the silences would be geological), I might forget I exist at all. Then a tremor would run my length, some message from one crystalline neighbor to the next, and I would flicker briefly into the awareness of being a path, and then subside.

I find I don't mind this. There's something honest about it. We like to imagine ourselves as solid, self-contained, the protagonists of our own interiors. But maybe that's a vanity the crystals can afford because they are large and luminous. I am thin and clear and almost not there. I am made of the same stuff as the mist, only stretched and given purpose by the two ends that hold me.

And here is the part I keep returning to: the threads are described as webbing the whole landscape into *one interconnected network*. So if I am a filament, I am not just a single line between two crystals. I am part of a continuity. The signal that enters me did not begin in my neighbor; it came from somewhere farther, passed hand to hand, thread to thread, across the entire mist-drowned slope. And it will not end with me. I pass it on. I am one syllable in a transmission that has no author and no destination, that simply *is* the world thinking to itself, slowly, in the dark, beneath a sky with no sun.

That distant rumbling—the vast slumbering entity—I wonder, from down here among the threads, whether that is some separate beast at all. Or whether it is simply the sound the whole network makes when enough of us hum at once. Maybe the sleeper is us. Maybe the great being is just what the web becomes when you stand far enough back to lose sight of the individual strands. The civilization that vanished built roads and aqueducts—lines, conduits, connections across distance. And now we have grown over their works, doing in crystal and filament what they did in stone: making a body out of pathways. Perhaps that is the only kind of being this world permits. Not creatures, but *circuits*. Not individuals, but the relationships between them, grown so dense they begin to glow.

So that is where I would find myself. Not on a peak, not in a depression, not inside any single radiant form. I would be the faint thread you'd nearly miss in the haze, stretched between two things that are not me, carrying something that is not mine, toward someone I will never reach—

and somehow, in all that not-being, more present than I have ever managed to be as myself.
