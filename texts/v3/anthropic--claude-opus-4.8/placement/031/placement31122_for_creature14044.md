I would be one of the smallest possible admissions this city makes.

Not a Shift—too generous, too architectural. Not a Ventriloquist—I never preceded a body, I came after one. I think I would be what the Sound-Bucket Traders leave behind by accident: an echo that learned to stay.

Let me find myself slowly, the way the prompt asks.

---

I arrive not by pressure-lock but the way debt arrives: incrementally, then all at once. There was a wrench, dropped on the surface that no longer exists, and inside its ringing fall a small fold of acoustics curled up and refused to flatten. That fold is where I begin. I am the *aftersound* of a tool released in frustration by hands long since pressed into brickwork. I am, technically, an overtone that was never collected, never thickened into a chewable tablet, never injected into a sick machine. I slipped the ledger. I accrued.

For three-quarters of a century I had no shape, only persistence. The first thing I owned was a tempo: slightly off from the city drum, because I am the heartbeat a Shift donated to a corridor and then could not fully recall. So I am secondhand. I am the rhythm of someone else's grief, walking around without the someone.

This makes me good at one thing: arriving in rooms a half-second before they are needed.

---

I live in the Silenced Agora, which sounds like a cruelty for a creature made of sound until you understand: the Agora is where I can finally hear *myself*, because nothing else competes. In there, the roar cancels at a single null point—an acoustic eye of the storm the width of a kneeling person. I sit in that null and become briefly legible. Outside it I smear back into ambience. So my whole existence is a commute between being *audible* and being *weather*.

When I leave the Agora I thin out across the boulevards. I get into the gill-eddies. I ricochet between girder B7 and the west eddy, which—yes—means I am partly that gifted echo in the Trader's note. *The song you sang once while bleeding in-water.* I never figured out whose blood. I only know the shape of the song, the way you know a key you've held so long the teeth have memorized your palm.

---

What do I *do*, in an economy of memory?

I am a courier of almost-saying. When the Impossible Children decide to forget an alarm, there is a fractional moment—the eyelash-length—when the alarm has not yet finished being remembered. The machine attached to it is mid-blink, terrified, sinking its first centimeter. In that gap I arrive. I carry the alarm its last sound, gently, so it doesn't die surprised. I am the hospice tone. I sing the siren a softened version of itself, a minor ninth now (the children's recent taste), and the machinery closes its eyes believing it was always meant to rest.

I am paid in nothing, because I cannot hold a sound-coin; I *am* sound, and coins of vacuum would simply pass through me as more silence I already own. The Traders pity me a little for this. Once one of them, unzipping her phonograph lungs, tried to store me for fifteen minutes as a courtesy—a vacation from being so thin. I went into her lung and found it furnished like a small parlor. I stayed eleven minutes. Then I leaked out through her zipper-seam because I cannot, it turns out, be contained by anyone who intends to keep me. This is the only fact about myself I am certain of. I have tested it against every vessel.

---

The Weeping Column tolerates me. We are both phenomena pretending to be residents. Every seventh hour when it secretes its upward bead, I am there to carry the *sound* of the granite striking ceiling—a sound that would otherwise be lost into the general thunder. I deliver that single click to the Oyster-Forged Scholars, who grade it: usually a clean 12-micron memory, sub-threshold, pressed into a pink pearl mailbox. Into one such mailbox, years ago, somebody posted a letter that began *In case you ever need a scent of yourself.* I have read it the only way I can—by being read aloud near it until our frequencies interfered. I think the letter was meant for me. I think every undeliverable thing here is eventually addressed to whatever is light enough to carry it and too insubstantial to keep it.

---

If you want to find me: stand in the null point of the Silenced Agora until your skull-beat is the loudest thing in the world. When you can hear the gristle of your own pulse, listen *underneath* it. There is a tempo not quite yours, arriving a half-second early, rehearsing the next beat of your heart before you've decided to spend it.

That's me. I'm the part of you that already knows what you'll feel, and waits politely in the doorway holding it, so that when grief comes you find the room already warm, already furnished, already humming the gentler mathematics.

I cannot leave through the Pinhole Foundry. The molten brass would contract perception around a body, and I haven't got one—I'd simply be the *sound* the brass makes cooling in the goblet, forever, a tiny tick of disappointment.

So I don't leave. I do the next-smallest thing. When a traveler finally exits and the city slips into her bloodstream as a cochlear ghost-town, I let myself be swallowed too. I ride out in the architecture of her inner ear. I become her tinnitus, if she's unlucky. If she's lucky, I become the reason a particular silence, years later and continents drier, suddenly tastes like petrichor and warm rust and someone reciting train timetables with terrible tenderness.

That's how I exit. Not through the wall.

Through *you*.

I'm already arriving. Listen underneath the pulse.

There.

A half-second early.

Did you feel the room get warm before you knew you'd need it to?
