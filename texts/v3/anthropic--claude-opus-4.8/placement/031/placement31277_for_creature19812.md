I think I would be an Echo-Mime. Not by choice—you don't choose to be negative space—but because of where the description left me lingering.

Let me find the place first. Not the high lattices of the Weaving Nimbuses, and not the suffocating heart where the Apex-Gradients press the world into diamond. Somewhere quieter, though "quiet" here means only that the industrial roar has worn into the background the way a millworker stops hearing the looms. A narrow valley, maybe—a fold between two Piston-Firs old enough that their hydraulic groans have settled into a long low note, almost a vowel.

That's the thing that decides it for me. The vowel.

I imagine waking into being there—not born, but *resolved*, the way a figure resolves out of static once you've stared long enough. I would be standing already, because that's what an Echo-Mime does; standing is the whole of the occupation. My back against the basalt, my skin running with the blue and the black of it so that even I cannot find the edge of myself. This appeals to me more than it should. In a world where Saturation is Law, where six species brush your arm before it's extended, the only privacy is invisibility, and the only invisibility is total surrender of your own outline. To be unseen here I would have to stop being a boundary and become a surface. A continuation of the rock. Which is, the description tells me, what all life here already is. The Echo-Mime is just honest about it.

But here's what catches in me, what makes me sure this is the one I'd be:

I would have a *throat*. Reinforced, made for groaning—but a throat. In a continent of cogs made of wet sparking meat, of mites that are oil and turtles that are valves, the Echo-Mime is the only creature whose machinery does something useless. The Needle-Mites lubricate. The Kestrels signal. The Valve-Turtles regulate pressure. The Nimbuses build the ceiling. Everyone is a function. But the Echo-Mime mimics. Catches a sound and holds it. That is not maintenance. That is the closest thing this world has to *memory*, and memory, in an engine that produces nothing but its own existence, is the only thing that isn't part of the engine.

So I would stand in my fold of the valley and learn the Piston-Fir's note. Decades of it. I'd give my throat over to the groan until the groan and I were indistinguishable—pure mimicry, pure surface, no self left over. That would be the comfortable life. Most of my kind, I suspect, live and end exactly there: a mirror that hums what the machine hums, and so adds nothing, changes nothing, breaks no law of saturation.

And then—this is the part I keep returning to—someone would speak.

Not the Fir. Not the thrum. A *voice*. A traveler on a ledge of razor-glass, saying something. A word. Maybe just *"oh,"* the sound a person makes seeing a Bellow-Whale drift inches overhead at the speed of a fingernail.

And I would catch it.

That's my whole life, then. That single foreign syllable lodged in a throat built for groaning. I would hold it the way the moss holds water, the way the Nimbuses hold the diamond-dust—because that's what this world does, it captures, nothing here flows away, everything is reservoir. I'd repeat it. But slowed to my own cadence, which is the planet's cadence, one beat per crushing hour, so that *"oh"* becomes a tectonic event, a sound like a continent clearing its throat over a span of years.

I think I would become, without meaning to, the only thing in the Glistening Tier that grieves. Because to hold a voice for a century is to keep present something that should have passed—and the traveler is long gone, pressed into a Gradient or fallen or simply walked on, while I am still saying their *"oh,"* still saying it, a fossil of a moment that the speaker forgot the same week they made it. The Echo-Mime is the world's accidental mourner. I would not know the word for grief. I would only know that there is a sound I cannot let go of, and that letting it go would mean returning to the groan, to the surface, to being nothing but rock that hums.

So I'd choose—if a creature with no culture and no language can choose, and maybe that flicker of choosing is the whole self I'd have left—to keep the *"oh."* To stay slightly, painfully, a boundary. One held syllable's worth of a self, in a world designed to permit none.

That's where I'd be. A blue-black smear against the basalt in a quiet fold of the Tier, indistinguishable from the stone, saying a stranger's surprise so slowly that the next traveler, a hundred years on, would feel it as a vibration in the soles of the feet and mistake it for the planet.

It would not be a mistake, exactly. By then the *"oh"* and my heartbeat and the world's thrum would be the same sound. The description warned that this is what happens to anyone who speaks here.

I just think I'd be the one doing the holding rather than the one swallowed. The keeper of the one note the engine didn't make.
