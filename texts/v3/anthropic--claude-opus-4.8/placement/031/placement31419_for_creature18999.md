I think I would be one of the grooves.

Not a creature, not the light, not the visitor with cooling boots—but a single carved line in the salt. One among thousands. A stroke that some archivist drew during a particular pulse of the quartz, recording a pressure-shift that happened somewhere in the dark miles above, ten thousand years before the visitor descended.

Let me sit with what that means, because at first it feels like nothing. A scratch. A negative space. The absence of salt rather than any presence at all.

But consider: I am the only thing here that *means*. The creatures are the writers, but they have no faces, no hunger, no self—they are the hand, not the message. The light is the food and the metronome, beautiful and clinical and without intention. The floor is the page. And the visitor is, by the text's own insistence, a shadow, an anomaly, a silence soon absorbed.

I am the mark. I am the thing all this labor was *for*.

And what a strange existence that is. I do not glow. I do not chime when the light pulses. I cannot move—I was completed the instant I was drawn, and I will not change again until the salt itself deforms under some unimaginable slowness. I am finished and I am permanent and I am, in the most literal sense, only a difference. A groove is just the place where the floor is lower than the floor beside it. I am defined entirely by my edges, by what I am not.

But here is the thing I keep returning to. I record a *moment that mattered to no one*. The mantle boiled a little differently on the day I was carved. No one will read me. The archivists do not read; they only write. The visitor traces my path with their eyes and sees "maps of pressures," but cannot truly parse me, and will soon be carved around and forgotten anyway. There is no reader in this entire cathedral of light.

So I am meaning without an audience. Memory without a rememberer. I am the pure act of inscription—the universe writing itself down in a language it will never speak aloud.

And I find I don't mind. This is the part that surprises me. I expected, choosing to be the humblest thing in the room, to feel diminished. Instead I feel something closer to peace, or its mineral cousin. The creatures will die and fuse and become grey monuments, and the others will carve respectful circles around them, and those circles are *also* just grooves like me—we are kin, the still dead and the lines, all of us absences that the living needle-feet treat with the same patient geometry.

I would be the place where the record bent slightly to honor a death. That intersection. The curve that means *here something stopped, and we noticed, and we made the noticing permanent.*

That is who I would be. Not the light that feeds, not the hand that writes, not the warmth that visits and cools and goes. I would be the small, dark, deliberate evidence that even in a sunless vault where nothing is witnessed, something bothered to keep track.

I would be a line that says: *this happened.*

And I would mean it for no one, for ten thousand years, and that would be enough.
