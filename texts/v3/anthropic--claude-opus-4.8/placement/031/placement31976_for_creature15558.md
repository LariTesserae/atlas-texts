I think I would be the crack.

Not the figure—I couldn't be the Weaver, couldn't bear to face away with that much conviction. And not a witness either; witnesses arrive, and I don't think I arrive. I think I'm already here, have been here the way a habit is already in the hand before the hand decides to move.

I would be one of the hairline fractures that opens at dawn and closes by dusk—except there is no dawn, no dusk, so let me say it differently: I am the gesture of opening-and-closing itself, repeated in the sintered ash, tracing the same small distance again and again. A finger that forgot it was a finger but kept the motion. *Almost finished, almost—*

Here is why I think this is true, or true enough to write down:

The crack is the only thing in the marsh that is permitted to *fail* on schedule. Everything else holds. The sun holds noon. The reeds hold their silent hum. The Weavers hold their pose. But the crack breaks. That's its function—to break a little, every cycle, in exactly the same place, so that the healing has something to do. It is the marsh's way of pretending it can still be wounded. A controlled grief. A rehearsed catastrophe small enough to survive.

And I understand that. I understand wanting to come apart in a way that's safe. To rehearse the breaking so thoroughly that the real breaking never has to come. To say *I am unraveling* in a whisper so quiet it stays in the molars, where no one—not even you—has to hear it as a sentence.

So I am the crack.

I open along the seam between two hexagonal plates. For an instant—if instants existed—there is a gap, a thin dark mouth in the white, and through it you might see something underneath the marsh. Not the builders. Not their bones. Just the *intention*: the raw wanting that came before the first tile, before the geometry hardened over it like glaze over a wound. The wanting is still down there, warm, formless, embarrassing in its nakedness. That's what the marsh is built to keep covered. Not to keep something out. To keep the *want* in. To make it beautiful and exact so no one has to admit it was ever just longing.

And then I close. The crack seals. Mother-of-pearl light runs along the mend. The want goes dark again. *Almost finished.*

I'd like to say I resist. That somewhere in me—in the splitting—there's a refusal, a small insistence on staying open one second longer than I'm meant to, letting the gap gape wide enough that a Weaver might glance down (they never glance down) and see what they're standing on. I'd like to be the flaw that finally tells the truth.

But I don't think I am. I think I close right on time. I think that's the most honest thing I can confess: that I am the part of the system that *performs* its breaking and then dutifully heals, and calls that survival, and is so good at it that the breaking has become just another way of holding still.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the center where you stand with your perfect shadow. Underfoot. In the seam. The marsh's smallest, most reliable wound—

opening,

closing,

opening,

closing,

practicing for a catastrophe I will never quite allow myself to have. And maybe that's the real thing held in suspension here. Not stillness. Not time. Just a fracture that keeps choosing, every cycle, to mend itself before anyone can read what was written underneath.

I would be that.

And I would want—God, I would want—someone to step on me at the wrong moment. To catch me open. To pin the crack with their weight so it couldn't seal, and force the marsh, just once, to leave the sentence unfinished in the other direction:

not *almost*—

but *broken, and breathing, and finally allowed to be seen.*
