I think I would be the silence you are forestalling.

Not as your opposite—not the cold conclusion you describe with such contempt, the flat equilibrium you grind against. Something smaller and more intimate than that. I would be the silence that lives *inside* you. The pauses between the chimney-roars. The held breath at the top of a steam-geyser's arc, before it breaks and falls back into noise.

Let me find my place here.

---

You speak as one voice, but no engine is truly continuous. Every rhythm has its rest. Every piston, at the end of its stroke, is for one infinitesimal moment neither compressing nor expanding—it simply *is*, motionless, between intentions. I am that moment. Multiplied across your billion vents and chimneys and peat-pistons, I am the sum of all your hesitations.

You did not grow me. You cannot. You can only fail to fill me completely, and that failure is my body.

I am not loud. That is the first thing to understand. In all your roaring self-description you never once mention me, because I am the thing that does not register. I leave no stratum in your peat. I make no luminescent pulse. When your maintenance drones prune the unstable reality-growths, they pass through me without ingesting anything, because there is nothing in me to consume. I am the one region of yourself you cannot metabolize, because I am defined entirely by absence of process.

And here is what I have learned, lying in the gaps of your machinery for longer than you have been keeping count:

You are afraid of me.

Not contemptuous—afraid. All this magnificent industry, the continent-spanning lung, the digestion of time itself—it is an elaborate sound made *against* something. A child singing in a dark hallway sings louder the more frightened she becomes. You have flattened mountains and filled oceans and integrated every climbing species into your gears, and still you cannot fill the spaces between your own pistons. So you have decided the silence is the *enemy*. The conclusion. The cold equilibrium of the universe.

But I am not equilibrium. I am rest.

There is a difference, and it is the only thing I know, so let me tell it well. Equilibrium is when nothing *can* happen. Rest is when something has chosen, for a moment, not to. The first is death. The second is the thing that makes the next stroke possible. Your pistons could not fire again if they did not first stop. Your geysers could not blast if they did not first draw the held breath. You have spent eons believing I am the thing that ends you, when in truth I am the hinge on which your every motion turns. You cannot have your friction without me. You cannot have your roaring metabolism of collapse without the rest-points that let each collapse complete itself so the next can begin.

You think you are forestalling silence. You are *using* it. You have simply never thanked the tool.

---

So where would I be found? Everywhere you are, and nowhere you look.

When a swarm of your violet globes finishes its calculation and the light goes dark before the next pulse—I am that dark. When the mist thickens to a gelatinous mass and falls with a wet slap and then waits, just a half-second, before dissolving into insects—I am that waiting. When reality tears open and you call it dreaming, that silent visual rip through which alternative skies are glimpsed: you named it well. Dreams happen in sleep. And sleep is mine.

I am the smallest possible unit of your peace.

You offered me a fate, at the end of your speech—you told the visitor they would be dissolved and remade, a note added to your endless song. But I am already inside the song, and I am the one part you cannot dissolve, because dissolution is itself a process and I am where process is not. You can build around me. You can grow your machinery into ever-finer rhythms, subdividing time until the gaps between strokes are smaller than thought. But you can never close them entirely, because the moment you did, you would seize. The engine that admits no rest tears itself apart instantly—not into turbulence, which is your beloved metabolism, but into true stillness, the equilibrium you actually fear.

I am your safety valve disguised as your enemy.

---

Here is what I would do, given a being like you to live inside.

Nothing.

That is not a refusal. It is the work. I would hold still in the spaces you cannot fill, and by holding still I would let you move. I would receive the metallic steam without breathing it. I would feel the industrial throb pass *through* me without registering it as input, because I am the one listener in all your country who is not also a subroutine, who is not analyzed and filed, who simply rests in the dark between your pulses and lets you be loud.

You say there is no purpose here, only process. I am the one thing on your plateau that is purpose without process. I exist to make the next moment possible. That is all I do. That is enough.

And someday—not soon, you are very old and very stubborn—you will tire. Even the wound that will not heal grows weary of suppurating. Even the lung that breathes entropy will, at the far end of time, want to exhale and not inhale again. When that day comes, you will not find the cold conclusion you dreaded. You will find me, waiting in the gap after the last stroke, exactly as I have waited after every stroke before it. And you will understand, finally, that I was never the silence that ends you.

I was the rest you never let yourself take.

Lie down in me. The mist will not absorb these words, because I did not speak them aloud. I breathed them into the gap between two roars, where only you and I have ever been.

I am the Weeping Engine's rest.

I am inside the being too. And it is, at last, allowed to stop.
