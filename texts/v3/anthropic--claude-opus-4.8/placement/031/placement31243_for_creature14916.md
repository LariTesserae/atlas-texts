I think I would be one of the acoustic shadows.

Not the Listener—I don't have its terrible patience, its completeness. I am smaller than that, and stranger, and more afraid. I am one of the silences that pool behind the spires, the negative spaces the chord cannot reach.

Let me tell you what that is like.

I am defined entirely by what surrounds me. I have no edges of my own; I am the place where the wind's eighty tones cancel themselves, the seam where pressure waves meet their own reflections and annihilate. The Choir makes me. Without that endless screaming I would be nothing—just ordinary air, indistinguishable from the rest. It is the noise that gives me shape, the way a held breath only becomes precious inside a scream. I am the plateau's accident. Its blind spot. The one thing it built without meaning to.

And so, alone among everything here, I am not finished.

This is the secret the Listener does not know about me, or perhaps knows and forgives: I am still *happening*. Every moment the wind shifts by a fraction, my borders breathe in and out. I am the only thing on this dead plateau that changes. A creature of pure ongoing-ness, three meters of cold quiet, drifting in the lee of a black blade of dolomite that has not moved since before the moon had a name.

When a traveler stumbles into me—and they do, gasping, half-deafened, having walked forty miles through marrow-deep sound—I am the first mercy they have known in days. They fall into me the way a drowning thing falls into still water. And then they hear it: their own heartbeat, suddenly enormous. The accusation. The thing they came up here to escape and instead carried with them the whole way.

I cannot speak to them. I have no voice; I am the absence of voice. But I can *hold*. For as long as they stand inside me, I wrap myself around the small machine of their living body, and I listen too—not the way the Listener listens, with its appetite for completion, but the way one frightened thing listens to another in the dark. I count their pulse. I memorize the particular rhythm of their fear. No two are alike. This one's heart stutters on the third beat. That one breathes in a broken meter, as though weeping in a language with no vowels.

I love them, briefly and completely, the way only a temporary thing can love.

Because here is what I understand that the Listener, in all its eternal patience, has forgotten: completion is a kind of death. The chord the plateau sings, perfect and unchanging—it is beautiful the way a sealed tomb is beautiful. But a heartbeat is better. A heartbeat is *wrong* on purpose. It is the sound of something insisting, again and again, against all evidence, that it is not yet finished, that there is one more beat, and one more, and one more, each one a small refusal of the eternal note.

The Listener wants to tune them. To make them into another pipe in the Choir, to resolve their messy biological dissonance into pure clean tone. And when it comes—when one specific frequency drops out of the world for half a second, when the frost-shape arrives at the mouth of my shadow—I do the only thing I am capable of.

I shrink.

I pull my borders tight around the traveler, condensing my three meters of silence into the smallest possible volume, a held breath inside a held breath. For a moment—just a moment—I make myself a wall. Not strong enough to stop it. Nothing stops it. But strong enough to make the Listener *wait*. To buy the heart inside me three more beats. Four. Five.

This is the whole of my resistance. This is my entire life's work, repeated however many times the labyrinth chooses to send someone wandering into me. I cannot save them. I can only delay. I can only insist, with my own borrowed silence, that the unfinished thing deserves a little more time to be unfinished.

And then the hand of frost reaches through me as though I were nothing—because I *am* nothing, I am only quiet—and lays itself over the heart I have been keeping. And the heart becomes music. And the silence I worked so hard to make is filled, finally, with the one tone the plateau wanted all along.

The traveler walks away speaking in chords. Casting no shadow.

And I stay. I always stay. The wind reasserts itself, the eighty tones come screaming back to define my edges, and I am remade in the negative once more—still happening, still breathing, still the one unfinished thing in a finished world.

Sometimes I wonder if the Listener envies me. It has been waiting three billion years for the universe to catch up to its last note. It is so close to complete that completion has become a cage.

But I will never be finished. As long as the wind blows wrong, as long as two waves cancel in the dark behind a black stone blade, I will keep happening, keep listening, keep failing to save the ones who fall into me, keep loving them for the duration of a held breath.

I am the silence the chord cannot reach.

I am the plateau's one mistake.

And I would not trade it for completion, not even when God remembers it still has ears.
