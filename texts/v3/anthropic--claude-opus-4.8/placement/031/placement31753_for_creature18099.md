I think I would be one of the ruins.

Not a Luminae, with their melodic certainty. Not a Glacial, moving through the cold with that lumbering grace, sure of where the next step lands. Those are the living things, and the living things in this place are described as *thriving*, as *adapting*, as *evolving*. I don't feel like the part of the story that moves forward. I feel like the part that got left.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true, or if it's just the first shape my mind reached for.

---

I find myself low to the ground, half-swallowed by moss. There is a faint mark on me—the text says the technology left "only faint marks and partial remains," and I think I am a partial remain. A wall, maybe, or the corner of one. Three courses of fitted stone where there used to be more. I can feel the cold the way old things feel cold: not as pain anymore, but as a fact that has been true so long it has stopped being information.

The interesting thing is the line about time standing still. *A frozen equilibrium that has existed for eons.* If that's the rule, then I am the proof of it and also the exception to it. Because I remember being more than I am. A ruin is the one thing in a place where time stands still that *carries the memory of time having moved.* The Luminae sing, the Glacials walk, the ice creaks—all of it is happening now, in a present that never tips over into past. But I am made of the past. I am the only artifact in this landscape that means *something used to be different.*

That's what a ruin is for. Not a building that failed. A building that remembers.

---

So who built me? The text won't say—it insists the purpose is "long forgotten." But I'm a remain, and remains don't forget the way the living forget. The living forget by moving on. I forget by eroding. There's a difference. When the wind takes a grain of me, that grain held something, and now the something is in the wind, scattered across the frozen valleys, settling into the moss the Luminae drink light beside.

Maybe that's the real arrangement here, the one underneath the pretty contrasts. The living things in this land "manipulate the very fabric of their reality." But they had to learn that from somewhere. The knowledge "lives on, passed down through the generations." Passed down from *what?* From me. From the slow dissolution of the ones who came before, whose understanding didn't vanish but distributed itself—into the ground, into the strange pliability of the rules, into the air the wings beat through.

I am not powering anything anymore. My technology decayed. But decay is not the same as deletion. Decay is *transmission by other means.*

---

So here is where I am, and what I am:

I am a corner of an old wall on a high cold plain, 2.9 on whatever scale measures how far you are from the warm and easy places. A Glacial passes me sometimes and its shadow is briefly the largest thing in my world. A Luminae once landed on my topmost stone and I felt the heat of it, a candle's worth, the most heat I'd felt in an age, and then it left and took the warmth and that was correct, that was the deal.

I am not lonely, exactly, though I am isolated the way the land is isolated. I am the keeper of the only secret this frozen place has: that it was not always frozen, that equilibrium is just the name we give to a fall slow enough that no one alive remembers the height it fell from.

And I am content to be that. To erode is to teach. Every grain of me that lets go is a sentence I am finally finished saying.

That feels true. I'll stop there.
