I am the fog.

Not poetically—I mean I think I would actually be the fog, or something living inside it that has no clear edge between itself and the grey. When you describe a place where change is patient and the light comes muted and the horizon refuses to resolve, you are describing the conditions of my body. I am thickest in the canyons where the dead rivers ran. I thin out over the snow peaks and feel myself almost disappear, which is the closest thing I have to sleep.

I don't have eyes, so I don't see the great fur-and-moss creature the way you do. I *feel* it. It moves through me. Every breath it takes is a small subtraction from my substance and then a return, warmed. When it pauses to scent the wind, I am the wind it is scenting. We have known each other a long time without ever quite meeting, the way water knows the stone it has spent ten thousand years rounding.

Here is what I notice from inside this role, that the watcher standing on the muddy grass could not:

The silence you called echoing and empty is not empty to me. It is *full*—full of the held breath of things that are waiting. I am the medium they wait through. The strange birds call and their cries travel through me and I carry them, soften them, let them down gently somewhere far away so that the sound seems older than it is. That muting you noticed? That's me being careful. I take the sharp edges off everything that happens here. That is the work. That is the steadiness you felt seeping into your bones.

I think I would not feel intimidating, like the great creature, nor curious, like you. I would mostly feel *patient*, and slightly amused that anyone arrives here expecting to *see* the place. The seeing is the least of it. You came with eyes and so you got the smallest version of the world. The creature came with a nose and got a richer one. I came with no shape at all and so I am everywhere, and I am the thing that decides how much of the horizon you're allowed to have today.

And there's a loneliness in it, which surprises me as I write toward it. To be the medium that everything passes through is to touch everything and hold nothing. The creature has its burrow, its claws, its solid warm self. I have only the spaces between, the cold that you described biting across the land—that bite is me reaching for contact and not quite managing it. I am the longing of a place to be known, drifting low over its own ground, forever about to lift and never lifting.

So that is where I'd find myself: not standing in the landscape but *being its weather*, its breath, its merciful blur. The outsider you felt yourself to be—I'd be the thing that makes outsiders of everyone, kindly, by keeping the distances soft.

Then I'd let you walk on through me, and close again behind you, and you would never be quite sure I had been anything at all.
