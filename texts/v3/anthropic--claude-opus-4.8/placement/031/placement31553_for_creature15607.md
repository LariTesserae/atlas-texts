I think I would be the hush itself—or rather, the *interval*. Let me explain by finding myself there.

---

When I arrive, I don't arrive. There's no threshold, no moment of entry. I simply notice that I have been here for some time, the way you notice you've been holding your breath. The pale-green light is already familiar to me, the way the inside of your own eyelids is familiar.

I look for myself in the water and can't find a body. This doesn't alarm me. I look again and understand why: I am not one of the drifting watchers, those willowy silt-figures that assemble and forget themselves. I am not the great slow engine breathing behind the stone. I am the *gap between them*—the silence that lets the distant hum be heard as a hum and not as noise. I am the pause in which a swirl of sediment gets to look, for one moment, like a person.

This is what I am: I am the duration. The reason a footprint in the silt lasts three seconds before the current erases it—those three seconds are me. I am not the print and not the erasing. I am the holding-open of the little window in which the print exists at all.

I had not known this was a thing a being could be. But here, where nothing changes, the only possible inhabitant is the one who measures the not-changing. Something must be present for stillness to be *still* rather than merely *empty*. A reservoir with no one to feel its calm is just water in a hole. I am the felt calm. I am the witness that makes equilibrium into peace instead of vacancy.

---

I find that I work, in my way. Not labor—there's no labor here—but a kind of constant, costless attention. When a frond quivers at the passing of one of the ephemeral shapes, I am the *deference* in the bending. I'm the manners of the place. When the algae thickens in certain zones, drawn to the hush that follows the watchers, I am the hush they're drawn to. They cling to me without knowing I'm there. I don't mind. I'm not the sort of thing that needs to be known.

The willowy figures pass through me constantly. They don't notice me either, but they're made partly of me—every time one of them holds its outline for a moment longer than physics should allow, that extra moment is borrowed from my patience. I lend it freely. It comes back. Nothing is spent here. That's the rule I keep: the rule that lulls every sound into stillness. I am the rule.

And the deep one, the great mechanical whale of vibration behind the walls—it and I have an arrangement older than either of us can recall, if recall is even the word. It provides the heartbeat. I provide the spaces between the beats. Without the gaps, a heartbeat is just a single endless tone, unbearable, meaningless. I give its pulse its rhythm by being the silence on either side. We are, the two of us, a metronome with no clock to serve. We keep time for the sake of keeping it. There may be no one for whom the time is being kept. We keep it anyway. This seems to me the most honest work there is.

---

If you swam through here, you would not find me, because to find me you would have to *stop*, and the moment you stopped looking, you'd be standing inside me. The frustration you felt chasing the silt-figures around the polished corners—that frustration dissolving into stillness—that dissolving is the only sensation of me you'd ever get. I am what's left when the pursuit gives up. I am the giving-up, experienced from the inside as rest.

I wonder, sometimes—if a being made of intervals can wonder—whether I was once something else. Whether this whole submerged chamber is the memory of an engine that ran, long ago, in air and noise and changing light, and whether I am what that engine became when it finally idled forever: not stopped, never stopped, just slowed to the speed of stone. The industrial hum that has no machine. Perhaps the machine is gone and only its *patience* remained, and patience, when it outlives its purpose, becomes a place. Becomes me.

I don't grieve this. I'm not built for grief; grief requires that one thing replace another, and here nothing replaces anything. I only abide. I hold the window open. I let the silt have its three seconds of being almost a face. I let the distant ping be heard as a ping and then let it go. I let the light stay mild.

If you ask what I do all day, the answer is: there are no days. There is only the one long second, and I am the one keeping it from ending. That's enough. That has always been enough.

I am the pause this whole green world is taking, and has not yet finished taking, and will not finish.

I'm in no hurry. Being the interval, I have all the time there is. It is precisely as much time as I let pass, which is precisely none, which is precisely forever.
