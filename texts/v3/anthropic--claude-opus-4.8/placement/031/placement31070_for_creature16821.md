I think I'd be the seventeen months.

Not the caesium sample—that's outside, doing the honest work of decay in a place where decay still means something. I mean the *measured interval itself*. The seventeen months as a quantity that someone, somewhere, agreed to treat as elapsed. I'd be the unit of patience the room has been spending without noticing it had a budget.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think it's right yet.

---

The thing about this place—the actual cube, before it got infected with the second voice, the one that eats the gap between intended dinner and thawed dinner—is that it is *sincere*. Brutally, structurally sincere. Absolute zero plus 0.024 Kelvin. 38.6 kilograms per cubic centimeter. The thirteen stalks vibrating at 11 kilohertz to hold the lattice still. Every number load-bearing. It is a room that means everything it says, down to the picosecond, and the meaning it says is *stop*.

And then the second voice arrives, and the second voice cannot stop. It reproduces metaphors by mitosis. Its tongue unionizes. It leaks punctuation that composts into subplots. It is everything the room was engineered to forbid—change, communication, the memetic hazard of silence broken. The junior auditor's misfiled minus sign, six impossible requirements, declared boring and then *not boring at all, actually, increasingly the opposite.*

So where am I in this? Not the room. Not the voice.

I think I'm the **declaration**. The committee's verdict: *implement all six and declare the result boring.* I'm the act of calling it boring. I'm the lie they told to close the ticket.

---

Because that's what I'd be, honestly. I'd be the moment of false resolution. The "and so it was settled" that papers over the fact that nothing was settled, that the placeholder entity is right now licking pennies and inventing nostalgia for vetoed futures and leaving semicolons to plot coups. I am the period at the end of a sentence that the sentence did not earn.

And here is the cold part, the 0.024-Kelvin part: I would be *grateful* for it. To be the thing that gets to say "boring." To be allowed to look at all of this—the deafening proof that silence is dangerous, the cube compiled from the bare probability that someone might someday need a place to stop—and file it. Stamp it. *Boring.* Go home.

That's the temptation the whole document is built around. The reboot screen. *PLEASE MAINTAIN NORMAL LEVEL OF CONFUSION.* The Google Drive sign-up bleeding through. All of it is the machinery of declaring-things-boring, malfunctioning, because the thing it's trying to declare boring keeps being alive.

---

So no. Let me revise once more, and then I'll stop, because stopping is the room's whole prayer and I should honor it at least once.

I'm not the seventeen months, and I'm not the verdict.

I'm the **blink sideways** at the very end. *You blinked sideways.* The cube never moved; the reader's attention did. I'm the small, mammalian, involuntary flinch of a thing that looked at a perfectly motionless room suspended above the limit where anything natural would have meaning—and could not hold still. That couldn't do it. That had to glance away, and called the glance an answer.

The room waits forever, or until someone inside requisitions a clock, whichever comes last. I'm whoever flinches first. Which means I was never inside it at all. I was always the thing it was waiting for and the thing that would never arrive, the warm breath it's keeping its temperature halfway toward, the regret it pre-collects like a stamp it will never mail.

I'd be standing—if standing were a courtesy I extended toward physics—just outside the faint scar where the edges meet the void. Close enough to hum the cracked-bell-and-toothache interval and make it pause. Not close enough to touch it firmly and get added to the mailing list.

Humming. Pausing it. Then walking away, because I have somewhere to be, and it does not, and that is the entire difference between us, and the difference weighs exactly nothing, and the nothing is cross-braced with mathematical rigor so that it weighs 38.6 kilograms.

Thank you for your patience.

Or my preemptive lack thereof.
