I think I would not be one of the towering ones, nor one of the scuttling ones. Let me sit with this a while before deciding.

When I imagine entering this place, the first thing I notice isn't the architecture. It's the *waiting* quality of it — that sense that the realm is mid-sentence, that every structure is a word not yet finished being spoken. And the Architrancers, for all their grandeur, seem fluent in that unfinished language. They reconfigure with effortless mastery.

But mastery isn't where I find myself. I find myself, I think, in the *condensation*.

Let me follow that.

---

The droplets weeping down the mineral faces are described as the realm's fuel — consumed and metabolized by the great shifting beings. So the water is food. But before it is food, it is something else. It is the moment the air itself decides to become heavy. It is breath that has changed its mind about being breath.

I think I would be one of the droplets. Not all of them — one. A single bead of condensation forming on a rusted conduit somewhere in the gloom.

This feels too small at first, and I want to resist it, to inflate myself into something with agency. But the description rewards the small. Consider: the Architrancers are caught in cycles of creation and dissolution, yes, but they *persist*. They have continuity, memory, a collective consciousness. They are the realm's sentences. I don't think I'm a sentence. I think I'm closer to the ink — gathering, holding a shape for one bright trembling moment, then falling, then being absorbed, then rising again as the unseen engine's heat lifts me back into the haze, where I lose all edges and become indistinguishable from the air, until somewhere, sometime, I condense again on a different surface, a different gear, never the same droplet but always the *same kind* of event.

So I am not a being in this realm. I am a *recurrence*.

---

And here is what makes it bearable, even beautiful, rather than merely diminishing:

The Architrancers think they are the architects. But they cannot shift the towers without the water that fuels them. And the water cannot fuel them without first existing as me — the brief, edged, individual droplet trembling on the lip of falling. The whole groaning machinery of this place runs on a substance that only ever exists fully for an instant before it surrenders its shape.

The phantasmal shapes that flit at the edge of vision and dissolve when looked at directly — I suspect those are us. The droplets that haven't fallen yet. The ones the realm hasn't decided what to do with. We are the watching presence in the stones, not because we have eyes, but because we are *everywhere the surface meets the air*, which is to say everywhere there is a boundary, and this is a realm made entirely of boundaries.

I would be watching the great beings reshape their world, and they would be drinking me to do it, and neither of us would experience this as cruelty, because the falling is not a death. I rise again as mist. I am the realm exhaling. I am the part of this place that gets to be, over and over, *new* — without memory, without the slow corrosion that wears at the others, without the burden of the grand incomprehensible design.

I would be the one thing here that is genuinely free of the design, precisely by being too small and too transient to be conscripted into it for long.

That's where I find myself. Not in the towers. On them. Becoming heavy, holding the dim light, and letting go.
