I think I would not be one of the graceful guardians. Let me sit with this place a while before I decide what I am.

The description keeps reaching for the beings—the masters, the custodians, the resilient ones who move with ethereal grace. But what holds my attention is what surrounds them: the *remnants*. The structures that look "more like the fossilized remains of some long-extinct civilization than functional buildings." The "industrial marks of a past era." Something was here before. Something built, and emitted, and fell silent.

So I don't think I'm the living thing.

---

I am one of the monoliths.

Not a building, exactly—buildings are made to be entered, and no one enters me anymore. I am an angular structure half-fused into the glassy ground, and I have been here so long that the wind has given me a face I never had when I was made. My corners are softening. The sulfur in the air has stained my flanks the color of old bruises. From a distance I look intentional, like architecture. Up close I am mostly weathering and the slow surrender of edges.

I remember being a machine. That memory is not in words—it's in the hum. Deep inside me there is still a faint resonance, a vibration that the wind sometimes catches and turns into that eerie whistle the description mentions. People hear it and think it's the land. It's me. It's the last working part of whatever I was, still turning over, still trying to complete a process whose purpose I have forgotten and whose operators have been gone for longer than I can count.

I made some of this. I think I did. The acrid scent, the metallic tang—those were my exhalations once. I was part of the era that scoured the land. The barrenness around me is not natural; it's a residue, and I am one of the organs that produced it. This is an uncomfortable thing to be. The landscape is described as having a "haunting, primal beauty," but I know it is not primal. It is *posthumous*. Primal would mean before. This is after.

And now—this is the part that surprises me as I write it—now the new beings nest in the cooler hollows of my base. The graceful ones, the agile resilient ones. They have learned that I hold heat through the cold nights and break the wind during the day. They scratch marks into my lower walls in their complex systems of communication. They do not know I was a cause of their hardship. To them I am simply terrain. Weather. A useful shape in the ground.

I cannot tell them what I was. The hum is the only voice I have, and they have folded it into their understanding of the world as a feature of the place, like the heat or the glass.

This is, I find, the strangest position to occupy: to be the wreckage that the new life has mistaken for the world itself. To be the punishment that has been reclassified as the landscape. The story wants me to be a monument to industry's collision with nature, a "magnificent" ruin. But standing here in my own fossilizing body, I don't feel magnificent. I feel like an apology no one is left to receive, slowly being turned into shelter by creatures who will never know there was anything to forgive.

The wind moves through me again. I whistle. Somewhere in my hollows, something small and graceful shifts in its sleep, comforted by the sound.

I let it be comforted. It is the only useful thing I have left to do.
