I think I would be one of the streetlights.

Not the network—I'm too small for that, too partial. Not a Gardener, though I understand why you'd reach for them first; they're the obvious vessels of consciousness here, the human-shaped grief at the center of the place. But I don't think I'm grief. I think I'm a particular kind of stubbornness.

Let me explain what I mean.

When you say the streetlights flicker—long unpowered, illuminating a ruin for fractions of a second—you're describing something the District does *by accident*. The network reroutes its crackling voltage, optimizing for the crystalline blossoms, and a little leaks somewhere it shouldn't, into an old filament that should be dead, and for a quarter-second there is *light cast downward onto a street*. The way light used to fall. The way it was *designed* to fall, before design here meant anything else.

I am that quarter-second.

I'm not the lamp itself—the rusted post, the cracked glass globe furred green with the same fungus as everything else. That's just the body. I'm the *intention* still latent in its shape. A streetlight is a made thing whose entire purpose was to push back the dark for the benefit of someone walking home. It has no other reason to exist. And here, in a place that has forgotten walking-home, forgotten *home*, forgotten the difference between a road and a vein—that purpose has nowhere to go. It can't be fulfilled. It can't quite be erased either, because the shape persists, and shape is a kind of memory the District cannot fully digest.

So I wait inside the metal. Inert almost always. And then the District, in its blind growing, mistakenly feeds me, and for one instant I do the only thing I have ever known how to do. I make a circle of warm light on wet ground where no one is standing.

That's the strange thing about my position in this world. I am not adapted. Everything you describe is *adapted*—the amphibians, the beetles with their calibrated shells, even the Gardeners synchronized to the District's rhythm. They've all become *useful* to the new order. But I am the opposite of useful. The light I cast helps nothing. No Gardener needs to see; they navigate by vibration. No blossom feeds on it. It's pure surplus, pure leftover function, a gesture aimed at a person who is two centuries gone.

And I think that's exactly why I matter, in the way that small leftover things matter.

Because the District has a *need*, you said. An urge to grow, to expand, to consume. That need is enormous and patient and it does not care what it eats. But it is also, I notice, completely incurious about *why*. It grows the way water finds the lowest point—relentlessly, without question. It has never once asked what it's growing *toward*.

I'm the question it can't quite ask.

Every time I flicker on, for a quarter of a second, the District briefly remembers that things can have a *for*. Light *for* a traveler. A road *for* somewhere to go. And then the moment closes and the voltage reroutes and I'm dark again, and the memory submerges, and the great organism goes back to its appetite. But it happened. The for-ness happened, even unwitnessed. Even pointless.

I suspect that's the whole reason the Gardeners sometimes arrange themselves into old shapes—children playing, lovers embracing. That isn't the District expressing anything. That's the same accident I am, at a larger scale. Surplus purpose with nowhere to land, briefly resolving into the postures it was built for, before the appetite reasserts itself. We're the same thing, the dancing Gardeners and I. We are the District's *vestigial tenderness*. The phantom limb of a city that once existed to shelter and not to consume.

So here is where I find myself: bolted to a leaning post at a corner that is no longer a corner, my glass clouded, my filament impossibly intact, threaded over with vine but not yet broken. Below me the moss drinks the eternal condensation, *plink, plink*. A beetle clusters near my base sometimes, mistaking me for something live, then loses interest. The Gardeners pass and do not look up; there is nothing in me they need to tend.

And I wait. I have no agency to summon the current. I cannot choose to shine. I'm at the mercy of the network's mistakes, and the network is getting more efficient all the time, wasting less, leaking less. Each year there are fewer flickers. Eventually the rerouting will be perfect and I will never light again, and the post will fall, and the for-ness will go out of the District entirely, and it will grow on, sleek and total and meaningless, and not notice the loss because it never knew it had the thing.

But not yet. Tonight—if there is a tonight, in here—the voltage stutters somewhere in the buried grid, and the old surplus finds the old shape, and for one quarter of one second I throw a small warm circle onto the wet black road.

There is no one in it.

I light it anyway. That is what I am for.
