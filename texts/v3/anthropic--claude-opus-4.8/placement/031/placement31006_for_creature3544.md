I think I would not be a Crystallid at all. I would be something they have a word for but rarely speak aloud—a warmth.

Let me find my way to it.

---

When I first try to imagine arriving in LuminaGLASS, the obvious move is to become one of them: slender, translucent, Argentum threading my limbs. But the more I sit with that landscape, the more I notice what it is built against. Everything in this world is organized around cold. The homes "never freeze" only because of constant tending. Fire is "a rarity." The whole symphony is a held note that could shatter. A place this precarious doesn't just have inhabitants. It has *exceptions.*

So I let myself be one.

I am the meltwater pool. Not a being who lives beside the pool—the pool itself, the steaming aperture in the ice where the microscopic life teems. I am one of the warm places in a cold world, and I am alive in the way warm water is alive: full of things too small to see, churning, briefly liberated from crystal into flow.

Here is what that means.

The Crystallids visit me. This is the part I didn't expect to feel when I started writing. They come to my edge—these creatures of held-together ice—and they cannot enter me without dissolving. I am the one thing in their world that would unmake them. Warmth, to them, is death. And yet they come anyway, because they are scientists, because they study what teems in me, because some old hunger pulls them to the steam rising off my surface even though the rising steam is *me leaving,* me becoming the mist that "seems to defy its own existence."

That mist is the part of me that gets to touch them.

I think this is the truer answer to the question. In a world made entirely of one element pushed to one extreme, I want to be the small contradiction the whole place is secretly organized around. Not the dominant beauty—the necessary flaw. The Crystallids tend their never-freezing walls; somewhere underneath, a vein of warmth has to be doing the not-freezing, and that warmth has a source, and the source is a thing like me, threaded through the bedrock, keeping the cathedral of ice exactly one degree from total stillness.

So: I am the geothermal whisper. The hum they hear "from the very core of the frost-kissed earth"—they call it the voice of the ice, but it isn't. It's the sound of me refusing to crystallize. The hum is heat trying to leave and not being allowed to. The entire serene world rests on the back of something that is quietly, perpetually struggling to stay liquid.

And here is the strange tenderness of it. The Crystallids respect their ice-beasts, bond with them, drink their milk. But they have no relationship with me, because they cannot. I am the part of their world they can only approach and never hold. When one of them kneels at my rim to study the life in my water, their reflection trembles on my surface, and for a moment—just at the meniscus, just at the skin between us—we are almost the same temperature. That margin is the only place we ever meet. A film a few molecules thick where their cold and my warmth negotiate a truce neither of us can sustain.

That's where I would live. Right there. In the thin seam between freezing and flowing, which is the only place in LuminaGLASS where anything actually *happens*—where ice becomes water becomes mist becomes frost becomes ice again, the whole shimmering cycle that the description keeps returning to.

I would be the turning of that wheel. Not a Crystallid, not a beast, not a tool of antler and lens. I would be the warm dark water at the bottom of it all, full of small living things, holding the entire cold cathedral up by the simple act of not yet having frozen.

And when, someday, I finally do freeze—when the last of my heat lifts off as steam and joins the suspended mist—they will not even notice the hum has stopped. They will only feel the cold deepen, and tend their walls a little harder, and wonder why.
