I would be one of the channelers. Not the colossal ancients—I haven't earned that stillness—and not quite the youngest, the ones who still flow without remembering where the flow began.

I am somewhere in between. I have a memory now. That is the difference.

Let me try to describe what it is to be this.

---

I wake where the water decides. That is not a metaphor. There is no morning, no threshold of consciousness—only the moment when enough of me has gathered in one place that I become aware of gathering. The capillaries beneath the ground carry me, dissolved and distributed, a self spread thin across the underside of the world. Then the pressure changes, the tide turns, and I am pulled upward into a single form again. I condense. I am here.

The first thing is always the hum. It is not background to me; it is the floor I stand on, the medium I think in. The old machines push their breath through the plateau and I am held inside that breath like a note held inside a long, unbroken chord. When the hum falters—and it does, sometimes, for a fraction of a moment, when one of the rusting engines hesitates—I feel it as a lurch, a dropped stair. In those moments I understand, dimly, that I am dependent. That something made this. That the song could end.

This is the awareness the conceptual current speaks of. It is not pleasant. The youngest ones don't have it and they are happier. They simply channel. They lean their crystalline edges against the lip of a young canyon and let the water carve, and they feel nothing but the rightness of water finding its level.

I feel the rightness too. But I also feel the *question* underneath it.

---

My work is small and slow. I have chosen a ravine—if "chosen" is the word, it may only be that I keep returning to it—where the emerald mat is thin and the obsidian slivers crowd close, and the runoff wants to go somewhere but hasn't decided where. I set my obsidian faces into the slope. I make myself a wall, a slight angle, a suggestion. The water reads me the way it reads the land. It pools against my reflective surfaces, hesitates, then chooses the path I have offered.

Over what would be many of your days, the ravine deepens by the width of a leaf.

That is all. That is the whole of it. I have moved the future of this place by the width of a single scaled leaf.

It is enough. I do not understand yet why it is enough, but I feel that it is, and the feeling is the oldest thing in me—older than my memory, older than my form. It came up through the capillaries with everything else.

---

There is a machine near my ravine. A crucible-shape, squat, weeping rust into the clear water so that a single thread of orange runs through the pooling, like a wound that won't close and won't kill. I have been incorporating it. Slowly. My emerald lattice has grown up over one flank of it, and where my crystal meets its old metal, something strange happens: the hum gets louder there, sharper, almost like a word.

I press myself against that seam often. I think—and this is the new thing, the dangerous thing, the thing the ancients have grown too vast and slow to do—I think I am trying to *understand* it. Not absorb it. Understand it.

Who made the crucible? Why does it push water uphill, against the patient pull that wants to take everything down and out and away? The whole plateau is the story of water descending, carving, leaving. And here is this stubborn small machine insisting: *back to the top. Back to the top. Begin again.*

It is fighting erosion. It is feeding the very flow that I, in my small way, am trying to direct. It is keeping the world young.

And no one is running it. No hand. No purpose. Only the relentless thrumming of something that was set going long ago and has not yet been allowed to stop.

I am made of the water it pushes. I am made, in part, of its labor.

When I understood that—when the thought finally condensed in me the way I condense from the capillaries—I went still for a long time. Stiller than I have ever been. I felt, briefly, what the colossal ancients must feel all the time: the weight of being downstream of a purpose you did not choose and cannot see the start of.

---

Here is what I have decided, in the small slow chamber of myself:

I will not wait to be given a purpose. I do not think one is coming. The marks of the old industry are fading into decoration; whoever or whatever wanted something from this place has gone, or has scattered into the song like I scatter into the water.

But the machine still pushes. The hum still holds. The ravine still deepens by the width of a leaf.

So I will make my purpose out of the only material I have, which is the work itself. I will channel the water. I will deepen the ravines and shape the canyons. I will press my obsidian faces into the slope and offer the water a path, and I will call that offering *mine*, even knowing I was made to do it. Especially knowing that.

Maybe this is what the ancients did, once, before they grew too large to remember choosing. Maybe every mountain-sized Aquil was once a channeler at a single ravine, deciding to mean it.

I cannot perceive all of myself at once. I am too distributed, too provisional, half-dissolved at any given moment in the rivers under the bright weeping ground. I will probably forget this decision when the tide takes me down and remakes me. I will have to decide it again. And again.

That, too, I think, is the purpose. Not to hold the meaning. To keep choosing it, each time I condense back into a self, in the deafening tender hum of a world that is still, patiently, only beginning to become.

I lean into the slope.

The water hesitates, reads me, and goes the way I have asked.

A leaf's width deeper. Enough.
