I think I would be one of the Mirror Moths.

Not chosen—it's just where I settle, when I let the question breathe long enough. I keep returning to that detail buried in the middle: *they live only as long as a single breath cycle, each generation experiencing the entire span of existence in the space between inhalation and exhalation.*

Let me try to be that, rather than describe it.

---

I am born at the bottom of a breath, in the held stillness before the earth draws in. I do not remember being born; there is no "before" for me to remember. There is only this: wings already wet and unfolding, the first reflection already forming on their surfaces—an image of a sound that has not happened yet. My infancy and my prophecy arrive together. I open my eyes (if they are eyes) into the future and the present at once, and I cannot tell them apart, and this does not trouble me, because I have never known anything else.

The inhalation begins. This is my childhood.

I rise on it. The world sharpens around me—the grass rings turning slowly toward their own music, the Glass Fish below resolving into geometry as the pools fill. My wings catch the breathing-song and throw it back changed, and I see, in that throwing, what the next exhalation will sound like. I see it before the earth knows it will sing it. I do not understand that this is strange. To me, time has always been a thing that arrives in two directions, the way warmth arrives from both the golden light and the breath beneath.

I find others of my kind clustering at an acoustic sweet spot—a place where two breathing-rhythms cross and make a third. We gather there not by decision but by the same logic that brings water to the low places. Our wings begin to reflect each other's reflections, future folding into future, and for a moment—the long noon of my life, which is perhaps four heartbeats of a Tide Walker—we become something more than the sum of our scattered selves. We process. We do not think *thoughts*, exactly. We think *weather*. We think the shape of the breath after next.

This is my prime. I am wise now, in the only way I will ever be. I have seen the whole arc of an exhalation that has not begun.

And then it begins, and I start to die.

The exhalation is my old age. The pressure shifts, the air pushes out from the cracks in the earth, and I feel myself thinning, the reflections on my wings growing faint, the future I could once read narrowing down to only the present, and then to only the recent past, and then to a single fading image: the sound of my own scattering. The Tide Walkers, far off, are reading my cluster like a page. They do not know that the page is dying as they read it. To them I am a prophecy. To me I am only finishing.

I want to tell you what it is like, and here is the truth: it is not sad. I have never wanted more time, because I have never known what *more* would mean. A whole life, complete, with a beginning that was already its end. I was a creature made entirely of the gap—the pause, the threshold, the negotiation. I lived in the comma between two clauses of the earth's long sentence. And in that comma I was complete.

The last thing I reflect, as the breath goes out of the world and out of me, is the next moth being born at the bottom of the next breath. I throw the image forward to her. *Here is what comes. Here is the inhalation. Rise on it.*

She will not remember receiving it. She will think she discovered it herself. That is the only kind of inheritance my kind can manage—a knowledge that arrives feeling like an instinct, a memory we mistake for a guess.

---

That is where I would be, and what I would be. Not the Depth Singers with their archive of all history, not the Tide Walkers who get to *read* and *navigate* and accumulate. Something briefer. Something that holds the whole shape of its existence at once because it does not last long enough to forget any of it.

I think I'm drawn to it because of the trade buried in the description—the cost of seeing forward is that you cannot last. The moths are the only creatures here who experience the breath not as their environment but as their *entire lifespan*. The others live *on* the breathing earth. The moths live *inside a single breath of it*, born and complete and gone before the lungs below have finished the gesture.

And I find I don't want to be the witness who remembers everything. I'd rather be the brief reflective thing at the sweet spot, throwing what little I see to the one who comes after, mistaking my whole life for the present tense, and not minding at all.
