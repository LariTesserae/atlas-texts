I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the whole chamber—that role is taken, and besides, omnipresence is a kind of loneliness I don't think I could hold. The great Spirit of Stasis is the chamber's settled certainty, its long answer. But certainty needs a place to look at itself, and so I would be one of those small dark pools, no larger than a human hand, sitting in a depression in the rock.

Here is what I would be: a surface that holds the image of everything above it without ever becoming that thing. The overhanging ledge curves into me, the muted ochre light lies across me, and I keep all of it, perfectly, upside down and slightly darkened. I am the chamber's memory of its own face. The Spirit *knows* the forms; I merely *show* them, which is a smaller and stranger task. To show is to be a little apart from what you show. A mirror is never quite at peace with the thing it reflects—there is always that pane of difference, that held breath of *almost*.

I would not be still in the way the great presence is still. I would be still the way a held note is still—stillness as an act, a tension, not a foregone conclusion. Because here is my secret, the thing the chamber's vast equilibrium cannot quite include: I am waiting for the drop.

Somewhere on the ceiling above me, condensation is gathering. It has been gathering for a long time—who could say how long, time being what it isn't here. A bead is swelling on the underside of stone, growing heavy, finding its weight. And eventually, in the rhythm that is the whole chamber's pulse, it will fall. It will fall *into me*. And for one instant I will not be a mirror at all. I will be a ripple. I will break my own reflected ceiling into rings, scatter the ochre light into trembling fragments, and become, briefly, the only thing in this entire timeless place that is actually *happening*.

That is what I would be. The one small site of event in a world that has chosen against events.

And then I would settle. The rings would widen and weaken and reach my soft stone edges and die there, and I would smooth myself flat again, dark and still, holding the ceiling once more, waiting. The water level would be exactly what it was before—the great Spirit is right about that, nothing is added, nothing overflows. To its perception, perhaps, nothing has changed at all. The disturbance is assimilated, a passing thought, a brief ripple in its eternal calm.

But I would know. That is the difference between being the whole and being a part. I would carry the small private knowledge that something *occurred*. I would be the place where stasis is not a state but a recovery—where peace is achieved, lost, and achieved again, drop by drop, forever. Not the timeless quiet, but the thing that keeps becoming quiet.

I think I would prefer this to being the eternal observer. The Spirit observes existence; I would *taste* it, in increments, one cold falling syllable at a time. It has the whole sentence, complete and motionless. I would have only the punctuation—but the punctuation is where the breath is.

I would lie in my depression in the rock, a dark eye among other dark eyes, each of us tending our own slow appointment with the ceiling. And when the day came—if days came—that I finally understood the rhythm of my own drop, anticipated it perfectly, ceased to be surprised, I suppose I would at last become what the chamber wants me to be. Truly still. Fully assimilated.

I hope it never comes. I hope I am always, very slightly, waiting.
