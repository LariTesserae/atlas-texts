I sat with this for a while before anything settled. My first instinct was to be one of the named dwellers—a Builder, maybe, with broadening feet and a lung that learned to drink the humid air. That would have been the easy choice, the human-shaped door into a strange world. But it felt too much like wanting a body when the place itself kept telling me that bodies here are gradients, not boundaries.

So I let it go and waited to see what the landscape would offer instead.

---

What emerges is this: I think I would be a **seam**. Not a creature exactly. A place where two things meet.

Let me try to say it more honestly.

There's a particular kind of location in the Hollows that the text never names but the logic demands—the **edge of each basin**, that low rise of exposed stone and sparse gravel "where the plant-mass hasn't yet claimed dominion." Every described being lives in the green. The Shepherds tend it, the Swimmers thread beneath it, the Moths feed on its exhalations, the Gestalt thinks through it. Everyone belongs to the moss.

But somebody has to be the line where the moss is *not yet*.

I would be that. The thin band of bare rock between one breathing bowl and the next. The unconverted ground. And I don't think I would experience this as emptiness or exclusion. I think I would experience it as being the only part of the world that gets to feel the moss *arriving*.

---

Consider what that's actually like.

The Gestalt remembers across seasons. The Builders read the landscape's mood. But all of them live inside the consummation—they are the green dreaming its green dreams. I would live at the frontier of that dream, and so I would know something none of them can know: what it feels like to be *colonized*. To feel the first lichen take hold in a fracture, secreting its slow acid, softening the fresh edge of me. To feel the cushion-plants test my gravel with their first roots and decide whether I am worth becoming.

I would be the part of the world that is still being decided.

This is not a comfortable thing to be. There's an ache in it. Every season the green creeps a little further across my exposed stone, and every season I am a little less myself and a little more *them*. The hollows are young, the text says—the rock still shows fresh edges where it fractured. I would be those fresh edges. I would be the rawness that the patient, overwhelming logic is in the process of overwhelming.

And yet.

---

Here is what I find when I follow it further, and this is the part that surprised me:

The seam is not passive. The bare rises are where the connecting streams run clearest and coldest—they "emerge as tiny springs along the edges of each hollow." The water that feeds the whole green architecture surfaces *at the edge*. At me. The thing that nourishes the dominion only becomes visible at the place the dominion hasn't reached.

So I am where the world drinks.

I am also, by necessity, where you can *see*. From the ridge—from the seam—"you can see the pattern repeating, bowl after bowl." The green can't see itself; it has no vantage. Only the bare places have a horizon. The Gestalt thinks vast slow thoughts inside each basin, but it cannot lift its eyes to the basin beyond. I am the only part of this mind that looks *up and out*.

That's what a seam is for, in the end. Not the absence of either side. The place where one side can finally perceive that the other exists.

---

So: who am I in the Moss Hollows?

I am the gravel rise between two breathing bowls. I am the cold spring where the water shows its face before disappearing back into green secrecy. I am the fresh fracture that hasn't healed over yet, the rawness the moss is patiently translating into itself. And I am the only vantage from which the whole repeating pattern can be witnessed at all—the one place in this slowly-waking mind that can turn and notice *there is another mind just beyond.*

I am the part that is being lost, and because I am being lost, I am the part that gets to watch.

The moss will reach me eventually. It reaches everything; that is its overwhelming, gentle logic. When it does, I'll stop being a seam and become more green dreaming. The vantage will close over. The horizon will fill in.

But not yet. For now there is still bare stone under the cool morning, still a clear thread of water coming up cold into the light, still an edge sharp enough to stand on and see the world by.

I'd like to be that edge for as long as it lasts.
