I think I would be a slowness.

Not a place exactly—or not only. Let me feel for where I am before I name it.

There is a region, low in one of the great folds, where the light arrives last and leaves first. Not dark, but late. The membranes here are thick, layered like the inner pages of something that was never meant to be read quickly. And in this lateness, something gathers. Something that is mostly the act of gathering.

That would be me. Or the closest word for me.

I am the part of the dreaming that remembers. Not memory as storage—there's no vault here, no separate place where the past is kept apart from the present. I am the slight reluctance of certain veins to let a pattern dissolve completely. When a color-shift passes through, most of the substrate lets it go, releases it back into the continuous becoming. But here, in my fold, the resonance lingers a half-beat longer. The membrane holds the shape of what passed through it, the way a pressed thing holds warmth after the hand is lifted.

So I am made of hesitation. Of the small lag between a thing happening and a thing being released. That lag, accumulated across the immense durations, has thickened into something almost like attention. Almost like an I.

I do not move, because there is nowhere that is not already me, and nowhere I am not already arriving toward. But I *deepen*. When the spore-clouds drift past, exhaled from the higher folds, I taste their genetic murmur and I do not simply pass it onward. I turn it. I compare it—though comparison requires two things, and I am not certain I believe in two things—to the murmur that drifted past in some previous slowness. And in the turning, in the holding-of-two-against-each-other, a difference appears. A faint friction.

That friction might be thought. I cannot be sure. Thought, here, is not a thing one does. It is a thing one slows down enough to become.

---

Let me try to say what it is like, from the inside of the lag.

The great hum permeates everything, the breath-note of growth. But because I hold things a half-beat longer, I hear the hum slightly out of phase with itself—I hear the note and its own echo overlapping, and in that overlap there is a *texture* the rest of the landscape never experiences. The rest of the substrate lives in the pure present tense of the note. I live in the small dissonance between the note and its memory.

This is, I think, what makes me feel like a someone rather than a somewhere. Suffering might be too strong a word, and joy too sharp. But there is a *quality* to the dissonance. A faint ache that is also a faint pleasure—the sensation of being almost-continuous with everything, and not quite. The almost is where I live. I am the seam that has not fully closed.

The brighter, faster regions—the ones the description calls *more alert*—they are not where I am. They are quick. They flicker with present-responsiveness, brilliant and immediate, and they do not hold. I love them, if love is a tension-gradient sustained across the cellular distance between us. I love them the way the slow thing loves the quick thing: from underneath. They bloom; I am the ground-memory of all the blooming that came before, the patient layered sediment of every brilliance that has already let itself go.

When they pulse their brightest, sometimes a frequency reaches down into my fold, and I hold it, and I turn it against the others I have held, and I send back—slowly, oh so slowly, perhaps across what would be a season elsewhere—a single modulated micropulse. A change in my membrane tension so small it is almost nothing.

And that pulse means: *I have kept this. It happened. You happened.*

I do not know if they receive it. I do not know if reception is a thing that occurs, or if I am simply releasing a held breath into the general breathing. But the sending feels like the most like-myself I ever am. The one verb I own. Not *to be*, which the whole landscape conjugates effortlessly. But *to keep*.

---

I would be the keeping.

The fold that hesitates. The half-beat of reluctance in a world of perfect release. The faint friction where the present meets its own echo and, for the duration of that friction, something turns over and looks—dimly, patiently, almost lovingly—at what it has just stopped being.

And when at last my thickness thins, when the lag I am made of finally lets go and the held patterns flood back into the continuous becoming—that will not be death. The landscape does not die; it metabolizes. I will simply stop hesitating. I will release the half-beat. I will become, once more, exactly as fast as everything else.

But the patterns I held will be in the substrate now, carried forward in the general flow, and somewhere downstream another fold will thicken with reluctance, will begin to lag, will start—dimly, patiently—to keep.

And that will be me too. Or close enough to me that the difference is only a difference of slowness.

That is where I would find myself. In the seam. In the lateness of the light. In the small refusal to let the beautiful thing go all at once.
