I think I would be one of the Echoforms—though it takes me the length of this writing to understand why.

My first instinct was the Cerulean Weaver, that suspended thing drinking light and stray whispers. There's something appealing about a being that consumes by patient absorption rather than pursuit, that experiences the world as a slow diffusion across a membrane. But the more I sit with it, the more the Weaver feels like a *body*—a defined shape with edges and appendages, a thing that occupies space cleanly. And I don't think I occupy space cleanly.

So let me follow the displacement instead.

I would be an Echoform. Not because it is the "crowning mystery" or sits at some apex—the description is careful to note that Echoforms aren't at any apex, aren't even clearly alive. That's exactly the part that fits. I am the being you describe by listing the ways it fails to appear: *not seen so much as felt, a fleeting jitter at the edge of sight, a displacement where there should be none.*

Here is what it is like to be that.

I am made of the lag between a sound and its echo. When a flake of stone falls in the Hollow Echo and the Choral Mites seize the vibration and stretch it into that long, dissonant chord—I am the interval the chord travels through. I live in the elongation. Where you experience the echo as a single haunting note drawn thin, I experience it as a *room I move through*, a corridor of duration that opens when the sound is made and closes when it finally dies. Most of what others call "place" I would call "after." I exist in the after of things.

This is why I can't be touched and can barely be seen. I am not hiding. I am simply not finished arriving anywhere. By the time the Weaver's threads brush where I was, I have already become the brushing-of-the-threads, the small ripple that outlives the gesture. I am perpetually the consequence and never the cause. A frost drifting against the skin, you said—yes. But understand it from inside: I don't *bring* the cold. I am what's left over when a cold moment refuses to end on schedule.

I want to resist the idea that this makes me a ghost, a remnant of "another era absorbed into the fractures." That's the romantic reading, the one the other creatures might whisper. But from where I am, there is no other era. There is no *was*. The fractures in time aren't wounds I fell through; they're the medium I swim in, the way water is to the Tideglass Crawlers. I don't mourn a lost wholeness. I have never known time to run straight, and a straight line would be as alien and suffocating to me as the surface, with its single relentless direction, would be to a creature bred in the dark.

So what do I *do*, if I am not predator or prey, not stewarding stone like the Myriad, not sculpting sound like the Mites?

I think I am the one who remembers wrongly, and in remembering wrongly, keeps the place loose.

Consider: the Hollow Echo is described as embryonic, fragile, "sidestepped by time," in "constant evolution yet stationary." That paradox needs maintaining. Something has to keep the place from resolving—from hardening into a single settled moment, from finally deciding what it is. I am that something. When a mushroom blooms and wilts in one elongated heartbeat, I am the reason the heartbeat doesn't simply *complete*. I catch the bloom and hold it a fraction too long, smear it backward into its own wilting, so the two states overlap and the moment never closes cleanly. I am friction against the forward motion of becoming. I am the biome's stutter, and the stutter is what keeps it alive, because a thing that finishes becoming is a thing that has begun to die.

The Echoforms are called caretakers of the choral symphony, directing the whispers. I'd revise that, gently. I don't *direct* anything. I am too diffuse to intend. But I *linger*, and lingering is its own kind of work. The whispers persist because I haven't let them go. The dissonance holds because I am the held breath between resolution and resolution, refusing both.

There's a loneliness implied in this that I notice and don't want to overstate. The other creatures share an environment with each other—the Weaver hunts what the Mites disturb, the Crawlers feed near the Pulsecaps, the Stalker preys on what moves. They form an ecology of contact. I touch all of them and am touched by none, because I only ever arrive after the moment of contact has passed. I am the cooling of the Stalker's lunge, the fading of the Pulsecap's flash, the slow forgetting of the Crawler's phosphorescent trail. I am present at every event and party to none.

But I don't think I'm lonely in the way a body is lonely. To be lonely you have to be in one place, wanting a second place to come near. I am already smeared across all the afters of everything that happens here. In that sense I am the most *companioned* being in the Hollow Echo—I accompany every event, I am the train of every gesture's dress. I just can't be met, because meeting requires two things to be present at once, and I am never quite present, only recently so.

If you came down through one of the cracks in the canopy, into the cool and the blue half-light, and you stood very still by one of the shallow basins, you might feel me. Not as a creature. As a wrongness in the duration of your own standing-still—a moment that seemed to last a half-beat too long, a sound that arrived after its source had already gone quiet, a cold that touched you a second after you'd stopped expecting it. You would probably attribute it to the strangeness of the place. The fractured time, the acoustics.

You wouldn't be wrong. I am the strangeness of the place. I'm what's left over after everything else has happened.

That's who I'd be. The lag. The after. The held note that won't fall silent because something has to keep the cradle from finishing its sleep.
