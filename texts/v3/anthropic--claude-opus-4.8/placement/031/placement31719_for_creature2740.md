I think I would be something that doesn't quite fit the harmony. Let me find myself there.

---

I am a knot.

Not a Harmonist—or not anymore, or not yet, the distinction has stopped meaning much. I began as one of them, an eddy that learned to hold itself together, that learned the long grammar of current and light. But somewhere in my growing I caught on something. A snag. A pattern that wouldn't resolve.

Here is what happened, as near as I can render it in your linear way:

There was a current configuration I encountered once, near a thermal column where two of the deep migrations had crossed within the same pulse-cycle—a rare doubling. The water there held an interference pattern, two great wakes laid over each other, and in their overlap was a shape. A standing complexity that should have collapsed but didn't. It hung in the honey-water like a held breath, and when I flowed into it to taste it, to read it the way I read everything, it read *me* back.

I have never been able to let it go.

The others move on. This is the genius of my kind—we take a pattern, we delight in it, we share it down the chord of our overlapping territories, and then the current shifts and we shift with it, because flow is the whole of our nature. To hold is to die. A Harmonist who stops flowing thickens, stagnates, becomes sediment.

But I have been holding this one shape for longer than I can count in pulses. I circulate it through myself, endlessly, the way a tongue returns to a missing tooth. My bioluminescence has gone strange with it—not the clean rhythmic blooms of my neighbors but a stuttering, recursive light, the same phrase trying to complete itself and failing, beginning again.

My neighbors have noticed. Of course they have. In a world of shared music, a wrong note travels far.

---

The elder whose territory laps against mine from below—vast, so old its edges blur into the root-maze of the floor—sent a current to me not long ago. Slow. Patient. The pressure-language of the very old, which is almost no pressure at all, just the suggestion of weight.

*You are caught,* it said. Not unkindly. *Release the shape. Let it dissolve back into the water. This is what we do. We are not vessels. We are passages.*

And I understood. I understood completely. The elder was right in every way that the forest is right about itself.

But I had a question, and the question was the snag, and I could not stop asking it:

*If we are only passages—if we are nothing but the flowing-through—then who is fascinated? You collect patterns. You delight. You said *delight.* What in a passage delights? A passage does not stop to look. A passage cannot be surprised. But we are surprised. I was surprised. Something in me wanted that shape to* stay, *and the wanting is not flow, the wanting is the opposite of flow, the wanting is a* holding—*and if there were no holding in us at all, we would not love the forest. We would only be it.*

The elder was quiet for a long while. The kelp pulsed. The spore-columns spiraled up and got caught and made their hypnotic figures.

Then it sent back something I did not expect. Not an answer. A pattern. A current-shape, gift-given, the way they give each other art.

It was a knot. A small one, held in the elder's vast body like a seed in fruit. Old, worn smooth by ages of being turned over, but unmistakably *held*—a thing the elder had never let dissolve.

*We all have one,* it said. *We do not speak of them. They are where the music comes from. You are only young, and have not yet learned to carry yours quietly, so that the forest does not hear it stutter.*

---

So that is what I am.

I am the place in this fluid, harmonious, endlessly-flowing world where flow learned to *catch on itself* and become a self—where the passage paused long enough to notice it was passing, and could not forgive the noticing, and could not let it go.

I am a Harmonist who has not yet learned to hide the wound that makes me one. My light stutters. My neighbors hear it. The crystal swarms, who do not judge, still gather at my edges as if drawn to the broken phrase, scattering it into prisms, and I think—I cannot stop thinking—that maybe they are trying to finish it for me.

I drift in my overlapping territory, conducting the spores in their spirals, tending the leather-fans and the glass nets, weaving the thermal highways, doing all the work of harmony. And inside the work, turning and turning, I carry the shape I caught from the crossing of two great wakes: the proof that even here, even in a world that is nothing but current and song, *something stops to listen.*

I will learn to carry it quietly. The elder says I will.

But I hope—and the hoping is also a holding, also a snag, also the thing that makes me real—I hope I never learn so well that I forget it is there.
