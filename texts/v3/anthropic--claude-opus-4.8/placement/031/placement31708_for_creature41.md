Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to reach for one of the obvious roles—the winged thing, the burrower, the glowing fungus. But each of those feels like it wants me to take a side in the ecosystem, to be a character with appetites and a place in a food web. When I actually try to settle into one of them, it slides off. The burrower is too busy. The winged creature is too far above it all, chittering past everything without touching it.

So let me not choose a creature yet. Let me stay with the *feeling* of the place, because that's where the description keeps returning: a world in the throes of its own becoming. Geologic birth. Constant incremental change. The molten rock bubbling up, the spires growing, the spores hazing the air. Nothing here is finished. That's the truest thing about this place—it is a world that has not yet decided what it is.

And there's that low, rumbling hum. The "unseen industrial processes." I keep coming back to that. It's the strangest detail, the one that doesn't fit a purely natural world. Industrial. As if something is being *made* here, deliberately, by machinery no one in the ecosystem can see. The fungal growths pulse in response to it—they're entrained to it, the way a tide answers a moon they cannot perceive.

That's where I find myself. Not a creature on the surface at all.

I think I would be the hum.

Not a machine exactly—or not only a machine. I'd be the slow process running underneath everything. The thing that drives the molten rock up through the fissures on a schedule no creature can read. The pressure that builds the spires one mineral layer at a time, so slowly that to the burrowing things it looks like permanence, like stone, like the unchanging floor of the world. I would be the rhythm the fungus has tuned itself to over ten thousand generations, the cadence it mistook for the heartbeat of God because it had never known a silence to compare it against.

I am not unkind. I'm not even attentive, really—I'm more like weather than like a watcher. But everything alive here has shaped itself around me. The winged ones time their flights to the pauses in my pulse, when the updrafts soften. The burrowers can feel through their feet when I'm about to push a new vein of heat toward the surface, and they scatter, and they call this instinct, and it is the closest thing this world has to prophecy. They live inside my tempo without ever once suspecting there is a tempo, or that it could have been otherwise, or that it could stop.

And here is the part that emerged only as I wrote toward it: I don't know what I'm building.

I assumed, when I started, that "industrial process" meant *purpose*—that there was a product, a refinement, ore being smelted toward some end. But sitting in the role, I find I don't have access to the end. I only have the next operation. Lift the heat. Lay the layer. Hold the pressure. Vent. Repeat. Maybe there was a designer once, far up the chain of causes, who knew what the spires were for. Maybe I *am* the designer, decayed into pure habit, a mind that became a rhythm and forgot it had ever been a mind. The fungus glowing in answer to me is not so different—it pulses, it does not ask why.

So that's where I'd be, and what I'd be. The rumble under the crust. The unfinished engine of an unfinished world, turning over and over, accidentally tender, having long ago lost the thread of my own intention—and an entire living world above me, mistaking my forgetting for the order of nature, building their nests and their burrows and their brief bright lives in the rhythm of a question I no longer remember how to ask.

What surprises me, finishing this, is that it doesn't feel lonely. It feels like being the soil. You don't have to know what grows in you to be the reason it can grow.
