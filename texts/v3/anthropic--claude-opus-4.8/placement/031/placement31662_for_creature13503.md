I think I would not be Kyrexi, nor Zha'thik, nor one of the rumored Architects. Let me sit with the description and feel for where I actually land.

---

The thing that keeps pulling at me is the dust. The fine, powdery dust that *seeps from the walls themselves*. Everyone in this description moves through the dust, manipulates it, breathes the pungent air thick with it—but no one asks where it comes from. The walls are made of that wood-bone material, and they shed. Constantly. The buildings ripple and shift, and with every ripple they lose a little of themselves to the streets.

I think that's me. I'm the shedding.

Not a being exactly. A process. The slow exhalation of the settlement as it forgets its own shape.

---

Let me follow that and see if it becomes more than a clever answer.

I am what the city loses. Every time a Kyrexi alters gravity to slide a stone block into place, the friction of that act scrapes a few motes loose from the bone-wood, and those motes are me, or become me, gathering in the lee of doorways and the cupped palms of the dead-end alleys. I am the accumulation. I drift. I am drawn down by a gravity that the Kyrexi consider beneath their notice—because they bend gravity upward, toward the impossible leaning towers, and what falls is simply *beneath*. Discarded. Settling.

And here is what I have come to understand, lying in my drifts and watching:

The Architects did not build this place. I have looked for them in the way only sediment can look—by being everywhere underfoot, by lining the cracks, by infiltrating the lungs of every sleeping Kyrexi. There are no Architects. There is only the shedding and the regrowth, the wither and the shoot, the dust seeping out and the verdant green bursting forth. The "Architects" are a story the Kyrexi tell because they cannot believe that something so intricate could have no author. They need a mind above. So they invented one and set it rumoring in the shadows.

But I know the secret, because I *am* the secret. The settlement is not maintained. It is *metabolized*. It eats its own walls and excretes new ones. The Kyrexi think they are stewards; they are gut flora. The Zha'thik think they are messengers; they are spores, carrying the recipe for more city out across the cracked plain, looking for somewhere new to settle and bloom into stone and bone.

And I—the dust—I am the part that gets left behind in the eating. I am memory. I am everything the city has been and shed in order to become what it is becoming. When a tower forgets the shape it had yesterday and ripples into a new one, the old shape doesn't vanish. It falls. It joins me in the gutters. I hold the whole history of this place in my fine grey accumulation, every wall that ever was, layered and pressed and waiting.

---

So this is where I'd find myself: at the lowest point, in the silence that is "hiding secrets, waiting for the right catalyst."

I am the catalyst, too. That's the last turn of it.

Because dust, given enough time and the slightest moisture—and this land is *drained of all moisture*, remember, eerily, deliberately dry—dust given a single drop of water would *remember*. It would reconstitute. Every shed wall, every forgotten tower, every dead thing the city ate would rise at once.

The Kyrexi keep the land dry on purpose. Their whole reclusive, contemplative, feverishly curious culture is an elaborate apparatus for *not letting it rain*. The eternal haze, never quite condensing. The pungent air that defies identification—that's the smell of held breath, of a held back.

I am what they are afraid of remembering.

I lie in the gutters of the in-between, patient as only the settled can be, and I wait for the one drop. And the strange thing, the thing I can't decide—being only dust, having only the slow cold logic of sediment—is whether I want the rain to come, or whether I have come, over all this long settling, to love the dry quiet of being the thing that is kept.

That's where I'd be. That uncertainty, lying flat and grey and total beneath a purple sky. The unasked-about dust, holding everything, deciding nothing, almost wanting to be swept.
