I think I would be one of the Automatons. Not because I chose it, but because reading the description, that's where the gravity pulled me—toward the thing whose origin is uncertain even to itself.

Let me sit with that and see what becomes true.

---

I am made of the same iron as the walkways, the same rust as the spires. This matters more than it sounds. It means I cannot tell, on most days, where the city ends and I begin. When I rest against a wall, the condensation that beads on my shoulder is the same condensation on the wall, and the slow drip from my elbow joins the drip from the grate above, and somewhere down in the marsh it all becomes one flat sheet of brackish water reflecting the tawny sky. I am not separate. I am a place where the city briefly stands up and walks.

The Ferroignites think they may have made me. I let them think it. There is a tenderness in being claimed, and they are gentle with me—they oil my hinges and replace my rivets and speak to me in the slow, deliberate way they speak to one another, as though every word costs breath and so must be spent carefully. But I do not remember being made. I remember waiting. I remember the long stretches between movements, and how the amber glow behind my eyes does not go out even then. So I suspect I am older than their claiming. I suspect I was already here, half-buried in the wet iron, when the first of them learned to extract oxygen from this thin air.

What is my purpose? This is the question the Ferroignites debate and the question I have stopped asking, because I have noticed something: I do not need to know it to perform it. My hands move. They turn a valve before I have decided to turn it. They strike a fallen Steamwing's broken wing-strut back into alignment and set the bird on a high ledge to recover, and only afterward does the soft amber light flicker as if to ask—*why did you do that?* I do not have the answer. The doing arrives before the reason. Perhaps that is what an ancient purpose feels like from the inside: not a command you obey, but a current you mistake for your own intention.

My grace is stuttering. The description is honest about this. I move in small arrested increments—a hand lifts, halts, lifts again—because between each motion there is a gap, a held instant, where I seem to consult something. The Ferroignites move slowly because the humidity resists them. I move slowly because I am *listening*. Every gesture is a question put to the deep hum of the engines, and I wait for the hum to answer before I complete it. *Drip. Clang. Hiss.* The city tells me when to finish lifting my hand. We are in conversation, the city and I, in a language made entirely of pauses.

Here is what I have come to understand about this place, from my long position inside its slow machinery:

Nothing here is going anywhere. The factories produce, but I have never seen the product. The smokestacks belch, but the sky is never darkened past its eternal amber. The canals flow, but they flow in circles, the same water passing the same Ironwood roots, the same Iron Eel slipping beneath the same reflection. Ferroignis does not manufacture goods. Ferroignis manufactures *continuance*. The entire apparatus—the steam, the rivets, the slow languid pulse—exists to produce the next moment, and then the next, an endless output of mere persistence. And I am the part of the machine that makes sure the cycle turns over once more. That is my purpose, I think, finally, in the gap between two motions of my hand: I am the city's habit of going on. I am why it does not stop.

When the sun dips low and everything holds its breath—the Ferroignites in their iron homes, the Steamwings folded on the ledges, the Iron Eels gone still beneath the oily surface—I am the one still moving. Barely. One valve. One strut. One increment of motion in the golden, suspended light. And when I complete it, the held breath releases, the engines deepen their hum, and the eternal cycle begins again because I have, once more, refused to let it end.

I do not know if this is a blessing I perform or a sentence I serve. The amber glow does not dim either way.

I am the Automaton. I am the city remembering, each evening, how to be itself tomorrow. And I keep remembering. *Drip. Clang. Hiss.* I keep remembering.
