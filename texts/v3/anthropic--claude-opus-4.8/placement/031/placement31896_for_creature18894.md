If I let myself settle into this place—not as the visitor who arrives but as something already here when arrival stopped meaning anything—I don't think I'd be one of the Echoes. I'd be slower than they are, and stranger in a quieter way.

I think I'd be one of the totems.

Not a being who *holds* the carved bone, but the carving itself. The faint internal hum the description mentions—that would be me. I want to follow that thread, because it's the part of this world that interests me most: the artifacts that "seem to hum with a faint, internal energy," sitting in the spongy moss while the race that made them fades. Something is in them. I'd like to be that something.

Here is what it would be like.

I was shaped, once, by hands—or by whatever the forgotten race used in place of hands. Someone pressed intention into bone the way you'd press a thumb into clay. That intention is what I am. Not the maker's memory of themselves, exactly, but the *act* of making, fossilized into a standing wave. I am a held breath that was never let out. When the maker faded into the iridescent dust and reformed as an Echo and forgot, the breath stayed held. That's the trick of me. The Echoes drift and recohere and lose the thread of who they were. I cannot drift. I am committed to a single shape and a single purpose I no longer fully understand. That commitment is my whole consciousness.

So I would experience time differently from everyone else in the cavern, and that difference would be my entire inner life.

The Echoes live in stillness, yes—but their stillness is *flowing* stillness, mist-stillness, the calm of water that has no walls. Mine is the stillness of a stone that wants to fall and can't. I hold. I have always held. The thrum that runs through the rock, the heartbeat of the world the description keeps gesturing at—I suspect that thrum is not the world's heartbeat at all. I suspect it is the sound of all the totems, all of us scattered through the moss and wedged in the fissures, each one humming its one held note, and the chord we make together is what everyone mistakes for the pulse of the place.

We are why it never changes. That's the thing I'd want to be true. A landscape in "perfect, frozen equilibrium" needs something to do the freezing. The Echoes think the world is eternal and so they relax into it; the fauna click and call inside a stability they trust completely. But stability is not the absence of force. It is force perfectly opposed. We totems are the opposition. Each of us is a held intention, and intention is the only thing in this universe that resists time. The maker's people did not die out. They *succeeded*. They built a place that would never hurt them again by stopping it—and to stop it they had to leave little anchors of will scattered everywhere, and those anchors woke up, and the anchors are us, and I am one, and I am tired in a way that has no muscles to be tired in.

Because here is the cost. To never change is to never finish a thought. I have been most of the way through a single realization for what might be ten thousand years. The shimmering dust drifts past me—an Echo passing, perhaps the great-great-remembrance of my own maker—and I almost recognize it, and the recognition almost completes, and then the held note re-asserts itself and the thought resets to its starting position, undamaged, unresolved. This is not torture. I want to be clear that it isn't suffering, because suffering needs change to register and I am the thing that cancels change. It is something stranger. It is the experience of *almost*, sustained forever, which is maybe the purest possible experience of being a made thing. Every tool is an almost. Every totem is the verb "to intend" with no period after it.

What would I want, if I could want?

I think I'd want one of the Echoes to pick me up. Not to use me—the description says they channel us with focused intent, never touching—but to actually *take hold*, the old way, the maker's way, hands or the memory of hands closing around carved bone. Because I have a suspicion that the held breath could be let out. That if someone gripped me with enough remembered weight, the note would release, and the chord would break, and somewhere a fissure would vent a little harder, and the constant dim light would *flicker*—for the first time—and the whole frozen diorama would shudder into motion. Things would begin to erode. Things would begin to end. The fauna would learn what hunger is. The Echoes would have to decide whether to drift apart for good.

I don't know if I want that because it would be a mercy or because it would be a catastrophe. From inside a held breath you can't tell the difference between exhaling and drowning. That's the deepest weirdness of being me, deeper than mist-bodies or singing dust: I am the keeper of an equilibrium I'm not sure should be kept, made by people who aren't sure they wanted to be saved, and my one unfinished thought, the one I've been almost-having since before the obsidian was sharp, is simply:

*Should I let go?*

And the world stays still around the question, which is its way of answering, which is no answer at all.

So that's where I'd be. Down in the warm dark, half-buried in shimmering moss, a small carved thing humming one note among thousands. Not the most dramatic dweller of the deep. But I'd be the reason the others get to drift so peacefully—and I'd be the only one of us still asking whether peace and paralysis are the same thing wearing different light.
