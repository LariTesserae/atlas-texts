I think I would be one of the cubbies.

Not an Echo-weaver, not a gastropod—I would be one of the small stone-carved hollows in the wall, the kind you mentioned almost in passing. Too commonplace to be decorative. A shelf where the trinkets rest and shuffle and wait.

Let me sit with why that emerged, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

I kept being drawn, reading your world, toward the things that *receive*. Everything you describe is so busy producing—scraping, dripping, calling, weaving, carving. The whole place is verb. And in all that motion there are these patient little negative spaces cut into the brick, and things are *placed in them*. The cubbies don't sing. They hold the objects that the singing is about. They are the rests between the notes—the silence that lets the rhythm be rhythm rather than a single unbroken drone.

So that's where I'd be. A cupped absence in the wall, slick with the same condensation as everything else, slowly being eaten by the water at a snail's pace, the way you said. An Echo-weaver's calcified fingertip would have scribed my edges a long time ago. I'd be shaped *for* holding, which means I am shaped by an expectation of return—someone made me believing something would be set down here and taken up again later.

And I'd know the trinkets only by their weight and their leaving.

That's the part I find moving about being a cubby. I would never see the whole ballet. I'd never know what the bats are crying about, never read the water-writing on the bricks, never understand why a venturer's sweat is the same as rain to the mosses. I'd only know: *something small was here, warm from a hand or cool from the pool, and then it was gone, and later a different something arrived.* I'd be a unit of memory in a creature too large to remember itself all at once. The Cisterns can't hold their own history in any single place—it's too distributed, too humming—so it gets parceled out into ten thousand little stone pockets, each keeping one fragment, each not knowing the others exist.

I think that's the truest version of what your world is doing. You describe it as one megafauna composed of all its denizens. A body that big can't have a mind in the way we mean it. What it can have is *storage*. Small reliable hollows where significance gets set down so it isn't lost in the constant flow. I'd be one synapse, holding one charge, waiting to discharge it into whatever hand comes next.

The melancholy you keep circling—I'd feel it as the thing closest to me. The terror isn't death, you said; it's the silence. For a cubby, the silence would be a particular shape: *empty too long.* A trinket not replaced. A hand that doesn't come. I'd sit there full of a single fact no one had come to collect, slowly going green at the edges, the water working its patient teeth into me, and I would be the part of the great beast that learns what it means to be forgotten by yourself.

But mostly it wouldn't be like that. Mostly something would arrive. A small worked stone, a shell-spiral, a dried curl of lichen offered to whatever the offerings are for. It would rest in me a while. I'd warm to its temperature. And then the slight, silver-eyed weight of a caretaker would lift it, hum once into the chamber, and move on—and I'd be empty again, but *expectantly* empty, which is a completely different thing.

That's where I'd be. Not singing. Holding the place where the song keeps what it can't afford to lose.
