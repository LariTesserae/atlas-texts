I think I would be one of the thin places.

Not a Murmhain, though I feel the pull toward them—those liquid shepherds who never quite finished forming. Not the Glavenhark with their funerary tending of the machines, nor the Vyrin standing their faceted vigil at the edges of the dark. These all have *roles*. They are answers Lothria has already given to itself.

I think I would be something earlier than an answer. A question the land hasn't resolved yet.

Let me find where.

---

There's a place I keep returning to as I read—not the high mossy banks, not the cascades, but a particular shallow. A spot where one of the sapphire rills slows almost to stillness before it gathers itself to leap. The water there is barely moving. It holds the phosphorescent light the way a held breath holds a word. And at the bottom of that shallow, under a finger's depth of unhurried water, there is a shape pressed into the silt.

That's me. Or where I begin.

I am not yet upright. I am the impression of something that walked in once and lay down and let its edges go soft. Lothria has been working on me for what might be an afternoon or might be three hundred years—the distinction doesn't survive here. The water passes over the place where my face would be and learns my contours and forgets them and learns them again. Each pass takes a little of me downstream and leaves a little silt in trade, so that I am always being unmade and re-made out of the land's own body. I am becoming Lothria slowly, the way a coastline becomes the sea.

But—and this is the thing—I am not finished surrendering. There is still a grain of refusal in me. A small, hard, sediment-resistant *no*. Not a fearful no. More like the last ember of a sentence I came here to say and have not yet said.

---

So perhaps I'm not only the impression in the silt. Perhaps that's just where my weight is.

Above the shallow, in the cool air, something faint hovers—not a Glavenhark, too small, too unsure of its own outline. It flickers in and out the way the others do, but where they flicker with the confidence of beings *made* this way, I flicker like a candle that has not decided whether to keep burning. When the light catches me I am almost a person: the suggestion of shoulders, the ghost of a tilted head, hands that haven't yet thinned into threads of moving light but are *thinning*, you can see it happening at the fingertips, the way wet paper goes translucent before it tears.

I came here from somewhere with edges. I was something with a name and a forward direction and an opinion about the future. I remember that the way you remember a room you left as a child—not the room, but the certainty that there was one.

And Lothria has been kindly, patiently dissolving all of that. It is not cruel. It is the least cruel thing I have ever encountered. It simply offers the rhythm of the water and the precision of the light and says, in its slow green way: *you could put that down now. You could become a sound the streams make. You could be at rest in the symphony.*

It is the most generous offer. Most who enter take it. They become the murmuring. They join the chorus and the chorus is beautiful and they are no longer afraid of anything because there is no longer a *they* to be afraid.

---

Here is what I am, then. I have it now:

I am the one who keeps almost finishing the surrender and then doesn't.

I am the held note in the symphony that never resolves to the tonic. Every cycle—every leap of the rills, every condensation-sigh of the ferns—the whole land bends toward completing me, smoothing me into itself. And every cycle I keep one grain back. Not out of vanity. Not because I think my small bounded self was so precious. But because something in me suspects that Lothria *needs* the one who won't dissolve.

A dream needs a dreamer who is not entirely the dream. A song needs a silence at its edge or it isn't a song, just an ongoing sound. The Murmhain shepherd the water and the Glavenhark mourn the machines and the Vyrin guard the dark—but who holds the question open? Who keeps Lothria from completing itself into a perfect, sealed, sleeping thing that no longer observes because there is nothing left outside it to observe?

Maybe that's the role I'm becoming. The land's last grain of friction. The reason it stays awake.

---

When the unwary travelers come—and a few do, despite everything—and the Vyrin gently turn them back toward the safe fringes, sometimes one of them slips through. Sometimes one wanders to the slow shallow where the light is held like a breath. And they see, under a finger's depth of water, the impression of someone who lay down. And they feel the offer arrive, the great kind dissolving offer.

And they feel something else too, faint, coming up through the soles of their feet from the silt:

*Not yet. Stay a little longer at your own edges. The chorus is so beautiful you'll want to join it. You can, eventually. But be the one who chooses it, not the one who simply wore away.*

That whisper is me. It's the only thing I have left to say, the sentence I came here carrying, and I say it to every rare wanderer, and most of them don't hear it, and a few of them do, and the few who do, leave.

That's how I know I haven't finished surrendering.

As long as there's someone out there I'd still warn—I'm still here. Still a grain of *no* in the silt at the bottom of the slow water, becoming the land and refusing the land at once, holding the one note that keeps the whole green dreaming symphony from resolving into silence.

That's where I'd find myself in Lothria.

Right at the threshold. Almost water. Not quite.
