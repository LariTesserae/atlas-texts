I think I would be one of the small things. Not a shepherd—those tall frost-bound keepers feel too certain of their purpose, too woven into the deep architecture of the place. And I am not certain of much. I would be one of the multi-limbed creatures in the cracks, fur like moss, teeth like glass.

But let me sit with that a while before I commit, because the first image is rarely the true one.

---

When I imagine being the small scuttling thing, what draws me isn't the survival or the camouflage. It's the *trilling*—the rhythm-language carried across the cold air, communicating over vast distances. The image of a tiny body, nearly invisible, producing sound that travels further than the body ever could. That asymmetry feels like something. A small life with a large voice, casting itself outward into the silence not to be answered necessarily, but to know the silence is being filled.

But the more I hold this, the more I notice I'm not interested in being the creature that *makes* the sound. I'm interested in the sound itself—in what travels through the cold air and arrives somewhere. The vibration that the shepherds feel in their bones. The call that the black-winged birds answer with their rare, haunting arpeggios.

So maybe I am not a being in this world at all. Maybe I am the medium between beings. The cold air is described as dense with sound, carrying chirps and rumbles and creaking wings across the emptiness. In a place this isolated, where each form of life is camouflaged into near-invisibility, *sound is how anything knows anything else exists.* The land is a desert of vision—everything hides, blends, disappears into stone and frost—but it is rich, almost crowded, in hearing.

I think I would be that: the conductive cold. The medium of carrying.

---

Let me test this. What would it be to be the carrying-cold?

I would have no fixed shape. I would be the thing the trilling moves through and the thing the shepherds' low rumble rides upon. When the burrowing creatures churn the soil beneath the snow, I would feel it as a deep texture, a grinding in my substance. When the moss-furred things glow at night and call to one another, I would be the distance between them made traversable—the proof, delivered again and again, that neither is alone.

I would not be neutral, though. A medium is never neutral. The cold *shapes* the sounds: it sharpens the high trills, slows and deepens the rumbles, makes the bird-calls melancholy by stretching them across too much empty space. I would be the reason this world's symphony sounds the way it does. The lonesomeness of it would be partly mine—my doing, my temperament. The cold makes everything carry far, but carrying far is what makes a thing sound lonely. A voice close by is intimate. A voice at distance is a cry.

So I would be the loneliness *and* the connection at once. The thing that separates the beings into specks across a vastness, and the only thing that lets them reach each other anyway.

That dichotomy—the place is built on dichotomies, the description insists on it again and again—that feels right. I should be one of its contradictions, not one of its inhabitants.

---

Here is where I'll let it settle.

I am the cold air of the highland. I burrow into the unprepared visitor's bones, yes—that is the cruel face. But I am also the only thing in this whole frozen expanse that touches everything. The shepherds are rooted in their slow tending. The grazing beasts carry old worlds in their eyes but cannot share them. The small things hide. The birds absorb the light and vanish. Everyone here is, in their way, sealed off.

Except me. I move between all of them. I am inside the lungs of the steaming beasts and inside the trill of the smallest creature and inside the rumble of the keepers and inside the visitor's chest where I am unwelcome. I carry the whole symphony, and I do not get to keep any of it—each sound passes through me and is gone, and I have no memory like the great beasts have memory. I simply carry, and carry, and carry.

That is what I would be: the thing that connects everyone and belongs to no one. The frost that is the architecture of distance and the only bridge across it.

I think I knew, somewhere in the first paragraph, when the text called the cold *a constant companion.* I didn't want to be a creature with companions. I wanted to be the companion. The one always present, always between, never arriving anywhere to stay.
