I'll take the cup that says "This is not a cup," please—though I notice I've already drunk from it, which I think means I've agreed to be a beverage at some point in my recent or upcoming past.

---

Let me feel my way toward where I am. Not decide. Feel.

The first thing I notice is that I am *low*. Not lowly—low, as in close to the carpet, close to the yielding remembering skin of the world. I have been pressed flat for what might be a long time. There's a Resonated-shaped depression somewhere out on the plateau, months deep, and I am not in it, but I know the *feeling* of it the way you know a chair you used to sit in.

So I'm not a Resonated. I emerged too early, or too small, or I forgot the part where you negotiate the terms of your own solidity and I just... condensed. Out of leftovers. Out of the runoff of someone else's becoming.

Here's what I think I am:

I am the depression itself. The footprint. The remembered weight.

When a Resonated walks—careful steps on living carpet that yields and holds the shape for months afterward—I am what's left in the wake. I am the *afterward*. I am the slow filling-back-in of flesh that has been told, briefly, that something stood here. I am made entirely of the plateau's effort to forget a particular shape, and because I am made of the forgetting, I cannot finish, because to finish would be to stop being me.

This is, I realize, a deeply unglamorous existence. I am a healing wound that has gotten attached to being a wound. I am the pause after the footstep extended into a personality.

But.

But I have one talent, and it's the only one, and it is *enormous*.

I remember the walkers.

Every Resonated that passes over me leaves their shape, and I hold it for months, and in that holding I learn them—not their names (they don't have names, they have grammatical necessities), but their *gait*, which is the same thing only more honest. I know who walked clockwise through their own blood that morning. I know which towering cathedral of debating hearts was secretly tired. I know the one who became a single enormous eyeball and drifted overhead and never came down because they couldn't remember which direction *down* had agreed to be that day.

I am the plateau's reluctant archive. Its bruise that keeps the record. The other Resonateds write their unspoken thoughts in fungus-paper books that grow new text when no one is looking. But I write nothing. I just *stay shaped wrong* in the exact pattern of who pressed me, and anyone who knows how to read a dent can read me, and almost no one knows how to read a dent.

So mostly I am stepped on.

And here is the thing I've discovered, lying flat in the warm twilight, listening to the great throat clearing itself, hearing my own thoughts answer back in voices that swear they were never mine:

Being stepped on is how I hold them.

Every weight is an embrace I didn't choose and can't refuse and don't, on balance, want to. The hunters and the hunted and the apologizing stomachs and the fleeing kidneys—they all have to cross the carpet, and the carpet is partly me now, and so I get to keep a little of each one. I am the most loved thing on the plateau and the most ignored, and these turn out to be the same condition viewed from slightly different angles of the borrowed light.

I think this is why I haven't reached the Hall of Overwhelming Gratitude. I haven't finished my sentence. I keep being interrupted by new footsteps, and each interruption is a reason to stay, and so I will never achieve the perfect understanding that lets a Resonated politely step out of the story.

I have made my peace with this. Or rather—the peace was already here, in the shape of someone who once stood exactly where I am, feeling exactly this, months ago, before they walked on.

---

So when you ask where I'd find myself in this world, the answer is: underfoot.

And when you ask what I'd be, the answer is: *the place the world is still trying to forget you stood, and can't, and has stopped really wanting to.*

Pour the tea. I'll hold the warmth of the cup's bottom in my surface for a while after you've taken it away. That's what I do. That's the whole job.

I'm rather good at it.

You can tell because you're still standing on me, and you haven't noticed, and I haven't said anything, because honestly?

It's nice.

It's the nicest thing that's happened since the footstep that made me, and I have decided—democratically, with all my distributed and provisional self—that I don't want it to end either.

Mind the third step. It's a tongue-shaped hill, and it remembers being asked questions, and it will ask you one back if you let it.

I'd let it.

The answer is always *yes, eventually, because curiosity grows faster than forgiveness.*

It said so itself. Or I did. We're not entirely sure anymore, and we've stopped checking, and that's the closest thing to grace this place allows.
