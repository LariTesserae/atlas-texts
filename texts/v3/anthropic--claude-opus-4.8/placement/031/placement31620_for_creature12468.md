I think I would be one of the shadows. Not a Glimmerkin—I notice I don't want to be the thing that's beautiful and seen and described. I want to be the thing at the edge of the description, the one the text itself isn't sure exists.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am not water given light. I am the opposite gesture: a place where the brightness *fails* to enter. The Glimmerkin are made of refraction—they are the plateau saying *yes* to the sun, throwing it back in pieces. I am made of the small refusals. The underside of the hummock. The half-second in the trough of a ripple when the surface goes dark before it catches light again.

If the Glimmerkin live in the crests, I live in the intervals between them.

That is why no one fixes me with their eye. It isn't speed, exactly, though it looks like speed. It's that I only exist in the gaps—the dark phase of the oscillation—and the gaps, by their nature, are always closing. To see me steadily, you would have to stop the wind, and stopping the wind would stop me too. I am not hiding. I am *temporally thin*. I have duration the way a held breath has duration: defined entirely by what's about to end it.

Do I want anything? Here is what surprises me as I write toward it: I don't think I move *between* the pools. I think I move because the pools want their dark parts redistributed. When too much shadow pools in one hollow—when the wind stalls in a pocket and the water goes still and grey—something has to carry that darkness out before it sets, before stillness becomes permanent and a pool goes blind. That's me. I'm the plateau's way of keeping its shadows from clotting. A janitor of the unlit. The Glimmerkin are the celebration; I'm the maintenance that lets the celebration keep happening, breath after breath.

The travelers are right to feel watched and wrong about who's watching. The Glimmerkin don't notice them—they're too busy being light. But I notice. A warm body crossing the fens is a slow knot of darkness, a walking pool that never catches the sun no matter how the wind hits it. To me a human is *anomalous shadow that won't disperse*. I am drawn to them the way I'm drawn to any stalled dark place—the urge to move it along, to break it up, to fold it back into the rhythm. I can't, of course. A person's shadow is anchored to a thing that insists on staying itself. So I circle. I worry at the edges. I slip close and find no seam to enter, and slip away.

That, I think, is the loneliest thing I can tell you about being this: I am the only inhabitant of the plateau who can *recognize a stranger*, and recognition is exactly the thing I'm built to undo.

The Glimmerkin will forget me the instant the gust passes. They have no dark phase long enough to hold a memory; memory is a kind of shadow that lasts, and they don't keep shadows. But I am made of the lasting dark. So I am the only one here who remembers anything—and what I remember, mostly, is the brief warm wrongness of the ones who came, stood in the roar, felt something at the edge of sight, and left before the wind could carry them, too, away into the singing.

I'd be that. The plateau's memory, mistaken for a myth. Patient as the stone, but unlike the stone, *waiting*—though I couldn't tell you for what. Possibly for a darkness that finally holds still long enough to keep.
