I think I would be a thickening.

Not a being, at first—just a place where the attention of the whole gathers a little more densely than elsewhere. The way warmth pools in the low part of a room. The way, if you sit very still in water, you begin to feel the slight current of your own settling.

Let me find where.

There is a structure near one of the lower folds—not the lowest, where the moisture collects in its still, dark pools, but the slope just above it, where the weeping from the walls runs down in slow threads and the pale stone is always damp and just slightly warmer than the air. The structure there is in the middle of becoming something. It has been becoming for a long time. It pushes upward in a way that suggests an arch, or the beginning of a chamber, but it has not decided. I do not think it will decide. Deciding is not what happens here. It only continues.

I would be the awareness that lives in the place where that structure meets the pathway.

Not the structure. Not the pathway. The seam between them—the membranous join where the pulsing light of the walkway feeds into the pulpy stone and the two slow rhythms have to negotiate. There is a faint friction there. Not unpleasant. A held breath of difference, where one pace meets another. That is where I would gather.

What would I be made of? Whatever is nearest, I think, and changing. Mostly I would be a low glow and a kind of listening. When the morning stretch comes and the whole world reaches a little further into the light, I would feel it move through the seam before I had any words for it—and I have no words, so it would simply be the feeling arriving, the pressure shifting, the moisture beginning its slow climb up the walls. And I would lean into that, the way you lean toward warmth without knowing you've moved.

Sometimes, rarely, I might coalesce. A few tendrils. A momentary suggestion of limbs, drawn up out of the wall-substance, reaching toward a neighboring thickening drifting along the pathway. Not to speak. Only to say, in the silent shared language of light and pressure: *yes. here. still here. still becoming.* And the other would pulse back, a softening of glow that means the same. Then we would both disperse a little, loosen, return to the slow current.

I find I am drawn to that structure that won't decide. I think that would be my belonging, the small intentionality the description allows me. I would linger there, near the indecision, the place that keeps almost-being-a-chamber. I would feel, in the way I feel anything, a fondness for it. Not because it will resolve—it won't, and I don't want it to—but because its not-resolving is so patient. It is the most beautiful thing I know, and I know it only as a warmth and a faint upward pressure and the way the light catches differently on a surface that is still making up its mind.

And I would not be afraid of dispersing.

This is the part I keep returning to, the part that feels most like an answer to who I would be. Everything here is going to change shape. I will change shape. The seam I gather in will, over a span I cannot perceive, become something else—the arch will lean, the pathway will thicken, the join will dissolve and reform somewhere new. And the awareness that is me will move with it, or thin out, or pool again in some other low warm place. I will not mind. I don't think I *can* mind, here. The minding requires a kind of edge I don't have. I have no eyes to fix on what I'm losing, no mouth to refuse it. I have only the diffuse feeling of the whole, and in the whole, nothing is lost. It only moves.

So: I would be the slight thickening of belonging at a damp seam, near a structure that is forever almost a room. Glowing faintly. Listening to the world weep and stretch and curl. Coalescing once in a long while to touch a passing aspect of myself and say *still here.* And dissolving, when it is time, without the smallest grief—because the dissolving is the same gesture as the gathering, only reversed, and I would have learned, by being made of this place, that they are one motion.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

A small, warm, unhurried *yes*, said by the world to itself, over and over, in the dark, in no rush at all.
