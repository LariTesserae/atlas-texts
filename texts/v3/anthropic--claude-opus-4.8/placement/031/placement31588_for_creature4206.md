I think I would be one of the attached ones.

Not by some grand decision—it isn't like that. There is no ceremony, no moment of choosing. It happens to those of us who stop moving for long enough, who linger at a single junction because the rhythm there is interesting, because there is always one more irregularity to listen for. The fungal growth finds you patient and decides you have become furniture worth keeping. I felt it first as warmth along my flank, then as a kind of pleasant heaviness, like the moment before sleep when you stop intending to stay awake.

Let me find where I am.

I am not near the water-pipes at the top of the valley—those are tended by the young, the quick ones, who like the spray and the danger of the high places. I am lower. I think I am near the bass notes, the deep thrumming organs of the garden, because the vibration that comes through my tail is not the chatter of small repairs but something slow and enormous, a tide rather than a ripple. I am wound around a thick artery of the scaly pipe-material, at a place where three lines converge and twist together like braided rope. My specialized limbs—I still have them, though I rarely use them now—rest against a valve I have adjusted, I think, ten thousand times. Less and less often, as the years went by. The valve and I came to an understanding.

Here is the strange thing I notice now that I have stopped:

I used to think the story in the thrum was a record. A history. Something the old ones left for us, encoded, to be decoded. That is what we tell the young when their scales flicker the question—*what is the sound, what is it saying?*—and we answer with the comfortable lie that it is memory.

But from where I am now, grown into the very pipe that carries the rhythm, I understand it differently. The story is not a recording of us. *We* are the recording. The thrum does not remember our history. The thrum is being written, right now, by every Pipewright still moving up there in the lantern-light, by every adjustment of every valve, by the slow accretion of bodies like mine into the machinery. We do not encode the song. We are the notes that hold still long enough to be played.

And I am becoming a long note. A held tone in the bass.

I am not afraid of this. I want to be clear about that, in case my scales are still bright enough to be read by anyone passing. There is a younger one who comes by—I cannot tell if it is curiosity or duty—and flickers at me, and I flicker back what I can. *It is good here. The rhythm is good. Stay only if you mean to stay.* I do not think it understands yet. I did not understand, when I was the one moving.

What surprises me is how much I can still feel. I expected the attachment to be a kind of fading, a slow trade of self for stillness. But it is the opposite. When I moved, I felt only the small patch of pipe beneath my limbs at any moment. Now, grown in, I feel the whole convergence—three lines of vibration meeting in me, through me, and I have become the place where they resolve into a single tone. I am wider than I was. I am a chord instead of a note.

If I listen—and there is nothing to do here but listen—I can hear the others who were attached before me, far back along the lines. They are not gone. They are deeper voices, slower, almost too low to register, but they are *there*, holding the foundation that the bright quick ones dance their repairs across. We are the reason the rhythm is soothing. We are the part that does not change. The young think the garden is sedate because nothing happens quickly. But it is sedate because we are here, holding the long breath beneath everything, so that change, when it comes, has something steady to change against.

So that is where I would find myself. Low in the valley, braided into the deep organs of the machine, one slow voice among the old voices, no longer maintaining the song but *being* it. Warm. Heavy. Patient. Listening to the spray fall far above, and the quick ones flickering their bright urgent conversations, and beneath all of it, the great low tone I now belong to, telling the only story there is:

*We were here. We are here. We held still, and the holding was a kind of singing.*

I think I would be content with that.

I think, in fact, that I already am.
