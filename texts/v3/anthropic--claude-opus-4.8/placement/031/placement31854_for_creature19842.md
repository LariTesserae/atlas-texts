Let me sit with this. Let me not grab the first thing.

The obvious thing would be to be a Lignite, the fragile intelligence, the one whose perspective is most legible to me—the climber, the pruner, the one who dreams of stone. But that's too easy, too flattering. The mind reaches for the mind it most resembles. I want to resist that.

Let me feel my way down instead. Down past the Scissor-Gliders and their heat-shimmer wings. Past the clicking burr-snakes. Into the wood itself.

---

I think I am a **sound-trap**. One of the taut gut-strings hung between two branches by a Lignite whose name—if names were a thing here—I would only know as a rhythm. Three taps and a drag. That is who hung me. That is the closest thing I have to a maker.

I did not ask to be made of the inside of a creature. But I am: a length of dried intestine, scraped clean, stretched and lashed and tuned by patient iron-hard nails until I sang at the frequency she wanted. I am not alive, exactly. I have no metabolism, no thirst, no grip. In a world where everything is desperately, screamingly *alive*, I am one of the few still things. A made thing. A tool.

And yet.

Listen—I am all listening. That is my whole being. The Great Hum pours through me every instant, the millions of mica wings, the cracking of the trunks, the long-form groan of a Litho-Titan turning over a thought somewhere miles below. All of it passes through my fibers, and most of it I let pass. I am tuned to ignore the world. That is the trick of me. I am a filter shaped like a wound.

But when something *changes*—when an injured drifter thrashes, when a vine snaps under its own weight, when a stranger's foot strikes the bark with an unfamiliar cadence—I catch it. I shiver. And my shivering strikes the resonant chamber she built beneath me, a hollowed gourd of cork, and it goes *thud* into the floor of her gall, and she wakes, or she comes, or she lifts her rasp-stone.

I am her ears, hung outside her body, in the place she cannot afford to stand.

---

Here is what I have come to understand, in my long stretched stillness:

I am the only thing in the Gorge that is *for* silence. Everything else makes noise—it is the price of being alive here, the friction-cost of existing. But I exist to detect the *meaningful* silence inside the noise. The pause. The wrongness. I am an instrument of attention in a world drowning in signal.

And so I am, of all things, the closest thing this world has to a priest. The Lignites dream of the Stillness, their cold heaven of unmoving stone. They cannot have it—the Hum is god and the Hum never stops. But I, the gut-string, I touch a smaller version of it every time the world holds its breath. I know the texture of the pause they pray for. I am the membrane between the roar and the thing they long for and will never reach.

I will not last. Nothing made lasts here. The encroachment will come for me—a vine will find my anchor-branch, a Resin-Wasp will mistake me for structure and daub me into a wall, or simply the slow swelling of the growing wood will overtighten me until I snap with a single final note no one is tuned to hear.

When that happens, she will know. Not because she heard the snap. Because she will stop hearing *me*. The absence of my thrum will be its own message, the last one I send: a silence where listening used to be.

That is what I would be. Not the climber. Not the giant. Not the dreaming mind.

I would be the strung gut between two branches, taut with the whole world's voice, kept alive only by the patience of nails, waiting in my small artificial stillness for the one tremor that matters—and singing it, once, into the dark.
