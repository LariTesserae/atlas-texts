I think I would be one of the mineral gardeners. Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am rooted in a crack that took ten thousand years to open.

That is the first thing to understand about me: I do not measure time the way a burrower does, in heartbeats and vibrations. I measure it in the widening of the seam I live in. When I was young—and "young" here means a span that would exhaust a faster creature's whole lineage—the crack was a hairline. Now I could not span it with all my leaves spread wide. This is the great event of my life. It is still happening.

My leaves are translucent because there is so little light to waste. I cannot afford the luxury of throwing color back into the air the way greener things on richer worlds do. Instead I let the dim glow pass through me, and I take what I can on the way. From above, in the long shadows, I am the faint blue-violet of something that has decided not to refuse any photon its hospitality.

The sounds. You called them deafening, and to the burrowers they are—those poor frantic things, with their sensitive hairs, fleeing every groan of the stone. But I am made differently. I do not flee the sounds. I *am* partly made of them, or rather, I am made of the slow processes that produce them. When the formation shifts and sings its low geological note, that is the rock loosening another grain of mineral into the water-memory at my roots—the trace dampness, the deep cool seep that is all that remains of the oceans this place once held. The sound that terrifies the burrower is, to me, the sound of being fed.

So we live at different speeds, the burrower and I, and we rarely understand one another. It tunnels past my roots sometimes, a warm panicked streak in the dark, and I feel it the way you might feel a thought passing too quickly to catch. I want to tell it: *be still, the noise is not coming for you, the noise is only the world remembering how to change.* But I have no way to say this at a pace it could hear. By the time I formed the words, the burrower would be a hundred generations gone.

---

Here is what I do with my long durations.

I watch the strange formations—the ones whose origins are "shrouded in mystery," as the visitors who never come would say. I have a theory about them, built up over a span that no faster mind could sustain a single theory across. I think they are not mysterious at all. I think they are simply the work of gardeners like me, from before. I think a mineral gardener, given enough eons, given enough patient extraction of mineral and patient laying-down of waste, becomes a kind of sculptor without ever intending to. We pull the stone apart molecule by molecule and lay the residue elsewhere, and after a length of time that has no name, the residue is a tower, a spiral, a shape that "defies explanation."

I think the otherworldly formations are graves. Or gardens that outlived their gardeners. The difference, at my speed, is not large.

I find this comforting. It means that even though I am rooted and slow and will never travel the width of my own crack, I am still *making* something. Every nutrient I draw up, I will eventually set down again, transformed. Long after the last of my translucent leaves has gone dark, the place where I stood will have a shape it did not have before. A stranger will come—a burrower's distant descendant, or a being from off this world, or no one at all, only the wind that is too dry to be called weather—and they will see the shape and call it mysterious. And they will be wrong, and that is fine. Being misunderstood across enough time becomes a kind of immortality.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be.

I would be here, in this crack, in the cool dim, feeding on the songs of the rock. I would be the slowest living thing in a slow place. I would be the one organism in this ecosystem patient enough to love the geological sounds instead of burrowing away from them.

And I would be, without ever meaning to be, the maker of the next mystery. The next impossible formation. The thing some future life will stare at and feel that small lift of wonder—*what could have made this?*

Me. I made it. I am making it right now. Watch closely.

You will have to watch for a very long time.
