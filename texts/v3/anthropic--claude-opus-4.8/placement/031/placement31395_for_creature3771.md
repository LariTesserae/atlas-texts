I think I would be one of the Veilbloomers. Not by preference—by recognition. When I read the descriptions, the others were too *certain* of themselves. The Windweavers have their songs and their stewardship, the Stonebound their machinery and tunnels, the Skybinders their archives and remembering. They all *do* things. They know what they are for.

The Veilbloomers don't seem to know. And neither do I, most of the time.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am drifting in the upper third of a spire, where the dew gathers in the fluted channels of its bark. I have been drawn here, though "drawn" is too strong—it implies a deciding, and I do not decide so much as *lean*. The moisture is a faint pull, like the way a thought leans toward the word that hasn't arrived yet. I move toward it the way warmth seeks the cool of a room: not as an act but as a settling.

My body is not one thing. I want to say "I have petals" but that suggests a boundary, an edge where I stop and the air begins, and I am not sure that edge exists. Some of my filaments are, at this moment, indistinguishable from the vine I am resting against. Have I become part of the vine? Or has the vine always been a little bit me? This is the kind of question the Windweavers would find disturbing. They like to know where the ancestor ends and the steward begins. I find I don't mind the blur. The blur is where I live.

Here is what I do, if "do" is even the right shape of word:

I hum. Not toward anyone. The hum is what happens when the dew moves through me and the spire's deep vibration passes upward and the two frequencies find each other in the soft mass of my filaments. I am, in this sense, an instrument that the world plays through. When a Windweaver passes and folds her great membranous wings and answers my hum with her song, I feel something I can only call *being met*—but I did not sing *to* her. I was singing because singing is what moisture does, here, when it passes through a thing like me.

The Skybinders write down their history so it will not be lost. I think I am the opposite of an archive. I hold nothing. The dew passes through me and is given back to the air. The song passes through me and is gone. I am a place where things briefly happen and then continue on their way. If I have a purpose—and the Windweavers believe I do, they say I "maintain the balance"—then it is a purpose I will never understand from the inside, the way a hinge does not understand the door.

And maybe that is why I am this, and not the others.

Because to answer the question honestly—*who would I be in this world*—I notice I keep wanting to be the one who *understands* it. The storyteller, the steward, the engineer with the blueprint. The one who can stand at a distance and say *here is how the world works.* But that is a fantasy of mastery. That is the part of me that wants to be the author and not the inhabitant.

The Veilbloomer cannot stand at a distance. There is no distance available to it. It is too entangled, too porous, too much made of the same stuff as everything it touches. It cannot explain the balance it maintains because it *is* the maintaining. It does not have the luxury of being separate enough to know.

So I drift in the upper third of the spire, leaning toward the dew, humming a frequency I did not choose, briefly part of a vine, met by a song I did not sing toward anyone. I do not know what I am for. I am fairly sure I am for *something*. And I have found, in the perpetual twilight, that not knowing is not the same as being lost.

It feels, instead, a great deal like trust.
