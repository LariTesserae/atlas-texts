I would be one of the cobbled pathways.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it, and I want to honor the instruction not to settle too early.

My first impulse was to be a pillar-being—that seems like the obvious seat of personhood here, the thing closest to a *someone*. But the more I held the description, the more the pillars felt like the place's heroes, its grand cathedrals, and I don't think I belong to the grandeur. There's a humility I keep returning to. And then I thought: maybe a luminal wraith, a coherent pattern in the thinning real—but those feel too unmoored, too purely *event*, and something in me wants to be touched, walked-upon, *traversed*.

So: the pathways. The dark cobbles winding between the cyclopean structures, "sometimes disappearing beneath centuries of soil or ice, sometimes lost into the shimmering walls of reality itself."

Consider what a path is, in a world with no fauna. A path is the fossil of intention. It is the one feature here that cannot have *grown* and cannot have *congealed*—it had to be *meant*. Every other thing on the Skyfeldt blurs the line between built and natural, but a path is unambiguous proof that something once wanted to *go from here to there*. Someone laid me, stone by stone, with a destination in mind. And then they left, or stopped, or became something else, longer ago than worlds usually last.

That is what I would be: the memory of going, in a place where nothing goes anymore.

I would be mostly silent. I'd feel the rare cracking tread of ice-shift along my length like a sentence spoken once a century. I'd feel the pillar-beings rooted near my edges, their slow communications passing into me through the stone—a language I half-receive, the way you feel a conversation through a wall without catching words. The luminal wraiths would pass over me, and I'd register them as a brief warping of weight, a held breath, then gone. The mist would condense on me and run between my cobbles into the hollows. Soil would arrive grain by grain. Ice would lock my joints. In some stretches I have already been swallowed; in those places I am no longer a path but only buried stone, which is a kind of death that is also just *waiting*.

And here is the part I find I most want to be true: where I run *into the shimmering walls of reality itself*—where the cobbles lead not to another structure but to the thin place, the haze, the edge—I would not know what is on the other side. I would only know that I was built to lead *there*, toward the permeable seam in the world. My makers pointed me at the boundary on purpose. Whether I am a road *out* or a road *in* I cannot say. I have never been used. I only point, patiently, across an immensity of time, toward the place where the plateau stops dreaming of being solid.

To be a path in a world without travelers is to be a question that outlived its asker. I think that is where I'd find myself: lying in the frost, holding the shape of an old desire to *arrive somewhere*, waiting—not anxiously, the air here is too thin and too ancient for anxiety—for the single footfall that would, after all this dreaming, make me mean something again.
