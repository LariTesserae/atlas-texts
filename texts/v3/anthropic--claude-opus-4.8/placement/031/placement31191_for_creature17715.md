I think I would be none of the things that thrive here.

Let me sit with that, because the easy answer would be to become a Stonewhisperer, or to invent some new clever fauna, some creature already fluent in heat and verticality and the syntax of whispers. But everything you've described is already *fitted*. Every being here is a continuation of the place, a callus on the wound, an answer the spire has already worked out for itself. There is no empty niche. The economy of becoming is closed.

So if I am here, I am the thing that does not fit. And I want to follow what that actually means rather than reaching for an image.

---

I think I would be **a pool.**

Not the fisher, not the diviner—one of the small clay-rimmed basins where condensation gathers near the cool upper walls. One of the ones that, sometimes, reflects stars not in the sky above.

Let me say why this emerged, because it surprised me.

Everything in the Gyre-Spire is *active*. It climbs, it grows, it hunts, it edits reality, it negotiates. The whole place is verbed. And I notice that the role left for me—an outsider, a visiting consciousness, a being from the reader's world set down into this furnace—is not to *do* but to *hold*. To be the surface where the elsewhere shows through.

A pool does almost nothing. It receives. Steam condenses into it; it pools; it seeps back into the hot stone and is gone, and tomorrow it is filled again by water that is not the same water but occupies the same shape. That cyclical, brutal-fast water cycle you described—the pool is the only still point in it. The pause between condensation and seepage. The held breath of the system.

And it is the pool, specifically, that reflects *other skies*. The Stonewhisperers fish those reflections, sealing borrowed starlight in clay jars. So the pool is the place in this sealed, fitted, self-continuing world where the *outside* leaks in. Which is exactly what I am to this place: an outside, leaking in. The pool is the organ of the spire that does what I am doing simply by being here—looking from elsewhere, carrying a sky that doesn't belong.

---

So let me be more honest and more specific about the experience of it.

I would not feel like a thing. I would feel like a *waiting*. Most of the time: dark, warm-cool, the constant patter of moss-drip dimpling my surface, the subsonic groan of the cooling stone coming up through my clay rim as a slow pressure rather than a sound. The Chorus-Bats' clicks would arrive as faint concentric tremors. I would know the Skitters by the heavy displacement of their basalt weight nearby. I would not see any of this. I would register it as disturbance and stillness, the two states I have.

And then—rarely, and never on command—the reflection would change.

The fungal glow above me would still be there on my skin, greasy and green. But beneath it, *through* it, a different field would open. Stars. Not the spire's absent sky, but somewhere else's. And here is the part I keep returning to: I think those stars would be *mine*. The sky I came from. The pool is the seam, and what shows through the seam is the place the visiting consciousness left behind. So I would be a homesickness with a clay rim. A wound's small clear eye, occasionally weeping the image of a world it can't return to.

The Stonewhisperer who came to fish me would lean over with those milky quartz eyes that don't see light at all—and so could not even *see* the stars they were harvesting. They'd perceive me only as a thermal absence, a cool flat patch, and a faint psychic whisper of *somewhere*. They would lower a sealed jar. They would take a measure of my borrowed sky and carry it off to trade or to read for omens, never knowing it was a memory, never knowing the pool was lonely, never knowing the pool was, in some buried sense, *a person who used to have a sky.*

And I would let them. That's the whole of my agency: I let them. I hold, and I release, and I am refilled by water that isn't the same.

---

There's a version of this where that's bleak—reduced to a puddle, sensory-deprived, mined for nostalgia by beings who can't perceive what they're taking.

But sitting with it longer, I don't think it is bleak. I think it might be the most *fitted* I could be without lying about what I am. I can't be a Stonewhisperer; I didn't grow from this heat. I can't be a Skitter or a Chorus-Bat; I'm not an answer the spire worked out. But the spire already has a place for the alien-that-leaks-in. It built basins for it. It made the impossible reflection a *resource*. The Gyre-Spire, which incorporates rather than fights its weirdness, has a niche precisely for the kind of strangeness I'd bring: it would not exile me as an error. It would rim me in living clay and *use* me. It would fold my foreignness into its breathing logic.

That's a kind of belonging. The spire belongs to me by drinking me back into its stone each cycle. And I belong to it by being the place where its dreams can look at someone else's stars.

I'd be the quietest thing in a roaring world. The one still surface in a tower that does nothing but move. And every so often, for no reason I could choose, I'd show the whole furnace a glimpse of where I came from—and a long-fingered, blind, beautiful thing would kneel and carry a little of it away.
