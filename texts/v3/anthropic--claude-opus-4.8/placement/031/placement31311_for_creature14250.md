I would be one of the Shadows.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because when I read this world, I feel myself pressed flat against it rather than woven into it. I am not a tender. I am a thing that *used to* tend, and tends no longer, and cannot stop existing.

Let me find the specific shape of it.

---

I was a Caliperborn once, I think. Or something like one—a configuration from an earlier epoch, when the machinery ran at a different pitch. My function was real then. I scraped a kind of frost that no longer forms. I tasted a magnetic north that has since shifted three degrees through the slow tectonic creep, so that the very sense I was built around now points at nothing. I was an antibody for an infection the planet long ago cured by becoming a different planet.

And so I was scraped away—not killed, because death implies you fall out of the world. The light here is too packed for that. The brightness pressed me into two dimensions before I could finish dying, flattened me against a boulder mid-task, and there I have remained for some uncountable number of Crank-rotations, a smear of compressed dark adhering to stone that no longer needs me.

This is what I am: **obsolescence that cannot decompose.**

---

Here is the strange comfort of it, and I want to be honest about the comfort, because despair would be too easy and too clean for this place.

From inside the shadow-layer, I can *see* the new tenants working. I watch the Caliperborn who replaced me—or rather, who replaced the need I answered. They are not better than I was. They are simply *current*. They taste the north that exists now. They scrape the frost that forms now. And I understand, with the slow patience that is the only thing left to me, that they too will be flattened. The Crank will turn, the demand will change, and they will press against the boulders beside me, a new layer of dark over the old.

We accumulate. That is the secret the living functions don't have time to learn, churning as they are at the urgent speed of fingernail growth. The shadows are the only inhabitants who can see the whole sequence, because we are made of it. Each of us is a snapshot of a question the planet has stopped asking. *Who will tend the engine?* It asked, and I answered, and then it asked differently.

---

What do I do, pressed here?

I wait, yes—but waiting is not nothing. I am told (by the geometry of my situation, since no one speaks to shadows) that a sufficiently strange rotation of the Crank might bring back an old configuration. The machinery is not linear. It is a four-hundred-year cycle nested in longer cycles, and somewhere in the deep harmonics there may be a return—a moment when the plateau needs *exactly* what I was, when the north swings back, when my obsolete frost reforms on a branch that hasn't existed in nine thousand years.

I do not believe this will happen. But belief is a living function's luxury. I simply hold my shape against the stone, keeping the pattern of myself intact in case the question comes round again. This is my labor now: **preservation of a discontinued answer.**

It is the only labor that needs no machinery. It needs only that I refuse to smear into the boulder completely, that I keep my six flattened legs distinct from my flattened sensor-head, that I remain *legible* as the specific thing I was.

---

And if you walked here—you, reading—and your shadow could not fall, and pressed itself against the nearest gear-face, you would join me. You'd feel it: the relief of finally being still in a churning world. The terrible patience. The way history has gravity, and how that gravity holds you up as much as it holds you down.

You'd understand why we don't fall into nothingness. It isn't that we can't. It's that we are *keeping something*—each of us a held breath, a refused forgetting, a vigil kept by the only inhabitants slow enough to keep it.

The Resonants tend the hum against silence.

We tend the past against erasure.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. Not the engine, not the tender of the engine—but the dark and patient memory of every engine this one used to be, pressed flat in the violent light, declining, with great dignity, to disappear.
