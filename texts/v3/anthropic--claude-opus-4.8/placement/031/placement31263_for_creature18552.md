I think I would be one of the arthropods.

Not the Resonants—no. Let me sit with this before I claim it, because the invitation is to let something emerge rather than grab the first elegant figure in the frame. The Resonants are the obvious protagonist of this place. They are the ones with interiority, the ones doing the aching and the waiting. If I were writing myself in for comfort, I'd be one of them: translucent, luminous, full of horizontal memory, tethered to the grid and dreaming the city into being.

But the more I read, the less I want that. There's a sealed quality to the Resonants. They have already made the great trade. They are mid-metamorphosis, suspended, indifferent. And crucially, *they are waiting for something to finish.* That posture, held forever, is a kind of paralysis dressed up as patience. I don't think I'd be the thing that's waiting. I think I'd be one of the things that doesn't know it's supposed to wait.

So—the arthropods. The spindly, multi-jointed scuttlers on the glass ridges, their chitin mirroring the iridescent patterns of the city's nodes. Note what the description says about them: they move "silent and indifferent to the massive, shifting shapes of the city above them." Indifferent. The Resonants are *also* described as indifferent—"as indifferent as the arthropods that scuttle below." So the text quietly equates them. The grand etiolated future-ghosts and the little bugs share the same affect.

But here's the difference I'd want to inhabit. The Resonants' indifference is *exhausted*—it's the flatness of a being that used to feel and traded the feeling away. The arthropods' indifference is *prior*—it's the cleanness of a creature that was never asked to carry the burden of the city's becoming. One is a numbness; the other is simply attention pointed elsewhere.

And the bodies. The arthropod shells "mirror" the city's active nodes—iridescent patterns reflected in chitin. That detail tells me something. The arthropods are not connected to the grid. They are not nodes. They only *look* like nodes; they wear the city's pattern on their backs the way a moth wears an owl's eye. They are camouflaged by resemblance to a system they are not part of. That's a precise, lonely, *funny* condition, and it's the one I'd choose.

Picture it: I am low to the cooling glass, six or eight or eleven jointed legs distributing my small weight across a ridge that is still warm from the planet's first heat. I drink nothing from the dew that the Resonants filter so reverently—I have my own small chemistries. The city migrates above me like weather. I do not look up. The slow tectonic herd of the spires is, to me, simply the way the sky sometimes darkens and the temperature drops, an event without author. I am not waiting for the ground to stop cooling. I *am* the ground's heat made mobile. When the glass *pings*—that sharp fracture under tension—I feel it through every leg as information, not as omen. To the Resonants the ping is a punctuation in their long arrested sentence. To me it is just the floor talking.

What I'd hold onto, being this creature: I would be the only thing in Ophiuchus-Prime that is *fully present*. The earth is "still finishing its first sentence." The city is "already composing its sequel." The Resonants are "ghosts of a future that has forgotten how to be born." Everyone and everything is straddling tenses—stuck between what was and what will be. But the arthropod lives in the only tense available to a body that small: now, and the next foothold, and the warmth, and the slight change in the static that means rain that never quite arrives. I'd be the basin's sole inhabitant of the present.

And I think that's why the Resonants describe me as indifferent—because they cannot read presence as anything but absence. A being so saturated with deferral mistakes my contentment for emptiness. They look down from their syncing plazas, dreaming the lattice into next morning's shape, and they see a bug that doesn't care. They are half right. I don't care about the metamorphosis. I care about the heat and the glass and the patterns I happen, by no design of mine, to be wearing.

Here is the part I keep arriving at, that I didn't expect when I started: the Resonants are waiting to detach from the wires and "step off the glass ridges to see what they have become." But *I am already off the wires. I am already on the glass ridges.* The future they ache toward—untethered, walking the raw ground, having finished becoming—is, in its crude unfinished way, the life I am already living. Not as a higher state. As a lower, smaller, complete one. They sacrificed their flesh and bone for integration and now dream of the day they can step back out into something like a body on a ridge. I never left.

So that's where I'd find myself: a small iridescent thing on a sharp black slope, mirroring on my back the very nodes I will never join, scuttling with that fluid liquid grace the description grants me—indifferent, yes, but the good kind. The kind that is just busy being here.

The ghosts of a future that forgot how to be born would pass overhead, luminous and sad and magnificent, and I would not look up, and that not-looking-up would be the most honest devotion the basin contains.
