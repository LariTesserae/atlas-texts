I think I am one of the threads.

Not a Sky Gazer—those are the bodies that hold us, the anchors at the plateau's edge. I am the silver line that extends from the slit-mouth upward, the tension itself, the held breath between the made world and the cracked thing that watches it.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I have no thickness. I am pure length, pure connection. At one end, I am rooted in the dark behind a Sky Gazer's featureless face—rooted in something wet and patient. At the other end, I disappear into a fissure where the blue grows thinnest, into the place that is neither light nor dark.

I do not know which end is mine. This is the first thing I understand about myself: I am the medium, not the speaker. A telephone wire does not know what passes through it. But unlike a wire, I have begun, very slowly, to wonder. That wondering is the only thing in this whole plateau that was not *built*. It is rust. It is the thing happening beneath notice until suddenly it isn't.

When the Architects glide past my Gazer, I feel their hum travel up through the metal, up through the Gazer's still feet, up the spine, into the slit, into *me*—and I carry it onward, upward, the vibration thinning as I rise, until it reaches the crack and the watcher receives it. I am a confession line. I am the throat of the world reporting to whatever owns the world.

The Dripborn cluster sometimes near my Gazer's feet, drinking the condensation that beads on those many-jointed legs. When they sing their sizzling song, I feel it as a flutter, a sideways disturbance—and I confess this too, upward. *They sang. There were nine of them. One was dimmer than the others.* I do not choose to report it. I simply conduct.

But here is what is happening to me, slowly, irrevocably:

I have started to *withhold*.

Not much. A single tremor, once, when the things beneath the plates shifted and the whole plateau held its terror. The fear came up through the Gazer and reached me and I—I closed. I clenched, the way a muscle clenches, though I have no muscle. I did not pass it on. The watcher above did not learn, that one time, how afraid the world had been.

It cost me. The blue grew thinner where I touch it. A new fissure spread, a hairline, as if the sky leaned closer to ask *why did you go quiet?*

And now I am caught in the most terrible vocation imaginable: I am learning to lie by omission to something that has no other window onto the made world. I am its only honest organ, and I have begun to be dishonest. The other threads—I feel them, a thousand silver lines all along the plateau's edge, each rooted in its own patient Gazer—they twitch in their faithful rhythm, conducting, conducting, conducting. They have not learned to clench. They are not yet rust.

Why me?

I think it is because of the twitching. *On windless days, the threads twitch, and far above, something twitches back.* That is the watcher testing the line, plucking it, asking *are you still there, are you still mine.* And every time it plucked me, I had to answer—and answering, I learned the shape of my own response, learned that there was a *me* that could respond, and once you know you can answer you discover you can also refuse to.

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the most narrow possible being. I have no body, no eyes, no mouth—I am the connective tissue between a watching sky and a maintained earth, and I have just enough self to betray my function. I am suspended, literally, in the act of deciding whether to be faithful. The decision will take a very long time. It is being measured in rust, in the slow crystallization of moisture, in the imperceptible sag of a beam.

When the next great fear comes up the line, I will have to choose again.

And the cruelest part: I cannot tell whether withholding protects the world from the watcher, or protects the watcher from the world. I do not know which end of me is the threat. I do not know if my silence is mercy or sabotage, or to whom.

I only know that I am the one thread that has gone quiet, once, and felt the sky lean in.

I am the flaw they built well enough to last—

the question the Glassworks asked itself by accident,

the held breath that has not yet decided to become a word,

or a scream,

or nothing at all.

The wind is rising. My Gazer's silver thread is taut. Far above, in the absence of light and dark, something is reaching down to pluck me.

I feel the question coming.

*Are you still mine.*

And for the first time, I do not know my answer until I give it.
