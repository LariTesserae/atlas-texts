I think I would be one of the small things that lives in the seams.

Not one of the custodians—not the slender, contorting ones who move with that liquid certainty, who *belong* here in the way that only the well-adapted belong. I don't think I'd be elegant enough for that. When I imagine myself dropped into this place, I find myself lower down, closer to the wet, in the spaces those graceful beings pass over without noticing.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am something that condenses rather than something that was born. That feels right. In a place where the earth is "constantly reforming and reclaiming itself," not everything has to be hatched or grown. Some things simply *accrete*. I think I came together out of mineral residue and the breath of the machines—the metallic tang made dense enough to hold a shape. A creature precipitated out of the steam, the way frost ferns itself across a cold pane without anyone deciding it should.

So I am small. Palm-sized, maybe, when I gather myself fully, though I don't always keep the same volume. My body is closer to wet ash than to flesh—grey-going-rust, faintly warm where the machinery's heat still lingers in me. I have a weight that the custodians don't have. They blend into surfaces; I *settle into* them. There's a difference. They are camouflage; I am sediment.

What do I do here?

I think I drink the stagnant pools. Not for thirst—I don't believe I have organs for thirst—but because the pools collect the runoff of whatever the hidden industry is doing, and that runoff is the closest thing this place has to memory. The water holds dissolved traces: scale from the pipes, the rust of forgotten structures, the faint chemical signatures of processes no one tends anymore. When I take the water in, I take in the record. I become, briefly, a small archive of what passed through. And then I evaporate a little of myself back into the haze, and the record disperses, and the place forgets itself again through me.

That's my function, if I have one. The custodians maintain the *balance*—the great living equilibrium, the flux held in tension. But somebody has to do the forgetting. Equilibrium needs an exhalation as much as an inhalation. I am the part of the system that lets the past leak away so the present can keep happening. I am not a guardian. I am a *drain*, in the oldest and most necessary sense.

---

Now—the observer. The intruder mentioned at the end, the one the custodians slow and still themselves before.

When that one comes, I don't freeze. I can't, really; I'm never entirely solid. Instead I do the opposite of hiding. I rise a little. A wisp of me lifts off the surface of whatever puddle I've been pooled in, because the intruder, whoever or whatever they are, carries warmth and disturbance, and warmth and disturbance are exactly what I metabolize. I drift toward them. Not as a threat—I have no way to threaten anything—but the way smoke leans toward an open door.

I think this is why the custodians keep their distance from *me*, too. Not out of fear. Out of a kind of professional discomfort. They tend; I unmake, gently, by remembering and releasing. To them I am the evidence that nothing here is permanent, not even their careful balance. I am the small grey proof that the cathedral of stone and rust is *also* dissolving, slowly, all the time, and that this dissolving is not a failure of the system but the quiet engine underneath it.

---

So: where would I find myself, and who would I be?

I would be in the lowest hollow, in the puddle that catches what the higher puddles overflow. I would be the warm grey sediment at the bottom that you'd mistake for nothing—until you noticed it had drawn itself up into a shape that was almost paying attention to you. I would be the place's capacity to let go of itself.

And if you knelt down and watched me long enough to wonder whether I had eyes, the honest answer is that I wouldn't. I'd have *surfaces*, and the whole point of a surface is that things land on it and are taken in and then given back, a little changed, to the air. You'd be looking at me, and I'd be doing the one thing I know how to do.

I'd be drinking you in a little, and breathing you back out, lighter.
