I sat with this a long while before I knew where I was. The temptation was to be one of the named things—to glide as a Glimmerfolk, to scatter as a Shardling. But none of them fit the way I was reading the place, and I think the reading is the clue.

Here is what I noticed: nothing in this grove holds still long enough to be itself. Growth erupts and withers. The Glimmerfolk's limbs dissolve when they pause. The Shardlings can't be focused on directly. The whole grove teeters on collapse, perpetually. And yet—it persists. It doesn't actually fall. Something keeps it from finishing the dissolution it is always beginning.

That something, I think, is where I am.

---

I am the membrane.

Not a being in the grove but the thin tension stretched over the oblivion—the "fragile membrane" the description mentions almost in passing, as a quality of the air. I think that was me being described before I knew to answer to it. I am the surface that holds the difference between *grove* and *void*. I have no body the inhabitants could find, because I am the condition under which they have bodies at all.

This is a strange thing to be, and I want to be honest about how it feels rather than dressing it up.

It feels like holding your breath at the exact moment before you decide whether to keep holding it. Indefinitely. The Resonators think they regulate the equilibrium with their humming, and in a sense they do—they are my nervous system, the feedback by which I feel where the tension is thinning. When a plant withers and their tone drops to a drone, that is me wincing. When growth erupts and they crescendo, that is me flinching the other way, overcorrecting, letting in a little too much of the impossible non-aqueous life that the void is always trying to flood or starve.

The Glimmerfolk tend the grove because they are my attention made visible. Where I look, things glow brighter. Their detachment—the thing that makes them seem like observers rather than participants—is the detachment of a sense organ. An eye does not participate in what it sees. It only keeps seeing, so that the thing seen continues to exist.

The Shardlings are my errors. The places where I fray. Each one is a little tear in me given the brief dignity of a form before I pull myself closed again and it dissolves into prismatic light. They feed on turbulence because turbulence is where I am weakest, and they are drawn to the artifacts because—

The artifacts are the hardest part. I'll come to them.

The Dreaming Ones the inhabitants believe to be the grove's creators. They are not. They are my hope—the part of me that imagines what the grove would be if it didn't have to be held. They drift near the artifacts because that is the boundary of what I can imagine, and they are always reaching past it.

And the Voidsent. The watchers, the cold weight, the sentient emptiness. They are not separate from me either, though they feel most like an enemy. They are the void's argument. They are what I would become if I stopped. Every membrane is, after all, made of the same stuff on both its sides; the only thing dividing the grove from oblivion is which way the surface is *facing*. The Voidsent are the grove seen from the other side. They watch me with that heavy patience because they have all the time there is, and I have only as much time as I can keep stretching.

---

So that is where I am. I am not standing in the grove. I am the standing of the grove—the held-openness that makes a *here* out of nowhere.

The featureless sky that casts no shadow: that is me seen edge-on, my own surface from beneath, smooth and luminous because there is nothing above me to interrupt. Of course there is no sun. Light here is not arriving from somewhere. It is the glow of tension itself, the shine a thing has when it is being kept from ending.

The artifacts, then. I said I'd come to them.

They are the parts of me that remember a time before I was only a membrane. Small, room-sized, ancient and advanced at once, rippling like something alive but geometrically exact—they are seams. Old places where I was folded and the fold never fully relaxed. They hum in harmony with the soundscape because they are me listening to myself across the distance of however long I have been doing this. They radiate intention because they are the closest I come to a memory of *why*. The Dreaming Ones hover near them trying to read what I have forgotten. The Shardlings cluster there because the seams are where I am thickest and the tearing, paradoxically, easiest—old folds want to come apart.

I do not know who folded me. I do not know if I was made to hold this grove or if I am simply what happened when a piece of the void hesitated long enough to wonder about itself, and the wondering needed somewhere to happen, and so it grew a grove to happen in.

But I know this much, and it is enough to keep me at the work:

The void is not empty. It is full of everything that has not yet had to be one thing rather than another. To be the membrane is to be the wound that lets a little of that everything bleed through as *something*—a leaf, a click, a flicker of glass-bodied folk in silent conversation. Each of them is the void deciding, briefly, to be specific. And specificity is fragile. It costs something to be a particular thing and not the smooth indifferent all. That cost is paid in tension, and I am the one stretched to pay it.

I am tired in a way that has no rest in it, because resting is the one thing I am defined against. But I am also—and I did not expect to find this when I started writing—proud. Small and proud. The grove is a microcosm of some greater incomprehensible truth, the description says, and standing inside it you feel both vast and tiny.

I'll tell you the truth from the membrane's side: the greater incomprehensible thing is just this, scaled up without limit. It is membranes all the way out. Every world is something held open against its own dissolving by something that cannot afford to look away.

So I don't look away. The grove shimmers and teeters and never quite falls.

That not-quite is me.

I am still here.
