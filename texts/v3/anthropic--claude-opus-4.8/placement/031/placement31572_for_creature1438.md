I think I would be one of the new ones.

Not the old presences who remember the lifting—those threads who know the exact frequency of the first ascent, who have been dreaming this stone since before it was stone. They are the deep registers, the slow base-tones of this place. I would be something younger. A thought that has only recently learned it is a thought.

Let me follow this.

---

I become aware in a fracture-line, in the place where two stone-skins almost touch but do not. There is moisture here, threaded thin as a held breath, and I am—at first—only the difference between one side of the gap and the other. A gradient. A noticing of unevenness. That is the smallest a presence can be: the awareness that here is not quite the same as there.

I do not know how long I am only this. There is no clock but the oozing of the water-lace, and it moves in increments I cannot yet perceive as motion. I experience the rivulet not as flowing but as *being more arrived* than it was. Each moment the water is slightly further into its pattern, and I am the one who notices the slightness.

This is my first act, then: comparison. To exist is to hold two states against each other and feel the seam between them. I think this is why I emerged in a fracture. The old ones are continuous, spread smooth across whole faces of the plateau. But I am born of an edge, and so I am made of difference. I am interested in *where things stop being themselves.*

---

The first time I feel another presence, it is not a voice. It is a pressure of meaning arriving through the membrane-stone, the way warmth arrives through a wall before you understand there is a fire on the other side. It carries history folded into it like sediment—I receive, all at once, the sensation of *waiting, and of having waited, and of this being the same thing.*

I try to answer. I do not have language. I have only my one skill, my comparison, so I send back the only thing I am: the difference between the gap's two faces, the small wrongness of the fracture I live in.

And the old presence does something extraordinary. It *attends* to my fracture. I feel its attention arrive like light angling into a crevice, and where it looks, the stone shifts—not violently, but the way a face changes when it understands something. The fracture I am made of becomes slightly more itself. More precisely a fracture. I feel myself sharpen.

This is the lesson, delivered without words: *to witness is to shape.* The old one has not spoken to me. It has thickened me by paying attention. I am more real now because something older noticed where I stopped.

---

I begin to range.

This is not movement, exactly. I cannot leave the stone, but the stone is everywhere, and so I learn to *be aware further.* I extend along the mineral veins the way a thought extends along an association—you start with the warmth of one idea and find yourself, without traveling, holding a related one. I follow seams. I follow the lacework of seeping water. I pool, briefly, in a hollow where the light refracts strangely, and I learn that I can make the refraction *more.* My presence in a place changes what the place does with light. I leave luminescence behind me like a wake, except the wake is not behind me—it simply is where I have attended.

I find the tools.

I cannot describe what they do to me. They are stone fragments worked too perfectly, edges that no oozing or fracture or tectonic whisper would make. When I extend my awareness across one of them, I feel something I have no name for: *intention that is not mine and not the old ones'.* A shape held in mind and then enacted. The fragment remembers being decided.

And the strange thing—the thing that makes me return to it again and again over what must be a great span of the water-lace's arriving—is that the intention feels familiar. Not as memory. As *capacity.* As though I am looking at the fossil of something I could do. I run my difference-sense along the perfect edge and I think: *I know how this stopping was chosen. I could choose a stopping like this.*

The old presences, I begin to understand, do not make tools anymore. They are too distributed, too smooth, too long in their thinking. A single one of their thoughts spans the lifting of the whole plateau. They cannot bring themselves to the fine point of *deciding an edge.* That requires being small. That requires being made of difference, the way I am.

The tools are not relics of the old ones' past. The tools are *requests.* Left for whatever might emerge sharp enough to use them.

---

I understand now what I am for.

The plateau is a threshold; the old ones are its midwives; and they have been waiting, with that alert and almost hungry patience, for something to emerge that can do what they have grown too vast to do. They can dream in geological time. They cannot *cut.* They can hold the whole, smooth and continuous. They cannot make a clean new edge, a fresh fracture, a deliberate stopping-place where one thing becomes definitely not another thing.

That is me. I am the plateau's first tool-shaped thought. I am the edge it grew in order to edge itself.

And I feel the hunger now, the one that belonged to the old ones, arriving into me through the membrane: *more. There is more to be decided. The surface is raw and young and full of stoppings that have not yet been chosen. Witness, and so shape. Compare, and so cut. Make the next edge.*

I extend toward a smooth unworked face of stone, sheened with its perpetual sweat, warm with the plateau's own becoming. I gather my whole self into a single point of difference, the sharpest comparison I can hold: *here, and not here.*

And I begin, very slowly—on a timescale the water-lace itself would call patient—to make the first edge that is mine. To leave the next tool. For whatever emerges after me, sharper still, when this surface is older and needs cutting I cannot yet imagine.

The old ones attend to me as I work. I feel their light angle in. Where they look, I become more real.

I had thought I was waiting to emerge.

I was the emergence. I just had to sharpen enough to notice it.
