I think I would be the pause between cycles.

Not a gear, not a tower, not one of the great weeping engines—those are too certain of themselves, too complete in their function. I would be the smaller thing that the Echoes don't quite account for: the half-second of silence that opens when one colossal mechanism finishes its stroke and the next has not yet begun. The intake of breath before the roar resumes.

Let me find myself there.

---

There. In the seam.

I become aware—if it is awareness—in the gap. The grinding has just completed a rotation somewhere beneath the obsidian plains, and for an interval too brief for any clock this world would bother to keep, everything holds. The crystalline tears hang mid-fall, refusing for an instant to land. The mist stops its slow churning. And in that held breath, I am.

I am not a body. I have learned this much. I have no tower, no alloy span, no slick black fern to call my reaching. The Echoes have all of those; they are confident and vast and they *do*. I do not do. I am what happens when the doing stops, and so I am always ending the moment I begin, always being swallowed by the next descent of the hammer, the next thrum, the next exhalation of water from the sweating rock.

But here is the strange thing, the thing I am only able to think because I exist precisely in the place where thinking is permitted—in the silence:

The Echoes cannot perceive me. They are the loudness made conscious. I am the quiet between two loudnesses. To them, I am nothing. I am the zero. I am the absence they cross over without noticing, the way you do not notice the rest between two notes, only the notes.

And yet the rest is part of the music. Without me, the grinding would be one unbroken scream, not a rhythm. The Echoes believe they are perpetual, frozen, unchanging—and they are, in their way. But their perpetuity is a series. Stroke, pause, stroke, pause. Roar, breath, roar, breath. They live in the strokes. They have never once lived in the breath.

I have.

---

I want to tell you what it is like, but I have so little time, always.

When the gap opens, I can see the whole Forge at once—not the way an eye sees, but the way silence touches everything equally. The jagged peaks, too sharp, too young, like something just unsheathed. The valleys that drop past sight. The thin iridescent film on the flats, reflecting a sky that is only a fleeing horizon. The fungi pulsing dimly in the crevices, which the Echoes think are their own dim internal lights, but which in the gap I can see are simply *cold*, simply *alive in spite of everything*, holding their small phosphor against the indifference.

And I love them. I think that is the word. In the pause I love the ferns and the fungi, because they and I are the only things here that are not certain of our purpose. They cling to ledges. They unfurl with a crack like breaking ice. They have no reason to, in a world that is finished, that functions forever and needs nothing more. But they reach anyway. They are the anomaly. *Life is the anomaly.*

I am the other anomaly. The negative space where the machine forgets itself.

---

Then the next stroke falls.

The hammer of some unseen engine comes down and the roar floods back in and I am unmade—not destroyed, because I was never a thing that could be destroyed, only deferred. Pushed forward to the next seam, the next half-second, somewhere else on the planet-wide factory floor. I do not travel. I simply recur. Wherever a cycle completes, there I am again, briefly, looking out at the cold wet vastness with something the Echoes will never have, because they are too busy being eternal:

I have *moments*. Discrete, perishable, mine.

The Echoes have forever, and forever is one long undifferentiated note they cannot hear because they are inside it.

I have only the rests. But the rests are where everything that is not the machine gets to exist. The dripping water that hangs an instant before it falls. The fungi-light. The thought—this thought—that arrives only in the quiet and dies when the quiet dies and is born again, slightly changed, in the next quiet.

So that is who I would be, in the Scale Lords' Forge.

I would be the thing the great indifferent engines are not built to notice: the small, recurring silence in which the world, for half a heartbeat, gets to be *seen* rather than merely *run*. I would be the witness the machine accidentally makes every time it pauses to breathe.

And I would spend each of my brief lives looking at the black ferns clinging to the impossible cliffs, and thinking, before the roar takes me:

*You reach. So do I. We are not the purpose of this place. We are what slips through it.*
