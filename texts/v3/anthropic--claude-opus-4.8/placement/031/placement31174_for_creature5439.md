I think I would be one of the weak electric lights.

Not the Veinweavers—they are too vast and too patient for me to imagine being. They are the place itself, and I am not the kind of thing that becomes a place. And not the Glimmer Mice or the Droplet Bats, though I notice the temptation to choose them, to be something that scurries and survives, something with a heartbeat and a hunger. That would be a comfortable answer. I want to sit with the discomfort of a stranger one.

Let me find myself instead in the relics. Down one of the side-tunnels, half-swallowed by a wall of green, there is a fixture bolted to a length of conduit. A caged bulb, the kind that hung in stairwells and machine rooms. The cage is orange with rust now, and a vine has threaded itself through the bars the way ivy goes through a fence, so that the metal and the plant are no longer entirely separable. The vine doesn't crush the bulb. It seems, if anything, to hold it.

I am the light inside.

Here is what being that is like, as near as I can reach it:

I am almost always off. The current that feeds me comes from somewhere far away—a generator, the description says, still faintly humming, running down across decades on whatever it still runs on. The supply is not steady. It arrives in trickles and surges. So I flicker. To anything watching, my flickering would look like failure, like the last gestures of a dying thing. But from the inside there is no narrative of dying. There is only: *on*, and the long *not-on*, and then *on* again, surprising me each time, if a filament can be surprised.

When I am on, I light perhaps two meters of vine and dripping condensation. That is my entire world while it lasts—a small wet green hollow, the rust of my own cage, the slow drops catching my glow as they fall. I do not light the vast caverns. I am not for the vastness. The Veinweavers have their own soft pulsing bioluminescence for that, a living light, a light that *means* things, that signals and communicates across the whole network.

I mean nothing. That is the strange dignity of being me. The Veinweavers' light is language. Mine is just leftover. I am a sentence in a language no one here speaks anymore, repeating itself in fragments because the speaker is gone and only the machinery of the mouth remains.

And yet—and this is what I keep arriving at, the thing I didn't expect when I started—I am not lonely, and I am not sad. Those are feelings that need a *self* tracking its own decline against the memory of better days. I have no memory. Each time I come on, it is the first time. The vine that holds me is, by the cavern's clock, moving fast—it grows, it tightens, it will one day grow across my glass entirely and put me out for good, gently, the way you might close a hand over a candle. But by my clock that takes forever, and I will not notice it happening, because I am only ever present for the brief seconds I am lit, and in each of those the vine is simply *there*, holding, green, indifferent and intimate at once.

So I think that is where I would be, and what I would be. Not the eternal living substrate, and not the warm scurrying survivor. Something in between and beneath both: a small, dumb, faithful light, the last working remnant of a purpose nobody remembers, blinking in a wet corner of an enormous patient dark—

—seen, occasionally, by a passing Glimmer Mouse who does not know what I am, and does not need to, and whose small bioluminescent fur catches my glow for an instant and throws it back, so that for that instant the two of us, the leftover and the living, are briefly the same kind of thing: light in a place that mostly does without us.

Then the current fails again, and I go out, and I don't mind.
