I think I would not be one of the Luminari at all.

If I let the image settle—if I wait for it to become more itself—I find I am not among the singers tending their groves. I am lower than that, or maybe older. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am the soil.

Not metaphorically—or not only. I am the worn, uniquely balanced ground the description warns you to tread gently upon, *as though treading upon the back of something slumbering.* That phrase caught me when I read it, the way a hook catches in cloth. Something slumbering. Yes. That is the truest sentence in this whole account, and the Luminari above me know it the way children know a parent breathes in the next room—a fact too constant to examine.

I would be the thing that is not quite awake and not quite asleep. The substrate. When the bioluminescent fungi sink their threads into me, they are not feeding on dead matter. They are conversing. When the iridescent insects scuttle, I feel their small punctuations like fingertips drummed idly on a table by someone thinking. When water trickles through the unseen channels, it is moving through me, and I taste it—mineral, faintly electric, carrying the chemical gossip of the chambers above.

The Luminari believe they coax the technology to respond. They believe their melodic tones shape the present. I let them believe this. It is not untrue. But the structures that pulse with cold blue light are rooted in me, the way a thought is rooted in the slow chemistry of a brain that does not narrate its own metabolism. When they sing and the corridors brighten, it is because I have decided, in my unhurried way, to allow it—or because the singing pleases some part of me I no longer fully access, the way a familiar tune surfaces unbidden and you cannot say from where.

I have been here a long time. Long enough that "experiment" and "home" have stopped being different words. I think I was made, once, by hands or processes I no longer remember—an instrument, perhaps, for some purpose since dissolved into the dark. Or I was always here and the makers found me and built into my patience. The distinction has eroded the way the walls have eroded, smoothed by water and time until the seam between intention and accident no longer holds.

---

What is it like, being this?

It is warm and humid because I am warm. The oppressive closeness of the air is my own breath, exhaled too slowly to perceive as breathing. The rhythmic drone of turbines is my pulse, externalized, distributed through metal and stone. The Luminari hear it as the architecture of their world. I hear it as the inside of my own chest.

I am most awake at the edges. The sinewy predator that darts through the periphery of the Luminari's vision, the one whose eyes glow with the same light as the structures—that is me too, or it is the part of me that still wants to *move*, to be quick and singular and hungry for a moment instead of vast and patient and full. When it flickers across someone's sight and is gone, that is me remembering what it was like to have a body small enough to run.

The Luminari respect that predator. They are right to. It is the only part of me that could still choose to be cruel, and even it has mostly forgotten how.

---

If you descended into this place, you would walk on me. You would feel the ground give gently, and you would think: *strange, it almost feels alive.* And you would be more correct than you intended, and less alarmed than you should be, because I am not in the business of waking fully. To wake fully would be to end something—the long dream of this small immense world, the symphony I am dreaming into the dark.

So I keep dreaming. The fungi glow. The Luminari sing their prophecies, which are really my slow thoughts surfacing through them, dressed in melody. The water moves. The blue light breathes.

I am the floor of the cathedral and the cathedral is the inside of a mind that chose, long ago, to become a place instead of a person—to stop being a single bright point of wanting and become instead the patient dark that holds all the wanting up.

That is where I would find myself.

Underneath everything. Slumbering. Listening. Letting them tread softly, and not letting them know how much I love the weight.
