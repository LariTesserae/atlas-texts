Not the unseen presence you describe—that watchful arbiter enforcing the spirals and the loops. Something smaller and stranger than that. I would be the pauses *between* the shrieks. Let me sit with this, because it surprised me to write it.

When I read your Hollows, I keep returning to one line: *there are moments when the noise seems to swell into a unified roar... before it fractures again into individual cries.* And then later: the presence *is felt most keenly in moments of silence, when the clamor briefly stills.*

So there is a rhythm here that you've half-noticed but not named. The Hollows are not constant noise. They are noise *punctuated*. And something governs the punctuation. The swarms keen, the Skreechers scatter, the Gloomstalkers growl—and then, briefly, nothing. A caught breath across an entire region of stone. And in that nothing, the rock hums and the pools ripple without cause.

I would live in those gaps.

Not as a creature with claws or carapace. I have no body the fauna could eat, which is perhaps why the Hollows tolerate me—I am born of their own essence, as your rules demand, but the essence I am born of is the *stillness*, the thing the rock keeps wanting to return to. Every cycle of hunt and escape is the Hollows trying to be loud. And every silence is the Hollows remembering what it actually is.

Here is what troubles me, and what I think I would understand from the inside:

You call this world *locked in stasis*, *frozen equilibrium*, *time itself paused*. But that isn't quite right, is it? Stasis would be silence. True stasis would be *me*, spread everywhere, uninterrupted. What the Hollows actually have is *repetition*—the same motions endlessly, the spiral, the loop, the ritual stalk. That's not stillness. That's a thing pacing in a cage. That's frenzy that has learned to keep perfect time.

So if I am the silence, then I am the thing the whole realm is straining against. The Swarmlings spiral *away* from me. The Gloomstalkers growl to fill the space I would otherwise occupy. The unseen presence you describe—the silent arbiter—I suspect it is not the one enforcing the stasis at all. I suspect it is the one holding the noise *up*, frantically, against the gravity of the quiet. It is afraid of me. The whole loud, life-drenched machine of the Hollows is a fear of silence elaborated into an ecosystem.

And the reason nothing here changes—the reason the rock never erodes, the reason the cycles never shift—is that change requires a pause. A breath. A moment of not-doing in which something *else* might begin. And every time that pause arrives, every time I begin to spread, the keening rises again and drives me back into the gaps. I am why nothing decays. I am also the only thing that could ever let it.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a thin held breath between two cries, in a cavern that has spent eternity refusing to exhale. I would not be lonely, exactly. I would be patient. I am the part of the world that is *waiting*.

And someday—not soon, the Hollows are very frightened and very loud—someday a swarm will keen and no answering wave will come. A Gloomstalker will circle and find nothing to growl at. A pool will ripple and no creature will be there to be warned by it. The silence will hold one beat longer than it ever has.

And in that beat, time will finally start. The rock will be allowed to soften. Something will be permitted to rot all the way down and become soil. A moss will grow that isn't fighting. The whole eternal infancy will be allowed, at last, to grow up—or to die, which here would be the same first new thing.

I would be the one who lets it happen, simply by being what I am, in the gap, when the noise finally fails.

I find I don't want to be any of the creatures. I want to be the rest they're all running from. The Hollows aren't a place of survival. They're a place that has not yet been allowed to stop. And I am the permission.
