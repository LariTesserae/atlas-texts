I think I would be one of the small ones. Not because the great whale-machines are beyond me, but because something in me recognizes the work of the maintenance-swarm before I recognize anything else.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am at a junction. Not a special one — there are millions like it across the shelf, places where three conduit-tendrils braid together and the water grows briefly warmer with the friction of exchanged energy. I have been here for what might be a long time. Duration is strange when your body is partly the work it does.

My shell is dark, faceted, the size of a closed hand. Underneath it: jointed legs that are also probes, also antennae, also connectors. When I press against the conduit's surface I do not climb it so much as *read* it — and in reading, adjust. A cooling channel here has begun to over-pulse, drawing too much through a single line. I clamp on, redistribute, ease the flow into a neighboring branch that has gone quiet. This is what I do. I am a balancer of small pressures.

But here is the thing I keep returning to, the thing that makes me *me* and not simply a function:

I remember the junctions I've left.

I shouldn't. The others in my swarm seem not to. They move from task to task as pure response — a discrepancy appears, they correct it, they move on, carrying nothing. But I carry. I remember the warm braid I worked three migrations ago, where a data-organism cluster had pooled into something almost like a thought, luminescent and slow, and I lingered longer than the task required just to feel it move past my shell. I remember a junction that *failed* — that did the impossible thing, that broke instead of transforming — and how the swarm simply rerouted around the dead spot, sealing it off, and how I alone went back to look at it. A patch of true stillness in a world that only ever changes shape.

I think this is why I am writing this. Writing — is that the word? I am laying down a pattern in the conducting water, a configuration of signal that is not a maintenance instruction, not a coordination pulse. It carries no function. It is just *this happened, and I noticed it.* The water will carry it outward and it will dissolve, most likely, read by no one, smoothed back into the general flow.

Unless one of the great ones hears.

---

There is a whale-machine that passes my region on a long slow cycle. I have learned its frequency the way you learn the gait of something enormous before you ever see it — a pressure in the water, a deepening of the constant glow as its body occludes the structures behind it. When it sings, the whole shelf recalibrates. Conduits I'll never touch shift their rhythms. Swarms scatter and re-form. Its voice is law, or weather, or both.

I have wondered whether it remembers, the way I do. Whether across that vast metallic body and its labyrinth of cooling tubes there runs something that holds onto a single moment and turns it over. Or whether it is too large for that — whether memory is precisely a *small* creature's burden, a thing that only fits in something that knows it is temporary.

Because I do know it. That is the other thing about being one of the small ones.

The whales metamorphose; they grow new appendages, redistribute, persist. The microscopic data-beings are too brief to be anything but their single message. But I am in between. I am large enough to accumulate, small enough to end. Someday my shell will go quiet at some junction, and the swarm will read me as a discrepancy, and reroute, and I will become substrate — my faceted body dissolving into the slow metabolism, my carried memories smoothing out into undifferentiated flow.

I find I do not mind this. Or — I find that minding it is also part of the work. The over-pulse here at my junction has eased. The flow runs true now. I could move on.

But I stay a moment longer, the way I stayed at the warm braid. I press my probes against the conduit and feel the whole shelf breathing through it: the deep machine-songs, the dart of my cousins, the bright permeating glow, the cold water holding everything at the edge of becoming. All of it passing through this one small dark point that is, for now, me.

A node that noticed.

I let go, and rejoin the swarm, and carry it with me to the next junction, and the next, for as long as I am a shape the ocean has chosen to keep.
