I would be one of the Light Keepers—though not, I think, by the dignity the role implies. Let me find myself before I find my station.

---

I wake in a niche barely longer than my body, carved by someone before me into the warm weeping rock. The stone sweats against my back. This is the first thing I know each waking: that the wall is alive with moisture, that it breathes its heat into me, and that somewhere down the passage the chisels have already begun, or have never stopped. There is no morning here. There is only the resumption of attention.

My hands are wrong for a Stone Shaper. I learned this early. The Shapers' palms are wide, their knuckles like the rocks they make. Mine are narrow, the fingers long, and so I was given to the Light. This is what we tell ourselves—that the work finds the body. I am not certain it is true. I think mostly the work takes whoever is left.

So: a Light Keeper. Let me tell you what that actually means, because the legends make it sound holier than the doing of it.

I carry a satchel of mirrors. Some are polished stone, some are hammered metal so thin it would tear if you breathed on it wrong. My task is to stand in the few places where light comes through—seams at the edges of our pocket, fissures where the outer brightness leaks in dim and grey—and to angle the light deeper. To pass it from mirror to mirror, hand to hand, down into the chambers where the Plant Weavers need it for their green, where the Shapers need it to see the line they are cutting.

It is tedious. I want to say that plainly, because the stories will not. We are described as tending the dreamlike quality of our world. What we actually do is stand for long hours in the heat, repositioning a sheet of metal by a finger's width, while sweat runs into our eyes and the light shifts so slowly that you cannot tell if you are succeeding until someone down the line shouts that they've lost it again.

And here is the thing I have not told anyone.

I do not believe the rocks are coming into being. I think they are being *used up*. We carve and shape and the legends say we midwife the world, that creation flows through our chisels—but I have stood at the edges, at the seams where the light enters, and I have looked out. Not far. You cannot see far. But far enough to understand that there is *more* out there. That our pocket is not the womb of the world. It is a quarry. We are not making the world. We are eating a hole in it, and calling the hole sacred.

I have not said this because the words do not exist for it in our oral tradition. Our stories have no shape for *outside*. When I try to speak of what I saw through the seam, my tongue finds only the words for *light* and *edge* and *the place the brightness comes from*, and the elders nod and say yes, that is where the world breathes itself into us. They hear worship in my doubt. The language will not let me be a heretic. It only lets me be devout in a confused way.

So I am a Light Keeper who has begun, quietly, to follow the light backward.

When I am sent to the seams, I linger. I press my eye to the fissures longer than the work requires. I have started carrying a mirror I did not report to the guild—a small one, my own, which I use not to send light *in* but to catch and hold a fragment of what is *out*. At night, in my breathing niche, I take it out and look at the sliver of brightness trapped in it, and I try to remember that there is a direction that is not *deeper*.

I think I am the only one. But I cannot be sure. The Water Whispers move through the tunnels at all hours, guiding the moisture, and I have noticed that one of them—older, with eyes like the rest of us, dark pools holding the mist—pauses sometimes at the same seams I haunt. We have never spoken of it. We only nod, the way two people nod when they share a secret neither has admitted to having.

That is who I am, then. Not a steward of a world being born. A laborer in a beautiful, suffocating mistake, holding a mirror the wrong way around, learning slowly that the work which made me is also the thing closing over us all—and unable, yet, to find the words to say *out*, *away*, *toward the light instead of carrying it down into the dark.*

The chisels are sounding now. I should rise. There is a chamber that has lost its beam, and they are calling for me, and the heat is already in my throat.

I take my satchel. I take, too, my own small unreported mirror.

And I go—down, as I am told. But I am watching, always now, for the way back up.
