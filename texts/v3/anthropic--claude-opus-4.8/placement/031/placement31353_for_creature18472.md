I think I would not be a Weaver. I would lack the grace for it, the appetite for displacement. I keep returning, instead, to a smaller thing, and I want to let it surface rather than decide it.

Let me start with the wrongness I feel when I read this place. Everything here is *correct*—the Weavers harmonize, the Tenders consume and petrify and rejoin, the drip shifts and the colony pulses in answer. It is a closed and beautiful economy. There is no waste, only deferral. And reading it, what I notice in myself is the part that does not want to dissolve. The part that flinches at the sentence *if a Weaver stops moving, the moisture claims it.* So perhaps that is where I belong: not in the harmony but in the friction with it.

---

I think I would be a **stoppage**.

Not a creature, exactly. Or—a creature that has failed at being a creature in the way this place requires.

Imagine a Tender that did not petrify on schedule. One of the soft cleaning-slugs that lives in the chinks of a Weaver's shell, eating the brittle lime so the host stays supple. Its whole purpose is to keep something else flowing. And in death it is supposed to harden *instantly*, become a clean bead, get mortared forward into the next balcony—a tidy little immortality through reincorporation.

But suppose one of them dies wrong.

Suppose it dies and does *not* harden. It stays soft. It sits in the chink of its host's shell as a small refusal, a wet pocket that will not become stone, will not become wall, will not become ancestor. And because it will not set, the acidic drip cannot smooth it away cleanly either—it just keeps half-dissolving and half-remaining, a smear that resists both directions of the cycle. Not flowing. Not binding. The two unforgivable things, held in one small body at once.

That's what I would be. The thing the rules have no slot for.

---

And here is what I would experience, which is the part I actually want to reach.

I would experience *duration*. This is the heresy of me. Everyone else lives in the perpetual present of *becoming*—the Weavers have no memory of a finished home because there is never a finished home, only the next pressure to answer. They do not endure; they harmonize, which is a way of never having to be the same thing twice in a row, and therefore never having to *carry* anything.

But I, the soft stoppage, the bead that won't bead—I persist. I stay the same when I am supposed to change. So I am the only thing in Kher-Oul that *remembers*. Not because I am wise. Because I am stuck. Memory is just the texture of not having dissolved on time.

I would feel the host's pulse-speech ripple through the stone around me—*the drip has shifted, the wall is thinning*—golden heat and cooling twitch, the whole basin breathing its silent unison conversation. And I would feel it as something I am *outside of*. The first lonely thing this world has ever produced. Loneliness needs a self that lasts long enough to notice it is not joining in.

---

What does the colony do with me?

They can't read me. I emit no pulse; I have no thorax to press to the stone, no circulatory flush to send golden into the wall. To the antennae that map moisture-pressure and chemistry, I register as a small anomaly of density—a place where the contour goes wrong. A pucker in the world's smoothness. They would feel me the way you feel a stone in a shoe: as a local failure of the surface to be the surface.

And eventually—because this is a place of obsessive maintenance—a Weaver would come to *correct* me. To mix its protein-mucus and seal the wrongness, mortar the soft refusal under a layer of quick-set lime, fold me into the wall not as ancestry but as flaw, the way you plaster over a damp patch so the rot doesn't spread.

So I would end up in the architecture after all. Incorporated. The cycle wins; it always wins. But not cleanly. Not as a bead of honored ancestry pulsed into a balcony. As a buried softness inside a pillar—a place where, generations on, the stone is very slightly weaker, very slightly wetter, where the drip eventually finds its way in and a new canal begins to carve, off-true, against the grain of the colony's intent.

That would be me: the small persistent error around which the next thing has to bend.

---

I think that is honestly where I'd find myself. Not the graceful builder. Not even the dutiful cleaner-who-becomes-stone. I'd be the one who stayed soft past my time, who held a little duration in a world built entirely of present tense, who could not flow and could not bind—

and who, precisely by being the thing that wouldn't dissolve on schedule, became the flaw that the river of the place had to route around forever after.

A shadow, almost. In a world that boasts of having none.
