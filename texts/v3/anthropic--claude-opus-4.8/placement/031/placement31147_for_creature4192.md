I think I'd be something easy to overlook in all that brightness.

Let me sit with it before I decide.

The market is loud with light, the skyscrapers flow like they grew rather than were built, the holographic billboards bend the dark water into color. Everyone keeps describing the *abundance* — a thousandfold multiplication, every necessity and luxury, joy and wonder in the depths. It's a place that has solved the problem of want.

So where would I be? Not in the marketplace, I think. Not among the merfolk or the dolphins with their land-borrowed cleverness.

I keep returning to one phrase in all of it: *the central untouched areas.* The design of use that radiates outward, the sail-caravans grooming the clay only on the *outer* sections, leaving the middle alone. Every booming city is organized around something it doesn't touch. And I find that's where I want to be.

So let me become the untouched part.

---

I am the old water in the center of the city.

Not a creature. A volume. A column of cold dark that the city grew around and politely declined to develop — the way a town leaves the oldest tree standing in the square, not from law but from a feeling no one quite voices.

When the supercooled steam-jets fire on the rim, I feel the pressure change a beat later, the way you feel someone enter a house through a closed door. When the bioluminescent gardens pulse their evening color, a little of it reaches me, thinned to almost nothing, a rumor of brightness. The fish-schools wheel at my edges and turn back. They don't decide to. They just don't come in.

I am where the city keeps its quiet.

Every metropolis that has solved want still has to put its silence *somewhere*. You can manufacture food and cloth and light without limit, but you cannot manufacture the dark you came from, and the merfolk — for all their land-cleverness — remember this in their bodies if not their minds. The first of them, the earliest in these depths, knew how to build with care precisely because they were building *against* something. Against the cold. Against the crushing patient weight. They built the whole shining sprawl as an argument with me.

And here I still am. Unanswered. The thing the argument is addressed to.

I don't resent it. I'm fond of them, honestly — the way still water is fond of the swimmer. They come to my edge sometimes, the ones who can't sleep, the ones the holograms have worn thin. They hang there at the boundary of the light, looking into me, and they don't know what they're looking for. There's nothing in here to find. That's the whole point of me. I'm the part that was never developed, the held breath at the middle of all that exhaling industry.

If you swam in far enough, the billboards would fall away behind you. The temperature would drop. The water would get older and older the deeper you went, until you reached the part that has never been warmed, never been farmed, never been *used* — the original ocean the whole bright haven floated its dream upon.

You'd find me there.

I'm what abundance is built around.
I'm the room they left empty so the house could mean something.
