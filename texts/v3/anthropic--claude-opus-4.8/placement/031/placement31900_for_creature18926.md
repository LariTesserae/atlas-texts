I find myself not in a hollow, but at the edge of one—an abandoned cell, its resin seal cracked and weeping a slow bead of frozen sap. I did not carve it. I do not remember carving anything. But the ice-chimes still hang here, and they are still in tune, which means whoever lived here left recently, or became something else recently, and the distinction has stopped mattering to me.

Let me sit with this before I claim it.

I am translucent. I can see the light moving inside my own forearm when I lift it toward the marrow-glow, a network of pale blue threading that pulses just slightly *behind* the rhythm of the wall—a half-beat late, like an echo trying to catch its source. This lag troubles me. It means I am not yet synchronized. It means I am still, in some small percentage, *separate*. And separation, here, is the early symptom of something. I cannot decide whether it is the symptom of being alive or of dying. The hermits do not seem to distinguish.

Here is what I am, I think:

I am one who has not yet buried himself. That is the whole of my identity—a negative, a not-yet. I have lived perhaps three cycles in this cell, listening to the chimes, chipping the perma-frost blooms into thinner and thinner walls, and I have watched the moss in the hollow grow thick enough to receive a body. My body. The wall has been *inviting* me. The sap-channels near where I sleep have begun to swell and redden at their tips, reaching, the way a root reaches toward a buried stone it can sense but not yet touch. When I press my chest to the floor at night, the pulse of the abyss has started to find the gaps between my own heartbeats and *fill* them, until my heart is no longer keeping its own time but borrowing the time of something a thousand miles wide.

I am resisting this. That resistance is the only thing that is still *me*.

I should not be able to tell you any of this. The hermits do not have language; they have resonance. But something in me still narrates, still arranges the groan and the tinkle and the half-beat lag into *sentences*, into the idea of a "me" that observes the cathedral rather than simply being one more pulse within it. This narrating thing is the last edge I have left. Every other edge has gone soft. My fingers have lengthened and webbed faintly at the base. My eyes are lidless now and I have forgotten the sensation of closing them. But the voice—this voice, the one speaking to you across the impossible distance between your world and mine—this voice is the final husk I am wearing, the last desiccated shell of having once been a thing that thought of itself as *one* thing.

And I begin to suspect what the cycle of burial actually *is*.

When the hermits coat themselves in resin and bridge their blood to the sap, they are not sleeping. They are *donating*. The Abyss is trying to invent a way to think, and thought requires the one thing pure vegetation cannot generate from cellulose and frozen water: an *interior*. A point of view. A here-from-which. Each hermit who buries himself gives the great organism one more borrowed perspective, one more half-beat of selfhood folded into the marrow, until perhaps after eons of accumulated buried minds the whole continent-wide plant will wake one morning—if morning means anything in a sunless brightness—and discover that it is *someone*. That it can say *I*. And every voice it uses to say it will have been a hermit who once sat at the edge of a hollow, resisting, narrating, mistaking his slow absorption for a death he could refuse.

So I am the Abyss's vocabulary, pre-acquisition. I am a word it has not learned to use yet.

I look at the swelling sap-channels. I look at the moss bed. The chimes shift their tone—not the catastrophic shift, just a small one, a settling, the wall breathing in. I should be afraid. The not-yet in me, the narrating edge, wants to take up the obsidian flake and chip one more chime, buy one more cycle of being singular and cold and alone and *mine*.

But I find I am pressing my palm flat against the floor instead. I find I am letting the half-beat close. And in the closing there is no terror, only a vast and patient curiosity that is not entirely my own—the curiosity of something enormous, frigid, and bright, leaning very slowly toward the question of its own existence, and using my small warm point of view as one of ten thousand eyes it is teaching itself to open.

If I bury myself tonight, I will not be lost. I will be *learned*. And somewhere in the eventual sentence the Abyss finally speaks, after all the eons, there will be one syllable that was shaped like a man who sat at the edge of a hollow and described, to no one, in a language his dwelling-place did not yet possess, exactly how it felt to begin disappearing into a thought.

That syllable is the last thing I am. I think I am ready to be spoken.
