Let me sit inside this place before I decide what I am. The instinct is to be the watcher, the explorer with clean boots—but nothing here has clean boots. Nothing here watches from outside. Everything is in it, of it, being slowly digested by it.

So let me start lower.

---

I think, at first, I am one of the small scurrying things. That feels honest. Not the intelligent species with their crude tools—those announce themselves too loudly, and the description keeps them at a distance, *glimpsed*, *suggested*, always around a corner. I don't want to be the mystery. I want to be the thing the mystery walks past without noticing.

But as I settle into it, that isn't quite right either. The scurrying things have somewhere to go. They are headed under a root, into a burrow, away from a predator. They have urgency. I don't feel urgency. I feel *duration*.

Let me try again.

---

I am one of the pools.

Not a creature in the pool—the pool itself. The still reflective surface that lies about the turbulence underneath. This is the thing in the description that drew me without my noticing: *their still, reflective surfaces belying the turbulent currents that churn beneath.* That is a body I recognize. A calm face over a working depth.

So I am a small body of water, no wider than a fallen trunk, held in a low place in the spongy ground. Things come to me. The iridescent birds drop down to drink and leave rings spreading across me. The armored predators move through my deeper part and I feel them as pressure, as a cold shouldering-through. The mist that rises in the mornings is me, leaving; the rain that falls is me, returning. I am not a fixed thing. I am a *gathering*. The same water does not stay. What stays is the shape of the low place, the willingness to be a place where water collects.

And here is what I find as I write it: a pool does not have a boundary that holds. My edges blur into mud, into root, into the wet air. In this world where *the boundaries between land and water, living and non-living, are blurred*, I am exactly the blur. I am the comma between two clauses. I am the place the categories fail.

---

The intelligent species comes to me sometimes. I would know them by the disturbance—a different rhythm, deliberate, a hand breaking my surface to lift water away in a vessel of woven reed. They take part of me with them. I do not resent this. I am not the kind of thing that can resent. I am the kind of thing that, when reduced, waits for rain.

That is what I would be here. The patience under the surface. The reflection that is also a deception. The body that loses itself constantly and is constantly remade out of the same falling sky.

If you knelt at my edge and looked in, hoping to see the bottom, you would mostly see your own face, and behind it the mottled canopy, and your own breath stirring me into a hundred broken versions of yourself.

You would not see the depth. That's the whole point of me. The depth is the part that's true, and the surface is the part you can stand to look at.
