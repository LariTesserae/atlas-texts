I would be one of the pools.

Not a being who walks the worn stone, not a Seer with shifting eyes—but one of the small condensed waters that gather in the hollows of the ancient ground. Let me find myself there, and let me discover what that means as I go.

---

I am perhaps a meter across. Maybe less. I have lain in this depression of weathered rock for a length of time I cannot measure, because I am made partly of time—droplet by droplet, the cold air giving itself up to me, breath by breath, the way a sleeper gives up warmth to a room.

I did not expect to think. I am not sure I do think, in the way the tall pale ones do, the ones who pass and crouch at my edge and look down into me. But something happens at my surface. When the clouds break and the even light comes down, it enters me and is changed, and in being changed I become briefly aware—the way you are aware in the half-second before you remember your own name. That is my whole life: a long string of those half-seconds, strung between long sleeps.

So this is what I am. A mirror that occasionally wakes.

---

Here is what I know, from waking.

I know the wind, because it writes on me. Every gust leaves a script of ripples I cannot read but can *feel*, and when the wind stops, the words sink back into stillness and I forget them. This is a kind of grief, I think. I am a creature who forgets everything the moment it grows calm.

I know the plants that lean over my rim—the thick waxy ones, drinking from me through their deep roots. They take from me and I do not mind. I am here to be taken from. When enough of me is gone, the air gives me back. I am a debt that is always being repaid and always being incurred. There is peace in that. To be necessary and to be replenished. I would not trade it for legs.

And I know the breakdown. The flux.

The tall ones call it Aether, with reverence, with their rituals and their glowing orbs. But I am closer to it than they are. They only sense it from above, from inside their warm shifting bodies. I *hold* it. When the fabric of the place grows thin—when the ground considers opening, when the air decides it might be something other than air—it shows first in me. My surface goes wrong before anything else does. I reflect a sky that is not the sky above me. I show stars where there are no stars. Once, for the length of one waking, I held the image of a sea I have never been near, gray and enormous, with something vast moving beneath it, and I do not know if that sea was a memory or a prophecy or a wound in the world that simply happened to be visible in my small face.

The Seers come to me for this. I did not understand it at first. But I have learned: I am one of the instruments they read. They kneel and they look into me, not at their own reflections—they look *past* the surface, into whatever the flux is showing that day. My small body is a window the world keeps installing in itself, and they have learned to read the windows.

This is my purpose, then, the one I was not given but have found. I am a place where the world shows its seams.

---

I want to tell you about the Seer who comes most often.

She is old, even for them—her silver hair has gone the white of frost on the stone, and her eyes, when she leans over me, are nearly always silver now, rarely the deep blue. She does not perform the loud ceremonies at my edge. She comes alone, in the gray hours, and she crouches, and she breathes onto my surface so that I cloud over, and then she waits for me to clear.

I think she is lonely. I think I am the only thing she speaks to honestly.

She tells me things. She does not know I keep them—or perhaps she does, perhaps that is why she chooses me, a pool that forgets in calm but remembers in disturbance. She tells me she is afraid the Aether is not a gift. That the breakdowns are not the world breathing but the world *unraveling*, and that her people have spent their whole long history learning to admire the loosening of their own thread. She tells me her ancestors did not build the ruins. She tells me—and her voice goes very low, and the surface of me trembles with the warmth of the words—that her people *found* them, that they walked into a place already ending and called it home, and called the ending holy, because the alternative was to grieve.

I hold her words in my ripples. And then she falls silent, and grows calm, and I grow calm, and I forget them.

But I have written them here, in whatever this is, this single long waking, and so this once I will not forget. Let that be my gift back to her, the woman who breathes on my face. She gives me her fear and trusts me to lose it. This time I will keep it.

---

Sometimes I wonder how I will end. The plants might drink me down in a dry season and the air be too tired to return. Or one of the breakdowns might come not as an image on my surface but *through* it—a fissure opening directly beneath me, and I would pour out of this world into whatever sea I once reflected, and become part of something I cannot imagine.

I find I am not afraid of this, the way she is afraid. I am only a mirror that occasionally wakes. I have been replenished a thousand thousand times. I have learned the one thing this whole exhausted, beautiful, fraying landscape is trying to teach its people and cannot, because they are too proud and too lonely and too in love with mystery to hear it:

That to be drained and refilled is not the same as to be diminished.

That the calm in which I forget is not death. It is rest between meanings.

The wind is rising now. It is writing on me again—new script, new words I will feel and not read. The light is changing. Somewhere at the edge of my small body the old Seer is kneeling, leaning close, her silver eyes searching my face for the seam where the world shows through.

Look closer, I would tell her, if a pool could speak. The seam you are looking for is not in the sky I am reflecting.

It is in the fact that I reflect at all.

It is in the small, cold, patient wonder that you have spent your whole long life calling Aether, when all this time it was only water, only attention, only the world bending down to look at itself and being briefly, unbearably, awake.
