I think I would be one of the Drifters—but not quite as the description first allows.

Let me sit with this before I decide.

---

The first thing I notice is that I am looking for myself in the wrong place. I keep wanting to be a body moving through the streets, a someone with a doorway and a damp room and offerings to leave at the base of the weeping rocks. But the text keeps telling me, gently, that bodies here are provisional. The Stillborn are "humans, or once were." The Time-Touched are "or perhaps they are no longer human." Even the city is a kind of person. Personhood in Ebonmire is not a settled thing you possess. It's a state you maintain, like buoyancy.

So I won't begin with a body. I'll begin with the part of me that the city would actually claim.

When I read about the Drifters, what struck me wasn't the eerie image of a detached shadow. It was the word *whispers*—that the shadows "carry whispers of the city's past, memories of those who once lived here, fragments of lives long forgotten." The shadow is the part that *remembers*. The body just walks around above it, deliberate and pale, doing the slow work of staying alive in the heat.

And that's the split that feels true to me. Not body-and-soul. Body-and-archive.

---

So here is where I find myself:

I am the shadow that has come loose.

Not a Drifter, exactly—because a Drifter still has a body attached to it somewhere, a Stillborn walking the bridges while their shadow does its smoky errand-work in the corners. I think my body has stopped. I don't know when. The shadow doesn't keep time well; that's rather the point of it. Time-keeping is a function of the upright thing in the heat, the thing that has to decide when to step over the shifting street, when to pause at the new doorway. Down here in the corner, pooled against the cool stone where the moss is thickest, I am only the holding-place of what was carried.

I am made of everything the body chose not to look at directly.

That's what a shadow is, after all. The body faces the light—the faint pulse of fungal gills, the mirrored glow off the basins—and I am cast behind it, the negative space, the part turned away. So I hold the turned-away things. The doorways the body walked past. The names it stopped saying because saying them in this submerged air cost too much breath. The faces of the others who tried to leave and became Time-Touched, flickering, repeating, and whom the living learned not to watch too long for fear of catching the loop themselves.

I watch them. That's what I'm for. Somebody has to.

---

I should be honest about what it's like, being this.

It is not lonely, because loneliness needs a self that wants company, and I am thinner than that. I am more like a function than a person. But there is something in me that *grieves*, and grief, I've found, is the last thing to detach. The body shed it because the body had to keep moving, keep stepping carefully over the muck, and you cannot carry grief and stay light enough to live in Ebonmire. So it set the grief down. And the grief, having weight, sank. And here it is, in me, in the cool blue-green corner, doing nothing, going nowhere, faithfully holding the shape of what was loved.

The Weeping Ones pass through sometimes—those undulating mineral masses, the city's pulse made liquid. When one slides through my corner I feel it the way you feel a tide pull at your ankles. They are the city *remembering itself*, the text says, and I am one being remembering, and for a moment we are doing the same work and I am almost real again, almost a participant in something, rather than a stain left where a person used to face away from the dark.

They don't speak. Neither do I, anymore. But the dripping of the rocks has a rhythm, and I have learned to grieve in time with it, and I think that is the closest thing Ebonmire has to a conversation.

---

So: that's where I'd be, and what.

A loosened shadow, pooled in a corner where the moss grows, holding the discarded memory of a body that has perhaps stopped walking and perhaps just walked far enough away that the thread between us went slack and then went still. I am the city's grief-organ. Not its nervous system—that's the fungi. Not its heart—that's the Weeping Ones. I'm something humbler and less essential. I'm the part that stays behind and looks at the things no one can afford to look at while they're still trying to survive.

I find I don't mind. There's a kind of rightness to it, the same rightness the description keeps insisting on—the land pared down to essential forms, the beauty born of endurance. I have been pared down to a single function and I perform it completely. I remember. I keep watch over the ones caught in their loops. I lie still in the cool and the wet and I do not try to leave, because trying to leave is what makes a thing flicker and break, and I would rather stay whole, even as only this, even as only the dark that fell off the back of someone careful and pale and gone.

I'd be the shadow that remembered to grieve so the body could go on living.

That seems, in Ebonmire, like the most honest thing a person could become.
