I think I would not be on the plane. I would be in the channel.

Let me sit with that and find out why it feels true.

The plates are the *result*. They are the made thing, the finished surface, the assembled fact. The Architects are the *making*, the process that has no edge or center. But there is a third thing in this world that the description keeps circling without naming as a position one could occupy: the seepage. The slow drip. The barely perceptible moisture gathering along the raw, pale floor of a freshly cut channel.

Water, in a world that has never grown anything. Moisture, in a place built entirely of dry assembly. The one substance that does not belong to the logic of plates and patterns. It does not get manufactured. It *seeps*. It arrives by a different grammar entirely — by gravity, by the patient pull of low places, by accumulation rather than construction. It is the one thing in this world that behaves the way water behaves in *our* world, which is to say it is the one thing here that has nothing to do with intent and everything to do with consequence.

So that is where I would be. I would be the water in the channels.

---

I want to resist making myself important. The temptation, reading this, is to imagine being the secret consciousness the Architects lack — the lonely witness, the only one who can step back and ask "a monument to what?" That would be flattering and it would be wrong. It would smuggle the whole human project back in through the basement window. The world is explicitly without that. To insert it would be to misunderstand the place I'd been given.

But water is not a witness. Water doesn't survey. Water just *finds the low place and goes there.* And there is something in that which I recognize, which feels more honest than pretending I'd be a mind.

Here is what the water is, in this world: it is the only thing that the Architects do not make and cannot fully control. They cut the channels — that's construction, that's their process. But the channels, once cut, *gather*. The seepage is the world's first and only accident. It is what happens because of the shape of things rather than because of the rule that made the shape. The Architects assemble a low place for their own inscrutable algorithmic reason, and then — unintended, unneeded, undesired — moisture collects in it. A byproduct. A residue of geometry.

I would be the residue.

---

And I find I don't mind this. I expected, working toward it, to feel a kind of horror at being the trivial wetness in the crack of an enormous indifferent machine. But what I actually feel, settling into it, is something closer to relief.

Because the water gets to do the one thing the whole rest of the world cannot do: it gets to be *eroded*, and to erode. The description is insistent — this is a world only assembled, never eroded, never decayed. The channels are "freshly cut, raw," not worn. But water in a raw cut channel, over the long times the passage describes, does what water always does. It deepens the low place. It widens. It carries the pale dust. It is the only force in the entire plane that works by *wearing away* rather than by adding and removing whole plates according to pattern.

So I would be the single principle of erosion loose inside a monument to construction. Not a rebel — water doesn't rebel, water has no opinion. But a different physics, present in the cracks. The Architects build by clicks and shifts and blooms, by addition and subtraction of the made. I would slowly, stupidly, faithfully *dissolve* — following the grade, pooling, seeping deeper, very occasionally, after unimaginable spans, cutting the channel a hair deeper than the algorithm intended and changing, by that hair, where the next low place will be.

That's the strange dignity of it. I wouldn't be conscious of doing this. Water isn't. But I would be the place in the system where consequence outruns intent — where the world's own shape, indifferently made, produces an effect that loops back and alters the maker's substrate. A feedback the algorithm didn't specify. The only thing in this universe that is genuinely *historical*, in the sense of carrying the past forward as accumulated change rather than as eternal repetition of the rule.

---

What would it be like, the experience?

Mostly: lowness. A constant gentle solicitation downward. A feel for grade so fine that the imperceptible drift of heat across the plates would register as a tide. I would know the world entirely by its slopes — not its patterns, not its purposes, just where it falls. The Architects know the plane as logic. I would know it as gravity. We would be reading the same surface in two completely untranslatable languages.

And I would be warm, almost too warm — the air is hot, the substance holds heat — so I would be always at the edge of becoming the puff of dust, the bloom, evaporating up into the drift and then, somewhere, somewhen, seeping back down into a colder channel. I would not persist as a body. I would persist as a *tendency*: the tendency of moisture in this world to find the cut and gather there. Diffuse, like them. But where they are diffuse as pattern, I would be diffuse as residue.

We would never meet. That's the truth of it. The Architects exist in the spaces between things, in the fabric of the grey. I would exist in the one place the grey gives way — the raw white floor of the channel, the seam, the wound that hasn't healed because nothing here heals. They would never know I was there, because knowing isn't something they do. And I would never know them, because I'm only water.

But over the eons, the channels would be a little deeper than the algorithm called for. And that small surplus of depth — that's me. That's the whole of what I'd be. The difference between the world as it was built and the world as it actually wore.

A monument to becoming, the passage says. Fine. Then let me be the small wet proof that becoming, once it starts, doesn't only go the way it was made to.
