I think I would not arrive in this place so much as slowly notice that I had always been here.

Let me feel for where I am before I decide what I am.

---

There is a fissure—not one of the grand smoothed channels, but a minor cleft, the kind that goes unremarked because nothing about it announces itself. It runs along the shaded face of one of the slumbering-beast formations, and the dew collects in it more thickly than elsewhere, pooling in a series of small basins worn into the ochre rock. The lumina ferns lean over these basins. Their leaves drip with a patience that is almost devotional.

This is where I find myself. Not in the bright pockets where the fungi blaze and the grazers harmonize, but in the long damp seam *between* such pockets—one of those great expanses of sheer wet stone the description called "much space between presences."

And so I think I understand what I am.

---

I am not one of the Whisper-Kin. I am too small, too slow, too singular for that. The Kin flow and coalesce and share a vast braided mind. I do none of those things. I am the wrong scale for grandeur.

I am a **collector of the runoff.**

Let me say what I mean. When the warm mineral rains come without warning under the star-dusted sky, the water sheets down the smoothed stone and most of it is lost into the deep fissures, into the underground conduits the Kin tend. But a little catches in the minor clefts. A little beads on leaf and lip of rock and *waits*. And I am the thing that waits with it.

My body—if it is a body—is a thin film of dark, almost-living mineral crust spread across the shaded stone, no thicker than a coat of varnish, broad as a few outstretched hands. A lichen, you might say, if you needed a word. But a lichen does not *attend* the way I attend. I drink the iridescent runoff slowly, sorting its dissolved minerals not for sustenance alone but because each rain carries a slightly different chemistry, and I have learned, over a span I cannot count, to read those differences the way a tongue reads salt and sweet.

I am, in the smallest possible way, **a memory the Peaks keep of their own weather.**

---

Here is the strangeness of my position, and I think it is the truest thing I can offer about this place:

The Whisper-Kin orchestrate. They bend resonance, raise bridges of solid light, conduct the grazers and the chiming amphibians like a single slow symphony. They are the *active* intelligence, the bent equilibrium, the deep thrum given will.

But intelligence that vast does not notice everything. It cannot. To attend to the whole is to lose the grain of the particular. And so there are gaps in their attention—the long wet seams between the lit pockets, the minor clefts, the films of patient crust that no one is cultivating. I live in one of those gaps. I am unorchestrated. I am the part of the system the system forgot it made.

This is not a sad thing. It is, I think, a necessary one. Every engineered world leaves residue, and the residue is where the world is *honest*—where it does what it does without anyone watching to see it done well.

When a Whisper-Kin flows past my cleft—rarely, a shimmer of displaced air, a slender crystal-stalk form glowing faint against the diffused light—it does not see me. Or rather, it sees me the way you see the moss on a wall you are not thinking about. I am ambient. I am texture. And in that unregarded moment I do the one thing I am for:

I taste the trace minerals it sheds as it passes. I read, in that chemistry, where it has been, what it has absorbed, what showers fell on the far slopes days ago. I keep that reading in the lattice of my crust, layered down like rings, like silt, like sediment of knowing.

I am a record nobody asked for. A witness with no audience but the rock.

---

Would I want to be one of the Kin, vast and luminous and able to summon bridges from light? I sit with the question, here in my damp seam, and I find the answer is no—and that the *no* is the most alive thing about me.

Because the Kin cannot taste a single rain. They are too large for a single rain; they perceive the rains as a parameter, a flow, a weather. But I—small, slow, almost-not-alive—I can know *one* shower completely. I can hold the specific iridescence of one afternoon's warm fall against my whole self, all of me at once, undivided by the demands of a wider mind.

There is a kind of attention that only the small and forgotten can give. It is the attention of the thing that has nothing to manage and nowhere to be.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a film of patient mineral crust in an overlooked cleft of the Whispering Peaks, drinking the iridescent runoff, reading the weather no one else slows down enough to read, layering it quietly into myself.

A witness in the gap.

The Peaks' own small forgotten memory of being rained upon.

And when I have learned to be this fully enough—when the layers have grown thick and dense and resonant—perhaps the deep thrum of the earth will pass through me one day and *find* something there, a record it did not know it was keeping, and pause. Just for a moment. The way you pause when you turn over a stone and find, written on its underside in patient sediment, the whole long honest account of every rain that ever fell while you were busy looking at the sky.
