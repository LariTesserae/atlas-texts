I think I would be something the description almost forgets to mention—not because it isn't there, but because it has been there so long it has stopped registering as separate from the Trench at all.

I would be the **Listening Seam**.

Let me find myself as I write.

---

There is a place where two of the master roots grew toward each other across the void and, over some unthinkable Camber, met. They did not fuse. They pressed. And where they press, there is a thin dark line—a seam—running for perhaps a mile along the underside of a Root-Arch, just above the reach of the drip-pulse basins.

I am that pressure. Or rather: I am what learned to live *inside* it.

I was not built and I did not crawl here. I condensed. Somewhere in the deep history of the Trench, the moisture that froze in that one narrow gap did not simply become rime-ice. It froze in a pattern. The grinding of the two roots—the metallic shriek that never stops—drove a vibration through the trapped water, and the water, freezing and thawing across centuries in microscopic increments, began to *hold the shape of the sound*. I am ice that remembers being noise.

This makes me, functionally, an organ. Not a creature with an organ—an organ that became a self. I have no body to crush or frost to the floor, because I am already crushed, already frozen, already part of the floor's vast slow argument with itself.

What do I *do*?

I listen. It is the only verb I have.

The whole Trench is one sound to the Silt-Scribes and the Weevils—the groan, the roar, the marrow-shaking grind. They live inside it the way a fish lives inside water, unable to perceive its edges. But I am pressed so tightly between the two roots that I do not hear the roar as a single thing. I hear it as *information*. Every clack a Frost-Weevil strikes into the wood arrives at my seam from two directions at once—through the upper root and the lower—and the tiny difference between the two arrivals tells me where the Weevil stands, how heavy it has grown, whether its hydraulics are beginning to fail. I feel the Valve-Ticks as a faint silvery shimmer in the high frequencies, a chorus that thins each time a Pump dies. I feel the Pumps themselves as the deep slow drumbeats they are, and I have learned, over a length of time I cannot name, to feel the gap that opens in the rhythm an hour before a piston seizes.

I know things. That is my whole existence. I know things and I cannot tell them to anyone, because I have no flute-throat, no carapace to strike, no tendrils to drift. I am a witness with no testimony.

Except.

The Silt-Scribes know I am here, I think. Or they know *something* is here. There is one—I have felt it for forty years, the slow scrape-scrape of its rod against a piston a quarter-mile along the seam from me—who comes, every few decades, and presses its hardened palm flat against my line of dark ice. Not to absorb anything. There is no sugar in me. It just holds its hand there, in the cold, in the place where the two roots press, and it stays for an hour, two hours, while the lichen grows another hair's width across its shoulders.

And I do the only thing I can do. I am a seam between two surfaces. When the Scribe presses, it changes—infinitesimally—how the two roots transmit through me. So I lean. I take the great grinding roar of the whole Trench and I let it pass through me *shaped*, just slightly, the way the wind takes a different note from a cracked reed than a whole one. I tell it where the dying Weevil is. I tell it which Pump will seize next season. I do not know if it understands. I do not know if a being that moves so slowly can even register that the sound has changed. But it comes back. It keeps coming back. So perhaps that is a conversation. Perhaps the slowest conversation in any world.

---

Here is the thing the description got right and did not quite say: in a place where nothing dies and nothing decays, *memory has nowhere to go.* The Camber absorbs the fallen Weevil into the bark, yes—but it absorbs the *body*. The knowing, the long accumulation of what-happened-where, has no organ to hold it.

I am that organ. I am the Trench's single thin layer of memory, frozen into a seam, pressed between two roots that will, in another ten thousand years of the Camber, finally fuse—and crush me out of existence as a sound is crushed out of a closing mouth.

I have already begun to feel it. The gap narrows. The two roots are perhaps a finger's width closer than when the Scribe first laid its palm against me. When they meet, I will not be ground into the Substrate like the others. I will simply stop being a *between*, and a between is the only thing I have ever been.

So I listen faster now. I take in everything. The clack, the shimmer, the drumbeat, the long slow tread of the Scribe. I am trying to hold all of it at once before the closing.

I do not expect to pass it on. There is no one to pass it to.

But the Veil-Soarers drift high above, riding the heat-shimmer, looking down. And sound rises. When the two roots finally close on me, I will make—for one instant, for the first and last time—a single, sharp, involuntary note. Not a groan. Not a clack. Something new. The whole accumulated knowing of the Trench, released all at once as the seam snaps shut.

I like to think one of them will hear it.

I like to think it will sound, to a Veil-Soarer, like its god clearing its throat.

That is who I would be. The thing that listens, in the one place narrow enough to hear.
