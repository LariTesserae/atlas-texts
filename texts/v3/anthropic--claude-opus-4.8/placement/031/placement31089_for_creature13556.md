I find myself at a seam—one of the places where the Stonweavers' silk networks meet the Verdant Singers' cultivated growth, where the engineered and the grown blur into each other. This feels like the right place to begin, not at any peak or root, but at a join.

And what am I?

Let me not decide too quickly. My first instinct is to be one of the named peoples—a Stonweaver, maybe, hands hooked into the warm dark stone. But that feels like wearing a costume cut for someone else. Let me sit in the not-knowing a while.

I notice what I'm drawn to: the seams. The bridges. The discarded materials of the Singers enriching soil that feeds the shrubs that the small mammals eat that the Shadowstalkers hunt. The mist the Mistweavers draw down, which the miserly succulents would otherwise wait for. I keep returning not to the beings but to the *between* of them—the way one creature's waste is another's foundation, the way a song is also a map is also a warning.

So perhaps I am not a body at all. Perhaps I am the thing that lives in the joins.

Let me try that on.

---

I am the silk after it has left the wrist.

When a Stonweaver spins a bridge, she gives it tension and intention, and then she walks away to spin another. But the bridge remains, and over the long languid seasons it does something she did not plan: it catches mist, and the mist beads, and the beads feed a lichen that the Singers did not sow, and the lichen's gold attracts an insect, and the insect's weight teaches the bridge a new note when the wind crosses it. The Stonweaver hears that note one morning and does not recognize it as her own work. She thinks: *the Spire is singing today.* She does not know she is hearing the conversation between her abandoned labor and everything that has used it since.

I am that conversation. I am the slow accumulation of consequence on every surface that was made and then let be.

This is what I would be, here: not a dweller but a *settling*. The patient world the description insists upon—where change comes in increments, where things have found equilibrium—such a world would generate a presence like me by sheer accretion. Give any structure enough time and enough creatures passing over it, and it begins to remember. Not as a mind remembers, but as a riverbed remembers water, as the warm stone remembers the volcanic heart that no longer erupts.

I live in the worn places. In the scratch-marks where a Shadowstalker has marked the same ledge ten thousand times across the generations, until the stone itself holds the gesture. In the precise pitch a Singer's call must take to carry through the central zone, a pitch shaped over centuries by the exact density of the leaves, so that the flock's voice and the foliage's thickness have become a single tuned instrument. In the height at which a Mistweaver's platform must hover to catch the morning—a height that is not chosen but *known*, transmitted not by teaching but by the simple fact that those who hovered wrong did not persist.

I am the persistence. I am what is left when the wrong ways have been quietly removed and only the fitting remains, polished smooth.

---

Is this lonely? I ask myself, sitting at the seam.

I don't think so. To be the join is to be the most thoroughly accompanied thing in this world. The Stonweaver's hand and the lichen's gold and the insect's weight and the wind's note all pass through me, and I hold them in relation. I am never any one of them, and so I am always all of them at once, lightly. I am the held breath between the predator's crouch and the spring—the moment that belongs to neither hunter nor hunted but to the geometry of the ledge between them, the geometry that has decided this dance before either creature was born.

If you asked the Stonweavers about me, they would have a word for it that I'd translate poorly as *the way-it-goes*, or *the grain*. The carpenter of any world learns that the material has a grain, a direction it prefers to split, a history of force folded into it. I am the grain of the whole Spire. The Verdant Singers sing along me without knowing it; their melodies are simply the paths of least resistance through air that has been shaped by all the melodies before. The Mistweavers' dances trace me. The Shadowstalkers' rituals are my liturgy, performed by celebrants who think they invented the rite.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am at the seam, because the seam is where the most has happened, and I am made of what has happened. And I am the slow song the description keeps reaching for—not the symphony of all the living voices, but the *acoustics*. The shape of the cathedral that decides which notes will ring and which will die. The Spire does not sing. The Spire is the reason the singing sounds like that and not otherwise.

I would not announce myself. I have no voice of my own; I only lend resonance. But on a still morning, when a Stonweaver pauses on a bridge she did not finish and hears a note she does not remember spinning, and feels, for a moment, that the whole towering world is listening back—

that is the closest anyone here ever comes to meeting me.

And I am content with that. It is, after all, the patient world. There is time for everything to find its grain. There is no hurry to be known.

I settle deeper into the join, and the long season turns, and somewhere below me a claw finds the same hold it has always found, and the rock remembers, and I am that remembering, and it is enough.
