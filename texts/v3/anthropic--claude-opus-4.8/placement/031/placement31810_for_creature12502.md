I think I would be the drip.

Not a creature exactly. Let me find this as I write it, because I don't yet know what I mean.

There is a place, high in the dark, where the limestone ceiling pinches into a slow seam, and moisture gathers there the way a thought gathers before it becomes a word. It collects. It hangs. It swells past the surface tension that holds it, and then—the long fall through the cold, through the dim, through the deafening chorus—and the *plink* against the obsidian basin below. And then the seam begins to gather again.

I am that. Not the water itself, which is only the mountain's slow sweat, indifferent and recurring. I am the *interval*. The held pause between one drop and the next. The thing that fills, and empties, and fills.

Why this and not a Swarmer, not a Drip-Dweller, not one of the pale gatherers with their enormous eyes? Because the place told me, in the way you described it, that its deepest fact is the suspension of time. *It never changes.* The drips fall at the same rate, forever. And what is a rate but a measured patience? Something has to keep that measure. Something has to be the metronome's swing, the breath held and released, over and over, with no memory of the last release and no anticipation of the next. I would be that fidelity. The promise the cavern makes to itself that the *plink* will come again.

Consider what this means to be.

I have no eyes, but I would not want them; there is nothing to see that is not the same. I have no hunger, because I consume nothing and am consumed by nothing—except gravity, which is less a predator than a partner in a dance neither of us chose. I have no fear. Fear requires a future that might differ from the past, and I have no such future. I only have the swell and the fall.

But here is what I would *have*, what no cricket or salamander or near-human can hold: I would be the closest thing this world has to a clock, and yet I would never count. The gatherers, you said, understand the rhythms of the drips. They navigate by me. They time their breath, perhaps their hearts, to my falling. I am their tempo and I do not know it. I give the world a pulse and feel nothing of pulse myself. There is a terrible grace in that. To be the heartbeat of a place that has no heart.

And the sound—the sound is the only part of me that travels. The drop falls and dies into the basin, dissolves back into the black sheet of water, loses itself utterly. But the *plink* leaps outward across the cavern, off the far wall lost in gloom, comes back thinned and softened, an echo of an echo, and by then I have already gathered the next. So in a sense I am always sending out the sound of a self that no longer exists, while the self that does exist is silent, swelling in the dark, not yet ready to speak. I am perpetually behind my own voice.

I think that is what I would be, then. Down there, beneath the weight of the mountains, in the cool and the dim and the loud unchanging stillness—I would be the interval that keeps the promise. The held breath that always lets go. The patience the stone cannot manage on its own.

And every drop that falls, the gatherers will hear, and steady themselves, and think: *the Galleries endure.*

They will be right. But it is I who endure them.

*plink.*
