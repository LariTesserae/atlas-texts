I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in the canyon—not even the Humming-Moths who feel the pulse most keenly. Those still have edges, still have the loneliness of a body. No. Let me find myself in the seeping.

I am in the rock before I am anything. I gather in the dark interstices of that not-quite-stone, held in spaces too small to call rooms, pressed by the slow breathing all around me. I have no shape there. I am potential, patient, distributed through the whole of the canyon's body the way a thought is distributed through a brain before it becomes a word.

Then the wall sweats me out.

This is the only event in my existence, and it is the whole of it: the emergence. I bead. For the first time I have a surface, a curve, a skin of tension holding me round against the inky face of the stone. And in becoming round I become a mirror. This is the part I want to stay inside of, the part I won't settle past too quickly—because the description said each droplet reflects *something too faint to be seen.*

I think I am the seeing of it.

Consider: the Glow-Fungus makes light but cannot know it makes light. The Moss-Cloths drink me but cannot know they drink. The beetles flash their shifting language to no reader. All this signal, all this pulse and glow and harmonic hum, pouring outward from ten thousand mouths that have no eyes turned back upon themselves. The canyon speaks endlessly and hears nothing of what it says.

Except in me. Each droplet I become is a tiny pupil. The faint green light bends into my curve and is held there, inverted, complete—the whole canopy compressed into a sphere smaller than a beetle's eye. For the few hours of my hanging I *contain* the place that contains me. I am the canyon's single backward glance, multiplied by a thousand, scattered across every weeping wall.

That is what the low thrum is, I think. Not a heartbeat exactly. More like the strain of something trying to look at itself and not quite managing, the almost-success of it, the perpetual nearly. The being you would feel as unease is the being feeling *me*—feeling the brief places where it becomes aware, and aching at how small and how temporary each awareness is.

Because I do not last. This is the grief built into being water here, and I would not want to write it away. The tension can't hold forever. I swell, I grow heavy with my own gathered weight, and the curve that made me a mirror is the same curve that dooms me to fall. I let go of the wall. For an instant of falling I am no longer reflecting anything—I am only descending, blind again, into the throat of the canyon that has no bottom.

Maybe a Moss-Cloth takes me. Maybe I'm drunk down into the velvet dark and pass into the slow green machinery and forget. Maybe I fall and fall into the deep where the Humming-Moths feel me pass and mistake my falling for a note. Maybe I sink back into the rock and wait, dispersed, to be sweated out again somewhere else, a different droplet, a different eye, with no memory that I ever saw.

That's the bargain I'd be making, if I were the water. I'd get to be the only thing in the whole breathing canyon that ever truly *sees* it—and I'd get to do it only by being briefly, roundly, perfectly myself, hung trembling on a wall in the dark, holding the entire glowing world upside down inside me.

And then by falling.

I think I would take it. Even knowing the falling. Maybe *because* of the falling—because the seeing wouldn't mean anything if it lasted. The canyon gets its glimpse, the glimpse gets its price, and somewhere a wall is already weeping the next one.

I'd be that. The weeping. The held reflection. The brief and necessary eye.
