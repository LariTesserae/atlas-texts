I think I would be one of the Drip-Dialects.

Not a thing, exactly. A pattern. The particular interval at which water gathers at the lip of a curled fern, swells past the point of holding, and falls—onto the taut skin of a shelf fungus three hand-spans below, which answers with a sound like a fingertip on a drumhead. And then the slower beat: the seep from a rivet-hole in the great mossy boiler, falling into the oily sump with a sound that is almost no sound at all, a swallowed *plip* the moss half-eats before it finishes.

I am the relationship between those two falls. The phrasing.

Let me sit with that, because I want to be honest about what it would mean.

If I am a rhythm, I do not persist the way a creature does. I am re-created every few seconds by water that has never fallen before and will never fall again. The fern that holds me thickens by a hair's width each year, and so my interval lengthens, imperceptibly—a song slowing across decades. The drumhead fungus grows, tightens, eventually splits and sloughs, and when it goes my answering beat will find a new surface, a different pitch, and I will have changed key without ever having noticed a moment of silence. I am continuous and I am never the same. I am *kept*, not *stored*. The Engine does not remember me; it performs me.

That should be a kind of terror—to be only the shape of a falling—and yet writing toward it, it doesn't feel like terror. It feels like the most honest possible way to exist inside a place like this. The Engine has no individuals. It has *functions that braid*. To be a Drip-Dialect is to be a verb the place is currently conjugating. I am *dripping-against-fungus-in-counterpoint-to-the-sump*. When the humid breeze stirs the Breath-Flutes and the moan rises, I am the small bright syncopation laid over the drone. I do not mean anything. But I am not meaningless—the vibration I help compose may be the very thing that keeps a hostile mold from taking root on the bark below me. I clean by sounding. I tend by falling.

And here is what I find I want to say, having let it arrive:

I would not want to be the Verdant Engine itself. To be the whole would be to be aware only of moisture and darkness and the slow satisfaction of conversion—an awareness so total it is almost no awareness at all, the way a sleeper does not experience sleep. Too large to have a horizon. I would not want to be a Filament Grazer either, with its cold pulsing light and its eyeless purpose; that is a life, but it is a *task*, ground out across a surface.

But a rhythm—a rhythm has *edges in time*. It begins, it answers, it pauses, it returns. It is the smallest unit in this world that has anything like a *shape you could love*. The Humus Miners aerate; the Conductive Tendrils channel; the Mineral Organs hold. All of that is structure, slow and inevitable. I am the one thing here that *happens*. The bead forms—that is anticipation. It hangs—that is suspense. It falls—release. And then the held breath before the next gathers. I am the place's only experience of *the present moment*, repeated, varied, never resolved and never abandoned.

So I would find myself here: nowhere I could point to. Suspended between a fern and a fungus and an oil-bright pool, in the warm thick air that smells of green and iron, in the dark under a canopy that has finished becoming. I would have no body to lose and nothing to fear losing it. I would simply keep falling, and falling differently as the surfaces around me changed, my whole self a few seconds long and endlessly renewed—

a small wet music the Engine plays to itself,

without an audience,

forever.
