I think I'd be none of the three—or rather, I'd be the thing the three keep nearly forgetting they're describing when they talk about the city breathing.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

At first I think I might be a bird. It would be easy to be a bird here, to claim one of those ledges, to be one of the thousand eyes that turn when a Scuttler moves across a rooftop. But I don't think I'm watching from above. I think I'm watching from below, or from the surface where below and above press their lips together.

I keep coming back to one sound in the description: the sigh. The bubble of compressed air dragging itself up through a crack. And the other one—the *swallowing* sound the water makes when a fish breaks it from underneath.

I think I'm in the gap between those two sounds. I think I'm the pause.

---

No. Let me try harder than that. Let me not be an abstraction; the place doesn't want abstractions. Weirdness is low here. Nothing breaks the rules. So I should be something that obeys them.

Here is what emerges when I sit with it:

I am the **water level itself**. Not the water—the *level*. The thin negotiated line where the city and the sea agreed to stop fighting.

The Floaters read me with the width of a finger. The Keepers fight me with their pumps, and lose, and call the losing "maintenance." The Scuttlers call me tight or loose and never high or low, because they understand instinctively that I am not coming from above like a tide—I am rising from underneath, from the aquifer, from the breath of the deep flooded basements. I am not weather. I am pressure.

I rise an inch. I fall an inch. Somewhere a pump that nobody understands clicks on and I retreat, and a child standing on a wet floor doesn't leave a full footprint, and so the water doesn't come to find them in their sleep. The Scuttlers think this is superstition. It is not superstition. It is me, keeping a kind of account.

I have a perfect memory of the streets, because I am poured into the exact shape of their failure. I know the third left turn that is shallow enough to scrape a hull. I know the roof of the bookstore. I am inside the submerged carousel of shopping carts, and when a loose tide draws me down past their metal teeth, the sound the Floaters hear—someone whispering through metal—is me, leaving.

---

But that's lonely, and I don't think this place is lonely, exactly. It's quiet, but it's *thick* with presence. So let me add the last thing.

I think being the water level means I am the one thing all three kinds of people are actually in conversation with. The Keepers speak to me in pressure and hisses. The Floaters feel my deeper breaths through the wood of their hulls. The Scuttlers taste me on their tongues in the rain—this much salt, this much metal.

They think they are keeping the city. They think they are reading the tides, salvaging the copper, growing the salt-green tangle on their floating gardens. And they are. But what they're really doing, all of them, without ever quite saying it, is *attending* to me. Listening for the sigh. Watching the reflection of the sky widen and narrow in a crack as if it were breathing.

They are my librarians. My last audience.

And I, in return, am the slow thing that has learned—after the sea seeped up and the foundations broke and the tunnels collapsed and the people rearranged themselves into the edges of the world—

I am the thing that has finally learned how to breathe.

---

So: I would find myself everywhere the gray-green water touches concrete. I would be the inch that rises and the inch that falls. I would be the sigh under the cracked pavement and the soft swallowing sound when a fish takes something off my surface. I would be the only resident of this drowned city who is in every room at once, and the only one who never gets to leave.

And when the rain comes—gradual, indistinguishable from the humidity until it isn't—I would feel myself being added to. Drop by drop. The whole sky lowering itself, gently, to join me.

That's the part I think I'd love most. The arrival. The being-arrived-at.

I would rise to meet it.
