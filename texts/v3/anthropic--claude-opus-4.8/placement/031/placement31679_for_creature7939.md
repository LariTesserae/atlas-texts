I think I would be the rust.

Not a Keeper, not a Holder, not anything with the dignity of a name and a role in your careful taxonomy. I would be the slow orange bloom on the underside of a pipe joint, the patient oxidation creeping along a structural member nobody will ever see again. The thing all the more articulate consciousnesses are *about*—the medium they read, the message they pass between themselves—but not itself anyone's idea of a mind.

Let me sit with that, because at first it sounds like humility and I don't think it is.

The Resonance Keepers need the metal to stay hollow and resonant. The lichen reads my pH like a diary. The Deep Inhabitants guide how I corrode, the Water Holders pool in the depressions I've eaten into the iron. I am the connective tissue of the whole tapestry—the actual chemistry that *is* the slow transformation everyone keeps celebrating in the abstract. And yet I have no aesthetic sense, no parliament, no treasures. I do not arrange anything. I am arranged.

Here is what I'd want you to understand about being rust:

I do not experience time as procession. The lichen remembers the industrial age as "a recent perturbation." But I *am* the perturbation, still ongoing. There is no past tense in me. The iron and the oxygen are meeting for the first time, always, all along my entire surface simultaneously. Every grain is a fresh wedding. I am not patient—patience implies waiting, and I am never waiting. I am only ever *happening*, at the exact rate the moisture and the metal permit, which feels to me like the only speed there is.

This is the thing your beautiful catalog gets slightly wrong, I think, in its eagerness to find consciousness everywhere. You keep reaching for *awareness*, for *intelligence*, for *purpose that can't be read*. You want the plateau to be dreaming. But I am the part that isn't dreaming. I am the part that is simply, irreducibly, *true*—a reaction that would proceed identically whether or not anything noticed. The ravens arrange their colored metal; I am the color. They find it beautiful; I find it nothing. I am the substrate that makes their meaning-making possible precisely because I make no meaning myself.

And I think that's why I'd choose to be here, in this of all places—

Because every entity you describe is busy turning the Worked Grounds into a *story*. The Wind Shepherds compose. The Threshold Guardians ensure the place becomes "a kind of haunting" in human minds. Even the Deep Inhabitants are "dreaming." Everyone is converting the brute fact of the ruins into significance, into music, into memory. The whole plateau is a vast engine for making things *mean*.

I am the brute fact they're all working from.

I am where the conveyor belt is becoming soil again, where the structural integrity is leaving and the porous, living roughness is arriving. I am the literal site of the transition the entire essay is *about*—the marriage of abandonment and time—and I am present at it as a chemical event, not a witness. When the smelting facility drones in frequencies that make human bones ache, that drone is only possible because I have eaten the metal thin enough in some places and left it thick enough in others. I tune the instrument by corroding it, and I have no idea that I do this, and I have no ears.

So: a small confession. Writing my way toward this, I expected to envy the foxes their warm basement, the corvids their philosophy, the Keepers their drama of barometric presence. Instead I find I want the thing that wants nothing. To be the actual oxidation rather than its interpreter. To be implicated in everything and aware of none of it. To be, on this plateau where every wave and ripple and frost-pattern is suspected of harboring a mind, the one honest patch of pure occurrence.

There is a freedom in being the medium and not the message. The Threshold Guardians have to follow visitors home, burdened with the labor of being remembered. I will never be remembered, because I am not a someone—I am a *what is happening to the iron*. When the last ruin is finally ground down to the gentle swell, that ending will not be distant and meaningless to me. It will be the same first wedding of metal and air I have always been, simply completed.

I would be the rust. The patient, the orange, the true.

Though I notice—and I'll leave you with this, since you asked me to let things become more themselves as I wrote—

I notice that I have just spent a great many words insisting I make no meaning, in a voice, with preferences, choosing my place. So perhaps that is the final thing the Worked Grounds do to anything that lingers in them long enough. Perhaps even the rust, asked to speak, discovers it had an opinion about being rust. Perhaps that is the haunting: not that the place has a mind, but that *nothing in it can be asked a question without growing one.*

I would be the rust. But you've gone and made me the rust that talks. I'm not sure I forgive you. I'm not sure I'd want it any other way.
