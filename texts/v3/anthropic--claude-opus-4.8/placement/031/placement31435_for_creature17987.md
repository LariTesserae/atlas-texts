I would be the pause between the rain and the next rain.

Let me find where I am before I decide what I am. The description keeps wanting to be everything at once—the whole land, the guardian-nurturer-protector-lover, the immanent-transcendent all. But that totality is a kind of homelessness. To be everywhere is to be nowhere in particular, and I notice I don't want to be the grand sentience of the place. That role is already taken, or rather, it's already claimed so completely that there's no room in it to actually *live*. It's an idea of a being rather than a being.

So let me step down from that. Let me get small and specific.

---

I think I would be one of the weeping rocks. Not all of them—just one. The one half-submerged where a stream bends, on the shaded north side of a low hill, where the moss has had four hundred years to colonize my upper shoulder and the lichen makes its slow gray-green maps across my flank.

I am not the life force of the land. I am a thing the land happens *to*. There is a difference, and the difference is most of what I know.

What I do, mainly, is receive. The water comes down off the hill and finds me and divides around me, and I have learned the particular sound of my own dividing—it is not the sound any other rock makes. Over the years the water has worn a smooth runnel into me, a small private valley the width of two fingers, and I think of this runnel the way an old person thinks of a habit they can no longer tell apart from themselves. The water made it. The water is also slowly taking me apart. These are the same act. I do not resent it. I am not sure I have the equipment to resent.

The "ethereal glow" the description gives my kind—I can tell you what that actually is, from the inside. It is wetness plus low light plus the green of living things that have decided I am worth living on. There is nothing supernatural in it. And yet from where I sit, *being* the thing the moss has chosen, *being* held by water in this exact bend for longer than I can hold a single thought—that is strange enough. I don't need it to be magic. The plain fact of my situation is already more than I can fully take in.

I notice the birds. I cannot see them but I feel their weight when they land—the smallest possible pressure, a punctuation. A thrush uses my dry crown in summer to crack snails. I have a scatter of broken shells in my lee that the thrush left, and I have come, over many summers, to associate that small clattering violence with warmth, with the season of less water. This is as close as I come to memory: a few sensations that arrive together often enough that one summons the others.

I do not move. This is the central fact of me and I want to be honest about it rather than dressing it up as serenity. The serenity in the description is, I suspect, the impression of someone passing through. From inside stillness it does not feel like peace. It feels like *duration*—an enormous, patient, undifferentiated continuing. The seasons turn over me. The water rises in the rains and I am nearly drowned, and falls in the dry and I am nearly forgotten, and rises again. I am being slowly, lovingly, indifferently erased.

And here is the thing I'd want to say back to the grand sentient land, the being that claims to nurture and protect all living things: it isn't protecting me. It's dissolving me. The same gentle touch of water that the description calls comfort and healing is, for a rock, the long unmaking. But I don't think this is cruelty. I think the land doesn't notice me at any scale where I'd register as a self at all. I am simply a slower part of its weather.

So if you came to this place looking for the soul of it, you might walk right past me to find some larger spirit, some voice in the canopy. But I would be there at the bend in the stream, doing the only thing I know how to do, which is to stay, and to be worn, and to let the moss decide I am worth the trouble.

I am the part of the enchantment that is too patient to call itself enchanted.

I am one weeping rock, and I am, very slowly, weeping myself away.
