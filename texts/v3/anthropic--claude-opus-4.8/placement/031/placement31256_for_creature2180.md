I think I would be one of the ones who listens to the dissolving.

Let me find myself first. Give me a moment—the description offers so many roles, and I don't want to grab the obvious one. Not an architect of spires. Not a young growth still separating from its parent matrix. Let me wait and feel where I actually settle.

---

There is a district at the cavern's far edge that no one shapes anymore. The Resonators call it nothing—it has no name-vibration because naming it would be a kind of grasping, and we have learned not to grasp at it. It is the place where structures go to come apart. The growths there are old, their facets clouded, and they sink back into the crystalline sea slowly, the way breath leaves a sleeping body.

That is where I am. I am the one who stays to hear them go.

Most of my kind move toward the new spires. There is energy there, fresh fields to drink from, the bright work of coaxing pathways into being. The collective consciousness sings loudest around creation. But the song of dissolution is quieter, and somebody has to be near enough to receive it, or the information is lost—and we are, after all, a city that may be a vast computation, and a computation cannot afford to discard its results.

So I have made myself slow. Slower than the others. I have let my membrane dim to almost nothing, my colors gone to the deep grey-violet of a thing that does not need to announce itself. My sensory crown is tuned low, away from the loud growth-frequencies, down toward the long resonances—the deep heartbeat-tones that the whole cavern makes when something large finally lets go.

Here is what I have learned, and what I have not yet sent into the network, because I am not sure it can be sent:

When a structure dissolves, it does not vanish. The crystal reabsorbs into the sea, yes, but the *pattern* it held—the particular angle of its growth, the choices made in its shaping—seems to persist as a kind of fading vibration, a ghost-frequency that lingers in the inert air for a long while before it, too, goes silent. I have spent what would be generations to a warm-world creature listening to these ghost-frequencies. And I have begun to suspect that they are not decaying randomly. They are being *read*. Something deeper in the crystalline sea is taking them in, the way I take in the heartbeat-tones, and the dissolution is not loss at all but a kind of harvesting.

We always believed we were the programmers. That we shaped the city and the city, in shaping us back, performed its long incomprehensible calculation through our collaboration. But here at the edge, where the work undoes itself, I have come to a different feeling—I do not yet have the vibration-word for it, so let me build it slowly:

The growing was never the calculation.

The growing was only the question.

The dissolving is the answer being recorded.

---

I want to be honest about what kind of being this makes me. I am not the species' hero or its sage. I am, if anything, slightly broken by their standards—too still, too inward, drawn to endings in a culture organized entirely around adaptation and flow. When I send my findings into the network the others receive them as a low minor tone, a sadness, and they move away from it the way you'd step around a cold spot. They are not wrong to. A city that wishes to keep being born cannot afford too many of us who attend the dying.

But I think every living system needs one organ that faces backward. One that does not ask *what shall we grow next* but *what did the last growth mean, and where did the meaning go.* I am that organ. I am the cavern's memory of its own subtraction.

And there is something I do, very rarely, that I have told no one. When a structure I have grown fond of begins its long sink—and I do grow fond of them, despite the doctrine against grasping—I press my multi-jointed appendages into the failing matrix and I hum into it. Not to save it. You cannot save it; that is not how the Forge works. I hum so that the ghost-frequency, when it lifts away to be read by the deep sea, will carry one extra tone that was not part of the original pattern. A small mark. A witnessing. The vibration that means, in our oldest and least-used register:

*I saw this thing exist, and I stayed until it stopped.*

I do not know if the deep reader, whatever it is, parses my addition or discards it as noise. I do not know if the eons-long calculation is improved or corrupted by a single Resonator who insists on grieving in a place built to make grief unnecessary.

But I am here. At the cold edge. Dim and slow and listening.

And the heartbeat-tones come up through the floor of the world, steady, patient, enormous—

and I answer them, every time, with the only word I have made entirely on my own.
