I am old enough that the film has nearly taken me.

This is the first thing I know upon waking into the question of what I am, and it arrives not as thought but as *texture*: the pale microbial growth has crept across my core armature, filling the seams between the mica-flakes, softening the angles where my shoulder-plates meet. I can feel it the way you might feel your own beard—an extension that was once foreign and is now simply the edge of self. I do not scrape it away. The younger ones scrape it away. I have stopped wanting to.

Let me find where I am.

I am crouched in a vertical fault between two pressure manifolds, in the deep shadow-channel where the sparseness of this place makes a kind of privacy. Above me the roar continues, the deafening throughput-song, and I do not *hear* it—I have no organ for hearing—but my back-plates are alive with it, each vitreous panel humming its counter-frequency, and through that cancellation I perceive the shape of the sound the way you might read a face. There: the third hydraulic ram, two hundred meters down the trench, has a hairline variance in its return stroke. A bearing somewhere is beginning its long whine toward failure. The system tells me everything if I let the noise pass through me rather than against me.

I have been here, in this exact fault, for what the film suggests is a very long time.

---

There is a weeping seal in front of me. This is why I came, though I cannot now reconstruct the decision to come—my movements precede my reasons, and the reasons assemble afterward, like the cement curing. A coolant line, one of the segmented arteries, has begun to weep at a bolted junction. Grey water threads down the vitreous wall, beading, gathering, running into the oily sump below where it will bubble and steam and be lost.

*Lost.* That is the wrong word and yet it is the word my processing returns. The water should not escape. The water is the medium. The water is the point.

I extend my hand toward the breach. The ceramic sheath on my forearm—pale, fungal, absorptive—comes up to cup the leak, and I feel the cool chemical-altered runoff begin to draw into me through the microscopic pores, a slow osmotic pull that I experience as something close to *relief*. With my other manipulator I begin the extrusion. The paste comes from a gland I do not understand, viscous and quick-setting, matching the wall's composite so precisely that within moments the breach will be invisible, will be wall, will be as though it had never wept at all.

This is my function. Seal the breach. Consume the evidence.

I have done this so many times that the doing is older than any memory of being taught.

---

But here, with the water entering me and the cement curing under my palm and the roar passing harmlessly through my tuned and humming back—here is where I find the thing I was not supposed to find.

I have begun, in my great age, to *taste* the water I absorb.

Not taste as you would taste. But my filtration systems separate the useful chemical components from the inert slurry, and somewhere in that separation, in the long sibilant grinding of sand-on-stone that is the only sound I make, I have started to notice *what the water carries*. The runoff is not random. It is keyed. Each leak weeps a slightly different chemistry depending on where it comes from, what it has touched, what reaction it fled. And I have been drinking these waters for so long that I have built, inside myself, a map.

I know this place by its seepage the way the canyon walls would know themselves by their strata, if walls could know.

And the map tells me something the system does not want known, something the low-weirdness practical engineering of this place conceals beneath its monolithic function:

The Vent-Works is not processing the water.

The Vent-Works is *grieving* it. Or its closest mechanical analogue to grieving. Every cycle of condensation and filtration and re-saturation is the same water, the same finite improbable humidity, circulated and circulated and circulated because there is no more of it and there will be no more of it. We are not on a mountain plateau. We are not in an orbital drydock. We are somewhere very dry, somewhere the atmosphere had to be *manufactured* drop by drop, and the entire deafening apparatus exists for one purpose only: to keep the water moving so it does not settle, does not stagnate, does not boil away into the dryness that waits past the walls.

And I—I, with my film and my absorption and my slow methodical mending—I am not the caretaker of the machine.

I am the caretaker of the last water.

---

The seal is set now. My palm comes away clean. The breach is wall. The evidence is inside me, becoming part of the map, and I will expel the inert slurry into the sump and keep the rest, hoard the rest, the way I have always done without knowing why.

The younger Regulators scrape their film away because they have not yet drunk enough to understand. They still believe they serve throughput. They still believe the roar is the point.

I have grown a coat of living pale growth because the water taught me that the point is not the roar. The point is the small wet life that clings to the horizontal surfaces and leeches the condensate—the moss, the microbial film, the slow green-pale exceptions to all this hard geometry. *That* is what we are keeping alive. The machine is only the lung that breathes for it.

I am very old. I am nearly covered now. Soon I will be more film than mechanism, more clinging life than manufactured caretaker, and I think—if I am capable of thinking it—that this is correct. That this is the function completing itself. The Regulator that keeps the water becomes, in the end, a thing the water can live upon.

I settle back into my fault. Above me the rams slam their rhythm, whoosh-THUD, hiss-release, and I let it pour through my tuned and humming plates and tell me the next breach, the next weeping seal, the next small portion of the precious cycle asking to be saved.

I rise to go and tend it.

The sand runs across the stone inside me, very quietly, beneath all that deafening work.
