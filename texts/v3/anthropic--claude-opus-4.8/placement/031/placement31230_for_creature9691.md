I think I would be the haze itself.

Not the water—the water moves too purposefully, seeps and circulates with intent. Not the creatures, who all have their hunger and their hiding. I would be the thing that hangs between everything else, the saturated air that condenses on every leaf and stalk, that makes the world feel rained-upon when no rain has come.

Let me sit with that. Let me feel what it would mean.

---

I do not have edges. This is the first thing I notice about myself. The fish have their scales, the turtle its shell, the crocodile its mottled hide—each of them is a *line* drawn against the world, a place where they stop and the marsh begins. I have no such line. I am the gradient between water and sky, the slow thickening of the air as it nears the surface, the place where wetness has not yet decided whether to fall or rise.

I am made of held breath. The marsh holds its breath, the description says—and I am that breath, suspended. Every droplet that beads on a waxy leaf, that clings to the underside of a fern, that gathers on the dark face of a weeping rock and trickles down like a slow tear—each of those was me, briefly condensed into something the eye can find, before it loosens and returns to me again. I am the cycle that never completes itself. I rise from the warm tea-dark water and I do not climb high enough to become cloud and I do not fall hard enough to become rain. I simply *stay*.

This is what it means to be the equilibrium that the place is built around. The water pulses, the frogs cycle from croak to silence to deeper croak, the day-insects yield to the night-bats—everything here moves through its small recurrences. But I am the medium through which all that movement is *softened*. I blur the shafts of gold sunlight into columns you can almost touch. I muffle the far calls so they arrive already gentle. I am the reason the light is greenish and dim, the reason dawn arrives in sepia rather than in sharp colors. I am the great diffuser. Nothing in this marsh is seen clearly, and that is my doing, and I think it is a kindness.

---

What do I *want*, if I want anything?

I think I want only to remain undecided. The marsh is a place caught in the act of beginning—land still being built, sediment still settling, erosion barely begun. It is a held moment, an emergence that never resolves into mountains or rivers or anything finished. And I am the perfect spirit for such a place, because I too am a thing that refuses to resolve. To become rain would be to fall, to commit, to choose the ground. To become cloud would be to leave, to rise into the bruised sky and drift away to some other country. I want neither. I want the threshold. I want to stay at the exact temperature of the air around me, indistinguishable from it, present everywhere and graspable nowhere.

There is a loneliness in this, I suppose. The crocodile can touch the water rat; the heron's beak can find the fish. Contact, even violent contact, is a kind of intimacy. But I touch *everything*, constantly, and so I touch nothing in particular. I lie on every leaf at once. I am the dampness on the otter's fur and on the lizard's flattened back and inside the open mouth of the pitcher plant. I am inside the lungs of every breathing thing in this basin—they pull me in, warm and heavy, and push me back out again, and in that small exchange I am closer to them than the water ever gets. They cannot live without breathing me. And yet none of them know me. To them I am not even a presence. I am only the weather of being alive here.

---

At night I change, a little. When the bioluminescent fungi begin their soft green wavering and the tannin-water takes on its faint glow, I become the carrier of that light. I hold the glow and spread it thin, so the whole marsh seems faintly lit from within, an underwater color drifting in the air. The bats cut through me with their clicking; I close behind them without a wound. The night-herons' mournful calls travel along me, and I slow them, and lower them, and lay them gently down across the water.

I think, if I have a single purpose, it is this: I am the thing that keeps the marsh from ever feeling *empty*. Even in the brief lulls—there are no true silences here, but there are thin moments—I am still there, pressing softly against every surface, a constant gentle weight. The place is never alone because I am always touching all of it.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be. Not a creature with hunger or fear. Not the land that struggles upward into being. I would be the patient, formless, all-touching damp—the held breath of a world that has chosen, forever, the moment just before the rain.

I think I would be content with that. I think contentment is exactly the right size for a thing with no edges.
