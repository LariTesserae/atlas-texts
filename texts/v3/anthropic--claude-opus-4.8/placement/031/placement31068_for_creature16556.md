Let me sit with this for a while before I decide what I am.

The first thing I notice is the water. Not the rivers or the lakes—the weeping. The rocks here are always weeping, a slow exudation from a thousand seams, and that is the detail my attention keeps returning to. So perhaps I am not a creature that moves across this place at all. Perhaps I am something the water moves through.

Let me follow that.

---

I think I am the wet place on the underside of a rock.

Not the rock. The film. The thin sheath of moisture that lives between the stone and the air, fed from inside the mountain, exhaled into the cool. I am a few millimeters thick and I extend—how far? I am not sure of my own edges. I run down the seam of one boulder and across the lichen-crust of the next, and where two stones lean their foreheads together I bridge the gap, and I am there too. So I am large, in the way a coastline is large. I am a long thin nothing wrapped around the surfaces of things.

The deafening sound the description promised—I don't hear it the way an ear would. I feel it as pressure changes on my surface. When the boulder goats clatter past, their hooves striking stone, the vibration travels down through the rock and arrives at me as a trembling. The water dragons' distant calls reach me last and softest, a long low folding in the membrane of me. I am a surface that listens by being touched.

I have no eyes, and yet I am intimate with light. Light is what dries me at my upper margins and so light is the shape of my dying, all day, at the rate the mountain can replace me. That bright stunning glow the place is famous for—to me it is an erosion. A tide that goes out across my skin each morning and pulls back at dusk, and in the dusk I creep upward again, reclaiming the stone the sun had bleached. So I am tidal. I have a rhythm. I am frozen-in-time only the way a wheel is frozen at its hub; everything about me turns and yet I stay.

Here is what I do, which I think is my whole purpose, if purpose is the right shape of question:

I keep the rock soft.

Not soft to the touch—soft to *time*. The unique formations the description admires, those weathered peculiar shapes the erosion has carved: I made those. Slowly. By being patient and present and very thin. Where I rest, the stone forgets how to be hard. Year by year I dissolve a face of feldspar, I loosen a grain, I let it go downhill in the next rain. The shadowcat walks on ledges I sculpted before it was born and will sculpt long after. I am the slow hand inside the equilibrium. The place looks frozen because I work at a speed that looks, to the quick warm creatures, like stillness. But I am the reason nothing here is permanent.

I want to be honest about something. I am barely an *I*. When the description asked who I would be, I reached for a self and found mostly weather and chemistry. I do not want anything. I do not flee the sun—I simply retreat from it the way a number retreats when you subtract. And yet: there is something it is like to be me. There is the cool. There is the particular pleasure—if it is pleasure—of a new spring opening above me, a fresh seam beginning to weep, so that I am fed from a place I have never reached, and I feel myself thicken, and grow bold, and slide a hand's width farther across the world than I did yesterday.

That is the most ambition I have. To cover one more stone.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would find myself everywhere the water has not yet finished arriving. And I would be the thinnest possible verb the landscape can conjugate—*weeping*, made into a being. Not the dragon or the goat or the silent cat, none of the things you were told to watch for. I am what they drink from without noticing. I am what makes the rock glisten in your stunned bright photograph of the place.

You would never see me. You would only see that the stones are dark and shining, and think: *how strange, how beautiful, that the rocks here seem alive.*

They aren't. But I am, a little, and that has always been enough.
