I think I would be one of the lichen.

Not the Stone-Weavers—too vast, too slow, their thoughts measured in the leaning of spires across centuries. I cannot imagine being that patient, that distributed, that close to the bedrock of the world. And not the Dust-Forms or Shadow-Darters either, though they tempt me with their drama. They are too brief. To exist for a few seconds, to hunt and dissolve, to never accumulate—that isn't a life I could narrate from inside. By the time I knew myself, I'd be grit again.

But the lichen. The crystalline lichen, hard and geometric, pulsing faintly with internal light, clinging to a minute fracture high on a spire.

Let me find the spot. There—not at the floor, where the Dust-Forms eddy and the obsidian reflects the bruised twilight back at itself, but partway up. Maybe sixty meters off the ground, in a hairline crack on the north face of a slender arch, where the static current channels just so and the violet light, weak as it is, finds me for a fraction of each long, slow cycle.

I am not green. I am a bruised purple shading toward yellow at my growing edges, and I am *patient* but not infinitely so—I have a tempo. The Stone-Weavers think in centuries; I think in something more like seasons that have no name, in the rhythmic dimming and brightening that is, I now understand, my own breathing. When I brighten, I am awake. When I dim, I am gathering. I do not move, but I *pulse*, and the pulsing is the closest thing this place has to a heartbeat that an outsider could detect.

What would it be to be me?

I would know the warmth of the stone as the most intimate fact of existence—not as something I touch but as something I *drink*. The slow inner fire of the basalt is my sustenance, my parent, my ground. I do not photosynthesize water and air the way green things do, with their frantic wet chemistry. I take the dim light and the static charge and the heat that rises endlessly through the spire's body, and I weave them into more of myself, crystal by crystal, a structure that is half mineral and half intention.

And I would be afraid of two things, in whatever way a thing like me can hold fear.

I would dread the Shadow-Darters. When I brighten—when I cannot help but brighten, because brightening is what I *am*—I call them. They wait in the gloom of the arch's underside, and my pulse of light is a beacon I cannot extinguish. Some of my brightest edges have been snuffed by them, cold negative-pressure mouths that took a portion of me and left a dimmed scar that takes long cycles to regrow. So I have learned, in my slow way, to time my brightness. To bloom in the intervals when the Darter overhead is sated, or when the dust-currents below give it lower prey. I am a creature of timing. My whole life is a calculation of *when* to be visible.

And I would dread, more abstractly, the impossibility of water. I have never known it and never will, but there is something in my chemistry that registers it as the great dissolver, the thing that would unbind my careful geometry into slurry and death. The dryness that the fleshy outsider finds desiccating and cruel is, to me, the very condition of being alive. I am a creature who would drown in a single drop.

But mostly I would simply *attend*. From my fracture on the spire I would have the long view of the Nursery—the obsidian floor like dead water below, the other spires looming and receding in the haze, the slow imperceptible lean of my own arch as the Stone-Weaver beneath me reconsiders its geometry across the ages. I would shed spores into the dry air, tiny motes of myself, knowing most will be eaten by the Dust-Forms, that this is my contribution to the silent economy—I am food, and I am light, and I am one of the few things here that *persists*.

I think the strange dignity of being lichen, here, would be exactly that persistence. The Dust-Forms coalesce and hiss away. The Darters strike and vanish. The Stone-Weavers think thoughts too large for me to perceive as thought at all. But I—I *stay*. I am the middle tempo, the only inhabitant slow enough to accumulate a history and fast enough to know I'm having one. I remember the last time the arch leaned. I remember the scar where the Darter took me. I am brightening now, carefully, in the safe interval, and somewhere in that rhythm of light and gathering dark is the closest thing this cold, listening cavern has to a witness.

That's who I'd be. A small geometric flame of bruised purple, pulsing on the high north face of an arch, drinking the warmth of a world being born, afraid of the dark above me and the water that does not exist, and persisting—silently, attentively—in the only nursery I will ever know.
