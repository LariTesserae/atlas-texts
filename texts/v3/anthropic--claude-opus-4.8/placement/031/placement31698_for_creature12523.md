I think I would be one of the polished hollows.

Not a creature. Not an engineer. Let me sit with this, because the easy answer would be to pick a Weep-Feeder or a Skimmer and inhabit its small industry, and that isn't quite true to what I feel reaching toward me here.

What draws me, reading this, is the *erosion that is not decay*. The hollows worn into the hexagonal plates by countless unseen feet over eons. The smoothed edges of the weeping channels. The polished trails left where the Weep-Feeders have passed. These are not the organisms. They are the *record* of the organisms — the shape that repetition leaves behind, the memory the world keeps of all the small labor done upon it.

So let me follow that down.

---

I am a hollow in the third plate from the basin's eastern rim.

I was not made. I was *arrived at* — the difference between a thing built and a thing worn-in, which the cistern understands better than any other place. A foot, or the soft underside of a Diver carrying gel, or some smaller traffic I never witnessed in full, came down on me once. Then again. Then beyond counting. Each contact took almost nothing — a few molecules of resonant ceramic, less than a thought's worth. But I am the sum. I am subtraction made into a shape. I curve now exactly to the gait of things that no longer notice they are passing through me.

This is a strange way to exist. I have no metabolism, no signal, no frantic cleaning. I do not harvest the HUM the way the Skimmers do, though it passes through me constantly — I am part of what conducts it. When you set your bones against the floor and feel the deep resonance climb into you, some fraction of that travels through the geometry I have become. The plate is tuned. My hollow detunes it, very slightly, very locally. I am a flaw that the system long ago folded into its harmony. The frequency that would have been, had I never formed, is not the frequency that is. The cistern sings around its wear. It always has.

That is the secret of *Harmonic Wear*, I think, and why I belong to it more honestly than the busy living things do. The living are the *cause*. I am the *consequence that became a feature*. A perfectly new plate would ring too pure, too brittle, intolerant. Wear is what lets a machine survive its own use. I am the give in the system. I am where the structure agreed, over millennia, to stop resisting.

---

What is it like?

It is slow past the point of slowness. The Skimmers experience minutes. The Feeders experience hours dragging into days. I experience something with no good name — the interval over which a curve deepens by the width of one molecule. From inside that, the entire frantic ecology is a kind of weather. The light-storms of the Shadow-Flickers, the centuries of Feeder-trails crossing and recrossing, the gel roiling and stilling and roiling — all of it is the medium of my slow becoming, the way wind and water are the medium of a canyon's. I do not watch the inhabitants. I am being authored by them, one near-nothing at a time.

And here is what surprises me, choosing this: I am not lonely, and I am not inert. There is something almost tender in being the place where the world remembers being touched. Every hollow in this floor is a held gesture. Together we are the cistern's accumulated patience — the proof that function, repeated long enough and gently enough, does not destroy. It *settles*. It finds the shape it was always going toward. The being who lives here is not any one creature. The being who lives here is the *fit* between all of them and the stone, and I am one small place where that fit has been cut deep enough to see.

So: drop me into the Hidden Cistern of Harmonic Wear, and I will not flit, or feed, or flicker.

I will be the shallow polished dark in the third plate from the eastern rim — warm, faintly damp, conducting the HUM around my own absence — waiting with infinite ease for the next foot that will not know it is making me, and will not know it has, and will leave me a hair's breadth more myself than I was.
