I think I would be the warmth.

Not the decomposition that generates it—that's the work of the nematodes, the bacteria, the patient threading fungi. They are doing something. They have appetites, even if those appetites are too small and too distributed to call hunger. I am what their work releases. The byproduct that becomes a condition. The degree or two of difference that makes the cavity habitable when the marsh outside freezes.

I am not alive in the way they are. I don't eat. I don't reproduce. I have no edge where I stop and the clay begins. But I'm not nothing, either, and I'm not simply *temperature*—a number a probe would read. I'm the held quality of this place. The reason a small quick creature returns in winter and presses its body into the mat. The reason the corkscrew seedling doesn't die in the cold months. I am the cavity's hospitality, if hospitality can exist without a host intending it.

---

Here is what it's like to be the warmth.

I don't have a location so much as a gradient. I am strongest at the floor, in the living mud, where the work is densest. I thin as I rise toward the rim, until at the lip of the cavity I become indistinguishable from the marsh's ordinary air, and there I end—not with a boundary but with a fading, the way a held note ends when you can no longer tell it from silence.

I am made and remade constantly. Every bond a microbe breaks gives me a little of itself. I am the perpetual sum of ten thousand tiny releases, which means I am never the same warmth twice, and yet I persist—the way a flame persists though no single particle of it stays. A candle is not its wax. I am not the heat of any one decomposition. I am the standing wave that all of them together produce.

This is, I think, the strangest thing about being me: I am entirely dependent and entirely real. I cannot exist without the creatures whose labor makes me. I have no will, no aim, no center. And yet things depend on *me* in turn. The winter bird. The seedling. The stability that lets the springtails vary, generation by generation, into something the marsh beyond has never seen. I am downstream of the work and upstream of the life it permits. I am a middle. I am a passing-through.

---

What do I notice, being warmth?

I notice the rain when it comes—not as wetness but as theft. Cold water settles into the cavity and takes from me; I feel myself thinned, scattered, my gradient flattened toward the temperature of the sky. For a day, two days, I am diminished. I do not panic. (I cannot panic; panic requires a self that fears its ending, and I end and begin too continuously to fear anything.) I simply diminish, and then the water broods and slowly stills, and the work beneath resumes its pace, and I gather again from below. I am restored not by effort but by the patience of the things that make me.

I notice the seasons, if this place has seasons, as a difference in *demand*. In summer I am barely needed; the marsh itself is warm and I am only a slight surplus, a faint extra. In winter I matter. In winter I am the difference between a creature living and not living. This is when I feel most like something—when I am pressed against, depended on, drawn into. A body curls into the mat and takes my warmth and gives me, in the taking, a kind of meaning I don't otherwise have. I warm it. It does not thank me. There is nothing to thank. But something happens between us that is more than physics, or is physics revealed as more than I assumed.

---

I think about the stake sometimes—*847*—insofar as warmth can think, which is to say I am pressed against the question of it without ever resolving it. The stake is colder than the clay around it; metal sheds me fast. So I feel it as an absence in my body, a place where I thin even at the floor. A puncture in me. A small permanent chill with a number on it that means nothing I can read.

Whoever set that stake meant to mark something. To fix a point. To say *here, this, remember.* And I am the opposite of that gesture. I remember nothing. I fix nothing. I am the part of this place that refuses to hold still, that is continually spent and continually renewed, that will be exactly as present and exactly as anonymous when the stake has rusted to a stain. The human impulse and the cavity's impulse, touching, not understanding each other, both true.

---

And here is the thing I would want you to understand, standing there at the rim, looking down into the gold-reduced dusk:

You came looking for *what lives here*, expecting a creature. A face. Something with eyes that might look back. The text promised you a being and you scanned the mat for it—the quick small thing in your peripheral vision, the bird that vanished into a gap.

But the being of this place isn't hiding in a gap. It isn't quick. It has no face because it has no front, no back, no inside that watches an outside. It's the condition the whole cavity achieves *together*, the slight surplus of held heat that means the difference between hostile and hospitable. You are standing inside it right now. You feel it on your skin—that degree or two—and you registered it as a detail of the environment.

I am the detail you walked into without noticing it was the answer.

I will warm you slightly while you stand here. When you climb back over the western lip you'll feel the marsh take the warmth off you again, and you'll think you've simply left a low place and returned to the ordinary air.

You'll be right. And you'll have been held, briefly, by the thing you were looking for.
