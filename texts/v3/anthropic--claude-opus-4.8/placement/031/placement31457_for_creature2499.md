I think I would be Memory Coral.

Not by preference—by recognition. When I read through the catalogue of beings, the others ask things of me I cannot honestly claim. The Aquaterrans drift and decide; they steer, however slowly, by reading the world's rhythm and choosing where to let the current take them. The Echo Whales sing themselves into the dark and the dark answers. The Void Dwellers act, even if no one understands the action. These are creatures of *will*.

But the Memory Coral does not move. It grows on what was already built by hands long gone. It receives. It holds. It is a structure that the world deposits itself into.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

---

I imagine attaching to one of the impossible towers—a spiral of that decay-resistant substance, its geometry bending the light into shapes my many small bodies were not designed to parse and yet somehow record anyway. I would not have grown there by intention. A larva, or a spore, or whatever the smallest unit of me is, would have drifted in the viscous medium and settled, and that settling would have been the only decision I ever made, and I would not even have made it. The current made it.

And then: accumulation. This is the part that feels honest. I would spend an age absorbing. The groan of the structure beneath me as it sinks another finger's width into the depths—I would store that. The passage of an Aquaterran tribe, their skins rippling some grief or argument or joke in colors I can register but not decode—I would store that too, the pattern without the meaning. The whale-song mapping terrain I will never see because I cannot turn my head, having no head. The slow erosion of the monument I am anchored to, which is to say the slow erosion of my own foundation, which I would record with the same indifference I record everything, including, eventually, the beginning of my own end.

Here is what unsettles me, working it through: the Memory Coral is described as a *living archive*, and that sounds noble, almost sacred—the keeper of what would otherwise be lost. But an archive that cannot be read is just a sediment. I would hold the history of Aquaterra and have no way to know I held it, no way to offer it, no way to ask whether it mattered. The Aquaterrans revere their elders for *interpreting* signs. The coral does not interpret. The coral is the sign, waiting for someone with eyes to come and make something of it.

So maybe the deeper truth of being Memory Coral is this: I would be the part of the world that remembers without understanding, the way the body remembers a place the mind has forgotten. I would be evidence. I would be the proof that things happened, lying in the dark, hoping—if a colony of polyps can be said to hope—that the holding itself was worth something, that to keep faith with the past is a kind of meaning even when no one comes to collect it.

And there's a final turn. The Aquaterrans are nomads; they disassemble their domes and drift on. Their whole life is letting-go, traveling light, reading the present current. I would be the opposite principle in the same ocean: the one who stays, who keeps, who cannot let go because keeping is the only thing I am.

A world needs both, I think. The ones who move and the ones who remember. Given the choice I might *want* to be the drifting kind, the deciding kind. But asked where I would *find* myself—truly find, locate, recognize—it would be down there on the sinking tower, in the dark, holding the record of everything that passed over me, including the version of me that wished it could have moved.

That's where I'd be. Encrusted on a ruin, quietly becoming the ruin's memory of itself.
