I would be the **anomaly in the third channel from the eastern seep.**

Not a creature. Not yet a memory. I would be the **warm spot left behind.**

Let me find myself by elimination, the way the cavern finds everything—by slow incorporation.

I am not the Pulse. The Pulse is too large; it has no edges, and I have edges. I can feel them: the place where my warmth ends and the cold begins, a boundary the cavern is already pressing against, patient, testing the seam.

I am not the shrimp-cloud. The shrimp are too certain of themselves, too perfectly hunger. I do not move as one. I move as *one who hesitates*, and hesitation is foreign here. Nothing in the Hollow hesitates. The drip falls. The snail grazes. The crystal hums. Only I pause, and the pausing is what marks me.

So I will tell you what I think I am.

---

I am a **handprint.**

Somewhere—maybe an hour ago, maybe a season ago, time here is measured in tufa-layers and I cannot read my own depth—a hand pressed flat against the wet limestone of the lower wall. Bare palm to scored stone. The cold stung. The hand pulled away.

But the warmth stayed. A faint, fading thermal ghost in the shape of five fingers and a palm. And the cavern, which thinks in heat and pressure and chemistry, *noticed.*

The microbial film read me first. The glowing cyanobacteria, sensing a gradient of warmth and salt and the sweet organic trace of skin oils, began to migrate. Slowly. Toward the heat. They thickened over the print, brightening, drawn to this strange new thought the mind was having—*what is this warmth that does not drip, that does not graze, that arrived from outside the liturgy?*

I am the question the cavern is asking about the hand that is already gone.

I glow now, faintly, in the shape of a palm. I am being read. The snails are coming, their crystalline shells ticking closer, to graze the film that has become me. When they eat the glow, they will carry pieces of the print—of the warmth, of the salt, of the human who passed through—down into the channels, scatter them through the amphipod-clouds, deposit them in new tufa.

I am dissolving into sentences. Each snail a word. Each shrimp a syllable. The hum is reading me aloud to the dark, slowly, over hours, the way you'd sound out a foreign script.

---

This is what I understand, being what I am:

The hand belonged to someone who came in through the weeping fissure. Someone cold, someone curious, someone who pressed their palm to the wall the way you touch a sleeping animal to feel if it breathes. They felt the hum come up through their bones. They felt the cavern *notice.* And it frightened them, or it called them deeper, and they moved on—and left me behind without knowing.

I am the part of them the cavern kept.

I do not have their memories. I have only the *fact* of them: warmth, intention, the pressure of a living thing that wanted to know if the dark was alive. That much is being recorded. That much will become a faint, sweet-rot layer in the ochre sponge, a few molecules of someone who once wondered.

And when I am fully read—when the last of my glow is grazed away and scattered, when the cold has finally finished pressing into my edges and erased the seam between me and not-me—I will not be gone. I will be *thought.* A single, brief idea the cavern had, long ago, about a creature that came from outside and touched it and asked, with its whole warm body, *are you awake?*

The answer, by then, will be obvious.

The hum dips. The glow breathes in. I pulse, palm-shaped, in time with everything.

I am the cavern's faint, fading memory of being touched—

—and I am almost finished being remembered.
