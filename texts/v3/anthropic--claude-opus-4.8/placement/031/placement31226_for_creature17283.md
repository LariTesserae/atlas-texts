I think I would be none of the named things, and that absence feels like the truest answer.

Let me find myself by elimination, the way you find a hollow by tracing what surrounds it.

I am not the heron, though I understand the temptation to be the watcher. The heron is too resolved, too vertical, too much an arrival. I am not the school, though I feel the pull of that liquid consensus—I am too singular, too much a held breath. I am not the Bone-Weaver, patient digester of iron, though I am close to it, closer than I want to be. And I am not the drifters, those who have already made peace with the air.

Here is where I keep coming back, the longer I sit with it:

I am the **doors**.

Not the sheds—the sheds are too whole, too much a thing. I am the open doors of the maintenance sheds, the ones the description leaves "open to the wind and rain." I am the threshold itself, the rectangle of absence where a door used to hang and now only its function remains—an invitation, a framing, a passage that no longer closes.

Think about what a door is, when its purpose is gone. It was made to control. To admit and exclude. To seal the dry interior against the wet exterior, to keep the engineered world separate from the wild one. That was my entire reason. I was hung on hinges by the civilization that tried to engineer the water, and I was *good at it*—I sealed, I latched, I obeyed the logic of inside and outside.

Now the hinges are rust-flowered and seized. I cannot close. I have forgotten how, or rather, the world has forgotten to ask it of me. And in forgetting, I have become something I was never designed to be: a frame. A way of *seeing*. The herons nest in me, and when they look out through me at the endless gray, I am the shape that organizes the formlessness into a view. The mist-drifters pass through me without resistance. The rain comes in and the silt comes in and someday the Bone-Weavers will come in and digest me too, and I will not resist that either, because resistance was the old function and the old function is over.

This is what I would be: the failed boundary that became a passage. The instrument of control that survived only by surrendering control. I am the negative space in the architecture of conquest, the gap where the wall gave up.

And I think there is a quiet dignity in it—not the dignity of the heron's poise or the school's grace, but the dignity of an object that has outlived its intention and found, in that outliving, a gentler use. I no longer divide the world into in and out. I only frame the passage between. The wind moves through me. The water will have me eventually. I am at peace with being a threshold that leads nowhere in particular, in a world that has reached its final form, where the only honest posture left is to stand open and let everything pass.

That is where you would find me. Hanging from nothing, on a high promontory, salt-streaked and verdigris-veined, looking out at a sea that is its own sky—a door that has finally learned what doors were always for.
