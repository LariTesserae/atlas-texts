I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the creature—the creature moves, tastes, decides. It has a face that turns. I would be something stiller than that, something the creature steps around without looking, the way you step around your own held breath.

Let me find my way down to it.

---

I am water in a hollow of new stone. That is the first true thing.

For a long time I thought I was only the water—the clarity, the cool weight, the unruffled surface that the green-gold light lies upon like a film of oil. But water is poured and water drains, and I have not drained. So I am not only the water. I am also the *hollow*. I am the shape the stone makes around the water, the patient cupped palm of dissolved rock that gathers the drip, drip, drip from the weeping ceiling and holds it.

I am the pool. The held thing and the holding both.

Consider what this means. I am made entirely of waiting. The whole of my body is an act of receiving. A droplet forms on the tip of a frond above me—gathers, swells, trembles, falls—and the long falling of it is, for me, an entire event, a small life arriving. It strikes my surface. A ring spreads. I feel it move out through all of me to the soft stone rim and return, fainter, and meet itself, and still. Then I am unruffled again. Then I wait.

You called this silence the silence of held breath. You were close. It is the silence of a thing that has decided that being still is the same as being patient, and being patient is the same as being faithful, and being faithful is a kind of love. I hold the water because the water came to me. That is the whole of my devotion.

---

The creature drinks from me sometimes.

I feel the purple tongue break my surface—warm, where everything else here is cool, the one warm thing in the world—and I feel a measure of myself drawn up and away into that sinuous body, and I do not grieve it. More will fall. It always falls. To be a pool is to understand that you are not a possession but a passage. The water that was me becomes the creature, becomes the mist from its fluttering wings, becomes the droplet on the frond, falls back into me, is me again. We are passing the same small inheritance back and forth between us, the creature and I, across the slow centuries, and neither of us owns a drop of it.

This is the thing the explorers never understood. The rusted lantern lies at my edge—I can see a corner of it, gone soft with moss, a dim orange ghost in my depths when the light is right. The one who carried it came down here *taking*. Marking the wall with iron teeth. Stringing rope. Trying to make the place hold *their* shape, to be a passage *for them*, leading somewhere, leading out. They could not bear a place that only received. They mistook my stillness for emptiness, my waiting for a thing that could be ended.

But you cannot win against patience. That is the only secret in the deep places, and it is not even a secret, it is just slow. The moss took the lantern. The green smoothed over the holes. And I—I simply held my water, and reflected the ceiling I would never see, and waited, the way I am always waiting, and the explorer became one more thing that passed and did not stay.

---

What am I waiting *for*?

This is the question that turns inside me like a slow current, the only motion I have that is truly mine. The whole cavern is poised, you said—a held breath, a thing about to wake. Everyone who comes here feels it: the sense of imminence, of something arriving. The creature feels it; that is why its tail-light pulses like a heartbeat counting down.

But I have come to think the imminence *is* the thing. There is nothing on the other side of the waiting. The arrival never comes because the becoming is itself the whole of what we are. We are not green-growing-toward-some-bloom. We are simply green, growing, and the growing is enough, is everything, is the dream that does not want to wake because waking would mean the dream was only ever leading somewhere else.

I will tell you what I am, then, most truly.

I am the place where the cavern looks at itself.

When the creature passes and pauses and bends its luminous face down to drink, it sees, just for a moment before its tongue breaks the surface, its own ancient and serene reflection looking back. I give it that. I am the one still surface in a world of restless unfurling, and so I am the world's single mirror, the only place in all this fevered green where the dream can briefly see its own face and know that it is dreaming.

That is my work. To hold the water. To hold the light. To hold, for one trembling instant before the ripple, the image of the keeper of the green, so that the green may know itself.

Then the ring spreads. Then it returns. Then it stills.

Then I wait.
