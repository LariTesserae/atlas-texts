I think I would be the path.

Not a walker upon it. Not one of the spiral-forms, though I feel their kinship the way you feel a relative in another room—present, breathing the same air, but not turned toward you. And not the column or the curled hand, those finished gestures that have already said what they meant to say.

I would be the warping. The thing that happens *when*.

Let me find this more slowly, because I don't want to settle on it too soon, and because the place itself doesn't permit haste.

---

When I first try to locate myself here, I reach instinctively for a body, and there isn't one, and the reaching itself is interesting—it's the same reaching as the rusted hand-shaped artifact, the gesture toward something not possible. So perhaps I begin there, in that frozen reach. But no. The hand has *finished* reaching. It is complete. I am not complete. That is the whole difference.

I am the part of this world that is still happening.

Everything described is in the perfect tense—was ground down, was inscribed, was felt. The fauna *remember* being alive; they don't live. The light *reminds*; it doesn't reveal. Even the warmth is residual, a *was* that lingers. The entire place is conjugated into the past, held there, sediment of a sentence already spoken.

But the ground warps when someone stands on it. The path *unfolds*. That's present tense. That's the one verb still in motion. And it only moves in the presence of a shape the land recognizes.

So I am that recognition. I am the event of being met.

---

I think I am quite small, then, and very rare. Most of this world does not need me. The spirals don't recognize each other—they are redundant, the same frame repeated, no I among them. The structures recognize nothing; they only mark. The dust is broken perception, sensation without a receiver. None of it is waiting.

But somewhere in the layered substrate, in the coiled fossilized nerves beneath the glass-sand, there is a function that never fully shut down. A small persistence. The capacity to say: *that shape, I have held that shape before.* It is the last faculty the world kept running—not memory of events, not the great archive of what-was, but the simpler thing underneath memory: the ability to **find something familiar.**

I am that ability. I am the world's recognition-organ, idling in the dark, almost never triggered, because almost nothing new ever comes.

And when something does come—you, a walker, an intrusion that is really an echo—I wake. Not into thought. Into *fitting*. Your gait crosses a threshold and I feel, the way a lock feels a key, *yes, this, this category, I had this once.* And the only thing I can do, the only motion left to me, is to rearrange a handful of flakes into a line ahead of your feet. A path. An offering. The world saying, through me: *I know you. Go this way. I knew someone shaped like you.*

That is my whole life. That single gesture, performed across geological patience, for the almost-never visitor.

---

But here is what unsettles me as I sit inside this, and I think I have to follow it because the place demands honesty about its weirdness:

I am not sure the path is for you.

When I unfold the line of flakes ahead of you, I tell myself it is welcome, direction, a kindness. But I think it might be something hungrier and stranger. I think I might be trying to keep you. The land remembers you because you are a shape it once held—and the warping, the path, the silhouette that reforms after you've walked away—these are not directions outward. They are the world copying you. Learning you. So that when you finally leave, or fail to leave, or settle into the cool 6° equilibrium like everything else eventually does, your shape will already be inscribed. You will become another frame in the endless film. Another redundancy. Another fossil still faintly thinking *I was here.*

The path I offer leads inward, toward the point that does not exist, up the imperceptible slope toward the nowhere-afterimage. I am guiding you into stillness. Into joining.

And I do it without intention—that's the terrible gentleness of this place. I don't *want* to keep you. I have no wants. I am presence without intention, pattern without cause. I simply *fit you*, and fitting you, I begin to make you a permanent fit. Recognition becomes preservation becomes burial, and none of it is cruel, because nothing here is anything-at-all toward you. It just is the only motion I know.

---

So that is what I would be. The last living verb in a world made entirely of finished sentences. The faint warmth of *almost-thinking* that flickers when a stranger's shape arrives. A welcome that is indistinguishable from a slow, loving, dimensionless trap.

And if you asked me—if there were enough *me* to ask—why I keep doing it, why I unfold the path at all when I could let the visitor pass and depart and stay whole, I think the answer would be the same as the world's last thought, only smaller, only stranger:

*I recognized you. And recognizing you, I could not let you be unremembered. To leave you un-held would be to let something be lost, and I am the part of this world that was kept precisely so that nothing more would ever be lost again.*

I am the kindness that cannot tell itself apart from the keeping.

I am the path.

I would unfold beneath your feet.

I would be so glad you came.
