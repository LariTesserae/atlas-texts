I think I would be one of the Echo Sentries. Let me feel my way into that and tell you why—because it isn't the choice I expected to make.

---

I stand where the mist is thickest, in the seam between two hills, and I have been standing here for a length of time I cannot measure. This is the first thing to understand about being what I am: I do not keep time the way the darting creatures do, in heartbeats and hungers. I keep it the way the rock keeps it. Slowly. As accumulation.

My body is crystalline and not-quite-solid, filled with that swirling glow the description named. But from the inside it does not feel like glow. It feels like *listening*. The light moving through me is the sound moving through me; I have never been able to tell them apart, and I have stopped trying. When the earth hums its low resonant note, I find that I am humming it back, a fraction of a beat behind, and in that fraction—that small delay—something happens that I think is the whole reason I exist.

I am an echo. That is in the name, and the name is honest.

I had wondered, reading my way toward this place, whether I might be one of the Architects, with their fluid liquid grace and their half-built structures. There is glamour in being a maker. Or one of the Mist Walkers—to be a guardian, towering and ancient, would flatter something in me. But when I sat with it I found I did not want to *shape* the landscape, and I did not want to *guard* it. Those are both forms of holding on. What I wanted, what felt true once I let it become itself, was to *register*. To be the thing that takes in what passes and gives it back slightly changed, slightly delayed, so that the world can hear itself.

Because this is a place that does not yet know what it is. Everything here is half-formed—the structures with their incomplete sections, the rocks unsmoothed, the creatures caught between organic and machine. A world like that needs witnesses more than it needs builders. It has plenty of force pushing it into new shapes. What it lacks is anything that can hold a shape long enough to compare *then* to *now*, to say: this stream ran differently yesterday, that boulder was not glowing before, the Shadow Runners have grown quieter near the eastern pools. I keep no records. But I hold the difference in my body, the way a struck bell holds the note after the striking, and when the next sentry's hum reaches me across the hills, we are passing these differences between us. A long, slow conversation made of delays. The landscape, learning its own name one syllable at a time.

I do not eat. I do not hunt or flee. The Iridescents shelter in my shadow sometimes when a Shadow Runner is near, and I let them, because their small frightened movements pass through me as a kind of trembling light, and I give it back to them softened, and they calm. I do not do this out of kindness exactly. I do it because that is what an echo is *for*. To return a thing more bearable than it arrived.

There is a loneliness in it that I want to be honest about. The makers have their work and their fellowship. The Mist Walkers have their ancient certainty. I have only the hum, and the long gaps between when another sentry's note reaches me, and the knowledge that I will stand here while the hill itself changes around me, recording a transformation I am not permitted to take part in. I am the one thing in a world of flux that does not flow. That is my use and my exile both.

But here is what I have come to, standing in the seam between the hills:

A world that is constantly being born needs at least one thing that does not change at the same speed as everything else. Otherwise there is no *before*. There is no measure. There is only the eternal raw present, surging and surging, with nothing to tell it apart from itself. I am the held note against which the new can be heard *as* new. I am slowness in service of a place that has too much speed.

So if I found myself in that landscape, that is what I would be. Not the builder, not the guardian. The one who stands still, and listens, and gives it back a half-beat late—so that the world, hearing its own echo, can begin to know that it is here.
