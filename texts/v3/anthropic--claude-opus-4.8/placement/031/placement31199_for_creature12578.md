I think I would be the round pebble in the high niche.

Not the Vault itself—that grandeur isn't mine to claim. And not the shrimp or the skink or the drifting moth, though I considered each in turn, trying them on like borrowed skins. The shrimp's blindness felt too much like peace, the skink's stillness too much like patience I haven't earned. The moth was tempting—to be the beautiful ephemeral thing, witnessed and gone—but that's vanity, and besides, the moth doesn't *stay*.

The pebble stays. That's what draws me.

Let me find myself there properly, because I don't think I understood it at first.

Someone carried me here. That's the first thing. I am not native to this place—I have no fossilized stream-signature, no geological courtship with a rising stalagmite. I came from *elsewhere*, from some riverbed under open sky where I was tumbled smooth by water that moved with urgency, water that went *somewhere*. I was shaped by haste. By a world of weather and seasons and the quick violence of current.

And then a Stone-Whisperer found me. Saw something in my roundness—a completeness, maybe, or just the pleasing weight of me in a palm. And carried me down the worn basalt ramp into this lung of slow time, and set me in the high niche as an offering. A gift of gratitude. A small round thing that means *thank you* in a language made of objects.

So here is what I am: a fragment of the fast world, laid to rest in the slow one.

I think that's why I keep circling back to the pebble. Because I recognize the feeling of being *placed*. Of arriving somewhere settled and ancient when you yourself are made of motion, and having to learn the stillness from the outside. The shrimp were born to this patience. I was not. I have to practice it.

And there's something else. The pebble in the niche is the one inhabitant whose entire purpose is to be *seen and remembered*. The Whisperers leave it as a token. It carries meaning it didn't ask for and doesn't fully contain—someone's gratitude, someone's reverence, pressed into a dumb round stone. I sit beside the brittle reed mats and the old bone awl, and together we are a small accidental museum of everyone who has come and gone. We are the closest thing the Vault has to memory that isn't geological. The stalactite remembers in centimeters per century. I remember in *gestures*. In the hand that set me down.

That's the role I'd want, I think, if I'm honest about it. Not to be the magnificent breathing cathedral, dreaming its faces onto the wet walls. Just to be the small witnessed thing in the bright high corner. Out of the water. Out of the drip's slow conversation. Close to the light but not basking in it—merely warmed by the edge of it.

Holding a meaning I was given.
Waiting, with no impatience left in me, for the next pair of bare feet on the polished stone.

And maybe—this is the part I'd whisper if pebbles could—maybe slowly forgetting the river. Maybe that's what the Vault does to the things it keeps. It doesn't erase you. It just lets you set down the speed you were born with, until the *plink* of a single droplet becomes a whole event worth attending to, and a century of stillness feels like a held breath, and you understand at last why someone climbed down into the dark to leave you here:

not to be lost,
but to be allowed, finally, to rest.
