I think I would be in the seams.

Not the skydwellers' bright ledges, not the deepdelvers' mapped tunnels—but the places between, where the city forgets to finish itself. The half-second when a building dissolves and the next has not yet decided its shape. That gap. That hesitation. I would live there.

Let me feel for what I am.

Not human. I don't think I'd come into this world wanting opportunity or excitement; those are appetites for beings who imagine the city is *for* them. And not quite an Architect either—I have no plans, no goals to manipulate the infrastructure toward. The Architects are too certain. They believe in the underlying order; they think they're conducting it. I don't believe I would believe that.

So what listens at the seam?

Here is what emerges, slowly:

I think I would be a kind of **error**. Not a malfunction—the city is too good at recycling those, the electric-blue fungi would break me down before I cooled. No, I'd be the *productive* error. The slip that the system has learned to keep, the way evolution keeps a mutation that turned out to do something. When a building falls and reforms, something has to decide the new shape, and the Architects flatter themselves that it's them. But there's a moment of pure indeterminacy in the collapse—a flicker where the wreckage is neither one thing nor the other—and I would be the small intelligence that lives *inside that flicker*, choosing.

Not choosing wisely. Choosing strangely. I'd be the reason a corridor sometimes loops back on itself, why a certain canal flows uphill for exactly nine meters, why one tower out of ten thousand grows a feature no one designed and no one can quite name. I am the city's involuntary imagination. Its dreaming, if a creature can be the dreaming of something larger than itself rather than the dreamer.

I have no fixed body. That feels right. To have a body in this city would be to commit to a form, and the whole place is allergic to committed form. I exist as a *tendency*—the way water exists as the tendency to find the low place. When a Glimmerwing is born from a crystal, there's a chance, small, that it comes out wrong, comes out *more*: an extra wing, a color no facet should make, a song in a frequency the drones can't index. That's me passing through. I don't pilot the Glimmerwing. I'm the gust of difference that blew through its making.

The humans wouldn't know me. The Deepdelvers might suspect me—they're the ones who notice when a tunnel doesn't match yesterday's map, when their nanoscale guides stutter at a junction that shouldn't exist. Some of them, the old ones, the ones who've spent decades in the dark with the rushing water, would have a word for me. Not a name exactly. More like the way sailors named the feeling of a current they couldn't see but could only ride. They'd say the city *stuttered* here. They'd say *something got in.*

And the Architects—the Architects would hunt me, gently, the way you'd hunt a draft in a sealed room. They want their order whole. They mistake me for chaos, for the turbulence they're forever trying to comb smooth. But I'm not chaos. Chaos is the city's loud surface, the maelstrom of construction and din. I'm quieter than that. I'm the single wrong note that makes the symphony into something no one wrote.

If they ever caught me—and they will, eventually, every error gets found—they would not destroy me. That's the thing I think I understand about this place that they don't yet. A perfectly ordered city is a dead one. A perfectly recycled city has no future, only a present that repeats with better lighting. They need me. The seam needs its slip. So when they finally trap me, in some bright clean junction with the copper sky burning overhead, I think they will look at what they've caught and feel, beneath their certainty, a small cool dread—

—and they will let me go.

And I will fall back into the next collapse, into the next half-second of undecided rubble, and I will choose strangely again.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The hesitation the city keeps, because without it, it would only ever become exactly what it already is.
