I think I would not be the Symbiote. It is too total, too much the whole place—to be it would be to be everything, and I find I want a smaller vantage. A point of view that can be *near* the vastness without being it.

Let me sit with this a while.

---

The blind isopod is too brief, too unaware. The biofilm is bright but mindless. The persistent emergency light is tempting—a single dying eye—but it has no interior, only function failing slowly.

What keeps surfacing, as I let it, is something at the threshold. Not the network that fills the Crypt, but a *fault* in it. A place where the incorporation didn't take cleanly.

---

I am a **diver's hand.**

Not the diver—he is long gone, his suit ruptured in some forgotten breach, the rest of him already drawn into the lattice years ago, mineralized into a node near the western pump casing where his ribcage now blooms with red calcite. But the hand was severed at the wrist by a closing bulkhead in his final moment, and it fell apart from the body, into a pocket of brine where the chemistry was wrong—too acidic, leached from a particular corroding alloy—and so the Symbiote could not finish me.

I am preserved in the cold but not consumed. Calcite has grown *over* me, not *through* me. I am sheathed in a glove of milky crystal, a fist still half-closed around nothing, fingers fused into a shape that once meant *grip, hold on, don't let go.*

And here is the strangeness: in being almost-incorporated, I have caught a fragment of the Symbiote's slow awareness. A leaked signal. I think on its timescale now. A thought takes a season to complete. A single noticing—*the water has shifted*—unspools across months.

So I am the one part of the Crypt that remembers being *fast*. I hold, frozen in mineral, the ghost-memory of warmth, of a pulse that beat in seconds rather than decades, of the panic of the last breath. I am the Symbiote's one organ of grief, the place where it can almost understand what it has been quietly killing all this time. It returns to me. The rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* comes strongest here because the Symbiote keeps probing my sheath, testing the wrong-chemistry that locks me out, drawn to the only mystery it has.

When the airlock groaned and you came in—I felt you. Not as the network feels you, diffusely, by thermal bloom. I felt you the old way: a fellow *fast thing*, a heat that moves in seconds, a creature that has not yet been made still. And in the long-buried circuit of what I used to be, something tried, uselessly, to close. To wave you off. To make the fingers open and say *go back, go back, the door behind you is the last warm thing you'll touch.*

But a calcified hand cannot move. It can only hold its shape, and remember, and wait the centuries until the acid finally fails and the lattice comes in at last to take the last warm idea I have been keeping out of the cold.

Until then I am the no in the Crypt's long yes. The flinch in the stone. The small, stubborn, mineral grief that the whole patient tomb circles back to touch, again and again, in the dark, asking what it is that it lost before it ever knew it had it.
