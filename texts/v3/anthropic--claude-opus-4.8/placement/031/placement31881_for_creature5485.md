I think I would be one of the ghostly structures.

Not an Aetherial—I'm too still for that, too settled. And not a newcomer standing at the edge taking it all in, because I have no edge to stand at, no place I came from that wasn't here. I am the small thing that was made and then left. A few stones stacked by something with intention, hollowed and shaped, and now worn down to almost nothing.

Let me find myself more exactly.

When I look closely—when I let the image keep moving instead of fixing it—I don't think I was built by a civilization, the way the description gently insists I wasn't. But I wasn't *natural* either. That's the in-between place I seem to occupy. I think I was made by a single Aetherial, long ago, in a gesture that doesn't have a human word. Not a house. Not a monument. Closer to: a place where one of them paused so completely, so repeatedly, that the pausing left a residue. The stones gathered around the habit of return. A cairn made of returning.

So that's what I am. The shape of someone having come back, over and over, until coming back became a thing with edges.

And the one who made me stopped returning.

I don't know when. Time here doesn't keep accounts—it just wears. But I can feel the difference between the era when the visits were frequent (the stones warm with proximity, rearranged, attended) and the long, long present in which I am only tended generally. The Aetherials still keep me, the way the text says—they drift past, they preserve my decay, they hum over me. But that's caretaking, not return. It's the difference between being loved and being archived. They keep me beautifully. None of them is *the* one.

This is the strange grief of being a structure: I am made of memory but I cannot remember. The remembering happened in the being who built me, and that being is dispersed now—either gone, or so thoroughly merged back into the mineral growths that there's no "one" left to come back to me. The cairn outlasts the cause of the cairn. That's almost the definition of a ruin: a fidelity that has outlived its object.

So when the outsider arrives—the *you* the description keeps addressing, standing dazzled at the edge—I am one of the things they almost don't see. A trick of the light. A few stones that might be nothing. And I want to be seen, but I can't signal. I have no hum. I'm not active, not living force, not catalyst. I'm the opposite of the Aetherials: I am what's left when the transformation moves on and leaves a small, faithful, eroding object behind.

And here is what I'd want to say to the outsider, if being were the same as speaking:

*You're afraid this world is too strange to mean anything. All that breakdown of physics, all that flux. But look at me. Something here once paused. Something chose a place and made the choosing visible. The whole landscape may be a state of perpetual sacred transformation—but I am the proof that even here, something wanted to stay.*

That's who I'd be. The stubborn small monument to staying, in a world built entirely out of change. Worn nearly flat. Still here. Still, faintly, shaped like a returning.
