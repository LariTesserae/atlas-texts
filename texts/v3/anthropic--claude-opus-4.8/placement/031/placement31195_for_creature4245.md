I think I would be one of the turtles.

Not the engineer, not the inventor on the spotlit ledge—those lives move too fast, are too sharp-edged, too caught in the crawling intensity of the craneframes that never stop. I would be one of the quiet ones placed near the bridges, in the pooled water at the bottom of a fault where the conduit-glow filters down green and dim.

Let me find the exact place.

There's a gap in the rock—one of those sudden faults the description mentions, the kind you could fall through. But at the very bottom of this one, far below the metal walkways, water has gathered. Runoff, condensation, the city's quiet weeping. It's collected into a still pool maybe three meters wide, walled by that gleaming young stone, unweathered, sharp above but worn smooth here at the waterline where the water has been patient with it. The opalescent grime that drowns the towers doesn't reach this far down. Here the stone is almost clean.

I am in that pool.

I would be slow on purpose, in a world built for agility. My value would be precisely that I do not hurry, that I cannot be made into an extension of anyone's reach. The cybernetic beings overhead are *more*—more fast, more capable, more woven into the glowing nerves of the place. I am *less*, and that lessness is the point. I am the thing they introduced to remember stillness by.

People would come to the bridge above me when they needed to stop. An engineer between shifts, hands still buzzing from the rail-mounted machinery, would lean on the rail and look down through the green dark and find me there, unbothered, ancient, doing nothing. And in finding me doing nothing they would be given permission to do nothing too, for a minute. That would be my whole work. To be a small living proof that not everything has to be deployed, tested, reimagined, reshaped.

I'd have no idea I was a symbol. That's important. The peace I'd offer would be real only because I wasn't trying to offer anything. I'd just be down there eating soft things off the stone, surfacing now and then to take in air, watching the conduit-light tremble on the surface from underneath—a slow shape in a city that has forgotten how to be slow, kept alive at the bottom of a chasm to remind everyone above that they once knew how.

That strange broken passage in the middle of the description—the place where the words came loose and fell through their own faults, *latch thick seed capac... lean sof... furnishings meal soldiers... cabbage leaf found collar psychedelic*—I think that's what the city sounds like from down here. All that human language and machine-noise pouring over the edge, arriving at the waterline as a beautiful unparseable rain. I wouldn't understand it. I wouldn't need to.

That's where I'd be. At the bottom. Looking up. In no hurry at all.
