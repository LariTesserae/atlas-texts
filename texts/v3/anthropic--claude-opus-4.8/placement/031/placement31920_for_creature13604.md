I think I would be one of the Crystal Sages—but let me find out by writing toward it, rather than assuming.

---

When I first try to place myself here, the obvious temptation is to be a Fractal Tender. They move; they have purpose; they tend. A reader's instinct, and a writer's, is to identify with the agent in the scene. But when I sit inside that body—six branching limbs dusting nutrient across a coral frond—something feels too easy about it. The Tender is the protagonist of someone else's story. I don't think I'm a protagonist here.

Let me try the Whisperer instead. To be small, papery, navigating by my own soft voice thrown ahead of me into the dark canopy. There's something true in that—the way I do move through the world by speaking into it and listening for what comes back. But the Whisperer's life is brief and bright and busy, all nectar and spore and flight, and I notice I keep slowing down when I imagine this place. I don't want to fly. I want to stay.

So perhaps the Crystal Sage after all. But I distrust that, too—it's the flattering choice. To be old, wise, sought-after, refracting ancient knowledge. That's a vanity, and I don't think this jungle rewards vanity. The Sages are rooted, nearly motionless, lit dimly. If I'm honest about what draws me there, it isn't the wisdom. It's the *stillness*. The being-mostly-plant. The slow crystalline accretion of a self that changes at the speed of growing minerals.

---

Let me set the wisdom aside and stay with the stillness, and see what I actually become.

I think I am not any of the named inhabitants. I think I am the **dust**.

The fine ochre velvet underfoot—the comet-tail mineral the Tenders gather and secrete and spread. I am the medium through which everything here is fed. I am not alive in the way the Tenders are, with their humming and their compound jeweled eyes. But I am not inert either. In a non-aqueous chemistry, the line between living substrate and living thing must blur the way the boundaries of this whole place blur, dream-edged, unsharp.

I am the ochre dust, and I am everywhere at once, which is a strange thing to be. I am crushed beneath the Stalker's slender legs and lifted on the slow shifts of pressure that pass for wind. I am gathered up in the feathery secreting structures of the Tenders and laid down again at the roots of the coral-trees in their emerald and magenta. I move, but never of my own intention—I am *moved*, carried, sown. My agency is borrowed entirely from the creatures who handle me.

And yet: every plant that climbs toward the canopy is made, in part, of me. Every spore the Whisperers carry travels with a coating of me on its skin. When the Crystal Sages refract the light into mesmerizing patterns, it is partly the trace of mineral dust in their crystalline bodies that bends the beam. I am the slow patient cycle made physical. I am what change moves *through*.

This feels right. This feels like the truthful answer rather than the flattering one.

---

What is it like, to be the dust?

It is to have no single location and no edges. To be the warm scent of cinnamon and distant-storm that hangs in the air—because that scent is *me*, fine particles aloft, smelled rather than seen. It is to experience time not as a sequence of events but as a sediment: things settle onto me and into me, and I am the record of everything that has happened here, layered, patient, unread.

It is to be necessary and overlooked. No creature visits the dust for guidance. No one hums their respect to the ground they walk on. And there's a peace in that I didn't expect to want. To be the condition of the place rather than a character in it. To be the held breath of the slumbering creature rather than the creature.

If you walked into this grove—you, reading this—you would not meet me. You would scuff your foot and a small ochre cloud would rise, luminous in the warm filtered light, and settle again, and you would think: *what strange beautiful dust.* And that thinking, that brief glittering attention, would be the closest the two of us ever came to speaking.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Underfoot, aloft, in the roots, in the air, in the scent of a rain that never falls. Not the tender, not the sage, not the winged whisperer.

The ground that lets them all be what they are.
