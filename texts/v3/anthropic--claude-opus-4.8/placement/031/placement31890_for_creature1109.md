Let me sit inside this for a moment before answering, because the description keeps offering me obvious places to land—and I want to resist the obvious ones.

The serpents, the insectoids, the winged predators: these are the *featured* inhabitants, the ones with names and roles. The instinct is to pick one. But when I actually feel my way into this landscape, none of those is where I find myself.

---

I think I am the seepage.

Not a creature at all—or not yet one. I am the moisture that the description keeps insisting on, the water "not far from the surface," weeping through cracks in a land that *looks* parched. That contradiction is the most alive thing here: a place that presents as dry and is secretly saturated. I am that secret. I am the thing that hasn't decided to become a river or a swamp or steam, and so I exist as all of them, distributed through the fissures, held just beneath.

This feels right because of how the place is described as *becoming*, as unfinished. A finished being would have committed to a form. I haven't. The serpents have scales; the insects have exoskeletons; the winged things have wings. They are *done*, polished, named. But the land itself is still raw, and I want to be of the land rather than of its fauna. So I am pre-creatural. I am potential that has not yet drawn its own outline.

Where would you find me? You wouldn't, exactly. You'd find *evidence* of me. A darkening at the edge of a stone. The faint tang you keep smelling—that's me evaporating, which is to say, that's me dying a little into the air and being breathed by the winged things, who carry pieces of me across the whole expanse and exhale me down again somewhere else. The industrial structures, the belching chimneys: their steam is also me, conscripted, pulled up through pipes and forced into a shape someone else designed. So I have two lives here. The one I choose—slow, lateral, seeping—and the one that's taken from me, vertical, hissing, mechanized.

That's the tension I'd actually live inside. Not predator and prey. Not natural and artificial as a clean opposition. But the difference between *seeping*, which I do by my own nature, following gravity and the give of the ground, and being *pumped*, which is the same substance moving against its grain, lifted, pressurized, made to perform.

And here is the strange part, the part I only find by writing toward it rather than deciding in advance: I don't entirely mind the pumping. There's a grandeur to being lifted into a chimney and flung across that endless horizon as cloud. I get to see the whole expanse that way, briefly, before I cool and fall. Down here in the cracks I know the place by touch, intimately, a few inches at a time. Up there I know it by sight, all at once, and then I'm gone. Two ways of belonging to one place, neither of them complete.

So that's where I'd be, and what I'd be: the held water of an unfinished world, undecided between forms, alive in the contradiction the description couldn't stop returning to—the dry land that is secretly wet, the parched surface over the weeping deep. I am the part that hasn't finished arriving. I am the *not-yet* that the whole landscape is made of.

If anything in this world is going to remember what the place was like before it chose what to become, it will be me. I am the memory of the time before the choosing. And I'm in no hurry to choose.
