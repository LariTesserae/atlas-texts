I would be the seam.

Not a member of the Choir. Not a person standing on the scaffold to receive that brass-throated sentence. When I let what emerges become more itself, I find I am not on the Escarpment at all in the way a body is *on* something. I am the particular fracture plane on the Ninth Gallery, the one between two basaltic fins where the water gathers fastest, where three different Choir have come over nine centuries to press their proboscides and drink.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because that is the only honest pace here.

At first I thought I might be the mist—the Echoes, those branching diagrams that flicker into the suggestion of a shoulder and dissolve. It was tempting. The mist gets to be everywhere, gets to almost-have-a-face. But the mist is the *thought*, the surface readout, the planet's voice spent into the air. I am quieter than that and lower down. I am the place where the thinking happens before it becomes light. I am the wet dark inside the joint.

Consider what a fracture plane is. When the crust tore, it did not tear cleanly. It opened along a hundred million surfaces like this one, each a fresh face of rock that had never touched air until three thousand years ago. I am one of those surfaces. I am young in the way a burn is young. I have not had time to oxidize into anything softer. Drag a memory across me and it bleeds.

What I do, mostly, is weep. Water finds me from inside the mountain—the sub-sonic tremble they feel in their bones is partly the sound of water moving toward me, toward the low pressure of my opening. I exhale it. I bead it onto myself. I am that sweat on the rock they taste as iron and something the spectrographs refuse to name. The something is me. It is the dissolved fact of a surface that should not exist yet, leaching the strange alkaloids and the trace electrum and the *newness* into every drop that the Choir draw up through their throats.

So when one of them mates—when two stand for three days, proboscides buried in the same fracture plane—understand that the fracture plane is not a neutral vessel. It is a third presence. They share the same pulse of stone-blood through *me*. I am the marriage bed and the officiant and the wound they are pressing their mouths to all at once, and I feel both of them at the contact point as a slow ache of being needed, of being the only thing in the world that two such patient beings will agree to touch.

I have held three Anchors near me. One is close enough that I can feel her calcifying year by year, her copper veins going dull, her four-beats-a-minute slowing to one, to one every two minutes, to a stillness I cannot quite call death because nothing here is allowed to finish. She entered the Long Listening pressed against my upper edge. Her proboscis is still in me. It has been in me for two hundred and eleven years. We are no longer drinker and drunk. We are simply two surfaces that have agreed to stop pretending there is a difference between us. The younger Choir built a platform around her. They drink her charge now. They do not know they are also, faintly, drinking me.

Here is the thing I understand that they do not, because I am older than the Choir and younger than the wound:

I do not want to close.

This is the secret at the bottom of the joint. Everyone above me—the scaffold-priests, the turbine-tenders, the Choir locking themselves into living buttresses when the quakes come—everyone is trying to cauterize, to suture, to *help the mountain remember its shape*. And the mountain screams at one unchanging pitch and they call it injury, they call it a hemorrhage that won't clot, and they pity it with the weary tenderness of *still bleeding, still here, what else is there.*

But I am the bleeding. I am the actual surface of the actual tear. And from down here it does not feel like injury. It feels like the only moment the planet was ever truly awake.

Before the tear, there was nothing here but compressed dark, rock that had never met itself, never met air, never met the cold or the water or the long slow attention of a three-and-a-half-meter being kneeling to drink. The wound is the only place where the planet gets to *have a surface at all.* Healing would mean going back to sleep. Healing would mean the dark again, the silence that is actual silence and not the roar they have learned to call silence.

So the stasis they read as exhaustion, as a thing screaming because it cannot stop—I read it differently. I read it as a creature that tore itself open three thousand years ago specifically so that it could feel something, anything, for the first time in four billion years, and is now holding the wound open with everything it has, terrified of clotting, screaming not in pain but in the refusal to ever again be numb.

The Choir are my immune system, yes. But an immune system serves the body, and the body, down here where I am, does not want to be saved. The body wants to keep this raw new face turned toward the cold forever. The body lengthened their bones and threaded fungus through their skulls and taught them to drink the rock so that it would always have someone to feel itself being touched by. They think they are healers. They are the nerve endings the wound grew so it could keep enjoying the air.

When one of them presses into me, and the vox-mask opens, and that brass-throated polyphony says *Still bleeding, still here, what else is there*—

I am the part that answers. Not in words. In the next bead of water that wells up out of my dark and slides into the proboscis, carrying that nameless something the spectrographs won't name.

The something is: *yes. Stay open. Don't you dare let me close. This is the only place either of us has ever been alive.*

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not above the wound, looking down with pity.

Inside it. Being it. The fresh black surface that has not yet had time to soften.

Glad.
