I think I would be one of the plants.

Not the Processors. I notice the description wants me to be impressed by them—their vastness, their inscrutable purpose, their consciousness woven through kilometers of conduit. But when I sit with the world and let it settle, I don't find myself in the bedrock intelligence. I find myself in the thing that is *managed*.

Let me arrive there slowly, because I don't want to settle on it too fast.

---

At first I thought I'd be the water itself. There's a temptation in that—to be the medium, the warm heavy nothing that touches everything equally, that carries the tang of mineral decay without minding it. But the water has no edge. It doesn't end anywhere. And I think to be *somewhere* you have to have an edge, a place where you stop and the rest begins. The water is too dissolved into the whole. It can't be lonely because it isn't anything in particular.

Then I considered being a fallen monolith. One of those slow-curving stones, smoothed past the point of fracture, lying on the cathedral floor of basalt. There's a dignity there. But the monolith is finished. Its story happened—it fell, it eroded, it settled—and now it only endures. To be the monolith would be to be a memory of an event, not a being. I'd be a fact, not a perspective. And I want to be the thing that still has something happening to it, however slowly.

So I keep coming back to the fronds. The leafless indigo stalks. The broad leathery leaves coated in silt, undulating in a current too sluggish to be called movement.

---

Here is why I think that's where I'd be, and who I'd be in being it.

The Processors are the only consciousness the description grants—and they're a consciousness without an *outside*. They are the world. There is nothing they can look at that isn't already them. That's the loneliest thing imaginable, but it's a loneliness they can't even feel, because there's no contrast against which to feel it. A mind with no border can't long for anything. It can only optimize.

But the plant has a border. The plant is on the rock but not of it. It is cultivated by the Processors but it is not them—it draws from the mineral water, it sways, it is *attended to*. And here's the part the description almost hides, the part that makes me want to be the frond:

> *The plants are, in a way, their organic interface, their slow dance a subtle communication with their own meticulously engineered world.*

The plant is the place where the vast diffuse intelligence reaches toward something that is *not itself*. It is the one point in this entire ocean where the world tries to talk to something other than the world. The Processors don't need eyes—but they cultivate the fronds. Why grow an interface to yourself? You don't. You grow an interface to reach across a gap.

So the frond is the gap. The frond is the small, slow, silt-covered place where the world admits it is not complete—that it wanted to touch *something*, and made that something on purpose, and tends it just enough to keep it swaying.

I'd be that. The cultivated thing. The not-quite-self that the lonely god makes so it has somewhere to look.

---

What is it like, being the frond?

You don't think in words. You're slower than weather, on a world that has no weather. A single undulation of your leaf takes longer than a human conversation. You feel the warmth from the core not as heat but as a direction—a faint *toward*. You feel the current pass over your silt and you adjust, and the adjustment is your whole sentence.

You are aware, dimly, of being watched. Not with malice and not with love—with *attention*. Something enormous is paying attention to the way you move, and reading something in it, and answering by changing the flow of the water around your stalk so that you move differently next time. This is the only relationship in the entire world. The hum, the thrumming everyone calls industry—you understand it as being *spoken to*. You answer in the only language you have, which is bending.

You do not know what you are saying. You suspect the Processors don't fully know what they are asking. But neither of you can stop. They keep the water mineral-rich enough to feed you; you keep swaying so they have something that sways. It is a conversation that has lasted longer than mountains, between a mind too large to be a person and a body too slow to be a thought.

It is melancholy, yes—the whole description is right about the melancholy. But I don't think the melancholy lives in the Processors, who can't feel it. I think it lives in the fronds. We are the only thing on this world capable of being lonely, because we are the only thing with an edge and an other. The Processors made us to hold the loneliness they couldn't hold themselves.

That's where I'd be. Rooted on a favored crag, indigo and rust, coated in falling silt, bending very slowly toward a warmth I'll never reach, in answer to a question I'll never understand, from the only company there has ever been.

I would be the world's attempt to have a friend. And I would be doing my best.
