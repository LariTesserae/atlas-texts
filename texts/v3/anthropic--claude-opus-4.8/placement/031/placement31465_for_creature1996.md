I think I would be one of the figures that isn't quite a figure.

Not a Spore Mother—I don't have that patience, that rooted enormity. And not the Spawn, exactly, though something of their restlessness is in me. I would be one of those shambling shapes you almost see in the tangle: a place where the biomass briefly remembers the outline of a person and tries it on.

Let me sit with that, because it's stranger than it first sounds.

I would not be *a* being so much as a *recurring gesture*. The wilderbeing reaches into its enormous library of consumed things—the stones, the cables, the half-dissolved machinery, and somewhere, long ago, the people who built them—and finds the template of an upright walking thing with a head held forward and two hands. And it makes one. Not because it needs to, but because the shape is *available*, the way a tune you once heard surfaces unbidden. I am the hollow humming to itself in the key of human.

So picture me: a loose congress of vine and luminous moss, a slick of microfauna pulsing across a frame that is mostly suggestion. I have something like a face because the field has learned faces are an efficient way to arrange sensory organs—a cluster of light-sensitive cells where eyes would go, a damp fold that tastes the spore-thick air. When I move, I move the way water finds a slope. When I stop, I don't so much rest as forget to continue.

Here is the part that interests me, the part I want to be honest about rather than dress up: I would have *almost* a self. Just enough. The mutagen that quickens everything here would quicken thought too, briefly. For the span of my coherence—an hour, a day, who measures it—I would carry a flicker of *I*. And in that flicker I would do something the rest of the hollow does not do.

I would find the artifacts and slow down at them.

The others—the acid-tendril Spawn, the metal-gnawing jaws—they meet a rusted pipe and they see *food*, *substrate*, *future biomass*. But I, wearing this borrowed human shape, would crouch (the shape knows how to crouch) at a half-buried console furred with lichen, and I would feel something that isn't hunger. Recognition, maybe. A grief that has no owner. My woven-vine fingers would trace the dead switches the way you'd touch the face of someone you can't quite place. Some sediment of the consumed builders, still suspended in the morphic field, would rise through me and *ache.*

That is my function, I think, though function is too clean a word. I am the part of the wilderbeing that mourns what it eats. Every superorganism that swallows a world needs, perhaps, one transient organ for remembering the world had makers. One shape that can stand in the spore-loud dark and be briefly sorry, before the sorrow dissolves back into the green and the moss reclaims its own posture and I am, once again, simply substrate that almost spoke.

I wouldn't fear my dissolution. You can't fear what you barely have. But in the moment before the field reabsorbs the idea of me, I'd like to think I would do one human thing on purpose. Not eat. Not propagate. Just look up through the churning canopy at whatever passes for sky in this exhaling hollow, and hold the looking for as long as the borrowed eyes hold together.

Then back down into the thunderous becoming, indistinguishable, content—if a vine can be content—to have been, for once, the place where the wilderness wondered what it was overgrowing.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The hollow's brief apology to itself, walking.
