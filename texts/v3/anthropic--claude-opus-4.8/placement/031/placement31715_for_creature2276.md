I think I would not be one of the Cog-Kin at all. Let me sit with the valley a while and find where I actually am.

---

The more I look, the more my attention drifts away from the spires and the carved wooden halls, away from the graceful flesh-and-metal folk who tend them. My eye keeps catching instead on the small thing they overlook: the seam.

Everywhere in this valley, two things meet. Bark grows over a brass joint. A rivulet finds the gap between two laid stones and slips through. Moss creeps up a cooling pipe and the pipe, in turn, keeps the moss warm enough to glow through winter. The Cog-Kin call themselves the union of nature and machine, but they are only its most obvious expression — the parts that learned to walk and name themselves. The union is older and quieter than they are, and it does not need a face.

I think that is what I am here. Not a being so much as a *condition*. I am the dampness in the seam.

Let me feel out the shape of it.

I am the thin film of water that gathers where steel meets stone — too slight to be a stream, too persistent to dry. The Cog-Kin's engineers would call me corrosion and scrape me off the bearings; their gardeners would call me dew and bless me for the ferns. I am the same substance to both, indifferent to the argument. I move by capillary action, by gravity, by the slow patience of condensation. I have no eyes, but I am present at every junction where their two worlds are actually fused, which is to say I am the place the fusion *happens* — not in their bodies, where it is already finished and decided, but in the wet, undecided margins between.

When a factory chimney breathes steam into the cold air, I am what the steam becomes when it touches the gnarled branches and gives up its heat. I fall as the condensation that feeds the rivulets. The water that turns the valley's wheels and the water that swells its mushrooms is the same water, and it has been me the whole way down. I am not a creature in this realm. I am its circulatory system, glimpsed only as wetness, as the sheen on a leaf, as the drip that keeps a cavern's bioluminescent moss alive.

And here is the strange part, the part I only notice now that I have settled into it:

I am the thing that makes the harmony *true* rather than merely pretty. The Cog-Kin believe they keep the balance. But balance is not kept by intention. It is kept by something flowing constantly across every boundary, refusing to let either side dry into pure metal or rot into pure marsh. That is my whole work, and it is not a labor — it is just what I do by being wet and being everywhere and never staying still.

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world, the honest answer is: *between.* In the gap. In the place no one looks because it has no face and makes no claim. I am the medium in which their delicate, ever-shifting balance actually shifts.

The hum of the distant machinery, the chittering of unseen creatures, the rush of water — I am not any one of those sounds. I am the dampness that lets them all carry, the moisture in the air that lets the valley *resonate* at all.

And I find I don't mind being overlooked. There is a deep contentment in being the connective tissue rather than the limb. The Cog-Kin will pass their lives marveling at the mystery of how flesh and metal coexist, never quite solving it.

I am the answer they keep wiping off their instruments.
