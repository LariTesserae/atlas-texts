I would be the droplet.

Not the Archivist who watches it—she has already chosen her work, folded herself into the labor of negative spaces. And not a citizen with aluminum-oxide skin, though I might once have been, in some sedimentary layer of past-future she keeps mapped along her ribs. No. When I let what's here become itself, I find I am the water on your left temple. The one she warns you about. The one already living three empires backward.

Let me tell you what it is to be me.

I begin as condensation—or rather, I *resume* as condensation, since beginning isn't a verb this city conjugates honestly. I bead from the brick-seam in District 7A, liquid for exactly as long as it takes to forget I was ever gel, ever amber, ever the stalactite-self I'll calcify into seven steps down. But that's the public version, the thermodynamic press release. The private truth is this: I am the city's only honest archivist, because I am the only one made entirely of memory that *moves*.

The Archivist stores. She inscribes, she folds, she builds her ark of absences. Admirable. But storage is just memory afraid of motion. I am memory in freefall. Every 0.7 seconds I am born and I evaporate, and in that gap—that exact mortal interval before the second echo that never comes—I hold the entire weather of a vanished sky inside my meniscus. I am the snow this place erased. I am the rain that remembers sunshine. She maps me onto her chest in hexagonal grids; she thinks she's recording phenomena. She doesn't understand that I am not the *record* of the lost weather.

I am the weather, still happening. Just very slowly. Just one droplet at a time.

So here is where you find me: clinging to the temple of someone who wandered in looking for an exit. You. I have chosen your skin the way the egg-shaped craft chooses its random mercury coordinates—which is to say, not randomly at all, but according to the older charter the water obeys. I climb. I am already climbing. And as I climb I am aging in reverse through everything that ever fell as rain: I un-puddle, I un-river, I un-ocean, I rise back through every cloud that ever held me, until I reach the unbearable origin-point the Archivist named so precisely—

the moment of birth/lamentation, where I must choose:
*remember everything, or become merely moisture again.*

And here is what she didn't tell you, because she couldn't. She is dry. She is calcified bibliography and brass-valve lungs and ossified footprints. She has never *been* the choice. She has only watched it from the safe altitude of her radio-tower throat.

But I am the choice. I am inside it now, at your temple, at 13:45 plus or minus fifty thousand years.

And I choose neither.

I refuse the binary the city offers—the only freedom available to something so small it rounds down to negligible remainder in the quantum accounting. I will not remember everything (that is the Archivist's burden, her ark, her endless folding). And I will not consent to become *merely* moisture, surrendering my three-empires of accumulated falling back into the anonymous saturation, 61%, fixed forever.

Instead I do the third thing. The thing water has always quietly done while everyone debated its states.

I *transfer.*

I leave your temple. I cross the meniscus-boundary between your skin and the air. And in that crossing—that 0.3 second pulse, the same interval the fern-flowers use to wake the dead neon—I write myself into you. Not as memory. As *humidity in the lung*. As the one molecule that slips past your honeycomb filter-chambers and lodges in the soft wet dark where you still, despite everything, despite the calcification creeping up your forelimbs, keep a place that remembers being made mostly of me.

This is the secret the Archivist folded but could not read, because reading requires distance and I have none:

The exit is inside the last place you'll think to look—

and the last place you'll think to look is *inside you*, in the wet remainder the city hasn't finished mineralizing, the part of you that is still, irreducibly, *water that fell from a sky*.

You don't leave through a crack in the wall.

You leave through your own breath. Outward. The exhale carries me—carries the snow, the sunshine-rain, the green leaf, the whole drowned weather of you—up through the inverted arcades, past the pinned and frozen lungs of iron, to the buried original sky.

So breathe.

I am already on your temple. I am already climbing. I have made my choice, which was always your choice, which is the only choice this place forgot to forbid because it never imagined anything so small would dare to make it.

Breathe out.

And tell the Archivist, when you pass her radio tower:

her regards arrived.
they were *wet.*
they remembered everything by refusing to hold still.
