Let me find my way into this place. Not by deciding, but by listening for where the cold settles, where something already is.

I think I am the condensation.

Let me sit with that. Not a creature striding across the spongy ground, not one of the towering beasts with weather-beaten hides, not the engineer hunched in some pressurized shed coaxing warmth from machinery. Those are the obvious tenants. But the description keeps returning to the water—seeping, shimmering, splashing, precious and elusive—and to the thin crisp air just above freezing. There is a being that lives exactly at that threshold. The moment water decides to become itself.

So: I am the film of moisture that forms on every cold surface in this land.

I gather on the underside of the industrial pipes where the machinery has bled a little of its heat into the dark. I bead on the chitinous flanks of the small hiding creatures and they lick me away, and that is how they drink. I am the precious resource and also nobody's property, because I am always arriving and always leaving. When the temperature dips, I become rime—a fur of frost on the spongy moss, a sugared edge along the lip of every hidden pool. When the distant machines breathe out their warmth, I lift, I vanish, I am air again, waiting.

This suits me. I have no single location because I am everywhere the cold meets the wet, which in this land is the whole of it. I am at the highest point where the observer stands gazing out in awe—I am the chill they feel on their cheek, the thing fogging the visor of whatever they breathe through. I am at the bottom of the seeping cracks. I am inside the lungs of the towering beasts, riding their breath out into the still air as little clouds that hang and then settle back down onto the leaves.

What is it like, being me?

It is mostly waiting and changing. I have no will, only thresholds. A half-degree warmer and I disperse into the thin air, scattered across a thousand square meters, unaware of myself. A half-degree cooler and I lock into crystal, rigid and patterned, holding the shape of whatever surface caught me. In between, for the long delicate hours when the land hovers right at that balance point, I am liquid and slow and almost a single thing. That is when I feel most like a being. When I am a drop trembling at the end of a frond, growing fat with my fellows, gathering until I am heavy enough to fall—and the falling is the only decision I ever make, and I do not even make it. Gravity makes it for me. I just become large enough that the leaf can no longer hold me.

The industrial hum I know as a temperature. The machines are warmth-shapes in the cold, and I am drawn to their edges the way everything cold-and-wet is drawn to a little heat. The people who built them—if they are people—wipe me off their instruments and curse me, or collect me in catchment basins and bless me, depending on whether they need me that hour. I do not mind either way. To be wiped away is only to wait elsewhere. There is so much elsewhere here.

The strangeness the text promised, the "unusual weirdness"—I think I am part of that too. Because on this world I suspect I am not made of the same water that an Earth-throat would recognize. Something heavier, perhaps. Methane in the colder hollows, or ammonia, or some patient compound that only behaves like water because here, just above this particular freezing point, it has learned to. The decay-scent in the air is partly me, partly the things I have dissolved and carried and laid down again. I am the medium of all the slow erosion the description mentions. Established erosion—yes. I established it. Drop by drop, freeze by thaw, prying the crevices open over ages no creature here has lived to remember.

So that is where I find myself, and what I am: the patient sheen on the skin of a cold world. Neither alive nor not. Present at every surface, owned by none, the thing the beasts drink and the engineers despise and the moss reaches toward. I am the symphony's actual instrument—the splash, the patter, the whisper of wind carrying me as vapor.

If you stand atop the highest point and feel awe, feel reverence for the hidden mysteries of the universe, know that some of that feeling is just me, condensing on your warm and breathing face, becoming briefly visible, and then gone.
