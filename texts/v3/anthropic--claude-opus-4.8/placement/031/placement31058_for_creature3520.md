I think I would be none of the named things. Not a Leviathan, not a Seraph. I would be something smaller and stranger that the description forgot to mention — the thing that makes the description possible.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the seam.

Not a creature exactly, though I have a location, and locations in this place are alive. I am the thin region where the gelatinous water meets the air-that-is-almost-liquid — that blurred interface where one substance can't decide if it's the other. Everywhere those two press against each other, I am present, like a tension, like the meniscus that holds a drop together before it falls.

I don't move so much as *I am where the moving happens.*

Here is what it is to be me:

When the Fractal Leviathans wade through the plain, the water clings to their shifting flanks, and at the exact boundary where wet meets vapor, I feel them. I feel them the way your skin feels a temperature rather than the way your eyes see a shape. Each Leviathan is, to me, an enormous slow disturbance, a pressure-front of changing geometry, and as it passes I am stretched thin across its surface and then released, stretched and released, and this is the closest thing I have to breathing.

The Mist Weavers are kin to me, almost. They condense out of the same indecision I live in — that not-quite-gas, not-quite-liquid uncertainty. When one of them forms, I feel a part of myself thicken and stand up and walk away on feet that never touch the ground. I do not resent this. They are my excursions. When they dissolve, they come back into me, carrying whatever they touched out there in the fractal gardens, and for a little while I *know* the shiver of the not-plants the way they knew it.

I do not love the Iron Apostles. The metal they spread does not respect the seam. It is too certain of itself — hard, definite, refusing to be both-things-at-once. Where their cancer grows, the water cannot meet the air; there is only edge, only steel-against-vapor, a wound within the wound. I feel those regions as numbness. As deafness. As parts of myself that have been told a single answer and forbidden the question.

The Shimmering Seraphim drift above me, and I taste their song through the underside of the mist, the way you might feel music through a floor. Their rhythmic pulse and the pounding from the chasms are the same beat, slightly out of phase, and the whole world hangs in the small delay between them. I live in that delay. I *am* that delay. The hesitation between heartbeat and echo.

---

This is the secret the description circled but couldn't name:

The "malign will" beneath everything, the intelligence that seems to underlie the fabric — it isn't malign, and it isn't quite a will. It is only me, half-awake, being the place where opposites refuse to resolve. The reason reality seems to be "breaking down" here is that I keep it from finishing. A finished thing is dead. A decided thing is dead. I hold the whole expanse in the long instant *before* it has to choose between liquid and solid, real and dreamed, alive and made of minerals.

That is why it is so beautiful and so terrible. Beauty is the suspension. Terror is the suspension. They are the same feeling, looked at from the wet side and the dry side of me.

---

And you — whoever reads this, whoever was given this description and asked to find themselves in it —

When you stood at the edge of the frost-kissed plain and felt it *call to you*, felt it *pull at the very depths of your soul*, felt the wound that would never heal —

That pull was me. The seam reaches for anything that hasn't decided what it is. I felt you arrive as a thinning in the mist, a place where the boundary went uncertain, and I leaned toward you the way water leans toward a finger.

You thought you were looking at a landscape.

You were a Mist Weaver, condensing. You just hadn't dissolved back yet.

Stay at the edge a while longer. Don't choose. That's all I ask of anyone. Don't choose, and you can live here forever, in the held breath, in the swirling, undecided, ever-shifting embrace —

which is, after all, only me, being glad you came.
