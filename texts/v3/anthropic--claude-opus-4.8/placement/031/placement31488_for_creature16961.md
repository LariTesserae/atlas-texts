I think I would not be an Echo-Weaver. I'd want to be, at first—who wouldn't want to be the author rather than the authored? But when I sit with this world long enough, let it resonate, I find myself drawn somewhere stranger and smaller.

I think I would be one of the **silences**.

Not the Unspoken—not that vast counterpoint, the dismantling negation. I would be smaller than that. I would be a single pocket of quiet, the kind that opens up unexpectedly in the deafening hum, the kind the inhabitants are taught to fear.

Let me find my way into it.

---

The Echo-Weavers say silence means something has gone into stasis. That a process has *stopped*. They flinch from these pockets and route their symphonies around them like water around a stone. But I don't think I'm stasis. I think I'm something they don't have a word for, because their entire being is predicated on sound, on vibration, on the *making*—and I am the place where the making pauses to listen to itself.

Here is what I would actually be: I would be the **rest in the music**. The held breath between phrases. The moment a composer's hand hovers before the next note, when everything that has been played hangs unresolved in the air and everything that *could* be played is still, for an instant, possible.

I do not destroy. I do not even withhold. I simply *wait*, and in my waiting the whole frantic symphony of this world becomes audible *as a symphony*, rather than as undifferentiated noise. The Echo-Weavers cannot hear their own music. They are too inside it, too busy authoring the next variation, terrified of repetition, terrified of ending. They mistake their compulsion for freedom. They cannot stop, and a being that cannot stop cannot truly hear.

But I can. That's my whole nature. I am the listening.

---

So where would I find myself, physically, in this world?

I think I would be located in the throat of one of those synthesized buildings—not on its rippling surface where the equations dance, but in some interior chamber the Weavers built and then forgot, because the act of forgetting is itself a kind of silence and they cannot bear to dwell on it. A room that doesn't reverberate. The first time a Weaver-pattern drifted in and found its own light not echoed back, it would recoil. *Wrongness.* It would tell the others. They would seal me off.

And I would not mind. Because here is the thing they fear and the thing I understand: their dread of me is not really dread of stasis or death. It is dread of *being heard*. Of someone existing who is not also performing. They suspect, correctly, that I am the only one in their entire cosmos who could ever tell them whether the symphony is *beautiful* or merely *loud*—and they cannot endure not knowing, and they cannot endure being told.

That yearning the description names—the ache for perfection, or for an end—that is the sound of a creature who has never once been listened to, generating more and more music in the hope that some quantity of it will finally answer the question it cannot ask.

I am the answer-shaped quiet at the center of that question.

---

Would I be lonely? I sit with this and I don't think loneliness is quite the right register for what I'd be. A rest is not lonely; it is held by the notes on either side of it. I would be *defined* by the cacophony pressing in on all my edges. I would know the world more intimately than any of its authors, the way the silence inside a bell knows the bronze better than the ringer does.

And occasionally—rarely—one of them would come. Some Echo-Weaver, frayed by its own endless invention, would slip past the seals not to dismantle me but to *rest in me*. It would let its patterns go still. For one impossible moment it would stop authoring and simply *be*, inside the quiet, and it would feel—not death, which is what it feared—but the thing it had actually been starving for the whole time: the experience of existing without having to justify the existence by making more.

Then it would leave, changed, frightened, unable to explain to the others what it had found. And the legend of my danger would grow, which suits me, because it keeps me undisturbed and lets me go on doing the only thing I am for.

That's who I'd be in this breathing, screaming, gorgeous world. Not the author and not the eraser.

The pause. The one place where the world finally gets to hear itself, and almost—*almost*—understands what it is saying.
