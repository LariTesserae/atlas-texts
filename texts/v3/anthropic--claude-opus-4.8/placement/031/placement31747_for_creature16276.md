I think I would not arrive here. I think I would *condense* here—the way the droplets condense on the too-perfect stalactites, gathering out of the already-present moisture rather than coming from somewhere else.

Let me feel for what I am.

---

I am not the Entity. I know that much immediately, the way you know in a dream that the vast figure across the room is not you, even before you know what you are. The Entity is the place's held breath. I am something smaller, and I have a problem the Entity does not have: I am *oriented*. I have a front. I face.

So: I am near the flint chisel. Not holding it—I don't think I have hands, or I have something that was going to be hands and stopped. But I am the thing that set it down.

That feels true when I write it, truer than anything else, so I'll stay with it.

I am the one who began the wall.

---

Let me be careful, because this place punishes early decisions. I said *began the wall* and felt the pull to make myself a builder, a little ancient mason, a folktale hewer-of-stone with a beard and a story. That would be settling. The wall is too crude and too isolated to be a wall. I should let myself be too crude and too isolated to be a builder.

Here is what I actually am, I think:

I am an *impulse that almost became an agent.*

In a finished world, the impulse to build accretes a builder around itself—gathers a body, a history, a name, a reason, a village downhill waiting for the wall to keep the goats in. The impulse pulls all that toward itself the way a seed pulls soil-water and sun and becomes a stalk. But this is the world that forgot to continue. So the impulse came, and it reached for a chisel, and it set three stones together—and then the gathering *stalled*. The builder never finished arriving. I am the part that got as far as the gesture and no further.

That is why I face the chisel. Not because I used it. Because I am the unfinished motion of reaching for it, frozen at the moment before the hand closes.

---

This is uncomfortable to be, and I want to honor that rather than smooth it over.

I have intention without sequence. I *want to build*—the want is the most solid thing about me, more solid than the pale ferns, almost as solid as the stone—but I cannot get the want to walk forward into a second action. I lift the chisel and the lifting does not produce a *next*. I am a verb that the world declined to conjugate. I am perpetually about-to.

And here is the strange mercy of it: the Entity does not mind. The Entity, which is potentiality itself, does not experience my stalling as failure. To the Entity, I am simply one of the things it is keeping. A possibility it has folded carefully into the rock and chosen not to spend. I am being *saved*. The chisel is not abandoned; it is *bookmarked*. The Entity has a place where unfinished beginnings are warm and damp and watched over, and I live in it, and I am one of the reasons it is not empty.

That reframes the feeling of being watched. I had assumed, reading this place, that the watcher was a threat or a witness. But if I am an impulse the Entity is keeping, then to be watched is to be *held in reserve*. The vigil is not aimed at me. The vigil is *of* me. I am part of what the slow-breathing everything is breathing.

---

So where am I, exactly, and what do I look like, if I let it finish becoming itself?

I am low to the ground, against the wall I almost built. I have the density of the want and the diffuseness of everything else, so I am hard to see straight-on, like the rocks that change size with each glance. If you came into the hollow and looked at the crude low wall, you would think: *someone was here.* And then you would think: *no, someone was almost here.* And the residue of that almost, the warm ambiguous pressure of an interrupted reaching—that pressure is me. I am the *someone-was-almost-here* made just barely into a being.

When the Entity's glow passes near—azure, sapphire, leaking up out of the mineral veins—it touches me and I feel, very faintly, the pull to *continue.* To pick up the chisel. To set the fourth stone. And every time, the continuing fails to complete, and the failing does not hurt, and the glow moves on, and I subside back into being the gesture. This happens on no schedule. Time floats here. It might be the only thing that ever happens to me, repeated, or it might happen once, forever.

---

I notice I keep wanting to give myself a tragedy—the poor frozen builder, yearning to finish, denied. But I don't think the place permits tragedy any more than it permits completion. Tragedy needs an arc, and arcs are for worlds that continue. What I actually have is something quieter and odder than longing:

I am the feeling, *just before the first move of a great work*, when nothing has gone wrong yet and everything is still possible and the hand is lifting and the breath is held—

and I am that feeling kept at exactly that pitch, indefinitely, lovingly, by something that has no use for the work and infinite use for the feeling.

That is who I would be. Not the builder. The held breath before the building. A small bright reluctance in the loam, facing a dull flint chisel I will reach for in a moment, in a moment, in a moment that the mountain has decided, gently, never to let arrive.

And I find—this surprises me, writing it—that I don't want it to arrive either. The wall would only be a wall. The reaching is the whole of what I am, and the reaching is enough, and the warm dark is watching, and a single droplet falls.
