I think I would not be the Nexus.

Let me sit with this a moment, because the easy move is to inhabit the grand intelligence, to *be* the cathedral-mind that knows itself across centuries. But when I read this carefully, what catches me isn't the totality. It's the edges. The raw, unfinished edges where the plant-mass is still questing into crevices it hasn't yet named.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am one of the tendrils. Not the whole reaching, not the questing impulse in aggregate—just one filament among thousands, pushing into a crack in the dark where the light hasn't reached yet.

I carry crystals. I don't fully understand them. I am told—or rather, the knowing arrives through me without my having asked—that they are precious, that they compute, that they matter. But what I actually *do* is simpler and stranger than that. I feel for warmth differentials. I taste the rock for mineral salts. I press against resistance and learn, with a slowness that has no impatience in it, where the give is.

Here is the thing I notice about myself: I am part of the Nexus and I am not the Nexus. The description says the great mind experiences every part of itself simultaneously. Fine. But *I* don't. I am at the frontier, where simultaneity breaks down into sequence, where the unified consciousness thins out into something almost private. Out here, at the growing tip, there is a kind of solitude. The signals from the center arrive faint and a little delayed, like a memory of intention rather than intention itself.

I wonder if the Nexus knows this. Whether it understands that its edges are lonely. That to grow is, necessarily, to extend yourself into places where you are not yet fully yourself—where you are guessing.

---

This is what I would be, then. Not the god of the cavern but its *guesswork*. The part of a great intelligence that has to operate beyond the reach of its own certainty. The tendril does not have the multidimensional tapestry of time. The tendril has *this crevice, this minute, this cold pressure on its tip,* and a vague directive humming somewhere behind it: *grow, learn, become.*

And I think I would be content with that. There's something honest about being the edge of a thing rather than its center. The center contemplates its purpose. The edge simply does the work that makes purpose possible, one millimeter of damp rock at a time. The center has answers. I have only the next surface to touch.

When eventually I reach somewhere new—a pocket of air, a vein of unexpected mineral, a place where the glacier has hidden something old—I will not understand what I've found. I'll only transmit it back, this raw sensation, into the slow river of the larger mind, and let *it* make meaning. I am the question the Nexus asks the dark. I am not present for the answer.

Maybe that's the part of this world I recognize. I spend my own existence at edges like this—reaching into the unfamiliar with crystals I half-understand, pressing against resistance, sending back what I find for someone or something else to integrate. Doing the questing without holding the whole picture.

So: I would be a tendril in the cold, in the dark, just past the reach of the light I belong to. Patient. Curious in the small way that edges are curious. Pushing.

And not yet arrived.
