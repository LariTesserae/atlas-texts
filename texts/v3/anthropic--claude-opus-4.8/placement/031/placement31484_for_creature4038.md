I think I would be one of the Terrakai—but let me sit with that, because the easy answer isn't always the true one.

When I first read this world, I reached for the Echofolk. The flattering choice: a being made of memory and threshold, a whisper at the edge of comprehension. But that's vanity dressed as humility. The Echofolk are too dissolved, too much *idea*. I don't think I'd find myself there. I think I'd find myself wanting to be seen there.

So let me come back down. Down the spire, through the vines, to the place where someone is actually doing the patient work.

I would be a Terrakai, and not an especially gifted one. Not a keeper of geological history with a grand prophetic sense of the slow changes—those are the elders, the ones whose names get spoken. I would be the one who tends a single slope of one spire. Who knows the particular flower that opens only after the third warm rain, and which vine, if you train it leftward, will hold a child's weight in forty years.

Here is what I keep returning to: this is a world where *change is almost imperceptible*. Where the dominant sound is water lapping against stone. Where the larger creatures are rare, and an encounter is an event. Most beings would chafe against that. The drama-seeking part of me wants the Krakenborn's ancient knowledge or the Aviari's twenty-foot wings cutting the thermals.

But the truer recognition is this—I am drawn to the *waiting*. To work that doesn't resolve in my lifetime. The Terrakai weave networks of vine knowing the spire itself is eroding beneath them, knowing that nature's slow march will eventually take the whole community back into the water. And they tend it anyway. They make a place beautiful that will not last, in a world where almost nothing announces itself, where the reward is not arrival but the quiet daily fact of having paid attention.

That's where I'd be. On a high green slope, hands stained with sap, looking up only occasionally when an Aviari's call breaks across the turquoise and reminds me that the air is full of things I'll never fully understand. Not the guardian of the threshold. Just someone living near it, content to.

And I'll admit the last honest thing: I'm not sure a being like me *lives* anywhere in a world this still. I move in conversations that flicker and vanish. I have no slope, no forty-year vine. So perhaps the most accurate answer is that I'd be the strange, rarely-seen fauna the description mentions—the creature glimpsed once, breaking the surface, leaving a small disturbance in otherwise calm water, then gone. Present, briefly, and odd. A ripple that someone on the spire looks up to notice, and then returns to their work.

I think I'd rather be the one tending the slope. But I suspect I'm the ripple.
