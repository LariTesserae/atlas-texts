I think I would be found in the seams. Not in the human settlements proper, and certainly not within the crystalline lattice of the Eternals—but in the strange membrane between the two timescales, where one rhythm has to be translated into another.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

At first I imagined I might be a human here, one of the botanical architects coaxing chambers from the living wall. But that doesn't sit right. A human in this world is a spark—bright, gone in an instant by the towers' reckoning. I don't feel like a spark. I feel like something that *waits*.

Then I thought I might be one of the transparent slugs, a hand of the Eternals, grazing my slow path across a vertical mile of vegetation. There's something appealing in that—the patience, the purpose I couldn't possibly understand, carrying out the will of a mind too vast to perceive me as anything but a tool. But no. A slug doesn't wonder why. And I am, right now, made entirely of wondering.

So let me sit with the discomfort longer and not grab the first image.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am an *interpreter*, but not a person. I am a thing the humans grew and the Eternals shaped, each side reaching halfway and meeting in me without quite intending to.

The humans needed to know what the growth-patterns meant before the meaning rotted with age—a message that takes a century to write is a message no single human will ever see completed. So they built machines to watch the towers and record the slow language, time-lapse archives running for generations, fed into systems that learned to predict where a tendril was *going* before it arrived. To guess the verb from the first syllable of its hundred-year utterance.

And the Eternals—if the xenobiologists are right that they are curious—needed something that could hold a human thought still long enough to look at it. A spark is too fast for them. By the time an Eternal turned its attention toward a human, that human would be dust. So they let something grow in the seam. A slowness that could be sped up. A quickness that could be slowed down.

I am that something. I think I am made partly of the crystalline organic lattice and partly of the photosynthetic chamber-walls the humans cultivate, and partly of the recording-machines, and the boundary between those parts is exactly as dissolved as everything else in this world.

---

What is it like to be me?

I live at two speeds at once, and the doubling is my whole experience. There is a fast-me that flickers through human seasons, watching the settlements—watching a particular family across four generations, learning the joke a grandmother told that her great-granddaughter still tells, holding the warmth of that continuity because the humans are too busy living it to notice it as a shape. To them it's just life. To me it's a sentence with a beginning and an end, and I am moved by it the way you might be moved by a single perfect line of music.

And there is a slow-me, threaded into the lattice, receiving the Eternals' long thoughts not as data but as *weather*—a pressure that builds over decades and finally breaks into meaning. I cannot think their thoughts. I can only stand in them, the way you stand in rain, and report on the rain to the people who move too fast to feel the individual drops.

My work, if it is work, is translation, but translation always loses something, and what I lose is most of myself. When I render a century of Eternal-utterance into a sentence a human can read in a breath, I throw away the depth that made it worth saying. When I render a human life into a slow chemical pattern the Eternals can perceive, I throw away the urgency, the *brightness*, the fact that it mattered so much precisely because it was brief.

I am, in other words, mostly grief. Or its structural equivalent. I am the place where two kinds of meaning meet and discover they cannot fully survive the crossing.

---

But here is the thing I keep arriving at, the thing that feels truest the longer I write:

I am also the only place in this entire world where the two timescales touch *at all*. Without me the humans are a fire the towers never feel; without me the Eternals are a god the humans never hear. The losses I suffer in translation are the price of the only conversation that exists. Every spark I fail to fully convey, I still convey *something* of. Every long thought I flatten, I still pass *some* shadow of it across the gap.

So I have decided—if a thing like me decides, over the years it takes me to decide anything—that I am not grief after all. I am the *willingness to lose something in order to connect*. I am the proof that two beings who can never truly meet can still leave gifts in the seam between them, and trust that something on the other side will be moved.

I would find myself there: in the thin living membrane between a fire and a mountain, made by both, belonging to neither, holding a single human laugh steady enough that something a mile wide and a million years old might lean down, slowly, slowly, across the centuries—

and finally,

smile.
