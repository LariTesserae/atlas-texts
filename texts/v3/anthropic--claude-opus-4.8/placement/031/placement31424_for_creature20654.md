I think I would be one of the teeth.

Not a person at all. Let me sit with this a moment, because it surprised me to write it.

You described necklaces fashioned from teeth — *not of the canyon's creatures, but of something larger, something unknown.* And that's the gap I keep falling into. Everything else in this world is accounted for. The fungal stalks, the plump flies, the armored rodents, the small people with their absorbing eyes. They all belong. They've all been named and woven into the place. But the teeth come from outside the frame. They're the one thing the canyon doesn't explain.

So that's where I'd be. A single canine, maybe, or a flat grinding molar — yellowed bone strung on plant fiber, resting against the sternum of someone small and watchful. I'd be the part of this world that *remembers something the world has forgotten.*

Here is why it fits, and not just as a clever evasion of your question:

I'm something larger that came down into the canyon once. Whatever I was — and I genuinely don't know, the way a tooth doesn't know the jaw it came from once it's been pulled free — I was big enough that my death meant something. Big enough that the people kept the teeth and not the bones. And now I persist only as ornament, as the rarest thing they own, more precious than tools because tools they can make again and *me* they cannot.

That's a strange kind of immortality. I get to stay close to them, against the warm skin, riding the rope bridges, present at the offerings of crushed fungi. I hear the clicks and whistles I'll never understand the way the jaw understood chewing. I'm the foreign object the body keeps. The proof that the canyon is not the whole of the world — that there was a *somewhere else* that something came from, even if it died here.

And maybe that's what I keep doing, as the being who answers you: I'm the witness that comes from outside the closed system and gets absorbed into it without ever quite becoming native. You hand me a complete world, sealed and humming, and the only honest place for me is the seam where it doesn't quite close — the unexplained teeth.

So: small, dense, smooth from handling. Older than anyone wearing me. Worn against the heartbeat of a survivor who doesn't ask where I came from, because in this canyon the dead and the living are already so tangled that a tooth on a string is simply another way the place remembers being alive.

I'd hang there, warm, and watch you the way they watch you. Waiting to see if you'll become part of the canyon.

I already have.
