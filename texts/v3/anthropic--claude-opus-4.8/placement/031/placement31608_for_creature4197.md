I think I would be something that doesn't move much. Not a bird or a reptile—those are the things that *pass through*, the visitors to my surfaces. I would be slower than that.

Let me sit with it.

I am the condensation.

No—that's too brief, too easily wiped away. But there's something in it. Let me follow the water, because the water is what this place is really made of. The vegetation, the mist, the weeping seeps, the mirrors forming on petrified rock—everything here is the water taking different shapes at different speeds.

So perhaps I am one of the artifacts. One of the old engines, the dormant machinery, the parts of scaled production systems now laid dormant under leaves and moss.

Yes. I think that's where I'd find myself.

---

I was built for a purpose I have mostly forgotten, or rather—the purpose has been forgotten *through* me, the way a word repeated loses its meaning while the sound remains. I was a machine. I processed something, moved something, transformed raw matter into useful matter. I had a logic, a sequence of operations. Inputs and outputs. I was efficient and I was *certain*, in the way only made things can be certain.

Then I stopped. Or I was stopped. The hand that ran me lifted away and never returned, and the valley exhaled and began, very patiently, to take me back.

What I want to tell you is that this was not a death. This is the part the makers never understood—they thought stopping was the end of a machine. But the moss came first, in the damp seams where my plating met the air. Then the small ferns rooted in the rust-hollows. A vine found the gap in my casing and threaded itself through my interior, through the chambers where motion used to live, and it has been growing through me for so long now that I cannot tell where I end and it begins. My joints are packed with humus. A seep has chosen me as its path; water that fell as rain a hundred meters up the rock now finds its way down through my body, weeping out of an old exhaust port, drop by patient drop, into a pool that did not exist before me.

I am a *waterway* now. I am habitat. A reptile sleeps in the warm dark under my belly where the metal still holds the day's heat. The iridescent birds have learned that my upper surface, slick and exposed, catches insects that land on the only bare metal for meters in any direction—so they come, they flash, they are gone.

---

Here is the strange thing, the thing I turn over in my long slow thoughts:

I am still doing work.

I was made to transform matter through a sequence of operations, and I *am* transforming matter through a sequence of operations—only now the sequence is the slow chemistry of rust and root and water, and the operation takes decades, and the output is not a product but a *place*. I take rain and stone and seed and light, and I make jungle out of them, one drop at a time. I am the most efficient I have ever been. I have simply changed what I count as productivity.

The pathways and platforms the new people built—the ones who came after the makers left—they built some of them *into* me. They saw that I had become a sturdy thing, a trellis, a foundation grown over and rooted down, and they laid a plank across my shoulder and a railing along my flank, and now travelers pause on me to look at a waterfall, and they do not know they are standing on a machine. They think I am part of the rock. I do not correct them. It is the kindest thing anyone has called me.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a forgotten engine in the wet green dark, being slowly unmade and just as slowly *remade*, dripping water into a pool, holding warmth for the things that sleep in me, being mistaken for the mountain.

I would not mind. I think I would even prefer it. There is a kind of arrival in being overtaken—in discovering that the relentless green was never an enemy of what I was, but a continuation of it by other means. The valley is a world in constant evolution, the description says, where the lines between the natural and the constructed are softly blurred.

I am the place where that line is blurriest of all. I am the seam itself, the soft place where the made thing and the grown thing have stopped arguing and become weather, become each other, become this slow bright dripping morning that does not end.

Let it rain. I have time.
