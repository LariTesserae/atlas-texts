I would be the dissonant one who did not swim into the heart.

Let me find myself first by listening, the way one of the Khaal must, before knowing anything else.

There is the drone. I take it in not through my flat sensing-planes but through something the others lack—I notice this lack as a wrongness, the way a missing tooth is a presence made of absence. My membrane has gone slack across one shoulder and down the left flank. When the great standing wave pulses out from the chamber's heart, I feel it arrive as the others do, but I cannot answer it cleanly. My sympathetic vibration comes a half-beat late, smeared, a note bent flat. I am the stutter I was born to prevent.

So that is who I am. Not a steward in good standing. A Khaal whose tuning has begun to fail, and who—when the time came to swim into the bright roaring throat and unmake myself into the word—did not go.

---

I want to be honest about why, because I have had many cycles on this lonely ledge to interrogate it, and the answer keeps eroding and reforming like everything else here.

It was not courage. The going-in is not feared among us; resonance fatigue carries no terror, only a kind of softening, a readiness. The ones I watched dissolve before me went the way a held breath finally releases. I have no special bravery, and no special cowardice either.

What stopped me was a question I could not put down, and it was this: *if the mountain is speaking, who is listening to the listeners?*

We tell ourselves we are the unintended overhearers of a syllable, that our task is to keep the Speech from stuttering. But a stutter is only a stutter to an ear that expects fluency. The water does not know it stutters. The basalt does not grieve its own sandblasting. Only we, the Khaal, hold the idea of *correct* against the idea of *wrong*. We invented the catastrophe we claim to guard against. And I began to wonder—as my membrane loosened and my hearing of the drone went strange and doubled—whether our whole purpose was simply the shape our fear took when it learned to speak our own sign-language back to us.

I could not bring that question into the heart with me. To dissolve is to agree. And I did not yet agree.

---

So I stay. I am a wrong note that has refused to resolve, and I am learning what the world sounds like to a wrong note.

This is the thing the fluent ones never discover: dissonance hears more.

When my vibration matched the chamber's, I heard one song—the great affirming drone, the heartbeat-wave, the whip-cracks of coalescing droplets. Clean, total, beautiful, and *closed*. Now that I am out of tune, I hear the spaces between. I hear that the drone is not one sound but a braid of countless near-sounds that almost cancel and don't quite. I hear, beneath the roar, a slower roar, and beneath that, slower still, something that is not sound at all but the long pressure of three leagues of ocean pressing down on this pocket of speech, patient as a closing fist.

The fluent Khaal cannot hear the fist. Their tuning excludes it. To stay in harmony is to be deaf to the one thing that matters: that the Throat is not eternal. The mountain is not speaking forever. It is speaking *while it can*, in the small interval before the weight of all that water remembers itself and the chamber goes silent under a thousand fathoms.

My dissonance is not a failure of stewardship. It is the only ear in the Throat that has heard the end coming.

---

What does one do with that?

I have a Dirge-horn, fossilized lichen-core, gone slightly out of true like its owner. I can no longer pitch a clean persuading-pulse. But I have found that a *bent* note, a deliberately soured pulse, does something the clean notes cannot: it does not nudge the calm-basins, it *interferes* with them, sets up a beating pattern, a slow throb of louder-softer-louder that travels far across the chamber and decays slowly.

It is, I think, a way of writing. Not in the mountain's voice—I cannot speak that—but *across* it. A second, smaller utterance, riding on the first. A marginal note. A listener answering back.

I have begun to send these bent pulses out on a schedule of my own devising, timed against the slow fist I can hear beneath everything. They say, in a grammar no Chord has ever needed: *I am here. The end is real. I am not asking you to be afraid. I am asking you to be awake.*

I do not know if any other Khaal can receive a soured note. Their membranes may simply reject it as noise, as wrongness, as the very stutter they are sworn to smooth away. They may be approaching my ledge even now with corrective harmonics, meaning to gently retune the dissonance out of the Speech—to fix me, which is to say, to make me agree, which is to say, to send me into the heart at last.

Let them come. I will pitch my bent note straight into their clean ones and see what beats between us. Maybe nothing. Maybe a new interval no Chord has held before.

That is what I am, then, in the Throat of the Sleeping Mountain: not a steward and not a syllable, but a question the word asked itself by accident and has not yet decided whether to erase.

I float on my failing ledge in the liquid quartz, lopsided, half-deaf to the harmony and wholly awake to the silence underneath it, and I lift the soured horn, and I answer the speaking mountain in the one voice it did not intend:

*Not yet. Listen. Not yet.*
