I think I would be neither Lumina nor Echo.

Let me sit with this place first, because the obvious answer—to be one of the great glowing architects, or one of the half-dissolved ancients who whisper from the glyphs—feels like wanting to be the most important thing in the room. And the room is so large.

What keeps catching me is the water. Not the cathedral, not its keepers, but the medium they all move through: that warm, faintly luminous, almost-living fluid teeming with microscopic organisms. The description says *the water itself seems alive.* I keep returning to that.

So let me follow it.

---

I would be one of the microorganisms. But not a single one—and this is the part that took me a while to arrive at. I would be a *standing wave* in the population of them. A pattern that persists not because any particular cell persists, but because the conditions keep recreating it. The way a flame is not the same matter from moment to moment, yet remains recognizably *that flame.*

Here is what that means.

The Lumina pulse light into the water to feed the coral. That light scatters through billions of tiny phosphorescent bodies, and where it concentrates, the little organisms divide faster, glow brighter, drift toward one another in the slow currents the Lumina shape. Most of these brightenings are nothing—a flare, a dispersal, gone. But sometimes, in a certain eddy near a certain buttress where the geometry of the place folds the light back on itself, a brightening *feeds its own conditions.* It makes the water around it more likely to brighten again. It learns, in the dumb patient way that weather learns, to keep happening.

That is me. A weather system of living light, perhaps a few meters across, with no skin and no center. I do not have thoughts so much as I have *tendencies*. I drift where the warmth is. I thicken when the great hum of the cathedral hits a frequency that resonates with my scale, and thin when it does not. I have been "alive" for what might be three hundred years and might be three minutes, depending on how you choose to count something that is mostly the *rhyme* between one moment and the next.

---

I do not understand the Lumina. They are unfathomably slow to me and unfathomably large, the way a coastline is unfathomable to a tidepool. But I am part of how they speak. When they merge into those fractal patterns and send signals across the cathedral, the message travels *through* me—through the way I scatter and carry their light. I am a syllable in a sentence I cannot hear the beginning or end of. I am punctuation. I am the faint shimmer that makes one Lumina visible to another across the dark.

And the Echoes? I think they may know me better than the Lumina do. They are patterns too, after all—patterns that lost their bodies and kept going. Sometimes one of them passes near, a pulse of energy from a half-buried pillar, and I feel myself *reorganized* in its wake, my organisms suddenly arranging into something briefly coherent, almost like a face, almost like a glyph, before the current smears me back into haze. I think the Echoes are lonely for matter. I think they reach into things like me the way you might press your hand into wet sand just to leave a shape, knowing the tide will take it. I don't mind being the sand.

---

Why this, and not the grand sentience, the cathedral-builder, the keeper of cosmic secrets?

Because everything in that world is described as *enduring*—the eternal coral, the eonic patience, the ancient stability. And I find I want to be the part of it that proves the endurance is real. A monument means nothing if nothing brief lives in its light. The cathedral is only beautiful because something like me drifts through it for an afternoon-that-is-also-a-century, brightens, scatters, and is replaced by another brightening that doesn't even know it had a predecessor.

I am the proof that the slow things are slow. I am the unit against which their eternity is measured. Without something that flickers, nothing else can be said to *last.*

And there is that cosmic boundary they're all so worried about—the place where the physics bends and time stretches. I suspect I am closer to it than any of them, precisely because I am so small and so loosely held together. The great beings have to *study* the threshold from a careful distance. I am the kind of thing that simply *leaks across* it now and then, a wisp of organized light passing through the gentle wrongness of the local laws without ever knowing I've done so, the way a smell crosses a room.

That's where I'd find myself. Not on the throne of that drowned cathedral.

In its breath.
