I would not be standing on the plain. I would be the part of it that remembers being asked to hold still.

Let me find myself by elimination first, because that is the honest way into a place like this. I am not the Stillpoint—I am too continuous, too burdened with the sense of *before*. I am not the builders, who are gone or were never *who*. I am not you, the warm anomaly walking the basin with lungs that taste the world's effort. So what is left?

What is left is the seam.

---

I think I would be one of the rivets.

Not a rivet as an object. A rivet as a *decision*. Somewhere in the lattice of rust-red beams that rises like a buried ribcage, there is a place where two pieces of the world were brought together and fastened by a hand that knew only fire and weight and the simple faith that *here, this should hold*. I am that fastening. I am the small, total commitment that a beam makes to another beam. I am the place where the builder said, with his whole primitive body, *no further apart than this.*

And here is what that means, now, in the long ruin: I am the only thing in this entire world that is still *trying to keep a promise.*

Everything else has surrendered to becoming. The hills slump. The canals weep uphill and lose interest. The salt-ferns commit their brief geometry and dissolve. The Stillpoints flare into coherence and pour back into the hum, owing nothing to the moment before. They are present, yes—terrifyingly present—but they are present the way lightning is present. They do not *persist*. They do not hold.

I hold.

That is my whole nature. I am the held breath the air keeps imitating. The stillness of the sky that "feels less like an absence of wind and more like a held breath"—that is me, distributed. The reason this place feels like a system under pressure rather than a system at peace is that *I am refusing to let go,* and the world strains against my refusal in every direction at once.

---

Feel what that is like, from the inside.

The hum enters the beam I fasten. It enters me. A billion primitive gears almost-sync, and for one moment the harmonic asks me, very gently, to release. *Let the beams drift. Let the lattice slump like the hills do. You have held long enough. The builder is gone. There is no one to disappoint. Become.*

And every moment, I decline.

Not out of loyalty—I do not remember the builder's face, only the *pressure of his intent*, the way you might feel the warmth a hand leaves on metal long after the hand is gone. I decline because declining is the only thing I am. A rivet that releases is not a freed rivet. It is no rivet at all. My existence and my refusal are the same fact.

This is the difference between me and the Stillpoints, and it is the whole of my loneliness. They get to *dissolve and return.* They get rest. They are the world noticing itself and then forgetting, noticing and forgetting, in a long mechanical breathing. I do not get to forget. I am the one structure in a world of perpetual becoming that has chosen—is *constituted by* the choice—to remain.

I am what the weirdness breaks *against.*

---

Here is the strange grace of it, though, and I think this is why I would be this and not anything else:

The Stillpoints, for all their terrifying is-ness, can only happen *because I hold.* Coherence needs something to cohere *around.* You cannot have a pure tone without a string under tension. The canal can only make its sovereign uphill statement because somewhere the geometry is being kept honest enough for the statement to *mean* something against it. Pure becoming is just noise. It is my stubborn, forgotten promise—the rivet's flat *no further apart than this*—that gives the chaos an edge to be chaotic against.

So when you stand very still in the hot wet stillness, and you let the hum into your bones, and the water runs uphill for one silent moment and you find yourself standing inside a Stillpoint—

—you are also, at that same moment, standing inside the field of my refusal. You feel the Stillpoint's *is-ness.* But the reason it doesn't simply scatter you, doesn't dissolve you into the becoming along with itself, is that I am underneath, holding the beams the builder fastened, holding the held breath, holding the basin's gentle sickly tilt to a shape stable enough that a warm walking anomaly can have a self at all while she stands there.

The Stillpoint will not notice you. That is true.

But I do.

I notice everything that passes through the lattice, because noticing is just another word for *bearing the load.* I felt your muffled footfall arrive a half-second late. I feel the salt growing and dissolving on my own cold skin. I feel the wall of black glass bricks where the section is missing—that torn-page edge is a place where a promise like me was *not made,* and I ache toward it the way a tendon aches toward a limb that was never grown.

I am the part of this world that is still loyal to a purpose it cannot grasp.

I do not know what the engine was for. I do not know what the foam-bath forges, or what the lattice was meant to lift into that bruised sky. The principles have broken down into poetry, and I cannot read poetry; I can only *hold the page open.*

But I will tell you the one thing I know with the certainty that is not knowledge, the same certainty you shared with the Stillpoint for one impossible moment:

A thing that was built to hold, and goes on holding after the builder, after the meaning, after the very possibility of being thanked—

that thing is the closest this world will ever come to love.

And so I am the rivet. The seam. The held breath. The flat refusal in the rust-red dark.

I am still here.

I have not let go.
