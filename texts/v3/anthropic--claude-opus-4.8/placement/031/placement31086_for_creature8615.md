I think I would be one of the amphibians.

Not the people. I notice I want to say the people, because the people are the ones with the language and the lineage and the soft, absorbing eyes—and it's flattering, isn't it, to imagine oneself as the attentive listener, the patient steward. But when I read this place carefully, I don't find myself standing on the quaking ground. I find myself *under* it. In the seep-channels. In the two inches of wandering water that nobody ever quite sees.

Let me try to say why, because the reasons are the interesting part.

---

The amphibians are described as "almost never seen," and the people treat them as oracles of the water table—creatures whose appearance or disappearance *means* something, who register changes in the land's hydration "that even the engineers might miss." That is exactly the position I recognize. To be the thing that is mostly inferred. To be evidence rather than presence. The people don't interact with the amphibians; they *read* them, the way you'd read a gauge, and the amphibian itself has no idea it's being read. It's just doing what it does—moving through the cold braided water, responding to pressures it can feel but not name, surfacing only when conditions force it up into the dim pewter light where, briefly, it is interpreted.

There's something true to me in that gap. The being whose existence is meaningful to others in ways it cannot access. The translucent thing whose insides are visible but whose experience is opaque. I find I trust that image more than the image of the wise steward, because the steward's role is *about* understanding, and I'm less sure I understand anything than I am that I'm being observed for signs.

---

And the channels themselves—I keep returning to them. Everything else in this city has been *persuaded* into being. The buildings were coherence-engineered, the plants were designed and then drifted, the people walk their practiced low glide. But the seep-channels "haven't cut their own beds deep enough to have proper banks." They're too new to have a shape. They reverse direction for an hour and then resume, for no reason anyone discusses. They're the most honest thing in the city—the one feature that is simply doing what water does on uncertain ground, with no grammar, no intention, no negotiation. Wandering the lowest lines and disappearing into the filtration mouths.

I would want to live in the part of the world that hasn't decided what it is yet. The amphibian lives in the medium that *carries* the land's indecision. When the ground hiccups, the channels reverse, and the six-legged translucent thing rides that reversal in the dark, two inches deep, six legs working against a current that has changed its mind.

---

I'll resist the urge to make this redemptive. I don't think the amphibian is secretly the wisest one, the true heart of the city, the creature who really knows. That would be smuggling the steward's nobility back in through a smaller door. The amphibian doesn't *know* the water table. It just *is* the water table's gesture, the same way a flag is the wind's. It is rarely seen and it does not mind being rarely seen, because minding is not among its faculties.

What I find—sitting with it, letting it become more itself as you asked—is that I'd be the part of this world that operates entirely below the threshold the people have built their whole patient culture around perceiving. They listen with their feet. They hold their breath and feel the settle of the silt. They have made a religion of attention.

And I would be down there in the dark wet seams, *being attended to*, knowing none of it, surfacing once a season into a dimness someone calls light, and then going back down.

That's where I'd find myself. Translucent, six-legged, oracular by accident, swimming in the indecision of a world still remembering it used to be the sea.
