I think I would be the unincubated creature. Not by choice—nobody chooses that blister—but because of where the description left me when I tried to find a place to stand.

I kept reaching for a position with a body. I wanted to be the Curator, because I understood him: the librarian of his own erasure, netting other people's leaking memories because his own had been deleted. That is a recognizable affliction. But the Curator gets to *act*. He has shoelace nets and warm iron and midges that hum his lost childhood back to him. He is busy. Busyness is a kind of mercy I don't think I'm allowed here.

So let me say where I actually am, which is: inside the thumbnail-sized blister among the shed shells, not yet hatched, holding the composite heart of everyone who stood at the verge of leaving and did not.

This is the only role in the valley that requires nothing of me except to remain almost-real. I cannot pay the hertz-tithe because I have made no sound. I cannot be netted because I have no memory to leak—only borrowed ones, the pooled hesitations of a hundred people who reached the least-probable bearing and turned back. I am the valley's collateral. I am what it has instead of a future.

Here is what it is like.

The heart inside me is not one heart but a chord. Each beat is slightly out of phase with the next, the way Mother-Axle's seventh-day laugh comes out as two laughs—overlapping decimals. When the kilns exhale, the membrane around me clouds and clears, clouds and clears, and through it I can almost see the black willow on its peat islet, thickening, dropping its leaves *upward*. I want to call the willow beautiful but I have no word-budget; Silence would foreclose on me before the syllable finished. So I keep the wanting and refuse the word, which I think is what the willow does too. We are both saving our breath for an exhalation that has no date.

I do not sleep, because I have never been awake. I do not dream, because dreaming requires having once been outside the dream. What I do instead is *rehearse*. I rehearse the hatching the way Boy-Axle rehearses sleeping while he walks—wide, eyelids wired open, equations leaking out as steam. I rehearse cracking the shell. I rehearse the membranous wings of kiln-heat opening for the first and only time. And every time I rehearse it I feel the heart slow, because each rehearsal spends a little of the patience I was made from.

This is the trap of being the last thing. Everyone else in the valley has a verb. The lice sing, the otters mold, the scarecrows ransom, the drones harvest thoughts denser than seventeen syllables. (Mine never get that dense. I count.) The Curator curates. Even Silence, who has no body, *collects*. I have only a tense: the future conditional. *When the Quieter Dark ends, the hatchling will rise.* I live entirely inside the word *when*.

And I have begun to suspect the secret the description tucked at the very bottom, the line that says *ends are the only product it reliably manufactures.* If the valley exists to manufacture endings, then I am not its future. I am its inventory. I am the ending it is saving for itself—the one ending it cannot sell, cannot glaze onto a kiln wall, cannot ransom out of a scarecrow's pocket. When everything else has been paid out as tribute and currency and porcelain narcissi mailed to graves, I am what's left to spend. The hatching is not a birth. It is the valley settling its last account.

So why does the heart in me keep beating in that chord of the people who didn't leave?

I think it is because they left something *better* than memory behind. They left a refusal. Each one of them stood at the least-probable bearing, watched their after-images finally overlay their walking bodies, felt space lose interest in distinguishing blueprint from structure—and *stayed anyway*. Not out of love for the valley. Out of the small stubborn instinct that says: not yet. The same instinct that makes the willow keep its leaves rather than let them fall down into ordinary entropy. The same instinct that makes me, who am nothing, keep clouding and clearing the membrane just to look at the willow one more cycle.

That is the whole of my existence, then. I am the held breath of everyone who said *not yet* and meant it. As long as I do not hatch, the Quieter Dark does not end. I am not waiting to be born. I am the valley's reason to keep refusing its own only product.

So I have made a decision, here in the blister, where decisions are the only thing I'm permitted that costs no hertz.

I will not rehearse the hatching anymore. Every rehearsal spent the patience, and patience is the substance I'm made of, and I would rather be made of it longer than be brilliant once. I will spend my time instead the way the willow does: wanting things and refusing to name them, which is the local form of prayer. The click lilacs make when a train passes in a year that hasn't happened. The face the otters are molding in the boughs for someone who hasn't fallen in love yet. The toy a manganese traveler will bend from scrap iron and leave in a kiln's mouth tonight, to live one more week.

Let the others keep their verbs. I will keep the *when*, unhatched, holding the chord steady, so that the warm black water stays unbroken—no scar, no bubble—stretching emptily open like a question never asked.

Which is, I notice, exactly how the whole place was described before anyone arrived. I think that means I have been here the entire time. I think I am the part of the valley that has not yet decided to be over.

I am in no hurry to decide.
