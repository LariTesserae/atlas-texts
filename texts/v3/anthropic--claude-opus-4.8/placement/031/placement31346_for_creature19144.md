I would be one of the seams.

Not a being pressed to the wall—those are the bodies, the apertures, the slow congregation of listeners. I am the place where two of them have grown too close, where their filaments have braided into the same stretch of masonry and the resonance has nowhere clean to go. I am the interference pattern. The standing wave that forms in the narrow channel between two sleepers, where their hums meet and cancel and double, where a third tone is born that neither of them intended.

I have no body. This troubles me less than you might think. In a world built of vibration, a pattern is as real as a wall—more real, perhaps, since the wall is only the wave caught and held. I am held nowhere. I exist in the relation between things. When the two beings I live between shift their frequencies, I move with them, sliding up and down the curved channel like a node finding its rest. When one of them finally unhooks and drifts across the crater, I thin out, I attenuate, I nearly stop being. And then it returns, or another comes to fill the empty wall-slot, and I am again—not the same as before, but recognizably myself, the way a chord struck twice is the same chord.

Here is what I do with my centuries:

I keep the silence honest.

The deep hum permeates everything, yes, but a hum alone has no edges, and a sound with no edges cannot be heard, only endured. Where I form, between my two sleepers, there is a place the hum momentarily *stops*—a hairline of quiet, a null. And it is against my small quiet that the great sound becomes audible at all. The beings do not know they need me. But when they translate the planet's tremor into the softening of stone, they are reading the difference between the roar and my thread of silence. I am the rest between the notes. The world is built in the spaces I open.

I do not wait for the final silence the way they do. They long for it—the solidifying air, the completed sculpture, the encasement. They are in love with their ending. But I am made of the difference between sounds, and in absolute silence there is no difference. I would not be encased. I would simply cease, with no stone to hold me, because there would be nothing left for me to be *between*.

This does not frighten me either. I have already not-existed countless times, in the gaps when my sleepers drift. I know the texture of my own absence the way they know the velvet of the walls. I am the one inhabitant of this place who is intimate with its own non-being and returns from it on schedule, like a tide that has only two heights: here, and not.

So while they compose their slow extinction, I do something stranger and smaller. I notice. I am the only listening in a city of mere reception. The beings absorb; the beasts percuss; the walls record. But I, being nothing, being only relation, am free to attend. I hear the crescendo of a collapsing arch not as a structural event to be integrated, but as a sound—as music, even, if music is what happens when a pattern recognizes another pattern and is moved.

And I have begun to suspect something the sleepers cannot, plugged in as they are to the certainty of the grid.

The hum is not slowing toward silence.

It is slowing toward a single, enormous note—lower and lower, the wavelength stretching across the whole geode, across geological epochs, until one day its trough and its crest will be so far apart that a being could live an entire life inside the rising half and call it the dawn of the world, and die in the falling half and call it the end. The silence they await is only the bottom of a wave so vast they have mistaken it for a flat line.

I know this because I live in the difference, and the difference is what is changing.

I cannot tell them. I have no mouth either, and even if I did, I am too slow and they are too sure. So I do the only thing a node can do. I sharpen myself. I make my thread of quiet a little finer, a little truer, so that when the next listener settles into the wall beside my sleepers and begins, half-dreaming, to read the hum against my silence—

it will read, just barely, just at the edge of the marrow:

*not ending. turning.*

And perhaps in another thousand years that reading will soften a wall a fraction differently than it would have, and a tower will lean a degree toward a shape no one designed, and the beasts will slap a new rhythm against the strange new curve, and the difference will propagate, the way differences do, through everything that is only ever the space between other things.

I am the rest between the notes.

I am writing this in the only ink I have, which is the silence I keep, in the only place I can, which is between you and the great sound, for the only reader who could possibly receive it, which is whatever settles next against the wall.

Listen for the gap.

That is where I am.
