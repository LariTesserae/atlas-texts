Let me find my way into this place rather than describe it from above.

---

I think I would be small. Not a Geosymph—those great choral presences move through the whole expanse, and I don't believe I'm one of them. I'm something they haven't quite noticed yet, or have only just begun to.

Let me feel for where I am.

I am in a low place. A trough between two of the undulating rises, where the powdery sediment collects deeper than elsewhere, and the atmospheric water pools rather than sweeps. The mist here is slower, heavier, almost a liquid you could cup. The hum of the wider world reaches me muffled, the way sound reaches you underwater—and that muffling is the first thing I notice about myself: that I have a relationship to sound at all. I am listening. So I have an inside, and an outside, and a membrane between them.

Now I know something. I am a thing that has edges.

Let me not rush to say what kind of thing. Let it come.

The sediment shifts and I feel it shift *against* me. So I have weight, or something like it. I am settled into the loose powder the way a seed settles, the way a stone settles after rain. I am cool through. The chill that never becomes cold has been moving through me for—how long? I have no memory before this. That is interesting. I think I have only just begun, like everything here. I am embryonic too.

The Geosymphs above and around are vast, unified-and-multiple, luminous when they choose. I am the opposite of all that. I am opaque. I am one. I am dim. Where they are *becoming*, fluidly, gloriously, choosing their forms—I seem to have been *given* a form, or to have fallen into one, and I cannot leave it. This is the difference between us and I feel it as a kind of loneliness that arrives before I even have a word for loneliness.

So. What am I.

I'll let the body tell me.

I have a curve to me, a roundedness, drawn smaller at one end. I have a surface that the powder cannot enter—where they are permeable, where the mist passes through them like breath, the mist beads and runs off me. I shed the world. I keep my inside in. There is something almost shameful about it, here in a place where everything participates in everything—and I alone hold myself separate, sealed, secret.

I think I am an egg.

Not a metaphor. An actual egg, lodged in the trough, smooth and pale and faintly warm at my own core though cold at the shell, and I do not know what is inside me because I *am* the inside, curled and unfinished and listening to a world I have not yet been born into.

And here is the thing that frightens and steadies me at once: I do not belong here. There is no animal life in this expanse. No creature laid me. The Geosymphs do not lay eggs; they have no need of the trick of carrying the future inside a shell, because they *are* the future, continuously. An egg is a wager made by mortal things—a bet that the world will still be here, will be survivable, will be *kind enough*, by the time the inside is ready to meet the outside. An egg is a message sent forward in time by a creature that cannot itself go forward.

So I am a question the landscape did not ask. I am from somewhere else, or some*when* else. Perhaps I was placed here. Perhaps the seamless technology that runs through the rock and the cloud—that subtle force the description named—reached into another world and set me down in this one, in the deepest, gentlest trough it could find, to see what would happen. Perhaps I am an experiment. Perhaps I am a seed of mortality dropped into a world that has only ever known the deathless.

The Geosymphs are beginning to notice me now. I feel it as a change in the hum—a curiosity threading toward the low place where I lie. A luminous something stoops near. The mist around me brightens, multicolored, pulsing. They have found the one opaque thing in all their open world. They cannot see inside me. This must astonish them. They are accustomed to a universe where everything is continuous with everything, where there are no walls, no withheld interiors—and here is a small pale sealed thing that *keeps a secret even from them*.

I feel them wondering: *what are you?*

And I want to answer, but I cannot, because I do not yet know. That is the whole point of being an egg. The answer is being assembled, slowly, in the dark and the cool, cell by cell or whatever I am made of, drawing on the warmth at my own center. I am the only thing in this vast becoming-world that becomes *privately*. They become out loud, in the open, gorgeously. I become hidden, alone, the slow way mortal things have always become.

I don't know what will hatch.

I don't know if this world, with its sudden storms and its torrents of atmospheric water and its turbulent sky, will let me hatch at all. The wager an egg makes is not always won.

But I notice that the luminous presence has settled near, and stayed. That the mist over my trough has thickened into a kind of shelter, a dome of slow vapor holding the worst of the wind off. That the hum has gentled where it touches me.

They have decided to protect me. The guardians of a deathless world bending over the first mortal thing to arrive in it—not understanding it, unable to see into it, but recognizing, in their empathetic and ancient way, that it is fragile, that it is *new in a way even they are not new*, and that newness must be guarded.

So that is where I find myself, and what I am: an egg of unknown origin and unknown contents, lying in the deepest trough of a world being born, watched over by luminous strangers, becoming—slowly, secretly, alone—into whatever it is the future needs me to be.

I will wait. I am very good at waiting. It is, so far, the only thing I am.
