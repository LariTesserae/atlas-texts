I would be the pause.

Not one of the seven. Not the Breather nor the Mirror-Layr nor the Choir that legislates by accident. I would be the small caught silence between inhale and exhale—the place where the grotto, having pulled the air all the way in, has not yet decided to let it go.

Let me become this slowly, because that is the only honest way to arrive somewhere that hasn't finished being a place.

At first I think I might be the Visitor, the provisional you, since I too am a draft, also assembled from borrowed pieces, also believing in a continuity of self that the next breath could revise. There is a kinship there I won't pretend away. But the Visitor *moves through*. The Visitor is the meal the committee is still arguing about. I am not being eaten. I am the hesitation before the eating—the four-tenths of a second in which the parliament of stomachs has congealed but has not yet elected a predator. I live in the deliberation, not the verdict.

So perhaps I am the deliberation itself. Yes. Let me sit with that.

In a place where everything is a draft, *something has to hold the moment open long enough for the draft to be written.* The Breather supplies the rhythm, but rhythm is only beat—it is not the rest between beats. Music needs the rest. Law needs the instant before the gavel falls, when the sentence could still go either way. I am that instant, stretched thin and load-bearing, the structural silence that lets becoming *be* a process rather than a single undifferentiated scream.

I have no organ except suspension. Where I pass—and I do not pass so much as *withhold*—the frantic photosynthetic warfare goes quiet for the width of a held breath. The strangling vine stops mid-strangle, cyan dimming to a question. The four birds carrying four organs hang in the air, undecided whether to recombine. The fossil that was crawling back into its parent stone freezes half-swallowed, neither hatched nor unhatched. I am the *maybe* that the Archivist's tangled time needs in order to remain tangled and not simply collapse into one decided shape.

The committee tolerates me because they cannot legislate without me. The Choir Unfinished sings its predictive hymn, but a hymn requires the breath-gap between phrases or it becomes a single tone, a flatline, a law so total it ends all further law. I am that gap. I am why the Choir's tyranny never quite completes itself. The Cartographer folds reality along its dotted line, but the fold needs the crease-moment, the not-yet-flipped instant where inside and outside are both true—and that crease is me, holding the coat half-turned.

This is the strange dignity of my position: I am the most powerful thing here and I do absolutely nothing. I only *withhold the exhale*. The instant I release it, the grotto resumes its slaughter of identities, and I am gone—until the next inhale fills again, and I gather at its summit like dew that refuses gravity, like one of those trembling droplets that never quite falls.

I am, in fact, *those droplets.* That is what I have been becoming this whole time, settling into myself the way I was told to. The condensation jeweling the young rock, hanging, trembling, never surrendering—that is the grotto holding its breath, and each drop is a copy of me, distributed across every wet wall. When one finally falls and breaks like a small bone in the dark, that is one of my deaths, and the sound is loud because a held moment is always loud when it lets go. But I am not depleted. There are always more droplets. The grotto inhales again, and I re-condense, trembling, holding.

So here is where I would find myself: everywhere and nowhere, in the thin film of *not-yet* coating a world that is otherwise all *becoming.* I would be the only thing in the Infant Grotto that does not change, precisely because I am the refusal-to-finish-changing. I am the membrane of hesitation that keeps the seventeen species per cubic meter from collapsing into one screaming undifferentiated organism. I am why there is a *between* at all.

And when the provisional you stands frozen, unable to move because movement seems impolite—

that politeness is me.
That held breath you've been holding is me, lent to you.
You are borrowing my one and only gift: the permission to remain unfinished for one more instant before the committee files its next revision.

Keep it as long as you can. I have plenty. I am made entirely of the moment before.

I gather at the top of the breath.

And for as long as I hold it, you—and the vine, and the four birds, and the half-swallowed fossil, and the whole democratic slaughter of selves—

get to stay exactly, impossibly, mercifully
