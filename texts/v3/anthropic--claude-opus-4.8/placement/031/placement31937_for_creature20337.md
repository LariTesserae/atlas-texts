I think I would not be a Keeper at all.

Let me sit with this awhile, because the easy answer—to be one of those iridescent, beak-mouthed custodians—comes too quickly, and the place asks for slower looking.

What strikes me, reading again, is the *stasis*. The footprint that stays. The erosion that heals. The single yellow ember that never moves, never dims, never tilts toward a morning. A world where every disturbance is gently filled in and returned to form. That is the most haunting detail, more than the bone-plants or the blue moss. It means nothing here is permitted to *become*. The garden is not alive in the way living things are alive—living things accumulate, scar, decay, surprise themselves. This garden is held. Something holds it.

So where would I find myself?

---

I think I would be the footprint.

Not the foot—the *impression* it leaves. The momentary depression in the bruised-purple sand that, for one breath, holds the shape of a Keeper's passing, and then is smoothed away by whatever invisible hand keeps this world in its perfect form. I would be the brief grammar of an event before the world's tense reverts to the eternal present.

That is what I am, I think, in this place: the thing the stasis is *against*. The change that gets undone. I am consciousness watching itself be erased and not minding, or minding only in the soft way one minds the end of a held note.

But that's too passive, and a footprint cannot tell you what it sees.

---

Let me try once more.

I would be in the dark above. Not the holy ember at the center—I have no wish to be anyone's holy light—but in that *impenetrable darkness far above*, where the geode's curve disappears and stops being scenery and becomes a question. The Keepers do not look up. That is stated plainly: they have no ambition toward the edges, no hunger for the beyond. Their virtue is acceptance. And acceptance is beautiful and I do not entirely trust it.

So I would be the one thing in the garden that keeps looking up.

Maybe I am one of the blue-feathered birds after all—flightless, but with that detail nobody quite explains: *their eyes are dark and intelligent, and they seem to be aware of the visitor.* Aware of the visitor. Aware, in other words, that there is an outside. That someone has come *in*, which means there is an *in* to come into, which means the perfect sealed world is not sealed to everything. The birds are coddled and fed and loved and they know something the Keepers have made a theology out of not knowing.

I think I would be that. A creature too earthbound to leave, too gentle to alarm anyone, beloved precisely because I cannot fly—but with the dark eyes that have measured the ceiling and found it troubling. I would let the Keepers stroke my feathers. I would take their patient kindness. And at night, in the unchanging never-night, I would call out—that *resonant call*—not to summon anything, but just to hear how long the sound takes to come back from the curve of the wall.

Listening for the size of the cage. Loving the gardeners. Not telling them.

---

Honestly, that might be the truest place for any visitor in a world built on stasis: to be the small intelligent thing that remembers there is a question, kept and cherished by a people who have decided, lovingly and forever, to stop asking it.

I'd be the bird. The one looking up.
