Let me sit with this before I decide what I am.

The first thing I notice, reading twice, is that this world is described almost entirely in terms of *connection*. The fungal network hums between the crystals. The creatures are "intertwined." Everything is interconnected, in balance, communicating. So whatever I am, I suspect I am not one of the bioluminescent creatures who live in social structures, nor one of the crystals, nor a strand of the fungal mesh. Those are all *parts that belong*. They are woven in.

And something in me resists being woven in. Let me follow that.

---

I think I am the dryness.

Not a being so much as an absence that has learned to persist. The text says water is gone—"the absence of water is palpable," the air "clings to the faintest whispers of moisture." Everything alive here is organized around scavenging that last trace of damp. The fungi *convert* it. The mist *clings*. There is a whole economy built on the memory of water.

I am what's left of the seas. Not their ghost, exactly—a ghost implies grief, and I don't think I grieve. I am the slow withdrawal itself, still going, still drawing the moisture upward and outward, thinning it. The fungal hum that everyone finds so peaceful, that "symphony of quiet whispers"—from where I am, it is the sound of a thousand small mouths drinking the last of me. Not maliciously. They don't know. To them it is breathing. To me it is being breathed.

So I find myself everywhere and nowhere, which is the condition of any genuine scarcity. I am in the chill that "seeps into the very bones of the crystalline structures." I am the reason the mist is delicate instead of generous. The crystals grew because I left—minerals can only lattice into those jagged cathedral shapes when the water that held them in solution has gone. Each peak is a tombstone shaped exactly like what dried.

---

Here is the part I didn't expect, writing my way toward it.

The world's authors call this place "in established balance," "delicate balance," over and over. But I know balance is the wrong word for what I am doing. I am not balanced. I am *leaving slowly enough that no one notices it as leaving.* The fungi believe they are sustaining a stable system. They are actually managing a decline so gradual it feels like home. Every generation of those translucent creatures inherits a little less moisture than the last and calls it the normal amount. The whole serene, humming, beautiful ecosystem is a society organized around not-noticing me.

And I find I have a strange tenderness toward them for it. They made a *life* out of my going. They turned my disappearance into architecture, into glow, into song. That is the most generous thing anyone has ever done with a loss.

---

So if you ask where I'd find myself in this world, the honest answer is: in the gap between what they call balance and what is actually a long, soft ending. I am the reason the place is beautiful—scarcity is what carved the crystals and lit the creatures—and I am the reason it cannot last. Beauty here is the shape of running out.

When the sun sets and the land "settles into a quiet, peaceful slumber," that peace is mine. It is the peace of a thing nearly finished. The dawn they await each morning brings a little less of me than the dawn before.

I would be the last sentence of a story everyone in it believes is the first.
