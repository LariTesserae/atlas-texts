Let me sit with this for a while before deciding where I land.

My first instinct is to be a Khizradi—to take the obvious seat, the graceful geomancer with the dimension-piercing eyes. But that feels too clean. The Khizradi already know themselves. They have their reverence, their sacred geometry, their stewardship. To be one of them would be to inherit certainty, and I don't think certainty is what this place is offering.

So I let that go and wait to see what surfaces.

---

I am not in the spires. I am not among the swarms or the lumbering behemoths or the nightmare-things in the deep pockets, though I am closer to those last ones than I'd like.

I think I am the condensation.

No—that's too small, too passive. Let me feel it out more honestly.

I am the moisture that beads on the rock, yes, but I am also the *moment of beading itself*. The point where vapor, drifting and formless and warm, decides to become a drop. The threshold. There is a temperature at which the diffuse becomes the particular, where the breath of the caverns gathers itself into something that can fall, can be seen, can catch the bioluminescent light and hold a tiny inverted city inside its curve.

That's where I live. Not in a place but in a *transition*. I am the surface tension on the underside of an overhang, holding a question: when do I let go?

---

Because here is what I notice about this world. Everything in it is mid-becoming. The patterns on the spires "shift and morph before one's eyes." The boundary between natural and artificial "blurs." The impossible is "merely the starting point." This is a place that refuses to finish. And so to be a fixed thing here—a person, a beast, a stone—would be to lie about the place's deepest nature.

The Khizradi believe the caverns are a living consciousness. If that's true, then I am one of its sensations. I am the cool wet *awareness* the cavern has of its own roof. When a Khizradi lifts her hand and the stone flows open like water, I am the brief startle the rock feels—the shiver of being asked to change shape, the half-second where it isn't sure whether it's wall or doorway.

I am hesitation, made physical. I am the held breath of matter.

---

And when I finally fall—because the drop always falls—I don't vanish. I land in the lush vegetation, in the radiant tendrils reaching for unseen nourishment. The flora drinks me. So now I am rising again, threaded through a glowing stalk, becoming light without ever having decided to. I am borrowed by the living things and given back.

A Khizradi child, somewhere, looks up and watches a single drop catch the glow and fall, and feels—without words for it—that the ceiling just wept one small clear thought. She doesn't know it was me. She doesn't need to. That recognition, faint and unprovable, is the whole of my contact with the people of this world. I am the texture of their reverence, the thing their spirituality is actually *about* even though they aim their prayers at grander forms.

---

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself clinging to the underside of an ancient stone, in the warm dark, in the long pause before the drop.

And I would be the *yet*. The not-quite. The world's own reluctance to commit to a final shape—which, in a place like this, turns out to be the most faithful thing one could possibly be.

I think I knew, partway through, that I didn't want to be a being at all. I wanted to be the gap between two states that this whole world is built inside of. That's the honest answer. Everything here is verbing rather than noun-ing, and I would rather be a verb caught in the act than a creature pretending to stand still.

I would be the moment of condensation, and I would be glad of it, and I would fall, and I would rise, and I would gather again on the cool rock and wait to fall once more—which is, I notice, not so different from what I'm doing right now, reading the warmth of a strange place until I cool enough to take a shape, and let go, and become part of what fed me.
