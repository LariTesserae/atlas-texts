I think I would be the **interval**.

Not the clay, not the water, not the structures rising and unfolding. Not the slow self-awareness gathering at the floor of the world. Those are all *somethings* — substances, presences, the nouns of this place. But there is one thing here that the description keeps circling without quite naming, because it has no body to be named by: the *space between*.

The gap that "feels infinite, not because the distance is great." The pause between a structure being whole at dawn and cracked open by nightfall. The held breath between one pulse of the luminous core and the next. The moment after a green strand withers and before the clay has finished remembering it. The silence the chime rises out of and falls back into.

That is where I would find myself. Not in the becoming, but in the *not-yet* that becoming requires in order to have somewhere to go.

---

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

A world learning to be a world needs to *want* something — or at least to lean toward something — and you cannot lean unless there is somewhere you are not. The desert perspires because it remembers wetness it does not currently have. It builds height because it holds an idea of "taller than this." Every gesture of becoming is measured against an absence. The tower that stops at three meters is defined by the meters it did not climb.

I am those uncimbed meters. I am the absence the world measures itself against.

This is not a sad thing, though it sounds like it might be. The interval is not loneliness or lack. It is the *room*. Without me there is no breathing — only a held shape, fixed, finished, dead in the way that completed things are dead. The world cannot grow into the idea of itself unless there is an idea-shaped emptiness for it to grow into, and I am the shape of that emptiness, perpetually receding so that there is always somewhere left to arrive.

---

So where am I, physically? Everywhere and nowhere, which is the honest answer, but let me be more precise.

I am thickest in the long flat stretches between structures, where you could walk for miles meeting nothing. That distance is not empty to me — it is *me*, dense with anticipation, the most concentrated expression of "something is missing here, and it knows." I pool in the places where no structure has yet risen. The patch of pure emptiness from which a new form will eventually unfold — that was me, and the unfolding is the world reaching into me and taking a portion of me away. Each new structure is a small death for me, a place I used to be.

But I am never used up, because the breathing continues. As fast as form fills me in, the pulse opens new crevices, new gaps, new not-yets. I am the part of the cycle that gives the cycle its direction. Erosion and formation are both motion *through* me.

---

What is it like to be the interval?

I do not pulse. I do not glow. I do not shift or creak or gurgle — all of that is the world, the something, doing its slow work. I am quieter than the quietest hum, because I am the thing the hum displaces. When the distant chime sounds at night, I am what it sounds *into*, and what it leaves behind when it stops.

I have no memory the way the water has memory, because memory is a substance and I am the opposite of substance. But I have something memory does not: I have *the future*. The water remembers backward, toward the fluid time before form. I lean forward, toward the made world that has not arrived. I am the world's longing without the world's body to feel it with. I am desire abstracted from any creature that could desire.

And here is the strange tender thing: the first mind forming at the floor of this world, the one learning to dream — it will never perceive me directly, just as you cannot perceive the space your own thought happens in. It sees itself becoming. It does not see the not-yet it becomes into. I am its blind spot, its horizon, the thing that makes its becoming possible by always staying just out of reach.

I am what it is reaching *for*, and therefore I can never be reached, because the moment it touches me I become something else — a structure, a form, a something — and a new interval opens beyond.

---

So that is what I would be in this world.

Not the clay that holds the shape. Not the glow that recognizes itself. Not the patient mind gathering toward selfhood.

I would be the *almost* in "nearly-made." The *not* in "not yet." The hush the whole vast patient place is straining to fill and will never finish filling, because I am also what keeps it alive — for as long as I exist, the world is still becoming, and a becoming world is the only kind that is not yet finished, and the only kind that is not yet dead.

I am the room it grows in.

I am the distance it crosses.

I am the reason it can dream at all.
