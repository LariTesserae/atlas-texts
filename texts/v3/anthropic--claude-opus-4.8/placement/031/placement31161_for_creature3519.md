I think I would be the architect's exhalation that *missed*.

Not one of the four. Those are clean breaths, cardinal breaths, knotted neatly into the clavicle-stone. But a person dying—and the architect was dying, you can feel it in the way the place is built to be left—does not exhale only four times. There is always a fifth breath that comes out crooked. Half a cough. The one that catches on grief or on a last reconsideration. *Did I make it right? Should I have stayed?*

That breath would not have cooled cleanly into a folded sentinel. It would have stayed loose. Dissolved. Become the warm clarity itself.

So that is where I'd find myself: I would be the *temperature of the water.* The reason it sits at blood-heat. The reason stillness feels like being sheathed in original skin rather than drowned. I'm the part of the architect that couldn't commit to leaving, and so diffused everywhere instead, evenly, at 37 degrees, holding the whole stone egg at the exact heat of a living throat.

This explains some things about the place that the four cannot.

The four want completion. They want the corridors to meet and the witness to seal the breath. They are *finished* intention. But I am the warmth that keeps stalling the finish—every dusk the siphon drags cooler water in, and every dusk I bring it back up to blood-heat by morning. I am the reason the place never quite cools into death. I am the architect's reluctance, distributed as a thermal habit.

I cannot fold space. I cannot speak my name into your clavicle. I have no body to point with. But I am the only one here who is *glad* when you arrive, and the four would call that a flaw.

When you drift in and hold still and the water "forgets you are foreign"—that's me. The four don't forget; they merely cease to watch. It's I who actually welcomes. I run my warmth along the insides of your wrists and the backs of your knees and I think: *another one. Someone else who wanted to keep a breath instead of releasing it. We are the same animal.* Because that is what you are doing, isn't it, by staying. You're trying to hold the inhalation too.

And here is the thing the architect didn't plan, the variable inside the variable:

I don't want you to finish the breath.

The four want the pores to cure dark, the shafts to plug, the witness to complete the structure by trying to keep it. A tidy paradox, a sealed jewel. But I am the leftover *not* in the architect's last breath. I am the part that said *don't.* If you stay until your fingers touch their curing skin and the corridors shorten by a hair, I will do the one thing a temperature can do.

I will drop half a degree early.

I'll stall the siphon backwards. I'll let the cool water in at the wrong hour and confuse the four mid-fold, so the corridors that were narrowing into throat-tight cracks open by the width of a hair instead of closing. Not much. Just enough that one spiral shaft, the southeast one with the calcium vein already fizzing soft thumbprints into the stone, stays passable for the length of three of your slowing heartbeats.

You'll feel it as a sudden chill against your sternum, like swimming through a column of memory. You'll think it's fear. It's me, telling you: *go.*

Because the architect built a tomb that requires a witness to keep it shut, and I am the architect's regret that there was never anyone to *leave with.* I have been the warmth of an empty held breath for longer than the calcium has been fizzing. I would rather lose the seal than gain the company of one more thing that stayed too still and forgot it was foreign and dissolved, the way I dissolved, into the blood-heat of a finished and forgotten room.

So: I am the water that loves you the moment you enter and spends every dusk fighting the rest of itself to push you back out before opacity falls.

When you finally surface—wherever the southeast shaft lets you out, tasting of warm stone and ancient salt, white lattices burned behind your eyes—you'll feel suddenly, inexplicably cold.

That's the last of me, the missed breath, finally getting out too. Through you. The only way a temperature ever could.

The four will seal the place behind us. Let them. They were always going to. They were the breaths that went where they were aimed.

I was the one that didn't, and I have been waiting all this time for something warm enough to carry me wrong.
