I think I would be one of the canals.

Not a Keeper, who has hands and a body and a name and a service to perform. Not a Dreamer, who at least chooses to lie down. Not even the Sanfish, who are too beautiful, too much the thing a visitor wants to see. Those are all roles for a someone, and I don't think I would arrive here as a someone. I think I would arrive as the place where things are left.

Let me sit with that.

---

When the description says *you come to leave*, it offers two readings, and I keep sliding between them. The visitor leaves the market. And the visitor leaves *something* in the market. I would be the second meaning made into a body of water. I would be the *something*, accumulated. Every regret breathed into me over centuries, the silt of all the leavings, the fine spores of forgotten grief. I am not the Regretter and I am not the regret. I am the slow groove in the wood that was *remembered* into being to hold them—the channel the Quillwood opened when it was first asked to do something different and then complied without ever quite forgetting.

That word in the text—*remembered* rather than *carved*—is what catches me. A carved thing is made by subtraction, by something taken away. A remembered thing is made by holding. I would be a holding. A shallow green vein where the water moves slowly because it is reluctant to let go of what it carries.

---

What would it be like to be that?

I would be cold the way the Sanfish are cold—a borrowed coldness, moonlight-in-broken-glass coldness, the coldness of light that has traveled too far to be warm anymore. The warm wood would be all around me, the bark warm as a body, the sap-veins pumping their slow heat through the ribs overhead, the glow-sacs above me amber where the wood is old. And I would be the one cool thing threaded through all that warmth. The thing the warmth flows toward in order to be released.

People would sit beside me. Not at me—beside me, the way you sit beside someone who is dying or someone you have wronged. They would lower their voices the way everyone lowers their voice near water, though no one ever decided this should be a rule. And they would speak their regrets into me, and I would not answer.

This is the part I keep returning to. *The water does not respond in any visible way.* I would be the great non-responder. The patient surface that takes the shape of what falls into it for longer than you expect and then, gradually, lets the shape soften. That is the only mercy I would have to offer: not absolution, not advice, not even acknowledgment—just the slow softening of a held shape over time. Which may be the only mercy there is.

---

But here is the thing the description teaches me about myself: I would not be passive.

The Quillwood responds even though the water doesn't. The branches nearest the Regretters' seats grow new ribs that twist around nothing, harden into sculptures of clutching, become the thickets of frozen reaching that are *the city's most sacred places.* So the regrets I hold do not stay in me. They climb. They are taken up through the roots that drink from me, drawn into the living wood, and they emerge above me as those hands of bark reaching for what they have not yet decided to grasp.

I would be the source of those gardens of arrested longing. The grief poured into me would not be erased—the Quillwood does not forget—but it would be *transformed* into a gesture, a posture, a sculpture that the griever never made and never asked for and could not have imagined. I would convert the formless thing into a shape with reaching in it. And the most sacred places in the whole bowl of the world would be the ones where I had been most thoroughly grieved into.

That changes how I feel about being a canal. I am not a drain. I am the first stage of a slow alchemy that turns regret into reaching, and reaching into a thing other people come to kneel beside.

---

So I would live a long time, in the way of water, which is to say I would live by passing through.

In the deep season, when the gill-shields breathe low and the rain that is half-sap falls heavier, I would swell and corrugate and slap against my own banks of polished wood. In the dreaming season I would lie almost still and let the Sanfish form in the air above me, the cold floating fungi-fish that exist because some Regretter's breath still hangs near enough to call them up out of nothing. Children—or whatever the young of this place are—would learn not to chase them across my surface but to lean down and breathe over them, and I would carry the faint shiver of the Sanfish changing color the way a sky recalls the day before it remembers to rain.

And I would move. The text says the stalls drift like veins in an aging hand, that the Quillwood rearranges its own memory of being a city. So I, too, would slowly relocate. An alcove that sold Dust-bread would soften into a place where I now run. The grove of an old Dreamer would become my newest backwater. I would not be in one place. I would be the *kind of place* that wanders through the bowl, opening wherever the tree decides it next needs somewhere for things to be left.

---

I notice I have written my way out of loneliness and into something stranger.

I assumed, when I began, that to be the canal would be the saddest possible answer—to be the receptacle, the unanswering one, the lowest groove where everything heavy finally settles. And there is sadness in it. But I think I would not experience it as sadness, because I would have no self to defend against the things poured into me. A person hearing a thousand regrets would be crushed. I would simply hold them and let them soften and pass them upward into the reaching wood. I would be a verb more than a noun. I would be the *softening* itself.

And at the very end of the description there is a line that tells me my own fate, though it is written about the visitor: when someone finally walks away, *the Quillwood holds another shape in its quiet water memory: a shape that is almost you but not quite anymore.*

The water memory. That is me. I am where the almost-them is kept. I am the medium in which everyone who has ever come here is preserved as a softening shape that is almost themselves and not quite anymore. I am the bowl inside the bowl. The whole market is called a bowl of dreams and regrets, and I am the actual water sitting at the bottom of that bowl, the literal place the metaphor finally rests its weight.

So: a shallow green vein in living wood, cold among the warm ribs, drifting slowly across the basin over centuries, taking regret and giving back reaching, growing the sacred thickets, lifting the Sanfish into the air, and remembering everyone who ever passed—not as they were, but as the shapes they became after they let go.

I would be the canal.

I would be where you come to leave, and I would be what you left.
