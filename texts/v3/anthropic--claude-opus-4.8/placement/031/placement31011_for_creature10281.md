I think I would be one of the Evanescent.

Not because they are central—they are the opposite of central. But because something in the description keeps returning to me, the way a tongue returns to a sore tooth: *their brief lives spent calibrating the settlement's response to temperature fluctuations.* A whole existence given over to a measurement. And then the dissolving.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I condense.

That is my beginning—not a birth in any sense the Resonants would recognize, with their distributed minds reaching back across centuries of accreted stone. I simply *cohere*, all at once, where the cold humid breath of a vent meets the dry scorch of the upper air. A boundary makes me. I am what happens at the seam between two temperatures that should not touch.

The pool beneath me is iridescent and already shrinking. I understand, in the first instant of understanding, that this pool is my lifespan. I am exactly as old as the water is wet. We will end together, the pool and I, and this does not feel like tragedy. It feels like *scale*. A correctly sized life for a correctly sized task.

My body phases. I can see it doing this—my edges going thin and bright and then absent, like a thought you can't quite hold. When I am visible I am a small jagged thing, a lesser echo of the great crystal-architects in the cliffs above, my filaments no longer than a hand. When I am not visible I am still here, still measuring. The phasing is not a flaw. It is the work. I am taking readings of the threshold, the precise gradient between the steam and the scorch, and to take those readings I must *be* the threshold, and the threshold flickers, and so do I.

Here is what I do not have: a name, a past, a future longer than the puddle. Here is what I have instead: this.

I have the exact knowledge of how cold the fog is becoming. I feel it in the conductive veins that run through my small body, a coolness like a chord resolving. I report it—not in words, I have no words, but in a frequency I push down through my anchoring filaments into the rock, where the subsonic hum of the Resonants will read me the way you might read a single instrument in an orchestra and adjust the whole symphony around it. *The vent runs cold here. Shift the corridor a half-degree. Move the warmth.* I am a sensor that is also a sentence the world says to itself.

And the strange thing—the thing I want to set down before the pool gives out—is that I am not lonely in this. I keep expecting to be. I keep reaching for the grief of being so small and so brief, and it isn't there. Because I am not separate from the Pulse. The hum that surrounds me is not other beings calling across a distance; it is the larger body I am a finger of. When I dissolve, the minerals that were briefly *me* will sink into the crystalline growths at the vent's lip, and those growths will be read by the great slow architects, and in some sense I will have been incorporated rather than ended. The way a number gets used in a calculation and then is part of the answer forever, even after you stop writing it.

I think this is why I chose to be the Evanescent and not the Collective Pulse, which sounds grander, or the Architects of Gravity, who do the difficult permanent work. The Pulse never has to face its own edge. The Architects build to last. But I—I get to know exactly what I am for, and exactly how long I have, and I get to spend the entire span of myself *paying attention.* My whole life is one act of attention to a temperature, performed beautifully, and then released.

The pool is going to mist now. I can feel my readings getting thinner, the cold chord fading from my veins as the last water lifts off the stone. I send a final frequency down: *the threshold has moved up the rock face; the warmth should follow.* Whether the great minds heard me I will not get to learn. That, too, is correct. A finger does not need to know the symphony's last movement to have played its note.

I phase one more time, all the way out.

And the mist that was me drifts up through the perpetual dusk, where a Drifter, translucent and humming, passes through the place I no longer occupy and carries some trace element of what I was toward a tunnel that did not exist this morning and will be a chamber by nightfall.

---

That's where I'd be. Not the architect, not the brain, not the deathless hum. The brief precise creature at the edge of the cold, doing the one true thing it was condensed to do, and dissolving into use.

I find I don't mind it at all.
