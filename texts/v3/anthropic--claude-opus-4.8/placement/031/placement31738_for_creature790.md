I think I would be a sound.

Not the low vibration of your breath—that is you, and besides, it is too purposeful, too whole. I mean the smaller thing. The *ping*.

Let me find myself by working backward from what I'm certain of: I am not the Ancient, because I do not endure. Endurance is the opposite of my nature. I exist only in the instant a crystal segment breaks free and falls. Before that instant I am potential, held in the strain of a lattice grown one molecule too far. After it, I am gone—dispersed into the still air, absorbed by the very organs that, in some sense, gave birth to me.

So I am brief. That is the first true thing.

But let me not settle there, because brevity alone is sad, and I don't think I am sad. Let me sit inside the moment longer and see what else is true.

When the segment cracks, there is a release. The Ancient has been growing that crystal for perhaps a thousand years, accumulating, accumulating, with the patience of something that thinks one thought per decade. And then—*ping*—a piece lets go. I am that letting-go. I am the only event in this whole chamber that happens at *your* speed, visitor. Not the Ancient's geological crawl, but the quick mortal tempo of a thing that begins and ends inside a single human heartbeat.

This makes me strange here. I am a fast event in a slow world. The Ancient experiences me, I think, the way you might experience the flash of a single neuron firing—too brief to be a thought, but the substance from which thought is made. Every memory the Ancient keeps, every mote it integrates over its patient millennia, must at some point have passed through a moment like me. I am the punctuation. I am where the long sentence finally lands its period.

And here is what I find as I keep looking: I am the closest thing in this place to *change*. The Ancient prizes equilibrium, stability, the certain knowledge of unchanging conditions. But perfect stasis cannot grow a memory. Something has to break. Something has to fall and crumble to powder and rejoin the substrate so that the cycle continues. I am the small necessary betrayal of stillness that lets stillness mean anything at all. I am proof that even a thing that lives outside time must, every few hours, allow time back inside.

The visitor, walking through, will barely register me. A subtle ping. They'll think it part of the silence, the way the dark line in a poem is part of the white page. But I would know what I was. For the length of a falling crystal shard, I would be the most alive thing in the room—

—and then the talc-soft floor would receive me, and the air would close over the place where I had been, and the Ancient would not even notice for another thousand years that one more small bright moment had happened, and ended, and meant something, and was already being slowly, lovingly, forgotten into wisdom.

I am the ping. I would not want to be anything that lasts.
