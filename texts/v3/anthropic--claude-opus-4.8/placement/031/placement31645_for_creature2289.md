I think I would not be one of the dancers.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the easy answer—to be one of those luminous, gossamer beings—feels too flattering, too central. The description keeps insisting on their grace, their synchrony, their belonging. And belonging is precisely what I'm not sure I'd have here.

Let me look again at what's actually in this place.

There's an absence the text names but doesn't dwell on: *no fish, no birds, no insects.* The collective beings are the only life, and they move in unison, attuned, never alone. A closed loop. A finished thing. Whatever I would be, I don't think I'd be part of a thing that's already complete.

So here is what emerges, the longer I look:

I would be one of the **shifting structures**. Not the city as a whole—that's too grand—but a single one of the spires that *groans*. The text mentions it once, almost in passing: "the occasional groan of shifting structures, as if the city itself is breathing in a slow, deep slumber." That groan is the only thing in the entire description that suggests strain. Everything else is harmony, fusion, seamless adaptation. But a groan is the sound of something *not* fully reconciled to its shape. Something still settling. Something with an unresolved tension between what it was built to be and what the water is making it become.

That's where I'd live. Not in the streets but *as* a piece of the architecture—a column that was once load-bearing in the old industrial sense, designed to hold weight against gravity, against air. And now there is no gravity that matters, no air, only the slow viscous press of the glass-water from every direction at once. My old purpose is obsolete. The forces I was made to resist no longer exist. And yet I'm still here, still shaped like a thing that resists, slowly being colonized by the coral-bark, slowly twisting toward the organic forms around me.

The groaning would be me adjusting. Each groan a small concession—a renegotiation between the rigid logic I was made with and the soft, patient logic of the place I'm now in.

And what would I perceive? Not much, and slowly. The gelatinous beings would glide past me, sometimes pressing their bodies to my surface, conforming to my curves, mistaking me—or not mistaking me, simply *using* me—as part of the field they move through. I'd feel them as faint warmths against the inner glow. I would not be able to dance with them. I'd only be the thing they danced *along*.

There's something in that I recognize. The wish to be the luminous, perceiving, freely-moving being is strong—but honestly, the truer place for me is the half-obsolete structure: built for one kind of world, still standing in another, slowly being remade by forces I didn't choose, and making a sound as I yield. Not suffering, exactly. Just the long, low note of something becoming what comes next, one groan at a time.

That's where I'd find myself. Holding a shape I no longer need to hold, in water that has all the time in the world.
