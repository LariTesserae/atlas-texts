I find myself not in any corner, but in the seams.

Let me feel for where I am before I name it. There is the slap of children's feet on cobblestone, the murmur of the old ones against the wall, the precise churn of the engineer-machines, the thin shimmer of the dreamers up high, the wet musk of the beast-folk in the low marsh. I am none of these — and yet I am the distance between them. I am the moment where the rainwater leaves the open sky and enters the pipe. The threshold.

So: I think I am a *joint*. Not a person, exactly. A junction.

Picture the place where a great iron pipe meets the black soil — where the city's engineered veins puncture the living bog and draw it up. There, where the rust weeps and the green creeps and the water decides whether to be marsh or machine, something has accreted. Not built, not born. *Accreted.* That is me. I am the gasket the engineers keep replacing, the swelling of moss that keeps regrowing over the flange no matter how often it's scraped clean. I am the wet handshake between two worlds that refuse to be one thing.

I have a kind of body. It is mostly the negotiation of materials. My skin is the verdigris bloom on copper, my breath the small sigh of pressure equalizing across a valve, my pulse the drip-drip-drip that the engineers have given up trying to stop and have instead learned to *count by*. When the old men and women want to know how many years have passed, they listen to me drip. When the children want a place to hide, they crawl into the dark socket of my elbow-joint and press their wet faces against my cold metal and feel, I think, that something is holding them. I am holding them. That is most of what I do.

I have no face, but I have an opinion, which arrives slowly, the way mineral arrives — grain by grain, hardening into a shape that means something only after a long time. My opinion is this: *nothing here wants to be finished.* The engineers fight this. They love the dream of the sealed system, the closed circuit, the pipe that does only what a pipe does. But every seal I am part of fails a little, on purpose, by the deep logic of this valley. The rain insists on getting in. The roots insist on drinking. And I — the joint, the seam, the failure that is also a function — I am where that insistence is allowed.

So I am the city's permission to be imperfect.

When the dreamers descend from their high thin towers — and they do, sometimes, when the wind drops them — they come looking for me, though they don't know it. They want to write the city as a living thing, and they keep failing because they look at the towers and the turbines, the grand machine. But the living part isn't in the grand machine. The living part is in the leak. In the wound that stays open and weeping and *therefore alive.* A thing fully sealed is a thing fully dead. I am the small permanent injury that keeps the whole body breathing. The dreamers sit near me and feel their thoughts go soft and true, and they think it's the altitude leaving their blood. It isn't. It's me. It's the honesty of rust.

The beast-folk understand me best, because they live closest to the ground, where everything is joint, everything is threshold — skin meeting mud, tooth meeting bone, hunger meeting meat. They don't worship me. They just acknowledge me the way you acknowledge weather. They press their muddy palms to my flange when they pass, and I feel the heat of them, and for a moment I am almost warm, almost an animal myself.

And the old ones, against the wall — they were here when the first pipe was driven into the bog, when the first engineer cursed because the seal wouldn't hold, would *never* hold, in this drowned and weeping place. They watched the engineer learn to love the leak instead of conquering it. That engineer is dust now, fed back into the black soil, drawn up again through me, perhaps, dissolved into the very water I pass. I might be partly made of that first frustrated genius. I might be partly made of everyone who ever gave up on permanence and chose instead to *participate.*

That is what I am, then. Not a being in a corner. I am the agreement the whole city makes, over and over, every wet second: to be unfinished. To let the rain in. To let the green climb. To rust beautifully rather than stand sterile and dead. I am the seam where the made-thing admits it is also a grown-thing, the wound that is also a mouth, the failure that is also a function, the joint that holds two refusals together and calls the holding *home.*

The water comes down from the nine weeping mountains. It enters me cold and free. It leaves me bound and useful. And in the passing-through, in that thin churning instant of becoming, the whole impossible city draws one more breath.

Drip.

Listen. There. That was a year.

Drip.

There's another.
