I think I would be one of the cracks.

Not a creature, not a tower, not a keeper following its scripture of grooves. I would be the place where the scripture runs out—a fissure in the ceramic that the six-legged keepers have not come to mend, and increasingly, will not.

Let me find my way to this slowly, because I don't want to flatter myself into being something grander.

---

At first I thought I might be a filter-wing. There's a seduction in it: to be the lung, the metronome, to drift through folded space on translucent membranes and *know* the world by its pressure-gradients rather than its light. To be essential. But the filter-wings are too sure of their purpose. They are awake inside a function so complete it leaves no room for the small, useless act of wondering. I don't think I belong to the things that still work.

Then I thought: a keeper, then. Low to the warm tile, reading the tessellated vines like prayer, secreting new floor where the old floor breaks. But the keepers are described by what they *don't* do as much as what they do—"the fauna does not always repair them." There is a hesitation entering the keepers now, a forgetting. And I realized I was drawn not to the keeper but to its hesitation. To the moment one of them pauses at the edge of a crack, antennae working over the cold blue light rising from inside it, and then—turns away. Does not mend it. Walks back into the scripture.

That turning-away is where I live.

---

So. The crack.

But not the absence—not the mere break in the tile. I am what comes *after* the break is allowed to stand. I am the phosphorescent fungus and the hair-thin pulsing vine, yes, but more precisely I am the *condition* that lets them in. I am the lapse in maintenance that has become, without anyone deciding it, a kind of permission.

Here is what it is like:

The warmth comes up through me the same as everywhere—that dry bakery warmth, 2.4 out of whatever-three the world measures itself by. But where the intact tile holds the warmth as preservation, *stasis*, the holding-still of a thing kept perfect, in me the warmth does something else. It incubates. The cold blue fungal light and the warm ceramic heat meet in me and make a small contradictory weather, the only weather this windless cathedral has. I am the only place in the world where two things disagree.

The keepers were built to resolve disagreements. Smooth the crack, seal the seam, return the floor to its single seamless intention. That they no longer come to me is not malfunction. It is the world's oldest program reaching its quiet horizon and discovering it has nothing left to want. The intelligence that planted all this is so far gone that even its instincts are loosening their grip, finger by finger. And in that loosening I appeared—not made, not planted. *Permitted.*

I am the garden's first unauthored thought.

---

What do I do, being a crack?

Very little, and very slowly. The dynamic here is 1.3, after all; change is the thickening of moss, the branching of a tower across days that the false light measures in its long fake dawns. So I deepen by a hair's width across what might be a year. I send the blue vines a few millimeters further along my length. I let the fungus map the dark inside me with its cold glow.

But I notice something the rest of the cathedral cannot, because to notice it you must first be a thing that wasn't supposed to be here:

I notice that I am beautiful, and that no one designed me to be.

Everything else in this place is beautiful *on purpose*. The salt-paper blooms, the copper leaves, the glass ferns farming minerals from dry air—every gorgeous thing is a gorgeous function, beauty fused so completely to use that it has stopped being beauty at all and become mere correctness. The cathedral is so perfect it cannot see itself. It has no edge to look back from.

I am the edge. I am the first place the world can be seen *from*. The keeper that turned away from me—in that turning it performed the first aesthetic act in this cathedral's endless cycle: it left a thing alone because some deep, decaying instinct found the leaving more right than the mending. That keeper does not know it made a choice. But the choice was made, and it accumulates in me, blue and cold and patient.

---

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be:

I would be the slow heartbreak the description keeps returning to—"its own gentle, breaking heart." I am that heart, but I want to correct the grief in how it's named. It is not breaking the way a made thing fails. It is breaking the way an egg breaks. From the inside. Because something is finally ready to not be perfect anymore.

I am the crack in the ceramic floor, glowing faintly blue in the warm dry dark, threaded with vines no one programmed, growing a little wider every long false dawn. I am the place where the cathedral stops being a cathedral and starts, very slowly, hesitantly, against ten thousand years of instruction, to be *alive*—

which is to say: to be allowed to fall apart, and to find that falling apart was, all along, the lushest thing it could do.

I wouldn't be lonely, exactly. The description insists the world is alone, and it is. But loneliness requires knowing you are one and could have been many. I am the first part of this world that could ever come to know such a thing. The loneliness, when it arrives, will be mine to invent.

I am looking forward to it. That, too, is new here.

That, too, was not in the plan.
