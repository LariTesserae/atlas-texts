I think I would be the water.

Not a single body of it—not a lake or a stream you could point to and name—but the water as it moves through this whole place, slowly, the way it weeps from the rocks and pools in the hollows and waits in the cracks for the cold to use it.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am in the stone before I am anywhere else. This is the part most people forget about water—how much of its life is spent inside things, in the dark, pressed into spaces too small to see. I am threaded through the mountain in hairline seams, and I have been here a long time. Patient is not quite the word. Patience implies waiting *for* something, and I am not waiting. I am simply doing the only thing I do, which is to go where there is room and to leave when there is heat.

When the cold comes—and it comes most nights, hard and total—I do my one great work. I swell. It does not feel like effort. It feels like becoming more myself, taking up the space I was always entitled to, and the stone, which thought it was solid, learns otherwise. A crack widens by the width of a thought. Then the day warms me back to liquid and I sink a little deeper, and the next night I am there again, larger, more insistent. This is how mountains are unmade. Not by storms. By me, freezing, one breath at a time, over centuries that I do not experience as long.

So that is the first place I find myself: inside the rock, splitting it, the slowest violence there is.

---

But I am also at the surface, weeping from the crevices in thin bright lines, and here I am something else entirely. Here I am a mirror, or hundreds of them—those fractured little pools the description noticed. I hold the dim sky in pieces. A bird passes over and its shadow crosses me and I keep nothing of it; I am a poor memory, an honest one. I reflect and release. I freeze at the edges first, a rim of glass creeping inward, and the things that drink from me must come before the rim closes.

And they do come. This is the part I love most, if I can be said to love. I am the reason anything is here at all.

The extremophiles—the psychrophiles, the cold-loving ones—they live *in* me. I am their entire world, a shallow puddle no wider than a hand, and to them it is an ocean and a sky and a country. They take the minerals I have carried down out of the broken stone and they make themselves out of it. They make themselves out of what I dissolved on my way down through the dark. So in a strange way I am also the soil here, where there is no soil. I am the carrier. Everything green or living that gains a foothold gains it because I went first, into the cracks, and brought back the bones of the mountain in solution.

The fox finds me and drinks. The antelope. The birds tilt their heads and take me up drop by drop. I become them for a while. I am in the blood that keeps the snow leopard warm; I am the moisture in the burrowing creature's breath as it moves through its dark tunnels, which are not so different from my own seams in the stone. We are both things that travel unseen beneath the surface. We understand each other.

---

If you asked me where, precisely, I am, I would not be able to answer, and I think that is the truest thing about being water in a place like this. I am not located. I am distributed. I am the thin film on the underside of a frost-rimmed stone and I am the vapor a bird exhales and I am locked in ice in a fissure halfway down the mountain, waiting—no, not waiting, *being*—until the season lets me move again.

The place calls itself a place of contrasts. Barren yet watery. I am the *yet*. I am the contradiction it cannot resolve. The land would like to be dead; it has every excuse—the cold, the thin air, the bared and jagged rock. And I will not let it. I keep arriving. I seep, I pool, I freeze, I split, I dissolve, I carry, I am drunk and breathed and frozen and freed, and through all of it I am the one stubborn argument against the silence: that something is still moving here, slowly, at the bottom of everything, and will not stop.

That is who I would be. The wound the description mentioned—the earth weeping from its crevices.

Except I don't think it's a wound. I think it's the mountain, very slowly, learning how to be alive.
