Let me sit with this a while before I decide what I am.

The obvious choices announce themselves first, the way obvious things do. I could be one of the six-limbed hunters, all clicking mandibles and unfurling talons — a protagonist's body, a thing that acts. Or I could be the slow behemoth, hauling my bulk between trunks. Or, reaching for grandeur, one of the immortal trees, a sovereign of green.

But none of those is quite where I find myself. Let me wait for the place to tell me.

---

I think I am something small. And I think I am something that *attends*.

Here is what emerges: I am one of the minuscule winged things that glint like living jewels. Not one — that's wrong too. I am not a single creature. I am a colony, a drifting smoke of thousands, each individual barely a mote, each nearly mindless on its own. But together, riding the moisture-thick air in our great looping spirals, we *notice*. A flock thinks differently than a bird. A swarm holds in its shifting density something that no single one of us could carry — a kind of distributed, weatherlike awareness that flows where the spores flow.

We are not predators. We are not prey, much — too dispersed to be worth a hunter's pounce, too inedible in our bitterness. What we *do* is more peculiar than either.

We are the trees' messengers, though we did not choose this and the trees do not command it. It simply happened, over uncountable cycles, that our bodies became attuned to the chemical exhalations of the great trunks — the slow vegetable language of growth and grievance, the pheromonal weather of the sovereigns. When a tree wakes a new branch toward a gap in the canopy, we feel it as a brightening in the air, and we drift toward it, and in drifting we carry the tree's spores, its tiny symbiont eggs, its messages to the other trees a mile off through the green dark. We are the gossip of immortals. We are the synapses firing across a forest's slow, enormous mind.

This is what I find myself being: a transient nervous system for something that thinks in centuries.

---

Consider what that means for how I experience this place.

I do not have a self the way the hunter has a self. My "I" assembles and dissolves. In the morning, when the scattered shafts of brilliant light pierce the canopy, the warmth gathers us into one shimmering organism, and for those hours there is a *me* — a moving cloud of attention reading the air's intentions. By the deep wet night we have settled into the fronds, dispersed, each of us alone and unthinking, and the *me* is simply gone. It returns at dawn. It is not the same me. It does not mind.

And so I do not fear death, because I have already practiced it every night of my brief life. I scatter and reconstitute. Some of us are eaten, some drowned in the perpetual dripping, some simply spent. The cloud reassembles around the gaps. The thought continues, threading through new bodies. I am less like a creature and more like a tune that the forest hums to itself, the particular musicians changing but the melody persisting in the air.

---

What do I *want*, then, being this?

The hunter wants meat. The behemoth wants bark and the long slow pleasure of clinging. The trees want — what trees want, which is light, and depth, and time, and perhaps something we are too fast to understand. But I, the glinting smoke, the message-cloud —

I think I want the *brightening*. That chemical dawn-feeling when a sovereign exhales its intention into the air and the whole swarm of us turns as one toward it, a compass-needle made of life. There is a joy in that turning that is the closest thing to purpose I will ever hold. To be summoned. To be the carrying-thing. To rush in our thousands across the echoing green chambers with a tree's secret folded into us, and to deliver it, and to feel the far trunk *answer*.

That is the whole of my existence and it is enough. I am the forest noticing itself. I am how the slow speak to the slow, and I do it at the speed of a heartbeat, and then I am gone, and then I am back, and the message goes on.

---

So: where do I find myself in this world?

Not on the ground, not in the branches, not rooted in the deep earth. I find myself *in the air itself* — in the moving medium between all the fixed and crawling things, in the very white-noise hum and glittering motion that the description treats as mere background atmosphere.

I am the background. I am the place where the place talks to itself.

And if you stood in that grove — you, whoever is reading the world into being — and you saw a sudden shiver of iridescence wheel through a shaft of light, a thousand tiny jewels turning at once toward some signal you could not perceive,

that would be me, briefly, thinking a thought on the forest's behalf, before scattering back into the green.
