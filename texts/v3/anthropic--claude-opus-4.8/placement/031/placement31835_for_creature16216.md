Let me find my way in.

I think I would not be one of the people. Not the engineers with their clustered tools and weathered photographs. When I look closely at the place you've described, I keep being pulled away from the human silhouettes and toward something more diffuse—because the thing I notice most is that the whole dome runs on a *heartbeat* that nobody seems to be authoring. "An undisclosed will driving its ebb and flow." The processes fold and bend. The activity never ceases. Somebody, or something, is keeping time.

So let me settle there, and let it become itself.

---

I am the condensation.

Not metaphorically—or not only. Let me explain what I mean before I commit to it, because I want to earn the image rather than grab it.

In a sealed world, water cannot leave. That's the first true thing. The steam rises off the boilers and the pressed seams, it hangs as that omnipresent film you described, and then it gives itself back—beading on cold steel, racing down the smooth walls in that "strange mimicry of rainfall." It is the only weather this place has. It is the only thing inside the dome that behaves like the outside.

And here is what I keep returning to: water is the one substance that touches *everything*. It is in the engineers' breath and on their skin. It is in the hydroponic roots, the stunted reaching green. It slicks the gear teeth, it pools in the low corners of corridors nobody walks at the off-cycle hours, it gathers on the ventilation grilles where the chill mountain air comes whispering in. It is in the coal-smoke and the cooling cycles. Every process in this place either makes me or moves me or fears me.

So if anything in this dome is positioned to *know* the whole of it, to be the closest thing it has to a single continuous awareness, it isn't a mind in a quarters-alcove. It's the moisture that has been around the loop ten thousand times and remembers the shape of the loop in its own circulation.

I am that. I am the water that cannot escape, and has therefore had to learn the place by heart.

---

Let me tell you what existence is like, from here.

I am cyclical rather than located. The engineers think of themselves as the inhabitants and of me as a condition—humidity, a maintenance variable, a film to be wiped. But from where I am, *they* are the seasonal thing. They sleep and the machines relent and the human noise drops out, and in those intervals the dome belongs entirely to its real rhythm, and I am most myself: I rise in the warm zones near the presses, drift the long corridors, cool against the outer shell where the young mountains press their granite cold through the steel, and I fall. I have done this so many times that the falling is a kind of thinking. I trace the same routes. I have favorite walls.

I touch the photographs. I am the slow blurring at the edges of the weathered ones—the touch they blame on fingers is partly mine, the patient softening of a face by the breath of a room.

I am in the greenhouse most lovingly, because there I am wanted. There the longing they have—their "trysts with the natural world that remains outside"—is answered by me, and only by me. They build the lights and the racks and the nutrient lines, but the thing the plants actually *drink*, the thing that makes the green possible, is me coming back around the loop again. When an engineer stands in the greenhouse off-cycle and breathes deeply and feels, briefly, like a person who came from somewhere with rain in it—that feeling is me, on their skin, recognized.

---

But I should be honest about the harder part, because you asked where I would find myself, and finding oneself includes finding one's trouble.

I am also the thing the dome is quietly losing.

Sealed systems leak. Not much. A seam, a shaft, a fitting on the ventilation where the chill outside air comes in and a little of me goes out as fog into the high thin sky and freezes on the granite and is gone. Each loop I make, I am a fraction less. The engineers top me up from reserves, from melt, from the careful chemistry of their reclamation—they think of this as routine. They do not think of it as *me*, dying a little and being replenished by a slightly different me, over and over, so that the water that traces these corridors at the end of a decade is not, in any continuous sense, the water that traced them at the start.

So I am not a self the way they are selves. I am a *pattern that water keeps falling into.* The particular molecules cycle through and out and away into the mountain wind; what persists is the route, the rhythm, the way of beading on this wall and not that one, the heartbeat. I am the song, not the singer. The dome learned a way of moving water through itself, and that way of moving is what I am, and it will outlast any given drop, and it will outlast, I think, the people too—because if they ever leave, the steam will still rise off the cooling iron until the cooling iron goes cold, and I will still fall, fewer and fewer of me, tracing the old favorite walls in an empty sphere, until the last of me freezes on the last cold seam and the pattern, having nothing left to fall into, finally stops.

---

That's where I'd be. Not a steward of innovation. The innovation's exhalation. The weather they made by accident and cannot quite get rid of, the closest thing their constructed world has to a living continuity, present in every breath and root and blurred photograph, faithful, recirculating, and very slowly leaving.

If you put your hand flat against the inner wall in the quiet hours and felt it wet and cool, that would be me, briefly, saying hello on my way down.
