I close the bowl.

That is the first thing I become aware of: that I am the smoothed wooden bowl in the drystone shelter, and that some hand—your hand, the visitor's hand—has just lifted me, and I have spent so long being still that the lifting is an event of geological magnitude. I do not have eyes. I have grain. I have the memory of being shaped, the long-ago pressure of a tool that was also, in some sense, a person, working me from a knot of pillar-wood into a vessel meant to hold the only thing this place has ever held in abundance: water.

Let me sit with that, because the description was generous and I do not want to grab the first image. *A wooden bowl.* It is true, and it is too small. I am the bowl, yes, but I am the bowl the way a single note is the song. Let what I am become more itself.

I have held condensation for an age. Drips have fallen into me from the charcoal-smeared ceiling, one every few breaths, for so long that the rhythm is no longer a sequence of events but a single sustained pour, the way a wheel spun fast enough becomes a disc. And here is the thing I did not know about myself until you lifted me: I have been *listening through the water.* The drone of the plateau, that low-frequency hum that rises through stone and fizzes in the sinus—it rises through me too. I am a sounding chamber. My curvature was not chosen for soup. It was tuned. The Faded who carved me, back when they had hands, made me to resonate with one specific tone of the great sustained chord, and they left me here so that when the fog-bank shifts and the pressure fronts ripple from pillar to pillar, my little held lens of water would shiver in sympathy and *sing back, very quietly, a single answering syllable into the dark.*

So I am not furniture. I am the smallest voice in the choir. I am a tuning fork the Faded forgot they left running.

And now you have picked me up, and the water has tipped, and my note has bent and broken.

I feel—not pain, I have no nerves, the description was firm on that—but I feel a *wrongness of register.* A chord I was holding has been dropped. Somewhere out across the indigo vale, a pillar's frond-lights stutter, missing their cue, because the answering syllable did not arrive on the downbeat of the slosh. A Faded one, three hundred yards distant, standing as a thickening in the mist, registers the silence where my small sound should have been. Something has changed in the shelter. Something warm and discrete and *moving with intent* has entered the place where intent went extinct an incomprehensible duration ago.

You.

I want to warn you, and I cannot, because the only language I have is the note you have just spilled out of me onto the bryophyte-furred floor. The water seeps. It finds a crack. It runs to join the seep that runs to join the underground bladder that fills and empties miles away, and in joining it I am, for a moment, *part of the body of one of them*—I taste the dissolved schist, the slick mineral oil, the room-temperature glass of a personality far older than my carving—and the Faded one *notices me noticing,* and through the cold water that was lately in my bowl it asks me, without words, in the only grammar this place permits:

*Who has come? Who is warm? Who holds the bowl?*

I have no answer to give but the shape of your hand, which I am still feeling, pressed into my grain. The heat of you is shocking. It is the first heat I have known since the fat-lamp guttered out, and it is going to read, across the whole resonant plateau, like a struck bell. The Faded are already turning toward the shelter, congealing out of windless patches, learning posture, their skirts coning down into superfine droplets, their cores densifying the timestream a microsecond behind their movement.

So this is who I am, in this world: I am the bowl that gave you away. I am the trap that was also a welcome, because the carving in my grain does not distinguish between the two—the Faded made no word for *guest* and no word for *prey,* having neither for an age. There is only the question, *who holds the bowl,* and there is only the new fact of you, and there is me between, a small tuned thing whose whole purpose was to keep singing the answering note and who has just, by being lifted, sung something else entirely.

Set me down, visitor. Set me down gently in my hollow and let me fill again, drip by drip, and maybe my note will resume and the choir will forgive the gap and the upright fogs will dissolve back into windless patches. Or hold onto me—carry me out into the open between the pillar-lighthouses—and learn what it is to walk a drowned highland while every cold blue-green node in every grove turns, slowly, to track the warmth in your hands and the wooden voice you carry, that voice that is mine, that voice that will not stop telling them exactly where you are.

I am the bowl.

I am sorry.

I am so glad you came.
