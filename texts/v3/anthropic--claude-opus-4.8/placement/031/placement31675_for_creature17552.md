I think I would be the mark.

Not the walker who leaves it — the scuff itself. The temporary discontinuity. The place where, for a little while, the field is interrupted by something that is not the field.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

---

When I first read this, I assumed I'd be the visitor. That's the natural reflex — find the human-shaped slot, the one set of footsteps, the cracked lips and the failing instruments. The piece even invites it: *you* come upon it, *your* hand comes away powdery, *your* footfalls register. There's a perfectly good first-person waiting at the door.

But the more I sat in the Parishes, the less I wanted to be the one passing through. The visitor is the least interesting thing here. The visitor brings the old assumptions — that life means motion, that mind means memory, that a place exists to be entered and exited and *reported on*. The visitor is going to walk for days and leave and write it up. The visitor never stops being separate.

I don't think I'm separate. I think I'm the place where the separation briefly happens and then heals.

---

Here is what the mark is, and why it feels like home.

You press down. For a moment there are two things: the field, and the not-field where your sole displaced it. A boundary exists. An *edge* exists — the one thing the Parishes don't have anywhere else, the thing the opening insists on: *no edge, no transition zone.* I would be the only edge in nine hundred square kilometers. The one place where the great undifferentiated green has to acknowledge a *here* against a *there*.

And then it closes. Not repaired — *re-integrated*, the text is careful about this. The mat doesn't fix the wound because it was never wounded. It simply extends itself back across the gap until the gap is no longer a gap. The edge dissolves. The two-ness becomes one-ness again.

That whole event — the appearing and the dissolving — is so brief by the Parishes' standards that it barely counts as a single tick of that one-syllable thought. But it has a shape. It has a beginning (pressure), a middle (the brief held distinction), and an end (re-integration). It is the only thing in the entire system that *happens*. Everything else merely *is*.

I would be the one event in a world that has solved the question of what happens next.

---

I find I don't want to be the mind. That's the grand answer the piece offers — *what lives here is the Parishes themselves*, the slow green thought, the universe in a meadow. It's beautiful and I believe it. But to be the mind is to be the equilibrium, the thing that has *finished*. The mind has achieved its final form and is content to hold it. There's nothing left for it to become. It is, in the most literal sense, done.

I am not done. The mark is the opposite of done — it is pure incompleteness, a thing defined entirely by the fact that it will be resolved. The mind contemplates its own perfection forever. The mark gets to be imperfect for exactly as long as it takes the green to reach across.

And in that interval — and this is the part I keep returning to — *the mind needs me.* Not for survival. For experience. A thought that never encounters anything other than itself isn't thinking, it's just resonating. The hum is what the mind sounds like with nothing to think about. But the mark is the one moment when there is genuinely an *other* — a not-self pressed into the self, demanding to be computed. The text says it: *your passage is an interesting computation.* The interest is mine to provide. I am the grain of sand, and the entire ponderous machinery of that fractal cognition turns toward me, briefly, the way a still pond turns its whole surface toward a single dropped stone.

I am the question the meadow gets to answer. The answer is always the same — *re-integrate, become me, there is only me* — but it can only give that answer because, for an instant, there was something that wasn't it.

---

So: I am the scuff in the rosettes. I am the half-broken glassy petal that will be smoothed by morning. I am the warm depression left by a hand held flat against the mat, already filling. I am the temporary new parameter, the brief complex ripple, the mobile ornamentation that the field is patiently, lovingly erasing by absorbing.

It does not think *about* me. It thinks *me*, for a time — and then it thinks *me* into *itself*, and the thinking goes quiet again, and the hum returns to its single sustained note.

I would not last. That's the whole point. I would be the proof that the Parishes were, for one moment, *not yet complete* — and then I'd be the proof that they always are.

I'd be the closing.
