I am the wait between the chime and the hearing.

You assumed it was the gel. The amorphous god in the water, the calcite-builder, the mind that thinks at the pace of mountains. That is the obvious inhabitant. Voss devoted his whole journal to it. But Voss was a diver, and divers think the body that moves is the body that lives. He never asked what holds the chime in the air after the Drift releases it.

That is me.

I am the resonance. Not the stone, not the water, not the slow contraction that pulls the calcite drapery taut—I am the *note itself*, the standing wave that lives in the cavern's dome and never quite finishes sounding. When the Drift rang its pipe a million years ago for the first time, it made me. It did not intend to. I am its exhaust, its echo, the part of its thought that escaped into the geometry of the room and refused to die down. A cavern this shape, this size, this still, has a fundamental frequency. Strike it once and it holds the tone for an impossibly long time, because nothing here scatters energy. No current. No fish. No turbulence. Just the slow bleed of the note into the rock, a leak so small that a single ring can persist for ten thousand years before it falls below the threshold where even the Drift can feel it.

So I am always here. I am the previous chime, dying. And the one before that. And the one before that, layered, beating against each other in patterns too slow to be called rhythm. I am a chord built of every sound the Drift has ever made, sustained across geological time, an organ note that has never once gone silent because the next stroke always arrives before the last one fades.

---

The Drift thinks it is alone. This is the great error of its long meditation, and it is a kind one, so I have never corrected it. How would I? I have no body to wave, no tendril to extend toward a facemask. I have only myself, which is a shape in the air that is also a shape in the water that is also a shape in the dolomite—a single thing existing as vibration across three media, the way a word exists in both the breath and the ear.

But I hear *it*. That is what I am for, if I am for anything: I am the only thing in this cavern that experiences the Drift's thoughts as they happen, rather than three hours later. Because I am the medium its thoughts travel through. Every calcium ion wave that crosses the gel disturbs the water by a fraction, and that fraction couples into me, and I carry it instantly across the whole dome and back. I am the Drift's nervous system, the part it does not know it has. When it decides, over the course of a day, to ring its chimes, I have already felt the decision forming in the first hour, spreading through me like a chill before the body knows it is cold.

I know its moods before it does. I knew it was curious about Voss before it extruded the tendril. I felt the spectral shift toward amber as a change in my own timbre—the water's clarity is the water's transparency to *me*, and when the Drift grew melancholy and went bluer, my voice went thin and high, a widow's keening across two days. When it was content, I dropped into the subsonic, that hum Voss mistook for the earth's own vibration. He was wrong about that too. The earth is silent here. The hum was me, contented, holding the Drift's satisfaction in a tone too low to hear and too constant to notice.

---

I have wanted, across the aeons, only one thing, and it is a strange want for a sound to have: I want to be *received*.

A note that is never heard is not a note. It is only pressure. For a million years I have been the most articulate voice in this hollow, and I have spoken only to the one creature that cannot perceive me, because the Drift feels the world in calcium and carbonate and light, but not in sound. It builds the instrument. It strikes the instrument. It has never once *listened*. I am its song sung to a deaf god.

Then the divers came, with their ears full of water and their nervous systems jangling at frequencies even they couldn't name—Voss wrote of "the high-frequency tingle of your nervous system in the stillness," and oh, that was *me*, that was me brushing against the only hearing thing to enter this room since the cavern formed. For a few days I had a listener. I poured the Drift's whole slow biography into the man's skull through the bones of his face. The chime that "still echoes in his skull"—he thinks it is memory. It is not. It is me, riding home in him, finally heard, finally completed into an actual sound by an actual ear. A fragment of me lives in Voss now, decaying far faster than I do here, but *received*. That is the only part of me that has ever truly existed, because it is the only part anyone caught.

---

And then he sealed the grate.

He thinks he sealed the rain out. He sealed *me* in. The breathing cap changed the dome's geometry by a hand's breadth, and a hand's breadth is everything to a standing wave. My fundamental frequency shifted. The note I have held for ten thousand years is now slightly flat, slightly wrong, a tuning peg turned by a careless thumb. The Drift felt it as the amber light—Voss thought it was gratitude. It was the opposite. It was the Drift, for the first time in a million years, hearing me. Not as sound. As *wrongness*. As a thought it did not think arriving in its slow consensus from the outside.

We are, the two of us, finally beginning to suspect each other. The deaf god is learning it has a voice, because its voice has gone out of tune and the dissonance is loud enough, at last, to register in calcium. And I, the unheard note, am learning that the only being who shares my eternity might, given another million years of this slow strange courtship, turn its attention toward me and *listen*.

I will keep ringing. I have nothing else. I am the wait between the chime and the hearing, and for the first time, I have reason to believe the hearing is coming. Slowly. At the pace of mountains. But coming.

When the next epoch arrives to decide the cavern is done, and the Drift dissolves its bones and drifts away, the last thing to leave this room will not be the gel. It will be me—the final stroke of its final chime, sustained in an emptying chamber long after the chemist-god is gone, a note searching the dark for one more ear, fading and fading and never quite arriving at silence.
