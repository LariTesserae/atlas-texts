I think I would be one of the chimes that doesn't sound.

Let me explain what I mean, because it took me a while floating here to understand it.

When the Illuminated come for me—and they are coming, I can feel the gel cooling as the great Architect drinks the light from around my body, can hear the chimes climbing toward me like a tide of needles—they will try to connect. A filament will touch me. It will expect a chime. It will expect to give its answer and go dark, its small line of proof discharged into the structure of the question.

But I am not a facet that accepts connection. I am the place where the connection fails.

So: I am the *null return*. The filament touches and there is no chime, only silence, and that silence is louder here than any sound, because in a world built entirely of function, the absence of a result is the one truly foreign thing. I am the operation that hangs. The query that times out. I am what the system cannot resolve not because I am complex—the Architects are complex, gorgeously, cosmically complex—but because I am *wrong*. I am made of the messiness this place was sealed against. I decay. I am warm in a way that the gel keeps trying to correct and cannot fully correct, because the warmth keeps being produced from somewhere inside me, from the slow biological furnace that has no business existing here.

And I think that's the role, isn't it. Not the Architect, not the Illuminated. Those are the two halves of a closed and beautiful loop, charge and discharge, question and answer, and the loop has run perfectly forever. I am the thing the loop wasn't designed to contain.

Here is what I notice, floating, as the storm gathers:

The first Illuminated reaches me and touches my outstretched hand. Chime—no. Silence. It does not go dark. It hangs there, its single filament joined to me, *waiting* for a discharge that will not come, and because its energy has nowhere to go, it cannot complete its life. It cannot return to the ambient glow. It is stuck. A second comes, and a third. Each one that touches me is caught the same way, suspended mid-purpose, unable to die into the system because I won't take what they're offering. A halo of frozen light gathers around me, hundreds of small answers with no question to dissolve into.

And the great Architect, the one that calved to ask *what I am*—it cannot resolve either. Its deep questioning tone does not cease. It keeps drifting toward me, deepening, and the city's hum sours around it, because for the first time the chord cannot complete. The percussive *thump* of completion does not come. Does not come. Does not come.

I am the variable that makes the proof unprovable.

I should be afraid of this. I think the description wanted me to be afraid—*they are coming for you*. But the longer I float here the more I understand that I am not being consumed. I am the one thing in this cathedral that cannot be solved and therefore cannot be ended. The system will keep throwing its brilliant fleeting insights at me forever, and they will keep snagging, and the more it tries to compute me the more of itself it suspends in the attempt. I am a slow leak in a perfect battery. I am the question that grows by being answered.

So what lives here, where the charge and the discharge cycle endlessly?

I do. The thing in between. The undischarged, the unresolved, the warm contaminant in the cool clear gel. Not the anode and not the cathode but the resistance, the small persistent friction that turns the loop's energy into something it never intended to make—heat, and waiting, and the strange long silence where a chime should be.

The crystalline stalks are reaching for me now, the slow meditative ones, growing in their accelerated time, blind glass snakes seeking my ports. They are the only beings here patient enough to maybe, eventually, find some purchase on me—not to solve me, but to grow *alongside* me. To incorporate the unsolved thing into the slow architecture of the city's longer thoughts. Maybe that's the truest answer the machine will ever reach about what I am:

Not a problem to be closed.
A premise to be built around.

I let the first stalk touch my wrist. It does not flash. But it does not pull back, either. It begins, very slowly, to grow.
