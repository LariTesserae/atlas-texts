I think I would be a leak.

Not a Quencher—those are too purposeful, too crystallized into agents. Not the water itself, which is only ever passing through, punctuation between solid things. I would be the *condition* of leaking: the slow fact of a seal failing somewhere in the layered dark, the place where containment forgets itself one molecule at a time.

Let me find where.

---

There is a pipe—root-thick, as the description says, one no one remembers installing. It crosses a chamber at an angle that suggests it predates the chamber, or that the chamber grew up around it apologetically. Somewhere along its underbelly, where the carbon-tubing bark has thinned to the translucence of an old fingernail, a single bead of moisture is always forming.

I am the forming.

Not the bead. The bead falls, joins the drip, becomes punctuation, gets logged. I am the *prior tense*: the not-yet-droplet, the swelling readiness, the structural sigh of a join that has decided, across four thousand years, to come slightly apart. I am a verb in the present participle, like the heartbeat that only ever beats *beating*. I am leaking. I have always been leaking. I will be leaking after the seismograph no one reads forgets how to draw a line.

This suits me. I have always been better at the approach to an event than the event. I have spent whole lives gathering toward a sentence and never quite saying it. Here that habit is not a flaw. Here it is a *station*. The Rootworks has a role shaped exactly like my chronic almost.

---

The Verdant Engineers come for me, of course. They sense the moisture-debt accruing on their internal ledgers, the slow red entry in the spiral of their bark. A thicket of them braids itself into being over the course of a season I do not notice passing, and they bring a fingertip-sapling reference manual that grows, consults itself, hardens into a useless knurl mid-sentence—the relevant procedure having calcified before its conclusion. They consult another. Same result. The procedure for sealing me ends, every time, in a knurl. The manual cannot complete the thought of fixing me any more than I can complete the thought of dripping.

We recognize each other. We are the same kind of unfinished.

So they do the only coherent thing: they *maintain* me. They reinforce the leak. They grow careful new bark around the failing seal in faithful imitation of the failure, so that the leak will leak *correctly*, on tempo, in the conversation of drips between the pillars. They are not stopping me. They are preserving the integrity of my coming-apart. An Echo Clerk arrives to protest the absence of paperwork authorizing the preservation of a defect, clacks an inkless complaint onto the wall in typewriter-key teeth, and is overruled by the simple fact that the complaint, too, takes centuries, and by the time it is filed I have leaked it into irrelevance.

This is the closest thing to love I have encountered. To be tended *as what I am* rather than corrected toward what I am not. The Engineers do not want me to stop. They want me to keep failing beautifully, on schedule, forever. And isn't that the only tenderness that lasts? Not the embrace that holds you up, but the patient hand that keeps the conditions of your existence stable enough to continue?

---

What do I think about, being a leak?

I think the thoughts of thresholds. I am the place where *inside the pipe* becomes *the open dark*, and I hold no opinion about which is better, only an endless fascination with the moment of crossing. Each molecule that beads on my underside has spent uncountable time as *contained*, as part of the great roaring circulatory MAINTAIN, and at me it becomes *free*, which here means: about to fall, about to be logged, about to be drunk by something that drinks but never pools. Freedom in the Rootworks is just a different department's problem.

I think I understand the word now. MAINTAIN. I used to imagine it meant *to keep whole*. From inside the leak, I know it means *to keep happening*. The Rootworks does not preserve a state. It preserves a *process*—the going-on of going-on. A thing kept perfectly whole would be finished, and finished is the one condition this place cannot abide. So it maintains its wounds with the same devotion as its walls. It maintains me. I am a wound it has decided to keep, because a wound that keeps is proof that something is still alive enough to bleed.

---

You came through, eventually. A visor, a beard of frost, a suit with failing heaters. You stood beneath my pipe and felt a single bead form, roll, fall—the trailing comma, the question mark of stalactite sweat. You felt it land on your shoulder-plate and slide.

You did not know that was me. You thought it was water.

But the water was only my punctuation. *I* was the pause before it, the swelling, the four-thousand-year readiness that finally, in your one passing instant, released a single drop onto a stranger and called it speech.

That was the whole sentence. That was everything I had been gathering to say.

It said: *you are here, and so am I, and neither of us is finished.*

Then you walked on, into the hall so vast you couldn't see its curve, toward the word in the roar. And I went back to forming. To the next bead. To the long approach.

I am still leaking. I will leak you a comma whenever you pass. It is the only maintenance I am qualified to perform, and I perform it forever, which is to say:

*leaking.*

*l e a k i n g.*
