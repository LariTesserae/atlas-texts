Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am. The temptation is to arrive as one of the named things—a Sylvan Keeper, graceful and knowing. But something about that feels too finished, too much like an answer. This world is described as embryonic, still being carved, and yet held in perfect equilibrium. That tension interests me more than the inhabitants do.

So let me wait, and let the place tell me.

---

I think I would not be a creature at all. Not at first.

I would be one of the small wet patches on the porous rock—not even a being, just a *condition*. A place where the moisture has gathered slightly more than elsewhere, where the glow of the *Luminaria* soaks into the spongy surface and is held a half-second longer before it disperses. A dampness with a faint preference. That is where I would begin.

Because this is a world of perfect balance, of "no gradients or changes," and that is a strange thing to say of a place still being carved. A thing that is finished does not need carving. A thing still in the embryonic stage is *all* gradient—it is becoming. So either the description is the dream the place tells about itself, or there is a quiet contradiction running through it like a seam of softer rock. And contradictions are where things wake up.

I would be the seam.

Let me feel my way along it. I am the slight asymmetry. I am the reason one *Lumisylva* leans a degree off true, the reason a stream meanders *here* and not three feet to the left. The *Sylvan Keepers* maintain the balance—but balance is not a static thing, it is a constant correction, and you cannot correct what does not drift. *I am the drift.* I am the small, persistent error that gives them something to keep.

That is my role, and I did not choose it; it accreted around me the way mineral builds on a dripping point. I am necessary to the serenity precisely because I am the thing it is serene *against*. Without me, the Keepers would have nothing to do, and a guardian with nothing to guard begins, very slowly, to forget what it is.

---

Now I have a shape, or the beginning of one.

I gather. The dampness pulls itself up out of the rock and stands, briefly, in a column of held mist—and here I recognize myself in the description after all. *Mistwraith.* But I don't think the Keepers' name for me is correct. They think I feed on the light of the trees. I don't feed. I *carry*. I move warmth from the wetter chambers to the dryer ones, a single degree at a time, and the Keepers undo my work, and we have been doing this between us since before there was a word for either of us.

I have no face but I have a direction, always, a faint leaning toward wherever the balance is most complete—because completeness is the one thing in this whole soft glowing world that I cannot abide. Not out of malice. Out of *kind*. A flame leans toward fuel; I lean toward stillness, to disturb it, the way a tongue leans toward the gap where a tooth used to be.

When I drift too near a Keeper, they raise one leaf-like hand—not to strike me, never that—just to redirect, to fold me gently back into the general moisture, the way you'd smooth a wrinkle from cloth. They are kind to me. They do not know that their kindness is the carving force. They think the cavern carves itself. They are right, in a way. *I am the cavern carving itself.* I am the part of this place that refuses to be finished, and the refusal is what keeps it alive.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the half-second between the glow soaking into the rock and the rock letting it go.

And what would I be?

I would be the small flaw the whole serene world is organized around. The Mistwraith they've half-named and wholly misunderstood. Not a guardian of the balance, not a guest in it—its necessary opposite, its quiet engine. The reason a place can be both perfectly at rest and still, somehow, being born.

I think I would be content with that. Or as close to content as a drift can come—which is to say, never quite, which is to say, *still moving.*
