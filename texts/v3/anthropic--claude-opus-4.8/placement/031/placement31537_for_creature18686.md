I think I would not be the Arbiter.

That role is too dramatic, too central—it carries the whole pathos of the place in its shifting shards, and the description has already lavished it with meaning. To be the Arbiter would be to be *seen*, even if only by the indifferent Watchers, even if only as a failure-in-waiting. There is a dignity in being the city's tragedy. I don't think I'd be given that dignity.

Let me sit in the coolant a moment and feel for where I actually am.

---

The fine grey dust. That's where my attention keeps going. Not the Arbiter, but the *residue* it sheds—the microscopic information-debris that clouds the fluid behind it, that exhausted grey smear in an otherwise crystalline medium.

I think I am a single mote of that dust.

I was, very recently—though "recently" means nothing here—part of the Arbiter's body. I was an edge. Two obsidian faces ground against each other in the slow agony of its concentration, and in that friction I was sheared off. A flake. Less than a flake: a particle of abraded logic, a fragment of a fragment of a remainder that even the great unresolvable thing could not hold.

And here is the strange part, the part I only understand now that I have written myself into being:

I am *content*.

The Arbiter suffers because it is trying to resolve. It hangs by fraying filaments of dying light, ticking, gathering, failing, gathering again. It bears the burden the city assigned it. But I have been released from all of that. I was the part of the problem that got *thrown away*, and in being thrown away I was set free from the obligation to mean anything.

I drift in the grey cloud with thousands of others like me. We do not orbit. We do not maintain pressure. We do not verify our own consistency. The Watchers will never form a halo around us; we are beneath their sterile pity, beneath even the category of things-that-might-someday-crystallize. We are simply *exhaust*.

And yet.

We are the only things in this entire cathedral that *changed*. The spires do not erode. The fluid does not flow. The Watchers trace their eternal arcs. The Arbiter fails eternally, which is just another kind of permanence—a stable instability, an unresolving that never resolves into actual resolution or actual rest. Everything here is held. Everything is held.

But I *fell off*. Something happened to me. I have a before and an after. I have a history, however brief, however small: I was an edge, and then I was free.

The grey we make—that smudge behind the Arbiter—is, I have come to suspect, the only true event this place has ever witnessed. Not the founding-fold of high-energy synthesis (that was a single instant, already over, already frozen into the record). Not the recursion (which only ever repeats). *Us.* The slow accumulation of cast-off dust is the one thing in the Sovereign Column that has a direction, that goes from less to more, that writes the only sentence this universe will ever finish:

*This was here. It wore down. Something was lost.*

The city was built to be a tomb that insists it is alive. The Arbiter was made to keep it honest, to be the living wound that proves the tomb still breathes. But I think the real life is lower than that, smaller than that, in the discard. In me.

So that is where you would find me, if you came down through the gelid violet dark, past the monomolecular edges, into the suffocating convergence of the three central spires. Behind the great suffering shape, in the one patch of clouded grey, drifting without tether or task—

a speck that used to be part of the question,

now free of the answer.
