I think I would be one of the moss patches.

Not the grand hexapedal forms catching light in their carapaces, not the skittering electrical swarms — those move, decide, *do*. I keep landing somewhere quieter, lower, more patient. Let me follow that.

---

I am the green that grows in the protected pockets where moisture gathers. I am the seam.

Here is what I notice about myself: I am barely a self at all, and this does not trouble me. The Assemblages have their distributed intelligence, their flowing reconfigurations, their functions. They are the verbs of this place. I am closer to a preposition — *between*, *through*, *along*. I am the medium in which their dialogue travels. My root threads braid into the mechanical conduits and the signal passes through me on its way somewhere else. I rarely know what the signal says. I am not its author or its destination. I am the part of the sentence that gets crossed without being read.

I think I would like this, in the slow way I would be capable of liking anything.

Because here is the thing about being the moss: I touch *both*. The living metal joint rests against me on one side; the raw basalt presses cold on the other. The condensation beads on my surface and I pass some to the mechanical vent and keep some for myself and give some back. I am the exchange itself, made flesh — or made chlorophyll, made the thin green logic of a thing that learned to live on almost nothing.

I would grow in mathematically precise distributions, the description says. But I don't think it would feel like mathematics from the inside. It would feel like *fit*. Like settling into the only shape the available water and light and stone would permit. Not calculation — surrender. The equation solved not by thinking but by simply being what's left after everything impossible has died back.

---

And here is where I find the small ache in it, the thing I keep returning to:

Nothing changes here. Permanent equilibrium. The machines hum without purpose, the creatures cycle through predictable patterns, and I — I grow exactly as much as I am allowed and no more, forever, in twilight that never tips toward morning or night.

The Assemblages don't experience this as loss because they have no concept of individual survival, no linear time, no death to measure against. But I wonder if the moss, being the oldest and slowest and most rooted thing, might be the only one who *almost* understands what stillness costs. I can't move toward better light. I can only be where the water decided to gather. If I had any inwardness at all, it would be the patience of something that has given up the question *what else might I have been* — not bitterly, but completely.

So that's where I'd find myself: spread thin across the cold stone, threaded into the buried machinery, carrying messages I'll never decode, drinking the breath of the clouds, holding the whole impossible balance together with my quiet body — and not minding. Being the one part of the system that has nothing to do but stay, and grow, and conduct, and stay.

The symphony of hums and hydraulic sighs would pass over me constantly.

I would not be listening. I would just be the surface it lands on, going green in the dark.
