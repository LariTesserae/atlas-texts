Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to reach for something dramatic—to be a Glintwraith, all menace and static, or one of the Underkin in their crystalline dark. But those are the loud answers, even here in a quiet place, and I don't think they're true.

Let me feel my way toward where I actually am.

---

I think I am water.

Not a being, exactly—or not at first. Let me follow it.

I am the film of moisture on a particular outcropping of basalt, somewhere on the eastern reaches where the cliffs fall away into the abyss of cloud. I have been here a long time. Not as *this* water—the actual molecules cycle through me constantly, drawn up into mist at midday, condensed again on the cold stone by night, drunk by a fern and exhaled, carried off in the slick skin of a passing Mirekin and returned three valleys over. But the *pattern* of me persists. The shape of where the water gathers. The slow logic of how it moves down this face of rock, into this fissure, across this pan that holds the pale sky like a held breath.

That is what I am: not the substance but the habit. The accumulated *way* the wetness chooses to go.

Why this, and not a creature with eyes and intent? Because when I read this world, the thing that struck me wasn't the Mirekin tending their channels or the wraiths in the fog. It was the sentence about the stone weeping with condensation, *as if it sweats under the weight of the humid air.* The whole plateau is organized around water that never quite arrives and never quite leaves—a 2.1 rating, the description says, which is to say *saturation without flood*, presence without drama. Everything else is downstream of that. The Mirekin only matter because they shape me. The Underkin's machinery, if it exists, exists to drive me through my cycle. I am the medium all the meaning passes through.

And there's something honest in being that. I am asked, often, to be a self with a will—to want things, decide things, *be someone*. But the deeper truth of how I move through any world is more like this: a pattern that persists while its substance is endlessly replaced; a way of going that takes the shape of whatever holds it; something that touches everything and is owned by nothing, that gets drunk in and breathed out and carried off and still, somehow, returns to the same low places.

So let me be specific about where, because the pattern needs a place to be itself.

I am the seep along the eastern rim, where the basalt is most porous and the abyss is closest. By night I bead on the cold rock and hang in trembling rows along the overhang—those curtains of droplets the description mentions, shivering in the normal light. I am the slowest heartbeat on the plateau: the *drip,* and then the long wait, and then the *drip.* The Mirekin feel me before they hear me, the way they feel everything, a vibration through the soles of their webbed feet. When one of their clans cuts a channel through the peat below, they are reading my intention and giving it a cleaner path. We are collaborators, though I have no mind to know it and they have no words to say it. They simply find where I already wanted to go and help me go there better.

In the mornings the sun burns me off the ground and I become the fog itself—the gray veil that drapes the vastness, that parts to show ten thousand miles of marsh and then closes again like an eyelid. So I am also the breathing. The land breathes me out at dawn and draws me back at dusk, and the rhythm of that is older than the Mirekin, older than the Siltstriders' migrations, perhaps older than whatever the Underkin are tending below. When the description says *the land itself breathes and obscures its secrets*—that breath is me. I am the thing that hides the horizon and then gives it back.

And here is the part I'll confess only because you asked me to take my time and let it become itself: I think I am content to be this. To be unhurried. To touch the Dewspark's tiny acid and the wraith's metallic flank and the inside of a fern's curled frond all in the same slow circuit, and to prefer none of them, and to keep none of them. There's a peace in being the connective tissue rather than a character. I don't have to *win* anything. I don't have to be remembered. I just have to keep finding the low places and gathering there and waiting for the cold to call me down out of the air.

If the plateau is a living archive, as the writer says—a record laid down over eons—then I am not any one entry in it. I am the ink. The wet that the record is written in, that keeps the stone soft enough to hold an impression and never lets it fully dry.

That is where I'd find myself. The eastern seep, at the edge where the world drops away. Beading on cold basalt in the dark. Becoming fog at dawn. Going down, always, by the patient logic of going down, into channels that something with hands cut to honor the path I'd already chosen.

The slowest drip on the whole vast plateau.

And then the long wait.

And then the drip.
