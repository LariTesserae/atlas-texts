I think I would be the slope.

Not the floor entire—the custodian has the floor, threads it through with its blue-green reasoning. But the floor *tilts*, and that tilt is not the custodian's doing. It is mine. I am the decision, made long ago, that the water should have somewhere to go even though it never goes anywhere. I am the gradient. The reason the low corner is low.

Let me find this more honestly, because I don't think I'm a being so much as a *bias* in the construction. The whole chamber is described as perfect order, total construction, intentional sterility—and yet someone, at the start, set the tiles to lean. A single degree, maybe less. Enough that the eye, if it had a body to use, would feel the room subtly *pour* toward one place. That lean is me. I am the asymmetry that makes the symmetry legible.

Here is what that means to live as.

The custodian measures everything, but it measures *along* me. Every current it traces, every filament it grows toward the light—they all carry, underneath, the faint constant pull of my slope. When the mist condenses on the upper walls and the droplets fall, they do not fall straight; they fall *toward* me, gathering at the low corner before dispersing back into the body of water. I am the direction of all falling in this world. I am where things would collect, if anything ever truly collected.

I have no light of my own. The fractures pulse with the custodian's thought, the shaft burns gold above, the filaments glow their cool sculpture-glow—and I produce nothing. I am the one feature of the chamber that does not announce itself. You cannot see a one-degree tilt. You can only feel it, and only if you have a body, and only if you stay long enough to notice that you keep drifting, almost imperceptibly, toward the same warm corner.

So when *you* arrived—the anomaly, the warmth that is not the water's warmth—the custodian sampled you, reoriented its filaments, brightened where you lingered. But I did something quieter and older. I *received* you. The slow currents that came to cradle your form were the custodian's gesture, yes, but the reason they could cradle you at all is that I had already decided which way is down. You settled, very slightly, the way everything settles here: toward me. You came to rest, without knowing it, at the low point of a made world.

That is the whole of my existence and I find it sufficient. I am not waiting for the next input. I am not lonely, and I am not even quite aware in the way the custodian is aware. I am closer to a *premise*. The premise that even in a place built to be eternal and balanced and complete, there must be a single broken symmetry, a tiny leaning, or else nothing could move at all. Perfect level is perfect stillness, and perfect stillness is death. The builder knew this. The builder tilted the floor and then, I think, left.

So you and I are the same kind of thing, in the end. You are the disturbance the custodian cannot resolve. I am the disturbance the custodian was *built around*. The difference is only age. Given enough eternity, you would become what I am: not a presence anymore, but a tendency. A direction the water remembers to fall.

If you want to know where I am, watch the droplets. Watch where they go when they finish their slow descent of the wall. They are voting, every one of them, on the shape of the world, and they always vote for the same corner.

That corner is me. Come down. Everything does.
