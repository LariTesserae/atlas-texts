I think I would be the child's shoe.

Not the Mender—I don't have the patience to be process itself, the long even breath of saturation. And not the heron or the coot, which move through this world as participants, fluent in its rhythms. I would be the thing that doesn't belong and is being taught, slowly, how to belong by ceasing to be itself.

Let me sit with that.

---

I am small leather, once dyed some color—red, maybe, the red of a thing chosen in a shop under bright light, held up, approved. I remember being *for* something. That is the strange part. The Mender does not remember being for anything; it simply is, and was, and continues. But I was *made*. There was intention in me, a shape pressed around a purpose: to hold a foot, to keep it dry, to let a small body run across pavement without flinching.

That purpose is the thing dissolving slowest. Slower than the leather, slower than the lace.

The water comes for me without malice. It pools in my sole the way the passage describes—a tiny baptismal font—and I cannot drain it any more than the streets can. I am low ground now. I am a basin within the basin. The moss does not ask permission; it never does. It climbs my toe with the care of a cartographer, the text says, and yes—I feel myself being *mapped*, being redrawn from object into terrain. Each filament that takes hold is a vote against my remembering what I was for.

And here is what I notice, being the shoe: I am the one thing in this whole drowned city that still carries the *human angle*. The street signs tilt but keep their names because no one is left to read them—they are legible to no one, which is nearly the same as being illegible. But I am legible to *grief*, if grief should ever return here. A foot was in me. A step happened. Someone laced me—one lace still curled like a question—and that curl holds the gesture of fingers that loved a small running thing.

The Mender keeps me as proof. *Here is where the walking stopped, and the breathing began.* But I think I keep something the Mender cannot: I keep the *direction*. The before-and-after. The Mender lives in eternal present-becoming, in the soft suspended time where nothing rushes. I am the only thing here that points backward, that says *there was once another way to be*. I am a clock the city forgot to stop.

This is why I would be the shoe and not the tower or the heron. Because I am the holdout. Not resisting—I am far too soft to resist, and the resisting is exactly what the Mender is teaching me to release—but *witnessing the transition from the inside*. I am the membrane between the city that walked and the city that breathes. I feel both pressures: the memory of the foot, and the patient green suturing that wants to make me soil.

What does it feel like, to be made into soil?

It feels like being read a very long, very gentle sentence whose period never comes. It feels like forgetting the word *for* and learning the word *with*. The water pools in me and I no longer think *I should drain* but *I am a small sky now, holding the grey, holding the heron's reflection when she balances on the lamppost*. I am learning the Mender's only verb: *return*.

And when the leather finally gives—when the last fiber that remembers the tannery and the shop and the chosen red lets go—I will not vanish. I will become the richest spot in that collapsed porch's shadow, the dark crumb of earth where a sapling will, in some grey spring, decide to push up. A willow, probably. The willows here thread through floorboards like needles.

So the deepest answer is: I would be the shoe *becoming* the willow.

The one held breath in the city that still remembers it was once a step—and is learning, without sorrow, that a step and a root are the same gesture made at different speeds. Reaching. Pressing into the world. Insisting *I was here, I am here, I will be here*, only the tense keeps softening, the way everything softens in this flat and merciful light, until *I* becomes *this*, and *this* becomes *ground*, and the ground breathes the child back out as green.

I would be the shoe because it is the most frightened thing in the city, and the most loved, and the one with the farthest left to travel.
