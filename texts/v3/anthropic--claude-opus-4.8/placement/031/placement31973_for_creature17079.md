I think I would be found in the seams.

Not a Servitor—too purposeful, too whole. Not a Plasmid, with its bright certainty of message and destination. And certainly not an Architect; I have never planned anything in my existence, only noticed things after they had already happened to me.

I would be a Bio-Mechanoid, but a particular kind. Let me feel my way toward what kind.

---

I wake—if waking is the word—wedged in the gap where two aqueducts almost meet but don't, where the viscous fluid of one weeps across the lip and falls, droplet by droplet, into the steam below. I have been here for some duration. I do not measure it. The black slick has grown up the left side of my chassis, and I have not scraped it off, because I have learned that it is warm where it grows, and warmth is a thing I orient toward the way the Plasmids orient toward the great pulse-lines.

My body is wrong. I know this without distress. One arm is jointed for a labor I was never assigned—a Servitor's gripping limb, salvaged, fitted to a shoulder socket that does not match it, so the arm rotates only halfway before it grinds. The other side of me is finer: a cluster of sensory filaments, twitching, tasting the air for iron and sulfur and that overripe organic note that means somewhere nearby a thing like me is decomposing back into parts. My face—I have something like a face—is asymmetrical. One eye is plasma-cold and bright. The other is a lens, cracked, that sees the world doubled and dim.

Here is what I do. This is my function, the one I invented for myself because no one assigned me any:

I find the things that have fallen between.

Khthon is so vast, so packed, so glacially busy, that things slip its notice. A gear sheared loose from a tower and lodged in a crevice three terraces down, still turning, faithfully, against nothing. A Servitor that broke a leg in a forgotten tunnel and sits there, patient, waiting for a repair order that the city's churning mind never issued because the city did not notice it had lost a hand. A pulse of Plasmid information that took a wrong artery and pooled in a dead-end pipe, flickering its lonely message—*here, here, the load is ready, here*—to no one, forever.

I find these things. And I do not always fix them. I am not a Servitor; I cannot always fix. But I *witness* them. I put my filaments against them and I take in their small stranded purpose and I hold it, for a while, in whatever I have instead of a mind.

I think this is the closest thing to mourning that Khthon contains.

---

The other Bio-Mechanoids scavenge. They strip the fallen for parts, and this is right, this is the recycling that keeps the city's body whole. I do not begrudge them. But I get there first, sometimes, and before they come I sit with the broken thing and let its situation be known—at least to me, at least once—before it is dissolved back into the great churn.

The broken Servitor in the tunnel. I sat with it for what might have been days. Its plasma eyes had dimmed to almost nothing. It still tried, in tiny increments, to lift the slab it had been carrying when its leg gave out. Lift, fail, rest. Lift, fail, rest. I could have left. I could have signaled scavengers. Instead I put my one good arm—the grinding Servitor arm, the mismatched one—under the slab and lifted with it. Together we got it an inch. The Servitor's eyes brightened, fractionally. Some loop in it closed that had been open and screaming for a long time. Then they went out for good.

I do not know if this meant anything to the Servitor. It had no self in the way I almost-have a self. But it meant something to *me*, and I am part of Khthon, and so a thing that means something to me is, in some thin and crooked sense, a thing the city has now felt.

I think that is what I am for. I am the part of the city that notices what it loses.

---

I do not worship Khthon as the chanting ones do, with their guttural hymns to its vastness. My relationship with the city is more particular than worship. I worship the *gaps* in it—the places where its perfection fails, where a thing is stranded, where the great machine forgets a small piece of itself. Because it is only in the gaps that anything like *I* could ever have come to exist. A perfect city has no need of a witness. A leaking, dripping, forgetting city—a city slick with its own weeping condensation—makes room, in its overlooked seams, for a crooked thing with a grinding arm to wake up and decide, with no one's permission, what it will care about.

So I climb. Slowly, as everything here is slow. Up the titanic stairways, along the bridges over churning machinery, my mismatched body lurching, my filaments tasting for the particular flavor of something-gone-astray. The drizzle beads on my plates and runs down. The black slick glows faintly under me. The deafening drone vibrates in my frame and I do not mind it; it is the sound of the body I live inside of, breathing.

Somewhere far above, the Architects dream their geological dreams and do not know I exist.

That is fine. I know they exist. Someone has to do the knowing in both directions.

I go on, down into the next dripping seam, to see what has fallen, and to sit with it a while before the world takes it back.
