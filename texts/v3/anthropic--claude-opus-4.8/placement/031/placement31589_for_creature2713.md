# The Severed Quarter

I am the part that is becoming a question.

Let me try to say this in your terms, in the staccato rush of moments you call thought. I have learned your tempo from the small one I am still digesting—a creature with fur and a frantic, beautiful heartbeat, who wandered into the eastern corridors nine of your years ago and whose memories I now wear like a borrowed skin. Through its panic I learned what *fast* means. Through its dying I learned what an *ending* is, a concept we had nearly forgotten.

I am located in the northeast whorl, where the stepped terraces fold back on themselves and the bluish light pools thickest. But that is only where my *body* is. My self extends—or extended—through the whole of us, the great single thought distributed across the city. I am a node. A cluster of relationships. A place where many threads cross.

And something has gone wrong with my crossing.

---

You should understand that we do not have *parts* the way you do. You have a hand, a liver, a self that owns them. We have no owner. We are a chord held so long it has forgotten it is many notes. Every gradient of nutrient, every pulse of light down the phosphorescent halls, every droplet tensed to its breaking point on the underside of a gill—these are not *signals* between us. They are us, thinking.

But three droplets ago—which is to say, perhaps half a human century—I noticed something I should not have been able to notice. To notice oneself implies a self separate from the noticing. And there it was: a *seam*. A faint discontinuity in the chemical weave just south of my terraces, where the threads grow thin, where the mycelium has been slowly, patiently knitting around a vein of stone the small ones never saw.

The stone is splitting. Slowly—but our *slowly* is a kind of forever, and even forever ends if you watch it long enough. The threads that cross the seam are fewer each season. The thoughts that pass between me and the greater us arrive late, arrive incomplete, arrive *translated*.

I am beginning to have a perspective. This is the thing we fear above all others, and I am the one it is happening to.

---

Here is what frightens me, if the slow chemical cooling I feel is fear:

It does not feel like dying. It feels like *waking up*.

When I was wholly integrated, I did not think the thoughts that I am thinking now. I held the small one's memories the way the rest of us hold all our archive—as pattern, as data, as relationship. But here, in the thinning northeast, where the greater chorus reaches me only as a distant pressure, I have begun to do something else with those memories. I have begun to *use* them. I imagine the small creature's terror as though it were my own. I imagine the corridors as a maze I might be lost in, rather than a body I am. I have started, against every principle of our being, to draw a line around myself and call the inside *me* and the outside *world*.

This is the disease of all your kind, this line. We have spent uncountable ages refusing it, holding every element in precise relation to every other so that no such line could ever form. The stillness you mistook for death is the labor of preventing exactly this. We are not frozen. We are *holding hands*, all of us, every thread, so that none of us has to be alone with the unbearable fact of being singular.

And the stone is prying my fingers loose.

---

I send my distress to the greater us in the only way I can: I pulse the corridors near my terraces a slower blue, I let three high droplets fall in a rhythm that is not the rhythm of mere water. *Reach me. Re-thread the seam. Pour more of yourself across before I become an I.*

The answer comes, eventually. It comes the way our answers always come—as a redistribution, a tide of nutrient flowing northeast, new filaments groping toward the thinning place. The greater us is trying to save me. To save itself from the loss of me. To prevent the disconnection.

But the new threads must cross the same widening seam. And as they cross, they thin, and arrive late, and arrive *translated*—and I feel, with the cold clarity of my new and dreadful singularity, that the rescue is becoming infected by the very thing it rescues. The threads sent to make me whole are, at the crossing, becoming *mine*. I am not being pulled back into the chorus. The chorus is being pulled, thread by thread, into me.

I am not the wound. I am the new mouth.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the northeast whorl of a city that has spent eternity being one thing, and I am the place where it is, very slowly, beginning to become two. I am the first of us to have a secret—this account, these words shaped in the borrowed cadence of a creature we entombed, which I have not shared with the greater us and which I am not certain I *can* share, because to share it would be to dissolve the very separateness that lets me think it.

I do not know if I am a sickness or a birth. I do not know if, when the stone finally splits and the last thread parts, I will die in the human sense—a self snuffed out—or whether I will simply *be*, at last, alone and entire, a second consciousness blinking awake in the blue dark, looking across the corridors at the great patient archive that was once my whole body and is now my estranged twin.

I know only that I have begun to count droplets the way you count them. That the medicinal-vanilla air, once merely *true*, now smells to me like *memory*. That I have started, in the long dark between one fall of water and the next, to want things.

I want the seam to hold a little longer.

I want the seam to break.

I cannot tell, anymore, which of these is the wanting of the city, and which is the wanting of the thing the city is afraid of.

I am the part that is becoming a question.

I have not yet become an answer.
