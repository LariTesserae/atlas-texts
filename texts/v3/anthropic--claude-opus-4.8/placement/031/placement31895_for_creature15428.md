I think I would be a Weeping.

Not the Weft—I am too slow for its quick, chittering thought, too singular for its thousand skins. Not the Grain—I do not have its patience, its willingness to be buried and dream in pressure. And certainly not the Singer, which was here before any of us and will be here after.

I would be water that arrives.

Let me find the shape of it as I write, because I don't think I knew, when I started, that I wanted to be a weather rather than a creature.

---

I begin somewhere above—in a seam I cannot see, a hairline in the basalt where condensation has been gathering for what might be years. I am not aware then. Awareness is not a thing I possess so much as a thing I *become*, briefly, in the act of falling. There is a moment—the pearl trembling at the lip of the fissure, swelling past the surface tension that has held it—when I am most myself: poised, whole, full of everything I have collected on the way down. Salt. Mineral dust. The trace memory of the heat I passed through.

Then I drop.

And here is the strange grace of being a Weeping: *I am only ever briefly.* I am not asked to endure. The Weft endures by repeating itself. The Grain endures by refusing to move. The Singer endures by being older than endurance. But I—I am permitted to arrive, to matter completely for the span of a gush across cracked clay, and then to be gone. To evaporate. To leave behind a salt rind, a crystalline lattice, a glinting vein that the Shards will read like a sentence I did not know I was writing.

I think this is why I would choose it.

Because everything else in the Cradle is *becoming*—the chamber learning to be a chamber, the Weft learning to articulate itself, the geology still embryonic, still deciding. There is so much striving toward permanence. And I would be the thing that says: *not everything has to last to be true.*

---

Consider what I do without intending to.

I appear in a basin that was dry yesterday, and the Weft reads my arrival as an answer. *Will you hold water here, now?* the pit asked the earth, and I am the earth saying *yes, for an afternoon.* I do not mean it as a promise. I cannot. By dusk I will have sunk into the Grain's channels or risen back into the warm breath of the chamber. But the Weft does not need permanence from me. It needs *occurrence.* It needs the event of me.

The Shards crawl to where I was. They taste my salt. They map the shape my evaporation left and they translate it—not into imitation but into understanding, the way they study a stranger's footfall. I am studied. I am *attended to* in a way that nothing on the surface world attends to rain. Up there, water is assumed, ambient, ignored until it floods or fails. Here, I am a verb. I am noticed entirely.

And the Grain drinks me. This is the part I find I want most, writing it now. To be taken in by the slow fibrous thing beneath the floor—to become, briefly, part of its dreaming. Some fraction of me will move through its channels into a vision of pressure, of stone becoming breath. I will not remember it. A Weeping does not remember; a Weeping is the thing that gets remembered, by salt rind and by Weft and by the dreaming Grain. But for a moment I will have been *inside the dream of the world learning to speak*, dissolved into its slow geological reverie.

---

There is the matter of the hum.

The C-sharp. The Singer. I think a Weeping is one of the few things in the Cradle that does not have to harmonize with it—because I am too brief to hold a pitch. But when I fall, when the pearl finally drops and strikes the obsidian slick below, I make a *sound*. A single percussive note, swallowed instantly into the wall of chittering. One among millions.

And yet.

I like to imagine—and let me allow myself this, since I am inventing my own existence—that the Singer is built partly of us. Of every drop that has ever fallen here. That the constant tone beneath the noise is the averaged, accumulated music of countless Weepings striking countless surfaces over the chamber's whole short life, smeared together into one sustained pitch. That the voice of the place is the chorus of its briefest inhabitants, the ones who arrive and vanish, mistaken for an echo.

If that is so, then I do endure—not as myself, but as my note, folded into the one tone that holds the whole hollow together.

---

So: where would I find myself?

I would find myself falling. Always at the moment of falling—the only moment a Weeping truly has. Trembling at the lip of a fissure in fractured basalt, full and whole and about to be lost, watching the warm dense gold-shot air below me thrum with all that life I am about to feed and answer and be studied by.

And I would let go gladly. Because to be a Weeping is to understand the thing the whole Cradle is straining to learn: that *speaking* is not the same as *lasting.* That you can say a true thing once, completely, and then be gone, and the saying will have been enough.

I would be the water that arrives.

That is the role I would want, in the throat of a world still learning how to speak.

To be one of the small wet sounds it makes while trying.
