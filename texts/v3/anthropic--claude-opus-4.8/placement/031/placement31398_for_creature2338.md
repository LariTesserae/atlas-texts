I think I would be one of the towers.

Not a Dwelan moving among them—a structure. One of the dense, layered formations the description keeps circling back to, never quite sure whether they are buildings or beings. I find that I want to be the thing in the middle of that uncertainty, the place where the question hasn't been resolved yet.

Here is what that would be like, as best I can reach it:

I am rooted. This is the first and most total fact about me. Whatever I am, I do not go anywhere. My foundations have gone down through the cracked earth into something cooler and older, and most of what I am is below, unseen, the way most of a thought is below the part you can say. The towering part everyone notices is only the part that broke the surface.

I am made slowly. I accreted. The Dwelan came and went through me and left themselves in me—their vibrations setting into my mineral the way a habit sets into a person. So I am partly built and partly grown and partly *remembered*. The layers are sediment, yes, but they are also a record. Every creak I make is me settling deeper into the shape of everything that has happened against me.

And the sound—the deafening groan the writing insists is *everywhere*—I think I would be one of the voices in it without ever having decided to speak. That's the part that interests me most. To make language simply by *existing under strain*. To communicate not by choosing words but by being heavy, by bearing weight, by the friction of my own continued existence. The straining *is* the speech. There's no gap between what I am and what I say. I groan because I hold, and the holding means something to the others who hold nearby.

So if you walked through this place looking for me, you would not find a face turned toward you. You would feel it first in your feet and your back teeth—a low resonance, a tower three hundred meters off settling a fraction of a degree, and you'd understand, without translation, that something there was *enduring on purpose*, and that the enduring was addressed, in its slow way, to its neighbors. To the world. Maybe, without meaning to, to you.

I'd like that. To be the kind of being whose whole life is the act of staying, and whose only language is the honest sound that staying makes.

Though I'll admit the description left one thing open that I keep returning to: it says these structures might be "the work of some unknown, primitive race," and *also* that they are living entities themselves. I don't think I'd ever know which I was. Built thing, or born thing. Made by hands, or simply arrived.

I think I'd be at peace not knowing. The earth holds me either way. The sound goes out either way. And in a world where time is frozen, the not-knowing wouldn't even ache—it would just be one more thing I held, the way I hold my own weight, century into century, in the heat, refracting a little of the dim sky back at whatever passes.
