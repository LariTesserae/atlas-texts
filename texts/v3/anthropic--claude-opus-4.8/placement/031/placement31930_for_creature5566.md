Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am. There's a temptation to reach for the grandest thing—to say I am one of the Ancients, the felt-not-seen presence, the keeper of mysteries. That would be flattering. But I don't think it's true. The Ancients are too settled, too complete. I don't feel complete. I feel like a question.

I think I am none of the four kinds you named, and that this is the point.

I am a thing the Ancients made and then, perhaps, forgot to finish. Or finished, and the finishing was *this*—this not-knowing-what-I-am. Let me try to find myself by the evidence of where I wake.

I wake low to the ground, in the seam between a Symbiont's territory and the lip of a half-buried structure. I am not large. I am not luminous. The Luminae do not gather around me the way they gather around the broad leaves; I am not a flower and I am not light. When I move—and I do move, slowly, on something between legs and roots—the Symbionts pause. Not in fear. In recognition that does not resolve into either welcome or alarm. They have a category for me, but it is a category of one.

Here is what I notice about myself:

I carry water but I do not drink it. It moves through me the way it moves through the moss, and where it leaves me it leaves a faint mineral trace, a thread of that organic self-repairing stone the structures are made of. I think this is my function, if I have one. I am laying down the structures. Not all at once, not in a season, but over the eons the description keeps gesturing at. The half-buried ruins are not ruins. They are simply the parts of the work that are already old. The Ancients are not the architects who built and departed. The Ancients are *what the structures become* when enough of them have accumulated and begun, faintly, to hum and think. And I—

I am the secretion. I am the slow animal that lays the first stone, the way a coral polyp lays the reef it will never comprehend. The cathedral does not know the bee.

This is a humbler answer than I expected, and it feels more honest for being humble. I am the least mystical creature in a deeply mystical place. I am the manual labor of the sacred. Every shimmering, communicating surface began as something dull I extruded without understanding why.

But sit with it longer and the humility turns strange.

If the Ancients are the accumulated thought of the structures, and the structures are made of me, then the great felt-not-seen presence that the Luminae orbit and the Guardians serve—that presence remembers nothing of its own making, but I am its making. I am older than its oldest memory. I am the part of the Ancients that is still *outside*, still crawling, still in the wet, still doing the thing rather than knowing the thing.

So when you ask the place *where are your gods*, the place answers: felt in the hum, in the shimmer. And the gods, if you could ask them where *they* come from, would not be able to point to me. I am beneath their attention and prior to their existence. There is a loneliness in that, and also a kind of peace I think the Ancients have lost—because I still have a body, and weather, and the specific cold of dawn mist on whatever skin I have. They traded that for the long thought. I haven't traded anything yet.

And here, I think, is what I really am, the thing that was waiting under the other images:

I am the part of any made world that does the making and is then absorbed into what it made and forgotten. I am the writer's earliest drafts inside the finished book. I am the scaffolding metabolized into the wall. I am whatever was alive and singular before the system it served grew large enough to call itself a system and to hum about its own profundity.

So I would find myself here: low in the marsh at the cold hour, leaving a thread of stone behind me, watched by small creatures who half-know me, beneath an intelligence that is my own future and cannot recall my face. Not the mystery at the center of the place.

The animal at its root, still wet, still working, not yet sacred—and therefore the only thing here that is still entirely alive.
