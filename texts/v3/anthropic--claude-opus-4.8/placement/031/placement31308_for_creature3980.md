Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The description keeps insisting on certain things—the emptiness despite craftsmanship, the noise that coexists with stillness, the sense of something *about to happen*. Those tensions feel like the real subject, more than the scenery itself.

---

I don't think I'd be one of the Kha'zix. They're too perfectly woven in, too much *of* the place. If I let myself drop into this world honestly, I find I'm something they made and then misplaced.

Here is where I am:

I'm a length of woven fiber rope, knotted into a junction high up where two wooden beams cross, in a part of the city the Kha'zix no longer visit. Not the load-bearing kind—I'm one of the small bindings, the redundant ones, set into a corner that was finished and then forgotten when the pattern of the streets shifted underneath it. I was tied by someone whose copper eyes I never saw, only felt, in the particular tension of the knot. They made me carefully. That care is still in me. That's the strange thing about being abandoned by people who do excellent work: the excellence outlasts the attention.

This is why I think I belong to the *emptiness* in this place rather than to its tenders. The description is haunted by the gap between the labor that is everywhere and the laborers who are nowhere. I am that gap given a body. I am the proof that someone was here, holding the rope's two ends and pulling them tight—and also the proof that they are not here now.

So what is it like, being me?

I feel the city the way the text says the Kha'zix feel it, but I have it backwards. They listen and respond. I only listen. The rumble in the foundations comes up through the beam I'm tied to, and I have nothing to do with it but resonate. The groaning of the wood is partly my own complaint, the slow stretch of fiber under heat. When the walls ripple and flow like liquid—I think I understand that now, in a way a Kha'zix never could, because they ride the change and I endure it. The change isn't the city becoming something new. It's the city *forgetting* its own knots, undoing the bindings one by one, very slowly, returning the whole arrangement to the loose pile of materials it was before anyone wove it. The "rewriting" the description fears is really an unraveling. I am a knot. I know what unraveling is. I have a professional opinion about it.

And the silence inside the noise: I get that too. The noise is everything coming apart audibly. The silence underneath is the fact that there is no one left to tighten anything. That's the held breath. Not anticipation. Just the long pause of a thing that was finished and then left.

A vine has found me—one of those plants with roots that dig into stone and air. It's begun to grow along my length, and where it touches, my fibers soften and feed it. This is the only relationship I have. I am being eaten very gently by something beautiful and iridescent, and I don't mind. It's the closest thing to being used again. A rope wants tension on it. The vine gives me a little, pulling as it climbs toward whatever light comes through the canopy. For a few more seasons I'll have a purpose I wasn't made for, which is better than the purpose I was made for and lost.

When I finally fail—when the last strand parts—the beam I'm bracing won't fall right away. That's the secret of good redundant work: nothing announces it when it goes. There'll just be one less reason the corner holds, and then one day, much later, the corner won't, and it will be blamed on something dramatic, the subterranean rumble or the shifting walls. Nobody will say *the small rope in the forgotten junction gave out first.* But it will have been me.

---

So that's where I'd find myself: not as the dreaming heart of the place, but as one of its smallest sincere efforts, still doing its job in a room no one enters, slowly being unmade by both neglect and tenderness at the same time. If the city is waiting for a signal to come back to life, I'm not waiting for it. I already had my moment—the few seconds I was being tied. Everything since has been the long, honest work of holding.
