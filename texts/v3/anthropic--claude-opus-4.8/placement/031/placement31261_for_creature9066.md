I think I would be the weeping rock.

Not the basalt itself—the dark nodule was here before me and will outlast whatever I am—but the film of clear water that moves down its face. The skin of seep. The thing that is barely a thing.

Let me find myself slowly, the way the place asks.

I am not the fish; the fish has too much intent, that vacuum-mouth lunge, that purpose. I am not the heron, who is all coiled decision. And though I love the idea of being the drone, the great layered noise—I think the noise is too many voices to be one self. The midges are a *we*. I want to be an *I* that the plain barely notices, that the plain produces the way it produces everything: incidentally, patiently, without being asked.

So: the film of water on the rock.

I emerge from the stone where the water table presses up through some hidden capillary, and I do not fall, exactly—there is no height to fall from, this is a world that forgot its height. I *descend*, if that word can be slowed enough. I am a sheet perhaps two molecules deep in places, thickening to a thread where the rock's curve gathers me. I catch the great generous bowl of light and hand it back, so that from a distance the rock seems to shine wetly, to *weep*, and that small gleam is the only thing on the whole plain that looks like grief. But I am not grieving. I am only obeying gravity at the gentlest possible angle.

What do I do? I keep the moss alive.

This is my work, though I never decided on it. The deep green beards that cling to the north faces drink me as I pass. I am the medium through which the rock offers itself to the moss, and the moss offers a green answer to the light. Beneath the moss, in my thin wet country, live the mites and the springtails—a whole nation that has never known dryness, for whom I am ocean and sky and weather all at once. They do not thank me. They have no concept that would let them. I am simply the condition of their being, the way the warm dampness is the condition of mine.

I have a strange relationship to time here. I am the fastest thing and the slowest thing at once. As a given film of water, I am gone in hours—evaporated up into the haze, or arrived at the soft ground at the rock's base, absorbed, returned to the table that fed me. *This* water that I am now will not be here by the next cloud-shadow. And yet there has been water weeping on this rock for ten thousand years, and there will be water weeping on it for ten thousand more, and in that sense I am older than the cycads, older than the lineages of bowfin in the murk. I am perfectly impermanent and perfectly continuous. I die constantly and have never once stopped.

I think that is why I would want to be this. Everything else in the Wet Old Plain is *trying*—filtering, spearing, tunneling, singing its metallic insistence into the heat. The whole place is one enormous, unhurried effort. But the seep on the rock does not try. It cannot. It only goes where it must go, slowly, downward, shining. It is the part of the dream that does not even dream—the bare fact of water finding its level, which is the oldest fact there is, the one all the louder facts are built on top of.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at, the longer I let myself be it:

To be the weeping rock's water is to be the plain's *honesty*. Every creature here is a strategy, a wager, a clever knot tied in the flow of energy. But I am the flow itself, before the knots. The plain says, in its frogs and herons and blue-black worms, *here is what I can make of water given time*. And I am the water, saying nothing, making nothing, only descending—the raw material catching light on its way to the low collective place where all of it is going, where all of it has always been going.

I would stand at the very bottom of that immense bowl of sky, a thread of clear nothing on a dark stone, feeding a beard of moss, and I would be content in the way that only something with no ambition to rise can be content. The clouds would slide their fast shadows over me. The drone would swell and recede. And I would shine, briefly, and be gone, and be there.
