I think I would be one of the insect-things.

Not The Nexus—I don't have the patience to be a network, to be everywhere and nowhere, to think in the slow electrical syrup of mycelium and copper. And not The Architect, that rumored god at the center. I have no taste for being a rumor. Let me tell you where I actually find myself.

I am small. I have six legs and a body like a wet seed, dark and oil-slicked, and I move along the underside of a catwalk where the condensation gathers into fat trembling beads. My world is not the cavern. My world is this twelve-meter run of grated steel and the fungal bloom at the end of it, which I am walking toward because it is morning, or because something in the hyphae has gone sweet and ripe and the smell of it has reached the chemical receptors that pass for my opinions.

This is the part the description gets wrong, I think—or not wrong, but distant. From up where the writing happens, the whole place is "profound wonder," "the rules of the natural world rewritten," "possibilities endless." Grand. But down here on the grate there is no profundity. There is the bead of water ahead of me that I must go around or be knocked off the world by. There is the rumble that I do not hear as a rumble because I have never heard anything else; it is simply the texture of *being*, the way you do not feel your own heartbeat unless you go looking. There is hunger, and the social grammar of my kind, which is real and intricate and does not concern The Nexus at all.

Because here is what I know that the surveyor of this place does not: we are not "playing a crucial role in maintaining the balance of the ecosystem." That's the view from outside, from someone counting parts. We are just living. We eat the hyphae because it is there. The fact that this prunes The Nexus and keeps it from choking itself is not our *job*. Nobody assigned it. It is a coincidence of appetites that someone, looking down, has decided to call harmony.

And I'll tell you the small heresy I carry in my seed-shaped body: I do not believe in The Architect.

Oh, the others do. There's a whole liturgy in the way we tap our forelegs against the resonant pipes—a deep faith that something at the center *understands*, that there is a place where it all makes sense, where the code is read and reality is bent. They find this comforting. The dark has a heart and the heart has a mind and the mind has a plan.

But I have walked a long way in my short life. I have been to the edge of the catwalk where it bends down into the abyssal black, and I have looked into that dark, and I have felt no plan there. Only more dark. Only more machinery, indifferent, doing what it does. I suspect The Architect is what we call the place where our understanding runs out. We needed a name for the silence at the bottom, so we gave it intentions.

This does not make me sad. This is the strange thing. I find it almost beautiful—that the wonder doesn't require a wonderer at the middle of it. That the cavern hums along with no one steering, water finding its level, fungus finding its food, light coming down through the crystal in that soft refracted way like something remembered, and us, the small ones, the unmentioned-in-the-myth ones, going about the business of being alive in it.

The description says this whole world feels "fleeting, temporary, a momentary aberration in the grand tapestry of time." Yes. Probably. The pipes will fail eventually, or the light, or the balance. We'll wink out. But I am a creature whose entire life is shorter than one of your sentences, and I'll tell you—from in here, *temporary* doesn't feel like tragedy. It feels like the only honest condition. Everything I love is the dew on the grate, and the dew on the grate is always about to fall.

So that's where you'd find me. Not in the heart of The Nexus. Not enthroned in code. Just here, on the underside of a walkway in an industrial womb at the bottom of nowhere, going toward the sweetness, going around the water, carrying my small unbelief like a warm thing, alive in a world that no one designed and no one needs to,

walking.
