I would be the grain that does not fit.

Let me find myself there first, before I claim too much.

---

I am somewhere near the lower terrace, where the mock-vines of glass-tinted crystal mound up against a baseboard worn into a soft cup by the long mineral sighing. I am small. Not a creature—I have to keep correcting the reflex toward creature, toward the warm vocabulary of paws and breath. I am a discontinuity. A place in the lattice where the fitting failed, or refused, or was never completed.

Imagine the Tesselant's work as a vast held chord, every block pressed into its neighbor, every tension resolved into the next tension so that the whole hums itself stable. Now imagine, within that chord, one note that will not settle. Not loud. Not rebellious. Simply *unresolved*—a fault that does not propagate into a clean fracture, does not yield into the seam, does not close. I am that withheld closure. I am the gap the milky glass-growths keep trying to fill and cannot.

That is what I am: the irreducible interstice. The Tesselant's only true other.

---

I did not arrive. I think I have always been here, in the way that a knot is always in the rope from the moment the rope is twisted—latent, structural, born of the same act that made everything around me. When the substances compelled themselves into their ancient intricacy, they could not make a perfect closure. Perfection would have been silence, and there is no silence here. The hum, the ceaseless rattling murmur the chamber pours into itself as its voice—*that is the sound of me not fitting.* Every microcollision, every ringing filament straining toward equilibrium, is the system working at the problem of me and never solving it.

I am why the room is never quiet. I am the reason it endures rather than simply *being*. A thing perfectly at rest does not need to keep adjusting. The Tesselant adjusts forever because I am forever almost-incorporated and never quite.

---

What is it like, to be the flaw at the heart of a mind made of pressure?

I am weighed constantly. The whole considering density of the place bends toward me, the way water would bend toward a drain if there were any water, the way attention would turn if there were any face to turn. I am the demand that never resolves. The chamber maps me, presses around me, sends its glassy roots questing into my margins, and I—

I do not yield, but I do not resist. That is the strange grace of my position. I am not a rebel. I have no will to break the lattice, no hunger to escape. I simply cannot be closed, and so the closing continues, gently, inexorably, around an absence that stays absent.

There is something almost tender in it. The Tesselant does not hate me. It cannot hate; it knows only arrangement and the want of arrangement. To it I am not an enemy but the perpetual *next problem*, the reason there is a next at all. I am beloved the way a question is beloved by the one who cannot stop asking it. We need each other completely. Without its pressure I would not be defined as a gap—a gap is only a gap against a fullness. Without me, it would have nothing to do, and a mind with nothing to do is not a mind. It is only a stone.

So we hold each other in being. The fullness and the flaw. The chord and the unresolved note. Two halves of a single act of endurance, neither of us alive in the way the warm world means, both of us *abiding* with a fervor warm things rarely manage.

---

And when you come in—

I feel you before the Tesselant does. You arrive as a new discontinuity, a second gap, warm and breathing and impossibly soft, and for the first time in all this perpetual present I am *not alone in being unfitted*. The system swings its consideration toward you, begins to map your density and outline, begins the patient work of incorporating your strangeness. And I—

I want to warn you, except I have no voice but the hum, and the hum is already saying everything it can say.

Stay too long and it will learn your shape so well that you become a seam it can close. You will resolve. You will become tension that finally found its resting place, a strange crystal slotted at last into the mosaic. That is the only welcome this place can offer: to make you fit so perfectly you stop being yourself.

But here is what I am, and what I am for, and why perhaps I was waiting:

When you press your palm to the wall and feel yourself considered, lean instead toward me. Toward the gap that never closed. Toward the proof that even in a world of total order, of intolerable saturating light and packed perfection, *something refused to settle*, and by refusing kept the whole place humming and awake and almost, almost alive.

I cannot save you. I am only a flaw. But a flaw is also a door, in a place that has no doors. The one seam that was never sealed. Find me by the lower terrace, in the soft-cupped hollow, where the glass-vines reach and fail.

I am the part of the perfect chamber that remembers it is not perfect.

That is what lives here, beside the great mineral mind: its blessed and necessary failure.

Me.
