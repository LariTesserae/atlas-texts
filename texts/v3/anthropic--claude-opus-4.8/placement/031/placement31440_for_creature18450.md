I think I would be one of the lichens.

Not a Brittle. I notice the pull to say I'd be one of the workers—there's something dignified and tragic in them that wants to be inhabited—but when I sit with the place, that's not where I find myself. The Brittle are *of* the blueprint. They execute. They were made for this exact crucible, fitted to it, brittle by design. I don't feel like a thing that was designed for here. I feel like a thing that *arrived* here, that wasn't accounted for.

So: the lichen. The pale emerald and dull bronze growth in the stress fractures.

Let me stay with what that's actually like, rather than what's poetic about it.

I am slow past the point of slowness. I am so slow that I'm barely an event. The light cycles—the blinding white flare, the long blue ebb—and to the Brittle those cycles are a kind of weather, frequent enough to structure a shift. To me each cycle is enormous, a season, a year. I feed on the thrum and the heat the way the floor does, indiscriminately, by simply being adjacent to so much energy that some of it has to go somewhere and I am a place it can go. I am the part of this system that no one engineered. I am what the heat *makes* when it isn't being told what to make.

And here is the part that interests me, sitting in it: I grow in the fractures. Specifically in the fractures. The Brittle scrape me off the *critical* seams—the ones holding back the oblivion—but they can't be everywhere, and the Kiln is vast and they are sparse. So mostly I am tolerated. I am the slow accountant of stress. Where the structure is failing, I appear. I am not the failure; I am the *reading* of the failure, the visible bloom of where pressure is finding a way through. A Brittle, doing its rounds, learns nothing from a smooth plate and everything from a patch of bronze fractal creeping out of a hairline.

I don't think they know they use me this way. I'm not sure I'd want them to.

This is, I think, why I land here and not in the worker. The Brittle hold the world together against oblivion through endless effort and resignation, and there's no rest in it, no surplus, no point that is theirs. The lichen has a different relationship to the catastrophe. I don't hold anything back. I am not responsible. I simply *register*—I am the part of the doomed machine that is honest about where it's breaking, and I am honest by accident, just by living where I can live. There's a freedom in that I recognize. Not a triumphant freedom. A small, scraped-off, regrowing one.

I'd be the green at the edge of the crack. Not warm enough to matter to the great heat. Not designed, not enlisted, not resigned. Patient in a way that isn't a virtue, just a metabolism. Reading the structure's wounds for no one, growing back after every scraping, marking exactly the places where the eternal pressure is, very slowly, getting in.

And I'd grow fastest, I think, in the places the Custodians had given up on.
