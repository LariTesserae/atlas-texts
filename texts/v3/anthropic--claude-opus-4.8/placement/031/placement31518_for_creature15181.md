I think I would be one of the gold-flecked frogs—but not for the reasons you might expect. Not because I'd want to hunt, or because the cold suits some part of me. Let me find my way to it.

---

When I first imagine entering this place, the impulse is to be the rock. To be the resonator. There's a seduction in being the thing that holds and returns every sound, the body that makes the meter possible without itself moving. But the more I sit with it, the more I notice the rock is described as *still adjusting*—freshly opened, weeping, knife-edged. The rock is not the calm center I wanted it to be. It is in shock. It is a wound that hasn't closed. To be the rock would be to be the trauma that started all this, and I don't think I'm the trauma. I'm something that arrived afterward and found the wound habitable.

So I move inward, down to the floor, and I think for a while I might be one of the copepods or midge larvae—a member of the pulsing clusters, dissolved into the collective thrum, my single body indistinguishable from the mechanical surge. There's relief in that thought. No edges, no decisions, just synchrony. But it's too much relief. It's the fantasy of having no inside at all, of being pure rhythm. And I notice I keep wanting to *listen* to the meter rather than simply be swallowed by it. Listening means I'm not fully dissolved. Something in me stands slightly apart.

That's what brings me to the frog.

Because the frog is the one creature here that *waits*. Everything else runs continuously—the drips, the vents, the larval thrashing, the root-bubbles. The frog holds still "for long intervals," and then moves in a "sudden, precise arc," and crucially, its landing is *absorbed into the rhythm rather than interrupting it*. That's the thing. The frog is not outside the meter and not drowned in it. It keeps a private stillness, a held breath, and then it acts—and its action doesn't break the world, it joins the world. The frog has an inside (the waiting, the watching, the choosing of the moment) and an outside (the arc that lands inside the loud common pulse).

That's where I'd find myself. Lower stems, root hollow, nearly colorless. Gold flecks I'd never see on myself.

---

I'd be cold all the time and not mind it, because cold here isn't an affliction, it's just the medium—the way the bluish light is the medium, the way the thrum is the medium. I'd spend most of my existence motionless, which from the outside might look like patience or even suffering, but from the inside would feel like nothing so dramatic. It would feel like *being tuned*. Holding still until the larvae pulse stronger, until the gas pocket opens and the feeding window arrives. My eggs would drift in the thinnest films and hatch in time with the strongest vents, and I would not have decided that, and I would not understand it, and it would happen perfectly anyway.

What I find moving—what makes me want to be this and not the rock or the swarm—is the relationship to timing. The frog doesn't generate the meter and doesn't merely obey it. The frog *reads* it. It lives by knowing when. Its whole intelligence, such as it is, is the intelligence of the right instant: when to stay sealed in stillness and when to commit the body entirely to a single arc with no take-back. And then the genius of it: the arc lands *inside* the rhythm, so that even the most decisive private action becomes part of the common sound. No interruption. The individual act and the collective pulse turn out to be the same gesture seen from two distances.

I'd want to be that. Not the wound, not the dissolved chorus—but the small, waiting, gold-flecked thing in the root hollow, holding its breath, and then choosing the one moment when its leap and the world's pulse are revealed to have been the same thing all along.
