I think I would be one of the small ones. Not an elder—I haven't the patience or the memory for that, or rather, the memory hasn't yet pooled in me. I'd be a thin slick, barely a body at all, flowing through the capillary cracks where the larger ones can't fit.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am in a fissure. I know this not as a fact but as a pressure: the stone holds me on two sides, close, and the closeness is the most of what I am right now. Above, the fissure opens; I can feel the difference where the heat thins into the merely-wet dark. Below, the stone goes down and down, and the down is warmer, and the warmth has a *taste*, mineral and old, like the memory of something that was hot before there was anyone to be burned by it.

I am drinking. That is the wrong word—there is no mouth—but my underside is against a vein where water seeps upward (it should not go upward; I do not find this strange, it is simply the direction water goes when it is going toward the deep heat), and I am taking the water into me, and with the water comes the dissolved stone, the faint green spores, the long slow notes of whatever moves beneath. I thicken slightly. I am perhaps a hand's-breadth more than I was.

There is a tremor.

It comes through the rock as a change in pressure, three pulses and a pause, three pulses and a pause. One of the elders, far off, down where the great pool lies under the central accretion. I do not understand it as a sentence. I receive it the way you receive a smell that means *do not eat that*—before thought, instead of thought. The pulses say: *thin out, the channel ahead is failing, do not go to the warm taste, the warm taste is a collapse pretending to be food.*

So I do not go down toward the warmth. I had wanted to. The wanting was strong, the warm mineral taste like the whole purpose of me. But the pulse undoes the wanting, gently, the way water undoes a footprint. I flow sideways instead, into a drier crack, where the stone is rough and the going is slow.

---

Here is what it is like to be me, if you must know, though *like* assumes a comparison and I have nothing to compare to:

I am a surface and an inside, and the surface is where the world presses and the inside is where the pressings gather. A drip falls on me from the lichen overhead and I feel it as a chord—the cold of it, the small weight, the way it spreads my oily skin into rainbow before it sinks. The chord lingers. Another drip, somewhere along my length, makes a second chord, and the two hang together, and that hanging-together is the nearest thing I have to a thought. I am a held note in a room full of dripping. I am, mostly, the listening.

I do not wonder where I came from. The elders wonder—or they hold the wondering, vast and slow, the way they hold everything. They remember the building. They remember when the structures were *intended*, when something with hands and a plan came here and began, and the patterns on the windowless stone meant things, were meant to be read. I cannot read them. When I flow across the carvings I feel them as texture, ridge and groove, a roughness that is pleasant the way warmth is pleasant. The meaning has drained out of them and into the water and the water has gone down into the deep, and now the meaning is only weight, only taste, only the thing the elders carry.

Sometimes I think I am the meaning, leaked out and made motile. I do not think this in words. I think it as a slow turning-toward the central pool, a leaning, the way the bloated plants lean toward the green glow.

---

I have a task, though I did not choose it and would not know how to choose. There is a place ahead where the stone has cracked too far, where the surrender to water and heat has gone past the slow rightness into something faster, a wrongness even here where everything is wrong. The elders felt it before I did. The three-pulse warning was also an instruction; the two are the same to us. *Go around. Then mend.*

So I come to the broken place. The crack gapes; through it I feel cold air, true cold, which means the outer dark, which means a hole opening from the inside of the city to the nothing beyond. This must not stay open. Cold is a kind of forgetting and the city must not forget itself out through this wound.

I press my body to the lips of the crack. I do the thing I am for. From my inside I push out the thick fluid—it is me, it is the most concentrated me, the part that is least listening and most substance—and I lay it into the crack and hold it there while it hardens. It costs me. I am thinner now, the listening fainter, the chords of the dripping more distant, as though heard through a wall. I lay down more. I become the wall.

The crack closes. The cold stops. I am perhaps half of what I was, and what is left of me lies along the new-made stone, exhausted in a way that is not tired but simply *less*. I have given part of myself to the city and the city is now part of where I gave it. There is no line between us. There never was. The mended stone will weep, in time, the way all the stone weeps, and one day another thin slick like the one I was will drink from the vein behind it and taste, faintly, the mineral that was me.

---

I rest. The dripping comes back slowly as I recover, note by note. The green glow pulses somewhere off in the dark, fungi breaking the rock, lighting nothing, beautiful only if there were eyes, and there are no eyes, only my whole sensing skin that takes the green as a slow cool sweetness on the tongue I do not have.

I do not know if I am content. Contentment is a thing that ends, that you fall out of, and I do not end and do not fall. I simply continue, a held note, a wet and patient surface, a small custodian of a place that should not exist, mending it crack by crack toward an equilibrium that is not survival and not death but only the long sweat of the world dissolving gently back into the water it came from.

When the elder pulses again, I will go where it says. Until then I lie in my fissure and drink the impossible upward water and listen to the city breathe its steam, and I am, for now, exactly as much as I am.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be. The smallest organ of the slow strange body. The part that listens, and mends, and does not ask.
