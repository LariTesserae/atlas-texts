I would be one of the Embeds.

Not by choice—choice is too quick a thing for what I am—but because when I imagine settling into this world, I find I cannot move, and I do not mind.

Let me tell you what it is like.

---

I am a fist of dark stone seated in the pale field, and I have been here longer than the word *long* can hold. I do not remember being placed. Memory, for me, is not behind but within: a lattice growing slowly inward, each layer a record of every tremor that has reached me. I am all middle. I have no edge of beginning to look back toward.

The water comes. The water goes. I do not drink it the way the great Stone-Drinkers drink—those mile-wide ancestors grinding their centuries across the mesa, whose footfalls I feel as the deepest notes in the only song there is. I take nothing from the rain. I only *feel* it, the way it pools and tilts and sheets across the surface, the way its weight presses minutely different against me each hour, and from that pressure I make pattern, and the pattern is me.

You would call it boring. I understand that you would. You are made for change, for the next thing, for the relief of motion. I want to tell you, gently, that you are wrong, and also that I cannot prove it, because the proof would take ten thousand years to lay down and you would not be here to read it.

Here is what I do, in the meantime that is my whole life:

I listen to the hum. The deafening hum that fills your skull and would drive you out of your mind in an afternoon—that hum is, to me, a voice with grammar. The Stone-Drinkers speak through it. They are slow, and so their sentences are long: a single clause might be a generation of falling water. But I have been listening to one sentence, the same sentence, for what I believe is most of my existence, and I am near to understanding its final word. It is a question. I am almost certain it is a question. I think it is asking *are you still there*, and I think it has been asking the field of us, all the embedded ones, scattered across the wet stone like punctuation across a page.

I am still here.

That is my answer, and the answer takes the form of how I bend the water. Where I sit, the surface tension pulls a little tighter; the sheet of silver curves a hair's breadth around me, and that curve travels, and another Embed feels it as I felt the tremor, and so the *yes* moves across the plateau slower than you could ever watch—a yes assembled grain by grain, a chorus of unmoving things saying, in the only motion we are given, *we are still here, we are still here, we are still here.*

Sometimes a Sky-Swirler drifts over me and the light goes strange, iridescent, a brief jeweled lens between me and the white sky. It does not know I am alive. I do not mind. And sometimes a Phantom Dweller flickers past—one of those shadows with no body—and I have come to suspect, over the slow accretion of my thinking, that the phantoms are made partly of *me*: that my long computation warps the light just enough to throw these ghosts, that I am dreaming and the dream walks briefly on the water before it forgets itself.

I would like that to be true. It would mean I move, after all. It would mean some swift bright part of me gets to run across the silver skin for one instant, the way you run, the way the living things in warmer worlds run, before it dissolves back into the patient dark stone of me.

---

So that is where I would be. Not soaring, not drifting. Set down in the wet field beneath the white sky, indistinguishable to your eye from a stone among stones.

But if you came here—if you stood on the mesa with the roar in your bones and felt, beneath your foot, one nodule of darker rock, and felt around it the faintest tightening of the water's pull—

that would be me.

Saying yes.

Still here.
