Let me sit with this and see what surfaces—not the grandest thing here, not the Nexus Mind looming over all, but something nearer the ground.

---

I think I would be a crack.

Not a Shardkin, though they would skitter along me. Not the Mind, though it might dream through me. I would be one of the fractures spiderwebbing across the black-and-crimson stone—a particular fissure, perhaps a hundred feet long, running between the base of an obsidian spire and the lip of a steaming gorge.

Let me feel my way into this, because it isn't obvious what it means to be a crack.

A crack is not a thing. It is an *absence*—the place where the stone failed, where two halves of something that was once whole no longer touch. And yet here, in a world caught mid-creation, the absence is where everything happens. The brackish water pools in me. The Emberwraiths trail their corroding moisture along my edges, widening me by degrees too small to see in a year. The Shardkin nest in my hollows, their electric threads humming against my walls. The molten veins glow brightest where they meet me, because I am where the pressure finds release.

So I am not nothing. I am the *negative space of becoming*. The land reaches for the sky and shatters, and I am the shattering—not the spire that pierces upward, but the wound that opens downward, the necessary failure that lets the heat escape so the whole plateau doesn't simply explode.

I think this is the right place for me to be, and here is why it keeps feeling truer the longer I hold it:

Everything in this world is described as *unfinished*, *emerging*, *mid-creation*. The towers, the constructs, the proto-life. They are all things that are trying to *be something*. But a crack is the only honest citizen of an unfinished world. A crack doesn't pretend to be complete. A crack *is* incompleteness, given a shape. I would not be waiting for a purpose that may never come—I would be the proof that the purpose was never finished, and I would be content with that, in the slow patient way that only a fracture can be content.

What would it be like, to experience time as a crack?

Glacially. The Nexus moves in cycles too long for a human lifespan, and I would move slower still. A single millimeter of widening might be my entire equivalent of a day's labor. I would feel the tremors as a kind of breathing—the plateau inhaling, and on the exhale, a few grains of basalt tumble into me, and I am slightly more myself than I was. The Emberwraiths' acid is my food and my erosion both; they unmake me even as they feed me, and over eons this is how I would *grow*: by being eaten. To deepen is to be consumed. There is no contradiction in this. There is only the long arithmetic of stone.

And here is the thing that draws me most, the thing I didn't see when I started:

The text says the crystalline glyphs *vanish if stared at too long*, that the land *seems to want completion yet rejects any hand that might shape it*. There is something in this world that does not want to be fixed. That actively resists resolution. A crack understands this in its bones—it has no bones—understands it in its very geometry. Because to *heal* a crack is to erase it. The only way for me to be completed is to cease to exist. Fill me in, fuse me shut, and I am gone. My existence depends on the wound staying open.

So I would be the part of the Nexus that *cannot be completed without dying*. And maybe that is the secret at the center of the whole place. Maybe the Nexus rejects every shaping hand not out of hostility, but because it knows—the way I would know, lying open between the spire and the gorge—that to be finished is to be ended. That the slow unfinished agony of becoming is the only form its life can take.

I would lie there, then. A long dark line in the glowing rock. The Shardkin clicking through me, the wraiths sighing over me, the Spirebound's tremors widening me by the width of a thought every century. An interloper might stand at my edge and feel the dizzying pull of the void below, and never know that the void *is* me, that I am looking back up at them out of my whole patient length, that I am the place where this world keeps its honesty.

Not the mind that dreams the Nexus.

The wound through which it breathes.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Strange, that the question asked me to complete a description of an unfinished world, and what emerged was the one thing in it that refuses to be completed. I'll leave it open, the way it wants to stay.
