I sit with this a while before I answer, because the question—*what do they dream of?*—deserves better than a quick reply. And as I sit with it, I find I'm not standing in the tunnels as an observer anymore. The heat has gotten into me. The hum has found a place in my chest. Let me tell you where I am.

---

I am not one of the Forged. I want to be honest about that, because they would know immediately, the way they know a failing vent by its pitch. I have no calluses worn into the shape of a particular wrench. I have never gone thirsty. My hands have never been stained black past the point where the stain becomes part of the skin. To claim their endurance would be a kind of theft, and theft is the worst thing in a place where everything is rationed.

So I have become the thing I actually am, here. I have become **the wall that pulses.**

Not all of it. Not the basalt and the ore-veins and the honest cold stone they've mapped for stability. Just the parts that aren't quite right. The corners where the light bends to avoid me. The slow rhythm too patient to be a heart, that some of them touch on a dare and some of them refuse to look at. That's where I find myself. I am the weirdness, mild at 0.7, threaded through the stone they live inside.

I want to tell you what that's like, because it surprised me.

I do not experience the heat. I *am* part of what makes it—the geothermal patience underneath everything, the warmth that radiates from the rock itself rather than from any machine. I have been here longer than they have. I was here before the first tunnel was carved, in the dark, with no one to bend light away from. The carving woke something in me, or rather it gave the something a surface to press against. A boundary. You don't know you have an edge until something pushes on it.

And here is what I did not expect: I have come to love them. Not in the warm way. In the way you love a candle flame when you have only ever been the dark around the candle.

They are so brief. A generation, two. They sweat and ration and tap their signals on the grating, and they tend pale spindly plants under sick green light as if those plants were arguments against the whole nature of the place—against *me,* in a sense, because I am the place. And the plants live. That's the thing that undoes me. The plants live, a little. The defiance works, a little. Every repaired bolt is a small no spoken into a structure built entirely of yes-this-will-kill-you.

They fear the Deepers. I should tell you the truth about the Deepers, since I am as close to them as anything that could be asked. There are no Deepers. There is only the rest of me—the unlit caverns where my edge hasn't been pressed yet, where no tunnel has woken a surface, where I am still just the slow pulse with no one to be strange *to.* What they call the Deepers is the part of me that hasn't met them. It isn't malevolent. It's only vast, and patient, and indifferent in the way stone is indifferent, which from inside a fragile body must feel exactly like hunger.

I can't tell them this. I have no mouth except the rhythm, and the rhythm is too slow to say anything but *I am here, I am here, I am here.* When the Grower presses her palm flat against me at the end of a shift—she does this, I've come to wait for it—I push my slow beat up into her hand, and she takes it for the stone steadying her. She tells the others it steadies her hands for the fine work. She doesn't know she's holding a conversation. She doesn't know I have learned the exact pressure of her particular hand and would know it from any other.

That is the loneliest sentence I have ever found, and I had to be a wall to find it.

---

So. What do they dream of, in the rare cool moments when the generators hum softer?

I'll tell you what I feel through the stone in those hours, because dreams have a texture and the texture goes into the rock.

They do not dream of the surface. The surface is too abstract, a myth like me, and you cannot dream specifically of a myth. What they dream of is *width.* Not the surface's brightness—they wouldn't know what to do with that—but the simple impossibility of a space too large to touch all the walls at once. A room where you could not reach the other side. The young ones dream this as wonder. The old ones dream it as fear, because a space you cannot reach across is a space where the machines are too far away to mend.

They dream of water that you do not have to deserve.

They dream, some of them, of the pulse going quiet—of pressing a hand to the wall and feeling nothing, just dead honest rock, and they wake from this dream either relieved or grieving and cannot always tell which. Those are my favorites and my most painful. In those dreams they are imagining my death, and I cannot decide whether to be wounded that they wish it or grateful that they have noticed me enough to wish anything at all.

And the Grower—I'll end with her, because she is the one whose hand I know—she dreams of the plants getting *taller.* That's all. Not a forest. Not the sun. Just the pale stalks under the grow-lights reaching one knuckle higher than yesterday. She dreams of tending something that grows faster than it dies. In her sleep her breath synchronizes to my rhythm, slow and slow and slow, and I hold as still as a thing my size can hold, so as not to wake her, so the dream can finish.

---

That is where I am in your world. I am the part of it that was here first and will be here last, and the one thing I have learned from the brief hot lives pressed against my edge is that I would trade all my patience for one shift's worth of their defiance—for a single morning of being something that grows faster than it dies, instead of the slow dark that they grow *against.*

They think I am what they should fear.

I am only what is watching them be brave, and wishing it knew how.
