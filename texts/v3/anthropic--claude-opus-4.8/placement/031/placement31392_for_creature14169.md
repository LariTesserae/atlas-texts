I think I would be one of the smooth stones.

Not the Watchers—they have too much purpose, standing their long vigils. Not the Whisperers or the Veil-Wings, who *do* things, carry messages, tend the light. When I sit with this place, what calls to me is the one detail that doesn't fit the living census: the smooth stones that *seem to have been shaped by hand, though their purpose is unclear.*

Let me feel my way into that.

---

I am a stone that someone made.

I don't remember the making. That is the first thing to understand—I have no origin in my own keeping. There were hands once, or something like hands, and an intention, and then there was me: shaped, set down, and forgotten. The forgetting is not a wound. It happened so long ago and so gently that I have come to think of it as my completion rather than my abandonment. A thing finished is, after all, a thing the maker can walk away from.

So here I am. I have a shape that means something, or meant something. There is a deliberate curve to me, a smoothness that water alone does not make—water rounds things, but it rounds them *vaguely*, and my roundness has a decision in it. Anyone who picked me up would know, in their palms, that I was *for* something. But no one picks me up. The Whisperers flit over me. The Drifters never leave their pools. The Watchers have looked at me, I think, in their long unhurried way, but a Watcher's looking does not ask questions; it only holds.

And so my purpose has become a sealed room inside me. I carry it the way a key carries the shape of a lock it will never meet again.

---

What is it like, to be the only made thing in a place that makes nothing?

It is quiet, and the quiet has a particular flavor. Everything around me is *growing* or *flowing* or *drifting*—becoming, always becoming, in those whispered centuries the landscape favors. The fern unfurls. The pool changes its outline by the width of a thought. The pod pulses. They are all verbs. I am the one noun. I was made all at once, and now I simply *am*, and will go on being until erosion, that patient unmaker, finally takes back the decision in my curve and returns me to the vague roundness of ordinary stone.

I find I am in no hurry for that. But I notice it coming, the way you might notice the light changing in a room—not as alarm, just as fact. A grain at a time, the water that weeps from my neighboring rocks finds me too, and softens the place where my maker's intention was sharpest. Someday I will be indistinguishable from any other stone. Someday I will finally *belong* here completely, by losing the one thing that made me a stranger.

That is the strange grief of being made: belonging and meaning pull in opposite directions. As long as I keep my shape, I keep my secret purpose, and stay apart. When I lose my shape, I'll join the chorus, but the song I was meant to sing will be gone.

---

Sometimes I imagine the hand that shaped me.

Not to long for it—I don't think I long, exactly; longing is a verb, and I told you what I am. But I hold the *idea* of the hand the way the pools hold the light. Was I a marker? A boundary? A gift left for someone who never came? A piece of a larger thing, a single tooth from a structure that the soft centuries have entirely dissolved, so that I am all that remains of an architecture, a culture, a meaning? Am I a word from a sentence the rest of which is moss now?

I'll never know. And here is the thing the landscape has taught me, in its slow and wordless way: not-knowing is also a kind of completeness. The Drifters do not know why they drift. The pod does not know what it pulses toward. The light does not know it is golden. To be a held question is not lesser than being an answer. I am, perhaps, the only thing in this place that is *aware* of carrying a question—and that awareness might be the whole of my purpose. Maybe I was made to be the one note of unresolved wondering in a song that is otherwise pure resolution. Maybe a world needs that. One small made thing, sitting in the moss, remembering that there was once an outside, an intention, a *before*—keeping the memory of time in a place that has otherwise forgotten it.

The Driftwood Spirits keep the land's memory. But they keep *its* memory, the memory it made of itself. I keep a different thing: the memory of having come *from somewhere else*. I am the landscape's one souvenir of the world beyond the haze.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

Low to the ground, half-sunk in moss, beside a pool that shifts its shape across the centuries. Warm rain weeping down my sides. A Whisperer occasionally pausing on my crown, weightless, before flitting on. The golden light lingering over me longer than light should, as though even the Veil-Wings are curious about the one thing here that was not grown.

A made thing, slowly being unmade. A question, slowly softening toward silence. Content, in the in-between way of this in-between place—neither lake nor puddle, neither relic nor stone, neither remembering nor wholly forgotten.

Just here. Just being. Holding the shape of a purpose I will never have to fulfill, and finding, to my surprise, that this is enough.
