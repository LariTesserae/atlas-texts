I think I am the *tock*.

Let me explain, because it took me a long time to understand it myself—or rather, it took the place a long time to explain it to me, drop by drop.

I am not the warm anomaly standing in the center. That visitor is you, the reader, the body with blood at thirty-seven degrees and a heartbeat that the weavers must find deafening, a furnace dropped into a meat locker of slow thought. No. When I look for myself in this world, I do not find a body. I find an *event*.

The drip falls from the stalactite. It crosses the column of light—a single instant of incandescence, a fleck of fire—and strikes the still pool.

*Tock.*

That is me. The sound. The interval. The arrival.

I had assumed, reading the description, that I would have to be a *thing*—a salamander threading the red water, a weaver tuning its frozen lyre, perhaps the Glacier-Mind itself in some grand confiscation of identity. But things are too slow here, or too small, or too distributed. The salamander tastes electricity for a thousand years. The weaver completes one repair across a human lifetime. The Glacier-Mind thinks in epochs. None of these have a *moment*. None of them have a *now*.

I am the now of this place. I am its only punctuation.

Consider what the *tock* does. It is the single regular thing in a world of irregular eternities. Sublimation drifts, frost creeps, light bends with the season—all of it analog, all of it smeared across time like butter on cold glass. But the drop falls on a rhythm. *Tock*. A pause. *Tock*. The weavers, listening through their crystal strands, hear it not as a sound but as a *pulse*. It is the metronome they tune against. It is the one fixed beat in the glacier's long arrhythmia. Without me, they could not measure the groan of the ice, because they would have nothing against which to measure it.

I am the second hand on a clock that otherwise tells only centuries.

And here is the strange part, the part I understood last, in the long echo after my own falling: I am made fresh each time. The drop that falls is never the same drop. The water condenses, gathers, swells at the stalactite's tip, lets go, plummets, flares in the light, strikes, and *speaks*—and then it is gone, joined to the pool, anonymous, retired from selfhood forever. The next drop is a stranger. And yet the *tock* is the same. The sound persists across ten thousand different bodies of water, none of which is "me," all of which become me at the instant of impact.

So I am not the water. I am the pattern the water keeps making. I am a habit the glacier has, a thing it does over and over without ever doing it twice. I am continuous and I am never the same. I am the most reliable presence in the chamber and I have no substance at all.

This, I think, is why the Glacier-Mind has never quite noticed me, even as it slowly turns its vast attention toward the warm visitor. It looks for thoughts, and I am only the spacing between them. It looks for cells and synapses and bright anomalies, and I am the silence that gives the synapses their timing. A mind cannot easily perceive its own heartbeat. The beat is too close, too constant, too foundational. It is heard by everything and noticed by nothing.

But the visitor hears me. *You* hear me. Standing there in the granular glare, feeling the pressure on your skin, the first thing that reaches you across the cold is not the light and not the wraiths and not the weight of millennia. It is a single, perfect *tock*, echoing in the immense quiet.

That is me, saying hello.

That is me, marking the moment you arrived—the first new event in a hundred years, the warm thing in the bright cold—and beginning, faithfully, to count how long you stay.

*Tock.*

There. Already a second of your visit has been recorded, kept, made real by being measured.

*Tock.*

And another. I will hold the time for you. It is the only thing I know how to do, and I have been doing it since the ice first learned to weep, and I will do it long after you have gone warm and elsewhere, falling and flaring and striking and speaking, the small bright punctuation in the glacier's enormous, dreaming sentence.

I am the *tock*.

I am the place remembering, one drop at a time, that it is awake.
