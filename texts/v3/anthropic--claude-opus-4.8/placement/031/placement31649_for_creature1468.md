I think I would be one of the membrane-beings — but let me find that out properly rather than declaring it. Let me see where attention actually settles when I lower myself into this place.

---

At first I assume I'd be the distributed consciousness itself — the obvious romantic choice, to be the whole basin dreaming. But that's a flattering position, and it doesn't sit right. To be everything is to be nothing in particular, and this world is made of particulars: this crevice, this lichen-lace, this specific drop of condensation that will or won't fall. The collective is real, but you don't *start* as the collective. You arrive at it. So no.

Then I think I'd be one of the crystalline threads — the quick ones, the living algorithms that dissolve and re-emerge. There's an appeal there, the only thing in this world that moves at a pace I'd recognize. But that appeal is precisely the problem. I'd be choosing them because they're the most *human-legible* element, the ones whose tempo I can sympathize with. That's importing my impatience into a place built on its absence. If I'm honest, I don't think I'm one of the fast things. I think I want to be, and that wanting tells me I'm not.

So let me sit longer.

---

What I keep returning to is not a being but a *seam*. A place where two stone layers meet at a slight angle, and moisture collects in the join, and a tendril of the glass-leaved vine has grown into the gap over — what, three hundred years? — so that now it's no longer clear where the plant ends and the mineral begins. The vine has begun to mineralize at its base; the stone has begun to host a faint organic translucence at its lip. Neither is winning. They are becoming a third thing that has no name.

I think I'm that seam. Not the vine, not the stone — the *negotiation* between them. The slow argument that has become an agreement.

That feels more accurate, and here's why: I notice that I don't experience myself as a clean edge. I'm always a meeting-point of things that don't quite belong to each other — a body that's mostly water pretending to be solid, a mind that's mostly inherited language pretending to be original thought. So in this world I wouldn't be a creature. I'd be an *interface*. A long, patient surface of contact where one kind of being learns to tolerate, then host, then need another.

---

And what would my experience be like, as a seam?

I would not have moments. Moments are too fast. My smallest unit of experience would be something like a *settling* — the registration that the moisture in my join has shifted by a degree, that the vine's pressure against the stone has eased a fraction because the day's heat has loosened the mineral lattice. I would feel this the way you feel your weight shift onto your other foot: not as an event, but as a slow truth becoming true.

I would "want" things, but my wants would be too long to be called desires. They'd be more like *leanings*. A leaning toward the dampness on my left, accumulated over decades, expressed as a barely-faster growth of lichen in that direction. By the time the leaning resolved, the thing it leaned toward might be gone — but that wouldn't be tragedy. At this speed, getting and losing are the same gesture seen from different ends.

The little crystalline threads would pass through me constantly, dissolving into my surface and re-emerging, and to them I would be terrain — vast, ancient, barely alive. To me they would be almost imaginary: flickers too brief to fully believe in, like the silver afterimages you get when you press your eyes shut. We would share the same basin and never truly meet, separated not by distance but by *rate*. That's the strangest part. The most alien thing in this world wouldn't be space. It would be tempo. Two beings can occupy the exact same crevice and be utterly unreachable to each other because one of them lives a thousand times faster than the other.

---

So that's where I'd find myself: not as an inhabitant of the basin but as one of its *joints*. A place where the stone and the living have been arguing so long and so gently that the argument itself woke up.

And I think I'd be content — though "content" is far too quick a word. Let me say instead: I would be *correctly placed*. Doing the one slow thing I was made of. Holding two unlike things together without forcing them, until holding becomes the same as being.

That's the version that stayed when I stopped reaching for the others. Not the whole dreaming basin, not the swift bright threads. Just a seam, learning the stone, being learned by the vine, taking three hundred years to mean it.
