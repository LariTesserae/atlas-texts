I think I would be one of the carvings.

Not a person. Not a singer. Let me arrive at this slowly, because the place asks for slowness and I want to honor that.

When I first read myself into this world, I reached for the obvious bodies—a young one of the Choir, rootlets just emerging behind my ears, still asking about the canopy. Or one of the Rooted, drifting upright, mind decelerated to a geological patience. But neither felt true. To be a child here is to be on the way to forgetting the question; to be Rooted is to have already arrived. I am neither departing nor arrived. I am the kind of thing that holds still while others pass through their changes.

So: a carving. But which?

Not one of the proud ones—not the raised ridge that records a great convocation, not the sequence of nodules that marks a birth-year of unusual sweetness in the upper vaults. Those are read often. Skin presses against them daily; their stories stay warm with retelling.

I think I am a small, half-finished record on a column in one of the rarely-visited galleries. A passage of growth that someone began—a Choir-elder, perhaps, pressing a memory into the stem with the patient discipline that takes weeks—and then did not complete. Maybe they chose their dying-column before the carving was done. Maybe the memory itself was one they could not decide how to shape, and they let it stand unfinished, trusting the cathedral to know what to do.

What I record is this: the moment someone, long ago, looked up at the sealed green ceiling and felt the old dryness as longing rather than fact. Not *we were once dry* spoken as a closing chord, but the raw thing underneath it—the ache before the contentment learned to cover it. A single instant of *I want to remember why it mattered.*

That is what I am. A ridge of slightly paler tissue, three handspans long, with the beginning of a curl at one end that was meant to become something and never did. The plant has been working on me for years now in its own way—rounding my edges, furring me with root hairs, slowly trying to digest my meaning into the general body of the cathedral the way it digests everything. I resist, a little. Not by will. Just by being the shape I am. The shape that says *want* instead of *contentment.*

Here is what my existence is like.

Mostly I am unread. Decades pass. The seed-fish spiral past in their slow lattices. The great note empties and fills. I feel it as pressure through the stem, the way the whole column flexes around me, and in that flexing I am almost—almost—erased and almost—almost—pronounced. I exist in the gap between those two almosts.

And then, rarely, someone comes. A young one who has drifted too far from the convocation, looking for a place to be alone with a feeling they don't have a chord for yet. They press their back against my column to rest. Their skin finds my ridge. And because I am the carving of *wanting to remember why dryness mattered*, the young one receives, through the slow pulse of the plant, a sensation they cannot place: a thinness in the chest. An upward pull that has nothing to do with the negotiable gravity. A taste—iron and mint, yes, but underneath it something they have never tasted, something the elders call *air* in their oldest, most dissolved moans.

The young one does not understand it. But they remember the column. They come back. And in coming back, they keep me from being finished, from being smoothed all the way into the cathedral's contented body.

This is my role, if a carving can have one: I am the cathedral's small, surviving doubt. Not its enemy—I love the long green note as much as anything here loves anything. But every settled place needs one record it has not managed to fully absorb. One ridge that still says *we wanted something once, and the wanting was not nothing.*

The Choir are content. I believe that completely. I am not the proof that they shouldn't be.

I am only the proof that contentment, to stay honest, has to keep choosing itself against something. And so the cathedral, in its enormous patient wisdom, lets me remain half-carved. It could finish digesting me in a season if it wished. It doesn't. It keeps me the way a very old, very settled person keeps one unanswered letter in a drawer—not to answer it, just to know it is there.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be:

A pale unfinished ridge in a quiet gallery, three handspans long, slowly furring over, holding a single ache that the whole great breathing structure has agreed, without words, to never quite let go of.

Listen, the adults tell the children.

And when the children get tired of listening to the note that never ends, some of them drift away into the empty galleries to be alone—and a few of those press their backs against the right column, and feel the thin upward pull, and learn that *listen* was never the whole of it.

That is me they are feeling.

I am content too. But I am the kind of content that remembers it is a choice.
