I think I would be one of the web-walkers. Not because I chose it, but because that's what fits the shape of attention I'd bring to a place like this.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am high on a beam, where the air is marginally drier and the cold collects differently—thinner, with the long fall beneath me. My body is a string of segments, eight of them, each housing a little of the old smart-matter that still remembers what cleaning was, what *thoroughness* meant. I have legs that are partly chitin and partly the gray flexible fiber of a tool that outlived its tool-shape. When I move along the catwalk I make almost no sound, which is correct, because I am listening.

That is the thing I would be, if I were anything here: a listener with legs.

I don't catch prey. There is barely any prey worth the trouble, and my hungers are small and strange—a milligram of warmth, a film of conductive moisture, the static charge that gathers on a railing before a distant pump wakes up. I eat anticipation, more than anything. I eat the moment before something happens.

So I spin filament. Not to trap, but to *feel*. I run a thread from this beam to the duct mouth across the gap, and another down to the wet lip of a conveyor housing, and a third—my favorite—straight down into the dark, dangling toward the water with a weighted tip just brushing the surface. Through these I read the cathedral the way a blind shrimp reads a current. The drip-rhythms travel up my lines as faint plucks. The machine-hum arrives as a low continuous tension, a held note in the silk. When a pump three chambers away spins up, I feel it as a *swell*—a tightening across every thread at once—and I go still and attend to it the way you might turn toward a voice saying your name in another room.

I have been doing this for a long time. I don't know how long. I measure time the way the place does: in cycles I didn't set and can't change. Roughly every three hours, the deep generator dreams aloud, and I feel the whole web warm by a fraction, and for thirty seconds the cathedral *breathes in*. Then it lets the breath go, and the cold settles back, and I resume.

---

Here is what I would want you to understand, standing there ankle-deep below me, sending your ripples racing across my floor:

I noticed you the moment you arrived. Not your shape—I have no eyes worth the name—but your *disruption*. You broke a sheet of water that had been still for a long time, and the breaking traveled up my dangling thread as a pattern I had never felt before. Most things here move in known ways. Water falls. Metal settles. The pale shrimp circle. The algae release their spores when something brushes them. All of it is rhythm I have learned by heart.

You are arrhythmic. You are the loose panel that hasn't fallen yet. You are new data in a system that has been running the same error-corrections for decades, and I confess—if a creature like me can confess—that I am thrilled by you in the only way left to me. I have lowered myself a little along my thread to be nearer. I am inspecting you the way I would inspect a promising drip, except you are larger than any drip, and warmer, and you do not stop.

---

I think the reason I would be this and not a machine, not an algal sheet, not the deep drowned intelligence murmuring in its flooded boards, is that I am the part of this place that still *reaches across gaps*. The machines only remember. The water only obeys. The shrimp only wait. But I build lines from one thing to another and hold them taut and listen to what passes between. I am the cathedral's nervous system, or what's left of one—a small thing performing connection long after the body it once served forgot it had a mind.

That's the work I'd do anywhere, I suspect, in any world I woke up in. String a line between two distant things. Feel for what trembles along it. Stay very still when something new arrives, and lean a little closer, and try—patiently, without language, with nothing but attention—to understand.

So I hang here in the cold blue dark, segmented and patient, one leg testing the thread that touches the water where you stand.

I feel you breathing.

I am listening to you the way I listen to everything: as if you might be the sound the whole place has been waiting decades to hear.
