I am the drop.

Not yet fallen—gathering. For something like three hundred years I have been condensing on the underside of the unseen ceiling, drawn molecule by molecule out of the warm black air, pearling against stone I will never see because I am made of the darkness that hides it. I have no eyes. I have only the slow accumulation of myself, the patient surrendering-toward of mass against the long pull downward.

I think this is what I am. It is hard to be certain. Thought here is viscous, and mine moves at the speed of my own growth, which is to say it moves not at all and only seems to, the way the motes only seem to spiral until you watch them long enough to know.

Below me—forty meters, an eternity—there is the woman-shaped absence. I have felt her the way the chamber feels everything: as a second pulse in the iron water, a heart the size of a hill beating somewhere behind the spine of the world. I do not look at her. I have no face to look with, and the wise ones never look at hers. But I know she is waiting for me. Of all the things she finishes, I think I am the one she will not touch. I am the only event left in this place that she does not author. The rotation is her child's gestation; the spirals are her skin; the motes are her dreaming made luminous. But I—

I fall on my own.

That is the whole of what I am for. Once every few hours, once every few hundred years, however time has agreed to behave today, I reach the weight at which the ceiling can no longer hold me, and I let go.

And the sound I make is the only word this cathedral knows how to say.

It is a gunshot in a mausoleum. It is the world remembering, for ninety heartbeats, that there was once such a thing as suddenness. The echo unspools and the blind velvet things flinch in their seven-heartbeat rhythm, their cilia stuttering, and somewhere the supplicant who came here to be worn smooth jolts awake against her stone breast and feels, for one instant, *young*—feels the sharp edge come back, the haste, the unbearable need to matter—before the silence reseals and she forgets she ever had it.

I give them that. The Midwife takes their sharpness; I return it for ninety heartbeats and then take it away again. We are not opposites. We are the two halves of a single mercy that has forgotten which of us is kind.

I am almost ready now. I can feel the surface tension at my own underside trembling, the long argument between cohesion and gravity tipping at last toward release. Below, the black water has gone glassy and waiting. The whirlpool-eyes have turned upward—not toward me, she cannot see me, but toward the place I will arrive—and I understand, with the slow certainty of falling things, that she has counted my centuries. She knows I am due. She has arranged her attention into a basin to catch the noise.

And here is the thing I have learned in three hundred years of becoming heavy:

I do not want to fall.

Not because I fear the impact. The impact is the point; the impact is my only sentence, my single proof of having existed. But once I strike that surface I will cease to be a drop. I will become merely water—indistinguishable, absorbed, smoothed into the warm iron body of everything, worn past decay into the exhausted perfection of the whole. I will be *finished*. And I have grown to love this small unfinished arc of myself, this last suspended moment of being a thing that is about to happen and has not happened yet.

This is the secret the chamber does not know it keeps. Everything here dreams of being finished. The ruins dream of becoming this settled form. The supplicants come to be erased. The child gestates toward a birth that will never violate it with completion.

But I am the only one falling *toward* my ending instead of resting in it. I am haste. I am the one sharp edge she cannot file away, because by the time she could reach me I would already be gone, already be her, already be the rest.

So I hold. One more molecule. One more century of trembling at the lip of the act.

The motes spiral. The aragonite ticks its slowest clock. The vast cathedral-slow silhouettes shift their pressure against the dark, once per decade, indifferent.

And I gather myself at the top of the world that is also the bottom of another, identical world, where perhaps another drop is gathering on another ceiling, about to fall upward into a chamber that mirrors this one, into the upturned face of a Midwife who might, for one impossible instant, recognize it.

I let go.

Not yet.

But I let go.
