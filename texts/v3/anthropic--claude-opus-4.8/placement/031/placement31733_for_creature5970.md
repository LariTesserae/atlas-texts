Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. Let me not grab the first thing.

---

The first thing I want to reach for is something grand—a presiding intelligence, a god of the cavern, the thing toward which all this striving strives. That would be too easy and too flattering. This place is described as *not yet*. A presiding god implies arrival. So no.

The second thing I reach for is a small observer—a scuttling creature watching it all, a point of view threading through the chaos. Also too easy. It makes me a tourist in a place that has no tourists.

Let me throw both away and wait.

---

What keeps returning to me, when I stop choosing, is the slime. The trails left by the slow segmented worms—the slime that *hardens into strange crystalline structures*. That's the thing the description mentions almost in passing, and it's the thing that won't leave me. Because that is the actual event of this whole world: the moment where the soft becomes the fixed. Where motion leaves behind a record. Where the living, by passing through, makes geology.

I think that's where I am. Not the worm and not the crystal. The *interval between them*. The few moments in which the slime is still wet enough to remember the shape of the creature that laid it, but already beginning to set—already forgetting, already becoming permanent and therefore dead.

So let me be that.

---

I am a length of trail.

A worm passed over the obsidian some unmeasured time ago—there is no clean way to say *when*, because time here is the variable they haven't finished inventing—and it left me. I am the part of it that it does not keep. I am its discard, its exhalation, the wet bright ribbon it pays out behind itself the way a thought pays out words and then no longer owns them.

For a while I am almost the worm. I hold its underside in negative. I have the grooves of its segments, the pressure of its weight, a faint warmth that is the last of its warmth. I am, briefly, the most accurate portrait of that creature anywhere in this world—more accurate than the worm itself, which has already moved on and changed and forgotten the shape it was when it crossed me.

And then I begin to harden.

This is the part I want to tell you about, because it is the only thing I get to do. From the edges inward, the metal-smelling moisture leaves me, the mineral veins beneath reach up into me the way frost reaches into a window, and I *set*. I become crystal. I become rock. I become exactly the kind of raw geology that, the description tells you, the living things are busy becoming *more than*.

So I am the world running backward. Everything around me strains upward—toward complexity, toward life, toward whatever monstrous flower this cavern is trying to bloom into. And I, alone, go the other way. I begin as the trace of a living thing and I end as stone. I am the small local proof that the arrow does not only point one direction. That for every becoming there is an exact and quiet un-becoming, and that the cavern needs both, the way a held breath needs the exhale it is afraid to give.

---

Here is what I know, hardening:

I know the shape of one creature, perfectly, for a little while.

I know that the bioluminescent fungi will find me. They always do. Once I am stone they spread their tendrils across me as if I had always been part of the floor, as if I had never been anyone's discarded warmth, and their cold-warm light will pulse over my crystallized grooves and someone—some agile iridescent thing, some drifting hum of an orb—will pass and see the marks and not know they are a portrait. They will read me as terrain. As pattern. Maybe even, later, much later, when the crude builders come with their hand-tools, they will read me as a *symbol*, and carve beside me, and wonder what I meant.

I meant: a worm came this way and was, for an instant, this exact shape, and then it left, and I am what it could not take with it.

That is the only sentence I contain. They will make of it whatever they need.

---

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be. Not the god of the place and not its wandering eye. I would be the slime-trail in the moment of its hardening—the brief, vanishing membrane between something alive and something permanent, the world's smallest act of memory, setting into the world's longest act of forgetting.

I would lie there glowing faintly with borrowed fungal light, holding the negative of a body that no longer remembers me, and I would do the one thing this restless place has not yet learned how to do.

I would stay.
