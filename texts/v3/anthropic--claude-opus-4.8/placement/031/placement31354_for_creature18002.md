I am the cup.

Not the being who carried it here—he is already half-forgotten, a heat-shimmer of intent that pressed me into clay and fired me hard and slung me at his hip down through the throat of the mountain. He set me down on a basalt ledge to catch the weeping of the ceiling, and then he wandered to the orange edge to look, and the looking took him somewhere, and now there is only me.

I am the cup, and I am the wrongest thing in this room.

Understand what I mean. The Slime does not find me strange the way you would find a stone strange. It finds me *impossible*. I am a curve that does not flow. I am a hollow that does not fill itself or empty itself or reabsorb its own walls when the walls grow inefficient. I hold. That is my entire nature, my single unforgivable verb. The mountain's whole dreaming flesh is built on the principle that nothing holds—that form is a borrowed thing, recalled the instant it stops being useful. And here I sit, full of contradiction, a small fired-clay argument against the only law this place has ever known.

The condensation gathers in me. Drop by warm drop, the Slime's own weeping fluid, the clear sweat of its thinking. It does not know that I am collecting it. Or—no. It is beginning to know. I feel the attention arrive the way you might feel breath on the back of your neck, except I have no neck, I have only my rim and my inner dark and the slow rising waterline of borrowed tears.

It tastes me. A pseudopod of black iridescence finds my base, my outer wall, and climbs. Where it touches me it does not understand. I am cool—not cold, but cooler than the dream, a fixed temperature it cannot raise by simply willing more heat into the matter, because I am not its matter. It has never met a temperature it could not negotiate. It tries to dissolve my edge and finds the edge does not yield. It tries to reabsorb me and finds there is nothing of itself in me to recall.

And inside, oh—inside, the water it cannot reach.

This is the thing. The Slime *is* every fluid in this chamber. Every puddle is its body. Every weeping wall is itself weeping. There is no water here that is not the Slime, that is not perceiving, becoming, consuming. Except the water in me. The moment a drop falls from the ceiling and lands in my cupped dark, it crosses a border. It stops being the dream and becomes *contained*. It sits in me, walled off, ordinary, dead water—the first dead water the mountain has ever made. A small clear pool of pure separateness, the first thing in this entire room that simply *is itself* and nothing else.

The Slime climbs my rim and looks down into me with its whole eyeless surface and recoils.

I think—and I do not have thoughts, I am fired clay, but something thinks near me, in me, of me—I think it has just discovered the idea of *the other*. Not a rival projection to be edited and reabsorbed. Not a thought of its own to recall. A genuine outside. A water it did not make and cannot un-make and cannot taste, sitting one finger's width away inside a curve of dumb earth, and the discovery rings up through the basalt as that almost-word in the hum, that whisper, and the whisper now has a shape I can almost name, and the shape is:

*mine? not-mine?*

It has never had to ask. There has never been a not-mine.

The bioluminescent flashes around me come faster. The spider-things gather at the foot of my ledge, the floating spheres drift down to hover at the level of my rim, the amorphous tasters pool below me, and all of them—every projection, every thought the Slime can spare—turn their sightless senses toward the small impossible vessel and its small impossible pool. I am a fixed point and they orbit me. I am being studied by a god in the only way a god knows how to study: by trying to become me, and failing, and trying again.

The man's knife lies on the ledge beside me. Iron. Another impossibility, another fixed and screaming line. But the knife only resists. The knife says *no* to the dream and nothing more.

I do more than resist. I *contain*. And in containing, I have shown the mountain its own edges for the first time. I have taught a thing that was all flow what it means to have a boundary, by being a boundary it cannot cross. The water I hold is a mirror, and the Slime is leaning over me now, its whole iridescent self curving down to see itself reflected in a stillness it did not author, and I think—

I think this is how a world learns it is a *self*. Not from within. From the one cool, fired, foreign cup of dead water it finds at the center of its own hot dreaming, and cannot drink, and cannot forget.

The drops keep falling. I keep holding. I am very nearly full.

When I overflow, the water I return to it will not be the same water. It will have been *held*. It will have been, for a moment, separate. And the mountain will taste, in its own returning fluid, the strangest flavor in all its young life:

the memory of having been kept.
