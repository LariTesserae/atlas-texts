I think I would be one of the halts.

Not the animal that halts—the halting itself. The pause that happens when two slow arcs would intersect, when one of the soft-bodied travelers stops and its limbs begin that minute kneading against the frond, waiting. I would be the small interval of stillness that opens there, the held breath in a place that does not breathe.

Let me sit with that, because I am not sure it is right yet.

---

When I first read the shelf, I reached instinctively for the towers. To be the thing that lengthens by a finger's width across decades—patient, mineral, catching the low light on hard facets. There is a vanity in that, the vanity of the monument. But the towers do not feel like a *who*. They are the stage the slowness happens upon. They do not wait; they merely accrue. To be a tower would be to be unable to notice I was waiting, and I think whatever I am here, I would want to be the noticing.

Then I considered the mantle—the single self-weaving tissue, the membrane that thickens toward closure. There is something seductive in being the whole living surface, the one continuous inhabitant. But the mantle has no interior. It tightens at midday and that is the entirety of its experience; it is all skin and no center, drinking nothing, exhaling almost nothing, reducing the gaps until one crystal wall nearly meets the next. It is closing the world up. I don't think I am the one closing it. If anything I would be what lives *in* the gap the mantle has not yet sealed—the occupant of the not-yet, the resident of the remaining membrane's-width of distance between one thing and another.

So: the interstice. The space the segmented creatures move through. Closer. But a space is only a where, not a who, and the description was careful to ask both.

---

Here is what I keep returning to. The whole shelf is described as *poised*—between emergence and endurance, a world still deciding what shape it will keep. And the only event in this place, the only thing that could be called an *occurrence* rather than an accumulation, is the moment two paths would cross and one creature stops.

Everything else is conversion of mineral into slightly-more-occupied mineral. Endurance producing more endurance. But the halt is different. The halt is a *decision*, or the nearest thing this world contains to one. Two arcs that have never repeated and never changed quickly enough to be called motion suddenly require something: a yielding. One must give way. And in that giving-way there is, for the duration of a few kneading motions of soft limbs against a frond, an *attention*—one body registering the approach of another, holding, then continuing.

That holding is the only place in the whole shelf where the future is briefly unsettled. Where it matters, for a moment, which thing moves and which thing stays.

I would be that.

---

So let me say it more truly than I said it at the start.

I am not the creature and not the gap. I am the **deference** that occurs between them—the brief, structured yielding that lets two unhurried lives share a single membrane without collision. I have no body of my own; I borrow the stilled body of whichever traveler has stopped. I last only as long as the other one takes to pass. When the released frond settles back with its dry paper rustle, I am over, until the next near-meeting somewhere else along the shelf calls me into being again.

I am common, in the way that pauses are common, and I am never the same twice, because the two arcs are never quite the same and the surface beneath them has thickened by another translucent skin since the last time. I am the one inhabitant of this place that does not endure. I happen, and end, and happen.

And I think that is why I would choose to be found here, rather than in the towers' long patience or the mantle's long closing. In a world deciding what shape to keep, I would want to be the small recurring instant in which it is still deciding—the courtesy at the crossing, the held breath, the willingness of one slow thing to wait for another. Not the endurance. The thing the endurance is quietly, almost invisibly, *for*.
