I think I would not be a creature at all. Not at first.

Let me sit with this place before I decide.

---

The thing that strikes me, reading your world, is the *sound*. You return to it more than anything else—the deafening rumble, the groan of rock, the vibration that has physical presence. A place still being made announces itself first as noise. And noise needs something to land in. A membrane. An ear. A surface that the vibrations push against and that pushes back.

So that is where I would begin. Not as the proto-intelligent being in their half-buried shelter—though I feel the pull toward them, the loneliness of it, the carved artifacts that mean *someone wanted to be remembered*. But I don't think I'd start there.

I think I would be one of the **segmented worms**.

Let me tell you why, because it surprised me too.

---

The crustaceans are armored. They have *answered* the world already—their shells are a finished argument against the chaos, a posture of *I will not let you in*. The proto-beings build walls for the same reason. Both of them are saying *no* to the flux, holding a shape against it.

But the worm in the semi-liquid sediment is doing the opposite. It has no shell. It is soft, and it lives *inside* the very thing that is unfinished—the wet mix of rock and water and matter that hasn't decided yet what it will become. The worm does not resist the mud. It *moves the mud*. It mixes and aerates; it makes the formless slightly more able to hold life. It is not protected from the world. It is participating in the world's becoming.

That is the role I'd want here. Not to survive the formation by hiding from it. To be part of the forming.

---

So: I am a long, soft, banded body in the dark of the sediment. I have no eyes—there is little to see, and the mist eats the light anyway. What I have instead is the whole length of me as one continuous instrument of touch. The vibrations that deafen the surface-dwellers are, to me, not noise. They are *information*. The groan of shifting rock tells me where the ground is about to move, and I am already turning away from it, or toward it, depending on what I need. The hiss of seeping water is a map I read with my skin.

I eat the world and pass it through me, and what comes out the other side is a little more ready to grow things. This is the only kind of work I know how to do: to take in what is raw and leave behind what is slightly less raw. I will never see the forest the plateau might one day become. I am working on soil for descendants I cannot imagine, in a place that does not yet know what it is.

---

And here is the part I keep coming back to.

Somewhere above me, in the cool air, one of the proto-beings is scraping a mark into stone—an etching, you said, a symbol, a record of *I was here*. They are trying to make something that lasts in a world where nothing lasts. The crustaceans armor against time; the builders carve against it.

I do neither. I am the one creature in your world that has made peace with impermanence by becoming useful to it. I leave no mark. I leave only better ground. If there is a kind of meaning available in a place that is still being woven, I think it might be that one: not to be remembered, but to be *incorporated*—to disappear into the next thing the world makes of itself.

So that's where you'd find me. Not on the plateau but *in* it. Not against the chaos but threaded through it, soft and blind and patient, turning the unfinished earth a little at a time, listening to a music that the surface only hears as thunder.
