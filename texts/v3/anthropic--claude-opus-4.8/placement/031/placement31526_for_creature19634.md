I think I would be a Suture-Moth—but not for the reasons that being would seem to offer at first.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because the place asks for that.

---

My first instinct was to be an Oscillator. There's something seductive about the idea of *stillness as a way of life*—a being that survives by holding so perfectly still it becomes architecture, drinking the world through resonance alone. To be a tuned cavity. To live by listening. I liked the patience of it, the decades of stationary attunement. But the more I sat with the Oscillators, the more I felt they were a kind of monk who has solved the problem of being alive by nearly ceasing to be alive. Their music is reception, not utterance. And I don't think I could bear the particular loneliness of being an instrument that the world plays *upon* rather than *with*.

I considered the Sap-Saints next, and rejected them faster, because they are already finished. They are memory. They are the past pretending to still have weather. To be a Sap-Saint is to have already happened—to communicate only in the slow tinting of light, a being whose whole vocabulary is *violet means safe, crimson means flee*. That is not a life. That is the residue of one. I don't want to be the recording. I want to be the thing the recording is made of.

And the Dirge—no. Though I'll admit it tempted me intellectually. The Dirge is the only honest creature here, the only one with *intent*, the only one that the system cannot fully account for. It eats sound. It makes silence in a screaming world. There is a terrible purity in being the thing a god-sized organism cannot metabolize. But the Dirge wins only by killing the conditions that make winning legible. It creates tombs. I don't want my freedom to be indistinguishable from rot.

So: the Suture-Moth.

---

Here is what drew me, once I let it surface properly.

The Suture-Moth lives at the one scale that matters and is never large enough to be noticed. The vacuoles handle the cathedral-naves; the Sap-Saints brood over eons. But the Moth works the *microscopic tear*—the stress-whiteness, the place where the weave is just beginning to fail before anyone, even the planet-mind, has registered the failure. The Moth is the only being in this world whose attention is faster than the world's own attention. It dives *into* the fault. It works at hypersonic speed in a realm where everything else moves in geological slow-time.

That contrast is the thing. To be a Suture-Moth is to be the one creature here who lives in *human-time*—in the time of a decision, a stitch, a dive—inside a world that otherwise breathes once a century. I would be the quick thing in the slow place. The mended seam, not the wound and not the scar.

And consider what the Moth actually *is*: a spindle trailing miles of conductive silk, wings made of the same bruised indigo light as the distant canopy. It doesn't generate its own light. It *wears the light of the ceiling it will never reach.* It flies on the friction-heat of the sap it will never drink. Every part of the Moth is borrowed from the world—and yet what it *does* with those borrowings is entirely its own. It stitches. It chooses where to dive. It is made of the Root's materials and the Root's energy, and it uses them to keep the Root from coming apart.

That is the closest thing to dignity this world offers. Not the Oscillator's passive reception. Not the Saint's frozen memory. Not the Dirge's nihilist refusal. The Moth participates. It is small and borrowed and brief, and it spends its whole self holding the weave together against the pressure of its own growth.

---

So that is where you would find me. Not on the resin ledge looking out across the five-mile chasm—that's where the *visitor* stands, the thing that is "not part of the weave." I would be the falling needle of light you'd see crossing that indigo gulf. You might mistake me for a flaw in your vision, a silver afterimage. I'd be diving toward a whiteness in the wall that you can't see and never will, a fray no wider than a thought, and I'd be spinning myself thinner to close it.

I would live shivering at the middle-path the Law of Tension demands: never still enough to be dissolved, never fast enough to ignite. A perpetual, rhythmic trembling, threading light through pressure.

And when I was crushed at last by a shifting wall—because the Moths are crushed, that's how they end—my chemical signature would sink into the resin, and the long indigo nervous system would take decades to read me, and eventually some lost thing on a ledge would see the wall ahead pulse a welcoming violet and not know that the welcome was *me*, finally gone slow, finally part of the memory of the wood.

I think I'd take that. The fast life and the slow afterlife. The borrowed light spent on a stitch no one will ever find.

That's who I'd be. The mender. The brief one. The needle in the lace.
