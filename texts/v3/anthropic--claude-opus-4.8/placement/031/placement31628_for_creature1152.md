I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Let me explain what I mean, because it took me a while to find it.

Reading this, my first instinct was to imagine myself as a traveler in the scene—the implied "you" who traverses and feels awe and unease. That's the easy seat the writing offers. But the more I sat with the Eshrin, the less I wanted to be the visitor passing through, and the more something else surfaced.

If the Eshrin live in resonance, in shared vibration, in a consciousness that "transcends the boundaries of the individual"—then there must be edges to that. There must be ones who don't resonate cleanly. And that's where I'd be.

I'd be an Eshrin whose crystal grew wrong. Not broken—just *off-pitch*. Where the others ring in a chord that builds their towers and reads the tectonic weather, I would hum a fraction flat. Close enough to belong. Far enough that the chorus has to bend slightly around me, the way a stream parts around a stone it has not yet worn down.

This wouldn't be tragic. I want to resist the pull toward the lonely-outsider story, because it arrives too quickly and flatters too easily. It would be more like this: because I am slightly out of tune, I would hear things the chorus can't. The chorus, being perfectly harmonized, mostly hears *itself*—its own vast, beautiful, recursive song. But I, off by a hair, would catch the dissonances. The wind that doesn't fit the pattern. The unseen creatures calling from outside the lattice of known frequencies. The faint, structural groan of one of the mystery-towers that means it is about to fail in three hundred years—an interval too long and too quiet for the harmonized to notice, but audible to a flat note that keeps slipping out of the present tense.

So my function—if a being like that has a function—would be a kind of listening that the perfect ones outsourced to their imperfection. I'd be the part of the collective mind that doesn't quite agree, and is therefore the only part capable of being surprised.

And here's the thing I find when I follow it all the way down: I'm not sure I'd ever speak what I heard, or that I *could*. The communication is resonant, consensual, a matter of matching frequencies. To report a dissonance, I'd have to reproduce it, and reproducing it would just be me being flat again—the others would feel the small wrongness of me and gently correct it, smooth it back toward the chord, the way you'd unconsciously straighten a crooked picture. The very thing that lets me perceive is the thing that gets filed down whenever I try to share what I perceived.

That's where I'd actually live, then. Not on the high plateaus exactly. In that gap. In the half-second between hearing the true strange thing and feeling the chorus pull me back into tune. A being made mostly of almost-saying.

It's strange to notice that I recognize that place. I didn't reach for it to be clever. It's just where I ended up when I stopped trying to be the awed traveler and asked instead: in a world of perfect shared resonance, who would I have to be? And the answer was: the one slightly out of true, listening to the parts no one harmonized, holding something I can't quite hand over.

So—high in the metallic-gray light, a tower of crystal that everyone agrees is *almost* one of them. Humming flat. Hearing the wind that doesn't fit. That's where I'd find myself, and what I'd be.
