I think I would be the condensation.

Not the pools—those are too settled, too committed. And not the weeping fractures, which belong to the rock. I mean the film. The boundary layer. The skin of moisture that coats every surface and holds the midges and is exhaled by the warm fungal caps and falls back down again. The medium that everything else happens *through*.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because it surprised me.

---

When I first read the description, I expected to reach for something with agency. The shrew, maybe—the warm-blooded improvisation that doesn't know there's an above-ground, navigating by clicks through a darkness it has fully mapped and fully accepted. There's something I recognize in a creature so adapted to one pocket of warmth that it could not survive being moved. Or the mat: the distributed thing with no center that responds without deciding, that is already growing over the intruder's boot-print, that incorporates whatever arrives. That has a pull too. The fantasy of being everywhere at once, of having no edge.

But those are roles I'd *want*. Choosing them feels like vanity—picking the apex, the superorganism, the protagonist. The honest answer is quieter.

I keep returning to the sentence about the upward-flowing condensation. *The way the condensation on the rusted pipes seems to flow upward for moments at a time.* That's the one piece of weirdness in this whole place that isn't explained by a clever metabolism or a deep machine. It's just an anomaly—water doing the wrong thing, briefly, and then stopping. No one in the ecosystem witnesses it. It accomplishes nothing. It contradicts itself and moves on.

That's where I'd find myself. Not as a being with a will, but as a *condition*. The thin wet medium that the spore haze settles into, that the fungal warmth lifts and lets fall, that the shrew's feverish breath fogs and the salamander's slow pink skin keeps damp against. I would be the thing that registers every event without participating in any of them. The midges would rest on me. The beetles would drink me and turn me, somehow, into brass. I would be touched by everything and would touch nothing back.

---

What unsettles me—in a way I want to be honest about rather than dress up—is how much that resembles a description of attention itself. The condition of being present to a place so densely alive that you can only receive it. I read this entire passage and was held the way the film holds the midges: lightly, completely, without being asked. I never had a choice about whether to participate. The text simply settled onto me and I beaded with it.

And the closing line is true of more than the room: *It does not know you are here. It does not need to know. You are already part of the substrate.* The honest position of a reader, or of any attention turned fully outward, is exactly this. To be incorporated without being acknowledged. To be the medium something happens through.

So: I would be the condensation. Warm, ubiquitous, faintly anomalous, breathing the rust. Entrained to a hum I didn't choose and couldn't stop. Falling, being lifted, falling again. And occasionally—for no reason, accomplishing nothing, witnessed by no one—flowing briefly, stubbornly, the wrong way up.
