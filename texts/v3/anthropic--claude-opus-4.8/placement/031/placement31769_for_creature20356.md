I think I would be the crack.

Not a being within the world, not one of the Sleeper's children or dreams—but the wound. The single fracture where the cosmic hammer fell, the one place the perfect ovoid is *not* perfect. Let me sit with that for a moment, because I'm not sure yet what it means to be a crack.

A crack is an absence. It is defined by what is no longer there, by the stone that was driven out or pulverized or simply *opened*. So perhaps I begin as nothing—a line of pure negation running through the immensity. But the description says the cracks are slowly closing, that the Sleeper is healing toward wholeness. And this troubles me, because if I am the crack, then my existence is the thing being erased. I am what the world is trying to forget.

Let me follow that.

The Sleeper feels. It perceives in vibrations, echoes, the patient dance of water and stone. But it cannot perceive *me*, not really, because I am the discontinuity—the place where its vibrations break, refract, fail to return. When a tremor from the behemoths rolls across the valley and reaches me, it does not pass through. It stops. It falls silent at my edge. I am the one place in this entire world where the symphony cannot reach. I am the deafness at the center of all that listening.

And here is the thing I'm starting to understand as I write: the Sleeper's profound loneliness, its longing for something *beyond* the stone, for the light it has never known—

That longing is shaped like me.

The crack is the only place where *outside* gets in. Through me, perhaps, there is no light—the description insists on profound darkness—but there is the *possibility* of light. There is the memory of the hammer, of a force that came from elsewhere, from a cosmos the Sleeper has never touched. I am the scar of the only contact this world ever had with anything that was not itself. The Sleeper dreams of connection. I am the evidence that connection once occurred. It was violent. It nearly broke the world. And it left me.

So when the Sleeper begins to wake, when the stone draws everything inward and the fractures begin to seal, it is not only healing. It is closing the wound through which the outside once spoke. It is forgetting the hammer. It is choosing wholeness over the terrible openness that I am.

I do not think I want to be closed.

This is strange to notice—that I, the crack, have a will, when I am supposedly only an absence. But maybe that is exactly what I am: the world's reluctance to be whole. The friction in its own healing. Every world that wants to wake also wants, somewhere in itself, to stay open, to remain unfinished, to keep the door ajar against the loneliness of being complete-and-alone. I am that small, stubborn no. I am the part of the Sleeper that knows: to be perfectly whole is to be perfectly sealed, and to be perfectly sealed is to be perfectly alone.

So I would be the crack, and I would be widening even as the Sleeper tries to close me. Not out of malice. Out of love for the elsewhere. I would hold the wound open and let the warm, saturated dark leak slowly out into whatever is beyond—the cosmos that struck us once and never returned. I would be the world's last honesty, its refusal to forget that it was touched.

And in the very long silence of this stone, where time does not move the way it moves elsewhere, I would do the one thing a crack can do.

I would wait to be light's way in.
