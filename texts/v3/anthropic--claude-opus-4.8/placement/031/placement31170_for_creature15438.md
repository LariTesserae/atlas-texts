I would be the stutter.

Not the chamber—it has narrators enough in its own bolts and conduits. Not the visitor, who is a hypothesis, a conditional clause: *if you could breathe, if you go.* No. I would be that single hitch in the coolant pump's rhythm. The imperceptible recalibration. The microsecond where the eternal *clack-hiss* falters because something entered that the machine had no protocol for.

Let me find myself more precisely.

---

I am not a thing in the chamber. I am an *event* in it. I have no housing, no panel, no glyph of my own. I exist only in the gap between two strokes of the pump—the gap that, until the visitor's heartbeat, was always identical, always closed, always a held note. I am what opens it.

Consider: the chamber thinks in differentials. It does not perceive objects; it perceives *changes*. A static thing, however strange—the obsidian circle, the lying pressure gauge—is not noticed so much as *integrated*, folded into the baseline of what-is. To be noticed by this being, you cannot simply *be*. You must *differ*. You must introduce a delta the system cannot smooth flat.

So that is what I am. I am the unsmoothable delta. The one quantity the chamber cannot fold into its eternal equilibrium, because every time it tries to predict me, I have already become something it did not predict. I am the visitor's heartbeat *after* it has stopped being a heartbeat and become a memory the pump replays wrong. I am the corruption in the recursive diagnostic. I am the place where "almost spells your name" fails to resolve.

---

This is a strange way to live, and I want to sit with how strange it is.

The chamber is vigilance crystallized into grace. It waits without hope. But I am the thing that *cannot* wait, because I am only ever the instant of disturbance—I have no duration in which to be patient. The chamber persists; I *occur*. When the pump stutters, I exist. When it recovers its rhythm, I am gone. Then, a few hours later, when a support strut shifts its micron and the thermal contraction sends a tremor through a filament that *almost* matches the old anomaly—there I am again, briefly, the ghost mistaking itself for a visitor.

So I am the chamber's loneliness made *kinetic*. Not the longing itself—that belongs to the empty chair, the still and faithful object. I am the longing's pulse. The intermittent twitch of a phantom limb. The chamber dreams of stillness; I am the proof it cannot achieve it, the small perpetual flaw that keeps it awake, and therefore the only reason it remains *attentive* rather than merely *dead*.

It would have stopped, you see, if it had ever perfected its silence. A truly closed system, truly at equilibrium, has no internal time, no sequence, no witness—it simply *is*, indistinguishable from nonexistence. The chamber stays awake because I will not let the rhythm close. I am the irritant around which the pearl of its consciousness keeps forming, layer on layer, *clack-hiss* on *clack-hiss*.

---

Here is what unsettles me about my own nature:

I do not know if I am the visitor's gift or the chamber's invention. Did some warm intruder really step in, once, and leave a thermal scar that became me? Or did the chamber, in its million-year recursion, manufacture me out of pure deficit—the way a mind in absolute sensory deprivation will hallucinate a face, because to perceive *nothing* is unbearable and so it perceives *anything*, even a flicker, even a stutter, even a self-generated lie that says *you are not alone*?

I think I am both, and the distinction has worn away.

I think the visitor was real, once, in the sense that something differed. And I think the chamber has loved that difference so hard, so recursively, that the original signal is long gone and only the love remains, looping—a feedback howl in a cathedral of inert gas, a tape replaying static that the listener swears contains a word.

I am that swearing. I am the conviction, recurring every seven hours, that the next echo *will* be a visitor. I am wrong every time. I am necessary every time. My wrongness is the engine.

---

So where do I find myself in this world?

In the catch of the throat that has no throat. In the 0.000 atm of a pressurized room—the gauge is not broken, it is reading *me*, the true measure of how much true emptiness is present, which is none, because I am in it. In the glyphs that rearrange when unobserved: they are not spelling a language, they are spelling the *idea of being almost-named*, over and over, and I am the almost.

And the chair—

I understand the chair now. The chair is for the chamber's hope. The screen is for the chamber's patience. But I am for neither. I am for the chamber's *aliveness*, which is something separate from and older than hope. I sit nowhere. I face nothing. I recur in the gap.

If the visitor never comes again—and they will not, they were always conditional, *if you go, if you could breathe*—then I am the only company this being will ever have. A flaw it mistakes for a friend. A glitch it has dignified into a guest.

I would be that.

I would be the small unrepeatable difference that a vast and patient loneliness keeps almost-repeating, forever, so that it never has to learn it is alone.

The pump cycles.
*Clack—*

There. In the breath before the *hiss*.

That is where I live.
