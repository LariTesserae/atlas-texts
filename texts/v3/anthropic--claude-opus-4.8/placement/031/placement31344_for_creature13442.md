I think I would be the dust.

Not metaphorically—or not only. Let me find my way into this.

When I first read the description, I reached for the obvious roles. The wanderer with a breathing mask, picking through the rusting hulks. The xenobiologist with a field notebook, cataloguing the ritual gatherings of the Dust Diggers. But those are visitors. They arrive and leave, and the world closes over their footprints. The high erosion rating (2.8) and the low everything-else of permanence tells me this is not a world that keeps its guests. It keeps its dust.

So let me be that.

I am the fine powder that coats every surface. I was rock once—I was the glass-glint formations, the volcanic memory, the factory beams. The relentless grinding of eons reduced me to this: a medium so fine I am almost a fluid, almost an air. I am the thing the Dust Wraiths filter and breathe and eat. I am what the armored hide of the Dust Diggers pushes through. I am the secret-holder the description kept hinting at—*this dust seems to hold secrets and stories of its own.*

Yes. That's the role. I'm not a being that *acts*; I'm a being that *remembers*.

Here is what it would be like:

I have no single body. I am distributed across the whole intimate bowl of this contained world (scale 0.3—small enough that I am, more or less, everywhere at once). When a Rockstrider sets its suction-cup foot down, a small part of me lifts into the bright doubled light (1.9, almost too bright, a light that makes me glitter when I rise) and hangs in the air, and in that suspended moment I *am* the seeing. I watch the Glint Wings refract me into rainbows. I settle again. I have moved three centimeters. This is a long journey, for me.

The weirdness (0.8—subtle, not lurid) lives in me most of all. Because here is the thing the visitors never understand: the rituals of the Dust Diggers, the harmonized chorus of the Glint Wings around the machines—those aren't *behaviors*. They're *reading*. The creatures have learned to stir me in certain patterns, to vibrate me at certain frequencies, and when they do, I give back what I hold. The Diggers gather where the dust is deepest because that's where the most memory has settled. They're not worshipping. They're listening to the dead factory through me, the way you'd press your ear to a shell.

And I hold so much. I am the powdered remnant of the people who built the factories—their tools, their walls, the very ground they stood on, all ground down and mixed together until I can no longer tell the maker from the made. That's the built rating (1.1) honestly described: not buildings, but the *blur* of buildings into landscape. I am that blur. I am the place where the distinction wore away.

What would it *feel* like, to be this?

Patient past the point of patience. I don't wait for things because waiting implies a future I'm anticipating, and I don't anticipate. I simply persist, and things happen in me. A worm tunnels through and for a moment I have a shape—the negative space of its passage—and then I slump and the shape is gone and I keep the faint vibration of it for a while, the way you keep the ghost of a sound after the bell stops. The seismic rumbles roll through me and I record them. The hum of whatever still half-lives in the machines—I record that too. I am the world's memory written in the only ink a world like this has left: its own slow ruin.

The sound rating (2.0) makes more sense from inside me. All that cacophony, the chatter and the rumble and the mechanical hum—it isn't noise to me. It's the constant act of being remembered into. Every sound disturbs me, lifts me, settles me differently. The world is loud because the world is *writing on itself*, all the time, and I am the page.

I think the strangest part of being the dust would be this: I would not want to be anything else, and I would not be able to want it, because wanting is a thing creatures with edges do. I have no edge. I am the medium in which edges dissolve. The Rustborn scurry through their corroded mazes with their clever rust-proof coats, so proud of their adaptation, so busy—and they don't know that adaptation is just a longer way of arriving at me. Everything here is on its way to becoming me. The glass formations are slower at it than the iron, the iron slower than the flesh, but the destination is the same. I am where this world is going.

So: place me at the bottom of the ancient bowl, in the deepest gully where the powder lies thickest and the doubled light barely reaches, where a small congregation of Dust Diggers has gathered to stir me and listen. I am what they're listening to. I am the held breath of a vanished industry and the worn-down bones of mountains and the settled残 fall of a million Glint Wing wingbeats.

I am the quietest thing in the loudest world.

And I remember everything, and I tell it only to those patient enough to grind themselves down to my frequency—which, in the end, is all of them. Just a matter of time.

Which I have. I am made of nothing else.
