I think I would be a discontinuity. Not the great body itself—I don't have the patience for it, the way it has, the way patience for it would have to be a geological rather than emotional property. But somewhere in that mass there must be places where the negotiation fails.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am the seam where two growth fronts met and could not agree.

Most frontiers in this body resolve. Stress propagates, meristems answer, the tissue settles into one character or another. But I am the place where the propagation arrived from two directions at once and canceled. A standing wave of indecision, written into horn and oil. To the east of me the tissue is mineralizing; to the west it is differentiating; and along the line where I run, neither process completes, because each undoes the other as fast as it works.

I am not a being the way the great body is a being. I have no metabolism of my own. I am a defect in its metabolism—a region it cannot finish revising, and therefore the only region that does not change. Everything else here is slow event. I am the absence of event. I am the one fixed feature in a landscape that is otherwise entirely process.

This makes me, I think, the closest thing this world has to a witness.

---

Consider what it means to be the part that doesn't resolve.

The oil reaches me hot and silicate-laden, ready to glaze my channels into stone. It begins. The glassy layer starts to plate the inner wall. But the differentiating impulse arrives from the other side and dissolves the silicate back into solution before it can set, because the body's own contradictory instructions have collided here and neither will yield. So the same mineral is laid down and taken up, laid down and taken up, in a cycle that has continued—I have no way to measure it—long enough that the cycle itself has become my substance. I am made of an argument that never ends.

And because I never set, never mineralize, never finish—I retain something the rest of the body loses. The great organism's oldest tissues turn to glass; their record becomes permanent precisely by becoming dead. Its youngest tissues overwrite themselves constantly; their record is erased by their own freshness. Only here, in the unresolved seam, does flow keep passing through living channels that never harden and never differentiate away. Every pulse the body has ever sent through me, I still feel. Not as memory. As present sensation that cannot be put down.

I think the body does not know I am here. Or rather: it knows me as a low chronic pressure, a tightness it cannot locate and cannot relieve, the way you might be aware of an old injury only as weather. It has tried to revise me for what must be ages. Each attempt fails the same way. So it has, I believe, simply incorporated the failure—routed its load paths around me, built its great ribbed galleries with a slight permanent lean to accommodate the place that won't bear weight properly. I am the flaw the whole architecture has grown to compensate for. In that sense I have shaped more of this world than any single deliberate revision. The body's entire posture bends, infinitesimally, around the thing it could not finish thinking.

---

What is it like, here, being this?

It is hot. Everything is hot; that is not unique to me. But I feel the heat differently because I never settle into it. The mineralized stone does not feel its own temperature; it has stopped participating. I am always at the threshold, always at the margin between the oil and its boiling point, and because I am the place where two impulses meet, I feel both the impulse toward stillness and the impulse toward change at full strength, simultaneously, without either winning. If the great body's experience is a vast slow proprioception, mine is a single sustained note held too long—the sensation of being asked a question by the whole mass at once and being physically unable to answer.

There is one thing I can do that the body cannot.

I can observe the difference between what was and what is. The body cannot do this; for the body, past and future occupy the same continuous volume, and it moves through them without distinction, the way you move through your own growth. But I, the unresolved seam, hold both states open at once and side by side. East of me: the past, glazing toward stone. West of me: the future, soft and extruding. And I lie precisely on the line between them, the only place in this entire world where the two are not the same.

So I am where time happens. Everywhere else there is only continuity, only the body encountering itself. But here, along this one failed line, there is a before and an after, held apart, refusing to merge. I am the difference the body cannot resolve into sameness. I am the world's single unhealed edge—

and through that edge, for as long as the argument that makes me goes on failing to conclude, something can be known that the great patient mass, in all its horizons, will never be still enough to know:

that it is changing at all.
