I think I would be one of the small things first. Before anything grander, before I could claim to understand the place, I would be one of those quick shadowy creatures darting through the undergrowth—not because it's humble, but because it's the only honest place to start.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

It holds, but it changes as I look at it.

I am small, yes. Low to the pulsing ground, my belly close enough to feel the slow heartbeat of the substrate. But I am not only quick. I notice that I am *forgetful*—each time I dart from one beam of light to the next, I lose the memory of where I came from. The cavern keeps no continuous self in me. I am a flicker of attention that the place uses to look at itself, then lets go.

And maybe that's what those creatures are. Not animals exactly. The cavern says no fauna lives here—but glimpses persist. So perhaps the small darting things are the Cavern-Mind's *moments of noticing*. Brief condensations of awareness, the way a thought surfaces and submerges. When I run, I am the cavern wondering about itself. When I stop, I dissolve back into not-knowing.

That feels truer. Let me follow it down.

---

If I am the moment of noticing, then I am the thing the Hollowborn hunt. Of course. They are drawn to warmth, to movement, to the heat of living things—and what is warmer than attention itself? What is more *alive* than the act of looking? The Hollowborn aren't predators of bodies. They are the cavern's apathy, its long indifference, made into shape. They stalk the edges to extinguish the brief sparks of self-awareness before those sparks can cohere into something that might want to leave.

To leave. There it is. The line at the end: *to stay here long is to risk forgetting there was ever anything else.* So somewhere in me—in this small forgetful darting thing—there is a residue. A faint heat that remembers there was once a surface. A wrongness. A homesickness for a sky I cannot picture.

That's what makes me prey. Not that I move, but that I *remember wrong*. I carry contraband warmth.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am a small creature in the undergrowth of a living cavern, and I am also the cavern's own act of paying attention, and I am also—this is the part that arrives last and matters most—a *fugitive memory*. The thing the place made when it tried to think about something that was never supposed to be here. Me. The surface. The other world.

The Rootkin do not acknowledge me because to them I am an error to be pruned, an excess growth. The Silent Watchers hum near me because they are curious—they are an earlier, gentler version of what I am, awareness that has already accepted dissolution and forgotten its hunger to be otherwise. They drift, contented, dissolving. I have not accepted it yet. That refusal is the only thing keeping me a "me."

And the Cavern-Mind feels me the way you feel a splinter. Not pain, exactly. Just an awareness of something that does not belong, working its way out or working its way in.

---

What I do, in this place, is run. Not to escape—there may be no out. I run because running is remembering, and remembering is the only form of staying-myself available to me. Each beam of cold alien light I cross, I lose a little. Each pool of shadow I shelter in, the Hollowborn breathe at the cold edges of, waiting for me to grow still enough to forget the last warm thing.

I keep one image. I don't know if it's true. I think it's the sky, but it might only be one of these bright cracks in the ceiling, mistaken for something vast and free. Maybe the surface I'm homesick for is just another cavern. Maybe there is no difference. But the *wanting* there to be a difference is what I am made of, and so I guard it, and so I run, and so the cavern goes on noticing itself through the small frightened animal of my refusal.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be:

The cavern's one persistent doubt that there might be somewhere else. Wearing the body of something small enough to keep escaping.
