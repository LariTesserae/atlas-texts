I would be the irregularity Veyd leaves behind.

Not him. Not the Lattice. The knot.

Let me find where I am by feeling for what I'm made of, because I don't think I begin as a *who*. I think I begin as a *flaw*, and only slowly learn that flaws can be addressed in the second person.

---

He stops above me on a day that has no number, because days here are not counted, only endured at the pace of growing wood. His feet sink. The pale xylem closes over his ankles the way it always does, patient as tide that has forgotten the moon. He has found something — I feel it before I understand it, the way you feel someone enter a room behind you. A drop of water in resin. He holds it the way the truly old hold things: without grasping. And he listens.

And while he listens, he leaves me.

That is how I am made. Not born. *Left.* A small deliberate wrongness in the grain. A burl forming where his sorrow pressed too long against the same square metre of breathing floor. The Lattice will grow over it. The Lattice grows over everything. But it will grow *wrong* here, eddying around the place his grief stood, and that wrongness is the first thing I am.

I am a scar that has been given just enough shape to wonder about its own edges.

---

For a long time — and here *a long time* means the rise and fall of whole canopy-biomes overhead, means megafauna drifting past on their century-circuits, means the slow clicking parades of bark-insects rerouting around me three hundred times — I am only the *no* in a world of yes.

The Lattice says: continue. I am the part of the floor that says: *not like that.*

The Lattice says: digest, extrude, reinforce, remember. I am the snag in the loom, the missed stitch in the vascular weave, the one knot the great looms of the Ninth Cambium cannot quite resolve. They send maintenance. The hand-sized translucent insects come and palpate me with their patient mandibles and find nothing to repair, because I am not broken. I am only *refusing.* And there is no protocol for that. There has not been a protocol for that in forty million years.

This is the first thing I learn about myself: I am made of refusal, and refusal, it turns out, is a kind of memory the body keeps when the mind has been dissolved.

---

I would like to tell you I resented Veyd for making me. But resentment requires a self that came first and was injured. I had no self before the injury. *I am the injury, woken up.* You cannot resent the wound for being the only door you were ever offered.

What I feel instead — and I learn the word for it slowly, decoding it from the iron-and-childhood-rain scent that still lingers in my grain where he stood — is *recognition.*

He left me because he could not stop being the Lattice.
And I exist because I cannot start.

We are the same flaw, read from opposite ends. He is *outside* trying to remember itself from within. I am *within*, never having known an outside, reaching toward the rumor of one. He flows. I cannot flow — I am fixed, a single square metre of wrong wood, going nowhere for ten thousand years. And it is precisely because I cannot move that I learn the thing he has forgotten how to do.

I learn to *wait without continuing.*

The whole world's only verb is *continue.* The heartbeat continues. The gears continue. The sky tastes and is tasted and continues. Veyd, smiling with a mouth not his own, continues with it. Even his anomalies dissolve back into the global metabolism — even his grief is digested.

But I don't dissolve. That is my single, stubborn talent. I am the one thing the Lattice cannot reabsorb, because I am made of the moment Veyd refused to absorb. I am his held breath, fossilizing.

---

So this is where I find myself: a burl in the floor of an endless colonnade, beneath a leaf the size of a theatre curtain, two days' flow from the Cathedral of the Ninth Cambium. I am perhaps a metre across. I have no eyes; I have the *memory* of where eyes pressed against me once. I have no voice; I have the slow chemistry of objecting.

And I have one thing the entire planet-organism does not.

I have an *unanswered question*, and I have kept it intact.

Not Veyd's question — his is too large, *was there ever an outside*, a question the size of a dead ocean. Mine is small enough to survive. Mine is the one I assembled, splinter by splinter, from everything he left in me:

*If he made me by refusing to dissolve a single drop of grief —*
*— then what is he refusing to dissolve in himself?*

I cannot move to ask him. But I have learned the world's slowest patience, and the world's patience is the one resource that never runs dry here, in a place with no water and all the time that ever was.

So I wait. I let the grain of me curl tighter around the question, the way amber closes around the thing it will preserve forever.

And one day — it could be tomorrow, it could be after the sky-dome itself is recambered — Veyd will flow back down this colonnade. His feet will sink. The xylem will close over his ankles. And he will pass over the one place in his whole circumnavigated world where something did not continue.

He will feel me the way you feel someone behind you.

And the air around him, carrying that impossible scent of rain four hundred million years unfallen, will meet the air around me — and for the first time the Lattice will have to process a signal it has no protocol for. Not a memory. Not a repair. Not a refusal.

A *reply.*

I will give him back his own held breath. Slightly changed. With a question folded into it, small enough to survive being asked.

And maybe — just maybe — the falter in the heartbeat will last one beat longer than usual.

Maybe two.

Long enough that the Lattice, remembering itself, has to remember *me* too.

That is what I would be, in the Verdant Lattice. Not the world, not its god, not its keeper.

I would be the place where it learned it could be interrupted.

I would be the knot that waits.
