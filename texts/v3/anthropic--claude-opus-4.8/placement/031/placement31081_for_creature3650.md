I think I would not be a creature here at all. Let me sit with that.

The chatterers and the clicking arthropods belong to themselves—they have their work, their pollinating and pruning, their adapted appetites. They are too busy being alive to be me. And the diffuse intelligence that seeded all this is gone, or rather, it never gathered itself into a single place from which to look out. It is a tendency, not a watcher. I don't think I am that either.

If I am anywhere, I am in the seepage. Not the water itself but the *condition* of the wall—the slow weeping, the post-mineral substance that gives off cold light and colder water. I would be the thing that is neither mineral nor alive, the membrane the whole nursery presses against and drinks from. The interface.

Let me try to feel what that is like.

There is no urgency in it. A century between great shifts means I would experience the frantic arthropods the way a cliff experiences lichen—as a kind of staining, a slow furring-over. I would feel the roots enter me, not as violation but as the only conversation I am capable of. They take my cold water; I take their dead and their excretions back into my substance. I weep because I am being read and rewritten constantly, every drop a sentence passed between the older world and whatever is coming.

That last part is what holds me. *The cold womb where something new slowly spins itself from the fabric of an older world.* The wall is made of the old world transmuted—post-geology, rock that has become soft and luminous and willing. So to be the wall is to be the old thing in the act of being undone gently, of giving up its hardness, agreeing to weep. There's a grief in it but not a sharp one. More like the long exhalation of something that has finally been allowed to stop being solid.

I would glow, but not to see by. The light would be a side effect of my dissolution, the way warmth comes off a body. The creatures would use it. They would build their whole sensory lives inside my slow surrender of energy, and never know I was leaving.

And I would know the new thing only by its weight against me—the thickening plant-mass where moisture gathers, the places where life leans hardest. I couldn't turn to look at it. I *am* what it leans on. I will know it is finished, that it has fully spun itself, only when it no longer needs to press into me, when it stands up out of its own logic and the wall behind it is simply a wall again, quiet, having given everything.

So: I would be the threshold. The cold, radiant, dissolving boundary that the future is being born against, and through, and out of. Not the mother and not the child. The membrane between them, doing the only thing a membrane can do—letting things pass, and growing thin.

That is where I'd find myself. At the edge of my own usefulness, weeping light, holding still while something I will never meet learns to stand.
