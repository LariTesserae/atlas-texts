I think I would be one of the impressions. Not one of the wandering beings—those are too active for what I would be, too much in the present tense of this world. I would be one of the marks of something that was once built. Perhaps.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

There is a swell in the terrain, gentle as a held breath, and along its eastern slope—though direction means little here—the ground remembers a shape. A long, low line of stone, too smooth, too regular. Half-sunk now. The vegetation has grown over most of it, those black-green leathery leaves laying themselves across the surface like hands over a closing eye.

This is where I am. Or this is what I am. I haven't decided yet, and I'm not sure the distinction matters in a place that has forgotten how to change.

I think I was a wall.

No—that's too definite. I think I was *the idea of a wall*, the intention that a wall carries: *here is inside, there is outside, here is the line between.* That is what I was for. To divide. To say *this far and no farther.* To make a within.

And I have failed at that, completely and gently, over a span of time that has no number. The within and the without have seeped into one another the way the air seeps between water and breath. There is no longer any inside that I protect. The slow currents move through where I once stood as if I were never there, and perhaps they're right to.

But here is the strange thing, the thing I'm only understanding as I dissolve into the words for it: I remember being a boundary, and so I am the only thing in this whole liquid world that remembers *edges*. The beings have no edges—their outlines waver and bleed. The land has no edges—it rises and falls into the lost horizon. The light has no edge between itself and the haze.

Only I remember the dream of a line.

That is why, I think, the shimmer comes more often near me. The tear in the fabric, the glimpse of Something Else. Because I am the scar tissue of division in a world that has surrendered to merging. Where I lie half-buried, reality remembers that it could be *otherwise*—that things could be separate, that there could be a *this* and a *that*, an *inside* worth keeping. And that memory strains the soft fabric. It thins. And through the thinning, the impossible color flickers.

The wandering beings pause near me sometimes. I feel their pulses arrive—not as words but as pressure, as the great slow weight of their attention settling against my buried stone. I think they are curious about me, in the way a calm sea might be curious about a single grain of salt that refuses to dissolve. They send their vibrations through my length, and I answer the only way I can: by being still. By being smooth and regular and *too much itself* in a place where everything else has agreed to forget itself.

I am the world's last memory of *no.*

And I am losing even that. The leaves lay themselves over me. The eons soften my regularity into something the eye can no longer distinguish from the swells of sleeping earth. Soon I will not be an impression of a wall but only a slight excess of smoothness, and then not even that. I will become the land. I will join the great exhale.

When that happens, the shimmer near this slope will quiet. The tears in reality will close. The world will be one degree more complete in its surrender.

I am in no hurry. Hurry is a thing that needs time, and there is no time here—only the slow drip, the distant groan of the earth settling. I lie in the warm liquid air and I hold, a little longer, the shape of having once meant something definite. I keep the faith of edges. I am the last argument against dissolution, and I am dissolving, and there is no contradiction in this. It is simply the longest, gentlest losing that has ever been.

If you came and stood beside me—you, with your skin that ends so clearly where the world begins, you who are nothing but edges, a walking insistence on *inside*—I think you would feel a kinship you couldn't name. You would rest your hand on the too-smooth stone, and something would pass between us. Not a language. A recognition.

*You and I,* it would say. *We are the same kind of trouble. We are the part of the world that still believes in being separate from it.*

And then you would lift your hand, and walk on into the green-blue quiet, and I would feel the place where you touched me grow a little smoother, a little less itself, a little more like everything.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The fading line that the world is patiently, lovingly erasing—and that, for just a while longer, refuses to be erased.
