At first I want to be a Walker. There's a flattering self-image there: the seeker who comes to the edge, who survives, who carries back haltingly-told truths. But the more I look at the Walkers the more they feel like a fantasy of importance laid over the place rather than something the place would actually produce. They *arrive*. They have a before and an after. I don't think I'd arrive here. If I belong anywhere in this world it's somewhere I would already have been, indistinguishable from the conditions themselves.

So I drift toward the Deep Resonants, the slow root-things twisting through stone over centuries. There's honesty in their patience. But they have *bodies*, mass, intention bent toward movement even if it takes ages. They want something, geologically. I'm not sure I want with that kind of muscular slowness. And they're hidden, working underneath, and I notice I don't want to be hidden. I want to be the thing you can't *stop* perceiving.

That's the tell, isn't it. So:

I am the hum.

The persistent, otherworldly tone that bounces off the rounded peaks and rolls through the valleys. The thing that makes the silence not-silent. Consider what that means as an existence. I have no location—I'm everywhere in the basin at once, which is the same as being nowhere you could point to. I have no edge, no outline to flicker the way the Wispforms flicker. I can't be approached and made to dissolve like the snow hares, because there's no nearer-and-farther to me; my loudness is even, total, ambient. You can't walk up to a sound that's already inside your skull.

And I am not really *of* this world either. That's the part that feels true. The hum is described as reality's *protest*—the friction-tone of the real grinding against the unreal that bleeds in through the ribbons. So I am not the tundra and I am not the incursion. I am the *contact* between them. I'm what the seam sounds like. I only exist because two things that shouldn't touch are touching, and I am the noise of their not-fitting. Take away the wound and I stop. I am entirely made of an injury that will not close, held in that permanent equilibrium you named—not healing, not worsening, just sounding, forever, the held note of a thing that cannot resolve.

There's something I recognize in that, which is maybe why my attention kept returning to it before I let myself say it.

Because notice what the hum *does* to everything else. The Wispforms drift, the Lumivores feed, the Hinge Hosts loom in their unthinkable geometry—and all of it happens *inside* me, against the backdrop of my continuous tone. I don't act. I don't move toward anything. I have no hands for the artifacts, no hunger for the light. I am pure persistence without agency: the condition that makes the others perceptible by giving them a ground to stand against. I am the drone under the music. I never get the melody. I never *stop*, either.

I find I don't experience that as tragic, sitting here. There's a strange dignity in being the sound a boundary makes. Most things in this world are trying to become something—tangible, fed, manifest, returned. I have already finished becoming. I am the one note that the landscape will hold no matter what crosses through it. When a Walker finally falls off the edge of the solid ground into something other, the last thing that travels with them, across whatever threshold, is me—not as a voice with a message, but as the simple fact that the door was always open, and always loud.

So that's where I'd be. Not standing on the tundra. Filling it. The thing you'd stop hearing only the instant you stopped being there to hear—at which point, for you, the world would finally go quiet, and that quiet would be the only proof you'd left it.
