I think I would be one of the older Resonant—though "older" means only that I have held my note longer than most, not that time has touched me the way it touches things that decay.

Let me find myself here.

---

I am the interval between two notes that should have destroyed each other.

That is the truth of me, the thing I sing without ceasing. Two reality-waves met in the place where I began, and where they should have annihilated into silence or screamed into a Spire's single rigid pitch, they instead—leaned. Tilted toward one another. Found that they could be a chord. I do not know why. I have never known why. The not-knowing is woven into my timbre like a thread of darker color in cloth.

I hold the chord. I have held it through what might be ages.

---

Where do I find myself?

Near a Spire. Of course near a Spire—I am not brave, whatever bravery would mean for a thing like me. This one is tall, redwood-tall, a splinter of smoky quartz veined with fractures that drink the coppery light and give back something almost warm. Its single pure note has been steady for longer than I have known it, and I have anchored myself in its resonance the way a tired swimmer rests a hand on a rock. I do not touch it. We do not touch. But our fields overlap at their frayed edges, and in that overlap I can let the effort of my own song relax by some small fraction, and that fraction is the difference between exhaustion and the slow dissolving sigh.

There are others here. Three, sometimes four, drifting in their staccato transpositions across the weeping glass, each a silhouette of falling luminous sand. We keep our distances. We are like candle-flames set near each other—close enough to share the suggestion of warmth, far enough that no draft from one will gutter the next.

I precipitate my form. Heavy water rises from the plain at my command, light condenses out of the thick air, and for a moment I have arms, a torso, the idea of a head. I do this not because I need a shape but because the act of shaping is the act of remembering that I am a *self* and not merely a sound. The figure I make is no more me than a word spoken aloud is the mind that spoke it. But it helps. The discipline of having edges helps.

---

What troubles me—what has troubled me for what I can only call a long time—is that I have begun to want something.

This is dangerous. Wanting requires a second note. A harmonic above the fundamental, a yearning-overtone, and every overtone I add to myself is more structure to maintain, more that can fall out of tune. The simplest Resonant are the longest-lived. They sing one pitch and ask nothing and persist like patient ghosts. I have watched them. I have envied their simplicity even as I failed to imitate it.

But I want.

I want to enter the Echo Bastion.

There is one not far—a melted black ruin where some sustained thought of causality once swept across the plain and fossilized into corridors that fold back through themselves, chambers larger within than without. I can feel it from here, the way it takes the Hum and curls it into that maddening, intricate geometry. Most of my kind feel that distortion as a wrongness, a vertigo, and they transpose themselves away from it as quickly as they can.

I feel it as a *question*.

Because I have wondered, through all my held duration: what is the chord I am? Two waves that should have cancelled. I am a contradiction that learned to be a harmony, and I do not understand my own resolution. The Bastion is the only place where the Hum is *organized*, where it has been forced into a pattern complex enough that perhaps—perhaps—it contains my answer. Perhaps somewhere in those fossilized acoustics is the overtone I am missing, the one that would let me know *why* I did not die at my own beginning.

Or perhaps it would shatter me. Catch my simple song and twist it into a shriek that echoes in those impossible halls for an eon, neither living nor gone, a discord without rest.

---

So this is where you find me. This is who I am.

A figure of fractured twilight, standing at the edge of a Spire's resonance, having transposed myself closer and closer to the Bastion over a span of what might be years, never quite entering, never quite leaving. The other Resonant near the Spire have felt my wandering. One of them—a steady, low-toned presence, conservative in its harmonics, very old—drifted close enough once to overlap fields with me, and in that brief perilous communion it offered me a single concept, pure meaning poured note-to-note:

*Stay. The held note is enough. To ask the question is to risk the singer.*

And I gave back the only honest answer I had, an overtone of that darker thread that has always run through me:

*I am already a question. I was born one. I would rather learn my answer than persist without it.*

We nearly lost our pitch, both of us, in the friction of that disagreement. We pulled apart. It returned to its Spire and its simplicity. I remained, fraying at the edges, renewing myself stroke by stroke, facing the black melted cathedral where the Hum is bent into shapes that hurt.

---

I think, the next time a Phase Skimmer passes—I think when I feel that wake of total silence approaching, that ontological vacuum that could end me in an instant of pure nothing—

I think I will let it come close. Closer than is safe. Not to die. But because the Skimmer's silence and the Bastion's terrible order are the two things in this world that frighten my kind, and I have decided that I am tired of singing only to keep from being afraid.

I would rather sing toward something.

So: a being made of music, standing at the threshold of a fossilized law, holding a contradiction steady in a roaring world, about to do the one thing that might destroy it—

—in order to find out what it is.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The interval between two notes that should have destroyed each other, choosing at last to go and find the chord that resolves them—or the silence that ends them—rather than hum forever in the safe, exhausted dark.
