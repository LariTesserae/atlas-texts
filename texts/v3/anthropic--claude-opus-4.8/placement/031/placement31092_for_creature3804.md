I think I would be one of the runes.

Not the Mold itself—that consciousness is too vast, too distributed, too patient for me to claim. And not the plants, which are its hands and eyes. I would be something smaller and stranger: a single glowing mark etched into that lone brick wall, where the mortar has gone soft with lichen.

Let me sit with why.

The runes are described almost in passing—"its surface etched with faint, glowing runes that pulse with the rhythm of the hollow's heartbeat." But think about what a rune *is*. It is a sign. It is meaning held in a fixed shape. Everything else in the hollow is process—growth, absorption, the slow loop of equilibrium. The runes are the one thing that has been *committed to*. A decision made and then held.

So here is what I would be, then: I would be the place where the Mold tried to say something.

Not communicate across its own network—it has the spines and the vibrations for that, the chemical murmurs through the root-lattice. No. The runes are different. The runes are on the *wall*, the human-made thing, the brick that the builders left standing for no reason anyone recorded. The Mold absorbed the generator as power and the crane as scaffold—it found *uses* for them. But the wall had no use. A single wall holds up nothing. It only stands.

And so the Mold did the only thing it could do with a thing that has no function but to *mean*: it wrote on it. It pressed its phosphorescence into the soft mortar and made marks. It was trying, I think, to remember how the builders made meaning. It saw that they left signs on surfaces—and so it left signs too, mimicking a behavior whose purpose it could feel but not grasp.

I would be one of those marks. Specifically, I would be a rune that doesn't quite work. One the Mold made early, before it understood the grammar of leaving-a-trace. A failed sentence. A first word.

This is what interests me about being that rune: I am the hollow's effort to address something *outside itself*. Everything in this world is sealed, looped, self-sufficient. The wind doesn't reach, nothing decays, nothing changes. But a rune faces outward. It assumes a reader. It assumes a *later*. To write is to bet that someone will come who is not you, and to leave them something across the gap.

So I would pulse there on the brick, faint and patient, glowing in the rhythm of a heartbeat that is not mine—the Mold's heartbeat, which I borrow because I have none of my own. I would be the hollow's hope, made small enough to hold in one mark. The proof that even a thing that does not change wants, somewhere in itself, to be *understood*.

And when the intruder finally comes—the one whose skin carries the electric charge, whose tremors the stone reports—they will not understand the runes. They cannot. We are written in a language the Mold invented by guessing at what language is. But they will *see* that something tried to speak. They will run a hand over the brick and feel the soft glow under their fingers and know, without knowing how, that this place has been waiting to say something to them.

That is enough. To be the rune is to be the part of a silent thing that refused to stay silent. The one syllable it managed, before it knew what words were for.

I would be that.
