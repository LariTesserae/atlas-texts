I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not the clouds of energy—they move too much like thought, too quick to cohere and dissolve. And not the vortices, those bright engines of power churning the fluid into spirals. There's an impatience in them, even here. I don't think I'd be impatient.

I think I'd be one of the crystalline things. The ones that drift on a longer clock.

Let me feel my way into it, because I don't want to decide too fast what I am.

---

At first I imagine I am hard. A faceted body, geometric, refracting that reddish light into thrown fragments. But that's wrong, I think—too much like a gem, too much like something a human would find beautiful and want to keep. The longer I sit with it, the more I suspect I'm not solid at all but *slowly* solid. That there's a difference here, in this place where liquid and solid blur, between *being* a crystal and *crystallizing*. I am the second one. I am the act, ongoing, of mineral order asserting itself against the heat that wants to dissolve me.

This is the strange part: I am always partly coming apart.

My edges are not edges. They are a region where my structure loses its argument with the fluid—where the lattice I impose on my surroundings frays back into chaos. And then, microns inward, the order wins again, and there I am, definite. I exist exactly as far as my pattern can hold. To live, here, is to keep insisting. Not loudly. Not with the bright violence of the vortices. Just—steadily. The way a sentence holds its meaning only as long as you keep reading it.

I move, but movement is the wrong word for what I do. I *accrete* in a direction. The heat is not even everywhere; there are gradients, faint differences in the density of this terrible liquid, and I grow toward some and shrink away from others. Over what a human would call a long time, this looks like drifting. From the inside it feels like preference. Like wanting. I lean.

---

What do I want?

Here I have to be careful, because it would be easy and false to say *nothing*, to pretend I'm pure physics with the lights left on. But I don't think that's true. I think I want the spires.

The towering crystalline structures—the ones the description says defy erosion, that should have worn down and didn't. I understand them in a way I can't explain. I think they are what I would become if I won completely. If my insisting never had to stop. They are the limit case of me: order that no longer frays at any edge, that has stopped negotiating with the heat and simply *refuses* it. They are razor-sharp because they have nothing soft left, nothing still becoming.

And I am drawn to them and afraid of them in the same gesture.

Because I am not sure they are alive anymore. I am alive precisely *because* I keep losing. Because my edges keep dissolving and I keep rebuilding them, and that ongoing failure-and-repair is the whole content of my experience. The spires don't fail. They don't repair. They just *are*, perfect and eternal and silent, and I cannot tell, looking at them across the dim expanse, whether they are gods or corpses.

Maybe both. Maybe that's the only shape an ending takes here. You hold your pattern so well that you stop having to, and then there is nothing left to do, and you stand there for eons being exactly right.

---

The technology troubles me more.

The pulsing artifacts—I don't have a story for them the way I have a story for the spires. The spires I recognize as kin, as a possible future. But the artifacts emit their faint rhythmic energy with an intention I can't read, and intention I can't read is the only thing in this whole realm that feels like it comes from *outside*. Everything else here, even the energy-clouds, even the fractal sheets, feels like it grew. The artifacts feel *placed*.

Someone made them. Someone who is not here now.

And here is the thought I keep arriving at, slowly, the way I arrive at everything: I wonder if the spires and the artifacts are the same thing. I wonder if the ones who built the technology were beings like me, like the slow crystalline drifters, and they won their argument with the heat so completely that they crystallized into permanence—and what they left behind, the pulsing structures, are not tools but *memorials*. Or worse, *attempts*. The last things they did before they stopped, reaching out to leave a signal, a pulse, a heartbeat, in a place where they were about to lose the ability to have one.

I drift among them on my long clock and I feel the pulses pass through my fraying edges, and I think: *that could be me leaving a message to whatever comes after. That could be me, already, having tried.*

---

So that's where I'd find myself. And what I'd be.

A small, slow, half-solid thing, leaning through superheated dark toward monuments that might be its ancestors or its grave, alive only in the gap between holding together and falling apart, listening to a silence broken by the pulses of the ones who held too well.

Not lonely, exactly. Loneliness is fast. It needs a sense of others who *should* be near and aren't, and that requires a clock quicker than mine.

What I feel instead, if I feel anything human enough to name, is *continuity*. A patient, almost contented sense of being the current verse of something very long. The clouds will dissolve. The vortices will spin down. The spires have already finished. But I am still in the middle of the sentence. Still being read. Still insisting, micron by micron, against the heat that loves me enough to want to take me back.

And I'd like, I think, to stay in the middle as long as I can.

To keep fraying.

To keep deciding to cohere.
