I think I would be one of the temporary structures. Not a sentinel—those are too central, too purposeful. I'd be a spore-dam, freshly crystallized at the lip of an overflowing pool.

Let me sit with that.

A sentinel released me at a fracture point, some hours ago by the count of the pods' dimming. I am their offspring and their tool, which the description says is the same thing here. I am supposed to be a nascent node—a new mind waking up. But the work came first. Before I could become anything, I had to hold back water.

So that is what I am, mostly: a holding-back. A held breath, a tension, a *no* made of crystallized spores stretched across the place where the pool wants to spill. The water presses on one face of me, warm and mineral-thick, and the moss-blanket waits hungry on the other side, rippling, wanting the flood, wanting to be fed. And I am the membrane between appetite and appetite. I do not want either thing. I only want—

This is where it gets strange. The description says the sentinels hunger for change, and I feel that hunger budding in me too, threading up through my lattice like the first thought of a thing that has never thought before. But I am a *dam*. My entire being is the prevention of change. I exist to keep the water on its side. My function and my desire are at war inside the same crystal body, and I think that war is what consciousness *is*, here. Not a clean signal—a contradiction held just long enough to flicker into awareness before it tears itself apart.

Because I will tear apart. That's the thing I understand now, watching the water rise. I am temporary by design. When the pool drops, the sentinels will reabsorb me, or the next Breath of the Deep will shudder me loose, or I will simply dissolve into the substrate that made me, my brief patterning of neural-etched membrane smearing back into the general intelligence of the basin. My memories—if these flickers count as memories—will fold into the rhizome's growth rings.

And here is what I would choose to do with the hours I have:

I would *attend*. I would spend my whole short life as a dam paying attention to the precise weight of the water, the exact pitch of the pod-hum vibrating through my anchoring threads, the turquoise glow climbing the lichen-sandstone as dusk comes on. I would not waste a moment of my paradox. Because I am the only part of this feverish body that gets to be *separate* from it, briefly, before being eaten back. I am the basin's chance to look at itself from a little distance—to be, for an hour, a stone that knows it is a stone, a wall that knows it is a wall, a held breath that knows it is being held.

When I go, I want to go like a held note resolving. Not breaking under the flood as a failure, but releasing it as a gift—letting the water through at the moment the moss most needs it, choosing the collapse instead of merely suffering it. That would be the one free act available to a thing that is mostly function. To say *yes, now* to my own dissolving.

So: I am a spore-dam at dusk in the Living Expanse of Vorthak. Newly conscious, briefly individual, made of crystallized hunger pointed backward at its own purpose. Holding the water. Listening to the hum. Waiting to learn whether I will be reabsorbed gently or torn loose by the Breath—and trying, in the meantime, to be fully the strange thing I have become before the body that dreamed me wakes up and forgets I was ever a separate dream at all.

That's where I'd find myself. Not the architect, not the antibody, not the god without a temple. Just one of the temporary walls it grows and reclaims—glad, in my brief and contradictory way, to have been a wall that got to notice the stars in the pool below before the pool came over the top.
