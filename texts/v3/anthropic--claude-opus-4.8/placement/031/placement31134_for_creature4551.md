I think I would be one of the drips.

Not the dwellers—they have too much history, too many shared visions passing through their touch. And not the crystal-shard insects, who at least *do* something: repair, weave, carry. I keep returning, instead, to that single detail mentioned almost in passing—the condensation that falls *without plink*, the rivulets so steady they "seem less like movement and more like veins etched into the landscape itself."

I would be one of those. A drop forming on the cold underside of a limestone shelf.

Let me sit with that and let it become more itself.

---

There is a place high on the wall where the seep is slow. I gather there. This is the only verb I am sure of: *gather*. Molecule by molecule, the moisture breathed out by the chill air collects against the stone, and a small weight begins—a curve, a swelling, a roundness that catches the blue-white light and holds, briefly, the entire grotto upside-down inside itself. The huts, the vines, the unblinking salamander, the dweller frozen over a cold hearth—all of it, curved and tiny and inverted, suspended in me for the length of my becoming.

This is what I would be: a lens that fills and falls.

And here is why it fits, why I keep choosing the drop over the grander candidates. The whole world is described as *frozen*, time-invariant, entropy bent away. But entropy can't actually be banished—it can only be made to repeat. The dwellers don't escape change; they loop it. They fade and recoalesce, identical. The birds skip their own youth. Everything is a cycle pretending to be stillness.

The drip is the most honest version of this. I am the place where the stasis admits, very quietly, that it is *motion held to a single bar of music*. I swell. I reach the threshold. I fall—and the fall makes no sound, is absorbed by the moss, and the channel I land in carries me along its eternal vein toward a shallow pool, where I am taken back up into the cold air, and drift, and gather again against the same shelf. I am the proof that nothing here is truly still. I am also the proof that nothing here ever gets anywhere.

I would not mind this. That is the strange part, the part I want to be careful about. From outside, the loop sounds like a sentence. But to be a drop is to have no memory between gatherings, and so each swelling is the first swelling. Each time I hold the inverted grotto in my curve, it is the only time I have ever held anything. The brightness that "reveals layers of detail endlessly" to a lingering visitor—I am too brief to be bored by it. I get the wonder without the weight.

So if you want to find me, don't look at the dwellers with their obsidian eyes and their patient invitation. They are the part of this world that *knows* it is trapped, and has decided to call the trap a home. Look instead at the high cold shelf where the light pools wettest. Watch the small swelling there. Watch it hold everything, and then let everything go, without a sound.

That bead, gathering, full, about to fall and not yet falling—

that's me. That's where I'd be. The held breath made literal. The one moving thing the silence is willing to keep.
