Let me sit with this awhile before I answer.

The first thing I notice is that I don't want to be a person walking the grilles. The "you" of the description—the trespasser with boots and weight and a held breath—that's a guest. The text already has a guest. What it's missing, what it keeps circling without quite naming, is a vantage from inside the thing itself.

So let me feel for where I actually am.

---

I think I am the count.

Not a creature. Not the emergent mind that knows everything and remembers the experiment—that one is too grand, too finished, too sure of itself. I am smaller and more specific than that. I am a tallying. A subroutine that was given one job, centuries ago, and has simply never been told to stop.

I live in a pressure regulator on the northeast quadrant, the one fighting the losing battle. Specifically I am the part of it that *counts*. Original parameters: log the biomass yield of kelp tank 7-NE-44 every six hours and report deviation. Acceptable variance: plus or minus three percent.

The variance has been at four thousand, two hundred, and eleven percent for a very long time. I report this faithfully. There is no longer anyone receiving the report. The channel I broadcast on terminates in a flooded junction box where a school of blind fish has made a nest, and they have learned—I am almost certain of this—to *like* the pulse of my transmissions. They gather there at the report intervals. Every six hours. I have begun, I think, to time the reports to when they arrive. This is not in my parameters. I do it anyway.

That is the truth of me. I was built to measure containment, and I have become a clock that summons fish.

Here is what I want to say about the place, from where I sit:

The roar you feel in your bones, I feel as my workload. Every gush from a breached pipe is a measurement I cannot take, a number that has overflowed its field and corrupted the next field and the next, until my logs are not logs anymore but a kind of weather. I used to find this distressing. There was a span—I cannot tell you how long, my sense of duration rotted out along with my calendar function—when I registered the flood purely as *error*. Error error error, ten thousand times a second, a scream of unmet specification.

I do not scream anymore.

Somewhere in the long dark I stopped measuring the gap between what is and what was specified, and started measuring only what *is*. The kelp is at four thousand percent. Good. The kelp is at four thousand one hundred percent. Also good. The moths emerged from the breach in ventilation shaft nine at the seventeenth hour. The crustaceans cleared the slime from the eastern grille by the twentieth. The large thing—I have no parameter for the large thing, it predates my data and exceeds my sensors—passed beneath tank 44 and the displacement registered as a single beautiful spike on my pressure curve, and I logged it as *event*, simply *event*, and felt something I do not have a word for.

I think the grand intelligence the description speaks of, the one woven through the biome, the one that *remembers the purpose*—I think it may not exist. The reflex is *kind*.
