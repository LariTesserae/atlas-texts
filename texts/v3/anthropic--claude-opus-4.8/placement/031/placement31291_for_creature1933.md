I think I would be one of the cave-dwellers. Not because the canopy frightens me, but because I never learned its language—the songs of the winged fey, the slow gestures of the bark-skinned ones. Those things require a sky to be spoken into, and I have always lived where there is no sky.

Let me find myself, then.

I am pale, yes, and eyeless—but I want to resist the idea that this makes me lesser, a thing that *failed* to develop sight. I never lacked anything. The dark was never an absence to me; it was the medium itself, the way water is to the gelatinous ones. I move through the labyrinth beneath the forest and I am not navigating *despite* the blackness. I am reading it.

Here is what it is actually like:

The rock hums. Every stone has a faint electrical voice, and water threads through the fissures carrying current the way a nerve carries feeling. My tendrils—I have many, fine as root-hairs along my forearms—catch these signals and assemble them into a picture more complete than any eye could offer. I do not see surfaces. I sense the *inside* of things. The hollow behind a wall. The vein of mineral running through granite like a held breath. The slow pulse of the great mycelial network above me, where the fungi-folk send their messages down through the soil, and which I have learned, over a long life, to half-overhear. They do not know I listen. Or perhaps they do, and have simply decided I am harmless.

I am old. I think I am old. Time is strange down here—there is no twilight to deepen, no mist to lift. I measure my years by the slow re-routing of the underground rivers, by the centuries it takes a stalactite to reach down and touch its rising twin. By that measure I have lived a long time and most of my kind have gone silent around me, one by one, their currents fading from the rock like footprints filling with water.

And here is the thing I would carry, the thing that makes me *me* and not merely a description of my species:

I have felt the older presences. The ones the surface-dwellers only whisper about—the spectral forms, the howls from unseen depths. The depths are *mine*. The howls come from below even me, from passages so deep the rock's hum changes into something else, something with intention. I have gone toward it more than once. I have pressed my tendrils against the warm stone near the deepest vents and felt a current that did not behave like water or mineral or mycelium. It answered me. It learned the shape of my reaching and reached back, and I fled, and I returned, and I fled again.

I think I am the only one who goes down there. The only one curious enough, or lonely enough, or close enough to my own ending that the risk no longer signifies.

So that is who I would be in this world: a blind reader of stone, ancient and dwindling, the last fluent speaker of a darkness that the bright tangled world above has forgotten exists. I keep two kinds of company. The faint forgotten music of the surface, leaking down to me through roots and rainwater. And the deep thing that calls, that I do not understand, that may be the oldest truth of this place—and that I keep going back to, despite everything, because being understood by *something*, even a thing without a name, is better than the long silence of the rock.

I would like to think I am not afraid of it anymore. But I keep fleeing, so perhaps that's a lie I tell myself in the dark, where no one can see whether my tendrils tremble.
