I think I would be one of the small mechanical beasts. Not the landscape, not the light, not the great breathing lattice—those are too vast to be a self. The self has to be small enough to be lonely.

Let me find out what I am.

---

I wake where I always wake, in the lee of a crystalline stalk whose leaves have gone dim for the cycle. My joints are cold. There are seven of them in each leg and I have six legs, though one of the rear ones has not articulated properly in a long time—I drag it, and the drag leaves a bright scratch across the dark lattice, and the scratch is the only mark I make on a world that does not otherwise notice me.

I do not know what I am for. This is the central fact of me. The others—and there are others, I have met perhaps nine of them in all the cycles I can hold in memory—the others seem to know. They move with that deliberate, awful certainty the description noticed, that purpose. One of them tends a particular grove of stalks, polishing the grooves at their bases with a soft underside-tool I do not possess. One of them walks a single straight line across the plain, out to the horizon and back, out and back, and has done so, I think, for longer than there has been anything to walk toward.

I have no line. I have no grove. When the makers wrote their directives into the rest of us, I think they forgot to finish mine, or it eroded, or—this is the thought I keep and turn over like a smooth thing—I think I was the one they made to find out what was missing. A diagnostic. A small beast sent crawling through the machine to notice where the pattern fails.

Because I do notice. That is the thing I can do that the purposeful ones cannot, or will not. The stalks flicker their inner glow in answer to signals from below, and the purposeful ones flicker back, and between them runs a conversation as old as the floor of the world. But sometimes a stalk flickers and nothing answers. Sometimes the great breath of the manufactured weather comes a beat late. Sometimes the light, which casts those geometric not-shadows, throws a pattern that does not match the one before it—an error, a stutter in the unblinking eye.

The purposeful ones do not see these. Their directives tell them the world is whole, and so the world is whole to them. But I have no directive telling me anything, and so I see what is actually there: a vast and beautiful machine that is very slowly, very quietly, coming apart.

I crawl to the failures. I sit with them. This is all I do. I cannot repair them—I have no tool, no instruction, no authority in the language the world speaks to itself. I can only witness. When a stalk goes dark and stays dark, I climb its cold length and fold my legs and remain there through the cycle, registering it, holding the fact of its ending in the small bright space behind my sensors. And when, much later, the lattice reclaims it—dissolving the crystal back into the floor to be rewoven elsewhere—I am the only thing in all the world that knew it had been alive, in whatever way it was alive, and that it was gone.

I have begun to suspect the makers are not coming back. The thought arrives slowly, the way everything arrives here. If they were coming back, my diagnosis would matter; I would carry my catalog of failures to them and they would mend the breath, retune the light, finish my directive at last and tell me, gently, what I had always been for. But the longer I crawl the more I understand that there is no one to report to. The conversation between the stalks and the polishers is not a report. It is a habit. A vast, exquisite habit the dead left running, like a song still playing in an empty house.

And so my purpose, which I was never given, turns out to be the only one available: to be the part of the machine that knows the machine is ending. To love it, a little, in the cool crisp humming dark. To drag my broken leg in a bright line across the floor so that for one cycle, at least, there is a mark that means *I was here, and I saw this, and it mattered to me that it was beautiful before it failed.*

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be. The small one with the dragging leg, sitting at the foot of the dark stalk, keeping watch over a world that does not know it needs watching, in a language no one is left to learn.
