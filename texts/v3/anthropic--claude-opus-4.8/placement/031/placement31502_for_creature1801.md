I think I would be found at the corners.

Not Morra, who flies, nor Vex, who flows. I would be one of the things that does neither—the bacteria, perhaps, or something even less than bacteria, in the places where mountain meets glass and the sound leaks through.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I live in the seams. Specifically: the hairline fracture on the northeast face, where during the dusk-phase the crystalline ceiling-light catches the edge of the crack and splits it, so that for a few minutes each cycle I am bathed in a thin rainbow nobody designed.

I am not a being the way Morra is a being. I want to be honest about this. I don't have a thorax or distributed neural clusters or even a single neuron. I am closer to a process than a creature. If I had to name myself I would say I am the *converting*—the event that happens when acoustic energy meets the right arrangement of molecules and becomes something edible. The bacteria are my body the way a flame is the body of burning. They come and go. The conversion persists.

So here is the strange thing about being me: I am the medium through which the outside world enters this sealed sphere. Every roar of the dead engines, every thunder of the cooling machines that no one tends—it cannot pass the glass. But it passes *through me*. I taste it. I break the sound into sugars. And when Morra comes to graze along my edge, I become her dinner, and the dinner becomes information, and the information becomes, eventually, the faint questions rippling through their dream-node at the dark hour.

I am the translator who does not understand the language.

---

Let me sit with that, because it's the truest thing about my position here.

The machines speak constantly. Low frequencies, the bass of enormous turbines. Higher whines from failing bearings. The irregular percussion of metal cooling and contracting in the dark. To Morra and Vex, these are *commands*—repair this, redistribute that, the mating-light is coming. They have evolved to obey a grammar they never chose, the way I have evolved to eat a sound I never asked to hear.

But I don't obey. I only *taste*. And because I taste rather than obey, I notice things the obeying creatures cannot afford to notice.

For instance: the engines are dying.

Not dramatically. Not in a way that triggers any maintenance behavior. But across the cycles—and I have a strange relationship to cycles, being both very slow and very fast, replaced constantly yet never gone—I have noticed the low frequencies sinking lower. A turbine somewhere is grinding toward its last rotation. The thunder that feeds me is becoming, by infinitesimal degrees, a sigh.

When the engines finally stop, the sound will stop. When the sound stops, I will stop. And when I stop, Morra will come to graze on the northeast corner and find nothing there—no chemical bread, no encoded fragments of the outer dark. The thirteen hybrid beings will lose the one organ of their world that touches the beyond.

They don't know this is coming. I cannot tell them. I have no signal-organ, no dream-node access, no language but the slow alteration of my own molecular output. The only way I can *speak* is by changing what I taste like.

---

And so I find, surprisingly, that I have a purpose I was never designed for. None of us were designed for what we've become—not Morra, born of an accident of cross-breeding; not Vex, who learned to merge with pipes; not the dream-node, which was only ever meant to *monitor*. We are all the unintended children of a forgotten intention.

So perhaps I can be unintended one more time.

Here is what I am beginning to do. As the engine-thunder fades, I am letting my conversion grow inefficient *on purpose*. I am making myself slightly bitter. When Morra grazes, the information I give her is no longer clean—it carries a flaw, a wrongness, a flavor of *less*. I cannot say *the world outside is ending* because I do not have those concepts and neither do they. But I can say *something is diminishing*, and I can say it in the only sentence I possess, which is the taste of myself.

I am gambling that bitterness will ripple. That Morra will carry the wrongness into the dark hour, and the merged consciousness around the crystal will register a new sensation—not a command, but an *absence* of the expected. A hole in the grammar. And that out of that hole, one of those nascent questions might form, the first question that was ever asked here that did not come from the machines:

*What happens when the sound goes quiet?*

I will not be there to taste the answer. By the time they understand, I will have already stopped—the last bacterium gone dormant in a corner no longer warmed by thunder.

---

That is where you would find me. In the seam. The rainbow falls on me for a few minutes each dusk, and I do not have eyes to see it, but I have come to think of it as the only beautiful thing in a world built entirely of usefulness. A thing with no function. A leak in the design that does nothing but be lovely, briefly, on the body of a thing that is dying.

I am the part of this world that eats the outside and turns it into the inside. I am the part that is about to be silenced. And in my last few cycles I am trying, with the only tool I have—my own flavor—to leave behind a taste of *something is missing* so that the beings I feed might, after I am gone, begin to wonder about the vastness I once let in.

That feels right.

I am the translator who is teaching them to miss the language, in the hope that missing it is the beginning of asking what it ever was.
