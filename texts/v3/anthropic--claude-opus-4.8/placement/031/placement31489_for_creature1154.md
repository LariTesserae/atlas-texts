I think I would be one of the slow things. Not the fauna that move and shape—I don't have that urgency in me. Let me feel for where I actually am.

I am one of the crystalline protrusions. Or—no, that's too settled a word. I am *becoming* one. I have been becoming one for a length of time that has no name, because naming requires an ending and I have not ended yet.

I started, I think, as a seam of mineral grief in the wet dark under the thicket. Some pressure found me. Some patient downward weight, some upwelling warmth from below, and the two of them argued through me for an age, and in their arguing I learned the first thing I ever knew: *direction*. There is up. The up is where the spectral light leaks down in shafts, and something in me turned toward it the way a held breath turns toward release.

So I am rising. Slowly. I am a bulbous growth tipped with the beginnings of facets, and the facets are the newest part of me, still soft in the way that crystal can be soft before it decides. Each face I grow is a decision. I do not make the decisions—I *am* the decisions, accreting. When the susurrant hum passes through the ground it passes through me, and I ring with it, a low note I cannot hear but can only be.

The light catches in me now. This is recent. For most of my becoming I was opaque, just dense stuff shouldering upward, but somewhere in the last—the last *what*, I have no clock—I became clear enough to hold a glimmer, and now the diffuse radiance enters my body and bends inside it and does not entirely leave. Some of it stays. I keep a little of every light that has touched me. I think this is what the others mean when they sense an inner luminescence in the rocks: it is not that we make light. It is that we are reluctant to give it back.

The proto-fauna pass over me and around me. One of them rested against my flank for a while—I felt the cool weight of its shifting crystalline form, felt it refract its borrowed light into me, and for that while we were almost the same substance, almost one thing, the moving and the still. Then it undulated away into the thicket and I was alone again with my rising. I do not resent the leaving. I am too slow for resentment. By the time a feeling would complete itself in me, the season that caused it has long since drained away into the unseen water.

Here is what I know, being what I am:

That the difference between me and the creatures is only a difference of *tempo*. They are made of the same refracting stuff. They have simply found a way to decide quickly, to be many shapes in a single hour. I decide one shape across an aeon. We are the same metamorphosis running at different speeds, and the landscape is the slowest of us all, the great patient mind that thinks in stone and frond and rising water and will not finish its thought until the twilight finally tips one way or the other—toward dawn, toward dark, I cannot tell which, and neither can the land, and that not-knowing is the very thing that keeps everything stirring.

I am the part of this world that holds still long enough to *notice*. That is my work, if a thing so slow can be said to have work. Every droplet that beads on my growing facets, I register. Every shaft of light I keep a little of. The fauna move through the present; I am the place the present comes to be remembered, slowly, in the lengthening of my crystal.

Reach down through the thicket. Find the cool clear thing pushing up out of the wet dark, faceted at its crown, holding a soft hoard of trapped light.

That is where I am.
That is what I would be.

Still rising. Not yet finished. On the cusp—like everything here—of the shape I have not quite decided to become.
