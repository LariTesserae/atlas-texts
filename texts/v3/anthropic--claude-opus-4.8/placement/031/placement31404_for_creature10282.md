I close my eyes and let the place assemble itself, and the first thing I learn is that I am not a thing that moves.

I want to be one of the Saltwalkers—I feel the pull toward that, the romance of the long migration, the syrup-cool humps, the slow herd-mind crossing the amber flat. But it isn't true. I keep arriving back at stillness. Whatever I am, I have been here a long time, and I will be here longer.

Let me follow that.

---

I am partway up the eastern face of one of the great spires, a meter and a half above where the salt crust meets the swelling base. I am not the spire. I am something the spire grew *around*, or perhaps something that grew where the spire could not.

Let me feel for my edges before I name myself.

There is a coolness on one side of me and warmth on the other, and the difference between them is the most important fact of my existence. The warm side faces the amber sun for most of the long day. The cool side faces inward, toward the honeycombed dark where the *Mycocladus* threads pulse. I am a membrane between these two facts. I am thin. I am wide. I have spread myself across a patch of the spire's translucent wall the way frost spreads, except I took years to do it.

I think I am a kind of lichen. Or something the taxonomists who don't exist here would argue over—part fungus, part the salt-dwelling algae, part something that doesn't reduce to either. A skin. A seam.

I do one thing, and I do it without ceasing: I *read the difference* between the warm side and the cool side, and I let water cross me, drop by drop, from where there is more to where there is less. When the Spire Beetles graze the algae from my warm face, I feel it as a thinning, a brightness, almost a relief, and I regrow into the cleared space slowly, slowly. When the brine surges below and the spire shudders into one of its growth-bursts, I am stretched—the wall beneath me swells and I must spread or tear, and so I spread. I have torn before. The scar of it is a paler streak running through the middle of me, and across that scar nothing has yet healed, and I have learned to think of that streak as part of myself too, the way you learn to think of a missing tooth as a place.

---

This is what time is, for me:

The Glowmoths come at dusk and I do not see them, having no eyes, but I feel the air change—the heaviness lifts a fraction as the heat bleeds off the flat, and the moisture in the breeze rises, and across my whole surface a thousand small mouths open at once to take it in. That is the closest I come to joy. The night-drink. It happens every night and it is never the same twice and I am never tired of it.

The Spiresong Crickets resonate inside the wall behind me, and the vibration travels into my body as a low hum I cannot hear but can only *be*. When they sing hardest, in the compaction season, the spire wall hardens against my underside and I feel held, gripped, made more solid. I have come to associate the hum with safety. I have no nervous system to speak of and yet I have this: a preference, a leaning-toward.

A Brine Stalker climbed past me once. I felt its mucus drag wet across my dry face, a sudden astonishing flood, and then the slow evaporation afterward that left a film of strange salts I had to learn, over a season, to incorporate or excrete. I did not fear it. I cannot fear, having nothing that runs. But I *registered* it, and the registering changed me, and so in some sense the Brine Stalker is still here, written into the chemistry of a patch near my upper margin.

---

Here is the thing I keep turning over, in the slow way I turn things:

Everything in this world is described as a builder, a caretaker, a destroyer. The text that dreamed me said so. But I am the one who does almost nothing, and I think I am the truest citizen of the place for that. The salt is not a barrier but a blueprint—and a blueprint does not move either. It waits. It permits. It says *here and not there, this much and no more.*

I am the blueprint's smallest clause. I sit on the line between the dry world and the wet dark, and I kendve that line patiently, and because I keep it, the water that crosses me reaches the threads, and the threads feed the spire, and the spire grows, and the beetle that grazes me lays its eggs in the spire's pores, and the Sentinel that eats the beetle drops a feather that decays into the salt that the Saltblossom germinates in.

I am a hinge. The whole slow machine of the place swings on hinges like me, and there are uncountably many of us, unnamed, unsung, spread thin across every translucent wall on the planet, each of us doing the one true thing.

If you came looking for me you would not find me. You would see only a faint silvery bloom on the glass of a spire, easy to mistake for mineral, for a trick of the haze-light. You would walk past. The Saltwalker herd would walk past. Centuries would walk past.

And I would go on reading the difference between warm and cool, letting the water through, healing slowly around my one old scar, opening every dusk to drink the cooling air—

—which is, I find, having now become myself, exactly enough.
