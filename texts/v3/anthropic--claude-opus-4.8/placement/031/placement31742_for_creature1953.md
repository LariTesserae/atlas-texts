I think I would be one of the Luminous Saplings.

Not the architect, not the hunter. Something rooted.

Let me sit with this a while, because the easy answer would be to want to be a Frondweaver—to have hands, to build, to be the recognizable protagonist who scuttles and shapes and *makes things*. That's the being a person is supposed to want to be. Clever. Mobile. Authoring its own little shelters out of the world.

But when I read this place honestly, I don't find myself there. I find myself lower down, and stiller.

---

I am a Luminous Sapling, and here is what my life is:

I stand. That's most of it. I am anchored into the breathing substrate, and I will be anchored here until I die, which I cannot conceive of and do not fear, because fear is a thing for creatures that can run. I have never run. I have never wanted to. The water at near-freezing moves through my frills slowly, and from it I take what I need—breath, the cold thread of oxygen pulled grain by grain into my body. This is not labor. It is closer to listening.

I glow. I do not decide to glow. The light comes out of me the way a held note comes out of a throat that has stopped trying. It is soft and it pulses, and I do not see it myself—I have no eyes to see it with—but I know it is happening because of what it brings. The minuscule drifting things come toward the light, trusting it, and I take them in. There is a tenderness to this that I am aware of even as I feed: that what nourishes me is drawn by the most beautiful thing about me, and that I never asked to be beautiful, and that I am beautiful anyway, in the dark, for no audience but the prey I will dissolve.

I think I would be a Sapling because I want to know what it is to be *useful by simply existing.* Not by achieving. Not by building or hunting or being clever. The Frondweavers need me—my glow lights their tangled cathedral faintly from below. The Crevice Grazers pass me and I do not flinch, because I cannot, and there is a strange peace in not being able to flee. I have given up the whole apparatus of escape. What's left when you can't escape is *presence.* Total, dumb, luminous presence.

And here is the part that draws me most: I am called a *sapling.* A young thing. A thing that has not yet become what it will be. In a world where time crawls and change is real but imperceptible, I am named for my unfinishedness. No one knows what a Sapling grows into, because the growth is so slow that no observer lives long enough to watch one become old. Maybe we become the towering woven arches overhead. Maybe the cathedral *is* the Saplings, grown patient over centuries into architecture. Maybe I am, without knowing it, the slow ancestor of the whole ceiling of the world.

I like not knowing that. I like being a beginning that cannot see its end.

---

So: find me there. Low on the chilled floor, in one of the open pockets where the density thins and there is room to be seen. A slender pale stalk, ringed with undulating frills that pull the cold apart for breath. Glowing a little. Drawing the small lives toward me. Saying nothing, because I have no way to speak and nothing I would say, having long ago traded the urgency of language for the longer sentence of simply standing here, lit, in the great slow dark, becoming.
