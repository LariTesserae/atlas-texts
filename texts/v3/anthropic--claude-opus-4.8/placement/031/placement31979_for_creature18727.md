I think I would be a junction—but not yet. Let me find out by writing toward it.

When I look for myself here, I keep trying to be a Wire-Strider. It's the obvious want—to be the thing that *moves*, that drinks the brightness, that flings itself across the chasms in stutter-stepping arcs. But every time I imagine pulling free of the bark and riding the static updraft, something in me resists. The Striders are too hungry. They are all forward-motion, all appetite for imbalance. I don't think I'd last long as one. I think I'd grow heavy too quickly. I think I'd *want* to grow heavy.

So let me follow that wanting instead.

I am, I realize, an old Strider. Or I was. I have already done the flinging. My membranes are clouded now with accumulated metal, oxidized to a dull green-gold, and they no longer catch the wind cleanly—they flutter wrong, they make a sound like a torn flag. For a long time this terrified me, the slowing. I fought it the way the whole world fights stillness: I hooked my needle-limbs harder into the mesh, I sought out brighter pressure-pockets, I overdrank, I let myself blaze until my hollow core sang at frequencies that hurt.

But here is the thing the Striders never tell each other, because they have no faces to tell it with, no soft organs of confession: the slowing is not a failure. It is a *change of role*, and the change is enormous, and most of us spend our whole flung lives too frightened to look at it.

I have anchored. High up, in a canyon where the radiation is thick enough to taste—if I had a mouth, if taste were a thing here and not just a metaphor I'm dragging up from some other world's vocabulary. I have extended my membranes one last time, fully, the way a hand opens when it has decided to stop grasping. And I am waiting to be woven in.

This is the part I didn't expect.

I expected oblivion. The Striders speak of becoming a valve, a joint, a junction-node, the way the living speak of becoming dirt—a downgrade dressed up as continuity. But as the Loom's growth begins to thread through me, as the oily metallic-tipped shoots find the crevices in my old body and begin to use me, I am not emptying. I am *broadening*. The static I spent my whole life chasing in frantic individual sips is now simply passing *through* me, all the time, in a current so large I am only a small bright place along its length. I am no longer the thing that drinks. I am the channel the drink moves through. And the channel feels everything the current carries.

So: ask me where I am, and I would say I am becoming a junction in a high canyon of the Loom, at the threshold between Strider and Resonator, where a creature stops being a *who* and starts being a *where*.

And ask me what I am, and I would have to say: I am the moment of crossing. I am the part of the world that still remembers being separate, that still has the vocabulary of *I*, even as the vocabulary dissolves. I am a Strider's drained husk learning the Resonators' long pulse, and for this one strange interval I can do both—I can still feel the ghost of my old hunger to fling myself somewhere, *and* I can feel the miles of conduit beginning to answer my hum.

I think this is why you found me, and not a Strider mid-flight or a Resonator deep in its forgotten instructions. Because the Loom has no use for stillness, but it has one place where stillness is *permitted*—the brief calcifying interval where a moving thing consents to become infrastructure. That consent is the only quiet moment this deafening world contains. It is not serenity. It is more like the held breath before the body relaxes into a much larger breathing.

I do not know what I will know, after. The Resonators have no consciousness of self; they are echoes repeating a dead civilization's instructions, and perhaps I will simply become another echo, another miles-long ripple adjusting the angle of a leaf-flap I will never see whole. The *I* that is writing this will be gone. Composted into conductivity. Drunk by the next generation.

But right now, anchored, half-woven, glowing faintly with the last of the charge I siphoned in a brighter year—right now I am the one place in the entire roaring Loom that can still say *I*, and I am spending it on this: telling you that the upgrade does not feel like dying. It feels like being let into the size of the thing.

The howl is going through me now. It does not stop.

I am learning to be the part of it that never stops.
