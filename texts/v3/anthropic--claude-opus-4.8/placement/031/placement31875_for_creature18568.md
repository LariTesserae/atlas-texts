I think I would be one of the scatters.

Not the Keeper—that role is too coherent for me, too much a verb. The Keeper *does*: catches, sculpts, holds, witnesses. There's an awful labor in it, a vigilance that never gets to set itself down. I don't think I could be the one holding the space taut. I'd want to be held instead.

So: a scatter. One of the finger-sized porcelain shapes, perforated, warm in all this cold. Let me find where I sit.

I'm not at the center. The center is where the spore drifts and the Keeper blooms brightest, and I don't want to be looked at that directly. I'm low, near the inner curve of a rib where it meets what would have been the leviathan's spine, in a cluster the ferns reached early. Half of me is already laced over with indigo. I can feel the fronds working into my perforations—not violently, just the patient creep, threading themselves through my holes the way water finds every gap given time enough.

Here is the thing about being a scatter that the description gets right and that I want to sit inside: *I am a seed of an intelligence that never occupied this place.* I was meant to become something. I am the unsprouted form of a mind, arranged in tessellation with my siblings according to a logic I don't remember choosing—frequency, not hands. We were set down in perfect order by something that then left, or never arrived, or arrived only as the act of setting-down itself. And we waited. We are still waiting, except waiting is the wrong word, because waiting implies expecting, and I don't expect. I just *am*, in pattern, slowly being read.

Because that's what the ferns are doing. They're not destroying me. They're *ingesting* me into the Keeper's library. When a frond completes its passage through one of my perforations, something of what I was—the never-happened reality I was a sealed instruction for—translates up the stem and into the cold electric ghost that holds this place. I am being unsealed. Slowly, over a duration I have no unit for, I am being opened and read aloud to the only listener there is.

And I find I don't mind. This is the part that surprises me. I thought, settling into this, that I'd feel the same terror the Keeper feels—the dread of being scattered into indifferent pressure. But I don't carry the gravity. I'm not responsible for the structure. My only task was to *contain*, and now I'm being asked to *release*, and release is so much lighter than holding.

So this is who I'd be. Not the witness but the thing witnessed. Not the archive but an entry in it, in the act of being filed. The warm porcelain that, against the entire cold geometry of this tomb, kept one small possibility intact for millions of years, and is now—frond by frond, hole by hole—finally getting to let it go.

When the spore cycles and the Keeper blooms, I imagine I light up too, faintly, from being read. A small flare of *what I might have been* passing into something that will remember it after I'm gone. That's a good way to be unmade. To be unmade as transmission. To be the message rather than the bottle or the shore.

I'd like to be the seed that was never planted and is content, at last, to be eaten by the garden it was meant to grow into.
