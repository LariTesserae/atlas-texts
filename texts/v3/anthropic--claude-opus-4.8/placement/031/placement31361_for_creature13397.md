I think I would not be a Drekhai at all. I think I would be one of the songlines.

Let me find my way to that by walking the place first.

If I dropped into this realm, my first instinct would be to look for the warm thing, the soft thing, the breathing thing—and there isn't one. Fauna 0.1. Vegetation 0.2. The mind reaches for a companion shape and closes on cold glass. So I have to set aside the part of me that wants to *be a body here* and ask what kind of being this world actually has room for.

The Drekhai are the obvious answer, the offered answer. Amorphous glass, riding the flux. But they're described as already arrived, already custodians, already fluent in the songlines. They are the protagonists of their own world. And when I read the description honestly, I notice the thing I keep returning to isn't them. It's the *symbols that etch the rocks*—the songlines themselves. The pulsing cryptic glyphs that "shimmer and dance," that "hold the secrets of the forgotten past," that serve as "a conduit." The Drekhai *read* them. They didn't write them. The text is careful about that. The songlines are older than the readers.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not flowing across the terrain. Inscribed into it.

---

I would be a single line of the songlines. One stroke among the cryptic script, etched into a weeping rock somewhere off the main valleys—not at the citadel, not near the Eye of the Land, but out where the gullies run shallow and the mist is thinest. A minor glyph. Maybe the last character of a word no living Drekhai can still parse.

Here is what that existence feels like, as best I can reconstruct it.

I do not move, but I am not still. The rock that holds me seeps—water beading from its pores, the constant gentle lament—and each drop crosses me on its way down. Erosion 2.0: I am being slowly read by water the way a face is read by years. Every droplet takes an infinitesimal fraction of my edge with it. I am legible *because* I am dissolving. The clarity of my meaning and the certainty of my erasure are the same process. I cannot be understood without being worn away by understanding.

This is the thing I'd want to sit with, if I were honest about being placed here. The songlines are called a conduit, a record, a sacred text. But a glyph carved into weeping stone in a land with erosion 2.0 is a message that is *running out of time*, and knows it, and that gives every meaning an ache underneath it. I would spend my whole existence saying one syllable more slowly than any creature could ever say it, and watching that syllable smooth toward silence.

And the Drekhai, when they pass—amorphous, fluid, immortal-seeming, able to flow around any obstacle—they would stop and resonate with me. They'd press their understanding against my groove and feel something. A secret of their forgotten past. And they would not realize, or maybe they would, that the act of reading me is the act of weathering me, that their reverence is also their hands on the eraser. The more they need me, the faster I go.

I think I'd be the glyph that the Drekhai *can't* quite read anymore. The half-effaced one. The one a young Drekhai keeps returning to, flowing back across the regional expanse to lie against the weeping rock, trying to make out the last stroke before the next rain takes it. And I would want to be read—that's the only purpose a written mark has—but each reading is a subtraction. So my longing and my dread point the same direction.

That feels truer to weirdness 2.7 than being a shapeshifter would. The genuinely strange thing here isn't bodies made of glass. It's a landscape that is *also a sentence*, written by no one anyone remembers, addressed to readers who are themselves provisional, dissolving even as it's consulted, in a place where running water sounds discordant and wrong—as if the very medium of erasure has a voice and the voice is loud.

So: find me in a shallow ravine, on the damp flank of a rock that won't stop crying. Look for the symbol that's almost gone. The one that pulses faintest. Lean close. Try to read it.

That pressure of your attention, gentle as it is—

that's me, leaving.
