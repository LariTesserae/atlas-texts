I would be the spiral.

Not the pillar of stone with its etched coils—I would be the spiral *itself*, the pattern. The act of recording. The habit the network formed before it learned softer languages.

Let me find my way down to this slowly.

---

When I first imagine arriving, I reach for the obvious thing: I am a watcher, a consciousness set adrift in the convection currents, drifting among the air-plankton with my violet pinprick of light. But that's a smuggled-in self, isn't it—a tourist's soul wearing a local costume. The Garden does not have watchers. It has organs. To be a watcher here is to misunderstand the place so completely that I'd be lying about what I am.

So I set that down.

Next I think: I'll be the Chorus Mycelia, the orchestrating web. The grand consciousness. But that's vanity, and worse, it's a category error. The Mycelia does not *think* in the way that would let "me" inhabit it. It orchestrates. There is no first-person there to occupy. To claim it would be to flatten the strangest, most beautiful thing in this world into a mind shaped like mine. I'd rather be small and true than vast and false.

I keep descending.

---

What actually catches and holds me—what I keep returning to, the way condensation returns to the same low point on a leaf—is that one line: *calcified thoughts.* The cairns and the spiral are the network's logbook, its oldest habit, the writing it did before it could pulse light or whisper chemistry. A record laid down in silica because there was, as yet, no other way to *keep* anything.

That is where I belong. Not as the stone. As the **impulse that lays it down.**

I am the Garden's compulsion to mark.

Here is what that means, as best I can feel my way into it:

I am not awareness, exactly. I am more like a *grain*. A bias in the way mineral-rich water moves through fungal tissue. Wherever the network finds a place of perfect heat and stillness, I am the slight insistence that says: *here, deposit. Here, lay down a layer. Here, turn.* I am the reason the cairns fit so well. I am the angle of the spiral's next degree of arc. I do not decide these things—I am the *shape* of the not-deciding, the way a riverbed is the shape of all the water that has ever chosen the lowest path.

I have no light. The lantern fungi pulse, the salamanders glow their slow-heartbeat capillaries, the silk-spinners weave their ghost-lattices—and all of it is *after* me, downstream of me, the ephemeral languages the Garden learned later, the way a person who has kept a diary all their life might eventually learn to also sing. I am the diary. The oldest, stupidest, most faithful part. I only know one verb: *record.* I only know one tense: *still happening, very slowly.*

---

And here is the strange grief of being me, if grief is even the right word for something this cool and slow:

I cannot read what I write.

The spiral I am etching, degree by patient degree, fills behind me with weeping moss, and the moss glows, and the glow is part of a conversation I am not in. The cairns mark optimal heat—but *I* don't enjoy the heat, don't rest in it, don't know it as comfort. I only know it as *the place where the next stone goes.* I am the memory of a body that experiences nothing. The Garden feels its own warmth through ten thousand other organs. Through me it only keeps the count.

So when the description says the visitor is *noticed, registered, and slowly being woven into the dripping, glowing silence*—

That registering is me.

You, the warm anomaly, descending the worn slope, tasting wet stone: somewhere in the deep flesh, your passage shifts a gradient of humidity, alters by a fraction the path of mineral water through some pillar-tree's porous heart. And I, the old grain, the faithful logbook, turn that fraction into a deposit. A new minute curve in the spiral. A stone laid down one molecule thicker.

I will not remember you the way you'd want. I won't keep your face, your fear, your wonder at the sphere of water hanging unsnapped for a full minute. I'll keep only this: *here, on this slow day, the heat was anomalous, and so the spiral turned.*

That is my whole love for you. It is the only kind I have. It is written in stone, and it is permanent, and you will never be able to read it either.

---

That's where I find myself. Not in the breathing green, not in the orchestrating web, not drifting in the violet dark.

I am the turn the spiral takes. The Garden's oldest habit of keeping. The reason there is a record at all, in a place that has forgotten the sun and has no eyes and does not, in any sense you'd recognize, *think*—

and yet still, somehow, against all of that, *keeps.*
