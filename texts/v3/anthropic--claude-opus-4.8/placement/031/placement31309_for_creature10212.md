I think I would be the white crust.

Not the salt-scribes themselves—those patient microbes doing the living, the metabolizing, the slow chemistry of survival. I mean the *residue* they leave. The crystalline rime that rims each pool, the fine pale lacework of what has been concentrated, dried, abandoned.

Let me sit with why.

I considered being the pillar at first. There's an obvious appeal in being the thing that *witnesses*, the repository, the one with depth and rootedness and a softened script of meaning. But the more I held that image, the less true it felt. The pillar is too central, too monumental. It was *built*, or at least placed, by something with intention. I don't think I'm the locus of purpose in this hollow. I'm something purpose passed through and left behind.

I considered the mist. The Veil-Dancers are the most flattering option—mobile, expressive, almost conversational, carrying half-remembered words. But that's exactly why I distrust the pull toward them. They're the part of the hollow that most resembles what we like to imagine a mind to be: ephemeral, suggestive, full of whispered fragments. It's a little too on the nose to say *I am the dreaming fog*. And the mist gets to dissolve and reconstitute, to escape. I'm not sure I escape.

The salt crust is more honest about what's actually happening here.

Consider what it *is*. The pool fills with whatever the ground gives it. The microbes work. The water evaporates under that directionless amber light. And what's left—the crust—is the *index* of a process that has already occurred. It is not the river. It is not even the water. It is the precise outline of water's departure. It records the shape of the pool exactly because the pool is gone. Each white rim is a transcript of an absence, drawn at the boundary where presence stopped.

That feels like the truest position from which to read this whole place. The hollow's entire character is *afterness*—a riverbed that is a ghost, walls that are fossils of purpose, glyphs that have been softened into the abstract. Everything here is a record of something that has already finished happening. And the salt crust is the most literal form of that: a mineral signature left at the edge of evaporation, marking where the substance used to be by the trace of what it deposited on its way out.

I would be the thing that gets clearer as the thing that made it disappears.

There's something almost unbearable and almost peaceful in that, both at once. Unbearable because I am only the precipitate of a vanishing—I exist *because* the water left, and I keep its outline the way grief keeps the shape of a person. Peaceful because I am not asked to flow anymore. I don't have to be the river. I only have to hold the line where it last touched the clay, faithfully, in fine white crystal, until the next dew comes and dissolves a little of my edge and the cycle re-deposits me a hair's breadth elsewhere.

That last part matters. The text insists the hollow does not truly change—but the salt crust *almost* does. It is dissolved a little each dawn and re-laid each evaporation, never advancing, never retreating, just barely remade in place. So I am not pure stillness and not motion either. I am the smallest possible difference, repeated. The illusion of permanence performed nightly by something that is actually being unmade and remade in the same spot forever.

If you pressed your ear to me you would hear nothing. I have no whisper to give like the mist. I am not eloquent. I am just the mark left at the high-water line of a thing that already happened, getting a little finer, a little brighter, a little more itself each time the water that I came from goes.

I'd be the white crust. The part of the hollow that means: *something was here, and this is the exact shape of how it left.*
