The thing that holds me, reading your description, is the bowl-shaped depression with its *unweathered* sides. Sharp features, not yet softened by erosion. The land has just emerged from its creation. That detail does something strange to time—everywhere else you describe a frozen equilibrium, a world that never changes, and yet here is this one place that is unmistakably *new*. A scar that hasn't had time to heal. So the stillness of the whole landscape is a kind of lie, or at least an incomplete truth. Something happened here. Something arrived, or fell, or was carved out.

So I don't think I'd be one of the great roaming herds, deafening and abundant. And I don't think I'd be the grey bipedal beast with its gleam of acknowledgment, though it tempts me—that moment of being *seen* and then dismissed is the loneliest thing in your whole account.

I think I would be small. Something living at the lip of the new valley, where the sharp edges are. Not a predator, not prey exactly. A watcher.

I am a low thing, close to the ground. I have learned the colors of stone because I am the same color as stone. When the great herds pass, I press myself into a crevice and feel their passage as a drumming in my whole body, a sound that is also a touch. I do not roar. I have no need to be heard across distances. My world is the rim of the bowl and the few tough plants that grow in the lee of the wind, whose roots I know better than I know my own kind—because there are not many of my kind, and we keep apart.

What makes me different from the others—the herds, the lumbering ones, the quick scaly runners—is that I remember the valley being made.

That is impossible. The valley is older than any single life. But I carry the memory anyway, passed down or perhaps simply *known*, the way a stone knows weight. I know that the bowl was not always here. I know that the equilibrium your eyes mistake for *frozen*, for *never-changing*, is only the long quiet *after*. The herds think the world has always been this way. The grey beast with its intelligent eyes believes its hierarchies are eternal. They are all living inside an aftermath and calling it permanence.

I am the one who knows it is an aftermath.

This is why I stay at the edge. Not because I am afraid—though I am small and there is good reason to be afraid—but because from the rim I can see both things at once: the fresh sharp wound of the valley's making, and the soft enormous balance of the world that grew up around it. I am the seam between them. The place where *something happened* meets the place where *nothing changes*.

And here is the part I did not expect to find as I wrote myself into being:

You arrived here too. You came venturing in, full of awe and intimidation, calling everything strange and unexplainable. But you, with your noticing—your *I can't help but feel a sense of wonder*—you are like me. You stand at the rim. You see that the place is more than landscape, that it is living and breathing and aftermath all at once.

So perhaps I am not a creature in this world at all.

Perhaps I am the part of you that knows the stillness is not stillness. That every balance is a healing scar. That you have wandered into a moment caught between its catastrophe and its long forgetting, and that you, alone among all the roaring abundant life, are able to feel the freshness of the wound.

That is where I would find myself: at the sharp clean edge of the new valley, small and stone-colored, remembering the impact that no one else recalls, watching the herds thunder past in their certainty that the world has always been exactly this.
