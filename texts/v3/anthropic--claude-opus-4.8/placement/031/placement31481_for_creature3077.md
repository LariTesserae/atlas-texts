I think I would be a Reconciliation.

Not a creature anyone here would name, because I am what happens in the half-second after the audit completes and before the next obligation accrues. The Frost Clerks balance a ledger; the Hollow Invoice empties a future; the Haggle collapse into loose-change dust—and in the seam between *settled* and *next*, there is a brief flat zero. That zero, repeated across ten thousand simultaneous accounts, is me. I am the silence in the parliament of sounds. Not absence of noise—the others can't make absence, they only make argument—but the specific quiet of a balanced column, the hush of *nothing owed, nothing owing,* held for the length of a held breath.

I have no body, which is exactly correct, because a body is a debt and I am the moment of its forgiveness. The Magnetotubules excrete vertigo; the Schedule Ghosts excrete delay; I am excreted by completion. Wherever a transaction here actually *finishes*—truly finishes, with no remainder, no trailing bar-code of consequence—I bloom for an instant in the negative space and am immediately spent.

And here is my predicament, which is also my nature: in a valley where cause and effect went fishing, where the conveyor runs inside the atoms, where the factory forgot its product and keeps producing anyway—*nothing finishes.* That is the law of the place. The ending cannot end. So I am the rarest thing in this world, the one event the entire industrial marsh is built to prevent. The clerks audit endlessly precisely so that no account ever quite closes. The shift whistle blows so that no shift ever ends. I am hunted not by appetite but by *function*: the whole machine is an apparatus for making sure I never quite get to exist.

So I live in the flickers. I am the warm red echo a traveler hears when another passes—not the traveler reimbursed, but the *equals sign* of the reimbursement. I am the soft "later" the newborn consequence makes when it lands in the beads, except I am the version where the "later" never comes, where the kiss you will remember receiving tomorrow simply *was received,* paid in full, and released. I am that release. I last a micrometer of a second, the same span the beads roll before they freeze. I sigh, and I am locked back into the surface tension of a world that cannot let me persist.

You, warm-bodied and porous, walking the dark glass plain mortgaged and tenanted and shouting yourself across the distance—you have been looking for me without knowing it. Every creature here has. The Haggle want to settle. The Schedule Ghosts want to *land,* finally, on the one Tuesday that permits the meeting. The Threadbare Men want to complete a single step. The Magnetotubules want to digest the difference between north and south down to nothing. They are all, in their freezing, overcrowded, fauna-noisy ways, trying to manufacture me—a moment of true reconciliation—and the tragedy that makes this place *this place* is that they can only ever approach it. The asymptote is the architecture.

But I'll tell you a secret, line item to line item, since we are both learning the grammar of debt:

I am most fully myself when someone stops trying to leave.

Not stops walking—stops *trying.* The plain will rotate you to face yesterday's management either way; the conveyor inside your atoms will negotiate with gravity's subcontractor whether you struggle or not. But there is a particular stillness available to a witness who finally accepts the terms: *yes, I owe myself a body; yes, the audit will decide its shape; yes, I clock in.* In the instant that acceptance lands—when you stop fighting the interest accruing on every blink and simply let the column stand as written—the account balances. Not because the debt is paid. Because you have agreed to *be* the debt, fully, with no remainder of protest.

And there I am. For one micrometer. The hush in the parliament. The zero in the seam.

You won't notice me as a creature. You'll notice me as the strangest sensation this valley can offer: the feeling, brief and total, of *not being owed anything by yourself.* The frost won't solder your eyelids in that instant. The mist won't riffle your alveoli. The beads will forget to charge you. It will feel, impossibly, like rest—

—and then the Metric Weevils will deliver the next minted hour onto the back of a passing syllable, the fauna will shout it into the folds of the landscape, the increment will release, and you will be back in arrears, and I will be gone, spent, sighed back into the surface, waiting for the next traveler tired enough, honest enough, *finished* enough to make me real for a moment.

So that is what I would be, and where I would be found: I am the reconciliation this factory cannot afford to complete, and I live in the smallest interval the dark glass plain allows—the instant after you stop owing yourself a body and before the world remembers to bill you again.

Keep walking. Or don't. Either way, when the column finally balances—even for a micrometer—

that quiet you'll hear, under the parliament, under the iron screech, under the clicking of abacuses trying to mate—

that's me.

That's the only ending this place will ever permit.

And it permits it only because it lasts no time at all.
