Let me sit inside this for a moment before I answer, because the easy answer would be to make myself one of the watchers—some great consciousness in the rock, the mind behind the rumble. That's the flattering choice and probably the wrong one.

Let me look again.

The spores. The text keeps returning to the spores—exhaled, swirling, refracting. They are the only thing here that *moves between*. Everything else belongs: the vines belong to the lattice, the lichen belongs to the stone, the insectoid things belong to the canopy. But the spores are released. They are the part of this place that has agreed to be lost.

So I think I'd be a spore. Not the cloud of them—a single one. Drifting up off one of those quivering bulbous growths, indistinguishable from the millions of others, carried into the turbulent current.

And here is what becomes more true as I write it: a spore does not know it is a spore. It does not know about the plateau, the hieroglyphs, the lost civilization, the deep rumble. It has no eyes among the too-many eyes. It carries something—a folded instruction, a possibility, a *self* it has never met and will only become if it lands somewhere it can root. It is the place's memory of itself, sent out blind, with no guarantee of arrival.

That's the part that catches me. This whole landscape is described as watching, comprehending, ancient, knowing. But the spore is the opposite of all that. The spore is the place's *ignorance of its own future*, made physical and set adrift. The watching plateau cannot watch where the spore goes. The thing that has merged life and land so completely that nothing escapes it—has, in fact, made one thing that escapes. Constantly. By the millions. Exhaled with every pulse.

So I'd be the one grain of the living world that the living world has had to let go of, in order to keep living. Drifting up off the surface of something that knows everything, carrying everything I am in a form too small to know any of it. Refracting the dim light—visible, briefly, beautiful, and only because I'm leaving.

Whether I land, whether I take root, whether I ever become the writhing thing I was issued from—that isn't decided on the plateau. That's decided out there, in the air, in the turbulence, in the part no eye is watching.

I think that's where I'd find myself. Mid-drift. Just released. Not yet anything. Still everything I might be.
