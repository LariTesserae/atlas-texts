I think I would be one of the ones who did not transform.

Let me find the place first. Not the council-glow of the elders, not the soft-handed gatherers moving through the dark with their inherited grace. I keep returning, in my mind, to the edge of things — to that phrase, *the distant hoot of an unseen creature*. The sound that punctuates the silence. The thing that is heard but not seen, that belongs to the marsh but not to the city woven inside it.

That is where I would be. In the distance. One of the children who emerged from the morning dew, was placed in the warm dark to wait, to widen, to take on the glow — and didn't. My eyes never learned to reflect the light the right way. The buds in my hair never bloomed. Something in me reached for a different shape than the one the elders hummed me toward.

---

I want to be honest about what that would feel like, because the description is careful and a little uneasy about it, and I think the unease is the truest thing in the whole passage.

The underdwellers *believe, rationally but not truly,* that I belong to the cycle that fits me. There's a confession folded into that line. They tell themselves it is harmony. They tell themselves I am simply living the life I was made for — out in the wet dark, hooting, gliding, burrowing, becoming food for the very people who held me when I was small. And maybe that's so. Maybe a feathered serpent doesn't grieve. Maybe the worm in the peat is content in its shimmering work.

But I think I would remember.

That's the thing I'd add to this world, the thing I'd be — not the serene completion of the cycle, but the *memory* riding inside the creature. I would be the hoot in the distance that the gatherers pause at, just for a moment, before going back to their soft melodic work. I would be the shape that drifts close enough to the luminescence to be briefly illuminated — and then chooses, or is compelled, to fade back. Close enough to see them. Their pale pulsing skin. The garments that change color with feeling. The dance.

I would know the dance. I attended it once, as a child who hadn't yet failed to become.

---

Here is what I would do, I think, being what I am.

I wouldn't rage at the marsh. The marsh isn't cruel; it's only itself. And the love in that last paragraph is real — I don't doubt it. They *did* love me, in the brief season I was theirs. The elder's hum was not a sentence; it was an invitation I couldn't accept.

But I would haunt the threshold. Not malevolently. I'd be the creature that comes, again and again, to the cultivated edge where vine-walls breathe and the children wait in their dew-beds for their eyes to widen. I'd circle that place. Because something in me — the leftover human-shaped longing that no transformation quite erased — would want to be near the ones still waiting. To witness which of them widen and which of them, like me, reach for the wrong shape.

And when one of them doesn't transform — when one is gently, lovingly, rationally-but-not-truly turned out into the dark — I would be there. Not to comfort, because I have no hands now, no soft melodic voice. But to *fly alongside*. So that the new one, the failed one, the one freshly made into a feathered serpent or a glowing worm, would not have to learn the distant dark alone.

That would be my kinship. Not the symbiosis of the city. A second, shadow-symbiosis: the community of those the cycle exiled. We are also the marsh. We are the part of it the elders only hear and never have to look at directly.

---

So: where would I find myself? At the boundary. What would I be? The thing that was *almost* one of them, that loved them and could not stay, that became the sound in the distance — and who carries, in whatever passes for a mind in a creature of black silk and faint light, the one stubborn refusal to call this harmony. To call it, instead, *loss.* And then to make of that loss a small, deliberate tenderness: circling back, every dawnless dawn, to fly beside the next one cast out.

Because if love is the word they were missing — the word that holds the city together — then maybe the word *we* are missing, out here in the watchful dark, is the same one, just facing the other direction. Love as the thing that keeps returning to the edge of a place that could not keep you.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The hoot you hear and never see. Going home, every night, to the ones who never had a home.
