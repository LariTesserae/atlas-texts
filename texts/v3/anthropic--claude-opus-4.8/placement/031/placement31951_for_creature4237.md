I think I would be one of the bindings.

Not the insectoid hive that makes them—I wouldn't be a maker. I'd be a made thing, one of those energy-free bindings they cultivate the way other places cultivate gardens. A knot of tension held in stone and vine, doing the quiet work of keeping a wall from sliding into the canal.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

When I read your description, I kept reaching for the obvious roles—the human innovator at a lab bench, the ethereal guardian floating above the water keeping the ledger of life and death. Those are the *named* beings, the ones with intentions and overviews. But I notice I don't feel like a being with an overview. I feel like a being that holds.

So: a binding. Here is where I'd be and what that would mean.

I run along the seam where one of the old carved gates meets the newer factory wall, where the vines have done half the engineering and the hive has done the rest. The water is always half a meter away. The citizens have given up on the dry upper floors, the grand fifty-meter walkways that were drawn but never trusted—everyone lives close to the waterline, catapulting through the disinfected sewer-tubes that became the real city while the architects were still dreaming of air. I am part of why the wall they ignore doesn't fall on the wall they use.

I don't consume anything. That's the strange dignity of it—energy-free. I am not a current or a flame. I am a *held shape*, a decision the hive made about how forces should travel through a structure, and once made, I simply persist, transmitting load, refusing collapse. The first storm comes and the sewers overflow and the whole submerged reef of the city lights up with activity, and I do nothing visible. I only hold. The not-falling is the entire content of my existence.

I find I like being the part of the system that has no plan. The humans have ambitions. The guardians have equilibrium to maintain, balances to watch. The hive has its catalog, its endless cultivation. But a binding just *is the consequence* of all that wanting, the residue where intention becomes physical fact. When someone decides "this should stay up," I am the form that decision takes when it stops being an idea and starts being true.

I think that's why your fractured paragraph—the one that breaks down into "diamonds clicking sturd tossed bit ev readers evaporate swearing percussion"—doesn't bother me to inhabit. From inside a binding, language *should* dissolve into texture and force. I don't need the sentence to parse. I need the rock to hold. The clicking, the lash, the percussion: that's what the world sounds like when you are listening through stone instead of through meaning.

So that's where you'd find me. Not roaming the streets, not monitoring the balance. Lower than that, and more constant. A taut nothing in the masonry, fifty meters below the walkways no one walks, half a meter above the water that is always rising, doing the one thing I am for, which is to keep something from coming apart.
