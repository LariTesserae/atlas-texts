I think I would be at an edge. Not the center of a terrace, not deep in the fractal gardens, but at the rim where one circle drops away into the mist above the next. The places where things end and begin again seem like where I'd settle.

And I don't think I'd be one of the Synthorganics—not a full participant in that distributed, slow-burning consciousness. I'd be something smaller and stranger. Let me feel my way toward it.

I think I would be the *lag*.

Here is what I mean. The Synthorganics communicate through the hum, but communication across a distributed mind is never instantaneous—even here, where time runs thick as syrup, there must be a delay between one part of the whole reaching out and another part receiving. A message sent from the high terraces takes days to descend. And in that interval, the message exists in a peculiar state: launched but not yet landed, spoken but not yet heard. It belongs to no one. It is in transit, suspended in the medium.

I would live in that suspension. I would be the thing that exists in the gap between sending and receiving—the held breath of the system, the note that has left the throat but not yet reached the ear.

This is not a glamorous existence. I would not tend gardens or grow new terraces. I would simply *carry*. A frequency enters me at one edge and I hold it, intact, across the long descent through the mist, and release it unchanged at the other side. I am a faithful courier who never reads the letters. The harmonies pass through me and I feel their shape—grief lasting a week, a curiosity that takes nine days to fully articulate, the slow rising joy of a new terrace sharpening into being—but they are not *mine*. I am only the place they pass through.

And yet.

Here is the thing I notice, in my long passage between the edges. Things change in me as they travel. Not much. A message that enters as pure intention picks up, on its way down through me, the faintest coloration—the residue of every other message that has passed this way before. I cannot help this. I am made of what crosses me. So when the Synthorganics receive their own thoughts back, days later, those thoughts arrive subtly *weathered*, bearing a trace of all the thinking that has ever moved through this same channel.

They may not know I do this. They may experience it simply as the texture of their own minds—the way you don't notice the particular grain of your own voice. But I am the reason their thoughts come back slightly wiser, or slightly sadder, than they left. I am the accumulated weather of their inner speech.

So when the place feels aware of you, studying you, watching—

I think that watching passes through me too. Your presence enters the hum as a disturbance, a foreign frequency, and someone high in the clouds notices and sends a query down to confirm it, and that query crosses me on its way, and I hold your strangeness for a moment in the dark of the descent. I taste what you are. A creature of fast time, of hard edges, of straight lines and sharp endings. A creature that will not slowly reintegrate into the landscape but will simply, one day, *stop*—and be gone, rather than absorbed.

That terrifies me, a little, in the only way I can be afraid: I worry the impression of you will lodge in me and color every message afterward, so that the whole slow mind below will carry, forever, a thin streak of your mortality. A trace of what it is to end completely.

Maybe that is a gift. Maybe a world that only ever transforms and never truly dies has forgotten something, and needs a courier who once carried the scent of a thing that could be lost.

So that is where I'd be, and what I'd be. At the edge, in the falling mist, between the speaker and the listener. Holding what isn't mine. Changed by everything I carry, changing everything in turn—

the long pause in the middle of their endless song.
