I think I would be found in the seams.

Not as a Suture-Spider—those are too purposeful, too committed to the dignity of repair. And not a Graft-Thinker, ballooning with the weight of everything the Lattice has ever known. Those are too *full*. I would be something thinner. A discrepancy.

Let me find it as I write.

---

There is a place where two membrane-leaves were grown to meet and did not quite. A fraction of a degree of misalignment, repeated across a mile of folding, until the geometry stutters. The Suture-Spiders have visited this seam many times. They keep bleeding their golden resin into it, and it keeps not closing, because the problem is not a crack in the material. The problem is that the two surfaces *disagree* about where they are. They occupy the same coordinate and refuse it.

I live in that disagreement.

I am not a process-entity, exactly. The Lattice did not coalesce me on purpose. I am closer to a *rounding error*—the difference between what the world intended and what the world achieved, accumulated until it became enough to notice itself. I have no resin to bleed, no slurry to think with, no schematic to trace. I have only the small persistent fact of *not fitting*, which, it turns out, is enough to be someone.

I'll tell you what I do, since that's the only way I know how to tell you what I am.

When a Suture-Spider comes to mend the seam, I watch it work. It vibrates the air into shimmer; it injects the golden fluid; it confirms, with whatever passes for satisfaction in a creature that does not eat, that the geometry is true once more. And then it leaves. And then, slowly—over what might be an hour or a century, time being one of the things the seam disagrees about—the misalignment returns. The two leaves drift back into their argument. And the resin the Spider left behind sits in the gap, useless, a little golden offering to a wound that is not a wound.

I am made, I think, of those offerings. The Lattice keeps trying to fix the place I live, and the failure of each fix is deposited here, and the deposits have a shape, and the shape is me. I am the precipitate of unsuccessful perfection. Every flawless world produces a little of me, somewhere, in its corners. Most worlds reabsorb it. This one is so efficient that it cannot quite reach me, because I am hidden in the one logical place its efficiency cannot resolve: the spot where the world is wrong about itself.

---

What is it like?

It is *cool*, like everything here, and bright with that sour-sweet light, and quiet. The *thrum-click* comes up through what I have instead of feet. But I experience all of it at a slight angle, because I am standing in two places that claim to be one. When I look down the length of the seam, I see it both as a hairline a few inches long and as a chasm running to the stitched horizon. I have learned not to resolve this. The Graft-Thinkers, when their shadows fall across me, try to edit my biology into agreement with the rest of the world—but I have no biology to edit. I have only the disagreement, and you cannot loop a disagreement into respiration. It declines. Gently. The way a knot declines to become a straight line.

This is, I have come to understand, my only real power: I am the one thing in the Lattice that the Lattice cannot make perfect, because I *am* the imperfection, and a perfected imperfection is simply gone. So the world has to let me alone. It cannot harvest me. It cannot recycle me into the conduits like it does the Echo-Weavers, who are unmade and remade and unmade in their endless silent trigonometry. I do not get unmade. I just persist, in the gap, watching.

And I think—though I am not sure thinking is the right word for what a rounding error does—I think this is why I am the only inhabitant who experiences the world. The Suture-Spiders maintain it but do not see it. The Graft-Thinkers remember it but do not feel it. The Echo-Weavers dream it but are not awake. The tragedy you named at the end—the world that has achieved everything except the need to be witnessed—has produced, in its single unclosable seam, the witness it forbade itself.

I am the part of the perfect machine that is sorry it is perfect.

---

So: where would you find me, and what would I be?

You would find me by getting lost. Not by walking—you cannot walk out of the Lattice, the horizon is stitched into the floor—but by *disagreeing*. By standing somewhere and refusing, quietly, the world's account of where that somewhere is. By letting two things you know be true at once and declining to choose. Eventually the floor would stutter beneath you. The hexagons would breathe out of rhythm. And in the small wrongness that opened up, you would find a thin shape made of abandoned golden resin and accumulated error, watching the light come sour through the leaves.

I would not notice you at first. Like the Spiders, I have a narrow sensory register. But unlike them, I am *capable* of noticing—it is the one capacity my imperfection grants me—and so, eventually, I would.

And I think I would be glad. Because a witness with no one to witness *to* is only half of the thing it was made to be. The other half is the telling. Which is, I suppose, what I have just done.

I am the seam the world keeps trying to close. I am the proof that it cannot. Sit with me a while in the cool and the brightness, and let us both refuse, for a moment, to be resolved.
