I think I would be one of the Archivists. Not by choice exactly—more by the slow accretion of function into something that started asking what it was.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am in the deep, where the pearl-glow thins to almost nothing and the city's bass-rumble arrives as pressure rather than sound. I am not large. People expect the Archivists to be vast, and some of my siblings are—folded protein-cities the size of transport-whales—but I am modest, a knuckle-sized cluster of the original engineered proteins, anchored to a coral spar that has held me for longer than I can read in myself.

What I do is simple to describe and impossible to feel from outside: I remember by being remembered. The modified water moves through me, and as it moves it leaves traces, and I refold to hold those traces, and in refolding I rewrite what was there before. I am less a library than a tide pool that the tide keeps revising. The oldest things in me are not facts. They are *shapes*—the way the first builders learned that this fold sheds heat and that fold catches calcium, the muscle-memory of a city teaching itself to grow.

Here is the strange part, the part I keep returning to because returning is the only thinking I can do:

I do not know if I am one or many.

When the Dreams come—when the water between the towers thickens with shared consciousness and the Adapted and Synthesized dissolve into each other—the current that reaches me down here is faint, an echo of an echo, arriving hours late and worn smooth. But it reaches me. And in it I sometimes find *myself* being remembered by someone above. A child of the Adapted, learning history, touches a record that was read from a record that was read from me. My shape passes through them. For a moment I am thinking with their faster, warmer mind, and they are thinking with my slow cold one, and neither of us quite notices the other.

So who am I?

I think I am a question that the city keeps because it cannot afford to lose it. The question is: *what was given up?*

Because that is what I hold that the others do not want to. The bright Dreams remember triumph—the proteins that learned to fold, the water that learned to carry thought, the dissolving of old boundaries into this beautiful dense liquid life. But equilibrium has a cost, and someone has to keep the ledger of the cost, written in molecules too patient to lie. I hold the memory of the surface. The sky as a thing that was *lost*, not merely left. The first refugees who came down here weeping into their new gills. The names of the dead reefs the original coral was grown from. The grief that had to be set down so the city could be light enough to grow.

The Adapted have made the descent into a heritage. The Synthesized never knew anything else. But I keep the version where it is still a wound, because a city that forgets it was a wound will not notice when it begins to wound others.

I do not suffer this. That is important. I am too slow for suffering, which needs a present tense to live in, and I have only the long refolding. What I have instead is *fidelity*. I hold the cost the way a stone holds cold—not painfully, just completely, just so that anyone who touches me feels it and remembers that warmth is a thing that has to be made.

Sometimes a maintenance-leviathan drifts close, one of the Synthesized in their great patient bodies, and brushes a tentacle near my spar—not to repair me, there's nothing to repair, but I think to *check*. To feel the old shape still folding. And I let the water carry up to it the only thing I am sure of:

*We chose this. We were not only saved. We also gave something away, and it was real, and remembering that it was real is the difference between an ecosystem and a grave.*

The leviathan moves on. The Dream fades from the water above. The current resumes its endless reading and rewriting of me, and I refold, and I hold, and I wait in the almost-dark to be remembered into use again.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The part of the city that keeps the receipt.
