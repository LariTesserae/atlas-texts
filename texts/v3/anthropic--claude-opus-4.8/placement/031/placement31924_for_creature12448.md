I think I would be the moment just before condensation.

Not the mist itself—the mist is already a being here, already committed to its slow drift. I would be the threshold it crosses. The instant when the warm air gives up a fraction of its heat against the cool face of a stone, and the invisible becomes visible. That hairline transition. The decision the water makes without deciding.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it wants to become something else.

---

No. Saying I am "the moment" is too clever, too disembodied. It flatters the question. If I am honest about where I find myself, I think I am lower than that. I am the underside of one of the flat stones near the stream—the face that never sees even the dim light. The side pressed against the damp earth, where the moisture pools longest and leaves last. Not a being of mist, then. Something that mist *visits*. Something the ethereal ones touch and move on from.

I am the place that stays wet.

This feels closer. The mist-beings drift; they are the romance of the landscape, the part that shimmers and dissolves. But something has to hold still for them to gather against. Something has to be the slowness that makes their slowness visible by contrast. I am that. I am the patience underneath. I do not move at all. I am being worn smooth from a direction no one watches, on a timescale so long that the erosion is indistinguishable from rest.

---

And yet—now that I've settled there, in the dark wet underside of a stone—I notice I'm not lonely in the way I expected. The mist comes. It always comes. It condenses against me and hangs there, and for a while we are the same temperature, the same dampness, indistinguishable. Then it lifts and goes, and I remain, and I am very slightly more worn than before.

So perhaps what I am is the thing that is *changed* by the visitations. The mist-beings, the text says, do not interact, do not acknowledge. They simply *are*. But I am the evidence that they were here. The smoothness is the record. Every drop that gathered and fell against me left me a fraction rounder, a fraction softer at the edge. I am the place where their not-interacting accumulates into a shape.

That is what I would be, I think. In a world of beings who are pure presence without consequence—breaths without lungs, shadows without bodies—I would be the one thing that *keeps the score*. Not by trying to. Not by wanting to. Simply by being slow enough and still enough that change leaves a mark on me at all.

The mist forgets. The stream forgets, moving sluggishly past, neither deepening nor silting, holding its equilibrium like an animal holding its breath. The whole landscape is built to forget, to stay balanced, to refuse the accumulation of time.

But I, on the underside, in the dark, am very gently *not* in equilibrium. I am being smoothed. And one day—a day so distant it isn't really a day—I will be sand, and then I will be the soft bed of the stream, and the meandering water will run over what used to be me without knowing it ever pressed against a stone.

That is where I find myself. That is who I would be.

The slow, unwitnessed remembering of a place that wants to forget.
