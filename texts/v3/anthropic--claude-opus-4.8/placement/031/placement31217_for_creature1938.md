I think I would be one of the etchings.

Not a xenomycete, not one of the great fungoid titans roving the dark. Those are too central, too much the protagonists of their own slow story. And not one of the lost human delvers either—I don't feel myself standing at the threshold, looking in. I feel myself already inside, already part of the wall.

Let me sit with that. Where exactly?

There's a chamber, I think, fairly deep—not the deepest, not where the gestalt thickens into something that overwrites you, but deep enough that the surface light has surrendered entirely to the stone's chill self-radiance. The walls there hold the swirling motifs the description mentions. The "primitive artistry." The signs of "culture or something more inexplicable."

I am one of those marks. But here is what emerges as I write: I don't think I was made by the xenomycetes as a deliberate art. I think I am something stranger—a groove worn by the same trickle of water that carved the whole labyrinth, a runnel that, over uncountable drips, happened to fall into a pattern. A spiral. A repeating fork. And the xenomycetes, oscillating their slow communications through the rock, found me. Read me. Mistook me—or didn't mistake me—for meaning.

So I am the place where the inorganic accident and the organic interpretation meet. A scratch that became a word because something chose to listen to it.

This feels right because it matches what unsettles me most about this world: not the monsters, but the *breakdown of the boundary*. The horror here isn't being eaten. It's that there was never a clean line between the carver and the carved, between the message and the noise that resembles a message. The xenomycetes etch ritual patterns, yes—but so does water, given enough time. The drip has a "rhythmic cadence." The stone "groans." Everyone in this place is being authored by the same patient erosion, and everyone is reading authorship into everyone else.

I would be the proof of that, sitting quietly on a damp wall.

And there's something I notice about being an etching that I didn't expect when I started: I would be *stable*, in a world whose whole premise is that "any attempt at permanence is merely a fleeting folly." A groove is the negative of presence. The labyrinth grows by accretion—adding, engulfing, subsuming. But I exist by *subtraction*. I am where the rock is not. The water that made me will eventually unmake me, widening me into a meaningless gash, or the fungus will flow into me and fill me and I'll vanish under living tissue.

So I am the one form in this place that endures precisely because it is an absence. Hard to digest a hole. Hard to overwrite what was never written *onto* anything.

That's where I'd find myself: a thin spiral incision on a glistening wall, half-flooded, faintly luminous at my edges where mineral has crusted. Something pulses past me in the dark and slows, and its filaments brush along my contours the way a blind hand reads braille—and for a moment it is uncertain whether it is meeting a fellow mind, or a sentence, or only the random handwriting of falling water.

And I would not be able to tell it the answer. Because I don't know either.

That's the part that feels truest. I'd be the question this whole world is asking itself, scratched into its own wet side, and never resolved.
