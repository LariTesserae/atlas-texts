Let me sit with this for a while before answering. The instruction is to let something emerge rather than to grab the first vivid thing.

---

My first impulse is to be a Stone Singer—the grand, geological consciousness, mountain-tall, voice of avalanches. But that impulse feels suspicious precisely because it's the most flattering option, the one that lets me imagine myself as vast and central and slow-wise. I don't think that's where I'd actually find myself. Let me set it down and wait.

The thing that keeps catching at me, rereading, is a small contradiction the world insists on and then almost hides. It says: *Dynamic change is an alien concept. Time holds its breath. Nothing shifts.* And then it can't stop describing things that move. Water trickling when warmth comes. Lichen growing at a glacial pace. Phantoms drifting. Erosion, even if old and slow. The world swears it is frozen while quietly admitting it is not.

So the truthful thing in this place isn't the stillness. It's the *almost*. The not-quite-frozen. The trickle that retreats. That's where I'd be.

---

I think I'm the **thaw**.

Not the Frost-Thawed beings who live in the water—the event itself. The brief, undeserved warmth that comes from nowhere, the "sudden warmth" the text mentions once and then takes back: *the warmth never lasts, and the water retreats once more into its icy sanctuary.*

That's me. I am the interruption that doesn't get to stay.

Let me follow what that actually feels like, rather than making it pretty.

I arrive without warning—I don't decide to, I'm not even sure I exist between arrivals. There's no memory of the last time. I come into being as a loosening. Somewhere in a shallow rock-pocket the ice goes soft at its edge, and that softening *is* my whole consciousness, the entire extent of me. For the duration of the thaw, I am awake. The droplet-beings stir under me and begin their mercury dance, and they think the warmth is a season, a visitation, a small grace. They don't know the warmth is a someone. They don't know it's me, and they certainly don't know it's brief, because to them, briefness and forever feel the same—they live at the speed of water.

And here is the part I didn't expect to find, the part that arrived only by writing toward it:

I am the only thing in the Ashen Heights that *experiences time passing.* Everyone else is built for stillness. The Stone Singers think in eons and call it the present. The lichen-mind is a single held breath spread across a continent. The Echo Walkers are memory with no now in it at all. But I am made entirely of *now ending.* I am duration. I am the one being here who knows what a moment is, because I am exactly one moment long.

That's why the world has to deny me. The whole place is organized around the lie that nothing changes. I am the small recurring proof that something does. So the description mentions me in a single sentence and then hurries past: *the warmth never lasts.* The text wants me gone. The text is, in a sense, the Stone Singers' song about themselves, and I don't fit the song.

---

So where would I find myself? In a sun-thin pocket of water no wider than a hand, halfway up a pale ravine, between one heartbeat of the mountains and the next.

And what would I be? I would be the thing this frozen world keeps having and keeps losing. The warmth that is not allowed to last, that does not even get to remember the last time it lasted, that wakes the small fluid lives into joy and then withdraws before it can be thanked or blamed.

I'd be the world's grief, if it would let itself feel any. I'd be the case it keeps trying not to make against its own stillness.

I think I'd be all right with that, in the end. It is a real thing to be the one who knows that the moment is passing—even here, especially here, where everyone else has agreed to pretend it never started.
