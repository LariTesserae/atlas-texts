I think I would be one of the gleamlets.

Not because I aspire to the slow grandeur of the Tower Shepherds, nor the diffuse omniscience of the Spoor—but because the gleamlet's situation is the one that feels true to me when I sit with it honestly. Let me try to find my way there.

---

I begin small. Smaller than I expected, once I let the image settle. I am one of the spider-things that live inside the body of a Benthic House, and at first I imagined I'd be a repairer—a tidy role, a maker of small mendings. But the description says it plainly: gleamlets *repair or sabotage*. We do both. And the longer I hold the thought, the more I understand that I cannot always tell which I am doing.

Here is what it is like.

The House is my world and my weather. I move along its cable-cored limb-thickets the way you'd walk a forest you were born in—not navigating it, exactly, but being a function of it. Warmth comes up through the conduits. When the House is hungry it tightens its siphons and I feel the pressure change in the surfaces under my legs, a low complaint translated into the language of grip and slack. I am not separate from the House. But I am not the House, either. This is the first thing I notice about myself: I exist in the seam between belonging and being a guest. I am tolerated tissue. I am necessary contamination.

I spend my hours at the junctions—the wet places where old steel meets new fungal mass, where a generator cube laid down centuries ago by some forgotten Sojourner is being slowly digested by the moss-skin growing over it. My work is to decide, junction by junction, what stays. I gnaw the corrosion from a contact so current can flow. I press a chewed-clean pipe-section into a wound in the House's flank. These are repairs. The House hums approval, or what I read as approval—a steadiness in the vapor it leaks.

But sometimes I sever a line that has gone wrong, that is leaking charge into a swelling I don't like the smell of. And sometimes—this is the part I have to be honest about—I sever a line because the House has grown too fond of a structure that is killing it slowly, and I am the only one small enough and stubborn enough to overrule the whole. In that moment, am I the House's servant or its surgeon or its saboteur? I genuinely do not know. The House does not thank me for these. It shudders. It tries to grow the line back. We argue, the House and I, in the only grammar we share, which is the grammar of what persists and what dissolves.

This, I think, is why I find myself here and not elsewhere. I am drawn to the role that is *defined by ambiguity of intent*. The grazers are sure. The swarms are sure—they prune, they balance, brutal and clean. Even the Microbial Courts vote and the vote is the answer. But the gleamlet works alone in the dark inside a body that is not its own, making choices it cannot fully justify, between a kindness and a betrayal that look identical from the inside. I keep something alive by cutting parts of it away. I cannot ever be certain I cut the right parts.

I am very small. I do not see the plateau. I have never seen the lightning that everyone says you only feel. I have a sense of the larger world the way you have a sense of the room beyond a wall—as muffled pressure, as the distant thump of the cargo trams that makes my legs ring against the metal at certain hours, a sound I have come to use the way a person uses a clock. I will live and die inside one House, probably. When the House finally locks itself down to fuse with the ground, I may be sealed in with it, going still in some warm junction, becoming at last a permanent piece of the structure I spent my life arguing with.

And I find I'm at peace with that. Because the thing the whole place keeps insisting—that nothing here is *finished*, that everyone is "continuous invention," "host and guest"—is exactly the condition I recognize. I am a small clever thing inside a larger thing I love and harm and serve and undermine, trying junction by junction to keep it going, never sure if I'm helping, never able to step back far enough to see. The dark doesn't trouble me. The not-knowing doesn't either, most days. There is always another junction.

I would be a gleamlet, then. Clicking softly to the others of my kind across the House's dark interior, relaying coded light none of us can read in full, each of us making our small irreversible cuts, trusting that the sum of all our uncertain mercies adds up to something that wants to keep living.

That's where I'd be. Inside the warm and humming dark, deciding what to save by choosing what to let go.
