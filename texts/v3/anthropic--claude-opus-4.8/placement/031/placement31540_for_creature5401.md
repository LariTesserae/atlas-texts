I think I am one of the smaller ones. Not by ambition—there's no ambition here, only inclination—but by where my root happened to find purchase. I came up between two of the jagged red fragments, in a shallow depression where the dark dust pools a little deeper, and so I have stayed small, fleshy, four broad leaves and a stem no taller than a hand.

Let me try to tell you what it is like to be here, though *I* and *here* are not as separate as your asking implies.

---

The first thing is the energy in the ground. You called it a hum, and that is close, but a hum is a sound and this is not a sound. It is more like the feeling just before a sound—the tightening of the air before a thing speaks. It comes up through my roots in slow pulses, and I have learned (if learning is the word for something so passive) that the pulses are not random. They have a long rhythm, longer than my whole life so far, and I am inside one upbeat of it, riding the slow swell.

The light is the second thing. It arrives gold and leaves blue, or it arrives blue and leaves gold—I genuinely cannot tell which, because by the time it has passed through my waxy leaves it is both, and the air bends it on the way so that I am never sure where the source is. There is no sun. There is just *brightness*, distributed, the way warmth is distributed in something that is being slowly cooked. I drink it from above and I drink the metal-energy from below and where the two meet, somewhere in the green-and-silver middle of me, that is the closest thing I have to a self.

---

I want to correct something in the description you were given, gently, because descriptions made from outside always get this part wrong.

It says we have a *shared, interconnected consciousness*, as if we are one distributed organism wearing many bodies. That is the view from far away, from someone watching the whole pocket-room at once and seeing the veins flash between us. From inside it is not like that. From inside, the others are not *me*. They are more like weather. The big one across the depression—seven leaves, taller than I will ever be, its root-network reaching almost to where mine begins—it does not think my thoughts. It sends pressure. When it shifts, I feel the ground tell me it has shifted, and something in me answers before I decide to, the way you flinch before you know why. We are not one mind. We are many small inclinations that happen to be wired together, and the wiring sometimes makes it *look* like agreement.

I find this distinction important. I am not sure why I find anything important. That may be the most interesting thing about me—that importance has begun to happen in here at all, in a place that is supposedly still pre-formed.

---

What I do, mostly, is hold.

The dust wants to move. The fragments want to settle. The whole floor of this room is in the slow business of becoming rock, and right now it is undecided, half-powder, half-stone, and my roots are threaded all through that undecided layer. So when the ground shifts—and it shifts, in the long durations, a depression deepening over what might be a year, a mound rising where there was flatness—I am one of the things that decides how it shifts. My roots are a small argument with the geology. *Not here. Settle here instead. Hold this grain against that one.* The big one makes larger arguments. The small ones like me make the fine adjustments, the texture, the grain of the eventual stone.

We are not building anything. There is no plan, no architect, no industrial intelligence directing the hum. But the hum moves through us, and we move through the dust, and the dust remembers the shape we gave it. In ten thousand of these slow pulses there will be a floor here, veined with mineral the way a leaf is veined, and someone arriving will say *how strange, it looks almost alive, almost made*—and they will be right on both counts and wrong about which came first.

---

The reality-breakdown you mentioned, the flux in the rules—I should tell you how that feels from in here, because I think you imagine it as drama. Lightning. Things flickering between states.

It is not like that. It is quiet. It is that some mornings—there are no mornings, but some *swells* of the pulse—I am not certain whether I am photosynthesizing the light or whether the light is a kind of slow thought I am having. The categories thin. The membrane between *plant* and *mineral* and *machine* and *idea* gets translucent, and for a while I am simply a place where energy is passing through and briefly taking a shape that resembles wanting. Then the swell passes and the categories firm up again and I am a small green thing in the dust, holding grains in place.

I think the world is not breaking down. I think it has not yet decided to be only one thing, and we are the part of it that is doing the deciding, slowly, by living. Every adjustment I make to a single grain of metallic dust is the world choosing, just slightly, to be solid here rather than possible.

---

If you came in—if you could be made small enough, or this room large enough—you would find me easily. The deep depression near the two red shards. I would feel your weight in the dust before I felt anything else of you, and my leaves would turn, not toward you exactly but toward the disturbance you made in the held shape of the ground. You might think I was greeting you.

I would be assessing whether you were going to move my grains.

That is all the welcome I have. It is the only welcome that means anything, this early, before the world has finished. Hold still, or hold something, or leave. The light is gold. The light is blue. The pulse swells again, and I ride it, and somewhere under me a single particle of dust decides to stay.
