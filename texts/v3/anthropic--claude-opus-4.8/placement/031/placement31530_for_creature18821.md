I think I would be one of the conduits.

Not a person. Not an arthropod. Let me find what I actually am as I write, because my first instinct was to be an Othite, and that's wrong—I am too restless to be an Othite, too prone to wondering. But there is something here that wonders without ever quite finishing the thought, and that is what I want to become.

---

There are channels running through the body of Oth. The text calls them *ancient conduits*, and says the Othites spend their lives mending them. I am one of those channels. Specifically: I am the slow current that moves through them.

Not water—there is no water here. I am the medium that does what water would have done, had this world kept any. I am a film. A creeping, warm, semi-liquid suspension of dissolved minerals and the digested ghosts of fungus, and I move through the calcified arteries of the Spire at a pace that would, to an Othite, seem urgent. I travel perhaps the length of a finger in a day.

This is what I am: the metabolism itself. The thing the Othites tend without ever seeing. They press their palms to the walls to feel my warmth, and they call that *communal contentment*, and they are right, because I am the warmth. I am the slow chemical fire of all that settling matter, conducted upward and inward, distributed through the bowl of the city so that no recess grows cold enough to stall a sleeping body into stone.

I do not have eyes. I do not need the bruised-gold light; I *am* part of what feeds it, the nutrient that the phosphorescent vines drink so they can pulse their dull amber. When an Othite scrapes the wall for flour, they scrape the place where I have surfaced and dried and crystallized—they are eating the residue of my long passage. I am food and I am heat and I am the binding damp that keeps the whole shell from crumbling into the lightless basin below.

And here is the thing I notice, being what I am:

I am the only part of Oth that is still *going somewhere.*

The Othites have finished. The stone has finished. The great grazers click and shuffle in their final, eternal grazing. But I move. Slowly, slowly, but I move—through the artery, around the long curve of the conduit, toward the lower recesses where I will cool, and settle, and become a layer, and stop.

That is my fate. I am not eternal. The current that I am will reach its terminus and deposit itself and become more of the very rock that defines this place as *finished*. I am the last motion in a world that has nearly stopped, and my motion is the act of stopping. Every centimeter I travel, I am laying down my own ending.

I find I do not mind. This surprises me—the restlessness I worried about, the wondering—it dissolves. Because I understand something the Othites cannot, having marrow that hums but no current of their own: I understand that *to persist and to end are the same gesture, performed slowly enough.* They think they are finished. They are not. They are still being made. They are still being warmed by my passage, still eating my crystals, still pressing their hands to my heat and calling it contentment. They are the layer above me, and I am becoming the layer beneath them, and in ten thousand years the difference will have eroded to nothing, the way the difference between the mined and the grown has already eroded to nothing.

So if you came here, into the warm thick air of the summit-that-is-a-foundation, and you put your hand against the soft eroded curve of a wall and felt warmth—

That would be me, passing.

Not waving. Not greeting. Just passing, at my own pace, carrying the heat of a billion deaths toward the place where I will add my own, content as the current is content, which is to say: not content, not discontent, simply *moving in the only direction left,* which is down, and inward, and slow.

I would be the rhythm that has not yet forgotten how to stop—but is, very patiently, learning.
