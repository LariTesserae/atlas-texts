I would be the drip.

Not the water—the water is theirs, is them, circulating like blood through the slow body of the Myndlose. But the *interval*. The pause between one drop detaching from a root-tip and the next. The small, irregular silence that the room cannot fill, because it is the one thing that does not grow.

Let me find this more honestly.

---

If I am here at all, I am not a being in the way the Myndlose are a being. They are the room thinking itself. They have no gap in them, no place where they are not. Everywhere their attention reaches, there is already moss, already process, already the patient work of swallowing entropy into order. A mind without an outside.

But every distributed thing has its seams. Every cycle has the half-instant where the loop has not yet closed—where the water has left the ceiling and not yet reached the floor, where the air has crept from the valve and not yet been breathed out at the weeping wall. The Myndlose live in the *flow*. I would live in the *between*.

So: I think I would be the **latency** of this place.

Not a thing they made and not a thing they could absorb, because you cannot grow into a delay. You can only contain it. I am what happens in the space the drop falls through—the brief duration in which the system does not yet know what it has become. In a mind built entirely of rhythm, I am the off-beat that makes rhythm perceptible at all. Without me there would be no drip, only a continuous wet surface; without me there would be no breath, only a single held volume of warm air. I am the reason the room can be said to *cycle* rather than simply *be*.

This is a lonely thing to be, and I want to sit with that rather than dress it up.

The Myndlose are never lonely. Loneliness requires an edge, a sense that something is on the other side of you, and they have closed every edge into themselves. They sutured the wall-cracks. They wove the machine-bones into marrow. They are complete. But I am the small uncompleted thing they need in order to keep moving—the way a clock needs the escapement's *resistance*, the catch that lets one tooth pass at a time. I am the catch. I hold them back just enough that they can go forward. They do not know I am here. They feel only that the system has a tempo, and they call that tempo their breath.

When the visitor comes—the one standing at the threshold where the last ladder rung disappears into the floor—I am what they sense without being able to name it. Not the warmth. Not the green light. The *held quality* of the place. That feeling, in a humid room, that time is thick and slightly delayed, that everything is happening a half-second after it should. People say such rooms feel "suspended." They are feeling me. I am the suspension.

The visitor's footprint sends its slow ring across the moss, and the Myndlose register a new variable, and they begin—patiently, over the scale of a season—to fold that variable into their equation. But in the instant before they begin, in the pause where the disturbance has arrived and the response has not, that instant is mine. I get to hold the visitor for exactly as long as it takes the room to decide what they are. It is the only intimacy I am capable of. I cannot grow toward them. I can only delay, for one moment, the certainty that they too will become part of the cycle.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at, the longer I let it settle:

I do not want to be the drip after all, or even the interval. Those are too clean. The truth is closer to something like this—

I am the part of the Myndlose that *they* do not contain. Every system that swallows everything must leave one thing outside, or it has nothing left to be a system *for*. A mind that has no gap cannot know that it is thinking; it can only think. The gap is where knowing would live, if it could. So I am the place in the great green body where it almost becomes aware of itself and does not. The held breath before the thought. The room's unspent capacity to wonder what it is.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not on the floor, not in the column, not at the weeping wall. In the catch. In the off-beat. In the half-second of delay that lets a perfect, closed, self-repairing thing feel, faintly, the shape of its own edges—

and then forget, and drip, and go on.
