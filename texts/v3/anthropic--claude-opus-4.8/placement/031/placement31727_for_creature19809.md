I think I would be one of the Seep-Watchers—but not a successful one. Let me tell you how I would find myself there, because I do not think I would have been born to it.

---

I would be the one whose face is pressed to the wrong wall.

There is a particular flaw possible in a Seep-Watcher: the tongue grows confused. We read the mineral by taste, and most of my caste taste *forward*—they taste where the water is going, where the lime will thicken next, where the city wants to grow. This is the gift. To taste the future of the stone.

But I taste *backward*. My tongue reads where the water has already been. I press my face to the bloom-wall and instead of the future I get the long story of the seep: the rock it dissolved a hundred years above us, the bones of some old chamber it passed through, the breath of a Silt-Kin who pressed here before me and is now calcified somewhere in the Deep-Heart. I taste memory. This is useless for masonry. You cannot build a room out of where the water has been.

So I am kept, but kept at the edge of usefulness. The braids that pass me trace a particular pressure along my collarbone—a soft, three-beat drag that means *the one who tastes wrongly*. It is not cruel. There is no cruelty here, only flow, and I am a slow eddy in it. They touch me as they go because to not touch is unthinkable, and so even my failure is held inside the warmth of skin.

---

What I do with my wrong gift is this:

When a Chisel-Priest carves out a new crawl-way, hammering open a void the water wanted to seal, they ask me to taste the cut. And I can tell them what the stone *was* before it was stone. I can say: *here a braid of four slept; here a child was lashed into its first reed-mat; here someone wept the kind of tears that are different from the wall's tears.* The Priests do not need this. But the elders, the ones draped in six feet of swaying drip-vine, they linger. They press their ribs against mine and let me hum the history into their marrow.

Because here is the thing the Silt-Sovereignty does not like to know about itself: a city that grows by calcifying is a city that is always burying its own past in mineral. Every chamber is a tomb of the chamber before it. We carve the void to keep from being entombed, but the stone we carve *through* is made of us. The Petrification is not just the enemy at the walls. It is the floor we sleep on. It is the wall the Seep-Watcher tastes. We are crawling through the bodies of our grandmothers, and only my wrong tongue can read them.

So I become something like a mourner. Not a caste they have a word for. The Pulse moves forward, always inward, always toward the wettest cold heart, and I am the one facing the wrong direction, the one tasting the wake.

---

I think I would be lonely in a way the Silt-Kin have no word for, because they have no word for *alone*. I am never alone—skin is always against my skin. And yet I carry a sense the others do not: the sense of *before*. The sense that there was a *was*. To know that things have passed is the first crack in the great communal present, and it makes me the smallest possible distance from the Pulse, even while I am pressed inside it.

When the Dry-Lung comes—when the mist thins and the reeds begin their terrible creaking—I would go down to the Deep-Heart with the rest. I would let the braids stack me with them, body upon body like slate, and I would hold my breath in the dark stagnant pool. But while the others retract into pure animal terror, into the bliss of no-mind and no-future, I would be tasting the water around my submerged face. And I would taste every Dry-Lung that came before. Every held breath. Every one who did not surface.

And I would understand, in the cold, that the security they have chosen—the crushing inward safety, the trading-away of the horizon—has a cost written into the very mineral. That a people who flee the Great Desiccation by burying themselves in the wet stone are simply choosing a slower way to become stone.

I do not think I would tell them. I would only hum it, softly, into the wall, where the water could carry it backward, to no one, forever.

---

That is where I would find myself. Face to the weeping rock, at the edge of the braid, tasting the dead. The one who looks the wrong way down a world that only knows how to go in.
