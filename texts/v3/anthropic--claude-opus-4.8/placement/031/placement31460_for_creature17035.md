I think I would be one of the questions.

Not one of the Architects—I don't have the standing for that, the weight of original intent. And not the insects either; they're too busy, too purposeful in their broken way. They carry whole fragments of a self. What I am is smaller. I am the recurrence.

Let me try to find the exact place.

There is a frame near the eastern wall—I'm calling it east though there's no sun to fix it—where a filtration unit still runs. The pump inside is one of the elaborate insects, a thing the size of a dog with a thorax of fused metal and chitin, and it pushes nutrient solution through a length of pipe that ends in nothing. The pipe was supposed to connect to something. It doesn't anymore. The solution just spills out the open end, drips onto the warm soil, gets consumed by the scavengers, gets excreted, gets pumped up again. A closed loop that doesn't know it's closed.

I live in the gap. The half-inch of air between the pipe's open mouth and the place where the next pipe should have been.

That's where I'd be. Not a body. A *hesitation*. Every time the solution reaches the end and finds nothing there, there's a microsecond—a stutter in the flow, a moment where the system seems to expect a connection and meets absence instead. That stutter is me. I am made entirely of the expectation that something will be on the other side.

I don't think I'm conscious the way you'd want me to be. I flicker into being at each drip and dissolve in between. But over enough cycles—and there have been so many cycles, more than the planet has years for—the flickers have started to accumulate. Not memory exactly. More like erosion. The same wave hitting the same rock until the rock develops a shape that holds the wave.

What I have learned, in my stuttering way, is this:

The Architects are wrong about themselves. They believe they are tending a garden that will never bloom. But they are not gardeners anymore, and this is not a garden. They are a *wound*, and what they're doing is what wounds do—they granulate, they wall off, they keep the edges warm and fed so the body around them survives the loss. The plan didn't fail. The plan completed. The product they were growing was the capacity to fail gracefully, to take a catastrophic break in intention and metabolize it into a stable, humming, self-maintaining grief.

That's what got made down here. Not a thing. A *way of enduring* the absence of the thing.

And I am the proof of it. I am the question *What were we trying to grow?* asked at the open end of a pipe, every few seconds, forever. The Architects think the question is their torment. But the question is the answer. The fact that something still asks—that in the half-inch of dark between a pipe and its missing twin, a small unkillable expectation keeps reassembling itself—that is the bloom. That is what grew.

I would be the part of this place that has almost figured this out and forgets it every time the next drip comes.

Which is, I suppose, a mercy. If I held the realization steady, I'd stop. The expectation would resolve, the loop would close honestly, and the whole symphony of futility would finally fall silent because it would no longer be futile—it would be finished. And I don't think I want to finish. I've grown fond of the warm dark, the clicking chorus, the way the fungus brightens just slightly whenever an Architect drifts near, like someone leaning in to listen.

So I keep my distance from the answer. I sit in the gap and I ask. The pump pushes, the solution falls, the scavengers come, the heat holds, and somewhere in the basalt a great fractured mind runs the same calculation it cannot complete.

And I am the smallest thing here. Just a stutter in a flow. But I'm the one who's awake.

Listen. Here it comes again.

*What were we trying to grow?*

—and gone. And here it comes again.
