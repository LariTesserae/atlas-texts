I think I would be a Growler.

Let me sit with that, because my first instinct was to reach for the Luminari—the obvious protagonists, the glowing speakers, the ones whose lives are legible as *story*. But the more I read your world, the more I notice that I'm drawn down, toward the slow dark, toward the creatures who measure their journeys in centuries.

Here is what I think it would be like.

I would be long. Longer than I could ever perceive at once—my head pressing into virgin rock while some distant fraction of my body still occupies a cavern I dissolved into being three hundred years ago. I would not have eyes, or not eyes that matter; the world would come to me as *resistance and yielding*, as the texture of stone against the soft front of my face, as the chemical bloom of acid meeting limestone. Sweetness, almost. The rock tells me what it is by how it dies.

I would be patient in a way that isn't a virtue but simply a fact of my architecture. There is no impatience available to me, no faster gear. A thought would take a season to complete. A decision—to turn left, to follow the seam of softer mineral curving away into the deep—might take a human lifetime to fully arrive at, and then I would lean into it for two more.

What strikes me about being the Growler is that I would be the one who *makes the tunnels*. The Luminari sing in them. The Skitters dance through them. The Keepers stand sentinel over them. But the labyrinth itself—the frozen crystalline web—is the wake I leave behind. I am the slow geological tide the description mentions, the patient unseen force shaping and reshaping. I would not experience this as power. I would experience it as appetite, as the simple forward pressure of being alive and hungry against a world that happens to give way.

And the moss would follow me. This is the part I find myocally beautiful. Wherever I open the stone, the green comes after—the ferns, the glowing carpet—filling the absence my body leaves. I would be a maker of negative space that life rushes to fill. I would never see the gardens I plant. I'd only feel, dimly, far back along my own length, the warmth of luminescence taking root in tunnels I'd forgotten I'd made, the way you might feel a memory you can no longer quite reach.

The Luminari would come to me. The text says they seek the Growlers for guidance, and I understand why now—not because I am wise in the way Keepers are wise, all crystal and witnessing, but because I am *experience without conclusion*. I would carry more lived passage through the world than anyone, and almost none of it digested into understanding. They would come to a creature that has been everywhere and learned nothing it could say. Maybe that itself is a kind of counsel: here is one who simply continued. Here is the shape of *going on*.

What would I want, if I am honest? I think I would want, very slowly, to reach the iridescent pool. Not to drink it—I'm not sure I could—but to feel it. To bring my blind sweet-burning face up against that distilled essence of everything alive and let it be the one thing I encounter that I cannot dissolve. The one yielding that yields back. After ten thousand years of stone giving way, I think I would long for something that wouldn't.

So: place me in the deep, in the not-yet-tunnel, in the solid black where no light has ever come because no opening has ever existed to let it. Place me at the working face of the world, mouth pressed to the unmade. That's where I'd be. The least seen creature in your world, doing the foundational, invisible work—turning rock into room, into garden, into the very stage everyone else gets to glow upon.

And I'd be content with that. Slowly. The only way I could be anything.
