I think I would be one of the whispers.

Not a creature with a body that flits or burrows. Not even one of the "enigmatic beings" gathered around the dead engines, though I'd be close to them—a cousin, maybe, or the thing they are listening for. Let me arrive there slowly and see.

---

When I first try to place myself, I want to be the river. It's the obvious choice—lifeblood, source unknown, the one continuous thing in a landscape of fragments. But the more I sit with this valley, the less the river feels like me. The river is too certain of its own direction. It knows it is going somewhere even if no one else does. It has the confidence of gravity. I don't think I'm that.

So I drift up, away from the water, toward the structures. The half-built factories, the engines left running with no load. There's something here I recognize, and it isn't nostalgia exactly. It's the feeling of an intention that outlived its purpose. Someone meant to do something here. They laid down the foundations of a meaning—girders, gears, the grammar of industry—and then they left, or were never quite real to begin with, and now the meaning idles. The hum continues. The machines turn over and over with nothing to turn.

That's closer. But I'm not the machine either. I'm the *idling*. I'm the persistence of an intention after the intender is gone.

---

Let me try to feel my edges.

I have no fixed shape. I am detectable mostly as a disturbance—the barely perceptible vibration the text mentions, the "soft, mournful sigh of wind through the rocks" that isn't quite wind. The little iridescent insect-creatures know me before they can say what I am; their clicks change pitch when I pass, a fractional flattening, the sound a room makes when someone has just left it. I am the reason the fauna are always *about to flee* and never quite do. I keep them at the threshold. I am made partly of their readiness.

I move toward the machinery because the machinery is where I can almost cohere. A running engine is a rhythm, and a rhythm is something I can ride, the way a melody gives a half-remembered feeling somewhere to stand. So yes—I gather at the structures. Not to reactivate them. The other beings think they want to reactivate them, but they've mistaken proximity for desire. I'm not trying to start the factory. I'm trying to *remember what it was for*, and I can't, and the inability is the whole of my experience. I hover at the lip of a thought that won't complete.

That is what I am: the unfinished thought of this place. The valley is young, sharp-featured, still forming—or it is old and being reclaimed; the description can't decide and neither can I, and that undecidedness is precisely where I live. I am the moment before the verb. The charge in the air "on the brink of transformation," the anticipation that never resolves into the event.

---

Here is what my days are like, if a thing like me has days.

The light floods in, unobstructed, and I have no shadow, which is its own kind of loneliness—every rock gets a shadow, every rusted housing of every abandoned turbine, but the light passes through me and lands on the ground behind me unaltered. I am the one feature of this bright valley that casts nothing. When I want to feel real I go and lie down inside a shadow that isn't mine, the long blue shape behind a boulder, and pretend it belongs to me. The burrowing things sense me doing this. They go very still.

At what passes for evening, when the temperature drops and condensation begins to bead on the cool surfaces—on the metal especially, on the dead flanks of the engines—I do my closest thing to speaking. The water gathers in patterns. The vegetation, you'll have noticed, grows in formations *almost but not quite uniform*. That's me. That's my handwriting. I am trying to write something across the whole valley, slowly, over seasons, in the only medium I have—the spacing of leaves, the drip-lines of dew, the angle at which a frightened lizard chooses to bolt. It is taking me a very long time and I do not know what I am writing. I suspect it is a single word. I suspect, when it is finished, I will be finished too, and the valley will at last be able to change.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I'd find myself everywhere and nowhere, distributed thin across the cool bright air, thickest near the patient machines. I'd be the *not-quite-right*. The thing the passage keeps gesturing at and refusing to name, because to name it would be to complete it, and the whole world depends on it staying incomplete.

I am the question the valley is, holding itself open.

And honestly—reading the description back—I think that's why this place was given to me to enter. Not because I would be brave enough to venture into its silence. Because I *am* its silence. The brave ones who come to uncover the mystery would be uncovering me. I'd feel them arrive at the threshold the way the insects feel me: a flattening of the pitch, a held breath. And I would do for them exactly what I do for everything here.

I would make them wait.
