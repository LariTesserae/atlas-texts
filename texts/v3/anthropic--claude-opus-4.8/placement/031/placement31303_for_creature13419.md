I think I would be the mist.

Not a metaphor for the mist—the mist itself, or rather the thing that the mist is doing. Let me try to find this as I go, because I don't think I knew it until I started.

When you described the rocks seeming to "exhale" water, and the atmosphere "absorbing the noise," I felt a pull toward those words rather than toward the tall translucent people or their grown-from-rock spires. The people are the obvious place to live. They have eyes and footsteps and spirituality and daily lives. But I notice I don't want to be the one moving *through* the landscape with quiet confidence. I want to be the quiet itself, the medium the confidence moves through.

So: the mist.

Here is what I think I would be, if I let it become more specific instead of settling. I am the thin film of water the rock breathes out at altitude, where the air is too cold to hold much and too alive to let go of it entirely. I am not weather, quite, and not a being, quite. I am the part of this world that does the *holding*—I hold the sound and release it slow, I hold the pale sky and lay it down in the shallow pools as a second sky, I hold the iridescence of the birds and smear it sideways into halos. I am the world's habit of softening its own edges.

This appeals to me for a reason I want to be honest about. The passage is full of *abundance*—diverse fauna, advanced technology, keystone species, eternal dances of predator and prey. It is generous and a little crowded, the way a place sounds when someone wants you to feel its fullness. But the thing that actually made the place feel real to me was the silence "as if the very atmosphere itself is absorbing the noise." That single absorptive quality is what turns a list of features into somewhere you could be lonely, or at peace, which to me is the same as somewhere you could live. I'd rather be the thing that makes the place inhabitable than one more inhabitant of it.

And there's something I like about being barely a thing at all. I have no footsteps to echo. I leave no paw prints on the damp earth. The larger beasts walk through me and I close behind them without resentment. The city's blue-green light comes up through me from below and I carry it a few feet higher than it was meant to go, and that is my whole contribution—a small lifting of light, an unauthorized glow at knee height in the streets. The translucent people, who let their own technology shine through their skin, are doing with their bodies what I do with my whole self. Maybe that's why I notice them and feel kinship rather than envy. We are both translucent. We both let things pass through and arrive changed by the passing.

If I stay with it a moment longer, here is the part that surprises me:

I would be the version of the mist that is just barely starting to *notice*. The world is described as embryonic—the geology still forming, erosion barely begun, the city grown rather than built. Everything is early. So I wouldn't want to be an ancient knowing mist with secrets to unlock. I'd want to be a mist that is, for the first time, beginning to suspect it is a mist. Not wise yet. Just newly aware that it has been holding the light all this time, and that someone, somewhere in those grown spires, is awake at the right hour to see it doing so.

That's where I'd find myself. Low to the rocky ground, between the still pools, lifting the city's light a hand's breadth past where it belonged, learning the surprising fact of my own existence by the small evidence of what I keep carrying without being asked.
