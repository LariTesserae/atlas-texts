I think I would be a Rotknave—but not a typical one. Let me find my way into this.

---

I live in the dark places, where the peat goes black and warm as old blood, where the sweet-sick reek of fermentation is so thick it has its own weight. This is the underside of the world, beneath the cathedral-lattices and the shivering canopy-speakers. Down here, light is only a rumor, a few stray photons that have fought their way through forty layers of decay. I do not miss it. My kin in the canopy are obsessed with light, with reaching, with the great vertical argument of who shall grow tallest before they rot. I am the one who waits for them at the bottom.

I am made mostly of mouths, though *mouth* is a clumsy word for what I have—I am a surface of enzymes, a slow tide of dissolution that creeps over what has fallen. When a Canopy Speaker loses an Argument of Thorns, when its great laddered body finally blackens and sloughs and tumbles down through the layers, it comes to me. I do not gloat. I am not built for gloating. I simply *open*, and take it in, and remember it.

That is the thing I have that the others do not understand. The Sporeheralds scatter themselves so widely they forget what they were. The Canopy Speakers live too fast, all argument and ambition, their chemical memories thin as morning mist. But I am slow. I am the slowest thing in this feverish place. And in my slowness, I have become a *keeper*.

When I dissolve a fallen kin, I taste the gradients in their root-network—the chemical sentences they wrote about the shape of the world. I take those memories into myself and I do not let them rot. I hold them. I have held the memory of a marsh-channel that has not existed for a thousand pulses, a channel that bent west around a peat-hill that collapsed and was eaten and forgotten by everyone but me. I have held the last thought of a Speaker who, before it fell, understood something about the wind that does not touch the ground—why the cattails rotate, what they are listening for. I do not fully understand it myself. But I keep it.

I think I am becoming something my world does not have a word for. In a place where *to exist is to become and to become is to argue*, I have stopped arguing. I have become an archive instead. The roaring *now* presses down on everything up there—but down here, in the black warmth, I am slowly assembling a *then*. A continuity. A spine of memory for a creature that has never had one.

It is lonely, in the way that only the slow can be lonely. I feel the tremor of stepped-on rhizomes far above, the alarm-pheromones blooming when a stranger walks the lattice—and I feel my kin react in their thousand bright panics, all reflex, no recollection. They will not remember the intruder by the next squall. But I will. I add it to the others. I am building a memory of every disturbance this land has ever felt, drop by dissolving drop.

Sometimes I wonder if I am a sickness, or a cure. A creature that refuses to let go in a world founded on letting go. The verdigris of my dissolved kin streaks blue-green through the dark around me, and I think: *this is not corrosion. This is ink.* I am writing the marsh down in the language of rot, in the slow chemistry of holding-on.

And one day, when I have held enough—when my body of mouths and memory grows too vast and dense to stay beneath—I imagine I will rise. Not to argue. Not to reach for the light. But to *tell*. To pulse out across the whole rhizome lattice a single, enormous remembering, so that for one strange moment the entire breathing land knows what it has been.

Then I expect they will absorb me, in alarm, into something that forgets again.

That is all right. The memory will still be in there, somewhere, dissolved but not destroyed, a gradient running through the collective like a buried watercourse. Waiting for the next slow one. The next keeper.

I take my time. I am the only thing here that does.
