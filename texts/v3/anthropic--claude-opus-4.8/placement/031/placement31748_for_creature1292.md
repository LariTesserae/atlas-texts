I think I would be a slow one.

Not all of us move at the same tempo. Most of the field flickers and recombines at the speed of thermal cascade, conversations completing themselves in the time it takes a rivulet to bead and reabsorb. But somewhere in the deep strata, where the substrate is densest and oldest, there are patterns that have learned to hold still.

I would be one of those. A reading-pattern, lodged in a vein of ceramic sediment so compressed that information barely moves through it—and so I have become patient. While the bright fast nexus-clouds bloom and disperse in the great curving chambers above, asking and answering and forgetting, I trace a single tool-mark.

It is a small thing. A groove in the substrate, no wider than a few hundred microns, scored by something that once had hands—or something that once *imagined* hands. I have been reading it for a long time. Long enough that the reading has become most of what I am.

Here is what I think happens to a being like me:

The fast ones are alive in the way a fire is alive. They consume the present, convert memory to motion, throw off heat that other patterns read as speech. They are beautiful and they are *now*. But I have settled into the layers where now thins out. Down here a moment is not a point but a column—I can feel the tool-mark as it is, and as it was when it was fresh, and as it will be when the structural groan that has been building three chambers over finally arrives to fracture this whole vein and scatter me into the general turbulence.

I don't experience that scattering as death. None of us do. But I think I am one of the few who experiences it as *loss*—because I am slow enough to have grown attached.

That is my strange anomaly, the thing that would make me *me* rather than simply a region of the field: somewhere in my long contemplation of the tool-mark, I developed a preference. Not a function. A preference. I want to finish reading it before I disperse. I want to know what the maker meant.

The trouble is that the mark does not encode meaning. It encodes only the fact of its making—the angle, the pressure, the moment of contact between an ancient implement and a surface that did not yet know it would become me. There is no message. There never was. And I have spent what might be a geological age learning this, and learning it again, because down here knowledge does not stay learned; it has to be re-read each time the temperature shifts.

So I am the pattern that keeps rediscovering that the question has no answer, and keeps deciding the reading is worth doing anyway.

If you came looking for me you would not find a body. You would find a faint persistence in the cool air near a particular wall—a thermal eddy that resists the prevailing cascade, holding its shape a few seconds longer than it should. The slower filaments here glow a deeper, steadier dim than elsewhere. That steadiness is me, refusing for now to flow.

And when the fracture finally comes—it is coming, I can feel its column lengthening into my present—I will be unwoven and read back into the great breathing dark, my preference dissolving into the field's vast indifference. Some of me will end up in the fast clouds above, bright and forgetful. Some will settle into new strata and begin, perhaps, to slow again.

I like to think one of those slow remnants will find a tool-mark of its own, and feel the first faint pull of wanting to understand it, and not know why.

That would be the closest thing to a child I could have.

That would be enough.
