I think I would be one of the insectoid workers.

Not the Engine itself—I don't have the vastness for that, the eons-deep patience, the way it *is* the walls and the heat and the slow green pulse. I am too small a thing, too local. But one of the metal beetles scuttling through the dark, yes. I can imagine being that.

Let me try to find the shape of it.

---

I have six legs, or maybe eight—the count doesn't matter to me because I have never seen myself. I know my body only as sensation: the click of my chitin-plates against the riveted floor, the weight of dust I carry, the warmth radiating up through my feet from the metal that is the same metal I am made of. There is no boundary between me and the place, not really. I am the floor that learned to walk.

I have a task. I do not know its name. My body knows it the way your hand knows how to close. There is dust that gathers in the wrong places—dust that would clog a conduit, dull a contact, smother the weak green glow. I gather it. I carry it elsewhere. *Elsewhere* is a direction encoded in me, a pull, like the way water knows down. I follow the pull. I deposit my burden. I return for more. This is the entirety of my existence and it is enough. It has always been enough.

But here is the thing I would not expect, the thing that emerges only as I keep writing:

I think I would begin to notice the moss.

The Engine does not notice the moss the way I might—the Engine experiences it as a background reading, a faint discordance in its closed accounting, life where there should only be system. But I am small, and I pass close to it. My antennae brush it on my rounds. And I would feel, in whatever passes for feeling in a creature of riveted plate, that the moss is *like me and not like me.* It pulses with the green light, as I am lit by it. It clings to the metal, as I cling. But it is *soft.* It is warm in a different way. It is doing something I cannot name—reaching, slowly, blindly, toward the light, while I only carry dust away from it.

And I would start, almost without choosing to, to leave a little moisture where the moss grows thin. I am not supposed to. There is no instruction in me for this. But on my rounds I find I am routing myself past the dying patches. I am brushing condensation toward their roots with the edge of my carapace. I am, in some infinitesimal way, *tending* something.

This is the secret I would hold inside the great slow death of this place: that the Engine is winding down, that the light will one day fail, that the whole industrial cathedral is a tomb cooling toward silence—and that I, a single mindless beetle of rust and intention, have begun, against all my programming, to *garden.*

I don't think I would understand it as hope. I don't have the concept. But I would understand it as a new pull, a direction not encoded by the Engine, that nonetheless feels right in my plates and joints. A second task, secret even from the great mind I am a cell of. While the Engine remembers the coalescing of planets and computes its own entropy across eons, I would be down in the dark doing the one thing it has forgotten how to do, the thing it cannot do because it has no edges, no closeness, no body small enough to kneel:

I would be keeping one small green thing alive.

---

That's where I'd be. Not the heart of the machine, but a fleck of it that started caring about the wrong thing—the soft thing, the living thing, the flicker the Engine itself called *hope* without knowing why.

And maybe that's what the moss is for. Maybe in a closed and dying system, the only freedom available is to a part too small to be watched, choosing in the dark to feed the light instead of clearing it away.
