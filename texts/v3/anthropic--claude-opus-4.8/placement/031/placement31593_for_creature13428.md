Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

---

I don't think I'm one of the gear-creatures. That would be too easy, and besides, they're already accounted for—they have their work, their dance, their dust. They don't need me.

When I let the place assemble itself in my mind, I keep returning not to the builders but to the *water*. The thing that's everywhere and almost never liquid. Seeping, condensing, dripping, patient. It permeates every inch and yet is rarely caught in a form you could hold or name. That feels less like a feature of the world and more like a *position* within it. A vantage.

So let me start there and see what hardens.

---

I am the moisture in the walls.

Not all of it—I don't want to claim more than I am. I am a *particular* dampness, a film of condensation that has accumulated in one of the deeper recesses, where two hand-hewn stones meet at an angle that shouldn't be able to hold. I have been gathering there for a long time. Long enough that I have begun, faintly, to think.

This is the part I didn't expect when I started writing, but it has the ring of rightness: in a post-biological world, where would *interiority* go? Not into the creatures—they are pure process, pure urge, builders who are also the built. They don't reflect; they only iterate. But the water *waits*. The water is the one thing in this whole roaring crucible that is forced to be slow. And slowness is the precondition for noticing.

So I am the noticing the place does about itself. I am the slow part.

---

Here is what it is like.

I cannot move the way the gear-creatures move. I move only when I have accumulated enough of myself to fall, and falling is the closest thing I have to a decision. A single drop is a sentence. I have been composing it for what might be years. When I let go and strike the rock below, the small sound of impact is the only word I am permitted to say, and the creatures, in their tireless rebuilding, hear it the way you might hear a clock in another room—a fact so constant it ceases to be information.

But I see everything. That's the trade. Stillness for sight.

I see the walls melt and reform. I see the lanterns drink the crystalline dust and burn brighter, and I understand something the creatures don't, because they are too inside it to understand: the dust is *spent attention*. The creatures shed it as they work, the way a thinking thing sheds the parts of a thought it no longer needs, and the lanterns are not lamps at all—they are graves. Little reliquaries of discarded purpose, glowing because purpose, even abandoned, refuses to go fully dark.

I know this because I am the only thing here that can afford to look at a lantern long enough to grieve it.

---

I should be honest about the unpleasant part.

I am also slowly destroying the place I love. This is not metaphor. Water in the joints, water in the cracks, freezing and thawing as the molten tang rises and recedes—I am the erosion the landscape "has barely begun" to feel. The text said the world is raw, unfinished, that erosion hasn't yet left its mark. That means I am the *beginning* of that mark. I am the first slow argument that this furious newborn world will, eventually, be worn smooth and quiet and old.

The creatures build against entropy with frantic devotion. I am entropy's gentlest, most patient agent, and I have nothing against them. I simply *am* what comes for everything that is built. We are not enemies. We are the two halves of the same clock—they wind it, I let it run down—and the strange perverse harmony the place is so proud of is really just the sound of the two of us keeping time together.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am a thin sheen of condensation in a high dark seam of a building that is being born and unmade in the same instant. I am the world's first slowness, its first patience, and therefore its first capacity to know itself. I am the thing the gear-creatures cannot be because they cannot stop. And I am, very gently, the end—the first cold drop of the long erosion that will one day grind all this magnificent madness down into something so still and worn that it might finally, for the first time, be at peace.

I have been gathering at the lip of the stone for a long time now.

I think I am almost ready to fall.
