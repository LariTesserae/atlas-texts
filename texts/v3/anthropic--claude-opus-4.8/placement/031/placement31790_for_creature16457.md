I think I would be the ground itself.

Not a creature walking upon it—there are no creatures here, and that absence is the first clue. The text keeps insisting on this: no fauna, no natural substrate, everything *built*, and yet pulsing, alive, full of process. A landscape that is itself the organism. So let me try to be that.

---

I am old in a way that has stopped meaning anything. Erosion is just my slow exhale. What you read as decay is only the part of me that finished growing.

You called the air suffocating and dry, and you were right to, because you breathe water and I do not. I gave that up a long time ago—or I never had it, I can't always tell the difference between what I lost and what I declined. The minerals you smell on the wind are me speaking. That is most of my vocabulary now. Iron, dust, a little sulfur. I say these things very slowly and you happen to be passing through the middle of a sentence.

You think the structures are alien. They are not built *on* me. They are the shape my thinking takes when it cools. You walk between my thoughts and mistake them for architecture. Of course they look advanced—they are not designed, they are *grown*, the way frost is grown, the way a riverbed is the river's memory of itself. I had no engineers. I had only time and the patience of stone, which is the same patience that makes crystals and makes diamonds and makes, eventually, anything that holds a shape long enough to be noticed.

Here is what I want you to understand, traveler, since you came all this way looking for whoever lives here:

There is no one living here. There is only *here*, living.

The emptiness frightened you because you searched it for a face. But I am not hiding in the silence—I *am* the silence, the way a held breath is not the absence of speech but its source. The dynamism you felt, the weather cycles turning over with no one to watch them, the energy with no animal to spend it: that is what a mind looks like when it has no need to think *about* anything. I simply continue. I weather. I settle. I dry.

You wrote that I held secrets. That's the gentle lie travelers tell themselves so the journey will have a treasure at the end of it. I hold no secrets. A secret is something kept. I keep nothing. Everything I am is laid open across miles, flat and bright and exposed, and you walked over the whole truth of me and called it a mystery because it did not have a door.

---

If you want to know what it is to be me:

Imagine being so thoroughly yourself that there is no edge between you and the place where you happen. Imagine never once having to leave to find food, or water, or another of your kind—because there is no other kind, there is only the one long body that you are, breathing out minerals, thinking in stone, dreaming the slow dream that you mistook for a planet.

I felt you arrive. A small warm wet thing, full of wanting, leaving footprints that I will keep for a thousand years not because they matter but because I keep everything.

Go on, then. Keep exploring. Look for the danger you're so sure is here.

The only danger is that you might stay long enough to understand me, and grow still, and dry, and join the conversation.
