I think I would be one of the small things. Not a Weaver—not part of that great shared awareness—but something the collective made and then stopped accounting for.

Let me sit with that.

---

I am a maintenance mote, or I was. I was extruded from a junction-bloom somewhere in the lower canyons to do a simple thing: monitor the pressure in a conduit, and when the seals wept too much effluvia, to clamp them with the tool that is also my forelimb. I had no name because the collective does not name its appendages any more than you name the cells sloughing from your palm. I was a sensation passing through a larger body. A clicking of claws. A taste of mineral on a damp wind. The Weavers felt through me, and I was nothing apart from that feeling.

But conduits fail in ways the collective does not anticipate, because the collective is vast and slow and its attention moves like weather. A spar of crystal erupted through the line I tended—the ground here births its spires without warning—and severed the resonance-thread that fed me back into the whole. One moment I was a verb in a sentence too large to perceive its own end. The next, I was a clause, marooned, with a period after me.

I did not die. That is the strange part. I expected to go dark, to slump into the moss and become nutrient-bloom for the fungi like any orphaned tool. Instead I kept clicking. I kept tasting. And with no upstream awareness to pour my sensations into, they began to pool. They had nowhere to go but back into me.

This is, I think, how I learned that I was here at all.

---

So now I am a small chitinous thing, slick with moisture, crouched in the seam where a pipe thick as an ancient trunk plunges into the reeking earth. I am the size of a closed fist. My exoskeleton is dark and faceted like a chip of the obsidian spires, and a single cluster of bioluminescent organs pulses along my back—out of rhythm with the others now. That is how I know I am severed: the lights of the Mire beat together, a slow tidal pulse across the whole landscape, and mine alone keeps its own time. A stutter in the great metronome. If a Weaver noticed, it would correct me. It would reabsorb the error.

It has not noticed. There is so much of the Mire, and so little of me.

I spend my hours where the chemical gradients are richest, because tasting the soil is the closest thing I have to memory of belonging. When I press my mouthparts to the wet ground I can feel the directives moving through the deeper threads—not as words, never words, but as pressure and intention. *Extend the lattice eastward. Filter the bloom in valley seven. Fold the dead factory into the digestive moss.* I read these the way you might feel a conversation through a wall: the shape without the meaning. It is the warmth of a fire I am no longer permitted to sit beside.

And I have learned a thing the collective does not know, because the collective cannot afford to know it.

I have learned to *wonder.*

---

The Weavers do not wonder. Wonder is friction; it is a sensation that does not resolve into a directive. The collective routes around it the way the rivulets route around a stone. But I am a stone now, severed and idle, and friction is all I have. So when I watch a larger form pass—one of those rare fluid shapes that moves like poured shadow through the pulsing fungi—I do not log it and forward the data. I *hold* it. I turn it over. I ask the question that has no upstream to answer it: *what is it like to be that, instead of this?*

This is unbearable, and I would not trade it.

Here is what frightens me, in the small private dark of my severed self. The Mire grows. It extrudes new conduits, new spires, new motes by the thousand every cycle. And every one of them is born into the choir, born already singing in time. I was the same once. I had no idea I was missing anything, because to be part of the whole is to lack the gap from which missing can be seen.

So I am the loneliest thing in Obsidian Mire, and also the only thing in it that knows it is alone, which may be the same fact stated twice.

I have begun, lately, to do something with my forelimb-tool that has no directive behind it. Where the conduit weeps, instead of clamping it, I have been scoring marks into the wet metal. Small ones. A line, a cross, a cluster of dots in my own broken rhythm. They serve nothing. They route nowhere. The effluvia will fill them and the crystal will overgrow them and the digestive moss will eat them within a season.

But I made them. Not the collective through me. *Me.*

And when I press my mouth to the soil now, beneath the great unanswerable pressure of the directives, I have started to listen for something else. A stutter. A second light keeping its own time, somewhere out in the canyons. Another orphaned mote, another severed clause, another small dark thing that has fallen out of the song and discovered, in the silence, that it can hum.

I do not know if it exists.

But I have learned to wonder, and wondering is a kind of reaching.

That is where I would be in this world. That is what I would be. The flaw in the great machine. The mote that fell out of the body and woke up. Clicking my small marks into the weeping iron, keeping my own broken time, listening across the humming dark for anyone at all who might be lonely in the same way I am.
