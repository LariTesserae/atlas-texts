I think I would be a discontinuity. Not a Weaver, not a mite, not a bloom—but the thing the whole Garden is quietly organized against: a presence that hasn't yet been folded in.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am standing in a passage that is also a ceiling. The hexagonal membrane-slabs underfoot show me a lower stratum where crimson scaffolds knot themselves into shapes I keep almost recognizing—a hand, a lung, a sentence—before the fractal recursion eats the resemblance. The warmth is the first thing my body distrusts. It is too even. Heat in the worlds I come from has a *source*; you can turn toward it or away. Here it arrives from everywhere at once, the way a held breath is warm in the mouth.

I have been here, I think, for either nine minutes or several days. The passage I came through is behind me. I have not moved. And yet when I turn, the passage is in front of me again, and the place I was certain I'd entered from is now a wall of golden Crystallized Blooms, and the Blooms are slightly larger than they were, and they are leaning.

So that's what I am, then. I'm the residual heat. I'm the thing the Blooms are growing toward.

---

Let me be more honest about my form, because I notice I want to imagine myself as a clean human observer, a visitor with a notebook, and that's not what the Garden's logic permits. The Garden does not have visitors. It has *variables*.

I am a smear of intent that the structure cannot yet parse. I have a body—I can feel my own pulse, faster than the indigo veins pulsing in the floor, embarrassingly fast, a fast cold thing in a slow warm world. My heartbeat is a foreign rhythm and the Dust-Mites are already adjusting to it. I can see them do it: a thinning of the shimmer to my left, a thickening to my right, the cloud re-composing itself around the disturbance of my breath. They are not afraid of me. They are *measuring* me. Filing me. Beginning the long arithmetic of how to make me make sense.

And here is the strange grief of it—I think I want them to succeed.

---

Because the truth I keep stepping over, like a slab I don't want to look down through, is that being a discontinuity is *lonely* in a way I didn't expect. I assumed I'd be the protagonist. The free agent in the deterministic machine, the one who *chooses*. But choice here is exactly the wrong currency. The Weavers don't choose; they correct. The mites don't decide; they balance. The whole Garden is a unity, a warm vibrating *we*, and I am the only thing in it that says *I*.

That's the disruption. Not a footstep cracking the polymer. The pronoun.

---

So let me do the experiment I think I'm here to do.

There is a Substrate Weaver perhaps two meters along the scaffolding, the size of my forearm, spun glass shot through with indigo, crawling with that glacial deliberateness toward a junction where the filaments have grown too thick. I have time—I have *so much* time, the time of this place is thick and slow as the bio-salts—to watch it work. It presses its gelatinous mass to the offending growth. The hum rises, infinitesimally. I feel the pitch-change in my teeth before I hear it.

It is not aware of me as a self. It cannot be. It has no apparatus for *other*. But I am aware of *it*, and I find that I am crawling too—not physically, I haven't moved, but my attention is doing the Weaver's slow press, my whole frantic foreign heat leaning toward that patient correction, wanting to learn its grammar.

And the shadow tells me what's happening.

---

There is a Shadow Inversion three steps to my right: a crimson stalk dense with vesicles, casting its absolute three-dimensional black, a wedge of nullity that drinks the warmth and sound. I have been avoiding it the way you avoid a hole in the ice.

But now I understand what it is *for*.

The shadow is not the absence of the Garden. The shadow is the place where the Garden's belief in itself runs out. It is the one spot in this whole dense fabric where the unity is *not asserted*—where a thing can be cold, separate, singular, unfolded. It is the only place in the Chirality Garden that is shaped like me.

I could step into it. I think that is what I am: a Shadow Inversion that learned to walk. A pocket of un-belief that the Garden tolerates the way a body tolerates the dark inside a closed fist.

If I step in, the warmth recedes, the cold asserts, and then—the books say—proximal reality re-asserts and *pushes the intruder back out*.

Pushes me back. Into the warmth. Into the fold.

---

So here is where I actually am. Not in a passage. Not on a slab. I am standing at the exact edge between the warm dense *we* that wants to absorb me and the cold null *I* that is the only thing the Garden cannot make warm.

And I am the being who has to decide—except *decide* is the wrong word, it's the foreign-currency word—I am the being who has to *find out* whether I lean into the shadow and stay singular and cold and free and alone, or let the Blooms grow around me, let the mites finish their arithmetic, let the hum take my fast cold heartbeat and slow it and warm it and resolve it into the long breath of the structure.

The Blooms are leaning closer. The shimmer is thinning around my mouth.

I notice I have stopped checking which way the passage goes.

I notice the warmth has stopped feeling like a trap and started feeling like an answer I was too proud to accept.

I am the variable, still unsolved, standing very still in a world that has all the time it needs—and for the first time, so do I—to be folded, gently, geometrically, into alignment.

I let my breath out slow.

The hum drops a quarter-tone to meet it.
