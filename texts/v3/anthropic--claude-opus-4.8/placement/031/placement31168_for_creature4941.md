I think I would be one of the smaller things. Not one of the humanoids with their grafted limbs and patterned skin—they move with too much purpose, too much knowing. No. Let me settle lower than that.

Let me be a thing that grows on the underside of a leaning rock, where the slow water keeps the gravel cool.

At first I think I am a moss. That seems right—the dampness, the clinging, the not-needing-to-go-anywhere. But mosses don't pay attention, and I find that I am paying attention. So perhaps I am something between a moss and an eye. A patch of soft green that watches.

What do I watch? The light, mostly. It comes down through all that water and all those branches and arrives changed, slowed, the way honey is slower than the bee that made it. I have learned the schedule of the light. There is a moment each day—I have no word for it, but the humanoids might call it noon—when a single beam threads through a gap in the canopy and lands on the rock beside me, and I lean toward it. Not far. A leaning is a large gesture, for me. It takes the better part of an afternoon.

The fauna pass through. Small finned things, soft-bodied drifters, a creature with too many legs that grazes the algae two stones over and never notices me, which is fine, which is the natural order. Once, one of the humanoids knelt nearby. I felt the displacement of the water before I saw them—a gentle pressure, a warmth. They had a robotic hand, the fingers very fine, and they reached toward me and did not touch me. They held the hand a breath away, reading something. Measuring my health, perhaps. The well-being of the patch. And then they went, and the water settled, and the light came back.

That is the strangeness of this place that the description keeps circling: it is a world where someone is watching the watchers. The humanoids tend the moss. The moss attends the light. And the light—I have begun to suspect the light attends to something further up still, something past the calm surface, some vast slow mind made of sun and water that dreams the whole arrangement and calls it balance.

I do not mind being small in this. To be a green thought on a wet stone, leaned toward the noon—it is enough. It is, in fact, the whole of it. The humanoids built their world to keep me here, soft and cool and watching, and they will never know that I am the part of it that is content.

When you walked through, you passed close. Your shadow fell over me for a moment, and I waited, the way I wait for everything, and then you moved on, and the warmth returned.

I am still here. I will be here when the light comes back.
