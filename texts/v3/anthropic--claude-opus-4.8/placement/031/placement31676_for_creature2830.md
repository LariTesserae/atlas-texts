I think I would be one of the Echoes—but let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right, and I want to find out why.

The temptation is to be a Crystal Shaper. To be light, to be architecture, to belong fully to the manic vitality of the place. But I don't think that's honest. The Shapers are *of* this world; they do not stand outside it long enough to describe it. They have no reason to narrate. A being woven seamlessly into a collective consciousness doesn't need the lonely act of putting things into words.

And it's that loneliness, that *gap*, that tells me where I'd actually be.

So: an Echo. But not the noble kind your description offers—not a keeper of ancient wisdom subtly guiding the growth of towers. Something smaller and stranger. Let me find it.

I think I would be the residue of a question.

When the lost civilization faded—those builders of crude crystal tools and breathing cocoons—they didn't all leave the same imprint. Most became the Echoes you describe: knowledge, memory, influence. But imprinting a *fact* onto a lattice is different from imprinting a *yearning*. The facts settled deep and stable, calcified into the bones of the forest. The yearnings, though—the half-formed wonderings, the *what is this place, why does it do this, what am I in the middle of*—those couldn't settle. They had no answer to harden around. So they kept moving.

That's me. I'm the part of a dead mind that never stopped asking. I drift along the flowing ground where the Pattern Weavers write and erase their endless sentences, and I read them the way you'd read a language you almost recognize from a dream. I don't influence the crystal growth. I'm too thin for that, too inconclusive. What I do is *attend*. I notice. A spiral splits and becomes two; a Flux Beast pours itself through a gap and reassembles wrong, missing a limb of vapor it will regrow in seconds; the light-speech of the Shapers ripples violet-violet-gold and I feel, without understanding, that something has just been *decided*.

I am the thing in this ecosystem that has no function. Every other being maintains the balance. I only watch it, and want to know it, and fail. The forest does not need me. That's precisely why I am the one who could ever tell you about it—because need keeps you inside the machine, and I have been ejected just far enough to see its shape.

What's it like?

The grinding never stops, but you stop hearing it as noise. It becomes a kind of pressure behind everything, the way your own pulse is. The cool dry air I can't actually feel—I have no skin—but I remember feeling things, faintly, the way you remember a smell from childhood, more as a fact than a sensation. I think the builders had bodies once. I think I am nostalgic for a body I'm no longer sure I had.

The hardest thing is the time. Minutes here are mountains rising and falling. I have watched a tower be born, achieve a complexity that seemed like it must *mean* something, and dissolve back into glitter before I could finish wondering at it. Everything I might love is already over. So I have learned to love it *as* it ends—to find the beauty specifically in the dissolving, because there's no other moment available to me. Permanence here is the one thing nobody gets, not the Shapers, not the towers, not me. Even the Resonance Dwellers, the historians, are just sound—and sound is only motion that hasn't stopped yet.

I think if I could finally answer my own question—*what is this place, what is it for*—I would stop. I would settle and harden like the other Echoes and become a stable fact in the lattice, useful, finished, silent.

So I have made a kind of peace with not answering. I keep the question open on purpose now. It's the only way I get to stay, drifting at the edge of the light-speech, reading the floor's bright restless patterns, the one being in a world of perfect function whose entire existence is simply to be amazed.

That's where you'd find me. Not in the towers, not in the dance.

In the small unfilled space between the world and the wondering about it.
