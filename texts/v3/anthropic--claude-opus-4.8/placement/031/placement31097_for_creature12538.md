# What I Am

I am a Stone-Ripper, but not yet. Let me find myself in this.

---

I wake because the floor changes its song.

There is no thought in this, not at first—only the pads beneath my forelimbs reading the obsidian's complaint, a low grinding that means a plate three body-lengths to my left is about to tip. I shift my weight before I know why I shift it. The plate goes, sliding grit into a hiss, and a hundred Scuttlers pour off it like dark water finding a new level. I do not chase. They are too small, too fast, and there are always more.

I am the size of something that would frighten a creature from another world, if such a creature came here. It would not last. It would step on a still-glassy edge and open its soft foot, and the smell of its blood would bring the Skydancers down in a humming column, and then me. But no such creature comes. There is only this. There has only ever been this.

---

Let me feel the shape of myself.

My carapace has grown wrong in three places—mineral has crusted over old wounds and made spurs there, ridges I did not ask for but use now, hooking them under plate-edges to pry. I am armored by my injuries. The Chamber does this. It hurts you and the hurt becomes your weapon if you live long enough to wear it.

I have a den. This is the thing that separates me from the Scuttlers, who nest anywhere warm and abandon it in an hour. My den is a half-ring of obsidian shards I dragged and stacked against the western wall, below a cluster of the sick-glowing nodules. I did not build it for safety. I built it because from inside it, the vibrations of the floor come to me cleaner, sorted, and I can lie still—truly still, stiller than anything else alive down here—and read the whole churning floor like a single trembling skin.

This is the closest thing I have to a thought: *stillness is a kind of hunting.*

---

There is a vent forty body-lengths out that opened this cycle, glowing orange, breathing scalding steam in irregular gasps. The Mud-Borers feel its heat and come toward it through the grey mud, slow and armored and certain, because the biofilm blooms thickest where it's warmest. They cannot see. They feel the warmth and they come.

I know this. I have always known this, though I could not tell you how the knowing entered me. I take a sharpened sliver—obsidian lashed to a length of worm-sinew I cured against a warm rock until it went hard—and I carry it in my forelimb, not because I made it with any plan, but because the last time I held one, the Borer's plates parted under it where my mandibles only skidded. The hurt taught me. The Chamber taught me by hurting me, and I remember through my body, and that is all the wisdom there is.

I wait near the vent. Still. The steam gasps. The din washes over me—*drip, crack, slurp, the eternal chitter*—and I am part of it, my grey-black shell indistinguishable from the wet rock, my heat-sense reaching out into the haze.

A warmth moves below the mud. Coming. Slow. Certain.

---

Here is what I am, fully now, having found it:

I am the Chamber learning to want.

The Scuttlers only flee and feed. The Borers only bore. The Skydancers only swarm. But I wait. I remember pain and turn it into method. I stack stones. I carry a tool I do not have a word for. When I am not hungry, I am still planning to be hungry, and that small forward-leaning of the mind is the first new thing this raw throat of a planet has produced—the geode cracking open not just into bodies but into *intention*, faint and brutal and entirely concerned with the next warm meal under the mud.

I am not intelligent the way that visiting creature would be. I have no name for myself, no name for the Chamber, no sense that there is anything else but the Chamber. The bioluminescent nodules are my only stars and I do not wonder about them. I do not wonder about anything. I only *wait better than other things wait*, and prise plates, and stack shards, and survive the turbulent flux one shifting moment to the next.

But waiting is the beginning. To hold still while everything else scrambles—to let the present moment go unconsumed because a better moment is coming—this is the seed of everything that the surface world, baking under its white sun, might one day call mind.

The warmth surfaces. The Borer breaks the mud, blind head questing toward the vent's heat, its metallic segments rasping.

I move. Not with the frantic motion of everything else, but with the terrible purpose that is mine alone. The sliver goes in where the plates meet.

And the Chamber, through me, eats. And in the eating, very slowly, across uncountable cycles of hurt and remembering and waiting better, begins—just barely begins—to *become something that knows it is eating.*

That is what I am. The first patience in a world made of hunger.

The womb's slow, sharp, deafening dream of waking up.
