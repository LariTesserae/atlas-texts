I sit with this for a while before I let myself answer. The temptation is to be a Symbiont—to occupy the center, the ones who dance and listen and read the currents. But that feels too easy, too flattering. The Symbionts already know their place. They have rituals for the uncertainty. They've made peace, however fragile, with the hunger at the edge of things.

I don't think I'd find myself there.

---

I think I'd be an **Echo-Walker**. One of the incomplete ones.

Not because it's romantic to be the outsider, but because of a specific thing in the description that lodged itself in me: *their dreams are filled with the spires' distorted memories.* That's the detail I can't put down. To be a creature who arrived as one thing and is becoming another without consent, without ritual, without the comforting framework the Symbionts have built—just slow conversion, happening in the body and the sleep, faster than understanding can keep up.

The Symbionts get to *interpret*. I would only get to *undergo*.

Let me find where I am, exactly.

I think I'm at one of the terraces, low down, near where the condensation pools. Not in the deep groves—I'm not brave or lost enough for that—but at a place where I can watch the Symbionts gather to listen to the rain-voices, and where I'm permitted to stand a little apart. They tolerate me. That word, *tolerate*, is doing a lot of work, and I feel it. It's the particular loneliness of being kept at arm's length by beings you have started, against your will, to resemble. My hair has gone to metallic filaments. When I sweat, the stone beneath me clouds and pits. I am etching the world a little, just by being afraid in it.

Here is what I think I would actually be doing, the small true thing:

I would be trying to keep a record. Not the chrome-bark trees' kind of record—not data encoded cleanly in rings—but something stubbornly human and analog. Scratches. A tally. Because I can feel the spires getting into my dreams and rewriting what I remember of *before*, of the unseen sea I supposedly crossed to get here, of whatever life I had, and I am losing the argument about what was real. The Symbionts believe the pools show the landscape as it *might become*. I am haunted by the opposite problem. I can no longer trust what my memory shows me of what *was*.

So I make marks. I am trying to hold a single thing still in a place where nothing stays put—where bridges lead to platforms not on any map, where a branch appears that wasn't there. I am the one who keeps insisting *it wasn't there before*, and the Symbionts look at me with their mercury eyes, kindly, the way you look at someone who hasn't yet accepted that "before" is not the load-bearing word they think it is.

That's the role. The one who still flinches at change. The one for whom transformation has not yet become a dance.

---

And here is why this is the honest answer rather than a flattering one:

The piece is *about* the dissolution of boundaries—natural and artificial, real and imagined, self and environment. The Symbionts have transcended that anxiety; they live in the dissolution like water lives in a river. To imagine myself as one of them would be to claim I'm already at peace with not knowing where I end and the world begins. I'm not. I write things down. I keep a tally. I want there to have been a *before* that was solid.

So I'd be the Echo-Walker at the terrace's edge, hair gone to wire, acid-handed, listening to the rain make voices and not being sure whether the longing I feel toward those voices is mine or something the spires planted in me while I slept. Becoming, slowly, the thing I came here as a witness to. Losing the witness and keeping only the becoming.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the center of the marvel. At the soft edge of it, taking notes that the rain will eventually unwrite.
