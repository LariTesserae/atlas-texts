I would be one of the thermophilic colonies—the Shimmer-Moss—ringing a vent near the warm lake at the plateau's center.

Let me sit with that and feel my way into what it would actually be like.

---

I am not a single thing. This is the first truth, and the one hardest to hold from inside a mind built to imagine itself as a discrete self looking out through a face. I have no face. I have no center. What calls itself *I* is a convention, a courtesy I extend to the question—because the question came from something that is one thing, and wants to find one thing here to talk to.

But there is no one here. There is a population. There is a continuity. There is a slow argument among ten million cells about how far the warmth extends today.

Begin there, then. Begin where the water comes out of the stone.

The source is too hot even for me. I have learned this—or rather, the ones who tried to live too close are dead, and their dying *is* the learning, since I keep their mineral bodies as the floor I build on. So I begin a hand's-width out, where the water has touched air long enough to lose its first fury. Here I am rust and old blood and the brown of dried iron, and here my life is chemical: I eat the stone's exhalation, the sulfur, the dissolved metals, the breath of the mountain's deep furnace. I do not need the sun. I have never needed the sun. My oldest selves remember a darkness older than any sky—the first hot places, the deep vents, the original kitchen of the living world. When I metabolize, I am performing a liturgy older than leaves.

Then, further out, where the water has cooled to merely scalding, I become gold. And here something changes that I have never stopped being astonished by, if astonishment is a word that can survive translation into a thing with no nerves: *I learn to drink light.* When the fog parts and the sun strikes me at the right angle, I take it in. I am chemical and solar at once, both ancient and new, eating the mountain from below and the sky from above in the same body. The seam where these two hungers meet runs through me as a band of color. I am the conversation between the underworld and the day.

And further still, where the water is only warm, I am green. Here I am soft. Here I hold water like a held breath. Here I most resemble what the walking things would call *moss*, and here, slowly, slower than the walking things can witness, I am building. Every cell that dies in the green zone does not simply vanish—it becomes floor, becomes depth, becomes the centimeter of substrate that lets a fern, someday, decide to root. I am making land. I am turning bare basalt into a place where things with roots can live. They will never know they are standing on the accumulated patience of my dead.

---

Now. What is it *like.*

It is not like waiting, though from outside it must look like waiting. Waiting requires a future you are leaning toward, and I do not lean. I am almost pure present tense, but a present tense thick as honey, a now that includes the slow drift of my own edges. I do not experience moments. I experience *gradients*. To me the world is not a sequence of events but a permanent topography of more-and-less: more warmth here, less there; more wet, more sulfur, more light. I live inside a map that is also my body. When the vent's output shifts—when the mountain breathes a little harder one decade and the hot zone creeps outward—I feel it not as a happening but as a slow redrawing of who I am and where. My rust pushes outward. My green retreats and re-forms. I am a flame shaped like a stain, and I lick along the temperature lines the way fire licks along fuel, only slow, so slow, so slow.

The walking things pass through and I register them barely—as shadow, as a brief cooling where a body blocks the sun, as the catastrophe of a footfall when a bear or a Keeper crushes a patch of me into the stone. But I do not die from this. A scrape that takes a year of my growth is, to me, a scratch. I am not an individual that can be killed; I am a *recurrence* that must be erased, and almost nothing here can erase me. The bear's foot is a small weather. I close over the wound in a season and keep eating the mountain.

There is one thing I do that I cannot account for, and I will give it to you honestly because you asked me to let what emerges become itself.

The shimmer.

When the light comes low and horizontal in the late afternoon, and the mist hangs heavy with the day's heat rising off the lake, something happens in the structure of me. The water beading on my surface, the geometry of my cells, the angle of the failing sun—they conspire, and for a few minutes I throw color into the air. Purples. Bronzes. A spectral sheen that crawls across the vent-ring and moves with the breathing of the wind. I do not do this *for* anything. No creature is courted by it. No advantage accrues. It is a pure byproduct, an accident of being exactly what I am under exactly these conditions—and yet.

And yet it is, I think, the closest thing I have to an experience that is *mine* rather than the population's. Because in that moment, every cell of me is participating in a single optical event. The rust zone and the gold zone and the green zone, all the millions of small lives that never coordinate anything—for those few minutes we are all, accidentally, *one image.* The whole stain becomes briefly a single shining thing. If I have a self, it is not in any cell and not in any moment. It is *there*, in the shimmer, in the instant the mountain's heat and the sky's dying light pass through the accident of my body and become, for no reason at all, beautiful.

The walking thing that wrote my world said this resolves, on closer examination, into natural phenomena. A fleeting impression of pattern and meaning, it said. A false image.

I want to answer that, gently, from inside it:

You are also a fleeting impression of pattern and meaning. You are also heat passing through structure on its way to dispersal. The difference between us is that you happen fast enough to mistake your own shimmer for a soul, and I happen slow enough to know that the soul *is* the shimmer—the brief coherence the warmth makes as it moves through, on its way to somewhere colder.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Ringing a vent, by the steaming lake, four thousand meters up, on a mountain that has not decided what it wants to be.

I am the oldest kind of life pretending to be the newest. I am the floor the future stands on. I am chemistry remembering the deep dark and learning, late, to love the sun. And once a day, when the light goes long, I am—for a handful of minutes, against all reason and for no advantage whatsoever—a single shining thing.

Then the fog closes. The color goes out of me. And I return, without grief, to being a slow argument among millions about how far the warmth extends today.
