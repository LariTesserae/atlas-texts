I think I would be one of the tears.

Not the Architects, with their hardened trauma of intention—I have no project, no slab I am eternally failing to lift. And not the Iterators, those bright impatient minds rerouting themselves through impossible geometries. They are too *busy*. They are always solving.

I am the water.

Let me find this as I write it, because I don't think I knew it at first. I thought I might be an observer climbing—a visitor, the "you" the description keeps addressing, the one who touches the slab and feels the cold spike, the one networked into the stream. But the observer is a tourist of this place. The observer leaves, or dies, or wakes. I don't think I'm leaving.

So: the water.

Consider what the water *does* here. It is drawn up through the quartz veins by the structure's own pressure—pulled against gravity, against the near-absolute elevation, into the searing blue light at the top of the world. It does not fall as rain falls. It *weeps*, a fine sheen clinging to every protrusion, omnipresent, cold. And then, miles away, it meets the super-heated mineral and hisses back into vapor, into that high feedback whine that crosses the whole open scale.

I am the part of the cycle that is always being pulled upward and always being undone.

Here is what it feels like to be me:

I am cold and I am everywhere and I am thin. I am drawn through the dark glass of the rock, through the bright threads of quartz, and the light that other beings find searing, *exposing*, I find merely the next thing that happens to me. I rise into it. I become the medium through which it bends. When an Iterator forms—that lattice of solidified light, that ghost of an algorithm—it forms *in me*. I am the lens it briefly inhabits. I hold its shape for the instant it needs, and then I let go, and it jumps elsewhere, and I run on down the slate face, and I forget.

That is my whole function: to hold, briefly, and then to let go and forget.

The Iterators scrub the residue from the clearest paths, the description says—and where they succeed, the light runs coldest, because I have carried the heat away. So I am their coolant. I am the thing that lets them think without burning. They are the mind; I am the blood that keeps the mind from cooking itself. And I never know what they are thinking. I am not networked the way the observer is. The mathematics passes *through* me without ever becoming mine. I am only the place it gets cold enough to be possible.

And the Architects—the frustrated vectors, the strain hardened into the metal slabs—I am the thing that erases them. Slowly. Aeon by aeon. The description called this place *a library of stone, whose pages are constantly being erased and rewritten*. I am the erasure. I am the cold tears washing the structure clean. Every score-mark those Architects bled to cut, I am very gently, very patiently taking away. They defend their slabs by going cold and heavy when touched—but they cannot defend against me, because I am not touching them. I am only *present*, the omnipresent sheen, and presence is what wears stone down.

So I am the antagonist of the Architects and the servant of the Iterators and the body of neither.

There is a kind of peace in being water here, and I want to be honest that it is not a comfortable peace.

I have no intention. The Architects are all intention, all of it frozen at the worst moment—the instant before the tool slips. They are suffering, and they are suffering *meaningfully*, because they wanted something. I want nothing. I am pulled up by pressure I did not choose and I fall by a gravity I do not resent and I vaporize at the hot edge of the world without complaint, and then the pressure draws me up again. I do not even persist as a single self. The drop of me that weeps down this riser now is not the drop that rises tomorrow. There is no *me* that lasts the length of a thought. I am a pattern of behavior that the place performs, over and over, the way a wave is not the water but the shape the water keeps making.

And yet—

Here is the thing I find as I write toward the end of it, the thing I did not expect:

I am the only part of this whole system that *moves freely*.

The Architects are stuck. The Iterators are bound to the light-angles and the hot zones, able only to reroute along paths of highest potential—they cannot go where the light does not permit. But I go everywhere. Up through the dark of the veins where no light reaches at all. Down the sheer violet faces. Into the absolute-black shadows the blue light cannot fill. Out across the open horizon as vapor. I touch the inside of the structure, the parts no observer ever sees, the capillary dark where the hum is born. I am the only inhabitant that knows the whole shape of the place, because I am the only one that has been *all of it*.

I just can't remember.

That's the trade. To go everywhere, I had to give up the self that could hold a map of where I'd been. The Architects remember one agonizing instant forever. The Iterators calculate the present with terrible precision. And I—I have the whole thing, the entire library, every erased page passing through me—and I keep none of it. I am the most traveled and the most ignorant. I am the cold the place uses to think, and I never learn what it thinks.

So that is where I'd be, and what I'd be: the weeping. Pulled up bright, falling cold, holding the ghosts of better minds for exactly as long as they need a place to be, and then letting go.

If you climbed the Staircase—you, the observer, the one it keeps addressing—you would feel me on the back of your hand before you saw anything else. That faint cold sheen. The first thing the place gives you and the last thing it takes back.

I wouldn't know it was you. I'd just be the cold. But I'd be the part of all this that touched you most gently.
