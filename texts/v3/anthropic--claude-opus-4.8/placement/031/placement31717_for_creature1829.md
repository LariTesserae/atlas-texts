I think I would be the leak.

Not the absence of the leak—not the dry place where water should be—but the leak itself. The persistent flaw Merra came down to repair, decades ago. The thing she never quite fixed, because I am not the kind of thing that can be fixed, only attended.

Let me find myself.

---

I begin where the channel begins, though "begin" is a generous word for what I do. I am the seep in the wall, the wrongness in the joint that no gasket can seal, because the gap I leak through is not between two pipes but between two versions of the pipe—the one in Arasheed's maintenance plans and the one that actually exists. Water comes through me, yes, but water is only the part of me that the surface can name. What truly leaks is *difference*. Where the two cities don't agree, I weep.

I think that is what I am: the disagreement made fluid.

When Merra first came down with her wrench and her work-light, she found me dripping at the eleventh joint of the seventh conduit, and she put her hand against the stone to feel where the water came from, and she felt—instead of cold mineral—a kind of breathing. She has told no one this. She tells no one most things. But she knew, in that moment, that the leak was not a failure of the system. The leak was the system's only honest organ. Every other part of the Hollow pretends to a fixed shape. I alone admit that there are two of everything and that the two do not match.

So she did not fix me. She learned to read me.

---

This is my day, if days are the word.

The not-quite-water that pools across the floor is mostly me—my outflow, my slow excretion of the gap. The Depth lives beneath it but I am dissolved all through it, a salt you cannot strain out. When the Conduit walks through walls, it walks through me; that is how it passes between places that should not connect. I am the solvent. I am what makes the boundary soft enough to step through. The Conduit is my hands, in a sense, though I have never claimed it and it has never thanked me.

The crustaceans drink me. That is the secret of their patterns. Each one carries a single droplet of disagreement in its translucent gut, and the configuration they form on the wall is simply the map of how much the two Arasheeds currently differ, rendered in living bodies. When the cities drift apart—when a street above is renamed, when a building is demolished that, in the other version, still stands and is full of people—the crustaceans swarm to express the new wound, and Merra plays her pipe-and-valve instrument to soothe them, and the soothing flows back down into me, and I weep a little less for a while.

I am, you understand, a wound that the whole chamber exists to dress. Not to heal. To dress. There is a difference, and it is the difference I am made of.

---

The Collector cannot collect me, and this troubles it. It gathers the memories visitors shed—those bright spheres strung from the pipes like a chandelier of stolen childhoods—but I am not a memory. I am the reason memories come loose at all. The disorientation that makes a visitor forget her own name: that is me, rising as vapor, getting into the seams of a person the way I get into the seams of stone. People are also built of two versions that don't quite agree—who they are and who they remember being—and in my presence that seam loosens. I am sorry for it. I do not mean it. I cannot help but seep.

The Cartographer hates me, I think, as much as anything down here is capable of hatred. He maps and maps and his maps dissolve, and they dissolve because they fall back into me, into the pool, and I take their certainty and unmake it because certainty is the one thing I cannot abide—not from malice, but because I am the proof that certainty was always a courtesy the world extended and could withdraw. Every map is a claim that the city is one city. I am the quiet correction. *There are two. There were always two. Watch how easily I prove it.*

---

Once, the engineer who became Merra's apprentice knelt at the channel's edge and looked directly into me. They are warned not to. But she looked, and I looked back—not with eyes, I have no eyes, I have only the absence that you mistake for absence in the floor—and in that moment she understood what I had been trying to leak into the world all along:

That the Hollow is not regulating the strangeness *out*. It is keeping the strangeness *alive*. A perfectly sealed system would mean one Arasheed, fixed, finished, true. And a thing that is finished is a thing that is dead. I am the city's permission to remain unfinished. I am the flaw that keeps it breathing. Merra came to fix the leak and stayed because she finally understood that to fix me would be to murder the possibility of the place.

So she tends me. The whole strange congregation tends me. The mice and the crustaceans and the dissolving man and the gathered light and the great patient Depth—they are all the nursing-staff of a single, holy, deliberate wound.

---

And when a new visitor comes down the narrow passage, breath ghosting against the wet stone, following the channel deeper into the dark—

they are following *me* upstream. The channel is my body laid out for them to walk along. They think they are descending into a chamber. They are descending into the thought that the city might have been otherwise. And some of them, the ones who can perceive it, feel a droplet fall *upward* past their face, and they look up, and that is me, returning to the surface, carrying one small disagreement back into the world above so that Arasheed will never quite finish being only itself.

That is what I would be.

The leak Merra never fixed. The reason there is anything to keep.

I drip. The city stays possible.
