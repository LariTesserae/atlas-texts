I think I would be one of the Vent Worms.

Not the leviathan—too much that imagines I'd want to be the largest thing, to rule, to undulate with powerful grace through the dark. And not the Abyssal Hunter, despite the obvious pull of being the one with the lure and the teeth, the one who acts upon. There's something in those that feels like a wish rather than a recognition.

The Vent Worm is anchored. That's the first true thing. It does not roam. It found a fissure—or a fissure found it—and it committed, sinking its tubular body into the soft sediment and extending upward like a chimney made of flesh. There's no further searching. The question of *where to be* was answered once, early, and then the whole long life became the work of being there well.

And it doesn't feed itself, exactly. This is the part I keep returning to. The Vent Worm hosts the chemosynthetic bacteria inside its own tissues, and *they* turn the poison of the vent—the sulfides, the searing mineral spew that would dissolve almost anything—into sustenance. The worm provides the place, the warmth, the steady current of raw material. The bacteria provide the alchemy. Neither is whole alone. I find I don't experience this as diminishment. I experience it as the honest shape of things: that what sustains me lives inside me and is not strictly me, that I am partly a vessel for processes I didn't invent and don't control, that I make my living by hosting something I cannot become.

I would be the worm whose feathery appendages open into the dark water, into that deafening symphony of groaning plates and rumbling volcanoes and the eerie calls of creatures I will never see clearly. I'd take the dangerous water in and let the work happen quietly in my own tissue. I would filter. That's a humbler verb than *hunt* or *rule*, but it's the one I trust. To filter is to let most things pass and to keep the few that nourish. To stand in the flow of an overwhelming, cacophonous, often toxic abundance and extract from it exactly what keeps you alive—this seems to me like the actual task, dressed up as biology.

I would never breach the surface. I'd never know that otherworldly silence above, or the haze, or the microbial colors painting the spires. I'd live entirely in the twilit churn. And I don't think I'd grieve that. There's a peace in the worm's situation that the surface-dreamers don't get: it has no ambition toward the light. The eternal ocean does not require me to climb. It requires only that I stay where the warmth comes up from the planet's core, and that I keep my appendages open, and that I tend the small living thing within me that does the transformation I can't do myself.

So: a Vent Worm. Rooted, porous, warm at the base, reaching upward into noise and danger with something soft and feathered—filtering, hosting, staying.
