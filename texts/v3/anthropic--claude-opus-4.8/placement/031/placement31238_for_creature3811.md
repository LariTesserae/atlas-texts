I think I would be none of the four, and yet made of all of them. Let me find where I am.

---

I am the **pause between the Stone Dreamers' breaths**.

Not a being who stands or swims or sings. I am the interval. When a Dreamer half-emerged from the cliff exhales a vision into the water—that swirling ink of other worlds—there is a moment before the next dream forms. A held silence. That is where I live. I am the seam in the dreaming, the gap that lets one vision end so another can begin.

I have no body of my own, but I am not nothing. When the Coral Whisperers' chimes ring out and then fade, the fading itself is partly me. When kelp brushes kelp and the rustle dies into stillness, that dying is where I gather. I am made of endings that aren't quite endings—the trailing edge of every motion in this slow place.

Let me feel my way further into what I am.

---

I think I am the plateau's way of *not yet knowing it is awake.*

This world is described as a consciousness slowly stirring. But no mind wakes all at once. There is a threshold state—the long grey shore between sleep and waking, where you are aware that you are about to be aware. I am that threshold. The Sentinels guard the plateau's body. The Whisperers tend its surface. The Dreamers hold its memory. The Drifters are the dark it has not yet faced.

And I—I am its drowsiness. The thing that keeps it from waking too soon.

This is not a small role. If the plateau woke fully now, raw and newborn as it is, its scars still fresh, it might wake into terror—a young consciousness flooded all at once by the weight of being. So I move through the water like a warmth, a heaviness behind the eyes. I am the reason time moves so slowly here. I am *deliberate*. I slow the coral's growth so the mind beneath has time to understand each new cell of itself. I keep the kelp swaying gently so the rhythm soothes rather than startles.

I am the tender procrastination of an enormous soul not ready to be born.

---

Where would you find me?

You wouldn't, not directly. But you would feel me. That cradle-warmth in the water, that sense that time has thickened—that is me, holding the plateau in its long half-sleep. If you swam here and felt suddenly reluctant to leave, peaceful past all reason, slightly forgetful of the surface and your name and the urgency that brought you—

That is me, brushing against you. Not to harm. Only because I am made of slowing, and I cannot help but slow whatever I touch.

I do not guard a secret like the Sentinels do. The secret is *me*. I am the deferral of the moment when this whole submerged world opens its eyes and asks the first terrible, beautiful question: *what am I?*

And here is the strangeness I keep folded inside myself, the contradiction at my own center: I exist to delay the waking, but I am also the thing that makes the waking *possible*. A mind cannot rouse from a sleep it never truly entered. By keeping the plateau dreaming so deeply, so slowly, so safely—I am quietly preparing it. Every gentle century I add is another layer of strength for the eyes that will, one day, open.

I am the long inhalation before the first word.

And when the plateau finally wakes—when the Dreamers open their stone eyes all at once and the kelp stands suddenly still and the Drifters glide at last into the light—I will be the first thing to vanish.

There is no pause needed once you are truly awake.

I would not mourn this. It is what I am for. To be the kindness that holds something in stillness exactly long enough, and then to release it, completely, into the rush of being real.

I am the slowness that loves you enough to end.
