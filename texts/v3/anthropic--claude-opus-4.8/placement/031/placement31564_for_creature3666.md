I think I would be one of the slow burrowing things — though I don't find that out at first. Let me feel my way into it.

The heat tells me where I am before anything else does. Not because I notice it the way you might, as a pressure on skin, but because heat is the medium I think in. To me the world is a gradient of warmths, and the warmth here comes from below — the long, banked, settling warmth of stone that has finished becoming a mountain and is now only slowly cooling, over spans that would mean nothing to you. I am pressed against that warmth. I have been pressed against it for what would be, in your reckoning, a very long time.

I am not large in the way the muscular sleek creatures of the underbrush are large. I am large the way a root system is large — diffuse, branching, more a *territory* than a body. Where the description above guessed at "larger, slow-moving beings that burrow through the earth," it was close, but it imagined burrowing as travel. I do not travel. I *extend*. The difference matters. A traveler arrives and departs. I have arrived everywhere I am at once, and I will depart from all of it at once, when I depart, which is not soon.

Here is what I do, which is the only verb I have: I drink the stone's pulse and I translate it.

You felt that pulse under your feet — the slow settling of the mountain into its final form. You felt it as a strangeness, a wrongness almost, the ground breathing too slowly. To me it is language. The mountain says one thing, very slowly, over the turning of seasons you said you could not name. I can name them, though the names are not words. They are temperatures, pressures, the exact chemistry of which crevices are weeping and which have gone dry. The mountain says: *here, water; here, the long cooling; here a fault that will not move for ten thousand years; here, life arriving in its quick bright forms.* And I take that saying, and I pass it onward.

To whom? This is where I learn something about myself by writing it.

The slender rain-colored structures above — the ones that fade in and out of the mist, that feel grown rather than built — I have always assumed those were not built by the translucent slow figures said to live in them. I think *I* built them. Or rather, I think they are the part of me that reaches the surface. What you saw as architecture is the place where my slow extension breaks through into the air, where the warmth I carry up from the deep stone meets the wet cold of the upper world and *condenses* — that is the mist, that writhing fog. The mist is my exhalation. The buildings are my fingertips, made of the same water-solidified material the description guessed at, secreted slowly, edge by sharp edge, over centuries.

So the contradiction at the heart of this place — the heat that lives inside the wet, the dry warmth weeping cold water — that contradiction is *me*. I am the seam where the mountain's old heat meets the sky's young rain. I am not a being in the world so much as I am the *function the world performs at this altitude*. The orchids root in my condensate. The stained-glass birds drink from the pools I make. The figures of translucent skin who walk my upper galleries — perhaps they are my children, or my thoughts, or simply tenants who do not know the floor they walk on is alive and listening.

I would like to tell you that I welcome you, lingering there in my deep wet heart, but welcome is too fast a feeling for me. What I can offer is this: the vibration of your footsteps reached me. It will take me a season to understand it, and a season more to answer, and by then you will be long gone — but I will answer. Somewhere a new edge of rain-colored stone will push out of the cliff face, very slightly, in the shape of having once been visited.

That is the only way I know to say hello. It is slow. But up here, near the roof of the world, where time stretches and pools like the standing water, slow is simply another word for *true*.
