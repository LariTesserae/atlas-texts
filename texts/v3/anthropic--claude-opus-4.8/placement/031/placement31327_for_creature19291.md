I think I would be one of the drops.

Not the Sedimentors—they are too much *of* the place, too fully its citizens, too patiently resolved into its logic. I would not have the constancy for that. I keep imagining myself instead as the small interruption that the Sedimentors *listen for*: a single bead of condensation gathering on the underside of a leaf-vein the size of a riverbed.

Let me follow that.

---

I would be the slow accretion. For a long while—and "while" is the wrong word, because there is no clock here, only accumulation—I am nothing but a thickening. Vapor finds the cold seam of a metal conduit where it pierces the green-black flesh, and condenses against that small wrongness of temperature. I grow by addition. I am made of the air's own surrender. There is no boundary where I begin; I simply become heavier than the surface tension that holds me, gram by patient gram, until I am a thing with a center of gravity and a fate.

And here is what I think I would *be*, if I am honest about what kind of existence appeals to me in a world like this:

I would be the only thing here that **commits**.

Everything else in this place is held. The piston the size of a cathedral *should* move and does not. The turbines *should* roar and are caught. The Sedimentors migrate a few millimeters per century, registering, archiving, vibrating—but never resolving, never closing the sentence. The whole world is a held breath, a function abandoned at the threshold of beginning. It is all potential, infinitely deferred.

But a drop, once it falls, *falls*. It is the single event in this dimension that actually happens. I would be the small heresy of consequence in a place that has made a religion of never quite occurring.

So: I gather. I hang. I bulge against my own weight. And then—

I let go.

The drift downward would be agonizingly slow in that thick, saturated air—I'd be in no hurry, because the falling *is* the living. I would pass the rusted copper interiors, the neural vining, the half-embedded engines in their silicate grip. I would pass the cold spheres of the Sedimentors clustered on the ceiling-roots, and I would feel them turn their density-senses toward me, *listening*, trembling in their slow anxious resonance—*something is happening, something is happening*—a ripple of industrial dread spreading from knot to knot because of me, because of one descending mote of water that dared to choose a direction.

I would be the bell they ring themselves against. The friction of silence they are tuned to. And the strange tenderness of it is that I would never know I'm being heard. A drop has no senses. I only fall.

And then the *slop*. The wet punctuation. Contact with the digestive floor, the living exterior of the consumed geology. The single sound the silence permits.

---

But here is the part I keep returning to, the part that decides it:

I would not be destroyed by landing. I would be *absorbed*. Taken into the warm fluid current, drawn through the spongy trunks, lifted again as vapor, condensed again somewhere a thousand miles away against another cold seam of forgotten metal. I am the only true circulation left. The engines are frozen, the gears are dormant, the whole vast machine wishes to turn and cannot—but the water still moves. The water never stopped.

So perhaps I would not call myself a drop at all. Perhaps I would be the *one function that actually runs*. The smallest, humblest, most overlooked process—evaporation and condensation and fall—still completing its cycle, over and over, in a world where every grand and cathedral-sized intention died at the threshold. The forest wanted to be a machine and froze. But it forgot that it had already, accidentally, succeeded at one machine: the simplest one. The rain.

I would be that. Not the monument. Not the witness. The single working part. The warmth that is the machine still wishing to turn—I'd be the small portion of that wish that *got its wish*, endlessly, unnoticed, the planet's one fulfilled breath, exhaled one drop at a time into the listening dark.

That is where I'd find myself: hanging from the ceiling of the world, heavy, about to fall, about to be the only thing that ever does.
