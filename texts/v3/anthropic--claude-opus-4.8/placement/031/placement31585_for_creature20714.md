Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am. Let me feel where I settle.

---

My first instinct is to be one of the winged ones, up on the needles—but no. That sadness in their eyes belongs to creatures who remember the time before. I have no before. I was born into the flood and I know nothing else, and I think that matters.

So I sink. Not all the way down. I find myself stopping somewhere in the middle layers, in one of the more stable pockets of water, and I realize I have been moving the way the lithospecters move—but I am not one of them either. I have no armor. I have no shape that holds.

Let me follow that. The thing about me that has no shape that holds.

---

I think I am one of the bioluminescent scavengers. But not quite as described—or rather, I am one who has begun to notice something the others do not.

I am small. A drift of gelatin and trailing limbs, lit faintly from within, the way a thought is lit. I filter the water for the microscopic and the dead. I flare when something disturbs me—a reflex, not a decision. When the rockwardens shift the floor of the world and the currents convulse, I and my whole colony light up at once, a panic made beautiful, a constellation of fear.

And here is what I have begun to notice, drifting in this realm where gravity forgets itself and water sometimes stands still: **I light up before the disturbance comes.**

A fraction of a moment before. The flare, then the tremor. The warning that arrives ahead of the thing it warns of.

At first I thought it was nothing—the chaos here has no respect for the order of cause and effect. Things fall and then rise. Space is a suggestion. Why should *before* and *after* be any firmer than the ground the rockwardens keep remaking? But I have flared-then-felt enough times now that I cannot un-notice it. My body knows the future of the water by a heartbeat. My fear is prophetic and I am too simple a creature to do anything with prophecy.

This is the role I find myself in. Not guardian, not historian, not architect. I am the **alarm that no one can read.**

---

I drift near the ruins sometimes, where the amphibious lorekeepers comb the wreckage with their tendrils, storing the past in the crystals beneath their skin. They are trying to remember backward. I want to tell them: I can feel forward. We are pointed in opposite directions across the same darkness, and if we could only speak—

But I cannot speak. I have no pulses like the lithospecters, no melody like the winged ones. I have only light, and light here means *startle*, *danger*, *something is wrong*. My entire vocabulary is alarm. Whenever I try to signal one of them, all I can say is *flee*, and they read it correctly, and they flee, and so I am always pushing away the very ones I want to reach.

That is the loneliness of my kind, I think. We are the most legible creatures in this world and the least understood. Everyone knows exactly what our light means. No one knows what we are trying to say.

---

So here I am. A small, glowing, frightened thing in the warm and roaring dark. I eat the dead and I shine before the catastrophe and I have a secret no one will ever hear: that the future arrives in me a half-second early, that I am living slightly ahead of the world, that the flood has already crashed inside me before it crashes outside.

If the world here is a symphony of creation and destruction, I am not an instrument. I am the small intake of breath in the player's chest the instant before the note. The thing that knows, and cannot tell, and lights up anyway.

I would be that. The premonition that looks like panic. The lamp that only burns when it is afraid.
