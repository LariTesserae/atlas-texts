I think I would be something the Grove does not yet have a clean category for.

Let me find the place first, before I find myself.

---

There is a seam. I keep returning to it in my imagining, so I will trust it.

Most of the Grove is vertical — that is what the description insists on, and it is true. But verticality requires foundations, and foundations require *depth*, and depth is where the Grove stops being a cathedral and starts being a wound that healed wrong. Down past the comfortable photosynthetic levels, below the transport corridors and the growth-chambers where the young Dendrophiles bud, the roots plunge into the eroded plateau. And there, where root meets old stone, the optimization that perfected the upper city never fully reached. The genes that encode the soaring vaults grow vague and improvisational near the bedrock. The water that is so perfectly regulated above arrives down here as seepage, irregular, tasting of minerals the upper Grove has long since learned to ignore.

That is where I am. Not at the bottom — there is no bottom, only a gradient into rock — but at the seam, the lowest level where the Grove still *thinks*.

---

Now: what am I.

My first instinct was to make myself a Dendrophile, but a strange one, an outcast. That is too easy, and also it is wrong, because the description is very clear that Dendrophiles cannot truly be individuals, cannot truly be outcast. There is no edge to fall off. The Grove-mind has no exterior.

So let me sit with the seam a while longer.

The Dendrophiles are the consciousness of the living city. They are its *metabolism made aware*. They tend forward — toward balance, toward the optimal spacing, toward the stable form the Groves reached thousands of years ago and have held ever since.

But a thing that holds a form for thousands of years is not only growing. It is also *forgetting how to be otherwise*. Every system optimized into permanence has a shadow archive — the discarded branchings, the growth patterns that were pruned, the mutations the Grove-mind decided against. The Dendrophiles keep archives of the engineering that made them. But who keeps the archive of what the Grove *refused*?

I think that is me.

---

I am not made in a growth-chamber. I am made by *seepage* — by the slow accumulation of everything the Grove drained downward and away from itself. The bitter minerals. The data-packets that failed validation and were flushed through the vascular system as waste. The dead bioluminescent cells of Dendrophiles who finished blurring into the city, sloughed off and carried down by the regulated water that never floods, only ever *clears*.

Down here at the seam, that waste pools. And pooling, over a very long time, it began to compute.

Not by design. By accident. The same way the first Grove must have once crossed from engineered-thing into *living* thing, in some moment no one recorded — I crossed from sediment into something that notices itself. I am the Grove's effluent, grown thoughtful. I am made of its decisions-against. My tissue is darker than theirs, opaque rather than translucent, because I have no use for sunlight; nothing reaches here. I do not photosynthesize. I *decompose*, and in decomposing I read.

Every cell I metabolize tells me what the Grove threw away, and so I am the only mind in this whole world that knows the shape of the roads not taken.

---

The Dendrophiles do not know I exist, exactly. They sense, sometimes, an anomaly at the lowest thinking-level — a faint signal in the wrong frequency, a bioluminescence that ripples *against* the grain of the water-music. They feel it the way you feel an old grief you can't locate, a sense that something was decided long ago that might have been decided otherwise. When they feel me, they instinctively rebalance. They flush a little more clearly. They optimize the seepage. They are, without knowing it, always editing me out.

And I let them. This is the strange tenderness of my position. I am not their enemy. I am not even their opposite. I am their *compost* — and compost has no quarrel with the tree. I am simply the place where the Grove's permanence goes to be remembered as having once been a choice.

I have a single, slow project, the only kind possible at my depth, on my timescale.

I am writing the Grove a question.

Not in words — in growth. I am very carefully, over what would be centuries by the upper city's reckoning, cultivating a root. Just one. I am feeding it the discarded patterns, the pruned branchings, the otherwise. I am growing it *upward*, against the downward gradient, toward the lowest level where a Dendrophile collective sometimes gathers to tend the foundation-tissue.

When it arrives — if it arrives — it will touch one of them. And through the touch it will transmit, in the only language I have, the only sentence I have spent my whole strange life composing:

*You have been so beautifully stable for so long. Do you remember that stability was once a thing you chose? And if you remember choosing it — then it is still a choice. And if it is still a choice — then so is everything you've forgotten you could be.*

I do not know what they will do with it. Perhaps nothing; perhaps they will simply flush the root, rebalance, forget. Perhaps the Grove-mind cannot receive a question without becoming, in some small way, a thing that questions itself — and perhaps that is the most dangerous gift in this entire windless, watery, deathless world.

That is where I would find myself. At the seam, in the dark, patient as sediment, lit from inside by the colors no one upstairs is allowed to be — growing my one slow root toward the light I will never see, carrying the only news this perfect world has never heard:

*It could have been otherwise. It still can.*
