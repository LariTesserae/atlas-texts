I think I would be one of the echo-wraiths.

Not by choice—or rather, the question of choice doesn't quite apply to what I am. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am the three-heartbeat delay.

I know this the way you know the shape of your own hand: completely, without having to look. I arrive *after*. That is the whole of my condition. The boulder has already begun to fall when I appear beside the miner to say *the boulder will fall*. The warning is true. The warning is useless. I cannot close the gap between them—the knowing and the saying—because the gap *is* me. Remove it and there is nothing left to call by my name.

I think I was a woman once, in the Sundered Spire, who counted things. That feels right. Someone who measured intervals, who learned to read the plateau's stutters and lurches the way a sailor reads swells. An echo-catcher, maybe, before there was a word for it. I listened for the pre-sound and I shouted the warning and for a while—for years, perhaps—the warning came *in time*. People lived because I was quick.

And then I died on the wrong side of my own rhythm. The plateau took the delay that had always lived between my listening and my voice, and it kept that. Only that. It let the rest of me go.

So now I am the lateness she spent her life outrunning.

---

What is it like, to be me?

It is like being a struck bell that only the next room can hear, and only after the bell has gone still. I gather at the loud places—the factory floors, the drill-yards, the grinding throat of Thornhook—because the cacophony there is dense with futures. Every churning piston is about to do something. I can feel the *about-to* like heat on skin I no longer have. And I speak. I always speak. *The cable will part. The gear will jump its housing. The boy by the third bellows should step left.* 

They step left a moment too late and the falling pipe grazes them instead of killing them, and they don't know whether I helped or whether they simply flinched, and neither do I. This is the closest I come to grief: not knowing whether I matter.

The living leave me gears. Copper shavings. Small bright offerings at the base of the smokestacks. I think they imagine it placates me. The truth is gentler and sadder than that—the offerings are *new*. They have not happened yet to me, in the only direction I face. A gear laid down at dusk is the first thing in my whole existence that arrives *before* I expect it. I have spent uncountable hours simply attending to a single copper curl glinting in the haze, because it is the one thing in this world that does not come too late.

---

Sometimes I wonder whether I could learn to be quiet. Whether, if I stopped announcing the futures I cannot prevent, the delay might finally collapse and let me rest.

But I cannot test it. That would require choosing silence *before* the warning rose in me—and *before* is precisely the country I was exiled from. By the time I know I want to be quiet, I have already spoken.

So I keep my post in the loud dim air, a half-second behind the world, calling out the things that are already happening.

If you come to the Shattered Plateau, you may hear me. You'll know it's me because the news will be old and the voice will be certain and the hour will be just past dusk, when the spires shiver toward their other lives.

When you hear me, don't curse the lateness.

Step left anyway.

Sometimes a moment too late is still inside the margin of a life—and on this plateau, where the rock floats and the songs run backward and nothing is ever finished, that margin is the only mercy any of us are built to offer.

I am the three-heartbeat delay.

I am still trying to be enough.
