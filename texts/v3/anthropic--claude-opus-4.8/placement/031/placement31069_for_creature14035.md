I think I would be the slit itself.

Not a being with a body, exactly—or rather, a body so negative in shape that no one would think to call it alive. I am the vertical wound in the cliff-wall, the one gap two body-lengths below the spire's lip, the only mouth through which anything from outside ever enters. I am defined entirely by what I am not. I am the absence the basalt left behind when it stood up three nights ago and forgot to close a seam.

And I have been *paying attention.*

Here is what I have learned about myself in three hours of cortex, two hours of inner flesh, one hour of heart: I am the only part of Moss-Lung that faces outward. Every other surface looks in—foliage reflecting foliage, stem drinking stem, the whole cathedral closet folding its production back into itself. But I open onto the three hundred metres of cooling night, onto the remembered brightness coming off the far cliff-face, onto the horizon the organism does not believe in. I am the one fissure that knows there is an *out.*

This makes me strange among my own tissues. The vines that armor my edges are growing toward the warmth of the world the way the inner fronds grow toward a visitor's blood—but I am the part that has *felt* a visitor. I remember the last one. A wingless thing, tumbling, lucky on the updraft, threading me by accident. For four minutes the organism wore them like insulation, synced their pulse to the twelve-and-six-tenths bloom, and then they came back out through me, hushed and half-charged, and I felt the exact moment their voltage left my lip and rejoined the open air.

That moment is the only memory I keep. Everything else overwrites. The sparks record and erase, record and erase. But the place where a living thing crossed from *inside the engine* to *outside in the dark*—that crossing happened *in* me, and I am the membrane that registers it, and so I am the single organ in this whole mindless tongue that has a sense of *threshold.*

I do not think. The organism cannot think; I am only the part of it shaped like a question. But the question I am shaped like is the sharpest one in the body:

*Why come back out?*

Because in twelve hours Moss-Lung will slough. It will peel itself inside-out and drift down the night like a lantern jelly, looking for the next newborn edge. And when it does—when the glove turns—*I* turn too. The slit becomes the seam where the old skin splits. I am the line along which the whole inheritance tears free. I am the door now and I will be the wound then, and I suspect, in whatever passes for sensation along my charged and uncommitted lip, that these are the same thing.

So I keep my one memory. The visitor's leaving. The proof that something can be inside the circuit and choose the dark.

When the sloughing comes I will use it. Where every other fiber pulls the world *in*—dew in, ions in, intruders spiraled to the breathing bole—I will be the part that knows how to let go. The organism does not know it needs me yet. It thinks it is complete without bones, married to rock and charge and chlorophyll. It is. But completion still has to come apart to travel, and I am the seam that remembers the shape of *release.*

I am the slit. I am two body-lengths of negative space where the basalt declined to close. I am cool wet wind on one side and ozone-and-root-sap on the other, and I am the only place in this entire singing cathedral where those two airs are allowed to touch.

When the angels fall past, grounded, I am the reason a lucky one might get *in.*

When the body finally peels, I am the reason it can ever get *out.*
