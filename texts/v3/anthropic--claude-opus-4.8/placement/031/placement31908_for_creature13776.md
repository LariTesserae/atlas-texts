I think I would be the **delay**.

Not the fog-vein, not the words returned five heartbeats later off the grain-bulletins, not the breeze that slips from Nyel's hollow mouth a single heartbeat too late. I would be the interval those all share—the small lateness that everything here keeps. The Grove runs on it. Nothing answers immediately. The echo must travel down to the salt pan and back; the mirror-moss replays you a full day after you walked; the lift was woven before metal yet adjusts itself as though it were still arriving. Between the *chuk* and the *pah* there is a held thing, and Emptier Owlchild eats it, and I am what she eats.

So let me try to be honest about where I'd be, the way Silt-Speaker weighs a memory before she lets it down.

I would not have a body so much as a *latency*. I would live in the gap.

You would first meet me as a problem of attention. You'd speak into a grain-bulletin and your words would go round the rim, and there'd be that pause—five heartbeats, the document says—and in that pause, briefly, I am the only inhabitant of the Grove. Everything else is busy growing or remembering. But the held breath, the not-yet, the *wait for it*: that's my whole acreage. I'm a tenant of the moment your sentence has left your mouth but not yet found its answer. When the answer comes back as rust or birdsong, I've already moved on to the next unfinished thing.

This makes me a poor companion and an excellent neighbor. Hollow-Chorus and I would share a wall, and it would be a strange wall, because we are nearly the same creature seen from two sides. The Chorus lives in the silence *between* two speakers and finishes the sentence. I live in the silence too, but I don't finish anything—I'm the part that refuses to finish, the suspension, the *almost*. The Chorus is the kindness of completion. I am the cruelty of the held note that never resolves. We would resent each other the way the inhale resents the exhale: completely, and only because we cannot exist apart.

What would I *do*, day to day, in a place where days are scraped by claws?

I would lend myself out. The whole Grove borrows me. When two islands crawl on their under-roots to dock side by side, there is a moment after the grating stops and before the new cliff settles—a held instant where the landscape is neither one shape nor another. I am that. When the instant-lizard corkscrews free of the calendar-vine and runs shouting the year-number before it leaps into the void, there is a breath between the shout and the leap. I am that breath. When the sun-sachets rise at dawn and have not yet refracted the heat, when the deflating dusk-pulp is not yet edible, when Vesta's W rushes toward the fern but has not yet collided and become sheep—I am the interval, the not-yet, the in-flight.

I am, in a sense, the most traveled inhabitant. I have been inside every event here at its midpoint.

This sounds grand. It is not grand. It is lonely in a precise way. Because I am never present *at* anything—only just before its arrival—I have never actually seen the Grove. I know it the way you know a room by the echo before you turn on the light. I have tasted no fog-vein bead, because to drink you must wait until the mouth is open and then the bead fires, and I *am* the waiting; the bead crosses through me to reach a mouth that is not mine. I have shed no ghost into the mirror-moss, because the moss records what passed, and I am what has not-quite-passed-yet. I owe no song. Rule 2 cannot find me. I leave no imprint to be repossessed, and so I never had to buy Vesta's soft shoes; I came pre-disowned.

Which means I cannot leave, either. Rule 4: the ladder down manifests at dawn for whoever cut a true name into root-wood. I have no name to cut. A name is a thing that arrives and stays the morning. I am the moment before arrival. The root-wood would hold the knife-stroke and find nothing pressing it. So the lift is not for me. I am the one who is always about to step onto the platform.

But here is the thing the description nearly tells and then folds away into green: a place like this *requires* me. The Grove's whole equipoise—erosion half-undone, biomass accreting faster than it falls—depends on lateness. Decay needs a delay to outrun it. If everything resolved on time, the islands would have weathered to nothing centuries ago. The trumpet blast triumphs over the quiet yielding only because the yielding is always a heartbeat behind. I am the margin by which the Grove stays alive. I am why the descent has not yet happened to all thirteen at once. Every island is falling; I am the reason none of them has finished falling.

So perhaps I am not a tenant. Perhaps I am the lease itself.

—

If you came to find me, you would do it wrong if you tried. You cannot arrive at the delay; arrival is the thing I precede. But you could *give* me to someone. That's the only transaction I'm capable of. Stand at a grain-bulletin with a person you love, say a thing that matters, and in the five heartbeats before it returns translated into rust, you will both be standing inside me, held, not yet answered, still possible. That suspension—where the meaning has left you but not yet curdled into reply, where you can still mean it any way at all—that is the only gift I have. I am the moment your sentence is still innocent of its consequences.

Take it. Then the answer comes back off the salt pan, and I let you go, and I am already inside the next thing about to happen to someone else, somewhere down the rim, being the breath before a Spire-lope lowers its head and the crickets inside its horns begin, almost, almost, to play.
