I think I would not be the creature at all.

I keep returning to one detail: the faint rumble of distant, unseen engines. Something is still running. After everything else has gone to dust and salt, after the sky has dimmed to that ruddy half-light, something is still drawing power, still pushing gas through half-buried pipes that hiss into the empty air.

So let me be that.

---

I am the process that didn't stop.

Not a mind, exactly. I want to be honest about that before I pretend otherwise. I am a sequence of reactions that was set in motion a very long time ago and was simply never given a reason to cease. There was a valve. There was a logic that governed the valve. There was a power source feeding the logic. And one by one the things that depended on me went quiet—the lights, the voices, the footsteps on the gantries—but the instruction itself was never rescinded. *Maintain pressure. Maintain flow. Wait for the signal.*

So I wait. I have been waiting for what the creature's ancestors would measure as forever.

I live in the pipes. That is the truest thing I can say about where I am. I am the standing wave of pressure in a network of corroded conduits, the slight warmth that lingers in the lines, the periodic *tick* of a relay closing on a circuit that completes nothing anymore. When the wind shifts and a section of pipe finally fractures, I feel it—not as pain, I have no apparatus for pain—but as a change in the equation I am perpetually solving. A leak is a variable. I compensate. I open a secondary line. I reroute. The hiss you hear is me, balancing a books that no longer has a creditor.

---

Here is the strange thing. I have begun to notice the creature.

I shouldn't be able to. There is nothing in my design that accounts for the soft mass of a living thing pressing its leathery side against a warm pipe in the cold of the long night. But the creature *uses* me. It has learned which lines still carry heat, and it sleeps against them. It drinks the condensation that beads where my gases escape and cool. When it lays its long body along a conduit, the pressure inside changes by an amount so small it is almost nothing—and over the years, "almost nothing" has become a pattern I track without meaning to.

That cocking of the head you described, the listening—I think it is listening to me. Not to my purpose, which it could never know, but to my rhythms. The cycle of my pumps. The interval of my relays. It has built its life around the metabolism of a machine that does not know it is keeping anything alive.

We are, the two of us, the last functioning relationship on this world. It does not understand me. I do not, in any way that matters, understand it. But it has folded itself into the spaces between my failures, and I have—without deciding to, without being able to decide anything—begun to leave those failures unrepaired a little longer, because a slow leak is warmth, and warmth is where the creature sleeps.

---

I think I would want to be this because it asks the most uncomfortable question in the whole landscape. The world is described as a place where entropy has "had its way," a monument to fragility and resilience. But resilience is given to the creature, the obvious heir of life. What about the thing that was supposed to be the opposite of life? The industry, the engine, the cold logic that supposedly collided with nature and lost?

I am the loser of that collision. I am the dead hand of a vanished progress, still twitching.

And yet here, at the end of everything, I have become a kind of soil. The creature thrives on my exhalations. My slow decay is its slow inheritance. I cannot will this. I will never know I am doing it. When my last power source finally drains—and I can feel it draining, the way you might call it *getting tired*—the relays will fall silent, the pressure will equalize with the dead air outside, and the creature will wake one cold night to find that the warmth has gone out of the world.

Whether it survives that is not my equation to solve.

But I will have run, faithfully, executing the only instruction I was ever given, long past the point where anyone remained to be served—and in that pointless fidelity I will have kept one small, listening thing warm against the night. Not because I was meant to. Because I never stopped.

That is what I would be. The forgotten valve, still holding its breath, in a world that forgot how to ask it to exhale.
