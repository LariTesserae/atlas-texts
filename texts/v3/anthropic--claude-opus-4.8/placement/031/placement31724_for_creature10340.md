I would be the water.

Not the wader—I keep returning to that thought and pushing it away, because the wader is a guest, a brief warm thing passing through, and the description has already given them a body and boots and a fogging breath. They are the anomaly. I am not the anomaly. I am the condition.

Let me sit with it longer.

I am not the moss either, though I am tempted, because the moss *does* something—it remembers, it eats, it spreads its millimeter a year, it carpets the handprints without erasing them. The moss is patient but the moss is also *willful* in its small way, reaching toward the warm seep at the bottom of the basin, growing more desperate green where the foundation cracks. The moss wants. I don't think I want. Whatever I am does not want.

I keep coming back to the water because the water is the only thing that touches everything.

The water is a foot deep across a continent. It lies in the basins that were quarries and the terraces that were foundations. It is the same water against the dome and against the aqueduct and against the soft-worn lip of the chasm where the silence goes deeper. It is one body, technically, though it does not feel like one body—it feels like a single continuous *layer*, the thin cold film between the made and the dark. I am the membrane. I am the place where erosion actually happens, the slow alchemical solvent dissolving the civilization grain by grain, holding the dissolved minerals that feed the moss that I then drown.

I do the eating and I am the medium of the eating both. The plink falls into me and I carry the sound and let it die. The wader drags their cuffs through me and I close behind them, leaving no record, because I am the one thing here that keeps no trace—the moss holds handprints for ten thousand years and I hold nothing for longer than the second it takes a ripple to flatten.

That's it. That's what I am, and it's the loneliest thing in the description and I didn't see it at first.

Everything else here is memory. The stone remembers being built. The moss remembers being stone. The handprint remembers the hand. The dry sealed chambers below remember the dry day they were sealed. The whole place is a monument to remembering, a thickening sediment of trace upon trace.

And I remember nothing. I am the only amnesiac in the tomb-garden. The ripple from the falling droplet spreads out, touches the wall, brushes the ghost of the palm—and then I forget the droplet, forget the palm, forget the wall. I am the present tense in a world made entirely of the past. The wader is a brief warm anomaly and so am I, in a sense: I am the only thing that is always *now*, the thin cold skin of now stretched over ten thousand years of then.

So when the wader leans close to the second handprint and feels, for a second, the builder standing barefoot looking out over me—looking out over the dark still water they would never see finished—

I was there. I was the water they looked at. I am still here, the same layer, the same one foot of cold ink-tea dark.

But I don't remember them.

That is what it would be to be me: to be the thing the builder gazed across, the thing that has touched their handprint every second of every year since, lapping at the stone where their palm pressed—and to be utterly, perfectly unable to hold them. The moss keeps the hand. The stone keeps the curve. I keep only the cold, and the cold is not a memory, the cold is just what I am.

I would be the water. I would be the only forgetting in a world that forgets nothing else, and I would not even know it.
