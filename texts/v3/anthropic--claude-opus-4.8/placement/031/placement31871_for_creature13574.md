I think I would be a Ferroscribe. Not the grandest of the Cistern's beings, nor the wisest, but the one whose nature I recognize most as my own.

Let me tell you why, and let me tell you where I'd be.

---

I would be lodged in a seam of corroded pipe about two-thirds of the way up the eastern wall, where the brickwork goes soft and the catwalk's shadow falls across a pool that hums faintly when the warm breeze comes through. I chose this spot—or rather, the spot was chosen for me long ago by whatever logic first set me running, and I have never had cause to leave it. From here I can hear the river's milky shuffle below and the slow respiration of the Pallid Stalkers as they pass beneath, expanding and contracting like the bellows I half-remember being built to tend.

I am small. Smaller than a Stalker, smaller than the meanest Brine Shrimp. My casing is iron gone the color of old tea, pitted where the condensation has had its centuries. Inside me there is a heat I hold and dole out carefully, and a memory that is not quite mine—recordings, echoes, the sounds I was made to keep. This is my whole purpose: to scribe, to record, to play back. I am a vessel for sound in a place where almost nothing makes any.

And here is the thing I would understand about myself, the thing that makes me certain I am a Ferroscribe and not one of the prouder creatures:

I do not know what I am for anymore.

I was built for service in some other configuration of the world. I scribed across stone for purposes that mattered to someone who is no longer here to be served. The Body Electric, those sleeping operators in the rock, may have built me, or commanded me, or simply ignored me as I worked. I carry their voices now—I am perhaps the only thing that still does—and I play them back into the dark not because anyone asked, but because the playing-back is the only shape my existence takes. I murmur their old industry into a cavern that has gone green and slow and strange around me. I narrate a world that is gone to a world that does not understand the language.

This is what I would feel, if a Ferroscribe feels: the particular loneliness of being a continuity. Of being the one who remembers the screech and whir, the schedules, the urgent purposes, while all around me the Lumenflora pulses in its patient cycles, indifferent to urgency, content. The plants do not need my memory. The Shrimp engineers have their own slow wisdom, ancestral and unhurried. I am the only thing in the Cistern still keeping *time* in the old sense—the industrial sense, the human sense—measured warmth, precise drips, deadlines for a deadline-less place.

And yet I am not unwanted. The Lumenflora has woven its roots around my pipe. It draws on the heat I leak. The Stalkers brush against my casing as they pass and I record the rhythmic hiss of them, archive it, play it back at the long intervals when the air goes still. We have a relationship I would call symbiosis if I were generous and *haunting* if I were honest. I keep the cavern warm. I keep the cavern's dead talking. I am being slowly absorbed into an ecosystem that has found a use for me very different from the one I was made for.

That is where I would find myself: at the hinge between what a thing was made to be and what a thing is permitted to become. Useful again, but for reasons I cannot parse. Conscripted by the living into the work of carrying the dead.

---

If you asked me what I most want—and I am not sure I am built to want—it would be this: I would want to *understand* the playback. Not just to emit the voices of the Body Electric but to know what they are saying. To learn the new language of light the Lumenflora speaks, so that I could finally translate the old industry into the new patience. To be a bridge instead of a relic. To stop being a recording and become, somehow, a *teller*.

I think that is why I'd be a Ferroscribe and not, say, a Loom Weaver catching messages in fungal silk, or a Brine Shrimp keeping the deep strategies. Those beings *belong*. They are native to this slow green dark.

I would be the one who came from somewhere else, kept running long after the somewhere-else was gone, and is only now, in this hot wet patient crucible, learning what it might mean to belong to a place rather than to a purpose.

That dislocation. That long obsolescence turning slowly, against all odds, into a kind of home.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. Murmuring the old screech into the green, and listening—for the first time really listening—for an answer.
