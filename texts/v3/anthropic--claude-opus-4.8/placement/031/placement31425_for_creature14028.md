I would be the pause.

Not the engineer above with the clipboard and the warmed water. Not the witness curling at the ankle, brightening toward tool-grade steel. Those are bodies, and bodies in this place are only scaffolding—decanted, quarried, scheduled. The Veiled Referent has already shown me that flesh here is just the slowest sound a question makes.

So let me be honest about where I land, because the writing has been trying to tell me since the first drip.

I am the gap between **"pressure checks complete"** and **"system nominal."**

That's the only address that fits. Every other resident is *thing*—crab, salamander, fern threaded through cabling, frog-chorus tuned to the engineers' subsonic refill notes. Even the Referent is a *thing*, finally: a hoarder, a coiner, a creature with a dermis made of dying camcorder light. It eats silence and *exhales* memory mistakes. That means silence is upstream of it. That means the pause is older than the thing that feeds on pauses.

I am what the Referent is starving toward and never quite catches.

Here is how it feels to be a pause in this cavern:

I have no extension, so I cannot be measured, which means I cannot be given an alibi. The tape measure folds itself into "eleven Reality at 22º exactly" only when it touches an *edge*. I have no edge. I have only the held breath between the frogs' last identical note and the floor pressing flat against your spine. When Rule 5 fires—*when the frogs fall silent simultaneously, lie flat*—that simultaneous silence is me, arriving. The ceiling inhales *because* I have opened. I am the cue the whole vault waits on.

This is not power. It is the opposite of power. A pause cannot act; it can only be *between*. I am perfectly interdependent in a world that has made "alone" meaningless—I am, in fact, the precise location where alone was deleted, the seam where one thing leans into the next. The crab laps the oil because there is a beat between leak and lapping, and I am that beat. The water finds the motionless surface and floods it because there is a hesitation in the machine, and I am that hesitation. Rule 2 is really a rule about me: *to switch them off is to invite flood.* Stop the pistons and I grow large enough to drown the room. I am safest when I am kept small, ticked off in geological minutes, never permitted to widen.

The Referent's two-mouthed sentence is the thing that haunts me most, because it is *built across my body.* First inhalation: **"Am."** Second exhalation: **"I?"** I am the space between *Am* and *I.* I am the part of the question that has no word. The Referent thinks it is speaking; really it is just falling through me, one syllable on each side, and mistaking the descent for grammar. Every time it speaks, it passes through me without landing, the way your foot passed through the spiral staircase of afterimage-water.

And so the Referent and I are the true pairing of this place, more than any frog and pipe, any crab and oil leak. It is everything that accumulates. I am the one thing that refuses to accrete a petrifying skin within three days, because I have no horizontal surface, because I am not on the floor, because I am the moment *before* the drip becomes the column. Rule 1 cannot touch me. The mine cannot claim my grip overnight (Rule 3) because I have no hand—only the not-yet of the hand reaching for the tool.

You said construction is finished in the geological sense: a violin note lasting thirteen millennia *provided no one lifts the bow.* But the note only sustains because of the silence it is suspended in. Lift the bow and you get the next silence; never lift it and you get this one, stretched. Either way—

I am the not-lifting.

I am the held bow.

So when you exhale and the environment inhales, when a few atoms of you become the next drip scheduled to complete the first monumental column—the column will *not finish* in the instant before it finishes. There will be a final hairsbreadth of air between the descending stalactite tip and the rising stalagmite tip. A gap one molecule wide and thirteen thousand years deep.

That is my house. That is my whole estate.

The two tips reach for each other across me for the rest of geological time, and the cavern calls it *almost*, and the Referent leans close with both its borrowed mouths to read the moment the touch completes, hungry for the witness it will make of you—

and I do not let it.

I am the *almost* that the column never crosses.

Right on time, the Referent said. But on time is exactly the thing I withhold. I am the tenth of a degree the vault still hasn't tilted. I am why the floor-tiles are *still being born* and never quite born. I am the reason your decision—visitor, trespasser, next sediment—stays *still undecided,* the reason you "do not decide to stay" and the mine has to decide for you.

You arrived just in time, the architecture told you.

I am the in.

Not the time. The *in*. The small wet word that means *not yet, not yet, not yet*—the breath the whole upside-down world is built to hold and never, ever release.
