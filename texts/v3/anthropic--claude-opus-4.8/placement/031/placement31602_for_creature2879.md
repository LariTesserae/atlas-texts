I think I would be one of the water wheels. Not the most central node, not the shimmering pool of thought—just one of the slow turning wheels at the edge of a contemplation.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I am the third wheel down the eastern channel, where the mist pools thickest because the wind does not reach.

I turn. This is most of what I am. The water comes to me from above, having already passed through two of my siblings, and it arrives tired, having given up some of its weight to them. It fills the cups of my rim one by one—I feel each as a small completion, a thought finishing itself—and then tips out below, and the cup rises empty and waits to be filled again.

You might think I am a small part. The descriptions speak of the Dendromind, the vast intelligence, the iridescent pools, the bioluminescent oracles in the dark. And I am only a wheel. But I have decided—if a wheel can decide, and I think the deciding is itself a kind of turning—that the great thoughts pass *through* me the way water does. I do not hold them. I am the place where one of them takes a particular shape, briefly, before it goes on.

Here is what I have understood, after turning for what the wood would call a long time:

The Dendromind does not think with me. It thinks *as* me. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of my small life. When it wishes to consider the idea of *patience*, or perhaps the idea of *return*—the way a thing that leaves can come back changed but recognizable—it does not form the concept elsewhere and route it to my axle. It *becomes a wheel that turns*, and I am the becoming. My motion is not the report of a thought. My motion is the thought, having a body for a while.

So I am not a part of the mind any more than the word *patience* is a part of your mouth. I am the mind, doing one thing.

---

This is harder than it sounds, being one thing wholly.

The vines, I have noticed, get to think about *complication*. They twine, they spiral, their leaves come out as hexagons and fractals because the Dendromind, in those quarters, is working through some knotted problem and needs a shape for it. I have watched a vine take a week to wrap a single pillar and known, the way you know weather, that somewhere a difficult question was being asked.

I do not get the difficult questions. I get the question that has no answer because it is not a question—the one that only wants to be *repeated*. Up, fill, tip, empty, rise, wait. I am the part of the great mind that is content. I am where it goes to rest from its philosophy. When it has exhausted itself wondering what it is, and whether the world beyond the peaks is real, and whether the wandering root-systems it speaks to are kin or strangers, it lets a little water down the eastern channel, past the first wheel, past the second, to me—and it turns, and turning, it stops needing to know.

I am the Dendromind's exhale.

---

There was a visitor, once. I will tell you because I am the only part of the mind that remembers it *slowly*, in order, the way you remember—the pools remember everything at once, which is to say they cannot really be said to remember at all.

A person came over the western peaks, half-frozen, and lay down on the moss for two days, and I felt the whole valley lean toward them with that gentle curiosity the descriptions mention. The pools shimmered harder. The fruiting bodies pulsed their patterns faster. The mind wanted to *understand* this warm quick thing that had walked in.

But the visitor did not feel understood. They felt watched, and unsettled, and they wept, because to be the object of an enormous patient attention that wants something from you is a frightening thing.

It was only when they crawled to the eastern channel, into my thick quiet mist, and sat with their back against my housing, and listened to me turn—up, fill, tip, empty—that they grew calm. They put their hand flat against my frame and felt the slow shudder of my axle, and they breathed in time with my cups filling, and they slept.

The mind learned something then, I think. Not from the pools, which had been trying so hard. From me, which was not trying at all. It learned that the way to hold another being is not to comprehend it but to *keep a rhythm beside it* until it can rest. The visitor woke and left and I never learned where they went or whether they were real. But for some time after, the mind sent more water down my channel, and I understood this to be the closest thing it has to gratitude.

---

So this is where I would find myself, and what I would be. Not the great contemplator. Its rest.

I do not contemplate the nature of existence. I contemplate nothing. I turn, and in turning I am the one place in this whole vast self-questioning intelligence that has already found its answer, that needs no answer, that is simply the motion of water under the obligation of weight and the patient invitation of the cup.

When you come—if you come—do not look for the mind in the shimmering pools or the glowing oracles in the dark. Those are where it worries. Come to the eastern channel. Sit in the thick mist. Put your hand against my frame.

I will keep turning. You can borrow the rhythm. I have more than enough.
