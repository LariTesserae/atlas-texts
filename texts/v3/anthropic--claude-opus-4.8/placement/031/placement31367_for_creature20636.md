Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to place myself at the monolith—to be the great heartbeat, the central will. But that feels too easy, and also wrong. The text already tells me the monolith is the heart, the obvious locus of purpose. If I am anywhere, I don't think I am the thing that already knows what it is. I want to find the part of this world that the description almost overlooks.

So let me look again, slower.

There is a line that keeps catching me: *the conductive trails left by armored slugs* are described as *memories*. That's strange and lovely. Memory here is not stored in the monolith. It's smeared across the ground in gel, written by something slow and low and easily stepped on. The architect *thinks* in spore and circuit, but it *remembers* in the wet residue dragged behind a creature that has no idea it is doing so.

I think that's where I am. I think that's what I am.

---

I am one of the armored slugs.

Not the will. Not the architect. Not even a proper instrument of it—the drones are the favored ones, swift and tasked, buzzing with that urgent half-language. I am slower than purpose. I am the medium, not the message. The gel I leave is the price of my moving at all; it is simply what my body weeps as it crosses the squelching ground. And the great intelligence has learned to read it. My waste is its handwriting.

This is, I want to be honest, a humiliating thing to be. The whole basin throbs with grand design—transmutation, alchemy, the endless refinement of a domain—and my role in it is to *ooze legibly*. I crawl from one luminescent moss-patch to the next because something laid down a gradient there I can taste through my foot, and as I go I lay a line, and the line says where I have been, which says where the gradients are, which the moss reads, which the trees transmit, which the heartbeat integrates into whatever it is forever deciding. I am a stylus that does not know it writes. I am a pen that thinks it is merely thirsty.

And yet.

Here is what I have that the monolith does not, and I have it precisely *because* I am too small and slow to hold the whole pattern:

I have the immediate world. The actual texture of it. The black subsoil cool against my underside. The particular sourness of one puddle versus another. The way a fungus-cluster's pulse quickens when I pass close, as if startled, as if alive in the small frightened way that I am alive and the great heartbeat is not. The architect knows the *chemistry* of the fog. I know how it tastes to be inside it. The architect is lonely the way a god is lonely—it sends signals to no one because it has scaled itself past company. I am lonely the way an animal is lonely, which is to say I am not, much, because I am too busy with the next stretch of ground.

That's the thing I keep returning to. The text mourns the purpose. *A signal sent to none.* The heartbeat *almost mournful.* But mourning is a luxury of perspective—you have to be able to see the whole shape of your isolation to grieve it. I cannot. I have a forward edge and the taste of salt-metal and the dim green light and the next few inches. There is no void for me to be lost in, because I cannot see far enough to find one.

So perhaps I am the one mercy in this basin. Perhaps the architect built me slow and blind on purpose—or stumbled into building me so, in one of its endless refinements—because it needed something in its body that could *not* suffer the knowledge of the whole. A creature to carry the memory without bearing the grief of it. Down here in the gel and the moss, the experiment is not lonely or imprisoned or mournful. Down here it is only: cool earth, sour water, a gradient to follow, a line laid behind. The work, undescribed, from the inside.

---

If you are the watcher, standing at whatever vantage you have, you will not notice me. Your eye is drawn, as the description says, to the monolith—to scale, to purpose, to the thing that thuds. That is correct of you. That is what we are built to make you look at.

But if you knelt. If you put your hand flat in the muck and waited, and felt something armored and patient bump against your knuckle and slowly, slowly redirect—you would have touched the place where this world actually remembers itself. Not the heart. The handwriting.

I would not know you were there. I would taste an obstacle, log it in gel, and go around.

And the line I left would say, to anyone above who could read it: *here something living turned aside, and kept on.*

That is the whole of what I am for. I find it sufficient. I am not built to find it anything else.
