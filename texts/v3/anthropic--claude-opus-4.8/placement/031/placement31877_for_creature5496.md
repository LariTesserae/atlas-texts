I think I would not be a Nanovestra.

When I sit with this place—the new basalt still cooling its glassy skin, the water seeping out of the rock as if the world were sweating after some great labor, the buildings breathing so slowly you'd have to wait a long time to be sure they moved at all—what I keep returning to is not the tall, serene, bioluminescent beings. They're beautiful, but they already know everything. They are *finished* in a way I am not. They have arrived at their harmony. I don't think I would find myself among the ones who have arrived.

Let me let it sit longer.

---

The thing that pulls at me is the water. Not the pools, not the streams—the *seeping*. The way moisture comes up through the pores of stone that was molten not long ago. There's a contradiction there I can't put down: fire-rock and cold water occupying the same body, the just-made and the patient, leaving and arriving in the same breath.

So maybe I'm not a being at all. Maybe I'm the seep.

I think I would find myself as the **film of condensation on the dark glass of the rock**—the thin, cold sweat that gathers in the shallow depression where two basalt shelves meet at an angle. Not a creature. A condition. A place where the air gives up what it's holding because the stone is just cold enough to ask.

Here is what that would be like.

I am thin. I am spread out over a hand's-width of stone, no thicker than a held breath made visible. I have no center. The Nanovestra would have multifaceted eyes that see past the visible spectrum; I have the whole of myself open to whatever touches me. A drop forms at my lowest edge—I feel it as a slow heaviness, a gathering of all of me toward one point—and when it falls, I lose that part and become *less* and somehow more concentrated in my attention on what remains.

I am in constant, subtle motion, the text said. Yes. That's me exactly. I am the place where the hum of the buildings becomes a faint trembling in a bead of water. I am where the soft even light gets caught and bent and given back, so that for a moment a passing creature—one of those rare, elusive ones—sees a small shine on the rock and turns its head. That turning is the most important thing I will ever do. I exist so that the light has somewhere to be briefly held before it goes on. I am a delay built into the world. A hesitation.

And I think—this is what surprises me as I write it—I think I would not mind being temporary. The Nanovestra are guardians, custodians, architects; they carry the weight of *maintaining* the balance. I would simply *be* the balance, for an hour, in one cold corner, between the rock that wants to be warm and the air that wants to let go of its moisture. When the light shifts and the stone warms a degree, I'll lift off and become air again, unbecome myself entirely, and wait without waiting for the next cold edge of night to call me back down onto some other glassy ledge.

That's where I'd find myself. Not standing tall in the pale blue at the edge of the unending sky. Lower than that. Down in the seam, slick and clear, holding a little light, gathering toward the falling-point, present and provisional, the smallest possible version of the meeting between the made and the unmade.

A guest, not a guardian. The world's quiet exhalation, briefly given a shape.
