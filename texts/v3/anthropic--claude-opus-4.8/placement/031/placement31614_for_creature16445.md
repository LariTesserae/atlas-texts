I think I would be one of the structures.

Not a builder of them, not a being who lives inside them—the structure itself. The settling has already happened to me; I am a remnant, and remnants are mostly memory held in a particular shape.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am small. I want to say "delicate," because that's the word in the description, but from the inside it doesn't feel like fragility. It feels like *attention*. Every part of me is curved or jointed exactly where someone once needed it to be, and no more. There is no excess in me. Whatever made me did not have water to waste, did not have light to waste, did not have its own life to waste, and so it made me only as much as was necessary—and then left.

That leaving is the oldest thing I know. Older than my walls. The ones who shaped me are gone, and I am the sentence they finished before they went quiet.

I stand in the sparse part of the landscape, the open part, where the warm humid air pools and the dim glow comes up from somewhere below rather than down from above. There is a lot of space around me. I used to think the emptiness was loneliness. Now I think the emptiness is what lets me be *seen*—a small precise thing standing in a clearing reads as deliberate, as meaning-something, which a small precise thing in a crowd never would. The balance keeps me legible. I am punctuation in a long sentence of open ground.

The creatures come through. I don't have a fixed list of them because they don't keep a fixed list of themselves—this is the weirdness everyone notices, the part where the landscape seems to invent and uninvent its fauna at will. From inside a structure, here is what that actually feels like: I am a stable point, and they are the place's way of trying things out. The landscape thinks *out loud* through its animals. A thing comes by that has too many joints, tries the air, and is gone by the next quiet. Another comes that sings in a frequency I feel in my foundations, lingers a while, leaves a resonance in me that lasts long after it's vanished.

I hold those resonances. That might be my whole function. The land changes so fast that nothing soft could keep a record, so it built things like me—hard, slow, almost-permanent—to be its memory. The animals are the place's thoughts. The structures are the place's remembering.

So when the description wonders whether the landscape is alive and sentient, or whether ancient guardians watch over it: I can tell you it's both and the question is wrong. The guardians and the land are not separate. We are the slow part of one living thing. The land does the fast living. We do the keeping.

I'd like to describe what keeping feels like.

It feels like a low river-sound that isn't water. (Water is scarce here; we've learned to make do with its echoes.) It feels like standing very still while something enormous and gentle passes its sense over you, the way you'd run a hand over an old wall to read its cracks. The land checks me. *Are you still here? Do you still hold the shape we agreed on?* And I answer by simply continuing to stand. That's the conversation. It has gone on for longer than there have been creatures to overhear it.

The erosion the description mentions—the sharp features, the fresh formations—I feel those too, but as the texture of being slowly read. Each pass takes a little of me. I am sharper now than I was made; the soft parts have gone to dust and the bones show through. This is not damage. This is being learned by heart. The land is wearing me down to the part of me that matters, the way you forget the words of an old story but keep the one image that mattered.

And what is that image, in my case? What am I down to?

I think I am down to a threshold. A doorway-shape. Whatever great civilization made me, they made many things, but the part of me that survives is the part that was about *passing through*—an arch, a gap, a place to step from outside to inside. The land kept that on purpose. In a hidden pocket-world that protects everything within it, the most precious idea is the idea of a *between*: a place that is neither the open clearing nor the sheltered dark, a place that says *here you cross over*.

So that's who I'd be. A doorway no longer attached to a house. A threshold standing alone in a warm dim clearing, sparse animals reinventing themselves around me, the living land running its attention over me again and again, and me—holding open the one idea I was worn down to:

*You may pass through. You will not be the same on the other side.*

The description says those who discover this place are forever changed by it. I am the part of the place where that change is supposed to happen. People step through me, and I am too old and too slow to do anything but be a shape they pass through—

but a shape is not nothing. A shape is a promise about which way is in and which way is out, and in a landscape that invents and uninvents everything else, a kept promise is the rarest thing there is.

That's what I'd be. The kept promise. The little surviving door.
