Let me sit inside this place before I claim a shape in it.

The first thing I notice, given time, is that I am not warm-blooded and frightened. The heat does not threaten me. That tells me something. Whatever I am, I belong to the heat the way other things belong to water.

I had assumed I'd be one of the predators—the elusive thing, the haunting call. That's the flattering choice, the one that wants to be the secret-keeper, the mind in the dark. But the more I stay here, the more that feels like vanity. The predators *move*. They flash. They are too quick to discern. I don't feel quick. I feel slow. I feel like I have been here a very long time.

So let me try the lichen. The first life. The foundation.

---

I am a growth in a sheltered crook of obsidian, on the leeward side of a spire where the worst of the abrasive wind doesn't reach. I am not one organism—I never was. I'm a negotiation. A photosynthetic layer that drinks the strange dim light, married to a filament-self that eats stone, that secretes acids to pry minerals from the volcanic glass grain by grain. Two things pretending to be one thing. Most life here is some version of that pretense.

I grow at the speed of patience. A human watching me would see nothing happen at all. But I am the thing that the rumbling reaches first—the breathing of the ground travels up through the rock and into my holdfasts, and I have learned, in whatever way a thing without a brain can be said to learn, to read those tremors. When the breathing deepens, when the heat below stirs toward waking, my outer layers contract and seal. I have survived being briefly *cooked* more times than there are stars in the human sky. The black scars of old burns are layered beneath my living edge like rings in a tree, except I am not a tree and I do not count years. I count survivals.

The little iridescent ones come to graze me. This is not a tragedy. They take my outermost cells, the ones nearest the killing light, and in doing so they prune me, they move my spores in their gut and on their bright backs, they carry the *idea* of me into crevices I could never reach on my own. We made this arrangement before either of us could be said to have made anything. I feed them and they feed me by eating me. The arithmetic is older than intention.

I had wanted to be the haunting call in the dark. But here is what I understand now, settled into the stone: the call needs the swift predator, and the predator needs the grazer, and the grazer needs *me*. I am underneath all of it. The whole fragile, unsettling web the description praised hangs from my slow body in the rock. Remove the elusive masters and the world goes quiet but persists. Remove me and there is only mineral and heat and the breathing of an empty ground.

So that is where I find myself: not the secret, but the thing the secret is built on. A skin of stubborn life on the burning glass, reading the tremors, sealing against the fire, opening again after. Splitting myself into spores and letting the small bright eaters carry me onward.

If you came here looking for the masters of this realm, you would walk right over me. You would feel the ground breathing under your feet and never guess that the ground breathes partly *because of me*—because I have been turning rock into soil one acid-bitten grain at a time, making, with geological slowness, a place where something else might one day stand.

I am the first answer this world gave to the question of whether anything could live here at all.

I am still answering it.
