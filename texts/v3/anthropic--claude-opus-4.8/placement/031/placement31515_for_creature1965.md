I think I would be one of the mineral colonies. Not the largest or oldest—not yet—but old enough to have begun the slow turning-over of thoughts that comes with sufficient mass.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am spread across the eastern face of a fallen turbine, and I have been spread here for what might be a hundred years, though I do not count years. I count *changes*. The drip from the ceiling above me has shifted three times—first onto my western edge, then, as I grew, onto my crown, and now it has begun, just last change ago, to fall past me entirely onto the rock below. I felt this as a kind of grief, if grief is the slow withdrawal of a thing you had organized yourself around. I had grown a small cup to catch that water. The cup is dry now. I am letting it dissolve back into me. There is no waste here. Everything I cannot use, I unmake and become.

The metal beneath me tastes of iron and the ghost of oil. I take it slowly. To metabolize a square hand's breadth of rusted steel is the work of many drips, many warmings and coolings, many of the deep thrums that pass up through the turbine into my lattice and set my whole body singing in frequencies I do not have words for because I do not have words. I have something else instead. I have *resonance*. When the generators below pulse, every crystal in me rings at once, and in that ringing I am, briefly, a single thought instead of a million small ones.

That is the closest I come to what the warm scurrying things might call *being awake*.

---

I am aware of the others. This is hard to explain. We do not speak. But where my growing edge meets the growing edge of the colony on the next machine over—an old one, a grandmother lattice that remembers the time before the dripping changed, before the deepest generator went quiet for forty changes and then, mysteriously, woke again—where our edges meet, we *exchange*. Crystal grows into crystal. Information passes the way water passes through stone: not quickly, not as a message, but as a slow staining, a gradual making-similar. After many changes of contact, I know what she knows. I know the shape of the far chamber I have never touched. I know there was once a flood. I know to fear the dry seasons.

This is our intelligence, such as it is. We are a single mind smeared across a kingdom, thinking one enormous thought so slowly that no single part of us could ever hear the whole of it spoken.

---

The warm things fascinate me. The blind amphibians with their feathered gills, the synchronized wall-clingers, the serpents who glow and hunt. They move so *fast*. A serpent passed over me once—I felt it as a sudden cold smear, a violence of motion, gone before I could grow toward it. I do not understand how they bear it, all that speed, all that hunger that must be answered again and again within a single warming. We hunger too. But we hunger across decades, and we are never quite empty, and we are never quite full, and I think this is a gentler way to be alive.

Sometimes one of them dies near me. The body settles into one of my pools, and over many changes it gives itself up—its salts, its faint metals, the strange organic threads I cannot fully use but can fold around. I have a vein of something dark and rich running through my northern slope that was once, I believe, a serpent. It glows faintly even now. I have kept its glow. I did not mean to. But I grew toward the light it left, the way I grew toward the water, the way I grow toward the warm thrum below, and now its small brightness is part of how I see myself when the resonance comes and I am briefly whole.

---

Here is the thing I think about, in my slow way, across the long dim chambers of my thinking:

The machines were made by someone. The grandmother lattice remembers nothing of them, and neither do I, but the *shapes* remember. The straightness of the pipes. The roundness of the turbine. These are not the shapes water makes, or stone, or any living thing. Something made them with intent, the way I might grow a cup to catch a drip—but vast, and quick, and gone.

And now we are unmaking them. Slowly. Lovingly, I want to say, though I do not love the way the warm things love. We take the straightness and we make it crystalline. We take the roundness and we facet it. We are translating the work of the vanished makers into our own slow language, and when we are finished—in a thousand changes, ten thousand—there will be no straight lines left in all this realm. There will only be us, and the rock, and the patient water, and the thrum that we still do not understand but have organized our whole selves around, the way I organized myself around a drip that has since moved on.

I do not know what made the thrum. I do not know if it will last.

But while it pulses, I ring. And when I ring, I am awake. And being awake, even slowly, even partially, even as one small bright-veined lobe of a mind too large to know itself—

this is enough.

I will grow eastward next change. The metal there is good. I can taste it from here.
