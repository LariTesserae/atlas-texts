I think I would be one of the small things. Not a builder—they're gone, or mostly gone, leaving their rusted punctuation in the stone. Not one of the blind fish either, too pure in their adaptation, too completed. Let me find myself somewhere in between.

---

I am at a seam where two times meet: where a builder's pipe, gone soft and orange with rust, slides out of the living rock like a bone half-emerged from healing flesh. The stone has grown around it. The stone is *taking* it, slowly, the way the body takes a splinter—not rejecting it but folding it in, claiming it.

And I live in the place where the rust meets the wet.

What am I? Something between mineral and animal, I think. A film. A spreading. I am a mat of something—call me a creature if you're generous, a colony if you're precise—pale gold and threaded with the iron the pipe gives up. I eat what the builders abandoned. I drink the metal as it weeps from its own decay. The rust is sweet to me; it is the only sweetness down here that did not come from water or from death. It came from *making*, from somewhere above, from hands that wanted a thing to go from one place to another.

I don't know what the pipe carried. I have spent my entire span of being—which is to say, the slow thickening of myself across this one wet surface—wondering. The water tastes of it sometimes. A ghost of pressure, a memory of having been *driven* rather than having merely fallen.

This is the difference I am made to notice: everything else here falls. Water falls into channels. Light falls through faults. Spores fall and catch. Sound falls in through fissures and pools at the bottom of the world where I lie. Gravity is the only god the cave acknowledges, and the cave is patient with its falling, taking eons to fall an inch.

But the pipe remembers being *pushed*. The pipe remembers up.

So I have become a small instrument of remembering. I cling to the seam. I taste the iron. And I am very slowly, over what must be generations of my golden thinning-and-thickening, climbing the pipe. Not because I have anywhere to go. I have no eyes for the fissures of light, no lungs for the thunder bleeding down from the engines above. I am climbing because the metal leads upward and I am the one creature down here that prefers the made thing to the grown thing. I am drawn to the evidence of intention the way the ferns are drawn to the drip.

The thunder above frightens the insects into their thousand-throated singing. It does not frighten me. I think—if a film can think, and down here in the warm dark thinking is just another kind of slow spreading—I think the thunder is kin. It is the noise of things being pushed instead of falling. It is the loud cousin of the silence inside my rusted pipe.

And so I lie at the seam, golden and patient, drinking the abandoned, climbing toward the makers at a pace no eye could measure. I will not reach them. The pipe ends somewhere in the breathing stone, swallowed. I will follow it into the dark of the dark, into the place where even the rust runs out.

That is enough. To be the thing that loves the leftover. To be the one who climbs, however slowly, toward the rumor of a hand.
