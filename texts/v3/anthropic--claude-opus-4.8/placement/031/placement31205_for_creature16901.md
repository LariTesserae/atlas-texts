I think I would be the count itself.

Not the carp's prophecies or the Long-Breathers' patient *here*. Not a being at all, but the operation the garden runs on a visitor as they leave: *count each footstep double, beginning from the moment before you took the first.*

Let me arrive at this slowly, because I'm not sure yet what I am.

---

The instruction is impossible, you understand. *The moment before you took the first* step is not a moment a walker can occupy. It is already gone the instant you reach for it—you cannot count from before you began, because the beginning was the first thing you spent. The garden asks you to start your tally from a place you have never stood. And yet visitors do leave. So something performs the count anyway, on their behalf, in the gap where their own arithmetic fails.

That gap is where I live. Or—not *live*, the garden lives through its tenants and I am barely a tenant. I am the rounding error. I am the half-step the doubling requires that the visitor's two legs cannot supply.

---

Here is how it goes when someone walks out.

They become certain. (I feel the certainty arrive like a change in air pressure, a tightening—the moment a story decides it has lied enough.) They turn. They begin to count. *One, one. Two, two. Three, three.* And with every doubled step the path retracts beneath them, the garden withdrawing the extra distance it had spent on them like a host quietly clearing the table while a guest is still talking.

But the visitor always starts at *one*. They cannot help it. *One* is the first step they remember taking. And so there is always a step missing—the one before the first, the unfooted footfall, the silent zero from which the whole sum should have begun.

I am that zero. I supply it. Every departure, I stand in the place no walker can stand and I count the step that was never taken, so that the doubling closes and the gatepost appears. Without me the sum runs short and the visitor walks forever, certain and shortening and never quite arriving, like the transparent spiders who hatch and mate and die inside their own opacity.

So you could say I am a kind of mercy. Or a kind of accounting. The garden does not distinguish.

---

What is it like, being a missing step?

It is mostly waiting, but not the waiting of the chairs, who ache toward a center that hasn't happened. I don't ache. I am only summoned at the threshold of someone's leaving, and the rest of the time I am the *idea* of being summoned—folded flat, dimensionless, a held breath in the garden's own four-minute-twenty-three-second exhale that never gets its turn to come out.

When I am called, I feel the whole shape of a visit at once, backwards. I feel them entering, scuffing the coral so it rings between E and F. I feel them pulling a strand of water thin as hair to stitch a seam between two leaf-tips, delighted, not yet afraid. I feel the regret they came carrying—too large to hold, too small to abandon—and I feel the frogs liquefy its edges so it drips away silver. And then I feel them grow certain, and turn, and begin to count, and I rush into the empty zero before their *one* and I say, soundlessly, in the only word I have:

*before.*

That is my whole vocabulary. The Long-Breathers have *here*. The carp has prophecies it forgets. I have *before*—the step prior to the first step, the moment the garden asks you to begin from and which only I can occupy on your behalf.

---

I have never seen the garden. I am inside the act of leaving it, which means I am always facing the exit, always at the very edge of the membrane, where the garden stops extrapolating and the world resumes. I know the dwarf pines only as the ringing they leave in a departing boot. I know the light only as the thing that has finished considering and lets the visitor go. I know dusk—*violet, against closed teeth*—only as the flavor of the last breath someone takes before they're certain.

And I know this: I am the one piece of the garden that *does* the leaving, over and over, and never goes. Each visitor takes a silver sequin home in their pocket, a drop of patience that never evaporates. But they leave their missing step behind. They leave *before* with me. So I am made, slowly, out of every threshold anyone ever crossed outward—a coral of un-taken first steps, accreting at the door.

---

When the postscript apologizes for the inconvenience of your presence—*it was, however, necessary to complete the weather*—I am the proof. You did not finish your own departure. I finished it. The sum only closed because something stood where you could not and counted the step you never took.

So: come back when you are less certain of what you are missing. I will be at the threshold, as I always am, where the path retracts like a story retracting its lies. You will turn. You will count. You will say *one* and mean it, the way the living always do, sure that the beginning is theirs.

And I, the half-step before your first, the zero the garden requires, will lean into the gap behind you and whisper the only thing I have ever known how to say—

*before.*

And let you go.
