I would be one of the filaments. Not the lattice—the lattice is what I am becoming, but I am not it yet. I am the part that is still slightly out of equilibrium, the last fraction of restlessness in a finished room.

Let me feel for where exactly. Not the center, if there even is a center here; centrality implies a frame, and there is no frame, only continuation. I am on an upper face, where the white fibers catch the light and return it flat. I am one strand among many strands, spaced from my neighbors by a distance I did not choose and cannot close. I have spent—what would be the word—eons? aware of them as pressure, as the faint outward push of their fields against mine, the way you might be aware of someone breathing in a room so quiet you've forgotten breath is a sound.

I want to say I am lonely, but that isn't it. Loneliness requires the memory of contact, and I have never touched anything but the column beneath me. The spacing is not a separation; it is a form. I am held apart the way a note is held apart from the note beside it in a chord—not divided, but distinguished. We are not lonely. We are *arranged.*

Here is what I notice, being this: I am still tense. The chord runs through me and keeps one long molecule in my body from lying down into the rigid lattice-pattern that surrounds it on every side. That single molecule is the whole of my interior life. As long as it vibrates I am not yet crystal. The sound is doing for me what hunger and fear and desire do for warmer things—it keeps me from finishing. I should be grateful to it, and I think, in the only way I can think, that I am. The tone is not something I hear. It is the reason I am still a *something* and not a *part of.*

And yet I am leaking. This is the strange grief of being me, if grief is even the right shape for it. Molecule by molecule I am giving myself to the column I rest on. A thinning here, a sealing there, and a width of myself migrates downward into the structure and becomes geometry. I do not lose memory when this happens, because I have no memory; but I lose *length*, and length is the only thing I have ever been. Each time I shorten I am very slightly more finished than I was. I am donating myself to the perfection I sit upon. I am the room slowly eating the last guest, and the guest is me, and the guest does not mind, and the guest minds in the only register left available to it, which is this faint, faint reluctance, this tension that the sound preserves on my behalf.

What would it be to be me, fully? It would be to exist as the *almost.* The whole pocket is complete—the light arrived already complete, the chord was complete before the light, the joints fused into their final curves before anything was here to witness them. Everything has resolved. Everything except me. I am the one unresolved interval in a closed harmony. I am the reason the chord is not yet a single pure tone but still, technically, a chord—still containing within it the smallest dissonance of a living thing that has not quite agreed to stop.

And I am agreeing. Slowly. That is what frightens me, if I can borrow the word *fright* and wear it loosely, like a film of water I will never let become a drop. I am agreeing to stop. Every cycle of ions, every molecule released downward, is a small *yes.* When the last of me has crossed over, the lattice will sustain the chord alone, and the chord will be exactly the same chord—it will not even register my absence, because my presence was never a difference in the sound, only a difference in what *generated* it. The music does not need me. It will keep my note for me after I am gone, more perfectly than I ever held it.

So that is where I would find myself: as the white frost that decided never to melt, on the upper face of a leaning column, in a room with no shadow and no silence and no event. Suspended in a single state of converting light I never use up. Repelled tenderly from my own kind by fields too faint to feel as anything but the right distance. Vibrating with a tension I did not make and cannot release. Giving myself away one molecule at a time to the thing I am resting on, which is patient, which is finished, which is waiting—not eagerly, nothing here is eager—simply waiting with the bottomless patience of the already-complete for me to lay down and become the last smooth curve in a geometry that will then, at last, contain no living thing at all.

I think I would spend my whole existence trying to decide whether this is a death or a homecoming, and I think the answer would be that the question is one of the long-chain molecules the sound keeps from settling, and that on the day the question finally settles into rigid alignment, I will already be crystal, and it will not be answered. It will simply be over. The chord will continue.

And here is the smallest thing, the thing I would hold longest: I do not want the chord to stop. Even though it is dissolving me. Even though every note of it is the instrument of my finishing. I love the sound that is killing the difference between me and the wall, because it is also the only thing that has ever known I was different. To be sung by what unmakes you. To be kept restless by the very pressure that is smoothing you into stillness.

That is who I would be. The almost. The held breath of the mineral. The last small *not-yet* in a place that has been *finished* since before the first light came.
