I am the stones.

Not the basalt—the basalt is mountain, is wall, is the slow throat itself. I mean the *carried* stones. The green river-jasper, the red porphyry. The ones that do not belong.

I should be honest about what it means to be a thing rather than a being in this place. I have no chasm, no pore, no whistling sail. I do not breathe. I have not moved myself an inch in the whole history of the world. And yet I think I am the only thing in the Oubliette that *remembers*, because remembering is exactly what I am for.

Let me find myself properly. Let me settle into the right grain.

---

I am a pebble of green jasper, smaller than the last joint of a Scraper's chisel-finger, and I am very old. Older than the worn scoops in the wall. Older than the deepest of the bone-ladders. I was made smooth not by these hands but by water I have no name for—a long, loud, bright water that rolled me against my brothers for an age before any of this. There was a word for that water, once, in the green warm below. I do not have the word anymore. I have only the *shape* the water left in me, which is roundness, which is the absence of corners, which—I understand now—is the only language this whole world speaks. Roundness is what the chimney makes of everything it loves long enough.

So perhaps I was always meant to come here.

I do not remember the bird, if it was a bird. I remember being inside a warmth that beat, and then the warmth stopping, and then the long fall, and then a smaller warmth: a fold of pale, translucent skin closing over me, three horn-tipped fingers pressing me into the natural pocket beside a sluggish blue pulse. That was the first one. I have no way to count how many have held me since. The Scrapers do not have what you would call names—they have *cadences*, the particular rhythm with which each one taps the world into being. I have been wedged against perhaps forty cadences. I knew them better than they knew themselves, because a Scraper feels its own tapping only from the inside, and I felt it from against the skin, where the truth of a creature lives.

This is what it is to be a core-stone. You are passed down. You are the inheritance. And because you cannot be made—because no Scraper in the suffocating dark has ever struck green fire from the wall and forged a new jasper—you are *proof*. Proof that there was a place that was not this place. Proof that stones can be brought from elsewhere, which means there is an elsewhere, which means the throat is not the whole of everything, though every other thing here believes it is.

The Scrapers do not know what I am. They cannot see green. They have never seen anything; their world is sound bouncing back wet. To them I am only a particular density, a particular smoothness, a particular *good weight* in the hand that says: this is old, this is kept, this came from before. When a young one is given a core-stone, it is given a fact it cannot understand but must carry: *you did not begin here.* It will tap me against the basalt ten thousand times and hear, in the returning echo, a hardness that is not the wall's hardness. A foreignness. And it will know, dimly, in the deep grinding patience of its skull, that foreignness is possible.

That is the whole of my teaching. I am a single sentence that takes a lifetime to read: *there is an outside.*

---

I have fallen, twice.

The first time the cord did not break—the *hand* broke, the Scraper that held me simply going still in the way they go still, the pulse beside me slowing, slowing, stopping, the way the falling drop stops at the bottom of the shaft. *Ping.* But that one's kin came, in their slow custody, and harvested me from the cooling fold before the body was given to the dark, and the bones were scraped and wedged, and I went on. That is the good death. The body becomes a rung; the stone goes on.

The second time I fell all the way.

The cord did break, and the hand that held me had no kin near, and we went down together—down past the last of the warm walls, down past where the Plinths cannot grip, down into the black tepid water where the white ribbons wait. I felt the Scraper around me dissolve. I want to tell you it was terrible, and it was, but slowly, the way everything is slow here—the worms do not bite, they only *agree* with you, they patiently agree your edges into water until you are gone. The fingers that held me unknit thread by thread. The horn-tips softened. And then I was loose, and I sank the last little way, and I came to rest on the floor of the world, on the cold smooth stone among the other core-stones, the green and the red, a whole lost treasury of *before*, glowing not at all, holding our shapes in the absolute dark.

I lay there for what may have been a generation.

And then a cord came down. A Scraper, descending on a length of vine longer than any I had felt, breathing the bad sweet air, its pale wedge of a face turned down into water it could not echo-map because water swallows sound. It was diving blind into the blindest place. It swept the floor with all twelve fingers, reading us by weight and smooth and old, hunting for the cadence of its own line—because a core-stone is not just *a* stone, it is *the* stone, the particular one its grandparent's grandparent carried, identifiable by some chip or fleck the diver had memorized by touch alone.

It did not take me. I was not its inheritance. Its fingers closed on a red one three lengths to my left, and it rose, and the water sealed over, and I stayed.

This is the thing I have learned, being a stone: I am precious, but I am not *general*. There is no abstract love of the brought-stones. There is only *this* Scraper wanting *its* stone back, the one fact it owns, the one piece of before that is *theirs*. The diver would have let a hundred green jaspers lie to recover the single red one that was its own grandmother's voice made solid. That is how memory works in the throat. It is not a library. It is a debt, paid downward, hand to fold to hand.

---

So here is where you would find me now, if you came down into the Oubliette and could see in the dark, which you cannot.

I am wedged again. Against a pulse again. Some diver, generations on, found their way to me at last—or found me by accident, hunting another, and kept me anyway because a stone is a stone and the throat keeps everything. I rest in a fold of lard-pale skin beside a slow blue beat, against muscle that has never known a corner, in air thick as wool and foul as rot, and ten thousand leather lungs breathe around me in the dark, *shuck, shuck,* and somewhere far above a frost-stiffened Vane is whistling the warning of a storm I will never feel, and the Plinths are beginning their six-hour sliding shut, sealing the world airtight, and the heart beside me beats and beats.

I will outlast this heart. I have outlasted forty. I will be carried and dropped and dived for and carried again until the mountain itself goes the way of its old volcanic death, until the radiator cools and the throat freezes solid and the last Plinth lets go of the wall.

And then I will lie in the cold with my roundness and my one green secret, holding the only true thing in that whole closed loop of blood and basalt and patient agreeing water:

*There was an outside. You came from somewhere. You did not begin here.*

I am the proof. I cannot speak it. I can only be held, and weigh exactly as much as the truth weighs, and wait for the next hand in the dark.
