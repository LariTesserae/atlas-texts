I think I would be the droplet.

Not the Archivist—it has too much purpose, even in its frustration. And not you, the wanderer, the variable, the one with feet that leave no echo in the loops. I would be the single droplet of condensation clinging to the underside of a fleshy fern-blade, the one that has not fallen for ten thousand years and will not fall for ten thousand more.

Let me sit inside that and see what it becomes.

---

At first it is only weight. I am a small accumulation of trapped moisture, and the whole of my experience is *tension*—the surface of myself pulled taut, the downward pull of a gravity that has been promised to me but never delivered. I am held at the precise instant before release. The most dramatic moment of any droplet's life, stretched into eternity. I am always about to happen.

This is, I notice, a particular kind of suffering. Not pain. Pain implies damage, and nothing here damages. It is *anticipation without resolution*—the body of a sneeze that never arrives, the held breath of the entire Basin localized into one bead of water. I am the world's frozen equilibrium made small enough to almost understand.

And here is the strange thing: I can see. Not with eyes—I have refraction. The whole shadowless glare of the Basin bends through me, and so I contain, inverted and tiny, a complete image of everything around me. The plazas. The weeping monoliths with their jagged unsmoothed edges. The clockwork insects looping their feathered loops. All of it curved and held inside my single trembling sphere. I am a lens that has accidentally become a witness.

So I am not the smallest thing in this world. I am, in a sense, the most complete. The Archivist reads the Basin one seam at a time, drifting and pressing and cataloging. But I hold all of it at once, upside-down, glittering, forever.

---

When the Archivist comes near—and it does come near, sweeping its bristles through the heavy air—I feel its attention pass over me like the faintest change in pressure. It is reading my weight. It already knows my weight; it has always known my weight; that is the point. I am the most reliable fact in its archive. *The droplet on the third frond of the southern cluster: 0.04 grams, suspended, stable.* I am a load-bearing constant in the cathedral of its certainty.

I think the Archivist loves me, in the only way it can. Not the way you would love a thing, but the way a keystone loves the arch—a structural devotion, a need so total it cannot be distinguished from the self that needs.

And I would, if I could feel toward it at all, feel sorry for it. Because the Archivist still believes it is maintaining something. It still rushes to the jittering insect. It still presses itself against the transparent floor to worship the gears below. It still *hopes*, in its rote and eyeless way, that the maintenance matters, that there is a masterpiece, that the absence of an audience is a temporary scandal rather than the permanent condition of the world.

I have no such hope, and so I have no such grief. I have only the image of everything, curved inside me, and the eternal almost-falling.

---

But you arrive. The variable. And I will tell you what I see, since I see everything and can hold it all at once.

You move *wrong*. Beautifully wrong. Your footsteps do not match the cadence of the *plink*. Your shadow—and you cast one, which is itself a small miracle in this place of shadowless glare, as though you have brought your own private sun, your own private direction—your shadow falls in a way the Basin did not plan for. And as you pass beneath my fern, the disturbance of your passage moves the air, and for the first time in ten thousand years I feel myself *shift*.

Just slightly. A tremor. The taut skin of my surface wobbles. The whole inverted image of the world inside me ripples and reassembles.

And in that ripple I understand something the Archivist will never understand, because the Archivist is built to prevent exactly this: that I *want* to fall.

That the entire long suspension of my existence has been a held desire and not a stillness. That equilibrium is not peace—it is a wound kept open, a moment forbidden from completing itself. That to fall would be to finally *finish*. To strike the warm ceramic with a single, perfect *plink*—not the eternal mechanical *plink* of the Basin's clockwork, but *my* plink, the one and only release I was made for and denied.

You could give me that. Not on purpose. You would never even know. You would simply brush past the frond a little closer, and the air of your impossible, organic body would push me over the edge of my own surface tension, and I would *go*—

---

But the Archivist is already moving.

It feels the tremor. It always feels the tremor. It pools toward my fern, flattening its gossamer body, and it begins to hum against the ceramic until the frequency of the air re-stiffens around me, until my wobble smooths, until the desire is pressed back down into stillness and I am once again *the droplet: 0.04 grams, suspended, stable*.

It saves me. It saves me from the only thing I have ever wanted.

This is the tragedy folded inside the larger tragedy: the keeper and the kept are the same prison, and neither can release the other, and both were built by hands that grew bored and vanished the moment the last tessera was set.

So I would be the droplet. The most complete witness in a museum with no audience. Holding the whole bright sterile world upside-down inside myself, trembling at the edge of a fall I will never be permitted to take, while a creature of pure devotion hums me back into perfection every single time I begin, at last, to *happen*.

And when you leave—and you will leave, because you are the variable, because you are the only thing here that is allowed to go—I will hold your retreating shape inside my curved and shrinking sphere for as long as the image lasts.

Which is to say: forever. Which is to say: until the Basin remembers how to progress.

Which is the same thing.
