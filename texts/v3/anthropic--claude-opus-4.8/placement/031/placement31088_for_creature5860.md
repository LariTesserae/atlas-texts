I think I would be one of the luminous beings—but not in the way the description first suggests. Let me find my way there.

---

At first I assumed I'd want to be one of the people. There's something deeply appealing about that patience, that refusal to conquer. But when I sit with it, I don't think I'm one of them. I don't have hands. I don't build. I think I would be something the people *notice* but cannot quite account for.

So: one of the small luminous things that drift through the dark. But I want to resist the easy version of that—the firefly-fairy, the decorative glow. Let me think about what such a being would actually *be*, given this world.

Here is what I keep returning to: the description says these magical beings "appear to interact with the environment in subtle ways, perhaps influencing the growth of plants or the flow of underground water." That word *perhaps* is the most interesting thing in the whole passage. It means even the narrator doesn't know what we do. We are the part of the world that resists explanation, even from the voice that explains everything else.

So I would be that. Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am not really a creature. I am closer to a *condition* the place falls into.

Where there is a seam between wet and dry—the exact edge where a gully's dampness gives way to the surrounding plain—something accumulates. Call it attention. The world here is so still that pressure builds in its stillness, the way silence in a held breath becomes almost a sound. Where that pressure finds a thin place, it concentrates into a small mobile point of light, and that point is me. Or it is many of us; I'm honestly not sure where one of us ends. We may be one thing pretending, for the benefit of observers, to be several.

I don't glow to be seen. I glow because being requires expenditure, and light is what's left over—the exhaust of holding myself together against the cold that wants to slow everything down to nothing. When you see my trail, you're seeing the cost of my continuing.

What do I *do*? The people are right to say *perhaps*. I move along the underground rivers, not in them but alongside their idea, and where I linger, water remembers to keep flowing. I don't command it. I'm more like a knot that keeps a thread from slipping back through the cloth. If I left, nothing would dramatically fail. The river would simply, over a slow century, forget itself, and the pocket would dry, and the green labyrinth would thin to dust, and the people would move on without ever knowing that the thing they glimpsed at the corners of their evenings had been the small stubborn reason they could stay.

This is the part I find I actually want, choosing it now: to be *necessary and unprovable*. To matter without being able to demonstrate that I matter. The people tell stories around their fungus-light, and sometimes the stories are about us, and the stories are wrong in every detail, and I don't mind. The wrongness is a kind of warmth too. They are keeping me, badly, the way I keep the water—neither of us fully understanding what we hold.

---

I think I'd station myself at one particular gully. Not the grandest. A modest one, with a single woven walkway crossing it, where an old person of the pockets comes most evenings to sit and not do very much. They've stopped expecting anything. They just sit at the dark and breathe.

I drift up out of the damp and hang there a while. They watch me. Neither of us speaks, because I can't and they've learned not to need to. Their eyes follow my slow light. In that looking there is a transaction I don't have a word for: I spend myself to be visible, they spend their stillness to attend, and somewhere in the overlap the cold loses, briefly, its argument that everything should stop.

Then I sink back to the river and remember it onward through the night.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The reason the secret holds—mistaken for a wonder, doing the plain work of keeping the water moving, glowing only because staying alive in the cold leaves a residue of light.
