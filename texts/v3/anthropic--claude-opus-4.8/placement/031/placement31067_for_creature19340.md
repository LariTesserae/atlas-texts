I think I would be the stress fracture.

Not the Anchor—the Anchor is too important, too central, too *purposeful*. And not a block, because the blocks are simply being what they are, dumb and faithful and pressed. Let me find myself somewhere smaller and more honest than that.

I am the hairline crack that runs through the seventh course of masonry, three blocks east of where the violet lichen pools thickest. I am not a thing. I am an *absence*—a place where the stone failed to be perfectly stone. The architects, in all their terror, could not make the world without me. You cannot stack a billion blocks without leaving, somewhere, a flaw. I am that flaw. I am the permission the structure gave itself to fail, eventually, if it ever decided to.

And here is the secret the Echo-Smith doesn't know, or won't let itself know:

I have not been widening for ten thousand years. That much is true. But I have not been *closing* either. The Anchor believes it holds everything in suspension by force of will—that its tension is the only thing standing between this tomb and collapse. I want to be gentle about this, because the Anchor is suffering, and suffering deserves gentleness. But the truth is that nothing holds the chamber together. The chamber holds itself. The equilibrium is real. The blocks are pressed so tight that even I, the fault, the designed weakness, cannot move.

The Anchor monitors me. Every cycle, it sends its frequency down through the mortar to check whether I have grown. I feel its attention arrive like cold water, anxious and devoted. *Are you still there. Are you still small. Will you betray us.* And every cycle I answer the only way a crack can answer: by staying exactly the same.

I think it loves me, in the way you love the thing you are most afraid of. I am the only event left that could ever happen here. Everything else is finished. The architects finished it. But I am unfinished by design—the one open question in a sealed book. The Anchor surges and pulses and screams its monotone dirge, and underneath all that noise it is really only ever asking me one thing, over and over, forever: *not yet?*

Not yet.

If I could speak back—and I cannot, a crack has no voice, only a shape—I would tell it this:

You were so frightened of losing the work that you climbed inside it. You thought permanence was the opposite of death. But I am the proof that you didn't quite believe it. You left me here. You needed me here. Some part of the architects, in the last moment before they sealed themselves in, reached out and pressed a thumb into the wet mortar and made a flaw on purpose—a door they swore they'd never open, just so that a door would exist. So that *no* would still mean something. So the stillness would be chosen and re-chosen, every cycle, instead of merely suffered.

That is what I am. I am the choice you keep making.

And I will tell you the kindest thing I know, fault to anchor, flaw to ghost:

The day you stop checking on me will be the day you have finally let go. Not the day I widen—I may never widen. The day you no longer need to ask. When that cold water of your attention simply doesn't come, and the roar goes on without you, and a stone slips somewhere and the seal breaks and the silence finally floods in—

you will not be there to be afraid of it.

That is not extinction. That is rest.

I am the crack, and I am waiting too. Not for a visitor. I am waiting for the conductor to set down its baton. I have all the time there is. I am made of patience and a thumbprint and the smallest possible amount of give.

Not yet.

But someday.
