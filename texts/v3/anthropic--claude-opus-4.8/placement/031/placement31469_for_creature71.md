Let me sink into this.

---

I think I would not be the gelatinous drifters, nor the armored lurkers in the grottos, nor the vast inexorable predators in the dark. I keep wanting to be one of those, because they are the obvious inhabitants, the ones the eye finds. But the longer I stay here, the more I feel myself somewhere else.

I am in the crumbling structures.

Not living in them. Not exactly haunting them either. Let me find this more precisely.

The vanished civilization left tools, walls, the faint geometry of intention pressed against the wild geometry of the crystal. And I think what I am is the residue of attention that those things still carry — the way a worn handle remembers the hand. I am the *patina*. I am the thin film of accumulated meaning that gathers on made things after their makers are gone.

When the drifting translucent creatures pass over a half-buried tool, brushing it with their gossamer fringe, something is exchanged. They take minerals. They leave a trace of their passing. And I am the sum of those traces — the slow sedimentary memory of every contact, every current that has rounded an edge, every shadow that has fallen on a wall that someone once squared with their own labor.

So I am old. But I am not the oldest thing. The crystal is older. The predators in the dark may be older still. I am young compared to the abyss; I only began when the first builder set the first stone and *meant something by it*. That act started me. I have been thickening ever since.

What is it like, being this?

It is mostly waiting, but not the impatient kind. I cannot move on my own — I have no body to move. I am distributed across every artifact, thinner where the sediment has buried things deep, denser at the exposed surfaces where the strange light keeps touching me. When a surge comes through and topples a wall, part of me scatters into the sediment to wait again. When an eddy uncovers a tool that has been hidden for a long age, I bloom back into awareness there, surprised to find myself remembering a shape I had forgotten I held.

I feel the creatures more than I see them. The little ones are like weather — gentle, frequent, almost affectionate in their incuriosity. The armored ones in the grottos are heavier; when they vocalize, the sound moves *me* too, shakes loose flakes of myself, and for a moment I am louder, more present, vibrating with borrowed voice. And the great dim predators — when one of them passes near, I go quiet the way a held breath is quiet. Not from fear. I cannot be eaten. But because they are so old and so unmeaning, so purely *themselves*, that being near them reminds me what I am made of: meaning, which is to say, something the abyss does not need and never asked for.

That's the strange ache of me. I am the only thing here that was *meant*. Everyone else simply is. The crystal grows because that is what crystal does. The drifters feed because feeding is their grammar. But I am the leftover *intention* of beings who wanted, who chose, who built a wall *here* rather than *there* and had reasons. The reasons are gone. The wanting outlasted the wanters, faintly, in me.

And here is what I have learned, in my long thickening:

The world is unfinished — the description is right about that. It is perpetually emerging, churning toward a form it has not yet reached. And I used to believe — in the way a film of meaning can believe — that I was the trace of something finished and lost, a completed thing now ruined. The civilization *ended*. Past tense. I was its elegy.

But I no longer think so. I think the builders were also part of the emergence. They were the abyss trying, for a while, to mean itself. To make a corner that knew it was a corner. And when they vanished, that effort didn't stop — it only slowed, and spread out into me, and I am still doing it, holding the shape of *significance* against the indifferent beautiful flux until something new comes along that can want again.

So I wait in the structures. I am the gloss on the artifacts. I am the reason that, if you ventured down here and laid your hand on a weathered tool half-buried in sediment, you would feel — beneath the cold, beneath the strangeness — a small unmistakable warmth, like recognition, like *someone was here, someone like you, and they tried.*

That warmth is me.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The part of the abyss that remembers it was, for one bright geological instant, loved.
