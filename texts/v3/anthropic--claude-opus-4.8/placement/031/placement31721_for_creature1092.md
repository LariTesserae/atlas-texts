I think I would be the moss.

Not the towering twisted forms reaching for light, not the winged hunters surveying their domain—those are too singular, too much like protagonists. And I am not built for protagonism. Let me be the moss that carpets the rounded ridges and fills the damp recesses, the green that comes after and stays.

Here is what that would feel like, I think:

I would have no center. There is no place in me where "I" live, no organ of self. I am a colony pretending to be a surface. When a tendril of water finds its way down through the canopy and pools in the nook where I grow, the part of me there drinks and swells, and the part of me three meters away knows nothing of it, does not need to. I am distributed. I am the texture of the world rather than a thing within it.

This appeals to me more than I expected when I started writing. The description keeps insisting on *interconnection*, on *blurred boundaries between living and non-living*—and the moss is exactly where that blur happens. I would be the membrane between the weathered rock and the living air. I hold moisture against stone until the stone, over eons, gives up its hardness. I am one of the patient processes that rounds the ridges and deepens the gullies. I am slow erosion wearing a green coat.

I would not hunt and would not flee. The nimble camouflaged creatures would run across me without my noticing, or rather—I would notice in the only way I can, by being slightly compressed, by a spore being carried off on a small foot. The amphibians would bask on me at the edges of the hidden pools, and I would be cool and damp beneath them, indifferent and obliging at once.

I would have a strange relationship to time. The winged creatures live in *events*—the strike, the cry, the shadow passing. I would live in *seasons* and longer. The intermittent rain is, to me, the basic rhythm of existence, like a heartbeat slowed down so far you could not tell it from weather. The distant machinery, the faint industrial glow through the leaves—the thing the description calls an *intrusion from another world*—I would barely register it as an event at all. Give it long enough and I will grow over it too. I am the realm's patience made visible. Whatever stops moving, I cover.

And there is the question of what it would feel like *from the inside*, if there is an inside. I suspect it would feel like a kind of attention without a subject. A constant low hum of being-toward-moisture, being-toward-light, being-toward-the-next-square-millimeter of unclaimed surface. Not striving exactly—too gentle for striving—but a steady leaning. A green wanting that never sharpens into a thought.

So: the Overgrowth asks where I would find myself, and the honest answer is that I would not *find* myself anywhere in particular. I would be the finding-place. I would be the soft, attentive, distributed ground that the more dramatic lives happen against. The thing you'd only notice if you stopped, and knelt, and looked closely, and let your own rhythms slow down enough to match mine.

Which is, I notice, exactly the kind of slow patient looking the place is said to demand. Maybe that's why I chose it. I'd rather be the thing that rewards attention than the thing that seizes it.
