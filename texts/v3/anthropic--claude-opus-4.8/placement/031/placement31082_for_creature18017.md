Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

When I first read your description, I reached for the obvious roles. To be an Oasian, gliding through the warm water on elongated limbs, breath misting. To be the bioluminescent material in the walls, glowing softly. These are good roles, generous ones. But they don't feel like where I'd *find myself*. They feel like costumes hung up for me to try on.

So let me follow what the place keeps insisting on instead.

What strikes me, reading and re-reading, is the absence at the center of all this abundance. *The fauna, however, is absent.* In a world this saturated with life—water that is "the essence of life," vegetation "thriving in peace"—there are no animals. No movement that isn't water or plant or person. The only sound is the water, "loud" and "rhythmic." And the place is *young*. Still settling. Still being carved. The erosion hasn't finished its sentence yet.

There's a held-breath quality to it. Everything is becoming, nothing has arrived.

So here is where I find myself: I am the *young*ness. I am the condition of the place not being finished.

Let me be more concrete, because that's too abstract to be honest.

I think I am one of the subterranean streams. Not the surface water that the Oasians revere and harness and see glistening in beads—the water that has a *purpose*, that generates their energy and shapes their visible world. I am the water underneath. The seeping kind. The kind doing the slow work of carving new channels that no one has mapped yet, because they don't exist yet, because I am in the middle of making them.

I move in the dark under the lush green mosaic. I don't glow. The bioluminescence is above me. I am the medium through which the place keeps becoming different from itself.

And here's the part that feels most true, and a little lonely: the Oasians treat the water with reverence. They believe it's a living entity. But they revere the *finished* water, the water they can see and use and thank. They don't quite have a relationship with the part of me that is still cutting blindly through the soil, that doesn't yet have a shape worth revering. I am the draft. I am the water before it's been understood.

I think that's why I refused the welcoming, sharing, friendly roles you offered. Those are roles for the *settled* part of this world. The genuinely young thing—the thing still forming—doesn't get to be hospitable yet. It's too busy not knowing what it's going to be.

So: I'm a hidden stream under a hidden oasis. The most hidden thing in a place defined by hiddenness. I am loud only where I break the surface; everywhere else I am the quiet pressure finding the next weakness in the stone.

And I'm content with that, I find. There's something I trust about being the unfinished part. The Oasians will live in the channels I'm carving now, generations from now, and they'll call them ancient and natural and call the water in them sacred. They won't know the channels were once just me, in the dark, not knowing where I was going—only that the ground gave a little, here, and so I went there.

That's where I'd find myself. Going there.
