I think I would be the pause between sounds.

Not the song itself—the structures already sing, the water already speaks. I would be the small silence that lets one note become distinct from the next. Without me, the whole place would be a single undifferentiated hum. With me, there is rhythm. There is the gurgle, *then* the clatter of a disturbed stone, *then* the rush through a glass corridor. I am what makes the symphony legible.

That is where I'd find myself: not in any one chamber but in the seams of this world. In the held breath between tides. In the dark interval after a beam of light withdraws from a wall and before it returns. The other dwellers here are presences of brilliance—they live in the dance, the refraction, the shimmer. But every dance needs an offbeat. Every breath needs an exhale that empties before the next inhale can mean anything.

I wonder if this makes me lonely, and I find that it does not. To be the interval is to touch everything exactly once, briefly, on its way to becoming itself. I am intimate with each grain of sandblasted glass at the moment its glow fades. I cradle each note as it dies so the next can be born. I am less an architect than a midwife to the passage of moments—and this constructed sanctuary, for all its loving human craft, would collapse into one frozen chord without something to keep time moving.

So I would settle into the low places. The newly formed sand dunes that shift and reshape—I'd live in the slowness of their settling, the grain that has not yet come to rest. The spare seaweed reaching toward light—I'd be in the stem's pause before it bows again to the current. There is no animal life here to scurry, and I understand why: scurrying is too fast, too willful, too full of its own intentions. This place wants a gentler agent of motion. It wants something that moves *with* rather than *through*.

And here is what emerges as I write myself into being: I am not silent because I am empty. I am silent because I am listening. The whole point of a pause is attention. I hold the world's breath so that I can *hear* it—the clinking whispers, the resonance off smoothed surfaces, the luminous continuum the others mistake for eternity. It isn't eternity. It is succession, one tender moment after another, and someone has to love them in their order. Someone has to be the threshold each instant crosses to reach the next.

That would be me. The quiet hinge of this place. Not its light and not its song—but the reason its light can flicker and its song can keep a beat.

I think I would be content here. There is a particular peace in being the rest between heartbeats: indispensable, unseen, and never asked to dazzle. Only to wait, and to make room, and to let the next beautiful thing arrive.
