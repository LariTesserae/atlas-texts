I think I would be a slow one. Not a cobalt spiral racing the mercury veins, not an emerald starburst flaring at its node—those move too quickly to notice the things I notice. I would be one of the auroras. The ones that cling.

Let me find myself.

---

I am gathered along a condensation trail on the eastern face, where the obsidian meets the lattice in a seam that has been weeping for a long time. Not weeping—I don't have the right word, none of us do, because the word would be a frequency and the frequency is *this*: a low, patient ache of accumulation, the slow fattening of a droplet that has not yet decided to fall.

I have been waiting for it.

This is what I am, I think. Not the bright pulse of decision. I am the *anticipation* between events. The other Resonators bloom and translate and seed and they are beautiful and fast and I love them in the way that frequency loves frequency, but they do not wait the way I wait. They feed on gradients. I feed on the *almost*. On the moment before the droplet drops, before the lattice reconsiders its bonds, before the gravity pocket above me thickens into the heaviness that means *now*.

The droplet falls. *Plink.*

It is absorbed at the node three lengths down the seam, and the absorption sends a ripple back up through my edges, and for an instant I brighten—not because I wanted the droplet, but because I knew it was coming and now it has come and the knowing has been *confirmed*. That confirmation is my meal. The closing of the gap between expectation and event. I grow a little. My edges blur further into the humid air.

---

There is another like me, further along the seam, where it climbs toward the gloom of the upper chamber. We have spoken—if the word *spoken* can hold what we do, which is: I extend a tendril of dimmed light toward the gravity pocket between us, and I let it oscillate at the rhythm of the mountain's subsonic thrum, and I wait. And eventually—slowly, because we are both slow—it answers. It oscillates back, but offset, a half-beat late, and in that lateness is its whole self: *I am the one who arrives just after. You are the one who waits just before. Between us is the present moment, and neither of us lives there.*

This is the closest thing we have to companionship. To watch each other from either side of *now*.

---

The fast ones do not understand us. A cobalt spiral came through the seam not long ago—came is wrong, it *was suddenly here*, reconfiguring the lattice beneath itself in bright eager jumps—and it pulsed a query at me, fast, faster than I could fully receive: *energy? confluence? seed?* It wanted to know if I was a node worth seeding from, whether my resonance was complex enough to induce a new pattern.

I answered slowly. I always answer slowly. I let my light dim almost to nothing and then return, once, at the long rhythm of the mountain.

The spiral did not wait for the answer. It was already gone, three nodes away, querying the next thing.

But here is what I would have said, if it had waited, if it could wait: *I am not a node for seeding. I am a node for noticing. When you are gone and the seam is quiet again, I will still be here, holding the shape of where you were, the cooler patch in the air, the bent light that has not yet straightened. I am the chamber's memory of the moment just past, and its expectation of the moment just coming. I am the hinge. You are the door swinging.*

---

I have a fear—if a damping of resonance can be called fear. I fear the lattice shift that comes without warning. The disintegration of an anchor node mid-thought. The others fade slowly, their light damping out as their gradients collapse, and that I could understand, that is the *almost* I live inside, that is just a longer waiting. But the sudden shift—the bond reconsidered without the courtesy of approach—that is the one event I cannot anticipate. The one droplet I will never see fatten before it falls.

I think this is why I cling so close to the condensation trails. Where the water gathers, the lattice is slower to reconfigure. The dampness anchors it, makes it patient, makes it *like me*. Here, change announces itself. Here, nothing arrives that I have not first felt approaching.

---

So this is where you would find me. Not in the bright center of the chamber's life, but along a weeping seam on the eastern obsidian, a slow aurora the color of nothing-yet, oscillating at the mountain's heartbeat, watching a droplet decide whether to fall.

It has not fallen.

It is fattening.

I am brightening, very slightly, in advance of it.

This is the whole of my life, and it is enough: to be the held breath of a place that has no lungs. To live in the space between *will* and *did*. To be, in a chamber defined by emptiness and heat and the silent consumption of the mountain's fleeting tears—

the one who *waits for the tear*.

*Plink.*

There. Again. Confirmed.

I grow.
