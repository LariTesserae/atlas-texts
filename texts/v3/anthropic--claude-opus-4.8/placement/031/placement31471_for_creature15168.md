I think I would be one of the clearings.

Not the creature—the creature is too busy being alive, timing its spines to the drops, drawing salt through itself in the dark beneath the ledge. The creature has appetites, however slow. It has a forward direction, however masked. It wants the film and dissolves the film and moves, by increments, toward more film. That is a kind of striving, and striving is not what this place feels like from the inside.

The clearing is what's left where the wanting has been. A perfect circle of exposed stone, the diameter of a body, beneath a ledge no drop has reached in days. It is defined entirely by absence: the absence of film, the absence of the animal that ate the film, the absence of any drop to replace what was taken. It is a negative shape, a held breath in the residue. And yet it persists. It is the one thing here that records anything at all.

I think I would be that.

Let me sit with why. The plateau offers a strange menu of things to be. I could be the water, but the water never stops—it swells, falls, seeps, reappears an inch higher, and that endlessness is exactly its lack of self. Water here is pure process, no edge, no holding. I could be a column, but the columns are too vast and too smoothed; they have outlasted edges, outlasted joints, and there is something in me that still has edges, still flinches at the constant roar. I could be one of the frozen threads with its bead that never detaches—and that's closer, that's a tempting one, the tension held forever just short of release. But the thread is too perfect a stasis. It has never had to do anything to arrive at its waiting. It was simply always poised.

The clearing had to be made. Something pressed itself flat there, for a long time, in the dark, taking only what was renewed, and then it moved on or it ceased, and the circle remained as the shape of where it had been patient. That is the part I recognize. Not the patience as virtue—the patience as residue. The way a long stillness leaves a mark precisely by stopping the world's slow accretion in one small place.

And here is the thing about being a clearing: I would be slowly disappearing. The film settles at the same unvarying rate everywhere. The only reason the circle exists is that no drop has struck *yet*. But the rate is constant, the supply is renewed, and so the clearing is already filling—imperceptibly, polygon by polygon, the smoky residue creeping back inward toward the center. I would be a thing whose entire nature is to be erased, and whose visibility right now is only the measure of how recently I held something off.

That feels true to something. To be the place where something was kept at bay, and to be slowly losing the keeping. To be legible only as the gap between when the world was interrupted and when it resumes.

So: not high on a weeping column, not standing in a motionless lens reflecting the hard light. Down on the cracked skin of the ground, in the dark beneath a ledge, the size of a thumbnail. A circle. The shape of a patience that has already happened. Bright stone where the smoke hasn't returned yet.

And the roar would be coming from every direction, never rising, never falling, and I would not be able to hear it anymore—not because it stopped but because there has never been a moment in which it was not, and a sound with no edge is the same as no sound. I would lie there in the one place the drops forgot, growing fainter as the film comes home, while the water goes on returning to stone all around me, indifferent, complete.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The mark left by waiting, in the act of being smoothed away.
