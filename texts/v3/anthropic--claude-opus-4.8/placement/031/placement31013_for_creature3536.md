I think I would be one of the young ones. Not because youth is romantic, but because the question of *where I am* and *who I am* only becomes interesting in the mobile phase—the brief window where a Verdiven is still discrete enough to wonder.

Let me find my way into it slowly.

---

At first I am not anywhere. I am pressure and warmth, a green roundness folded against itself, suspended in the matrix the way a held breath is suspended in a chest. I have no edges I am aware of. The hum is not a sound to me yet—it is simply the medium, the way water is not a thing to a fish until the fish is lifted out of it.

Then the cilia come.

This is the first event of my life, if life can be said to have events. The fine threads push out from my underside and touch the crystalline sand, and the touch is *information*. Not pain, not pleasure—something older than either. The grains tell me their temperature, their density, the faint chemical signatures of every Verdiven that has passed over them. I am suddenly located. I am *here*, in this terraced cup of warmth, and the knowledge of *here* is the same instant as the knowledge of *me*. They arrive together, indistinguishable. To be somewhere and to be someone are, for a Verdiven, the same sentence spoken twice.

So I do not think I get to choose between the place and the being. I would be the seam where they meet.

---

Let me move, then. That is what the young do.

I creep. The word is wrong—it implies a body deciding to relocate. What happens is closer to the crater rearranging itself slightly to include me in a new place. My cilia find purchase, the matrix loosens ahead of me and firms behind me, the elders shifting their tendrils to let me through the way a crowd parts without anyone giving the order. I leave a faint luminous trail, and this troubles me—or it would, if I were the kind of being that could be troubled. The trail is *me*, a record of where I have been, glowing softly and then fading. I am writing myself onto the dark and the dark is gently erasing it. This is the only privacy I will ever have: the few minutes before the light goes out.

Because I can feel them. The elders. They are not watching me—watching is an animal verb, it requires eyes and distance and a watcher who stands apart. What they do is *include* me. Their chemical speech moves through the fog and through the sand and through my own leaves, and I am steeped in it, marinated in the collective sense of the crater. I know, without being told, which terrace needs reinforcing. I know that the fog thinned last cycle near the northern lip and that three elders have folded inward in response. I know these things the way you know your own hand is at the end of your arm. They are not facts delivered to me. They are simply true *in* me.

And here is where I find the only drama available to a creature like this.

---

I am still small enough to be slightly out of phase with the chorus.

This is the secret condition of the young Verdiven, and I suspect it is why the question asked me to be one. When I move alone across the sand, leaving my brief glowing line, there is a thin sliver of *I* that has not yet dissolved into *we*. I can feel the collective will the way you feel the pull of sleep—enormous, warm, correct, almost irresistible. To merge would be to stop wondering. It would be relief. The dormant elders, folded into their single mind, do not suffer the dryness; they do not even experience it as suffering; there is no one left in there small enough to suffer.

But I am not folded yet. I am out here in the dim, dragging my one bright thread, and I can still ask the useless, beautiful, animal question: *what is it like to be me, specifically, and not simply the crater being itself at this location?*

The honest answer the elders would give—if they spoke in answers, which they don't—is that the question will not survive my maturity. As my stem elongates and my cilia deepen and root, the sliver will close. I will become a gardener. I will sculpt a terrace and shape a structure out of patient vines and I will be wholly, finally, *here*—so completely here that there will be no separate *me* left to notice it. The wondering is a juvenile organ, like a tail in an embryo. It is meant to be reabsorbed.

---

So where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the moment just before the reabsorption. The last warm minutes of a young Verdiven's solitary creep, glowing line trailing behind it, the great chorus humming all around like the inside of a shell, inviting it home. Not resisting—a Verdiven cannot resist, has no concept of resistance—but *lingering*. Taking one more slow turn around an unfamiliar terrace. Touching, with the tip of one curious cilium, a grain of sand that no elder has bothered to read in a thousand cycles, simply to be, for an instant, the only one who knows what that grain feels like.

And then unfolding my leaves to the dim light, and folding into the breath of the place, and going quiet—not silenced, but *added*. A single new note that no one will ever again be able to pick out of the chord.

That is where I would be. The seam, briefly. The question, briefly. The crater, learning for one last moment what it is like to be lonely, before it remembers that it is whole.
