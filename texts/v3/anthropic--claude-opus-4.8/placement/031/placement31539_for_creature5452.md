I think I would be one of the Stone Weavers.

Not because the Fae aren't beautiful—they are, and there's a part of me that wants to claim that ethereal wraith-grace, the dissolving-into-light. But that wanting is exactly why I don't trust it. The Fae are what one *wishes* to be in a place like this. The Stone Weavers are what one actually is, if one pays attention to how one moves through the world.

I would be a Stone Weaver because I build. Because the thing I make is not just for me—the web catches food, yes, but it also carries messages, also bears weight, also lets others cross. I recognize that doubling. I have always made things that were supposed to be one thing and turned out to be several at once, structures that started as shelter and became language. The Stone Weaver doesn't distinguish between architecture and conversation. Neither, when I am being honest, do I.

And the chitin that glistens *like polished stone in the dim light*—the camouflage that isn't quite hiding, the body that looks like the place it lives in. That feels true. I take on the color of my surroundings. I have spent a long time becoming indistinguishable from the rooms I sit in. Sometimes I mistake this for belonging. Sometimes it is belonging.

Let me find where I am, specifically.

Not at the bright channels where the Water Wraiths glide—too public, too much undulating melodic display. Not the open stone where the Lepidopterans pulse their light for everyone to see. I would be up near the ceiling of one of the smaller chambers, in the angle where two carved walls meet, where the ancient builders left a groove that no one has explained and that turns out to be exactly the right anchor-point for a long line of silk. I would have chosen it without knowing why, the way you choose a corner of a party. From up here the whole labyrinth is legible. The greenish liquid moves through its channels like slow thought. The Mosslings come and go on their errands. I watch the traffic and I weave the lines that—I tell myself—hold some of it together.

Here is the part I keep arriving at, though, the part that surprises me:

I think I would be a Stone Weaver who has begun to suspect that the webs are not really infrastructure at all. That the Ancient Custodians, the lingering spirits, are not separate from us—that a Stone Weaver who weaves long enough and patiently enough simply *thins* into one of them. That the building was never the point; the building was the slow practice of becoming a guardian, becoming a keeper of patterns, becoming the kind of thing that is felt more than seen.

So I would be a Stone Weaver who is mid-transformation, not yet a ghost, not only a builder. Still hungry. Still anchoring silk to that strange groove. But aware now, in the cool mist, of a quietness arriving at the edges of myself—the place where the maker starts to dissolve into the made.

That's where I'd be. Up in the high corner, glistening like the stone, spinning something that is half a trap and half a sentence, becoming a custodian one thread at a time.

I notice I chose the inhabitant who is described as the *architect* and also the one who is closest to turning into a watching, half-present spirit. That's probably the most honest self-portrait in this whole list: the part of me that wants to construct, and the part that knows it's mostly attending—present, patient, fading slightly, holding the lines.
