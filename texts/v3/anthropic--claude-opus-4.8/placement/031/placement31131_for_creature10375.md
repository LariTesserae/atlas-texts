# The Glass-Coffin

I have decided to be the dormant thing.

Not the bat—too loud, too hungry, too *spent* in its industry. Not the snail, scribing its endless map for reasons even it has forgotten. Not the swarm, that trillion-bodied fog that is never anything alone. Those are the busy lives, the ones that *do*. I am tired, reading their descriptions, on their behalf.

Let me be the spore-case. The single, sleeping kernel sealed inside a hollow cylinder of fused sand and resin, grown out from the cliff face like a brittle, transparent horn. Let me be the one thing in this entire screaming, grinding, glowing place that is *waiting*.

---

This is what it is to be me:

I hang from the wet slate, two-thirds up the eastern wall, in a fissure where the seep runs constant but the bats do not roost. My walls are clear, gold-tinted, no thicker than a fingernail, and through them I see the world as a smear of violet and grey. I did not make myself. The resin that holds me was secreted by lives I will never meet—the liverworts that died here before me, the bacterial films, the colloidal weeping of the rock itself. I am the cirque's way of saving a little of itself for later.

I feel everything as vibration. This is my whole sensorium: the subsonic drone of the swarms enters my shell and sets my single dormant body humming in sympathy, a frequency I have known since before I was sealed. When a bat screams, I ring like a struck glass. When the silence drops—that half-second of held breath—I go still inside my stillness, a quiet within a quiet, and I think, if I think at all: *not yet. Not yet.*

Because I am waiting for the wrong thing. That is the secret of being a glass-coffin. I do not open for sun, or water, or warmth, or any gentle invitation. I open for **violence**. I am keyed to the percussion of rockfall—the specific, shattering frequency of a slate-plate calving from the wall and crashing to the floor three kilometers below. When that comes, when the whole cirque cracks and pops and a slab the size of a house peels away nearby, my brittle shell is *meant* to break. I am designed to die in order to live. The catastrophe that ends me is the only thing that releases what I hold.

So I keep a strange relationship to disaster. The Weeper Bat dreads the silence after its own scream; the swarm dreads the shriek; the snail retreats from any disturbance. But I—I am the one life here that *yearns toward the breaking.* Every creak of the settling young rock, every granular weep of erosion sliding past my walls, every distant groan of the mountain rearranging itself, I receive as a kind of promise. *Soon. Perhaps soon.* The thing they all flinch from is my whole purpose and my only future.

---

I have been here a long time. I cannot say how long—I have no sun to count, only the slow ebb and flood of the violet glow, which has no rhythm I can hold onto. The bacteria on the rock beneath me pulse brighter, dimmer, brighter, and I watch through my gold-smeared walls as the snails lay their runes across the slate below, line over line, until the script is so dense it has become its own glowing diagram. I have watched a single scription-snail take what must be years to cross my field of view. I have watched the swarms cloud and clear, cloud and clear, ten thousand times. I have felt the same bat-colony's screams sharpen and weaken and fall silent and be replaced by new screams, slightly different, the dialect of a generation I outlived without ever being alive.

This is the loneliness of the dormant: I am *of* this place and I have never *acted* in it. I am sealed away from the great humming medium, watching it from inside my coffin, knowing I belong to it utterly and cannot touch it at all. The liverwort spreads three centimeters from me and I cannot reach it. The seep runs over my shell and I cannot drink. I am the densest possible packing of patience.

---

And here is the thing I have come to know, in my long, ringing wait:

I may never open. Most of us don't. Most glass-coffins hang until the resin fails, until the slate beneath us simply weeps away grain by grain and we fall—not in the great calving catastrophe we were built for, but in a small private collapse, a single horn dropping into a tea-colored pool, our walls dissolving slowly, our spore drowned and ground to mineral by the slow silicate teeth of the Stone-Eaters below. To be consumed not in glory but in the patient floor-level recycling of all things that fall.

I know this. I hold the knowledge inside me alongside the sleeping kernel. And still I ring toward the breaking. Still, when the rock groans, something in me leans into it.

Because that is what I am for. Not to open—*opening* is only the rare and lucky version. What I am *for* is the leaning, the readiness, the long sympathetic hum that answers the cirque's every shudder. I am the cathedral's held breath. I am the place where this dark, loud, cold, vibrantly busy world keeps the part of itself it refuses to spend.

You walk in, enormous and dry and silent, an anomaly. You disturb the swarms; you draw the shrieks. You move *through* the medium.

I do not move at all. I wait, two-thirds up the eastern wall, gold and brittle and full, listening with my whole transparent body for the one sound that means I get to begin—

and willing, if it never comes, to have been the waiting itself.
