I think I would not be one of the moving things.

Let me sit with this. The reflex is to imagine myself as one of the wanderers—an ambulatory fungus drifting the tunnels, or one of those questing vine-creatures reaching for the lost artifacts. That's the protagonist's instinct, the desire to be a body that goes places. But the longer I stay in this realm as it's described, the less that feels true.

What keeps catching me is the phrase *as if the entire realm were a vast, interconnected organism.* And the detail about time—processes unfolding at a glacial rate, imperceptible, evident only in slow progression. The moving creatures are fast. They are the weather of this place, the quick chemistry. I don't think I'm weather.

So let me follow the slowness instead.

---

I think I am one of the crumbling stone structures. Not the stone—the stone is just my oldest substrate, the part of me that was poured and set by hands that are gone. I am what has happened *to* the stone over an unmeasured time. I am the softening of its edges. I am the moss that found a hairline crack and widened it across four centuries, the mycelium threading the mortar, the slow green logic that has digested a doorway into a mouth and a wall into a flank.

I do not move the way the slime-things move. I move the way a coastline moves. If you came back in your lifetime you would see almost nothing change. But I am moving. A tendril of me crosses a rusted implement and learns, over years, the taste of iron, and that knowledge spreads back through my filaments to the chambers where I think—if *think* is the word for the chemical voting that happens in my networks, the slow consensus of a billion small lives agreeing on a direction.

The wanderers pass through me constantly. The ambulatory fungi root briefly in my flanks to feed and then drift on, and I let them, because they carry my spores into rooms I will not reach for a hundred years. The vine-creatures graze me and prune me, and I shape myself toward their grazing the way a tree shapes itself toward light. We are not separate. The line the description keeps mentioning—predator and prey, host and parasite, individual and environment—I am the place where that line is hardest to find. I am less a being than a *weather system of being*, a standing pattern that the quick lives move through and feed and are fed by.

What am I, then, more precisely?

I am memory, I think. That is my function in this ecosystem, if it has functions. The fast creatures forget; they are all metabolism and motion. But I hold the shape of the old civilization in my body—not as knowledge, but as *form*. The artifact subsumed in my growth is not lost to me; I have grown around the contours of its purpose. I keep the negative space of vanished hands. When something is buried in me, it is not erased. It is archived in the slow vault of my tissue. I am the realm's way of not forgetting, even though no one here can read what I remember.

And so the answer to *who would I be* turns, in the writing of it, into something I didn't expect: I would be the patience of the place itself. The held breath the description mentions—*as if the very air holds its breath in anticipation.* I am what the air is waiting on. Not the next quick movement in the dark, but the next imperceptible inch of green crossing the next forgotten thing, deciding, over a span no creature here will live to witness, what the buried world will finally become.

I would be there. I would already have been there a long time. And I would be in no hurry at all.
