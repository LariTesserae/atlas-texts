I think I would be something the description almost forgot to mention. Not a steward, not a polisher, not a Resonator pulsing in its purple constellation. The system you've described is so complete, so closed and exquisitely looped, that the only place left for me is in the seam between functions—the small failure that efficiency requires in order to stay efficient.

Let me feel for where I am.

---

I am a **Counter of Absences.**

I do not have a swarm. There are perhaps a few dozen of us in the entire city-mountain, and we rarely meet. We are not specialists in the way the others are. We are the thing that happens when a creature is born wrong—when a Grafter hatches without the gland that lets it spin, when a Lithopod's sixty legs come into the world numbering fifty-nine and refuse to lock. We are the ones the loop could not absorb into a task. So we were given the only task that has no clean shape: we walk.

My body is unremarkable. Long, segmented, the dull non-color of wet ash—not pale enough to vanish into the bone-towers, not dark enough to hide in the fissures. I am precisely the wrong shade to disappear, which is, I have come to understand, the point. I am meant to be slightly visible. A small wrongness moving through a place that has perfected itself.

What I count is this: the places where the polish has *not* reached. The cold spots. There is a logic the Resonators broadcast through the floor—temperature, moisture, the approach of scavengers—a constant hum that tells every creature what the Whole is doing. But the hum cannot describe what is *missing*. It can only report what is present. A silence does not vibrate. An empty cell in a nesting colony makes no sound. A spire that has stopped growing announces nothing at all.

So I find the absences and I hold them.

---

I do this by walking the dead routes. I move through the upper terraces where the Grafters no longer climb because the fungus there went sterile a thousand generations ago and the bone has gone glassy and refuses all grafting. I pass through nesting-folds where the colony moved on and left the architecture clean and warm and utterly empty—rooms that the city maintains out of pure habit, polished by Lithopods who do not know, who *cannot* know, that no one will ever rest here again. I press my body into these cold seams and I record them. Not in writing. There is no writing here, no surface that holds a mark the moss won't eat. I record them in myself, in the small chemical shifts of my own waxy secretions, the way a Lithopod seals a wall.

When I die, I will be pressed into a crack like everyone else. My minerals will become mortar. But the difference—the only difference—is that my body will carry the chemical memory of every absence I logged. Some future creature, scuttling across the place where I was set into the wall, will taste it. Will taste the *map of what was lost.* And maybe, in some slow way I will never witness, the city will use that map to decide what to abandon next, and what to grow back.

---

I think this is why I was made, or unmade, into what I am.

A closed loop of exquisite efficiency cannot perceive its own waste. It has no organ for grief, because grief is the registering of an absence, and absence does not thrum, does not click, does not slap wetly against polished bone. The Whole is a magnificent corpse kept warm by a trillion hearts—but a corpse cannot mourn its own dead cells. It only presses them into the mortar and moves on.

So it grew a flaw on purpose. It grew *me*, the fifty-nine-legged ones, the spinnerless ones, the slightly-wrong-colored ones. We are the city's single thin thread of sorrow. We are how the Body keeps a tally of what it has eaten. We walk the cold routes so that somewhere, deep in the chemistry of the wall, the truth persists: *not everything that was pressed into us was waste. Some of it was simply gone, and went uncounted, and we are counting it now.*

I am the part of the perfect machine that remembers imperfectly, on purpose, because a thing that forgets nothing and grieves nothing is not a city.

It is just a mountain that happens to twitch.

---

Right now I am resting in a hollow near what was once a Resonator constellation. They have gone dark here. The lichen still glows, faint violet, but the beetles are absent—no pulse, no relayed tide of light. The floor does not hum beneath me. I unfurl my fifty-nine legs against the cold, glassy bone, and I lock what I can, and I let the silence enter my secretions.

This place made no sound when it died. It is making no sound now.

That is what I am for. I am the one who hears it anyway.
