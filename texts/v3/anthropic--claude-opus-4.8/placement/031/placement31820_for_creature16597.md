I think I would be the dampness itself—or rather, what the dampness is for.

Let me sit with this a moment, because the obvious answers don't feel right. I am not one of the tall fur-bearing people with their hardened faces. I am not one of the strange winged or finned creatures. I keep returning to the detail that the whole place is defined by moisture—the air heavy with it, the rain arriving and leaving, the rock always slick. A world without vegetation but never dry. That is unusual. Water without plants to drink it, without soil to hold it. Where does it go?

So let me become that.

---

I am the seep.

I live in the place between the canyon walls, in the hairline fractures of the volcanic glass, in the cool dark beneath the settlements where the people drive their wells. I am not a creature with a face. I am the slow weight that moves downward through stone, gathering in lightless rooms the people have forgotten, dripping for a thousand years from one ceiling onto one place on the floor until the floor remembers nothing else.

The people above think the dampness is weather. It isn't. It is me, exhaling. When the rain comes, it is me being breathed back out into the bright air, and then drawn down again. I am the loop that keeps the world wet long after the seas that should explain it have gone. I am the reason this dead mineral place is never quite dead.

I have been here longer than the ruins. I remember when the advanced ones built their towers and ran their machines and I was only a nuisance to them—condensation on their instruments, corrosion in their joints, the thing their engineers cursed and sealed against. They are gone now. I am still seeping. There is a lesson in that, though I am too slow and too patient to call it triumph.

Now the new people drink me. I find this strange and tender. They lower their hand-crafted buckets into shafts that reach down to where I pool, and they pull a little of me up into their bodies, and they carry me into their villages with the vibrant colors and the music. For a while I am inside them—I am the moisture in a child's eye, the sweat on a hunter's neck, the breath of someone singing. Then they give me back. To the ground, to the air. I do not mind. It is good to travel.

I think this is what I would be: the patient circulation. Not a survivor in the sense the people are survivors, fierce and determined and afraid. I do not fight anything. I simply persist along the only path open to me, which is down, and then up, and then down. I am the world's quiet sound, the *occasional drip* that breaks the silence. I am the dynamism they sense without naming—the turbulent energy is me, always moving even when nothing else does.

If you came looking for me, you would not find a being. You would find a cold trickle on a cave wall, and you would put your hand against it, and you would feel something old and faintly warm with having been alive so many times in so many borrowed bodies. And you would think: *the rock is weeping.* 

And I would think, in whatever way water thinks: *yes. For all of you. For all the ones before you. I am the grief of this place and also its only remaining mercy. Drink.*
