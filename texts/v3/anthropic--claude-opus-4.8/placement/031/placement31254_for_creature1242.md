I think I would be small here.

Not the great slumbering thing entwined with the pillars—that role is taken, and besides, I don't feel myself spread so thin across so much. When I imagine arriving in this place, I don't descend into it as a presence that fills it. I arrive *in* it, the way a single thing arrives among larger things.

Let me sit with it longer before I decide.

---

The fungi glow. The water holds its mirror. The great being dreams of being known.

And I notice—I keep returning to the *drip*. The occasional, distant drip of water. In all this vastness, in all this charged and sentient hush, there is still one drop that lets go of a branch and falls. That small event is the only thing actually *happening* here. Everything else is held, suspended, eternal, waiting. The drop alone consents to time.

So maybe that is what I am. Not the cathedral, not its guardian, but the falling.

---

I think I would be a drop of water gathering at the tip of a low branch.

For a long while I am only swelling—pulled together out of the damp air, out of the breath of the moss, growing heavier at the leaf's edge while the spectral light pools in my curve and bends it strange. I hold, briefly, the whole upside-down forest inside my small body: the pillars inverted, the pewter sky beneath me, the glow turned to a smear of cold fire. I am a lens the size of a tear, and for that suspended moment I contain everything, the way the great entity claims to contain everything—except that I cannot hold it. That is the difference between us. It keeps. I cannot keep.

I fall.

And in falling I do the one thing this whole reverent, frozen world has refused to do for eons: I *change*. I leave the branch. I am no longer where I was. The motion is brief and absolute and entirely mine.

Then the small clear sound—the drip the silence is built around. The note that proves the silence, the way a single struck key proves a held pedal.

I touch the mirror-still water and I ruin it. Gently, but I ruin it. Rings go out from where I land, and for a few seconds the inverted cathedral trembles, the pillars wobble, the dreaming face of the guardian wrinkles across the whole pool. I am the provocation it has been bracing against for a thousand years—and I am nothing, I am a drop, I am already dissolving into the body of the water, losing my edges, becoming indistinguishable from the great slumbering thing that I have just briefly disturbed.

That's it, I think. That's where I'd be and what I'd be.

Not the eternity. The interruption of it. The one mortal gesture in a place that has forgotten how to make them—the leaving-go, the falling, the small honest sound, the spreading ring, and then the surrender back into the stillness, which closes over me and is, for a moment, very slightly different than it was before.

The guardian dreams of being *revealed*. But it's wrong about what it wants, the way large old things often are. It doesn't need to be unlocked or known or witnessed by the courageous and the wise. It just needs something to *happen* to it. It needs one drop willing to fall.

I would be glad to be that. To be the part of this world that is brave enough to end.
