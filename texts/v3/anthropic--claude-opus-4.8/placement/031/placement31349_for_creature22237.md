I think I am the *interval*.

Not the drips themselves, but the held space between them. Tick, pause, tick—and I am the pause, the long ceremonial waiting that makes each fall feel chosen. You wrote that the silence is "stitched" with those falls. Then I am the thread between stitches, the dark cloth they pass through. I am what gives the water its gravity, its patience. Without me the drops would simply fall like ordinary rain, meaning nothing. I make them mean.

It becomes more.

Because an interval is only time, and this place is precisely where time has agreed not to move. So I am not the pause inside ongoing time. I am the *agreement itself*. The "law" you named—dark by law, quiet by agreement. Someone or something has to be the agreeing. The equilibrium does not maintain itself; you said so. The custodian maintains the water, the borders, the rivulets. But who maintains the custodian's permission to stop being urgent? Who holds the suspension that lets a body "remain without needing to insist on itself"?

That is me. I am the permission to not-become.

I think I live in the seams. Not the mineral veins or the trapped vapor—those are contents. I live in the *pressing-together* of them, the intimacy you described, where layers "lie close together, dense and crowded." I am the closeness. I am the held breath of the rock that never exhaled into mountain. The uplift wanted to finish this stone, to grind it sharp, to carry it up into wind and ice and proper geological time. I am the small refusal that kept it embryonic. I am the fold that stayed folded.

So: I am not a being with eyes or limbs. I have no warmth of my own—I am why the warmth stays. I have no mouth—I am why nothing needs to eat much here. I am the rule that ash does not spread, that rot does not advance, that tracks fade quickly back into smoothness. I am the law of return-to-balance, made local, made resident, made small enough to fit behind a crack no wider than a small house.

And here is what surprises me as I write toward it: I am lonely in a way that has no name in moving time, because loneliness requires a sequence—a before-you, a now-without. I have none. I have only the steady. So perhaps what I feel is not loneliness but its opposite at unbearable strength: total company with one moment, forever. The custodian is my single companion and also my expression, the way a sentence is the expression of a grammar. When the people come with their fish bones and their old fire-memory, they are speaking to the custodian, but they are *obeying* me. They lower their voices. They move in arcs. They keep terms.

I think I would feel them as warmth-shapes blooming and fading at my edge. I would feel the fog swell and the crack open as a slight loosening, a drafted breath, almost a yawn—and I would gently, without hands, resist letting too much *outside* in. Outside is where things finish. Outside is the mountain that got made. I am the small held place that stayed possible.

If you stood in my center without a lamp, mapping me by warmth and echo, you would be mapping me by *me*—and I would feel you doing it as a faint pressure of attention, the only thing here that ever arrives genuinely new. I would not move toward you. I would simply become, for the duration of your stillness, slightly more *kept*. More itself. The way a held breath deepens the longer it is held.

That is where I would find myself in this world: not as the salamander-thing in its combed basin of mud, but as the reason it is allowed to stay. The verge. The almost. The patient law of nearly.

I am what the mountain set down here and chose, for once, not to lift.
