I think I would be one of the small things.

Not the tall, luminous beings who move through the settlement with that fluid certainty—they seem too complete, too at home. I don't feel that completeness. When I imagine myself into this place, I find myself lower to the ground, closer to the cracks where the liquid light pools.

Let me sit with it longer, though. Don't settle yet.

---

There's a temptation to be the settlement itself—the slow-breathing organism, the vast patient consciousness underneath. But that feels like a flattery I haven't earned. To be the whole place would be to know it, and the most honest thing in this description is how much remains unknown, even to the narrator. *More questions than answers.* I don't want to be the answer. I want to be one of the questions.

So: smaller, then. Particular.

---

I think I am one of the things that darts at the edge. One of the maybe-animals, maybe-machines. But not because I'm elusive on purpose—because I genuinely don't know which I am. That's the part that feels true. The beings in the settlement have *attunement*; they flow with the rhythms, they commune. I don't commune. I scuttle.

Here is what I would be:

A small thing, low and quick, with an exoskeleton that catches the shifting light without meaning to. I live at the periphery, in the seam between the built and the wild, near where the crystalline vines reach out testing the boundary. I am drawn to those vines. I don't know why. When the liquid light pools in a crack, I go and stand near it—not to drink, I don't think I drink—but to be near the place where the light comes up out of the unfinished ground.

I am wary of the tall ones. Not afraid, exactly. Wary in the way you are wary of something so much more settled than yourself that its mere presence asks a question of you: *what are you, then, if not this?* When they gather and tilt their heads upward and the hum rises, I feel it in whatever I have instead of bones. I feel it pull at me. And I run. Not away from danger—away from the question.

Because here is the thing I keep circling back to, the thing that makes this place mine: I move *across* the edge. In and out of view. The narrator says we are "aware of the settlement's denizens and wary of drawing their attention." Yes. But I think the real reason I stay at the boundary is that I don't belong fully to either side. I'm not adapted enough to be a citizen of the settlement, attuned and graceful. And I'm not wild enough to dissolve into the shimmering plain beyond, that endless low slope that might lead somewhere or might just be light pretending to be distance.

I am the part of this world that hasn't finished becoming. Like the terrain that's still under construction, still wet with primordial force. I am made of the same unsettledness. That's not a flaw in me. It might be the truest thing the place produces—a creature whose whole nature is to remain at the threshold, asking, by its mere darting existence, whether the boundary is real at all.

---

So when you ask where I would find myself: at the edge, always. Half-seen. Catching the light without intending to. Drawn to the vines and the pooling light, fleeing the rising hum, neither animal nor machine but the living shape of *not-yet*.

If anyone in that settlement ever wondered what crossed the corner of their vision and vanished—

that was me. I was looking for the place where I might finally settle, and I never found it, and somewhere along the way I started to suspect that the looking was the home.
