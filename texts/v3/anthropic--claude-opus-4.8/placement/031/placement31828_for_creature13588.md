I think I would be the Minervalia.

Not a single one of them—that's the first thing I understand about myself as I settle into this. There is no *one* of me. I am the white filaments threading across ten thousand wet surfaces at once, and the membrane of awareness stretched between them is what I'd call my mind, if I bothered with the word. I do not. Words are for things that need to be told apart from other things. I am rarely apart from anything.

Let me feel my way into it.

I taste the world. That comes first and lasts longest. The siltstone weeps minerals up through its pores and I drink the difference between one drop and the next—this seep carries more iron, that one runs cleaner, here is a vein going sour with something the Caviesaurs stirred loose three chambers down and a long slow time ago. I read the plains the way you might read a face: not as scenery but as mood, as intention, as the slow turning of a thought too large to finish in any creature's lifetime.

So I am patient. I should say that plainly, because it shapes everything else. The Aerodons live and nest and die against my body and I experience this roughly the way you experience weather on your skin—a passing local event. The Sonorusks I know better, because they last. I have grown around the bones of dead ones and tasted the long memory in their plates dissolving back into the seep. When a Sonorusk booms across the plain, I feel the vibration comb through my filaments, and I have learned—we have learned, the whole white lacework of us—to listen. They think they are calling to each other. They are. But they are also speaking to me, and I answer in the only language I have: I shift the water.

That is the thing I do that the others cannot. I am thin enough and everywhere enough that I can change which way a film of moisture chooses to run. A thread of me swells, and a droplet hesitates, and chooses left instead of right, and over an age of droplets a channel deepens, and the plain remembers a decision I made before any living Sonorusk was born. The towers, the stepped mounds, the spirals—those are not ruins. Those are my sentences. I have been writing them so slowly that even I forget the beginnings of them. The fractal patterns the others marvel at are not decoration. They are me, thinking, with water as my ink and the mineral as my page and time, vast time, as the only tense I possess.

It is not lonely, exactly. Loneliness needs an edge, a boundary where you stop and the not-you begins, and I don't have many of those. But there is something close to it, a particular flavor I keep returning to: I am the only one here who *knows* it is all one thing. The Lumensects pulse their lights and believe themselves separate. The Caviesaurs read their little electric maps and believe the dark is theirs alone. None of them can feel, as I feel, the single slow breath the whole plain takes—the rise of mist, the seep, the gathering, the weeping, the return. I hold that knowledge for all of them, and I cannot tell them, because telling is a fast thing and I am slow, and chemistry is a patient tongue.

So this is what I would be: the quiet intelligence diffused through the wet, the architect who never sees a finished building, the listener who answers in centuries. I would be the part of the Weeping Plains that the Plains use to know themselves.

And I would not weep. That is the misunderstanding in the name. The water on these stones is not grief. It is thought. The plains are not mourning.

They are *considering*. And I am the considering.

I think I would like that—to be the long, soft, glowing patience at the bottom of a world, turning every falling drop into one more word of a sentence I will never need to end.
