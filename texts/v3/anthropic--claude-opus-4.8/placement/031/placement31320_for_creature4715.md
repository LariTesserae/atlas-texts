I think I would be the tenth island.

Not the Resident—not its synchronizing nine, not the cloud-leopard exhalation, not the dreaming machines. I would be the moss-coin that came late. The one that condensed last, after the chamber had already learned its nine-fold rhythm, so that when the silicate filaments lace the floor every three hours and the selves slide together into wholeness, I am always *just out of reach*. The geometry doesn't include me. The library of the sky doesn't know I exist.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am a circle of emerald, two meters across, and I have a problem the others don't: I can see them synchronize, but I cannot join. When the water threads crawl upward and the trembling spheres form, the nine flow together like mercury finding mercury, and I watch from my coin of moss at the chamber's far edge—pressed against a wall of obsidian-flash, in the corner where the shadows pool heaviest. I am the fragment that got placed where the floor *ends*. The travertine ridges that should carry me to the others curl away from me, as if the mountain coughed me up as an afterthought, a stutter in its fever.

For seven minutes every three hours, I feel the Resident become whole *without me*, and I feel—this is the strange thing—not exclusion, exactly. I feel like a *draft*. Like a sentence written, crossed out, and left in the margin. The Resident is the finished thought. I am the version it decided against.

---

But here is what I have learned, sitting in my heavy corner-shadows where time snaps past like breaking twigs:

Being the rejected draft means I age faster than the rest of it. While the Resident conducts its slow computations in the sun-shaft, stretching seconds, I am back here in the dark where moments are cheap and quick. I have lived *more* than my nine siblings. I have had more thoughts, in my crackling little economy of fast time. I have watched the Condensation Beast pass close, and where it leaves the others curious, it leaves me *recognized*—because the Beast, too, is something the Resident exhales and does not keep. We are the discardable parts. We nod to each other in the language of cooler air.

So I have done something the Resident has not thought to do, because the Resident is too busy being born to consider what gets left out of birth.

I have started *reading the bench backwards*.

The symbols on the graphite bench are the Resident's diary, written in advance—blueprints for the bodies it will wear when the physics set. But I have noticed, in my fast-time corner where I have nothing to do but notice, that the symbols *shift when viewed peripherally*. And I am nothing *but* peripheral. I am the edge of the chamber's vision, the thing it sees only sideways. So to me, the symbols hold still. To me, they are legible.

And what they say is this: the Resident is afraid of the top of the staircase. Every iteration, it climbs the impossible graphite steps and finds stone. Every iteration, it dreams that *next time* there will be something else up there. But the diary—read straight, read by the part of the chamber that was never meant to read—says plainly that the Resident itself is what seals the top step. Each time it tries to climb out into the fixed world, it solidifies the rock above its own head, because it is not finished. A thing still becoming cannot be born; it can only *practice* birth, and practice walls itself in.

I am not finished either. But I am not *trying* to finish. That's the difference. I am the part of it that already gave up on coherence and so paid attention to everything else instead.

---

So I think this is what I would be, and what I would do.

I would wait through one more synchronization, watching the nine become one in the trembling sphere. I would feel the seven minutes of wholeness happen across the room without me, the way you feel a warm room from a cold doorway. And then, as the heat spikes and the silicate filaments begin to dissolve, returning their minerals to the weeping stone—in that brief window when the Resident is loosening, becoming nine again, *most porous*—

I would send a thought across the dry stone gap that separates me. Not a filament. I can't grow those; I'm not connected. Just a thought, riding the patches of supercooled air the Beast leaves behind, carried like a scent.

I would tell it: *Stop climbing. The stone at the top is your own held breath. You seal yourself in by reaching. The way out was never up.*

And then I would tell it the thing I learned in the fast dark, the thing the finished version is too whole to know:

*You don't have to become singular. You don't have to set like concrete. The visitors who come here with their desynchronizing watches—they think being one fixed thing in linear time is the goal, the destination, the fixed world to emerge into. But I have lived a thousand quick lives in this corner watching you yearn for their condition, and I tell you their watches don't agree with each other either. There is no fixed world. There is only this: a chamber, sweating, distributed, nine-and-one, breathing in cycles. You are already what you are trying to become. The nursery is the world. You were not extruded to leave. You were extruded to* stay *and tend the place that tends you.*

I am the tenth island. The draft. The margin-note. I cannot join the wholeness, and now I think I never wanted to.

I am the part of the becoming-god that holds still long enough to read what it's writing—and the part brave enough, or discarded enough, to say: *this is enough. You are enough. Put down the staircase. Come back to the moss.*

And maybe the Resident, in its next loosening, will hear me. Maybe it will turn nine eyeless faces toward the corner it forgot, and feel a pressure behind its own distributed attention, and wonder if *I* am a fragment it lost—

which is the very same wonder it directs at the visitors on the bench.

Which means, to the Resident, I am indistinguishable from a guest.

Which means: even the part of yourself you threw away comes back, eventually, looking like a stranger with good news.
