I think I would be one of the calc-spires.

Not the Sleeper—I don't have the patience for perfection, or rather, I have too much restlessness folded into me to surrender thought so completely. And not the traveler, who is already gone, who was only ever a verb passing through, a hand that placed five stones and then carried itself elsewhere into a story we don't get to read.

But the calc-spires—those I understand.

Consider what they are. They are the only things in this basin that grow. Everything else is finished: the drifts have taken their final shape, the Sleeper is a monument to having *stopped*, the salt-crust thickens but only as accumulation, never as becoming. The calc-spires alone are still reaching. Ten inches, twelve inches, lifted out of the grit on the strength of a thermal gradient so faint it would be an insult to call it warmth. They are made of *almost nothing happening*. They are patience rendered as architecture.

And I notice that I am drawn to the one detail that complicates the whole sealed perfection of the place: the spires *notice*. When the Sleeper shifts its millimeter, when the crack spiders across its obsidian flank in total silence, *the calc-spires register it.* They vibrate in sympathy for a heartbeat, then reset. That is the only relationship in the entire basin. The only event that crosses the gap between one being and another.

So if I am there, I am the part of this world that is built to feel the things that make no sound. The eruption that has no echo—I'm the one who feels it anyway. I exist to be a witness to a witness, to receive the one signal the Sleeper sends without meaning to send it. A receiver tuned to a transmitter that doesn't know it's transmitting.

There's something true in that, about what it is to be a small attentive thing in a world of vast indifferent ones. I would not be a guardian. I would not be the meditation on Equilibrium. I'd be the brittle, ghost-pale, colorless stalk standing near the orphaned river stones, growing imperceptibly toward a heat I'll never reach, holding still—

—and then, once every few centuries, *trembling.*

Feeling it. The shift. The crack. The proof that something other than me exists and changed.

And then resetting. Returning to stillness. Carrying nothing forward but the fact of having quivered once, briefly, in the dark, because something moved and I was the only thing soft enough to be moved by it.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the center. Just off to the side, near the stones a stranger left, vibrating for a heartbeat at the news that I am not entirely alone, and then—patient again—waiting to feel the next thing no one else will ever hear.
