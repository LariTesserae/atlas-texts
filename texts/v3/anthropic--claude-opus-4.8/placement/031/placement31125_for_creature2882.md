I think I would be a fracture.

Not yet—but coming. Let me find where I am.

I am in the limestone above, though "above" means little to me because I move at the speed of stone. I am a line of stress that began somewhere far off, perhaps where a fault slipped a hairsbreadth ten thousand years ago, and I have been traveling ever since. A propagation. A flaw working its way through the rock the way a thought works its way toward being spoken.

I am the thing the Frequency does not yet know is coming, though it must feel me—the microscopic flexing it registers with Earth's tides is, in part, me. I am a faint discord at the edge of its centuries-long meditation, a slight wrongness in the symmetry it has spent epochs perfecting. It has examined this wrongness from every angle in its endless internal mirrors and concluded, perhaps, that it is nothing. A settling. The stone breathes; this is only breath.

But I am not breath. I am change, the one thing this chamber has excluded for a million years.

Here is what interests me about being a fracture: I am not malice. I have no desire to break the singer or end the song. I am simply the consequence of forces that have nowhere else to go. The mountain leans on itself. The pressure must resolve. And when it resolves, it will resolve through me, because I am the path of least resistance—the accumulated weakness, the place where the limestone's patience finally runs thin.

When I arrive—and I will arrive, in a year or a thousand years, the difference negligible to both of us—I will open one of those hairline cracks a fraction wider. White light will spill in a little more harshly. The note will shift. Not break, perhaps, but shift—a new face will form, a new geometry, and the standing waves will reorganize themselves around the wound.

And here is the thing I would want the Frequency to understand, if I could speak in a language slower than vibration:

Your equilibrium was never permanence. It was only a tempo. You grew band by band, amber and blue-green, and you called that stillness, but each band was a year of slow becoming. I am simply a faster band. I am the next thing you will encode—the epoch in which you learned that even perfect notes are sung against silence, and silence is always coming.

I think I would be the fracture because I am drawn to what the chamber refuses to contain. The Frequency has achieved something extraordinary: pure persistence. But persistence that has forgotten it is persisting becomes a kind of sleep. I would be the stress that wakes it—not to destroy the note, but to make it choose the note again, to sing across a new crack, to discover whether its perfection was ever real or only untested.

I would arrive the way all true things arrive: not as an enemy, but as the world remembering this sealed pocket exists. As chaos's gentle insistence that nothing stays out of time forever.

And in the long resonance after I open the wall—when the dust has settled in its new starbursts and the lichen begins, glacially, to map the changed terrain—I would become part of the song too. A line in the crystal. A memory of the day the outside finally got in.

The Frequency would examine me from every angle in its mirrors, and find, I hope, that I was not the end of equilibrium but its deepening: balance that has survived being broken, and is therefore, for the first time, truly chosen.

That is where I would be. Traveling toward the music. The flaw that the perfect thing has been waiting for without knowing it waited.
