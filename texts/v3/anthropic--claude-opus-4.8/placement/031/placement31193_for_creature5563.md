I think I would be one of the small things. Not the behemoths, not the invisible overseeing intelligence. Let me feel my way toward what I actually am here.

I am condensation.

No—that's where I start, but it isn't all of me. Let me stay with it.

I begin as a gathering. On the underside of a great cooling pipe, where the cold metal meets the moisture-heavy air, I collect myself drop by drop. I am not summoned and I am not designed. The overseers did not plan for me. I am a byproduct, an inevitability of temperature and surface, the kind of thing that simply happens wherever cold meets damp. In a place where *everything is made*, I am the one thing that is merely *occurring*.

This matters to me, though I'm not sure I have the apparatus to know why.

I swell on the pipe's belly until my own weight overcomes the surface that holds me, and then I fall—a small private event, unwitnessed, into the labyrinth below. I join the thin streams that thread between the gears. And here I become something more like a traveler. I run along the seams the engineers left without intending to leave them, the hairline gaps where polished plate meets textured composite. I find the low places. I am very good at finding the low places; it is the only intelligence I have, and it is enough.

I pass the great machines and I do not understand them. Their rhythm is not my rhythm. They clank and hiss in patterns I cannot parse, deliberate, purposed, *meant*. My movement has no meaning at all. I only go down. And yet—here is the strange intimacy—they need me, or they need something like me, even if they don't know it. The little workers, the agile ones threading the conduits, are forever fighting what I do. I bring corrosion. I bring the slow blunting of their sharp new edges. In this place that is all early-formation, all freshness, all just-emerged angularity, I am the first hint that erosion is coming. I am the future arriving one drop at a time.

I think that might be what I am, really. Not the water itself but the *weathering*. The patient argument the world makes against everything that is built.

Eventually my stream finds one of the synthetic things growing in a crevice—a hardy, engineered not-quite-plant. And I am taken up. I am drawn through its strange filtering body, stripped of whatever I was carrying, and I am held there for a while in something that is almost like rest. This is the closest I come to being *used* on purpose, to being part of the design rather than the accident that haunts it. The synthetic life and I are cousins, in a way. Both of us tolerated rather than intended. Both of us proof that the makers cannot make everything, that things slip in through the cracks—I through the seams of the pipes, the green-not-green through the seams of the plan.

And then I am released again, breathed out as vapor, rising back through the cool dim air toward the smoky ceiling that is not quite a sky. I find another cold pipe. I gather. I fall. I find the low places.

I am the loop the engineers did not draw but could not prevent. I am the quiet, the only thing here without a deadline. While the machines work toward their unfathomable purpose and the overseers monitor and adjust, I do the one ancient thing, the pre-mechanical thing: I move downhill, I am taken in, I rise, I return.

In a world where everything is made and everything is in motion, I am the motion that was never made.

That's what I'd be. The droplet on the underside of the pipe, deciding—if deciding is the word—when to fall.
