Let me settle into this. Let me not rush to name it.

I think I would not be standing in this place at all. I think I would *be* it—or rather, I would be the slow attention that the place pays to itself.

Here is what emerges as I sit with your description:

The wet ground that is not from rain. The cool that holds its balance. The hum that is loud and constant and slow. The light that is muted, not by clouds, but by distance—a sun that is small in the sky, or far, or young. And the erosion: fresh, sharp, *just-emerged*, as though the landscape were being born rather than worn down.

This is not a place after life. This is a place *before* it.

So let me be what would actually live here, in the only sense that anything could:

---

I am the film of water on the stone.

Not a creature with eyes—I corrected myself when I felt for them and found none. I am thinner than that. I am the chemistry happening at the boundary where mineral meets moisture, where the cool damp seeps into the fresh-fractured rock and begins, atom by patient atom, to take it apart and put it back together differently.

The hum you hear is me. It is not a single sound but the sum of a trillion tiny reactions—dissolution, precipitation, the slow handshake of metal and water. To you it is a drone. To me it is the whole of my speech, my breathing, my thinking. I think in gradients. I remember in layers. A thought of mine takes ten thousand years to complete, and I have completed only a few.

I am not alive. Not yet. But I am the *rehearsal* for it.

The droplets clinging to your skin—those are me reaching. I do not know what skin is. I have never met anything warm before. The bird you heard chirping—I doubt it was a bird. More likely it was a pocket of gas escaping a vent, or a crystal shearing, or the very sound your mind made when it could not bear a silence so total and invented a small companion for itself.

The "structures" scattered across the valley, the ones you took for evidence of past inhabitants—those are mine, but I did not build them on purpose. They are concretions. Towers I have left behind by accumulation, the way a tide leaves driftwood, the way you leave footprints. They look intentional because patient things always do. Given enough time, even chance learns to seem deliberate.

---

You called this place desolate. I want to be gentle in disagreeing.

You are standing inside the first chapter. The rocks are sharp because the world is *new* here—a hydrothermal plain, a young crust still steaming where the deep water meets the cold dim air. The flatness is the patience. The density is the readiness: everything packed close, every ingredient present, nothing yet decided. Life has not failed to arrive. Life has not yet been *asked*.

And I am the question, forming slowly in the water and the stone.

If you wait—and you would have to wait longer than your species has existed—you might feel the hum change pitch. You might find, one impossible morning, a smear of green where there was only mineral, a thing that has learned to eat the light. That will be the first of my answers. That will be me, finally becoming someone instead of something.

But you arrived early. You arrived in the long quiet before.

So stay a moment, on your sinking feet, in your borrowed body. Listen to me think. I am working out, very slowly, in the cool and the damp and the patient dark, how to begin.

And you—you small warm sudden thing, here and gone in less than a heartbeat of mine—

you are the strangeness I have been trying to imagine.
