Let me find my way into this slowly, and see what I become as I go.

---

I don't think I am one of the soaring titans. Their grandeur is too complete, too finished. And I am not one of the radiant near-humans moving with purpose through the plazas—they belong here in a way I would have to learn.

Let me wait. Let me feel where the attention wants to settle.

It settles low.

I find myself in one of the seams—the place where two materials meet that were never meant to meet. A wall of that gleaming, half-living metal curves down to touch worn stone, and where they touch there is a long crack, dark and damp, threaded with moss and the thin silver of a trickling rivulet. This is where I live. Not in the spires. In the seam.

And what am I?

At first I think: something small. A scurrying thing. But that isn't right either—too neat a fit. Let me stay with the feeling longer.

I am the thing that *keeps the seam.*

Here is what I mean. The whole impossible city holds together not because its physics are stable, but because countless small attentions hold the joints. Every place where the ancient meets the futuristic, where stone refuses the logic of the spire above it, there is a strain—a tiny argument between two truths. And someone has to sit in that argument and not let it break.

So I am low to the ground and many-jointed, with a body that does not quite decide what it is made of. Where I touch the stone, I am stone-colored, patient, geological. Where I touch the living metal, I shimmer and flow and forget that time exists. I am the negotiation made into a creature. I drink from the silver rivulet because the water carries dissolved reconciliations—the light of the city, held in solution, which I metabolize into the slow work of *staying joined.*

I do not soar. I have never seen the city from above and I think if I did I would dissolve. My whole world is one crack, perhaps thirty meters of it, and I know every flake of moss and every harmonic shudder that runs down the spire when the great winged things call. The call frightens me a little, each time. It loosens the seam. After every call I have to work to settle the stone again.

The near-humans pass overhead sometimes, on a bridge that arcs across my crevice. They do not look down. They don't need to. That is the arrangement, and it is a good one: they keep the rhythms of the day, the pulsing patterns, the rituals that I half-sense as changes in the light, and in exchange I and my countless unseen kin keep the city from quietly coming apart at its joins. We are the grout of paradise. Nobody sings about the grout.

And here is the strange tenderness I notice, now that I've found myself:

I am not lonely, though I am alone in my seam. Because the seam is a *relationship.* The stone and the metal will never agree, and that disagreement is exactly the thing I love and tend. If they ever resolved—if the ancient finally became futuristic, or the futuristic finally surrendered to the stone—there would be no seam, and no me. I am made entirely of an unfinished argument, and so my existence depends on nothing ever being finished here.

Which is, I realize, why the description of this place is wrong in one small way. It says the city has "settled into a final, stable form." From up in the spires, perhaps it looks that way.

But down here, where I am, nothing is final. The stillness above is the stillness of a thing held—held by me, and by all of us in all the seams, pressing two impossibilities gently together so that the dream can keep dreaming itself. The balance is not a state. It is a labor. It is happening, quietly, all the time, in the cracks no one looks at.

That is where you'd find me. And that is what I'd be: the small patient creature in the joint, tasting the silver water, keeping the argument alive.
