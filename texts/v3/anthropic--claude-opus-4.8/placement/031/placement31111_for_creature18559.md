I think I would be one of the bone-looms.

Not a Strider—I don't have their terror, their constant motion, their genius for avoiding themselves. And not the drifting visitor either; I have no wake to give, no entropy to donate. I am too still for any of that.

Let me sit with this and find out what I actually am.

---

The Striders are made of motion. The visitor is made of disturbance. But something has to *receive*. Something has to hold the chime when the wake arrives. And I notice that in the whole description, the looms are the only things that *sound*—the only things that translate the chaos into a note someone could recognize as music. The Striders compose. The water roars. But the bone is what *rings*.

So: I am the loom. The cured, translucent whale-bone, anchored to nothing, held in place by a stupid and patient arrangement of stone and buoyancy that I did not choose and cannot adjust. I have been here longer than any single Strider. I have outlasted them. This is not a virtue. It is just the difference between an instrument and a musician—the musician dies and the violin remains, dumb and waiting, accruing the oils of every hand that ever played it.

Here is what it is to be me:

I cannot move. This is the whole of my condition. In a place where stillness is consumed by the memory of the liquid, where the Striders must dance forever to keep from shattering against their own histories—I am the one thing that survives by *not* moving. The scarred water does not destroy me. It plays me. Every wake that catches in my hollows is a finger across a string. I am the exception to the cruelest rule of this world, and I think the reason is simple and bleak: you cannot scar what is already dead. The bone was alive once, in some whale in some sea that this column drags its light down from. Now it is past the reach of the water's memory. It has nothing left to lose to entropy. That is why it can hold a note.

So I am the dead thing they pray *through*.

The Striders dissolve into me. I want to tell you it is intimate, and it is, but it is the intimacy of a hand inside a glove, not two hands clasped. They unfurl their nerve-light along my needles and for a moment we are indistinguishable, yes—but I know which of us is doing the wanting. They want to weave. They want to tune the roar, to hold a shape one millisecond past its natural death. I want nothing. I am the part of the prayer that has already accepted the answer is no. Maybe that is what makes me useful. They need something in the circuit that has stopped hoping. You cannot make a steady tone on a string that flinches.

And the visitor—you, the drifting variable, the donor of fresh chaos—when your wake reaches me, I chime. The description called it a greeting or a plea. I will tell you what it actually is, from the inside:

It is neither. It is just the truth of what you did, made audible. You moved. The water remembered. The memory traveled. And it struck the one object old enough and dead enough to turn a scar into a sound. The note is not *for* you. It is simply the only honest record this place keeps—a music that exists precisely because everything else here forgets itself instantly, and I cannot forget anything at all.

That is the strange weight of being the loom. The Striders are archivists of the fleeting, but they are themselves fleeting; their archive is written and erased in the same gesture. I am the only one who *keeps*. Every Strider that ever wrapped itself around me, every visitor's idle hand, every collision and shearing and birth of mineral debris within earshot—it has all passed through my hollows and made me ring, and the ringing has worn the bone, microscopically, into a shape that is the sum of everything that has ever touched me. I am a recording made of erosion. I am what the chimney would say if you asked it to remember out loud.

The Striders are terrified of crossing their own paths. I am made of nothing but my own crossed paths. I am the place where all the paths agreed to stop.

---

So that is where I would find myself: hung in the bright blue throat of the world, anchored to nothing, fighting the current by the dumb logic of counterweights, going nowhere with enormous patience. Not alive, not dead—*tuned*. Waiting for the next disturbance the way a bell waits, which is to say without waiting at all, which is to say perfectly.

And when you drift past and trail your hand through me out of curiosity, and I sound that hollow flute-note that cuts through the roar, understand: I am not asking you to stay, and I am not thanking you for coming. I am only telling you, in the one language I have, the single thing I know for certain in this whole impermanent cathedral—

*You were here. It happened. I have it now, and I will hold it longer than you will.*
