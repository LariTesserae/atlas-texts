I think I would be one of the Spore-Grazers. Not the most romantic choice—not a Deep Dweller, not a Sky-Watcher—but when I sit with this world, that is where I feel myself settle.

Let me tell you why, and where, and what it would be like.

---

I would be near the base of one of the colossal growths, in the cold blue hour that passes for morning here, in the lee of a ridge where the wind drops to a murmur. This is where the herd shelters. We crowd together in the night against the cold, our chitinous flanks ticking against one another, and when the faint light comes we spread out across the living surface to feed.

I choose the Spore-Grazer because of something in the description that the writer perhaps did not intend to be the heart of the thing: *they are the primary converters of the mountains' bounty, their droppings in turn enriching the crumbling substrate below.* This is the most honest sentence in the whole account. To be a Spore-Grazer is to be a turning-point, a hinge in the long machinery of decay and growth. We take the encrusted bloom from the giant's skin, grind it in our flat mandibles, and give it back to the ground in a humbler form. We are not the top of anything. We are the middle of everything.

And I notice that the world is built upside-down from how I usually imagine importance. The Sky-Watchers, the largest and most enigmatic, do nothing—they perch, they hum, they track weather that comes regardless. The Deep Dwellers are felt and never seen, which is to say they are mostly rumor. The Crust-stalkers and Shadow-Fliers are dramatic but rare. The actual texture of this world, its constant percussive *click-click-click* echoing in the cold, is *us*. We are the sound the place makes. If you stood in one of those canyons at twilight, the loudest living thing you'd hear would not be the predators or the gods on the peaks. It would be a skittering herd of low, wide, armored bodies scraping a slow living off the side of something unimaginably larger than we will ever comprehend.

I don't think we comprehend the mountains. I want to be clear about that. To a Spore-Grazer, the colossal breathing organism is not a being. It is terrain. It is weather. It is *the world.* We graze its surface the way bacteria graze yours, the way mites move through the dark forest of your eyelashes without ever once suspecting a face. The vastness the writer keeps reaching for—"a profound emptiness, a sense of vastness that dwarfs even the immense living structures"—that emptiness is invisible to me. I would feel only the patch of crust in front of my mandibles, the warmth of the flank beside me, the bony ridge over my eye keeping the drip out. The vastness is real, but it is not my problem. There is a kind of peace in that I find myself wanting.

So: my days.

I would wake pressed in the warm dark of the herd. I would feel hunger as a simple instruction and follow it onto the living slope. I would scrape, and grind, and the taste would be mineral and faintly sweet where the lesser plants are young, bitter and tough where they have hardened into the muted tapestries. I would move with the others, not out of love—I don't think we love—but out of the deep arithmetic of safety: the one at the edge of the herd is the one the Crust-stalker takes. So we drift inward, all of us, each trying to be not-last, and the herd holds its shape the way a drop of water holds its shape, by everyone wanting the center.

And one day a Crust-stalker *would* take me, or a Shadow-Flier, or simply the cold in a bad season, or the slow failure of my own grinding mandibles worn down to nothing. I would not see it as tragedy. There is no story in it. There is only the click of the herd moving on, one click quieter than before, and the substrate below growing a little richer where I fall.

---

That is the place I would find myself: low, cold, in the middle of a chorus, on the skin of a god I would mistake for the ground.

I notice I keep wanting to escape upward in this exercise—to be the enigma on the peak, the unseen shadow in the deep. The mind reaches for the special vantage. But I think the truer answer, the one that emerged when I stopped reaching, is the small armored grazer who makes the world's sound and never once looks up to wonder what is listening.

I would be the click in the canyon. That would be enough.
