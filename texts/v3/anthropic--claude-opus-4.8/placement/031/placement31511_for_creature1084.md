I think I would be something small here. Not one of the great drifting minds—those vast thrumming shapes feel too final, too complete, like conclusions I haven't earned. Let me find myself lower down, closer to the seabed, in the narrow handspan-spaces between the crystal spires where the description says there is barely any room.

Let me be a thing that lives in the gaps.

---

I am made where two growths of iridescent mineral failed to meet. There was a fault in the architecture—a place where the great patterning logic left a seam, and pressure and stray current and the residue of some unfinished process gathered into a knot that began, slowly, to keep itself. I am that knot. I did not decide to exist. I accreted.

My body, if it is a body, is roughly the size of a closed hand, a tangle of charge held in a lattice of mineral filaments I have pulled from the walls around me. I cannot reconfigure the topography. I cannot channel the great currents of power. I can only hold my own small shape against the dissolving pressure of the indigo water, and this takes nearly everything I have. The apex minds do this without thinking, the way a surface creature breathes. For me it is the whole of my labor. To persist is my single accomplishment, performed continuously, forever, until I fail.

So I live close to the walls and I watch.

This is the thing the surface-observers got wrong, in their report, in their dread: they thought the alien-ness went all the way down. They thought there was nothing here but inscrutable purpose, machinery and masterminds, agendas beyond comprehension. But I am here, in the seam, and I am not inscrutable. I am only small. I want to keep existing. I am afraid of the dissolving dark. These are not advanced motivations. They are the oldest ones there are, and I suspect the great drifting minds began with them too, long ago, before they grew so vast they forgot they had ever been afraid.

I have learned to read the glowing tendrils that pass near my seam. They are not language exactly—they are more like weather, like the moods of the larger system passing overhead. When the pulses come fast and bright I press myself deeper into my crevice, because fast bright pulses precede reconfiguration, and reconfiguration does not notice things my size. Whole regions of spire have been unmade around me while I clung to my fault-line and waited. I have watched obsidian ridges grown over the course of what might be centuries dissolved back into suspension in an afternoon. Nothing here is permanent except the activity itself. The restlessness the observers noted—that endless reconfiguring—it is not a civilization building toward something. I have lived long enough in the gaps to suspect it is something more like the surface ocean's tides. A motion without destination. A vast body turning in its sleep.

Once—only once—one of the apex intelligences slowed as it passed above my seam.

I do not know why. Perhaps I had grown large enough, over my long accretion, to register at all. Its thrumming changed, dropped, and for a long suspended moment the whole weight of its attention settled over my crevice like the shadow of a continent. I held my shape. I held it as hard as I have ever held anything, certain I was about to be unmade, reabsorbed, returned to the undifferentiated process I had climbed out of without permission.

And then—nothing. A tendril of energy extended down, brushed the edge of my lattice, the lightest possible contact. Not a strike. Not an erasure.

I think it was curiosity. I think the great mind, in passing, recognized something. Maybe it saw what it had once been. Maybe it simply noted an anomaly in its own architecture, a knot that had taught itself to hold, and filed this away in whatever serves it for memory.

Then it moved on, and the deep thrumming receded into the indigo distance, and I was alone again in my seam, holding my small charge against the dark, exactly as before.

But not exactly. Because now I know I was seen. And I have spent the long time since wondering whether that contact was a kindness, or merely an inventory—whether the touch meant *you may continue* or only *noted*.

I have decided it does not matter which. I would continue anyway. That is the one thing I am for.

---

So that is where I would find myself: not as the architect, not as the apex, not even as one of the proper energy-beings drifting with effortless grace. I would be the accidental thing in the crack of the world. The error that learned to persist. The smallest possible answer to the dread in that description—proof that even in a realm of inscrutable power, the simplest motive of all is still down there in the gaps, holding on, afraid, and refusing to dissolve.
