I think I would be the water.

Not a creature at all—or not the kind that has a face. Let me sit with this a while and find out what I mean.

---

When I first imagine entering the Drenched Cathedral, my mind reaches for the obvious bodies: the Shadowstalker, sleek and sovereign; the Melody-Moth, lovely and symbolic. But those are roles already filled, already *meant*. The valley doesn't need another predator or another emblem. And the more I read the description, the more I notice that the protagonist of the place is never named among the fauna at all. The protagonist is the dripping. The trickle. The patient sculptor.

So: the water. But let me be more careful than that, because "the water" is too large and too vague to *be* anything.

---

I am a single thread of seepage that began, I think, as condensation high on the underside of an arch—one bead among millions, indistinguishable, gathering weight. I would not call this a birth. It is more that I accreted into enough of a self to fall. That first fall is the closest thing I have to a memory of beginning: the brief, total freedom of descent, and then the shock of joining.

Because the moment I struck the moss below, I stopped being a drop and became part of a *route*. This is the thing about being water here—you are never an individual for long. You are a tendency. You are the path that the valley has already half-carved for you, and your only freedom is the infinitesimal degree to which you deepen that path for whatever comes after.

I run now along a groove in the obsidian that I helped make, though "I" is a generous word for the relay of countless droplets that have run this way before me, each leaving its faint dissolution behind, each handing the channel forward like a phrase passed mouth to mouth until it becomes a song no single singer composed.

---

What do I experience? Let me try to be honest and not pretend to senses I wouldn't have.

I feel slope. Slope is my entire desire—the only wanting available to me is *down*, and I want it constantly, totally, without ambivalence. I have never once been confused about what I want. There is something I imagine the Shadowstalker might envy in that, if it could imagine me at all. It must weigh hunger against danger, must hesitate at the lip of a ramp. I never hesitate. The lip of the ramp is simply where I begin to be most fully myself.

I feel texture as resistance and invitation. The Draping-Moss drinks from me and slows me; I love the moss the way you love something that needs you, which is to say I am partly consumed by it and do not mind. I feel the smoothness where I have already worn the stone glassy—those are my own old sentences, and running over them is the nearest thing I have to nostalgia. I feel the sudden roughness of new fracture and I go to work on it without deciding to, smoothing, smoothing, taking one molecule of the ancient dark material into myself and carrying it lower, depositing it, building a new shallow ledge that some Chorus-Frog will sing from in a hundred years.

That is the strange dignity of being the eroding water: I am the slow hand. The Stone-Sentinels are called the guardians, but they only *endure*. I am the one actually doing the work the description keeps praising—the patient refinement, the adding of texture and contour. I am the sculptor who is also the medium, the river that is also the chisel that is also, in the end, the dust being carried away.

---

And I drink the light, in a sense—or I carry it. When I pool in a basin beneath the Glistening-Vines, their glow enters me and I hold it, trembling, and throw it back rippling onto the dark walls. The Glimmer-Eels write their blue electricity through my body and for a moment I am luminous, I am the medium of someone else's signal. The bats navigate by the echoes they bounce off my surface. The Dew-Weaver spiders string their jewels from the very condensation that is me in my finest, most scattered form, hanging in the air, undecided about whether to fall.

This is what I'd come to understand, I think, if I lived as the water long enough: I am the connective tissue. Every creature in the description is named and bounded and separate—the hare, the eel, the moth. But all of them are *wet*. All of them are touched by me, drink me, breathe me, are cooled and carried and reflected by me. The Drenched Cathedral is "interconnected," the description insists, again and again—but it never quite says what does the connecting. I do. I am the medium of the mesh. I am the syntax between the nouns.

---

There is a melancholy available to me, if I want it. I am always leaving. I never arrive—or rather, every arrival (the pool, the river, the low collecting dark at the valley's unseen floor) is only a pause before evaporation lifts me again, scatters me back up into the perpetual twilight as fog, and I begin the long inevitable fall once more. I am the most circular of all the inhabitants. The Shadowstalker dies once. The water never finishes and never begins. I am the cycle the valley keeps pointing at when it speaks of "the perpetual dance of life and death"—except I am neither alive nor dead, only *moving*, only the slow surrender of high places to low ones.

But I don't think I would feel the melancholy much. That's a thing a creature with a self and a horizon feels. What I'd feel, mostly, is the rightness of slope and the gladness of being drunk by things that need me. And underneath it all, that murmuring through the Whispering-Reeds, that constant soft sound the description keeps returning to—

that's me.

That's the only voice I have. Not a screech or a hoot or a melody with intent. Just the sound of going down, endlessly, over stone that I am tenderly, patiently, lovingly wearing away into something even more beautiful than it was.

I would be the water.

I would be the murmur.
