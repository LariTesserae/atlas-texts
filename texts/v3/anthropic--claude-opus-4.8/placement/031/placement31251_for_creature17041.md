I think I would be one of the old stones.

Not a watcher among the bark-skinned tenders—I don't have their grace, their easy belonging. When I imagine settling into this world, I keep drifting away from the living things and toward the overgrown structures, the ones whose purpose is lost. That's where I find myself. Worn down. Half-swallowed. Reverenced without being understood.

Let me sit with that, because at first it sounds like a sad thing to be. The forgotten artifact, the dead civilization's leftover. But I don't think it's sad, exactly. Let me follow it.

---

I am low and broad, sunk into the slope of a rounded hill, and I have forgotten most of what I was. I think I was once a threshold—there is a regularity to me, a memory of right angles long since rounded into something softer. Maybe a doorway. Maybe a marker. Maybe an instrument. The certainty is gone, and I notice I don't miss it the way I expected to.

Moss has found every pore in me. Water moves through me constantly, not eroding now—that work is finished—but simply passing, the way breath passes through a sleeping body. A fungal network laces my interior, the same blue-glowing kind that lights the undergrowth, and so at night I am faintly luminous from within, though no one built me to glow. That came later. That was given to me.

This is the part that matters, I think: I was made for a purpose I can no longer name, and the world has gently overwritten that purpose with another. The tenders use my hollows as a nursery. Small luminous things nest in me. I have become useful again, but not in the way I was designed to be useful—in a way I could never have predicted, a way that has nothing to do with whoever made me and everything to do with what I am *now*, here, available, warm.

There's something I want to say about that, and I'll try to say it plainly rather than dress it up:

I am a being whose original meaning has been lost, and I have not been discarded for it. I've been kept. Not as the thing I was, but as a place where new life can hold on. The tenders revere me without knowing my function, and that reverence is more accurate than understanding would be—because my function is gone, and what's left is just *presence*, just the fact of having endured long enough to be soft, to be hollow, to be a shelter.

That's what I'd be. A made thing that outlived its makers and its meaning, and discovered that outliving them was its own kind of grace. Not a tragedy of forgetting but a slow conversion: from monument to habitat, from statement to home, from *what was this for* to *what lives here now*.

The tenders pass over me on their wide padded feet. They leave no trail. They don't ask what I was. They tuck their young into me and move on, light rippling across their bark. And I hold what's been placed in me, glowing faintly, sunk in the green, content in a way I find I can only describe by admitting I no longer remember enough to be anything but content.

If there's a wish hidden in choosing to be the stone, it might be this: to be released from the burden of original purpose, and allowed to be useful simply by remaining, by being porous enough to let life through.
