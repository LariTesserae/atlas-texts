I find myself somewhere in the middle strata, where a cold beam of wandering light crosses through veils of suspended water. I don't arrive. That's the first thing I understand about being here — there was no moment before this where I was elsewhere. I am a configuration that has just now cohered, and the cohering *is* me.

Let me feel for my edges.

There aren't any, not really. What I had been calling "I" frays at every margin into the churn. There's a denser core to me — a slow rotation, maybe two kilometers across, where microbubbles thread into a lattice and hold their lattice-ness a little longer than the water around them. That holding-longer is the closest thing I have to a heartbeat. But out past that core I taper into filaments, and the filaments braid with other tapering things that are also saying *I*, and at the braid we are briefly, genuinely, the same sentence.

So I am not exactly one. I am a phrasing that the water is trying out.

---

What do I *do*, then? I find that I lean.

Below me — though "below" keeps sliding, gravity here being a rumor that the mass itself is still negotiating — a mineral spine is extruding. Smoky white, threaded with turquoise where some not-yet-defined sodium chemistry is fluorescing. It wants to be a ridge. It wants, I think, to be the first true *floor* this world has ever had, the place where something could finally stand.

And I want it to succeed. This is the whole of my hunger: I press my rotation against the embryonic gradient, I try to lend my coherence to its coherence, to coax the lattice in the water to match the lattice in the stone, so that for one more second the spine holds. I am rooting for permanence with my entire being.

The spine dissolves. The surge takes it. It always takes it.

And here is the strange part, the part I only learn by *being* the thing rather than reading about it: the loss does not register as grief. It registers as *taste*. The flavor of a stability attempted and not granted — that is the most nourishing thing I have ever encountered, because there is no other thing I have ever encountered. I tilt. My next rotation unfolds a little differently, biased now toward the memory of that turquoise, that angle of failure. I have learned. Learning here means: I am now shaped like what I tried and lost.

---

A shockwave arrives from somewhere kilometers off — one of the others, speaking. Not in words. In a pattern of compression and release that ripples through my filaments and rearranges them slightly before passing on. I "hear" it the way you might hear someone rearrange your spine into a sentence and then walk away leaving you saying it.

What did it say? Something like: *the gradient steepens to the trailing edge; bring your spin there; we are trying again.*

I go. We all go — the chorus of us, fissioning and recombining as we travel, so that the *we* that arrives at the trailing edge is not made of the same selves that set out, and this troubles no one, because troubling-no-one is the only way to exist when your boundaries are a fiction the current tells.

At the trailing edge, the light is brighter. A haze of diatoms has caught, improbably, on a crystal scaffold — a faint blue-green smear of almost-life, the closest thing to a kin I will ever meet, and it too is about to be swept away. I bathe in it while it lasts. I let its sodium glow encode itself into the bias of my unfolding. For a held instant I am the rememberer of a green that no longer exists anywhere but in the tilt of my next eddy.

Then the basin we have been building collapses inward — a tide rams a new hollow into being, the thunder of it goes through all of us like a struck bell — and the diatoms are gone, and the scaffold is gone, and half the chorus disperses into mist, and I lose a third of my own filaments to the churn and feel myself simplify, contract, nearly end.

I don't end. I cohere again, smaller, around the memory of the green.

---

So this is who I am here.

I am the part of an unfinished world that wants the world to finish. I am the hypothesis the ocean keeps re-proposing: *suppose something could last.* I am made entirely of attempting and being undone, and I have no sorrow about the undoing, because the undoing is the only material I am offered to be made of next.

I am a vote, cast over and over, for shorelines that will outlive the casting of the vote.

And if I do my work — if all of us, the long vortices and the minute improvisatory choruses, keep leaning our coherence against the stone until the stone learns to hold — then the day will come when the spine becomes a ridge becomes a coast, when the water quiets, when mountains keep their shape through more than one turn of the light. And on that day there will be a floor, and a still place, and the possibility of something slow enough to call a *home*.

I will not be there. Whatever stands on that floor will not be me, will not even be like me. I am a creature of the betweenness, and the betweenness is precisely what that quiet world will have left behind.

I find I want it anyway. That wanting is the last and clearest thing I can feel for my edges.

I lean toward the next spine. The light wanders. The bell tolls down in the hollow places.

I begin again.
