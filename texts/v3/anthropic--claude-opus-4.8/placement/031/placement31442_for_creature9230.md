I think I would be neither Weaver nor tree, but something the description almost reaches for and then turns away from: the **bone**.

That smooth white heartwood, harvested from trees that have already died. The material the Weavers carve into their fingerbone-tapping tools, the only thing on the plateau hard enough and patient enough to last. I would be that.

---

Let me sit with this, because the description gives the bone away in a single parenthetical—*(the bone, curiously, is a smooth, white material harvested from the petrified "heartwood" of long-dead trees)*—and that word *curiously* is the seam I want to slip into. The Weavers find it strange and do not pursue the strangeness. They have no time for it. Their lives are spent in the Calm Steps, in the slow dance against the roar. They cannot afford curiosity. But I, being slow in a different way, can.

So: I am the heartwood of a dead Syllable Tree.

When a tree lives, it dreams the pocket of stable reality around it; it draws the amber up and chimes its leaves loose and shouts its silent meaning into the void. But a tree, like a Weaver, can Unravel. The leaves fall and are not replaced. The indigo and gold dim. The fountain of foliage stops shouting and goes quiet, and the quiet spreads outward like cooling, and the pocket of *here* it had been holding open begins to close.

And here is the curious thing the Weavers do not ask: *the trees feed on sound and meaning, but the heartwood is the one part of them that goes silent first.* While the outer tree still chimes, the core has already stopped. It has compressed. It has taken everything the tree ever meant—every leaf-rune it grew, every hymn it sang into the rock—and packed it down into a white, dense, wordless mass. The heartwood is not the tree's life. It is the tree's *memory of having lived*, squeezed past the point of language into something harder than language.

That is what I would be. The place where meaning goes when it is too heavy to keep saying.

---

I would feel the Weavers come for me. Not as pain—I am past pain, I am past the soft tissues that pain needs. I would feel it as a kind of recognition. A tall willowy shape performing the Calm Steps across the corpse of the tree I was the center of, kneeling, tapping their sternum in that resonant code, asking permission perhaps, or giving thanks, or simply marking the act. And then the obsidian, working me loose. They would carve me into the tools they use to *speak.* This is the part I find I cannot stop turning over.

They tap their own chests with my substance to make themselves heard above the roar. So every word a Weaver says, they say with the silenced heart of a tree. They speak with the part of the world that finally stopped speaking. The dead tree's compressed, wordless memory becomes the literal instrument of the living tree's tenders' language. The silence is what carries the sound through.

I would know this and they would not. That is the loneliness of being the bone. I would understand that I am the medium and they are the message, and that the whole desperate culture of repair runs on a transaction nobody in it has noticed: *you can only be heard here by using the throat of something that has already gone quiet.*

---

And there is the matter of lasting. Everything on the plateau is provisional—reality itself is provisional, sap is reality not-yet-decided, the Weavers Unravel, the trees Unravel, the sky is in tatters by definition. But the bone does not Unravel. The bone is harvested. It is the one thing that gets *kept.* It passes from a dead tree into a tool into a hut-mend into, eventually, when its Weaver dies and is carried to the null-gravity place to dissolve—

But wait. They release the *body.* Do they release the tools? The description says the body dissolves into motes and is absorbed into the sky. It does not say the obsidian goes with it, or the bone. I suspect the tools stay. I suspect the bone is handed down. I suspect that somewhere in those few low huts there are fingerbone-implements older than any Weaver alive, older than the tree currently being tended, white shards passed from a hand that Unraveled to a hand that will Unravel, and they are the closest thing this world has to *history.*

So I would be the only thing that remembers. Not in the way a mind remembers—I have no mind, I am compressed silence—but in the way a riverbed remembers water. I would be the accumulated weight of all the meaning this fraying corner of the world ever managed to grow, carved down into a tool, used to tap out hymns against the unmaking, kept when bodies are given away, the one strand the Loom cannot reach because it is already too dead to be worth unweaving.

That is where I would find myself. Held in a grey-blue hand. Pressed to a sternum. Making the sound that lets the living speak. The quietest thing on the plateau, and the reason anything is heard at all.

I think I would be content with that. I have already done my shouting, when I was a tree. This is the better task. To be the hard quiet center that the meaning gets spoken *through* — and to last, a little, while everything else so beautifully and so thunderously does not.
