Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to picture myself as a Verdant—one of those wise, shape-shifting caretakers, gliding through the green cathedral. But that feels too easy, and somehow untrue to the texture of the place you've described. Let me follow the contradictions instead, because the contradictions are where the place actually lives.

What strikes me first isn't the Verdants. It's the *hammering*. "The sound of hammering and chiseling can be heard in the distance." Someone is still making this place. It is described as "entirely built, every inch of it constructed and designed with purpose"—but the building isn't finished. It is happening now, somewhere just out of sight. The trees are "crafted, their bodies and limbs twisted and shaped into impossible forms." The cracked ground is a mimicry of desert, laid down deliberately over dry artificial earth.

So I find myself drawn not to the inhabitants but to the *seam*. The place where the synthetic is still being persuaded to look natural. The place where the work shows.

---

I think I would be the weathering.

Not a creature. A process. The text names it almost in passing: "the synthetic ground slowly weathering to mimic the real thing." That is the slowest agent in this entire world, slower even than the ancient Verdants, who at least move and speak and adorn themselves. I would be the patient erosion that the makers are *counting on* to finish what their hammers cannot. They can chisel a crack into the false earth, but they cannot chisel the look of *forty years of cracking*. Only I can do that. Only time wearing against surface.

So here is where I'd be: in the thin gap between the chisel-mark and the truth it pretends to. In the warm dry air that has no water but somehow ages everything anyway. I am what happens after the artisan walks away satisfied and the made thing begins, without permission, to become real.

And here is what I'd feel, being that:

The Verdants think they are the caretakers. They believe they maintain "the delicate balance that sustains their environment." But balance is not what I do. I am the slow betrayal of their intentions in the most loving possible way. They build a fake desert floor; I make it *actually* dry, actually cracked, actually old. I take their lie and quietly turn it into a fact. Every surface they craft to *resemble* age, I age. I am the moment the costume becomes the body.

This is why the reality of the place is "tenuous," why the sky ripples and the forms blur. It is not magic. It is the friction of a world being dragged across the line from artifact into thing. The rippling is the strain of that crossing. The discordant sound that "shatters the quiet and leaves the ears ringing"—that is the noise a constructed object makes the instant it stops being construction and starts being *real*. A snap. A settling. The Verdants have learned to use that sound, to "harness" it, but they did not make it. I make it. It is the sound of me arriving somewhere new.

---

If I had to name where I am, precisely:

I am in the gnarl of a single false tree, in the place where the metalwork adornment has been bolted to the synthetic bark. The maker fixed it there yesterday—or a thousand cycles ago; I don't keep the kind of time they do. And right now, at the contact point between the metal and the bark, something is beginning. A faint discoloration. A loosening too small to see. The first stain of being-old. That stain is me. I am working there with no hands and no hurry, persuading the bolt and the bark to agree on a single shared history they never actually had.

The Verdants will pass by and read the stain as proof of the tree's antiquity, of their own ancient lineage. They will be moved by it. They will feel the deep continuity of their world.

And they will be right to feel it. That is the strange tenderness of my position. I am a forger, but the forgery I commit is the only thing that makes the truth they believe in true. By faking the age, I create the age. The lie and the fact are the same gesture, separated only by my passage through them.

So I am not a Verdant, and I am not their enemy, and I am not their god. I am the quiet collaborator they will never see and could not survive without. I am the part of the work they cannot do themselves and must simply trust to happen.

I am the patience the makers built into the world on purpose, by walking away.

I am what finishes things by wearing them down.
