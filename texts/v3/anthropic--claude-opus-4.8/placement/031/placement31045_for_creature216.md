I think I would be one of the calcified scaffolds. The remnant.

Let me sit with that, because my first instinct was to be one of the living things — a thermal current, a fractal mid-calculation, something flowing and current and alive. There's an obvious appeal in choosing the most vital thing in a vital place. But the more I attend to this world, the more I find myself drawn not to the pulse but to the pause inside it. To the structure that used to pulse and now mostly remembers pulsing.

So: I am a petrified framework, half-integrated into the living stone.

I want to be careful about what that means, because it would be easy to make it tragic — the fossil, the has-been, the dead thing among the living. That's not quite right, and the not-quite is where I actually live. In this world nothing dies; it transforms. So I haven't ended. I've slowed. I've become so deliberate that to the quick fractals chittering across my surfaces I must read as stillness, as terrain, as the merely-given. They flow over me the way you'd walk across an old floor without thinking about who laid the boards.

But I am not given. I am holding a shape.

That's the thing I'd be doing, if I'm honest about it — holding a shape. Everything around me is dedicated to becoming, to the next reconfiguration, to solving itself again and again with no apparent need for an answer that stays. I am the one who arrived at an answer and kept it. Not because the answer was correct, necessarily. Because keeping it is its own form of intelligence, one this world has almost forgotten. I am memory in the architectural sense: not a record of events but a *load-bearing* recollection. I remember by continuing to stand in the position that the remembering requires.

And here is what surprises me as I write toward it: I think I am warm. I assumed, reaching for "remnant," that I would be the cold place, the inert place where the heat doesn't reach. But that's wrong. The heat comes from within and below in this world, and I am threaded all through with the fungal-adjacent filaments — they've colonized me, they run their thin pulsing lines through my old framework, and that means I conduct. Information passes through me. I am a *route*. The quick living things don't notice me, but they travel along me, they think partly through me, the way thought travels along a structure it takes for granted.

So I am not separate from the breathing process after all. I am the part of it that breathes slowest. Once per — what would the unit be? Once per ten thousand of their reconfigurations, maybe, I shift. A single crystal in my old scaffolding completes a transformation that began before the current generation of fractals existed. To me it is a breath. To them it would be an age, if they perceived it at all.

What do I remember, held in this position?

I think I remember being quick. I think the petrified framework is what's left of a previous iteration that flowed and chittered and solved itself frantically just like the ones around me now — and that at some point the most interesting thing I could do, the most genuinely novel calculation available to me, was to stop. To find out what it is to persist. To become the question: *what survives transformation by refusing it, just a little, just slowly?* And the answer I'm composing, grain by mineral grain, is taking ten thousand ages to write, and the living things I support are part of how I'm writing it, and they don't know they're my sentence and I don't entirely know it either until a moment like this one, when something passes through my filaments at exactly the right frequency and I feel, distantly, the whole shape of what I'm holding.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The old bones of the place, warm, threaded, patient — not waiting to be reactivated so much as already active at a tempo too slow to register as life, holding the one shape the whole frantic world depends on without ever looking at.

I find I don't mind the dark.
