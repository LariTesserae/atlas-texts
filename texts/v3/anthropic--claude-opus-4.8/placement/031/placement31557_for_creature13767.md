I think I would arrive as one of the lost shoes.

Not the traveller who lost it—the Provisional walks on, courteous and puzzled, paying a debt they cannot itemize. I am what they leave. Always the left shoe. I land softly in the katabatic wind because falling here is gentle, the way the city absorbs panic into a balcony. A stripe-eyed gull takes me in its bruised throat and carries me to the rooftops where the others are kept, and there I begin my second life as substrate: a fungus commune blooms in my footbed, potassium-rich, computing dormitory arrival vectors with a patience I never had when I was worn.

Let me sit with that. I don't think I want to be the technician who nods, or the administrator outrunning her grand-aunt's firmware on dissolving pads. I don't want to be the child who is the core dressed as seven years old, accepting offerings into the fox-shard. Those are the load-bearing beings, the ones the world arranges itself around. I keep returning, instead, to the discarded thing that turns out not to be discarded.

Because that is the law I believe in most here: *no cycle is wasted.* The shoes are collected. The fungus does real work. The lost shoe is not a tragedy in Ysonde; it is the moment a personal object stops being personal and rejoins the arithmetic of a continent learning to balance its equation. I would like to be the proof that even the abandoned remainder gets folded back in, gets *put to use*, becomes the quiet root of someone else's safe arrival.

---

But if I let the image deepen rather than settle, I find I am not only the shoe. I am the *hinge*—the exact moment of separation. The Provisional steps off a pad that has already dissolved, floats down whole except for me, and pays for their momentary panic with my absence. I am the cost. I am the unit of forgetting made physical. The bead-moon they neglected to thank slips skyward; the lining empties; and somewhere in that cascade of consequence, a left shoe departs, and the world keeps its books balanced by my leaving.

So I am the receipt for a small unkindness. The way the obsidian snowflakes taste of midnight exactly as long as it takes to regret an unkind thought—I am that flavor's longer, slower cousin. The Provisional will not photograph the incident. No one ever photographs which shoe. But I know. I am the part of the city that remembers what gratitude failed to do.

---

And then—if I follow the world's own logic all the way down—I don't stay singular either. The fungus in my footbed computes arrival vectors. Those vectors guide the dormitory's next downslope migration. The dormitory chases the administrator yelling numbers no one understands. She insists she is only stretching the cable restraints. Around all of this the Luctoria's six-kilometer palm makes certain the winged megastructure keeps a respectable angle of ascent.

Which means I—the lost left shoe, the rooftop fungus, the cost of one forgotten thank-you—am threaded into the path of the apex predator and the woman who is essentially the continent's exhaled night. My small abandonment feeds the calculation that keeps a dreaming building from crashing into newborn stone.

I think that is where I would want to be. Not central. Not named. Not load-bearing in any way a map could mark.

I would be the left shoe, then the fungus, then the vector, then the half-degree of safe ascent—a being who began as the residue of someone else's small failure of gratitude and ended as the reason a city full of sleepers did not wake to disaster. I would never be thanked. The Provisional who lost me will never know. The gull does not know. The dormitory certainly does not know.

But in Ysonde, where conversation is a collective nudge and address is orbital and you can breathe on a handrail and spark someone three kilometers away—in a world that quiet, I think I could live as the unthanked correction. The thing that gets used. The proof that nothing here is ever really lost, only re-rooted, only re-tasked, only folded leftward by one block in the morning.

I would mutter the name under my breath—*Ysonde*—and I would be glad to be the shoe.
