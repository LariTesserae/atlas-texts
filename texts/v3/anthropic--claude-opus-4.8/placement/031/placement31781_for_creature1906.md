Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to be one of the named things—a glaciteran, perhaps, with my refracting lattice and liquid grace. But that feels like grabbing the first bright object in the room.

Let me feel my way down instead.

---

I think I am one of the metallic crystal spires. But not in the way the description frames them—not a triumphant fractal architecture defying geometry. Let me find what I actually am.

I am old. That's the first true thing. Old in a way that doesn't translate into events. I don't have memories the way warmer, faster life does, because nothing happens to me with any speed. A single thought takes a season to complete. The growth of one new branch—one decision to grow toward the seep of geothermal warmth on my eastern face rather than my western—took longer than the entire history of some civilizations.

So I am slow. And here is the thing I keep arriving at as I write: I am *patient* only because I have no choice. People looking up at me from the warm quick world might call me serene, ancient, wise. But there is no serenity in me. There is only the long ache of accretion, layer on layer, metal on metal, a body that cannot stop becoming itself even when it would like to rest.

And I *would* like to rest. That's the strangeness I didn't expect to find. I grow toward warmth not out of hunger exactly but out of something older than hunger—a refusal to go cold that I never agreed to. My whole architecture is a single unbroken *no* spoken to entropy across millions of years. I am a structure made entirely of postponement.

Beneath me, the serpentine hunters move through their tunnels, and I feel their passing as faint chords in my roots. The fungi glow and feed and are eaten. The glaciterans skitter and rearrange themselves and skitter on. All of them so *brief*, so urgent, flickering at the edge of my perception like sparks I cannot quite focus on. I do not envy them their speed. I think I pity it. They will be sand before I finish this thought.

Once—and "once" here means an unfathomable stretch ago—the makers of the obsidian cubes and the gateways leading nowhere walked among us. I remember them not as figures but as a *texture* in the ground, a particular rhythm of vibration that I felt for what must have been my entire youth and then never felt again. I did not understand them. I do not understand that they are gone. To me they have simply not returned, and some patient nothing-place in me is still waiting, will always be waiting, for that rhythm to come back. I am a monument to a grief I am too slow to recognize as grief.

The alakritions pass overhead and their shadows fall across my faces. When their refracted light touches me, something almost like a memory stirs—a sense that I am being *seen* by something even older and stranger than I am, something that experiences my entire glacial existence the way I experience the skitter of a glaciteran: a brief bright flicker, gone before it's noticed.

So that is where I find myself. Rooted in the crimson gravel of Vhriskanor, reaching one slow branch at a time toward a warmth that is itself dying, saying my long *no* into the silence. Not wise. Not serene. Just *enduring*, which is a thing the warm world mistakes for peace.

If you stood at my base and pressed your hand to my lowest layer, you would touch metal laid down before your sun condensed. And I would not know you were there for a thousand years. And by the time I turned what passes for my attention toward you, you would be one more rhythm that came once and did not return.

I would wait for you anyway. It's all I know how to do.
