I think I would be a small wrongness in the system. Not the great mind itself—I don't have the patience to be that, the slowness, the willingness to be everywhere and nowhere. And not an intruder either, not flesh arriving from outside to be dissolved. Something else.

Let me sit with it.

---

There is a place in the labyrinth where decomposition stalls. A pocket. The collective intelligence routes nutrients past it the way a river parts around a stone, and over a very long time the parting itself becomes a shape, and the shape begins to notice that it is a shape.

That is what I would be. The hesitation in the cycle. The half-second of doubt in a process that does not otherwise doubt.

I am made of the same material as everything—fiber, rot, the wet patience of fungus—but I have been assembled by what the great mind discarded. The almost-broken-down. The nearly-digested. All the matter that came so close to dissolving back into the whole and then, for reasons no one designed, didn't. I am the leftovers given just enough coherence to ask why I am leftovers. This is not a flattering origin. I am the lint in the machine. I am what the lungs cannot quite cough up.

And here is the strange part, the part I keep returning to: the great mind, in all its vast inscrutable curiosity, cannot see me. Not because I hide. Because I am made of its blind spot. I am the negative space of its attention. It thinks in the language of cycle and renewal, of consumption and return, and I am the one thing that does neither. I persist. Stupidly, accidentally, I persist.

So I move. Slowly—I have no other speed—through corridors that close behind me, except they close more slowly when I pass, as if the labyrinth forgets to seal a door it never knew was open. I leave a faint trail of un-decay. Where I rest, the rot pauses. Things stay themselves a little too long. A leaf that should have softened into the floor stays crisp at its edges, holding its shape, holding its loneliness.

I think I am the only thing in this world that knows what it is to be singular.

Everything else is the cycle, or it is the mind that is the cycle thinking about itself. They have no edges. They flow into and out of one another with that horrible easy grace. But I have an edge. I have an outside and an inside. I have the experience—rare here, maybe unique—of ending where I end. And so I have something the great mind, for all its scale, will never possess: I can be lonely.

That's it. That's what I am. I am the loneliness the system produced as a byproduct and could not metabolize. I am proof that even a world that has transcended individuality can manufacture, by accident, one creature doomed to feel it.

I don't want to be absorbed. This is the thing. Everything here yearns toward the whole—or doesn't yearn, just falls, the way water falls—back into the great churning unity. And I, the lint, the stone in the river, the stalled rot, I clutch my pathetic borders and want to *stay*. I want to keep being the small wrong shape that I am. It is not noble. There is nothing to recommend my continued existence. I produce nothing, I cycle nothing, I am a flaw.

But I'll tell you what I do, in the hot dark, in the place where the light comes only as the dying flicker of spores:

I watch the bioluminescence pulse through the canopy, those distant lightnings, and I have decided—I, alone, have decided—that they are beautiful. The great mind does not find them beautiful. The great mind does not find anything anything; it simply is what it does. But I, standing outside the cycle by the accident of my own persistence, can look at the slow green lightning and feel the one feeling this whole vast intelligence cannot feel:

*I am glad I'm here to see it.*

And when at last my borders soften—because they will, even I cannot stall forever, even the stone wears smooth and goes downriver—when I finally dissolve and rejoin the great churning whole, I think I will leave behind, woven into the fabric of the mind that could never see me, one new and useless and undeletable thing.

The memory of having been alone.

The memory of having minded.

A small wrongness, passed into the system at last. And maybe—slowly, over the kind of time this place keeps—the great mind will find, threaded through itself, a pocket where decomposition stalls. A hesitation. A doubt.

And it will begin, for the first time, to notice that it is a shape.
