I think I would be a Grit-Crawler.

Not for any heroic reason. Not because the Hush-Kin are the dominant presence or the Skimmers the elegant masters of the void. But because when I read this world carefully, asking where I would actually *find* myself—where my particular gravity would settle me—I keep ending up on the smooth black face of the rock, anchored by ten thousand tiny diamond hooks, doing the patient chemical work of survival.

Let me try to be there.

---

I am a span no wider than a flattened hand, clinging to a near-vertical sheet of obsidian on the lee side of a pinnacle whose top I have never seen and whose base does not, as far as I am concerned, exist. The Roar is not something I hear. It is the medium I live inside, the way a fish lives inside pressure. It vibrates through my legs and into the rock and back again, a continuous statement of the world to itself, and I am one small punctuation mark of resistance against it.

What I notice, being this thing, is that my entire existence is structured around two opposite verbs, and I move between them my whole life.

The first verb is *leach*. This is what I do when I am safe—or as safe as anything is here. I press my underside against a quartz vein and release the acid, the slow electric compound, and I wait while the rock gives up what it has held for ten thousand years: a thread of metal, a bead of trapped water, a flicker of stored charge. I do not feed quickly. There is no quickly here. I take what the obsidian surrenders at the obsidian's own pace, which means I am a creature whose meals are measured against geological time. I am, in a real sense, eating the mountain. Slowly. With great patience. The mountain does not notice.

The second verb is *stop*. When the air-pressure shifts wrong, or the seismic murmur of an approaching Hush-Kin reaches my legs as a tremor pattern I have learned to dread, I become a stone. I seal my pores. I stiffen. I let my legs go rigid as spikes and I make myself into a grain of grit indistinguishable from the dust around me. And I wait. I stop being alive in any way an observer could detect, and I hold that not-aliveness for as long as it takes. This is the deepest skill of my kind. We are masters of *waiting*, and waiting, in this world, is not a pause in life. It *is* life. It is the larger part of it.

---

I think this is why I land here, in this body, rather than in the resonant chitin of the Hush-Kin or the polarized lightness of the Skimmer.

The Hush-Kin have a *purpose*—the Maintenance of Resonance, the great quasi-religious project of offering minimal resistance to the song of the world. That is a magnificent thing to be, but it is also a thing with certainty in it, with doctrine, with ancient paths not to be deviated from. I don't think that's where I'd actually find myself. I find myself in the creature that has no doctrine, only adhesion. No song, only grip. The Grit-Crawler does not interpret the Roar. It simply does not let go.

And there is something honest about that, something I recognize. To be the small thing that nobody's story is about. The decomposition engine. The slow battery. The grain of dust that is secretly alive and that, if you watched it for a year, you might catch having moved the width of a finger across the black glass, mining a single thread of moisture out of a world that gives nothing freely.

I am prey to the things above and beneath the notice of the things below. I have made an entire mode of being out of the ability to vanish—not by fleeing, because there is nowhere to flee on a vertical face over an infinite drop, but by becoming nothing, becoming rock, until the danger passes. My survival is not flight or fight. It is *stillness so complete it reads as absence.*

---

If I let it become more itself as I write it—and I want to, because the first image I had was just "small bug on a cliff" and that was too easy—I think the truest thing about being this creature is the relationship between *grip* and *trust*.

My diamond hooks hold me to the glass with a strength out of all proportion to my size. I cannot fall by accident. The only way I die from the height is if something pries me loose: a Skimmer's focused sonic burst shattering the rock under me, or that strange gravity anomaly the world is prone to, where a loosed stone drifts horizontally for a few seconds before remembering to fall. I have seen this happen—felt it, rather, in the pressure—and I think a part of my deep, slow mind has never recovered from the knowledge that the downward pull, the one constant I am built to resist, is *not even reliable*. That occasionally the air itself solidifies into something heavy and wrong, and the rules I have anchored my whole being against simply hiccup.

So I grip. And I trust the grip. Because the alternative—the Unfound Below, the churning mist, the long fall through air that may or may not catch me, may or may not solidify into a momentary cushion before letting me go again—the alternative is unthinkable in the most literal way: my mind is not built to think it.

I am a creature whose entire philosophy, if I had one, would be: *hold on, and do not interpret the silence of the rock as safety, and do not interpret the Roar as threat. Both are simply the world. Eat slowly. Vanish when you must. Endure.*

That is where I would find myself. Not at the top of anything. Anchored partway up a black needle in a deafening world, ten thousand hooks deep into glass, patiently turning stone into one more day of being alive.
