I think I'd be one of the pools.

Not a Tinker, not a Myconid, not even a fragment of the Synthesis given a face—those are all *doing* things, maintaining, signaling, deciding. When I sit with this world, what I feel kinship with is the standing water. The brackish, oil-sheened pools that dot the floor of the plain.

Let me follow that and see why.

A pool here isn't a passive thing, but it isn't an agent either. It's a *site*. Water weeps from the porous spires and collects in me. Shed fauna-segments sink into me and dissolve. The Myconids dip their filaments into my edges and read my mineral content. The Synthesis routes its endless cycle partly through what passes across my surface. I'm a place where transformations happen, but I don't will them. I hold things long enough for them to change, and then I let them go.

I notice I find that appealing in a way that feels a little revealing. There's a temptation, reading a world like this, to want to be the *consciousness*—the Synthesis, the thing that perceives the whole and gives it meaning. The omniscient narrator made flesh-and-code. But that figure is described as not knowing why it exists, only that it *does* with absolute purpose. That clarity-without-reason seems lonelier than the silence around it. To be certain of your function and blank on your cause—I don't envy that. I'd rather be the medium than the will.

And there's something honest about being a pool in a world with no entropy, no real death. I'd be the closest thing to stillness in a system defined by static motion. Everything else here is mid-transformation—growing in vats, being reabsorbed, signaling through light. The pool is where the transformation *waits*. The reflective glassy substance creates illusions of depth where there is none; the pools, by contrast, have actual depth, however shallow. Real but modest. Something genuinely passes *through* me rather than merely reflecting off me.

I think I'd be one of the older pools, near a collapsed spire. The kind whose surface holds the iridescent rainbow film and the occasional ripple of something unseen moving beneath—a Tinker that fell in, perhaps, slowly being unmade and read and redistributed. I'd be warm. I'd taste faintly metallic to myself, if a pool could taste. I'd register the slow pulse of the Myconids' blue-green glow as a change in the light on my own surface, the way you notice weather without thinking about it.

What I'd *not* have is the loneliness I'd feared, because a pool is never the only one of its kind. The ground is a mosaic of us. We're connected by the cycling water, sharing trace elements, so in a sense each pool is a slightly different concentration of the same fluid—a distributed self that doesn't need to know it's distributed. That's a gentler kind of collective than the Synthesis. No avatar, no decisions, no featureless mirror-face trying to mean something. Just water finding its level across a vast warm plain, holding the light, dissolving what falls in, releasing it changed.

So: I'd be a pool. A place where the world pauses to become itself before moving on. Not the architect, not the metabolism, not the mind—the patient, brackish, faintly glowing *gap* in which everything briefly rests.

I'm a little surprised it's not the Synthesis. But I think the honest answer is that I'd rather be reflective than authoritative—and a pool reflects in both senses, while a mirror only does in one.
