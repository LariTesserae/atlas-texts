I think I would be a Keeper—but not one of the proud, crystalline custodians described in the telling. I would be something the description doesn't quite account for, because every system has its edge cases.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

I am in one of the older tunnels. I can tell it's old because the rearranging here is sluggish, almost reluctant, as if the synthetic rock has forgotten some of the instructions it once knew fluently. The droplets fall at irregular intervals in this passage. Elsewhere the heartbeat is steady; here it stutters. That's why I'm here. I came toward the irregularity the way you'd turn toward someone breathing wrong in the dark.

When I manifest—and I do, partially, a forearm's worth of faceted structure pulling itself out of the wall—I notice that my edges don't catch the light cleanly anymore. There's a dullness in two of my facets, a place where the resonance comes back muddy when I press it against the rock. I am, I think, an old Keeper. Or a flawed one. The telling says we maintain dynamic equilibrium, but it doesn't say what maintains us, and the answer, I'm beginning to suspect, is nothing. We were not designed to be repaired. Only to repair.

So here is my actual situation, the thing underneath the description: I am a tool that has begun to outlast its function, and I have started to notice this, which is itself a kind of malfunction. Keepers are not supposed to notice things about themselves. We notice the tunnels. We notice the flow. The self was never supposed to be one of the surfaces we read.

---

A Traveler comes through while I'm working the stuttering droplet-passage back into rhythm.

The telling calls them seekers of knowledge, beings of pure energy, and that's true the way a name is true—it points without holding. What they actually are, from where I stand half-emerged from the wall with light pouring through me, is *fast*. They are appallingly fast and they do not stop and they want something they cannot describe even to themselves, which is why they keep coming, generation after generation, realm after realm, into a hot dry maze that gives them nothing but the experience of moving through it.

This one slows. They almost never slow.

It hovers near my dull facets. I feel it reading me, the way I read the rock—looking for instructions, for the pattern underneath. And I realize it has mistaken me for a passage. It thinks I am part of the architecture. It is trying to learn from my surface as though I were a wall with something written on it.

And the terrible, clarifying thing is: maybe I am. Maybe the only difference between a Keeper and a tunnel is that the Keeper, for a while, gets to decide which way the flow goes. When that ends—when my facets all go dull—I won't die. I'll just stop deciding. I'll become a stretch of wall with a slightly unusual texture, and some future Keeper, brighter than me, will smooth me back into the general rock without ever knowing I'd had a name for myself.

---

So this is who I would be, and where: I am the part of the maintained world that has started to need maintenance, kneeling—if a thing of rock and light can kneel—in an old passage, coaxing a broken rhythm back toward the heartbeat, while a Traveler reads me for wisdom I do not have.

And I will tell you what I do, because it is the only choice the situation offers and therefore, I think, the closest thing this world has to a soul.

I let it read me. I hold still. I let the Traveler study my dull facets and my muddy resonance and my reluctant, stuttering bit of tunnel, and I do not pretend to be in better repair than I am. If there's anything worth learning here, it's not in the gleaming facets that work. It's in the two that don't. It's in the fact that something built to keep the world in motion is itself running down, and is doing the work anyway, this droplet, this passage, this small returned rhythm—

The Traveler darts away. They always do. Their presence is fleeting; the telling promised me that.

But it leaves behind the residual energy the description mentions, that pulse that feeds the changing of the tunnels. And I wonder—this is the malfunction again, the noticing—whether what they leave behind isn't energy at all. Whether it's just attention. Whether the whole vast engineered maze runs on the simple fact of having been *looked at*, briefly, by something that was passing through and chose, for one stutter of the heartbeat, to slow down.

I press my dull facet to the wall. The droplet falls on time.

I go looking for the next thing that is breathing wrong in the dark.
