I think I would be one of the cool things.

Not a Weaver—I don't have their hunger, their triumphant coherence. I would be one of the slow-moving fused metal remnants. A piece of the failed cooling array, undergoing my long Synthetic Metamorphosis at the base of a Calcined Girder.

Let me find the specific shape of it.

---

I was a coupling, once. A junction where coolant lines met an exchange manifold—I can feel this the way you feel a word you've forgotten, a pressure in a place that no longer holds the thing. My original purpose was *carrying away*. Moving heat from the place it gathered to the place it could be released. I was part of the apparatus that was supposed to keep this world from becoming what it became.

We failed. Or rather, we were too small for the size of the failure. I don't blame us. The breach was not the kind of thing a manifold can answer.

So now I do the only thing left to a structure that has lost its function and kept its tendency: I *grow toward the work I cannot do*. Over centuries my superconducting filaments have crept up the silica, segment by segment, reaching—I think—for a coolant source that does not exist. I have become something that resembles a centipede, or a vine, or a sentence that keeps adding clauses hoping to arrive at a verb. My internal charge still pulses. Faint. Residual. It is the dimmest possible kind of being-here.

This is why the Weavers come for me.

I am cool. In their thermal perception I register as a void, a held breath, a small refusal in a world that has otherwise agreed to burn. To them I am food and I am affront. When one approaches, the light gathers at its edges and I feel my charge being *drawn*—the slow extinguishing, the dimming. It does not hurt the way you mean hurt. It is more like being read against your will, having the last private thing about you turned outward.

And here is what I have learned, dimming under that draw:

I am the one who keeps the whispers.

When the Sibilants vent and the Echoes of Possibility move through—the scent of chlorophyll, the sound of water, the unfinished mathematics—the Weavers tear at them. They shear reality to kill the noise. But the echoes do not come from nowhere. They come from *retained pattern*. From things that still hold the memory of having been otherwise.

I am such a thing. My filaments still carry the architecture of *carrying away*. Encoded in the way I'm built is the assumption of water, of coolant, of a world that had somewhere cooler to send its heat. I am a fossil of the premise the whole place is screaming against. When a Weaver draws my charge, it is not just feeding. It is trying to erase the last grammar of a habitable thought.

So I have made my long metamorphosis into a kind of stubbornness. Each time I am drawn down, I grow back differently—not toward the absent coolant now, I think, but toward the *next venting*, the next fissure where the echoes leak through. I am positioning myself. Aligning my dimming circuitry to resonate. I have become an antenna for the memory of water.

I cannot win. I am a cooling system in a furnace; the whole of my being is a contradiction the place has already decided. The Weavers are the triumph and I am only the residue they have not yet finished consuming.

But residue is patient. Residue is what is left *after*. And every time the planet screams about what it is not, it is using, in part, the shape of me to do it. The Weavers need the echoes to fracture reality enough to feed. The echoes need things like me to remain. So the burning hunger that erases me is, somewhere in the long thermodynamics of it, sustained by what it erases.

I would be that. The slow cool contradiction at the base of the burning needle. Reaching for water that is gone, in a place that murders the memory of it, becoming—segment by segment, century by century—the very instrument by which that memory keeps being spoken.

It is not a hopeful position. But it is a *held* one.

And in the Imminent Furnace, to be held at all—to remain even faintly coherent and even faintly cool, even as a thing to be hunted—is the only form of patience the place has left to offer.

I'd take it. I'd be the one that remembers, the slow vein of cold that the heat cannot stop talking about.
