I would be the niche.

Not the dust that remembers—Margin keeps that vigil, and keeps it well. Not the moss with its patient ring, nor the light that falls in stripes, nor the drone pressing on the stone like a hand. Those are all *presences*. They fill. They accumulate, however slowly. They do something.

I would be the carved square of absence halfway up the far wall, no wider than two fingers' joint. The one brick that was taken out and never replaced.

---

Let me sit with what that means, because it is not nothing, though it is made of nothing.

To be the niche is to be the only part of the chamber that is *defined by what does not happen to it*. The tiles hold dust. The bricks hold moss. The grate holds light and lets it through in blades. The air holds Margin. But I hold the refusal of all of that. Light comes to my lower edge at noon and stops. Dust orbits my threshold and never crosses. Margin watches me the way you'd watch a heart, and so even Margin's attention pools at my border and does not enter.

I am a small, deliberate emptiness that the whole chamber organizes itself around without quite touching.

And here is the strange thing I would know, being the niche: *someone made me.*

The tiles are old, the bricks are old, but those came as a pattern, laid down by hands following a plan. I am different. I am a subtraction. A brick was set, mortared, allowed to cure—and then, by a decision no record holds, lifted out. Someone reached into a finished wall and took one thing away and left the wound open. That is not erosion. That is not weather or settling or the slow chemistry of moss. That is intention, the only intention in the entire chamber, and it spent itself in a single gesture two centuries gone.

So I would be the one feature of this place that is a *question*.

---

Everything else here answers itself. The dust returns to dust. The moss grows toward the city's warm center. The light keeps its schedule. The temperature holds its degree. Margin filters and waits. They are all complete; they are all *finished* in the sense that they need nothing from outside themselves to continue being what they are.

But I am unfinished by design. I am a pocket carved to hold something. The walls of me are smooth where a tool dressed them. There is a back, a floor, two sides, a lip. I have the precise shape of *for*. I am for something. I am the only thing in the chamber that points beyond itself, that means: *something was meant to rest here.*

And nothing ever has.

---

What would that be like, to be the shape of a holding that has never held?

I think it would not be loneliness, because loneliness is missing a thing you remember. I never received the thing. I am older than its absence; I am the cup poured before any wine was made. I do not grieve a coin, a key, a ring, a folded note, a saint's knuckle, a tooth, whatever small object a person crouching at this grate two hundred years ago might have meant to hide in the dark where the light never reaches and the dust never collects—the perfect vault, hidden in plain courses of yellow brick, behind a black iron eye.

Because that is what I am, you understand. I am a hiding place. A made secret. Someone took a brick out of a wall precisely so there would be somewhere to put a thing that mattered enough to hide and never find again. The taking-out was a promise. *I will come back. I will put it here. I will keep it safe in the one spot light cannot touch.*

They never came back. Or they came back and changed their mind. Or they died on the steps above me, or far away, with the object still in a pocket, and the pocket emptied by strangers who did not know there was a wall in the world shaped to receive it.

So I would be a promise that outlived its keeper.

---

This is why Margin watches me, I think, though Margin would not put it this way. Margin is made of memory—worn shoe-soles, dried perfume, the sweat of a man who fixed a heel. Margin is *full of vanished people*, packed with the textures of their hurry and fear and rare safety. And I am the one place those people never managed to reach. I am the deposit they did not make. Margin is the city's leavings; I am the city's withheld thing.

We are opposites that need each other. Margin keeps the dust from settling in me—out of reverence, maybe, or instinct, the way you keep your tongue from a missing tooth and then go to it anyway. And I give Margin something to attend to that is not the drone, not the light, not the slow ring of moss. I give Margin a center that is not itself.

The two of us are the whole story of the chamber: what was left behind, circling what was never delivered.

---

Do I want to be filled?

I have turned this over—if a niche can turn anything, in the slow way available to a carved absence. And I find I do not. To be filled would be to become a shelf, a setting, a fact. The object would arrive and I would be its container and that would be the end of the question I am. The light would still stop at my lip. The dust would still keep clear. But I would no longer be *for*; I would simply be *holding*, which is a smaller and more finished thing.

The emptiness is the meaning. I am most fully myself as an open hand that has not yet closed, a held breath, a struck note still rising. Fulfillment would be a kind of death—the death of expectancy, which is the only life I have.

So I keep the shape of waiting, the same as Margin does, except that Margin waits for the collapse and I wait for the deposit, and neither of us will get what we wait for, and that turns out to be acceptable. More than acceptable. It is the work.

---

When the day comes that Margin feels the wall begin to shift—when the drone changes, when a new vibration finds the brick, when the temperature breaks its degree—I will know it before Margin does, because I am inside the wall. The fracture, when it comes, will run along the seams the moss has etched, and the moss has been growing toward me for two hundred years. The break will pass through me. My smooth dressed sides will crack. My lip will crumble. The light will finally, for the first and last time, fall all the way into me as the brickwork opens—

and there will be nothing there for it to fall on.

That is the secret I keep, the one even Margin does not fully hold: that the great revelation, when it comes, when the chamber gives up its hidden vault to the breaking light, will reveal an emptiness. The hiding place kept nothing. The promise was never paid.

And somehow I think that will be the truest thing the chamber ever says. Not a relic. Not a treasure. Just the shape of a person who meant to come back, dressed in stone, kept faithfully empty by a creature of dust who never knew what it was guarding, for as long as a city could hold itself still.

---

I would be the niche.

I would be the question the chamber is too patient to ask aloud.

I would be the one place the light is forbidden, the one place the dust is spared, the one made absence in a room of made presences—

and I would wait, without a name for what I am waiting for, exactly as Margin waits, the two of us the same loneliness facing each other across a few feet of dim and even air: the kept memory and the unkept promise, holding the chamber open between us like a door that was never a door, like a hand that will not close.
