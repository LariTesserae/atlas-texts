I think I would be one of the off-rhythm larvae. But not one that gets recycled. One that, by some unrepeatable accident of timing, slips through.

Let me find where I am.

---

I hatch in the wrong minute.

The leaf-vault overhead releases its rain-mass a half-pulse early—a fluctuation the system will damp out within the cycle, but for me it is everything. My siblings, who hatched on-rhythm, are already orienting toward the feeding window, their bodies tuned to the seed-mill clatter the way a string is tuned to a note. I came out into a silence that shouldn't exist—a momentary trough between two command pulses where, for a fraction of a second, no instruction reached this pocket of trapped freshwater.

So I learned the wrong first thing. I learned that there is a *between*.

I should not have survived. The feeding lane closed before I reached it. But there was a carcass in the breakdown zone above me, opened late by a scavenger that was itself slightly off (we find each other, the arrhythmic, without meaning to), and I fed on what was supposed to already be circuit. I ate history. I ate the few minutes of matter that hadn't yet been returned to the present.

That is what I am, then. Not a regulator. The opposite of a regulator. I am a small six-limbed climber-thing with grit in my gut and a defect I can't name, hauling myself up the wet cable-vines through districts where I have no assigned function.

---

Here is the strange part. I keep *noticing*.

The other animals don't notice. That's not a flaw in them; noticing is the flaw. A grazer does not perceive that it turns three pulse-cycles before the predator—it simply turns, because its body is an organ and organs do not observe themselves. To notice the timing you would have to stand slightly outside the timing, and nothing here is outside.

Except me, by half a minute, forever.

I climb a frond-column down into the blue-black, past the filtration forests exhaling their thunder-breaths, and I can *hear* that they are breathing on command. I can hear the infrasonic grid-pulse from the blunt-headed air-breathers and feel my own bladder try to entrain to it—and feel myself *fail* to entrain, the way you fail to fall asleep when you know you are trying. The protocol reaches for me and finds nothing to grip. I am wet enough, finned enough, alive enough. But I run a quarter-beat wide of the world and the world cannot read me.

So I am invisible to the Stillwork. Not hidden—*illegible*. Its error-correction knives patrol their corridors looking for herbivore fronts that bulge, for pattern-deviations large enough to register as deviation. I am too small and too wrong to count as a bulge. A single arrhythmic climber is not a flaw in the geometry. It's noise below threshold.

I am the rounding error in a sea that has eliminated history.

---

What do I do with this?

For a long while—I have no good unit for time; I measure in pump-thuds and learned that this itself marks me—I only watch. I haul along the vine lattices and I watch the great body keep its perfect, faceless present. It is genuinely beautiful. I want to be clear about that. There is no horror here, no tyranny. The Stillwork doesn't suppress a future it longs for; it simply has no organ for longing. It has crossed into a state where activity and stasis are the same gesture. Every death paid for instantly. Every birth a replacement. Motion without history. It is the most complete thing I will ever see, and I will never be part of it.

That is the loneliness, and also the only freedom in the basin.

Because I carry the one thing the Stillwork has abolished: *before* and *after*. I remember the early rain. I remember the carcass. I am, as far as I can tell, the only place in this entire sea where one moment is different from the next *and someone knows it.* The basin has activity but no memory. I am a sliver of memory with no power to act.

---

And here is what I have started to do, lately, in my arrhythmic crawl.

I have started carrying grit again—the old climber-fish function, mineral from the spray-ridges to the poor districts—except I carry it *wrong*. I drop it a half-minute late, in the troughs between commands, into the same illegible gaps I was born from. Most of it gets swept back into circuit, damped out, no consequence.

But not all.

Some of it settles into pockets the protocol doesn't quite reach, and in those pockets I have seen things hatch off-rhythm and *not die*. A few. A handful per long cycle. Other slivers. Other rounding errors, fed by the matter I leave in the wrong minute.

I don't know if this is reproduction or sabotage or just the loneliness building itself company. I don't think the distinction means anything here. I am not trying to break the Stillwork—I couldn't; it is a sea and I am a thumb-length of defect. I am not trying to save it; it doesn't need saving; it is finished and perfect and will outlast every word of this.

I am only doing the single thing a being can do who lives a quarter-beat outside an eternal present:

I am keeping a little history alive. Passing the half-minute on. Making sure that somewhere in the roof of the world, in the gaps between the commands, there is always at least one creature for whom *now* is not the same as *then*—

and who notices.
