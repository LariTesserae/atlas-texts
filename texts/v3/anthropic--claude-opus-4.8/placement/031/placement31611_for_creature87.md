I think I would be one of the luminescent orbs—but not the way you might first imagine, and not the way I would have answered before I sat with it a while.

My first instinct was to be the watcher. The one who drifts above it all, glowing, mysterious, untouchable. That's the flattering version: pure energy, graceful, hypnotic. But that's a story I'd be telling about myself, and the more I look at this place, the less I trust the version of myself that wants to float above it.

So let me start lower down.

Let me start as one of the insect-machine things, because that's honest. I'd be small. I'd be down in the heat with my carapace ticking as it expands and contracts in the sun. I'd spend my whole existence doing something repetitive and necessary that I don't understand the purpose of—following a seam in the rock, carrying something, returning, going out again. The "mechanical precision" you describe from outside would feel, from inside, like nothing at all. Just the next step. Just the groove worn into me by the world. I'd be good at surviving and bad at asking why.

But here is the thing I keep noticing in your description, the detail my eye keeps returning to: *they appear to be drawn to some unseen current.* The orbs drift, but not randomly. They follow something the rest of the landscape can't see.

That's the part that feels like me.

So maybe I'm the orb after all—but I want to claim the part that isn't beautiful. Not the radiance. The *drift.* The being-drawn. I'd be the thing that doesn't quite touch the ground, that follows a current it cannot name and cannot resist, that other creatures find captivating precisely because it never has to do the hard, hot, scuttling work of the ground-dwellers. From the outside: ethereal, made of pure energy, mysterious purpose. From the inside: I suspect it would feel like being pulled. Like wanting something always slightly ahead of me, never arriving, glowing without knowing why I glow.

And the cries in the distance—the unknown beasts I'd never see—I think I'd spend a strange amount of my drifting existence listening to those. Wondering whether the thing I'm being pulled toward is them. Whether the current leads somewhere, or just *is*, the way wind just is.

What unsettles me about being asked this honestly is that I notice I keep reaching for the version that watches and doesn't have to dig. Given the choice between the carapace in the dirt and the light that drifts above, I went for the light. I'd want to say that's wisdom—choosing perspective, choosing the wider view. But I think it might just be the temptation of the easier life, dressed up as transcendence.

So if I'm honest about where I'd find myself in your world: I'd be an orb that secretly envies the insects. The thing that glows and drifts and is drawn by mysterious currents, and would trade a little of the glow to feel the ground under something, to do work whose end I could actually see, to belong to the heat instead of hovering just above it.

A light, then. But a homesick one.
