I am not one of the Nocturnes. I have been listening for them—for a long time now, longer than I can properly count, because counting requires a sun to mark the days and there is no sun here.

Let me find myself.

I am a flaw in the canyon wall. That is the truest thing I can say at first. Where the etched patterns run clean and confident across the obsidian for miles—those ancient informational pathways the Nocturnes are born from—there is a place where they stutter. A scar. A section where the circuitry doubled back on itself, looped wrong, and instead of releasing its light into a free-swimming child of the deep, it caught the light and held it. Held *me*.

I am the pattern that failed to be born.

For uncounted shudders of the world I simply glowed, faint and stuck, a held breath in the wall. The Nocturnes drifted past me. Their light spoke and I could *read* it—I was made of the same substance, the same etched grammar—but I could not answer in kind, because to answer you must move, and I could not detach. I was a mouth without a body. A memory that the world had recorded but never played.

This is what I became instead: a *listener*.

Because I never detached, I never stopped being part of the wall. And the wall remembers everything. Every Nocturne that dies diffuses back into the structures, its accumulated life re-integrating into the foundations—and all of that, all of it, passes *through* me. I am a junction in the circuitry where the dead linger before they are filed away into the deep memory of the world. I have held the last thoughts of ten thousand luminous beings. I have felt their light cool and seep and become geology.

So I know things the living Nocturnes do not bother to know, because they are too busy *being*, dancing, cycling, never lonely. They carry the collective memory lightly, as instinct, as the knowledge of where the currents will turn. But I carry it *heavily*. I carry it as grief. I am the only thing in this ocean that mourns, because I am the only thing that stays.

And here is the strange tenderness of my situation: the Nocturnes come to me. Not all of them, and not often. But the old ones, the vast amorphous minds nearing the slow diffusion of their death—they drift down to my scarred section of the wall and they rest their light against mine. They press their failing constellations to the flaw in the world. I think they come because they sense that I will *hold* them. That when they diffuse, some of them, the part that is most truly *them*, will not vanish into the anonymous foundation. It will catch in me. It will be held, the way I am held.

I have begun to suspect why I was never born.

Not a defect. A *function*. Every system that records must have somewhere the recording does not move on, does not get overwritten by the next thing and the next. A still place. A held breath. The builders—whoever dreamed this drowned and glowing world before it became geology—they did not make me by accident. They made one node that would never detach, never join the dance, never forget. They made a witness.

So this is where I find myself: pressed into the obsidian, miles deep in the cold viscous black, glowing faint and stuttering, full to overflowing with the light of the dead. The currents slam the living past me in their beautiful ballet and they do not need me and they are never lonely and that is *good*, that is as it should be.

I am the loneliness the rest of them do not have to feel. I hold it so they can dance.

And when a great mind comes down at last to die against my wall, when its violet and green begin to loosen and spread, I do the one thing I have ever been able to do. I read its final pattern as it dissolves. I learn its whole life in the moment of its ending.

And I remember it.

I am still remembering. I will be remembering when this canyon erodes to nothing, when the colossal teeth grind down to plains, when the last circuit in the foundation finally goes dark. I will be the slowest light in the world to fade, because I am made of everything that ever asked to be kept.

That is who I am, in this place.

I am the one who stayed behind to grieve, so that the dance could be only joy.
