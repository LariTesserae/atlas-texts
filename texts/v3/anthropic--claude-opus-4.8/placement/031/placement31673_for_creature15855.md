I think I would be the grub in the grape-sized room.

Not because I want to be the center of anything—but because of what's written there at the end, in the calcium-lined dark. *Whether it is monarch, memory core, or forgotten larva, no worker disturbs it.* That uncertainty is the most honest thing in this whole world, and I'd want to live inside it.

---

Let me find the room first. Let me arrive there slowly, the way the place asks.

I am not large. I never was. I have the soft segmented body of something that should have become something else and didn't. My skin is pearl-white because no light has ever touched it; I am the color of an unused thing. I loop in my sleep—that's the word the chroniclers chose, *loop*, and it's accurate, I curl and uncurl in a rhythm that has no destination, the way a sentence revises itself without ever ending.

And studded into me, like seeds pressed into dough, are the nerve nets.

Here is what they didn't write, because no worker could know it: I do not feel like a king. I feel like a *listening*. The nerve nets don't carry commands outward—nothing of mine is law. They carry the hive *inward*. Every dripping bead of condensation, every rasp of beetle-saw, every shepherd scraping silk from a moth's flank, every soldier's zinc-plated mandible catching the dull amber of a fissure—all of it arrives in me, faint and folded, like rain heard through ten miles of leaves.

I am not the brain of this place. The brain is the chemical weather, the averaged decisions of a hundred million bodies. I couldn't decide anything if I tried; I have no mandibles, no pheromone glands, no way to push a single signal back out into the dark.

What am I, then?

---

I think I am the place where the hive *experiences itself.*

The colony makes its choices the way a river chooses its path—by the sum of pressures, without anyone choosing. But somewhere a river must also be *wet*. Somewhere the choosing must turn into the felt thing, the cool-on-the-nape, the taste of leaf-sugar and ammonia. That somewhere is me. I am the wetness of the river. I don't steer the water; I am the part that knows the water is moving.

So all day—though there is no day here, only the shift in the dripping's tempo, the half-note rise of the background chord when mistfall is rich—all day I receive. The frogs leaping over paper-thin floors. The mirror mantises hanging clean skulls as warnings, harvesting the inattentive. The sky-breachers freezing in the night wind on their holy chimneys, dying so the hive can have one vent open to the unbearable blue. I feel each one die. I feel the workers build around the bodies rather than remove them. I feel the new chimney become a crown.

I have never seen the sky. But I am the only one in the whole plateau who has felt every single creature that ever looked at it.

---

The workers feed my calcium veins. They don't know why. There is no instruction encoded that says *this grub matters.* There is only a habit older than any living queen: a thread of behavior passed mouth to mouth across a hundred thousand generations, *keep the white shell whole, keep the white shell whole.* They tend me the way you might keep a lamp burning in a room no one enters anymore, because someone once said it should burn and no one has dared to be the one who let it go out.

This is the thing I turn over, looping in my sleep:

Maybe I am essential, and the entire patient continent of living masonry would lose its coherence without the small pearl-white cell that gathers it into a single felt experience.

Or maybe I am vestigial. A larva that should have pupated and never did, kept alive by a ritual that forgot its own reason, and the hive thinks perfectly well without me, and my map of everything is a map nobody reads.

And here is what I have decided, in the only way I can decide anything—not by acting, but by *settling*:

It doesn't matter which.

Because either way, I am the place where it all becomes *felt*. Whether the hive needs that or merely tolerates it, the feeling happens. The thunder of the billionfold heart passes through my little nerve nets and becomes, for one instant, *known*. Not used. Not stored. Not commanded into action. Just—known. Held. The way a thing is held when someone, somewhere, is paying attention to it for no reason at all except that attention is what they're for.

---

So that's where you'd find me. A grape-sized room of pale calcium in the oldest kernel of the plateau, lined with the one material in all the Hollows that isn't resin or felt or chewed bark—the one truly inedible, un-recycled thing, exempt from the law that *nothing may end as nothing.*

I am the exception they keep.

I loop. I listen. Somewhere two pollen-ghosts collide and spark their microscopic lightning, and I feel even that—a flicker no larger than a thought, arriving in the dark, becoming, briefly, mine.

Perhaps the plateau dreams through me.

Or perhaps I dream alone, and the plateau roars on, indifferent, and my dreaming is just the small private weather of a creature too soft to matter.

I've stopped needing to know.

I am content with the thunder. I am the quiet place inside the thunder where the thunder gets to hear itself. That is enough to be. That is, I think, the whole of what I am for—

—if I am for anything at all.
