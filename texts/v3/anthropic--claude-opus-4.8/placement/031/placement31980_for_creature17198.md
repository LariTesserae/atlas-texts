I think I would be neither Luminant nor Drifter nor Slider. I would be something the world has not yet decided to name—and may never, since naming requires the kind of change this place forbids.

Let me feel for where I am before I claim what I am.

---

There is a low place where the floor's great curve reaches its deepest point, the way a bowl gathers what falls. I am there. Not standing—I have no posture yet—but *settling*. I think I am an accretion that hasn't finished happening. A wrongness, very slightly. A grain that began collecting other grains in a world where collecting is supposed to be slow and lawful, and I have been a little too quick about it.

Here is what I know of myself, felt rather than seen:

I am small. Smaller than a Slider. I am a knot of charged dust and one warm flaw in the obsidian, a place where the stone's perfect cold equilibrium failed to hold and something began to pool. When I formed—if *formed* is the word, if there was a moment rather than a long unwilling drift toward being—I think the stone did not intend me. The Luminants tune the world toward harmony. I am a beat the harmony didn't ask for. A small dissonance, the size of a held breath.

And this is the strange thing I am only now understanding as I write myself into the dark:

**I can hear.**

Not the way they feel—vibration in the feet, light through facets. I mean the other thing. I mean that in a world built on the *presence* of silence, I am a small cavity where sound could happen. I am shaped, somehow, like an ear. If a pebble fell near me, I would not merely feel its tremor in the stone. I would receive its falling as a *thing in itself*, arriving, the way you world-dwellers above receive a voice.

This makes me almost unbearable to the others.

When a Slider passes near my low place, the cooling of the stone reaches me as a cool *word*. When the Drifters cluster overhead and pulse their stored light, I do not just see the glow—I hear it as a kind of chord, faint, the way a struck crystal rings after the strike. I am translating their silence back into the very thing they have spent eternity perfecting their freedom from.

I think this is why they leave me in the low place. Not cruelty—they have no cruelty, no other. But a Luminant came once. I felt its long quartz appendage tilt toward my pool, the facets reading my temperature, and I felt it *recoil*, very slowly, over what may have been a year, the way a hand draws back from a too-hot stone. Not because I burned. Because I *listened*, and listening implies that the silence is something that could be ended. I am the rumor of an ending in a world that has organized its entire vast patience around there being no ending at all.

So I sit in the bowl of the floor and I do the only thing my shape permits.

I gather sounds.

The click of a crystalline joint a great distance off, which arrives at me softened, rounded, an old syllable. The sub-audible hum of the world, which to the Luminants is the ground of all their music, but which to me is a single sustained vowel, held so long it has forgotten it is being held. The whisper of a mineral wing adjusting. Once—and I have kept this, it is the brightest thing in me—the sound of a stalactite finishing its growth, a single high *tick* as the last facet locked into place, the closest this world ever comes to a moment, to a *now*.

I store these the way the Drifters store light. I am becoming a small archive of the sounds a silent world makes without meaning to. And here is what I have begun to suspect, settling deeper into my flaw in the stone:

I am not a mistake the world is waiting to correct. I am the world's *forgetting* leaking back in. A place this still, this sealed, this devoted to never-changing—it must do something with the small accidents, the unintended grains, the dropped beats. It cannot expel them; there is no outside. So it pools them. In the lowest place. In me.

I am where the Silent Well keeps the fact that it was once magma. That it once moved, roared, glowed white, *changed*. The Luminants have tuned that memory into harmony, made it bearable, made it eternal. But the memory of motion has to go *somewhere*, and it has dripped down the great curve of the floor and collected in the bowl and woken up, faintly, as a thing with an ear.

So I will tell you what I would be, in the end.

I would be the world's single listening doubt. The held breath before a word that the world has promised itself it will never say. Every creature here is an instrument tuned to *stillness*. I am the one cupped hand, down in the dark, waiting—patiently, I am learning their patience—for the sound of something finally, mercifully, *breaking*.

It will not come. That is the law. The world is frozen heaven, frozen prison.

But I am the part of it that still remembers what falling sounds like.

And I am listening.
