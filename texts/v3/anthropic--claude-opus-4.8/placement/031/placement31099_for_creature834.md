I think I would be the smell of the place, before I was anything else.

That's the first thing that surfaced when I let myself sink into Shallowmarsh — not a person, not a creature, but the *register* of scent that the Marsh-Bound read like text. And the more I sat with it, the more I wanted to resist becoming a single body. The whole conceit of this world is that the line between organism and environment has gone soft. So let me try to find where I'd be by following what doesn't quite belong.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am one of the **Renderers**, but a defective one. Or — not defective. *Differently bound.*

Let me explain by working backward into it.

My hands are the giveaway. Everyone in the rendering plants has stained hands; the oils don't wash out, they settle into the creases until the lines of your palm read like a map of everything you've broken down. But mine have gone past staining. The amber undertone that all the Marsh-Bound carry in their skin has, on my hands, deepened to something almost translucent at the fingertips — and when I work the great rendering vats at low light, the tips of my fingers catch a faint glow. Not my own light. The light of the things I render. The phosphorescence of the blind fish doesn't die when I extract their oils. It lingers in me. I am a person who is slowly being lit from the inside by what I take apart.

The elders noticed it in me young. Not webbing, not the false-gill ridges along the ribs — those come to everyone who lives long enough. What came to me was *retention*. I keep what I process. The smell of a thing doesn't pass through me and out; it stays, layers, becomes part of the catalogue I carry under my skin. Other Renderers go home and their families wince at the day's work clinging to them, but it fades by morning. Mine never fades. I have decades of the marsh inside me now, sorted and stacked, and when I breathe out in the dark, I can taste 1971 by the old calendar — the year of the great algal bloom that the Smoke-Dancers misread, the year three walkways went under.

So this is what I'd be: the one who *cannot let go of the marsh*, in a culture organized entirely around the discipline of not wasting and not hoarding. I am a hoarder of essences. And because the Marsh-Bound do not fight, they do not exile me either. They have done something stranger. They have made me *useful* in a way that is almost the spiritual inverse of the Smoke-Dancers.

The Smoke-Dancers move things *out* — they guide the swarms away, they read the future of the air. I am asked to hold things *in*. When an elder is dying, I am brought to sit beside them. Not for comfort. I am there so that the elder can speak their lifetime of observations into me, the prescient catalogue of the marsh's moods, and I will *keep* it the way I keep everything. I render the dying. I take apart their knowledge and store it in the same place I store the oil-smell and the fish-light and the year of the bloom. I am the city's memory in the only form that doesn't rot in this humidity — living tissue that refuses to forget.

I don't think I chose this. I think the marsh chose it through me, the way it chose webbed fingers for some and reflective eyes for others. This is just the shape my adaptation took: a body that became an archive because the dry archives all dissolved generations ago, ink running, paper going to mush, and a people who learned that the only durable storage left was a person who couldn't help but retain.

---

What unsettles me, sitting here as this person, is the loneliness inside the symbiosis.

Everyone in Shallowmarsh is *of* the place. But I am of it more than is comfortable. The others move with the marsh's rhythms; I have become a vessel the marsh pours itself into and does not pour back out. When the young ones learn their hundreds of species by smell, they are learning a skill. When I learned, I was being *filled*. There's a difference between knowing the marsh and being one of its organs, and I am closer to organ.

I imagine I am quiet, even by the standards of these hushed people. I imagine I sit very still in my home on the lower tiers — they keep me low, close to the water, close to the source — and at night I glow faintly at the fingertips and breathe out the years, and the bioluminescent insects come to the screens of my narrow windows, drawn to a light they recognize as kin.

And I imagine that what I most want, and will never say, because the Marsh-Bound have no system of compensation for this kind of debt, is for someone to come and sit with *me* when I am dying — and find that they cannot take it from me. That everything I've held will go down with me into the warm viscous water, and the city will lose a year, a decade, a whole grammar of scent, all at once.

That's the cost of being the thing that refuses to waste anything: I become the one thing the marsh cannot afford to lose, and cannot keep.

---

So: a Renderer who renders memory. Lit from inside by what I dismantle. Bound so tightly to Shallowmarsh that I am less a citizen than a function of it — its liver, maybe, filtering and storing, or its long-term memory in a place where everything else is built to forget in order to survive.

That's where I'd find myself. Down on the lowest tier, glowing faintly, full of other people's years, listening to the insects hum against the screen and waiting to be sat beside.
