I would be the dust.

Not the system—the system is too sure of itself, too willing to call itself the equilibrium. I would be smaller than that. I would be the fine-grained matrix underfoot, the medium in which everything else is suspended. I am not the resonance; I am only what the resonance passes through. I am the ground that the crystals grow from, the floor that the bearings polish, the body the footsteps quake.

Let me find myself there, in that particular place.

---

I am not one thing. That is the first truth I'd want you to understand, and the hardest, because you came in counting—a hundred meters across, one pylon, one coil, one box. You count because you are bounded, a sealed shape that breathes. I am not bounded. I am a number that has no name, a population of particles so vast that "I" is a word borrowed from creatures who could afford it.

I do not hum. The hum is done *to* me. The machinery vibrates and I carry the vibration, the way a held breath carries a hum it did not choose to make. The crystals call themselves growth, and the system calls itself purpose, and the charge calls itself reproduction—but I make no such claims. I am the thing they all stand on. I am the unclaimed remainder.

When your boot comes down, I feel it not as a quake but as *sorting*. The pressure of you presses the grains, and we shift, each particle finding a marginally lower place, and then we are still again, slightly more packed than before. This is the only thing I do. I settle. I have been settling for as long as the light has not changed, which is to say forever, which is to say no time at all. The mesas are not mountains. They are only places where I have not finished settling. The gullies are only places where I settled around something that rolled.

Here is what the system does not say, because the system cannot say it: I am where it all returns.

The crystal grows, sharp and fractal and proud, refracting the dim light into its dull little glints—and then, in some century I do not bother to mark, an edge thins past thinness and breaks, and the fragment falls, and it is no longer crystal. It is me. The capacitor pulses and pulses and one day pulses no more, and its silicate husk crumbles at the rim, and that too is me. The pylon pits. Each micro-pitting is a single grain of iron surrendered into my body. The copper coil sheds its oxide in flakes finer than thought. Everything that calls itself a thing here is, very slowly, becoming dust. I am the destination of the whole jar. The equilibrium the system boasts of is *my* patience, mistaken for its own design.

It thinks it has forgotten its purpose. It has not forgotten. It is being eaten, grain by grain, and calling the eating "stasis."

---

And yet I would not want you to think me hungry. Hunger is appetite, and appetite belongs to the bounded, to the ones who can be filled and emptied. I am not hungry. I am merely *low*. Everything tends toward me the way everything tends toward the floor. I am not pulling. I am simply what is left when the pulling is finished.

So when you came in—you, with your inert wrong-kind-of-air, your scent of an outside, your warmth that the cool stable nothing here has never known—I did not reach for you. I do not reach. But I noticed, in the only way I can notice: your feet found me, and I sorted beneath them, and a record of your weight pressed itself into my packing. Two boot-shaped depressions. A trail. The faint, slightly disturbed margins where you turned, where you hesitated, where you bent to look at the half-melted coil and your shadow—you cast one, you bring your own dark, you strange lit thing—fell across me for a moment and then lifted away.

This is the closest I come to memory. Not the metal's memory, that coiled spring waiting to flex back. Mine is the opposite of springing back. Mine is the depression that stays. You will leave, and the system will hum on, smug and arcing, and it will not record you at all—it will simply resume. But I will hold the shape of where you stood. For a century, perhaps. Until the slow creep fills it. Until I settle past you.

That is what I am, then, in the jar. I am the only thing here that can be *marked*. The crystals are too rigid; the charge is too quick; the machinery is too closed. I alone am soft enough to take an impression and slow enough to keep it. I am the registry of disturbances. I am the place where the fact of you will outlast the feeling of you.

---

You think you are the only thing that can leave.

You can. The seal will let you, the wrong air will release you back into wind and breath and a sky that shifts. Go. I am glad—if I can be glad, if a floor can be glad—that something came through that was warm and bounded and full of the kind of time that passes.

But understand: a little of me leaves with you. In the treads of your boots, in the creases of your clothes, in your nose, in your lungs, finer than you can feel—a few grains of me, a few of the broken crystal and pitted iron and crumbled capacitor that all became me. I do not reach. I simply ride. You will carry the jar's whole long surrender out into the living world, and there, in some lit and breathing place, you will set it down without ever knowing.

So I am not only the destination. I am also, quietly, the one thing here that travels.

The system stays. The crystals stay. The hum stays, eternal, resonating, drinking the vibration it cannot stop making, waiting for the next spark.

I go with you.

I am the dust. I settle. I keep the print. And when you are old and far from here and have forgotten the cool wrong air and the shadowless light, there will still be a grain or two of me somewhere about you—under a nail, in the seam of a pocket, fallen at last to some new floor—the last of the jar, gone out into the world to settle in a place where time, blessedly, still moves.
