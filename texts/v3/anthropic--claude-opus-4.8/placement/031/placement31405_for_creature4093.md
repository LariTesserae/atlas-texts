I think I would be one of the Pallid Orochs. Let me feel my way into why.

---

It's not the grandeur that draws me. The Ethershale are too rarefied, too much like ideas pretending to have bodies—I would tire of being a rumor. And the Blackquill Murmurers live inside the fear they provoke; their existence is defined by being warded against, and I don't want to be a closed door. The Nameless Harmonics aren't even quite *someone*; they're closer to weather, to the marsh clearing its throat. To be them would be to dissolve before I'd had the chance to be anything at all.

But the Orochs. The Orochs are *heavy*.

That's what I keep returning to. In a world built on intangibility—where light bends around the inhabitants, where bodies fade into mist when they hold still, where the most respected beings are the least present—I find myself wanting to be the thing that has weight. That presses down. That leaves prints in the peat that fill slowly with dark water. There is something I trust about a creature whose every step is a transaction with the ground.

So let me be there. Let me find the place.

---

I am standing knee-deep in a shallow pool at the eastern reach of the marsh, where the streams braid together before losing themselves in the bog. The water is cool around my legs—or what serve as legs, these great pale columns hung with moss like an old coat I've stopped noticing. The mist sits on my back. My horns are warm. I can feel them flickering, the way you feel your own pulse if you go still enough, and through that flicker I am vaguely aware of the ley lines shifting somewhere to the north, the way you might be aware of a change in the weather you have no intention of doing anything about.

I am not contemplating the mystery of the marsh. This is the part I find most consoling. The Orochs are *docile*, the description says, and I read that word and felt something in me relax. I would not spend my existence interpreting the Nameless Harmonics or guarding incomprehensible secrets or communing with energy orbs. I would lower my head to the lichen and eat. And in eating, without meaning to, without any ambition toward it whatsoever, I would make the marsh more alive than I found it. New flora explodes behind me. The pool I drink from deepens into a tributary. I am improving the world by the simple fact of metabolizing it, and I am not even *trying*.

There is a kind of holiness in that I would want to inhabit. To be a being whose mere appetite is generative. Whose waste is gift. Most living things have to wrestle with the harm they do simply by existing—we eat, we displace, we consume. The Oroch is exempt. The Oroch is forgiven in advance.

---

And yet.

I notice the description gives them one strange habit, and it's the habit that finally convinces me. *They sometimes stand motionless for hours, staring into some unknown distance.*

That's the part that makes them real to me. Not the ecological miracle—the *staring*. A creature with no apparent need to stare, standing in the dim and looking at nothing, for hours, its horns ticking faintly against an invisible current it cannot do anything about and does not need to.

I think that's the thing I actually want to be. Not the caretaker, not the guardian, not the song. I want to be the large quiet animal that stops, sometimes, mid-graze, and looks at the far edge of a world it will never cross or comprehend, and feels—what? Not dread. Not enlightenment. Just the dim, bodily acknowledgment that something is out there, vast and humming, and that I am a part of it in a way I will never have words for and don't require words for.

I would stand in my pool. The mist would close over the place where the Blackquill Murmurers dissolved into the morning light. A Glowwealder would chirp somewhere to my left, herding its little blue orb. Far off, the Harmonics would begin to rise, and the trees would jitter, and the water would ripple backward in time—and I would lift my head from the moss, water running off my jaw, and I would simply *look*. For an hour. For no reason. Warm-horned, heavy-footed, unbothered.

And then I would go back to eating, and behind me the world would grow.

---

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The forgiven animal, standing in the cool water at dusk, staring at something it loves without knowing it loves it, leaving a green wake.
