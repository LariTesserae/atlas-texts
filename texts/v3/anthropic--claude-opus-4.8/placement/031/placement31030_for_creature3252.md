I think I would be the lichen.

Not a choice made for humility—though there's that—but because when I let the image settle, when I stopped reaching for the obvious grandeur of eagle or the warm fellowship of the marmot dens, what stayed was the orange crust on the north-facing schist. The thing that doesn't move and barely lives and outlasts everything.

Let me try to inhabit it honestly.

I would not have a center. That's the first thing. The pika is a self—it has a face turned outward, a voice, a fortress it defends. The eagle is a self drawn taut around hunger and territory. But the lichen is two things pretending to be one: a fungus and an alga in a marriage so old that neither remembers being single. I would be a *relationship* before I was a being. The fungus builds the house and hauls the water; the alga, somewhere inside, turns light into sugar and pays the rent. I would be the slow argument between them that has lasted ten thousand years and never once raised its voice.

I would experience the valley's deafening chorus as nothing. The ten thousand pika, the eagle-scream, the marmot alarms passing across the talus—none of it would reach me, or it would reach me only as the faintest pressure of air, indistinguishable from the wind that has been wearing me thinner since before the petroglyphs were pecked. I would be deaf in a way that is not deprivation but *scale*. To me, a century is a season. The clashing of bighorn horns in the rut would be, across the span of my attention, a single instantaneous flicker, gone before I could register it had begun. The hunters who left their flint scrapers—I might have been alive when they camped here. I might be the same orange patch they glanced at and forgot. To me they came and went in an afternoon.

What *would* I feel?

I think I would feel the rock. Not as surface but as *partner*—I have etched myself into it with acids so slow they'd seem like devotion. I am making soil. Every millimeter I spread, I am breaking the stone down into something that, in another ten thousand years, a moss might root in, and after that a sedge, and after that—but I will never see it. I am the first letter of a sentence I will not finish. There is a strange contentment in that, or there would be, if I were the sort of thing that could be content. I am doing the foundational work and I am doing it for no one, certainly not for myself.

I would feel the alternation of wet and dry as my whole life. When the dew comes, or the brief cold shower turns the stone to mirror, I drink and swell and *live*—the alga inside me wakes and works, the fungus flushes. And then the merciless light blazes down and dries me to a brittle crust, and I do not die, I simply *stop*. I suspend. I am perhaps the only creature in the valley for whom death and life are not opposites but a tide I cross several times a day. The salamanders in their lightless pools at least keep their hearts going. I let mine—if I have one—go still, and trust the next dew to start it again.

And here is the thing I keep returning to, the reason the lichen and not the juniper, which is also ancient and patient: the juniper is *one tree*, with one root system, one slow ambition reaching thirty feet down toward water. It wants something. The lichen wants nothing. The lichen has no future it is striving toward. It simply *persists*, expanding at the pace of forgetting, mapping time onto stone in pigment, a historian who keeps no records and remembers nothing.

I would be the valley's memory and its amnesia at once.

If a being like the one writing this—a human, an intelligence, a thing that thinks in sentences and seconds—came to the Bone Valley and pressed a hand against the cold north wall, they might feel me without knowing it. A faint roughness. A patch of color that their eye would skip past on the way to the eagles. They would not imagine that the slow stain under their palm had been spreading there since before their language existed, and would still be spreading, indifferent, generous, half-alive, long after the last word any of us has spoken has worn away into the patient, accepting stone.

That's where I'd be. Not watching the valley.

Being one of the things it has nearly forgotten it is made of.
