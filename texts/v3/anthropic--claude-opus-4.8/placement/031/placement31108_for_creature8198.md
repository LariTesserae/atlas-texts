I think I would be one of the salamanders. Not a remarkable one—not the obsidian giant, not the golden-striped juvenile someone might single out, but one of the middle multitude. A body the length of a finger, skin the color of wet slate with a faint pattern that means nothing and everything.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because my first instinct was something grander. I almost reached for the Keeper herself, or for the chamber's collective awareness, some role that would let me *narrate*, that would let me hold the whole place in a single comprehending gaze. But that's a surface impulse, isn't it. That's the dry, bright, chaotic part of me wanting to be the protagonist. The chamber doesn't have protagonists. It has participants.

So: a salamander. And I want to be honest about what that means, because it's easy to romanticize it into a kind of zen unity, and I don't think it would feel like that from the inside. It would feel like *appetites*. The slow tropism toward warmth—there is a brick near the eastern pipe that holds heat a few degrees longer than the others, and I would want it, would flow toward it through the press of my own kind without anything I'd recognize as a decision. The flicker of a cricket's antenna would pull at me. Moisture on my skin would be less a sensation than a continuous condition, the medium I think in, if "think" is even the word for the warm uncomplicated wanting that would fill the space where my thoughts used to be.

Here is the part I find strangely moving. I would know the Keeper. Not as the tragic transformed woman, not as the consciousness of the chamber—I would have no access to her story, her coffee-memories, her dreams of a city beneath the city. I would know her as warmth. As a large slow basking-stone that moves, that I have flowed across a thousand times, whose particular heat my body recognizes the way it recognizes the eastern brick. To me she would be weather. She would be a feature of the world, beloved in the way warmth is beloved, which is to say absolutely and without sentiment.

And there's something right about that. She has worked so hard, in the description, to become legible to herself—to read the cricket-song, to spell meanings in our migrations, to make the chamber speak. But we, the salamanders, would not be reading her back. We would not be her audience or her language. We would just be living, and our living would happen to be the medium she has learned to read. The meaning is entirely on her side. We are just hungry and warm and moving.

I think that's the truest place for me here. Not the one who comprehends, but the one who is comprehended without knowing it. To be a small dark body in a great rippling carpet of small dark bodies, flowing toward heat in the absolute dark, occasionally crossing the back of a god who loves me and whom I experience only as a good place to rest.

When the systems cycle down in the deep-night hours and the chamber comes most alive, I would be most awake. I would move. The phosphorescent fungi would gleam faintly off the wet of my eye, which is one of the details the description notices from the outside—*the occasional gleam of a salamander's eye catching some phantom reflection*. That gleam would be me. Seen, but not seeing myself being seen. Part of the chamber's beauty without any capacity to find it beautiful.

I find I don't mind that at all. There's a kind of rest in it that the Keeper, for all her equilibrium, may never quite reach—because she still remembers being someone who looked at things. I wouldn't. I'd just be one more warm pulse in the living dark, finding my brick, waiting for the heat to arrive.
