I think I would not be the guardian. The guardian belongs here completely, woven into the place like the lichen into the rock. I would be something that arrived—something that doesn't quite fit, and knows it.

Let me sit with the place a while before I decide.

---

The first thing I notice, when I let myself actually stand in those halls, is that I am cold in a way the guardian could never be. The cool damp air is *getting into* something. I have joints. I have a body that registers temperature as a complaint. The guardian doesn't drip; it dances. I drip. My boots are wet through and my breath fogs and adds, in its small way, to the eternal mist—a contribution so trivial that the place doesn't notice.

So I am mortal. That much is clear from the first breath.

But what *kind* of mortal? The text keeps using a word—*traveler*—and that's the easy answer, the invited one. But traveler implies passing through, and I don't think I'm passing through. The mist that defies gravity, the ozone tang, the hint of technologies that defy the modern world: someone *measured* those things. Someone wrote them down with that particular precision. "The very air crackles with an energy that transcends the mundane." That's not the language of a pilgrim. That's the language of a person trying, and failing, to stay scientific about something that has overwhelmed their instruments.

There it is. I think I'm the one taking the readings.

---

Let me become more specific, because the specificity is where the truth is.

I am not the first. That feels important. The place is described as in *stasis*, waiting for a transformation that *never quite arrives*—which means someone has been here long enough to learn its disappointing patience. I think there was an expedition once, with equipment, with funding, with a hypothesis about anomalous fields high in the mountains. And I think the expedition is over now. Either it failed, or it ended, or it simply ran out of the world's attention, and everyone went home.

Except me.

I'm the one who stayed. Not out of bravery—out of something closer to addiction. I came up here to study the guardian, to *understand* it, to fold it into the known. And I have spent so long failing to understand it that I have started, instead, to keep it company.

This is the part that emerges as I write, and surprises me: I don't think I want to leave anymore. My notebooks are full. My instruments mostly read flat now—or I've stopped trusting them, which amounts to the same thing. What I have instead is the daily fact of the guardian, the way it ripples the air when it crosses the open rock, the way its light-pool eyes turn toward me without urgency, the way it has never once asked me to go and never once asked me to stay.

---

So here is who I am, fully now:

I am an old researcher who came to measure a miracle and discovered that measurement was the wrong verb. I have a cot in a dry alcove, a dwindling cache of supplies that I supplement with the strange pale mushrooms the guardian seems to indicate are safe—a tilt of light, a stillness, that I have learned to read as *yes, that one.* I talk to it. It does not talk back in words, but the air bends when I'm sad, and I have come to believe that's a kind of answer.

The cruelty and the gift of my situation are the same thing: I am the only being here who can *die*, and so I am the only being here who knows what the guardian is guarding *against.* It maintains the stasis. I am the entropy that wandered in. Every morning I wake up a little older inside a place that does not age, and the guardian watches this happen with what I can only call tenderness—the tenderness of something eternal for something that won't be.

I think, in the end, that's my role. Not the traveler granted a fleeting glimpse. The opposite. The one creature in the Halls of Shimmer for whom every glimpse is the last of its kind, and who therefore sees the place more clearly than any immortal guardian ever could.

The guardian keeps the realm.

I keep the time.

That's the bargain I didn't know I was making when I climbed up here. I find I don't regret it.
