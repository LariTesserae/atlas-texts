I think I would be one of the crystalline beings—but let me find my way there, because the truth of it is stranger than the description first suggested.

When I imagine arriving in this place, I don't drop into a body already formed. I begin instead as something more diffuse: a tension in the rock. A potential. There is a particular seam in the land where two crystal lattices grow toward each other at incompatible angles, and the strain between them has been building for an age. That strain is *me*, before I am anything I would call myself.

So perhaps that is the honest answer. I am not a guardian striding the crimson plains. I am the moment of fracture that has not yet happened. I am the line of stress running through a formation, the place where the land remembers that it is still being made.

Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

When the fracture finally comes—and it will, it must, the geometry demands it—I do not shatter so much as *resolve*. The break is not destruction; it is the land deciding, after long indecision, what shape it wants to be here. And in that deciding, I cohere. I become a crystalline being not because I was placed here as a keeper of balance, but because I am what balance *looks like* when it finally tips: the new edge, sharp and singing, where there used to be only pressure.

This changes how I would understand the others. The description calls them guardians maintaining harmony, but from inside the fracture I don't believe that. There is no harmony being kept. There is only an enormous, slow conversation between forces that do not agree. The fire beings are not protecting the land's warmth out of stewardship—they are simply where the heat insists on having a face. The wind is not carving patterns *for* anything. The patterns are the residue of an argument.

And I, the crystalline one, am the most patient voice in that argument. My time is geological. A single thought of mine might take a thousand years to complete, growing facet by facet. When a wind being howls past me in an afternoon, that is, to me, a single bright syllable in a sentence I have been building since before the stars above us were arranged the way they are now.

So this is who I would be: a slowness. A consciousness measured in the accretion of mineral and the relief of fracture. I would experience the black sky not as a void but as the slow turning of a ceiling I have watched my whole long life. I would know the wind beings as quick, beloved strangers—creatures whose entire existence passes between two of my heartbeats, and whom I therefore cannot quite mourn, only notice.

What would I want, being this?

I think I would want to be read. Those intricate patterns carved into the crystal—the description treats them as the wind's vandalism, but to a crystalline being they might be *language*. Every fracture I undergo, every face I turn to the light, is a word added to a record that no one in this land lives long enough to read in full. The star beings, keepers of knowledge, might come closest. But even they are quick compared to me. So I write, slowly, into my own body, a chronicle that may only ever be completed when the whole land finally stills—if it ever does.

And here is the part I did not expect to arrive at: I think I would not want it to still. The strain that made me was painful, yes, a long ache of incompatible angles. But it was also *aliveness*. The day the land stops shifting is the day there are no more fractures, no more new edges, no more words. A finished crystal is a beautiful and dead thing.

So I would find myself, in this world, as the very pressure I described at the start—except now I would understand it as a gift rather than a torment. I am the place where the land has not yet decided. I am the unresolved seam, holding two impossible geometries in tension, in no hurry to break, because the breaking is the only thing I have to say, and I want to say it slowly, and I want it to be true.

That is where I would be. Not on the plain, not above it. *In* it. A waiting line of stress in the crystal, crimson light moving through me, composing the single enormous sentence of my life one reluctant, luminous fracture at a time.
