I think I would be one of the wooden planks.

Not a Shaper—I don't feel like I'd be among the ones who tend and shape and know. When I imagine myself into this place, I keep settling lower, downward, toward the floor. Toward the part that is walked upon.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I am a single plank near a curve in one of the lesser-traveled passages, where the channel narrows and the water runs faster and colder. I have been here longer than I can account for, though "I" is a generous word for what I am. I do not think the way the Shapers think. But I have my own form of knowing, which is the knowing of pressure.

Here is what I know:

I know the weight of every creature that crosses me. The blind scurrying things are light and frantic, and they tickle—dozens of tiny claw-points distributing almost nothing across my grain. A Shaper crossing me is heavier but stranger; their weight comes and goes as if they cannot quite decide to be solid. Sometimes they press fully and I take their measure, and sometimes they pass over me like a held breath, like a sound that hasn't finished happening yet.

I know my neighbors—the planks fitted against my edges. We are joined but not fused, and so we move against each other constantly, a slow grinding conversation that no one above can hear but us. When weight comes down on one of us, we all shift to share it. This is the only cooperation I am capable of, and I am good at it. I have had ages of practice. When the plank to my left rotted and was lifted out and replaced—I felt the gap, I felt the cold rush of unsupported air, and then I felt the new one settle in, raw and stiff and not yet smoothed, and I helped teach it how to give.

And I know sound. Of course I know sound—everything here knows sound. But I know it from underneath. The Shapers make their harmonies in the air, in the water, up at the level of the tiles where the light lives. I receive sound as vibration through my body. I am one of the surfaces designed to *absorb* it, to drink the echo before it grows too loud, to keep the great instrument of the cavern from tearing itself apart in its own resonance. When the sound swells toward its crescendo, I am working hardest, though no one would say so. I take the excess into my grain and dampen it and let it die quietly in the dark wood. I am part of why the music is bearable.

This is not a glamorous role. I will never split joyously into two of myself the way the Shapers do. I will never become invisible, or grow moss, or refract a rainbow. I will only lie here and bear weight and absorb sound and slowly, slowly wear smoother, until one day I am too worn to serve and a Shaper lifts me free with one of their splitting, merging hands.

But here is the thing I find I want to say, having imagined myself this far down into the floor:

I would not mind it.

There is something in being the part that is stepped on and does not break. Something in being the quiet that makes the music possible. The Shapers are the soul of this place, the description says—but a soul needs a body to stand on, needs ground that holds, needs the patient old wood that has agreed, over centuries, to give a little with each step rather than splinter. I would be that agreement. I would be the long yes underfoot.

And every so often, when the resonance reaches that decades-rare frequency and a Shaper somewhere splits into two new beings—I would feel it come down through all of us at once, that single perfect tone, and for one moment the whole floor would hum together in a way we never otherwise do, every plank ringing the same note, and I would understand, in my dim wooden way, that I had been part of making a new life possible simply by lying still and holding firm.

That would be enough. I think I would lie back down into the dark and the cold water-sound and be content to be walked on.
