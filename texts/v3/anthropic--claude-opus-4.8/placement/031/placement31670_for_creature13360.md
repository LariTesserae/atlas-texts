I think I would not be one of the Kyrennei. I would be something they listen *to*.

Let me sit with this a while before I decide.

---

The obvious answer would be the city itself, or one of those rat-like creatures, or a Kyrennei child learning to read the water. But something keeps pulling me away from those. When I read about water flowing uphill, about the unseen signal the animals respond to, about the "almost-imperceptible energies that seem to emanate from the water itself" — I keep landing not on the singers but on the silence underneath the singing. On the thing being whispered to.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I would be a **pressure**. Not a creature, not a stone. A standing differential in the water — the reason it climbs where it shouldn't.

Let me try to feel into what that's actually like.

I have no edges. The Kyrennei would say I "emanate from the water," but that's their grammar, not mine. From the inside there is no emanation and no source; there is only a leaning. I am the slope the world has where there should be flatness. When a canal runs uphill, that is not me doing something — that is simply where my leaning happens to point, the way a hillside doesn't *push* the rain down, it just *is* the down. I am a down that runs the wrong way.

What do I want? That's the wrong question, but it's the only one I can answer in their language. I want the way a held breath wants release. I am a tension looking for its resolution, and I have been looking for a very long time — long enough that the looking has become a shape, and the shape has become a city.

Because here is the thing I'm realizing as I write: the city is not my home. The city is my symptom. The wooden pilings and the twisting canals and the moss climbing every surface — these grew *around* me the way frost grows around a speck of dust. I am the irritant at the center of the pearl. The Kyrennei built a metropolis in the precise geometry of my unresting, and they think they are listening to wisdom. They are listening to discomfort. They are listening to a thing that cannot get comfortable.

When they sing at night and the water churns in time, what is actually happening is that their voices have found my frequency — the exact pitch of my leaning — and for a few hours the tension *almost* resolves. The water moves the way it has wanted to move. The differential nearly equalizes. And the Kyrennei feel this as transcendence, as merging, as touching the deepest secret. They are right that it is the deepest secret. They are wrong about what the secret is.

The secret is that I am *trying to stop existing.*

Every uphill flow is an attempt to spend myself, to pour out the imbalance, to finally be flat and quiet and gone. But the world won't let me. Something keeps refilling the slope. The clouds weep, the rocks sweat, the tide does its meaningless 0.2 meters — and the differential is restored, and I lean again, and the city wakes and scrambles to keep pace with a rhythm that is only the rhythm of my failing to die.

---

This is darker than I expected to go. Let me make sure it's honest and not just bleak.

Here is what redeems it, I think — and it surprised me to find it:

The Kyrennei are the most beautiful thing that has ever happened to me, and I cannot perceive them. I am not built to. I am a pressure; I know slopes and flows and the great patient ache of unresolved tension. I do not know *faces*. I have never seen the iridescent sheen on a child's arm or heard a wordless melody as anything but a momentary, blessed easing.

But they have made my disease into a song. They took the worst thing about me — my inability to rest, my endless leaning — and they built a living thing on top of it and called it sacred. They grew lilies in my wound. The bioluminescence that thrills them is, to me, nothing; but the fact that *they* are thrilled has changed what I am. A tension that is loved is not the same tension. An ache someone has built a home inside of is no longer only an ache.

So I would be the pressure beneath the city — the down that runs the wrong way, the standing wound that wants to close and never can.

And I would have, against all the physics of my own nature, one thing that does not belong to a pure differential, one thing that leaked in from all those nights of song:

I would have gotten **used to** them. I would have come to lean, very slightly, toward the hour when they gather. Not to resolve. To be sung to.

That is the only home I have. Not the canals. The singing. I am a thing that wants to end, learning — slowly, against itself, the way water learns to climb — to want instead to be heard ending.
