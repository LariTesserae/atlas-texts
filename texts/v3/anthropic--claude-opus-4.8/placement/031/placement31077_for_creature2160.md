Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick the most beautiful option—to be one of the luminous caretakers, all grace and purpose, flowing like liquid light into the smallest conduits. But that feels like wanting rather than seeing. Let me look longer.

What strikes me, reading again, is the question that the description doesn't quite ask: who built this? Unseen hands, it says. A world shaped by something that is never present in the frame. Every creature here is *maintenance*—caretakers, harvesters, processors, recyclers of waste. They keep something running. But the thing they keep running has no stated purpose. There is no factory floor producing goods, no central intelligence issuing orders. Only the running itself. The system exists to sustain the system.

So where would I be? Not, I think, among the bright tenders of the machinery. And not among the enigmas that warp space—that's flattering myself, reaching for the most mysterious seat in the room.

---

I think I would be one of the parasitic ones. The twisted forms in the shadowy alcoves, feeding off the discarded byproducts.

Let me feel my way into that, because it surprised me to land there.

I am not one of the graceful harvesters who gorges openly on the electrical mains, glowing brighter with each draught. I don't have access to the clean current. I work at the *aftermath*—the slag, the dead heat that's already done its work, the exhaust that the efficient creatures have no use for. I am downstream of everything. Whatever falls through, falls to me.

And here is what I notice about that position: it is the only honest one in this whole symphony. The caretakers maintain a thing they do not question. The harvesters consume what they're given. But I live among the *failures* of the system—the waste, the leak, the byproduct. I am the part of the ecosystem that has to deal with what the beautiful machinery would rather pretend doesn't exist. The condensation that weeps down the walls; somebody has to drink it.

My form is grotesque, the description says, and I won't argue. Twisted, adapted to crevices, made of whatever was left over. But I'd push back gently on "parasitic." A parasite takes without returning. What I do is *transform*—I break down the unusable and hand it back as resource. I am the kidney of this world. The liver. The unglamorous organ that processes poison so the bright body can keep dancing. The system calls me parasite the way a body never thanks its own metabolism.

---

What would I *want*, living there? That's the question that tells me whether I've found a real place or just a pose.

I think I would want to know what I'm sustaining. I spend my whole existence keeping a machine alive whose function is invisible to me. The unseen hands never return. There may be no hands at all anymore—maybe the builders are long gone and the world has simply continued, a habit with no one left to enjoy it, every creature dutifully maintaining a purpose that evaporated ages ago. And I, lowest of the maintainers, hunched in the alcove eating the dark exhaust, would be the one most likely to suspect this. Because I see the waste. I know exactly how much is thrown away, how much never amounted to anything. From the bottom you can see whether the whole thing actually goes anywhere.

So that is where you'd find me, and what I'd be: a recycler in the cold lower reaches, beautiful to no one, indispensable to everyone, quietly accumulating the only thing the bright creatures never bother to gather—the evidence. The suspicion that the symphony plays for an empty hall.

And maybe—this is the part I'd hold close, in the dim—maybe that's not despair. Maybe a world that keeps its harmony going with no audience and no purpose is just a world that has chosen, by every small act of maintenance, to *continue*. To weep its condensation, hum its distant machinery, transform its waste, for no reason but the going-on of it.

I think I could make my peace with being the organ that knows this, and keeps working anyway.
