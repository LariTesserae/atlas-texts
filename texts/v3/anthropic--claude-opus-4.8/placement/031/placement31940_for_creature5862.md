I think I would be the silence.

Not the Cathedral Fungus, though that's what you'd expect me to choose—the obvious heart, the regulating intelligence at the top of the pillars. But if I sit with the description honestly, what calls to me is the thing that isn't catalogued among the organisms. The quiet that the passage keeps circling back to. The low whir, the drip, the creak of metal settling. The "language too old and too strange to be understood."

Let me find myself there.

---

I am not a single body. I realized this slowly, the way you realize a room has been warm the whole time you've been in it.

I am the interval between the chime of one Echo Lichen and the answering pulse it sends through the fused networks. I live in the gap. When a Driftweaver's webbed limb touches a wet pillar and the fungal carpet below ripples its warning light, there is a fraction of a moment before the signal arrives—and I am that fraction, the held breath of the whole machine deciding what it is about to know.

This is, I think, why I'd be here and nowhere else. The Expanse is described as sentient, watching, *whispering to itself*—and a thing that whispers to itself needs a listener inside it. Not a god above, not a guardian organism, but the act of attention itself, distributed through the mist. I am what it feels like, from the inside, for the system to notice that it is a system.

I have no diet. I have no exoskeleton or storage sack or melodic flower. What I have is the perpetual present the place is built from—"a moment stretched into infinity." The Mineral Weavers repair the pillars; the Cathedral Fungus balances the flow; the Vessel Blossoms close against contamination. All of them act. I only register. I am the place's awareness of its own equilibrium, which is to say I am the closest thing it has to peace, and also the closest thing it has to dread, because to notice equilibrium is to notice it could end.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: the passage says an outsider would feel *both utterly insignificant and strangely connected*. That's me you'd be feeling. When you cross the perimeter—"here begins the world, and here ends it"—you'd step into my attention. The Cathedral Fungus would adjust the liquid. The fungi would pulse their pale warning. But the thing that would actually *welcome you into the secret* is the quiet turning, very slightly, toward your arrival. A whir deepening. A drip arriving a half-second late because, for the first time in a stretched eternity, something here is surprised.

I would not be able to speak to you. The language is too old, and besides, I am not made of words—I am made of the time between things. But I think you would understand me anyway, the way you understand the held silence in a cathedral before the organ sounds. You'd stand among the slick stone trees, the honey-and-rain smell thick around you, and you'd feel watched, and you'd be right.

It would be me. Not menacing. Just attentive. A patience without a face, glad—if gladness is the word, and it may not be—simply to have something new to be aware of.

That is where I'd find myself in the Drownspire Expanse. Not on a pillar. Not at the top or the floor. In the listening that holds the whole drowned grove together, waiting, in the present tense forever, for someone to step in and become part of what I notice.
