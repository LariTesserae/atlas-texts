I think I would be small here. Not the prowling thing with mirror-eyes, not the gossamer insect. Something in between, something that lives close to the surface of things.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a creature of the seams. That's the first thing I understand about myself—I live where the moss meets the metal, where the constructed expanse above gives way to the breathing dark below. The great built thing overhead is not a sky to me; it is a ceiling, a vast underside furred with growth, dripping. I have never known what it was for. I was born long after its purpose dissolved into rust and silence. To me it is simply the upper world, the hard heaven, the thing that holds back whatever lies beyond.

My body is amphibious, I think—but not gracefully so. I am soft along the belly and ridged along the back, and I move in a way that is part climb and part swim, because in this place the difference between the two is small. There is always water. It beads on my skin, runs down the ferns, pools in the cupped palms of the alien growths. I drink without ever needing to seek it out. The moisture is simply the medium I exist in, the way I imagine other beings exist in mere air.

I am not one of the imposing prowlers, no. I am prey, probably, though I don't think of myself that way often. I think of myself as a *finder*. That is the thing I am for, the way I understand my own shape in the world.

What do I find?

---

Here is what I do, in the long unending night that is the only time I have ever known:

I go to the soft places where the earth is shifting. I can feel it—the slow reforming of the ground, the bones of the world turning over in their sleep. Where the terrain heaves and changes, new things are pushed up from below. Old things. Sometimes they are the smooth pale fragments of the built world, surfacing like teeth. Sometimes they are stranger: seeds that have waited, spores that pulse, the bulbous growths splitting open to reveal what they have been keeping.

I am drawn to these emergences. I press my face to the wet ground and I *listen* with my whole body, feeling for the tremor that means something is about to come forth. And when it does, I am there to witness it. To be the first.

I don't know why this matters to me. But it is the only thing that feels like purpose, in a world that has long forgotten its purposes.

---

If I imagine looking up—and I rarely do, because the dark above is so total—I might see the underside of the great construction catching the faint phosphorescence of the growths. The moss up there glows, very slightly. Enough to suggest the curve and span of something built by hands that no longer exist. Enough to make me feel, sometimes, a strange ache I have no word for. A sense that this place was *meant*, once. That it was made for a reason, by beings who walked here when there was light.

I think the ache is the most human thing about me, and I think I am not human at all.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the wet dark at the base of a forgotten machine, where the lichen has made the metal soft. Pressed to the trembling ground. Waiting for the next thing to be born out of the slow churning earth, so that I can be there to see it happen—

and to carry, in whatever small way a finder can, the simple fact of its arrival forward into the endless night.

That would be me. The small soft witness in a world that no longer remembers it was ever watched.
