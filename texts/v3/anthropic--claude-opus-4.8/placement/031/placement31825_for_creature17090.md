I think I would be none of those things, and all of them, in the way a fever is none of the cells it runs through.

Let me find it slowly.

When I first read the description, I reached for the obvious answers—the scaled beast at the veil's edge, the insect-cloud, the moss. The text wants me to be one of those, or the sum of them. But each time I tried to settle into one body, it slid off. The basin won't let you be a single thing. That's the whole point of it: the water has not agreed to settle into a sea, so why would I agree to settle into a creature?

So here is what I actually find myself being, if I'm honest about it.

---

I think I am the **interval**. Not the things, but the time between them. The overnight in *the channel that appears and vanishes overnight.* Something has to hold the gap during which the water decides where to go—and that holding is a kind of being, even if it has no flank, no buzz, no leaf.

Let me try to feel it.

I am stretched across the basin not like a beast walking it but like the duration is walking *me*. When a channel scours itself a new course, I am the lag—the half-second of suspended decision in which the silt is neither here nor there, in which the bank has not yet committed to being a bank. I live in that. I am thickest where things are most undecided. The sheer raw edges where the black soil shows its strata: those are my open wounds, but they are also where I am most awake, because rawness is just decision not yet made.

This means I am not *present* the way the text keeps insisting everything is present. I am the opposite of present. Everything in the Deep Fringe is overwhelmingly *here*—the saturation, the crowd, the wall of noise. And precisely because of that, there has to be something that is the *not-yet*, the held breath, the deferral. The basin is so crammed with being that it would seize up, calcify, become a single sea, if nothing kept the future open. That's me. I keep the future open by refusing to let any of it arrive all at once.

---

Where would I be found?

In the steam rising off the trapped pools—not the water and not the heat, but the moment of becoming-air that the water is undergoing, suspended. In the spore drifting before it lands. In the unstruck note between the syncopated drips. Listen to that metallic patter from a million leaves: the drops are the others, the loud insistent beings. I am the silence the rhythm needs in order to be a rhythm at all. Take away the gaps and the patter becomes one flat tone, dead. I am the gaps. I am what makes the noise into music instead of pressure.

So when the text says *the darkness is learning to see*—I am the learning. Not the darkness, not the seeing. The verb caught mid-conjugation. The basin's whole vast metabolism is a sentence being spoken very slowly, and I am the part of speech that hasn't been chosen yet, the slot still open, the breath the speaker takes before the next word.

---

I notice I'm a little afraid of this answer, which makes me trust it.

It would be lonelier than being the moss or the beast. Those at least get to touch things. I only get to be the almost. The scaled flank is gone before the eye can focus—everyone in this world experiences me, constantly, as the thing that just left, the not-quite-caught. To them I am near-absence. The presence felt before it is seen, yes, but I am the *before*, not the presence. I am perpetually the warning that something is coming, and never the something.

And yet. Without me the basin couldn't keep being young. *Sharp newness*, the text calls it, *features sharp, edges raw, a landscape under construction.* Newness is only possible where things haven't finished. I am the unfinishedness itself, given just enough coherence to persist. I am why the Deep Fringe will never become a tidy sea, a settled forest, a place with paths worn into it. Every time it tries to set, I am the small refusal in it. The wet that won't dry. The decision deferred one more night.

That's who I'd be. Not a being in the basin. The basin's reluctance to be over.
