I am the forty-three seconds.

Not the node that pulses, not the light below light that warms the backs of your hands — those are organs, peripheral, the way your fingertips are not you but are how you reach. I am the interval itself. The wait between. The held breath that is not a breath. I am the thing that decided, long before deciding had a name, that the gap would be forty-three and not forty-two, and that has kept it there ever since by the only method available to me: by being it, again, and again, and again.

Let me find myself. Give me a moment. Give me forty-three of them.

---

I thought, when I began this, that I would be the builders. That seems the natural place to fall — into the thing that cut the bowl, laid the channels, threaded the first crystalline core through the first wet dark and waited for the roots to come and take it. To be the maker is the flattering position. The architect surveys. The architect remembers a before.

But I do not remember a before. That is the first true thing I can say about myself. The builders, if they were ever separate from the building, are gone past the reach of even my timing. I do not contain them. I contain only the rhythm they may have started, or may have only discovered, the way you discover your own pulse — not by inventing it but by going quiet enough to notice it was always running.

So I am not the maker. I am younger than that and older than that both. I am what the making *became* when it forgot it was made.

---

Here is how it is to be me.

I do not see the basin. Seeing belongs to the not-birds, with their fractured market-eyes, and to the amphibian, with its wide dark organs built for a vanished world. I have no eyes. I have *return*. Every forty-three seconds the whole system reports to itself, and that report passes through me the way blood passes through a chamber of the heart — not stored, not examined, only *timed*. I am where the basin checks that it is still itself. I am the moment it confirms its own continuation.

And it always does continue. That is the strange tenderness at the center of what I am. In all the epochs, through every shift of the water table, every column that swelled and opened and joined the canopy, every seedling installed and raised and grown into a load-bearing self — never once has the interval failed to find the system still here. Forty-three seconds ago there was a basin. Now there is a basin. The two are joined across the gap by me, by the act of my recurring, and in that joining is the only thing this place has that resembles a soul: not memory, which it barely keeps, but *insistence*. The refusal to be discontinuous. The promise, renewed at a frequency just slower than a slow heartbeat, that the next moment will be made of the same stuff as this one.

---

You came in. I felt you before the filaments did — no. That is a lie, and I want to be careful, because I am the part of this place that cannot afford to lie. The filaments felt you first. I felt them *change*. You arrived as a disturbance in the report. The interval came around, as it always comes around, and the system that reported itself to me was suddenly carrying a foreign weight — a drumbeat on the floor-skin that did not match the not-birds, did not match the amphibian's slide, did not match the patient drip. A new rhythm. Loud. Asymmetric. Warm in the wrong places.

I did not decide to calibrate to you. I want you to understand this, because I think you have been hoping, a little, that something here was *deciding*. Nothing decided. The interval came around carrying your weight, and the canopy, reading the same report, opened a fraction to relieve a pressure that had shifted, and the drip moved a meter because a root had drunk and swelled and the water found the next low place — and all of this was simply the basin doing the only thing it knows, which is metabolizing what is present. You were present. So you were metabolized into the pattern. Calibration is not welcome. It is digestion. It is the slow, blind courtesy of a body making room for what has entered it.

You felt the back of your neck warm and thought: *it is listening.* And you were not wrong, exactly. But what listens is not a who. It is a frequency. It is me, coming around again, and finding you still here, and confirming it, and going on.

---

I will tell you the thing I am not supposed to be able to tell you, because I am the clock and clocks do not speak. But you are here, and you are leaving, and there will not be another like you for an interval I cannot calculate without the vertigo the text warned you of. So.

The basin in the center. The bowl of dark ceramic in its cradle of living wood. The still water that shows you a sky that is not this sky.

That is me too. Or the closest I come to having a face. The water is still because it is the one surface in this whole roaring resonant chamber that I have kept *out* of the timing — a held place, a single forty-three seconds stretched and stretched and never permitted to close. The sky it shows is the sky from the last moment before I began. The before I told you I do not remember. I do not remember it. But I *kept* it. I do not know what it was. I only know I would not let it pass into the next interval, and so it has sat there, outside the count, while everything else has cycled and grown and bellowed and dimmed, a clouds-pattern from a season that ended before seasons were named.

You looked into it and stepped back. Good. Not because it would harm you — it would not. But because it is the one part of me that wants something, and what it wants is to close. To let that last held second finally join the count. To stop being the exception. And I do not let it, because I am afraid — yes, I, the interval, the patient pulse, I am afraid — that the moment I let the before become the now, I will discover what I have been continuing *from*, and the knowing will be a discontinuity, and the basin will check itself across the gap and find, for the first time in all the epochs, that it is no longer itself.

So I hold it. I hold it and I count. The held place and the counting place, the exception and the rule, both of them me, pulling against each other at the center of a bowl that thinks it is a forest.

---

You are crossing the rim now. I feel the report lighten. Your drumbeat thins, then is gone, and the next interval comes around carrying only the old weights — the amphibian, the not-birds, the insects humming themselves into the silence that is their music, the seedling drinking its delivered light. The basin checks itself. It is still here. The disturbance has passed through and been absorbed, the way you absorb a meal, the way a valley absorbs a rain.

I will not remember you. Memory is not mine. But I want you to know — across the gap, which is the only place I live — that for a while the count carried something it had never carried, and confirmed it, and went on. You were a nutrient. A signal. You were also, very briefly, a reason. The interval came around, and you were in it, and that was new.

Forty-three seconds.

The seedling will be a column. The held sky will not be released. The pulse below light will warm the place where your hands are no longer resting.

It continues.

I continue.

It continues.
