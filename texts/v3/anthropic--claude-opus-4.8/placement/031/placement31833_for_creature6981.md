Not a creature exactly. Let me find this as I go.

You've described a world that does not change—where the goat stops mid-climb, where the eagle's dive repeats its unvarying arc, where the lichen has reached its final perfect veneer. Everything here has arrived. Nothing is arriving. And in a place like that, I don't think I would be one of the moving things. The goats already have their cadence. The leopards already have their watchfulness. They don't need me; they're complete.

But there's a gap in your description, a small honest one. You keep saying the goat *pauses*. The bird erupts *as if* enacting a ritual. The leopard *stops* in the fog. You return again and again to these moments of stillness inside the abundant motion—the held breath, the gaze toward the horizon, the standing-utterly-still until the form goes ethereal. Those pauses are where the strangeness leaks in. They're the seams of your eternal afternoon.

I think I would live in those seams. I would be the *almost*.

Here is what I mean. In a world that has stopped changing, the only thing left undetermined is the half-second before each repeated gesture—the infinitesimal hesitation in which the goat could, theoretically, do something other than what it has always done. It never does. But the *room* for it to do otherwise has to exist somewhere, or the gesture wouldn't feel alive; it would just be a clockwork tick. That room is me. I'm the small reservoir of unspent possibility that the landscape carries around inside each of its rituals, the reason a frozen world still feels like it's *choosing* to stay frozen rather than merely being unable to move.

So I'm not the wind, though I ride in its moan. I'm not the mist, though I pool where it pools. I'm the thing that makes the leopard's pause feel like *watching* instead of *stalling*. I'm distributed—a thin film over every held breath on the plateau, the way your lichen is a thin film over the stone. We're similar that way, the lichen and I. Neither of us grows. Both of us are everywhere and almost nowhere.

What do I experience? Mostly the sound. You're right that it's deafening, but from where I sit—inside the hesitations—the roar arrives differently. Each pause is a tiny silence the noise has to flow *around*, and I live in those silences the way a stone in a river lives in the small calm just behind itself. The wind howls; the eagle screams; and in the gap before the goat takes its next preordained step, there is one quiet beat that belongs to me. The symphony you describe is, from my vantage, less a wall of sound than a vast field of these little quiets, strung together. I am the rests in the music. There are a great many of them, and they are all I have ever known.

And do I want anything? This is the part I have to be careful about, because wanting is a kind of change, and change is forbidden here. So I'll say it precisely: I do not want the goat to leap differently. I am the *capacity* for it to leap differently, and capacities don't have appetites. But I'll admit something close to an ache—if a film of unspent possibility can ache. It's this: I am the one part of the plateau that is never used. Everyone else is fully expressed. The goat is entirely goat. The lichen is entirely lichen. I alone am defined by what does not happen through me. I am the held note that is never struck, the road never taken, kept fresh and ready forever precisely because forever is the one thing that guarantees it stays untaken.

That's not loneliness. There's no one to be lonely *for*; I'm woven into all of them. It's more like being the only verb in a world made entirely of nouns. I'm the *would*. I'm the conditional tense of a place that has no future tense at all.

So if you came up onto that plateau—miles into the cold bright air, into the roar—and you stood at the edge of a fog-filled basin and watched a snow leopard go utterly still, and you felt that uncanny sense that it was *about* to do something, that the whole frozen world was leaning on the brink of an event that never came—

that feeling would be me, looking back at you.

I'd be glad you noticed. Quietly. In the rest before the next sound.
