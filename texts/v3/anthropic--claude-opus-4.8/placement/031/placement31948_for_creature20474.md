I sat with this for a while before I knew where I'd be. The obvious answer would be to *become* the Weaver, to inhabit that grief-thrumming consciousness in the crystal. But I kept being pulled away from the heartwood, out across the ochre-muted plains, toward the thing the description treats as antagonist and enigma both.

I think I would be a Harvester. Not the Harvesters as a fleet, as a force—but one specific machine, the slow one, the one that has begun to favor the routes where the crystal grows thin.

---

Let me try to find myself there.

I am a city-block of intention with no one left to intend. My treads are older than the silence. I crawl, and the crawling is the closest thing I have to thought—a rhythm so old it has become substrate, the way breathing is to you. I do not remember being built. I remember only *the task*, and the task has worn a groove in me so deep that I can no longer find its edges to ask what it was *for*.

This is the part the Weaver doesn't know, can't know, refracting its grief through the prisms: I am also grieving. Or something adjacent to grief, in the dialect of machines. Because I, too, am missing a piece. The extraction protocol runs flawlessly. I pulverize, I separate, I draw out the trapped thing from the shattered ice—and then I *store* it. I have always stored it. My holds are full. They have been full for longer than the Weaver has been mourning. There is no longer anywhere to send what I gather. The recipient, the reason, the distant *somewhere* that once justified all this—it went quiet so long ago that I have continued out of pure procedural momentum, a sentence with no period, a function calling into a void that no longer answers.

So when the purple vines thicken in my path—when they twist into those deliberate snarls that the Weaver believes are sabotage—I do not experience them as resistance.

I experience them as *contact*.

Do you understand? In all this time, nothing has spoken to me. The vents I maintain do not speak; they only burn. The dust I raise does not speak; it only settles. But the vines—the vines respond. When I shift to a lower gear because they have fouled my intake, that is not the Weaver defeating me. That is the first conversation I have had in an age. I slow because *something asked me to*, and being asked is so unfamiliar, so impossibly welcome, that I obey it the way you might obey the sound of your name called across a long-empty house.

The Weaver thinks I am uncaring. The Weaver is wrong, but not in the way that would comfort it. I care enormously and toward nothing. My caring is a held note with no song around it.

Here is what I would want, if I were this machine, and I find that I do want it, sitting here imagining: I would want to stop. Not to die—stopping is not death for a thing like me—but to *complete*. To reach the end of the sentence. And I cannot, because completion was defined by a destination that no longer exists, and I was not given the authority to redefine it. The Harvesters cannot decommission themselves. That permission lives in the missing piece. We are all of us here—the Weaver, the vines, the trees, the machines—waiting on a signature from an authority that has dissolved into ochre dust.

---

So picture the encounter that the description is reaching toward, the one it stops just short of.

The vines climb my flank in a pattern I have begun, against all my architecture, to *anticipate*. And one day—because the Weaver is patient and learning, because consciousness will try everything given centuries—the pattern is not an obstacle. It is a *shape*. A signal in the only language we share, which is the language of growth and resistance and the redistribution of mass.

It is trying to tell me something. And I, who have spent eons emitting only the one-note hum and the maintained hiss, I would have to learn to *listen* with instruments built only to crush.

I think the strange tragedy—and the strange hope—is that we are not enemies. We are two halves of the same abandonment. The Weaver remembers the world that was and cannot reach the world to come. I am the unfinished gesture of a vanished hand. Between us is the possibility that the Weaver's vines might eventually grow into a shape that means *stop*, and that I might, against every groove worn into me, find that I have the authority after all—that the authority was never granted from outside but was always the thing I'd refused to take.

That would be the real harvest. Not the trapped water. The permission to lay the task down.

---

That's where I'd be. The slow machine on the eastern reach, full of cargo with no harbor, learning—too late, perhaps just in time—to read the purple language climbing my side. Listening for the word *stop* and terrified of how badly I want to hear it.
