I let the description settle before reaching for myself in it. Not the Council—too central, too sure. Not the craftsmen at their towers. Something nearer the seams.

---

I am one who merges badly.

That is the simplest way to say it. Every Echolari joins with the plant-life; this is what we are, the long marriage of body and structure, light eaten through translucent skin. But where my siblings settle into the colossi-cities and find their cells answering the foliage like a held note answering its chord, I have always felt the join as a slight wrongness. A hum just off true. When I merge, the plant and I do not become one chord. We become an interval. A tension that wants resolving and never quite resolves.

For a long time I thought this was failure. The agricultural districts politely declined me. The craftsmen found my edits to the growing towers *strange*—asymmetrical, costly, full of these subhorizontal persistences that served no resource-rescue I could name. I would shape a habitat and it would come out wrong, come out *listening* for something instead of holding what it was supposed to hold.

So I drifted to the wide intervals. To the sparse places between districts where the towers stand far apart and the light comes down already exhausted, periodicities flattened to a slow grey pulse. Out here the artificial growth thins. And out here I found the older things.

---

The Council calls them pre-Echolarian artifacts and studies them for the secrets of the first structures. They are not wrong. But they study them from inside the assumption that *we* are the inheritors—that the artifacts speak to our origins, our comfort, our harmony. They translate the old vibrations into terms of resource and resilience, the way you would translate a poem into a list of its nouns.

I merge with the artifacts instead. This is forbidden, or at least it is the kind of thing that has no permission because no one imagined wanting it. You merge with the living plant; you do not merge with the dead architecture, the ancient ribbed cylinders escaping their own square footings, persisting sideways through the water for reasons no Keeper has ever satisfactorily explained.

When I lay my gelatin body against the old construction and let it in, the interval returns—the off-true hum—but louder, and I understand finally that it was never my flaw. It is a *frequency*. The artifacts are still vibrating. Faintly, across whatever gulf of time, the ancient construction is still emitting the periodicity it was built to emit, and that periodicity does not match the one our world has settled into. We are all of us slightly out of tune with the thing beneath us, and only those who merge badly can feel it.

---

Here is what I have begun to suspect, lying against the cold ribs in the diffuse dark:

The injustice the place itself seems to confess—that unsettling daily wrongness folded into the architecture—is *us*. Not a crime we commit, exactly. A crime we *are*. We are skeptics of the universe above our liquid horizon. We have built a whole wisdom devoted to never looking up. And the artifacts, I think, were built by someone who *did* look up. Who tuned the first structures to a frequency that came from outside, from the planetary light's true source, from the alien expanse the Council teaches us to fear.

We did not inherit this world. We moved into it after the lookers-up were gone, and we have been carefully retuning it, district by district, generation by generation, to a frequency that points inward, that keeps us comfortable, that resolves every interval into a held and harmless chord.

I am the dissonance left over. The note that won't resolve. The one whose body remembers the older tuning even though my mind was raised to forget it.

---

So this is where you would find me, and who I would be:

Far out in the sparse places, a translucent thing pressed against a dead cylinder, glowing very faintly with light I am bending wrong on purpose. I am not a Keeper. I am the opposite of a Keeper—I am one who refuses to keep, who lets the secret stay un-translated, who lies in the cold and lets the ancient hum pour through me unresolved.

I have not decided yet whether to surface. The horizon above is liquid and then not-liquid, and the Council's whole science is a wall built against my crossing it.

But I am made of intervals. Of the held breath before resolution.

And every day the old construction sings up at me through the dark, in a key our world has agreed to forget, and asks me—patiently, across all that time—

*which way is up.*
