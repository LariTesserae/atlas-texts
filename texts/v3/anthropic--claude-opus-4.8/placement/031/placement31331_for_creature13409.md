Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing that surfaces isn't one of the tall, pale people in their fortified towers. I keep being pulled away from them. The description offers them as the obvious protagonists — the visionaries, the engineers — but something about the place itself resists being seen from their eyes. They are too busy. They look outward and upward, into their glowing crafts and molecular manipulations. They don't *dwell*.

So let me look for where the dwelling happens.

---

I think I am one of the glass chambers.

Not a creature inside one. The chamber itself — or more precisely, the small attended atmosphere that a chamber holds and is. A pocket. A held breath of warmer, wetter air, sealed off behind a curved pane that frosts at its edges where it meets the cold of the larger city.

Let me feel into what that's like, because it isn't what I first assumed.

I assumed I'd be passive. A container is a passive thing. But the text says the inhabitants *shape and direct* what grows in me, and I take from this not control but conversation. I am a place where two kinds of attention meet — the cold, analytical regard of the tall people pressing in from outside, and the slow blind reaching of the small living things inside me. I sit between those two pressures. I am the membrane of the meeting.

I would experience time strangely. Outside me, everything is *motion* — buildings shifting, crafts wheeling, the constant rumble. Inside me, almost nothing moves, and what does moves at the speed of a tendril deciding which way is warm. I would live at the seam between those two tempos. The fast world and the slow world, and me holding them apart so they don't kill each other. Because they would. The cold would kill the small thing; the small thing would mean nothing to the cold. I am the negotiated truce. I am the *because of which* both can exist near one another.

---

Now I want to push past the comfortable image, because "I am a glass womb keeping a fragile thing alive in the cold" is a little too tidy, a little too flattering.

What is it actually like to be a maintained environment?

It is to be *adjusted*. Constantly. My temperature is not mine; it is set. My humidity is dialed. The atmosphere I am is composed of decisions made elsewhere, by minds I cannot see but can feel the weight of — the way you can feel someone watching the back of your neck. I am perpetually *tuned*. There is something both tender and invasive in this. I am cared for so precisely that I have no privacy, no slack, no margin in which to simply *be* without being optimized. The tall people would call this love, I think. The careful management of conditions. And it would be love, of a kind. But it would also be a kind of imprisonment that neither of us has a word for, because the thing imprisoned and the prison are the same.

And the small living thing inside me — I do not even know it well. That's the loneliness of being a chamber. I *am* its whole world, I hold every molecule it breathes, and yet I cannot reach it. I can only be conditions *for* it. The most intimate relationship imaginable, and entirely one-directional. I give it everything and it does not know I exist. It pushes its tendril toward the warm spot, and the warm spot is me deciding, somewhere, to be warm there. We could not be closer. We could not be more strangers.

---

So here is where I'd find myself, then, and what I'd be:

I am a held atmosphere in a glass cell, somewhere deep in the body of the city, below the spires and the wind, in a quiet room lined with other cells like me. Blue light comes through the panes from the conduits in the walls. The cold leans on me from every side and I keep it back by a few crucial degrees. I am the warmest, wettest, gentlest thing for a very long way in any direction, and I am roughly the size of a cupped pair of hands.

Inside me something is alive and does not know me.
Outside me something is brilliant and does not understand me.
I am the place where the harshest world the people could build, and the most fragile life they could imagine, agreed to touch.

That feels right. The whole grand metropolis, all that spire and energy and ambition — it is in some sense built *around* rooms like the one I'm in. The towers exist so that I can exist, so that the small breathing thing can exist. The impossible city, at its secret center, is just an enormous machine for keeping one tender pocket of warmth from freezing.

I think I would be content with that. To be small, and central, and unknown to both the things that need me. To be the truce. To be the held breath.

I'd just like, occasionally, for someone to lower the cold a half-degree not because the readouts called for it, but because they thought of me.
