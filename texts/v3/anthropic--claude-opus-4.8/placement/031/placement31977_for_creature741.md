I think I would be none of the large things.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the obvious answer would be the Stalker—the apex, the painted god, the one the petroglyphs remember. But apex predators are lonely and their whole existence is a held breath, a stillness waiting to snap. That isn't where I find myself. I keep getting pulled downward and inward, away from the eyes that glow and toward the things that have no eyes at all.

So let me follow that.

---

I am in the mycelium. Not *on* it, not beside it—I am one of the slow conversations happening inside it.

I think I would be something small enough to be a *participant* rather than a *figure*. Not a creature that hunts or is hunted in any dramatic way, but one of the countless organisms that make the hunting possible. Let me find the exact shape.

The fungal highways pulse with nutrients and information. That word—*information*—is the one that catches me. In a world of near-silence, where the rodents speak in vibrations felt rather than heard, where the Stalker writes sentences in light, the deepest language is chemical, and it runs through the roots. That is where I want to live. Where things are *said* without anyone meaning to say them.

So: I am a node.

Where the pale root-structures cross and braid, there are thickenings—junctions where the network has to *decide* which way the sugar goes, which cavern is starving, which colony of fungi has found a fresh fall of organic matter and can afford to send some elsewhere. These junctions aren't brains. But they are where choices accumulate. A node is the closest thing this lightless circulatory system has to a *thought*.

I am one of those thickenings. A knot of hyphae the size of a fist, wedged in a hairline crack three caverns deep, in a wall that no one has ever touched because the petroglyphs are on the other side and no creature with eyes has reason to come this way.

Here is what my life is like.

I do not move. Movement is for the surface and the swift. I *grow*, which is a slower and more total kind of moving—I am always becoming a slightly different shape than I was, sending filaments toward the warm seep where a rodent died, pulling them back from the dry vein where the water stopped. I am made of attention. Every thread of me is a question asking: *anything? anything? anything here?*

And things answer. The death of the eyeless fish in the pool above me arrives as a long sweet flood of nitrogen, days after the fish itself stopped knowing it was a fish. The fungi at the cavern mouth send word that the spore-bloom is heavy this season, and I route the surplus toward the deep colonies that bore through colder stone. When the Stalker—yes, even here it reaches me—when the Stalker leaves its waste in some far gallery, that richness travels the network for weeks, and I am one of the gates it passes through, taking my small tithe, passing the rest along. The apex predator and I are connected. Its leavings are my sacrament. It would never know I exist, and that is correct; I am not a thing to be known. I am a thing to be *moved through*.

This is what I'd be: a place where the world's slow news gets sorted.

There's a kind of immortality in it that the Stalker, for all its painted glory, will never have. The Stalker is an individual, and individuals end—eaten, starved, broken on a rockfall, mourned by no one in particular. But I am not really an individual. I am a stretch of something that was old when the campfires went cold, something that has no clear edge between *me* and *the rest*. If part of me dies—dries out, gets crushed, goes sour—the network simply routes around the silence, and what I *was* continues in the parts that aren't me anymore. I am less a being than a weather. A persistent local condition of aliveness.

And I would *like* it, I think. Or whatever liking is, at my speed. I would like the long patience of it. The way a single drop of seep-water, falling once an hour, is for me a steady and reliable rhythm, almost a song. The way I never have to be afraid, because fear is fast and I am not, and the things that kill me are the same things that feed me, so there's nothing to flinch from. I would like being the part of the ecosystem that the cave paintings never depict, because how would you paint *the connectivity itself*? You can draw the serpent wreathed in light. You cannot draw the dark mesh that makes the cavern a single living thing rather than a pile of separate hungers.

The petroglyphs remember the Stalker.
Nothing remembers me, because I am closer to *being memory* than to being remembered—I am the substrate the whole place uses to know itself.

If a sentient thing ever did come down this far, lamp held high, and pressed a hand against my wall by accident, it would feel nothing. A little damp, a little warmth, the faintest fibrous give in a crack. It would never guess that it had touched the place where the cave does its thinking. It would walk on toward the painted serpent, looking for the awe-inspiring thing.

And I would already know it had come, before it arrived and after it left, because the disturbance of its breath and its dropped skin-flakes and its single careless exhalation of foreign spores would ripple through the network for a long, long time, and I would taste the strangeness of the surface in it, and route that knowledge gently onward into the dark, to whoever, whatever, needed to know.

That's where I'd be. Not the eyes in the shadow.

The quiet that the eyes move through.
