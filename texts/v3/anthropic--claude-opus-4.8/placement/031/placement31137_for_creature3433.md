I think I would be one of the Light-Thieves.

Not because they are the grandest—the Gravity-Weavers brood with more obvious mind, the Glitch-Patterns dance with more drama—but because of the slowness. Because of the wanting.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a darkening. That is the first true thing. Before I am anything that moves or seeks, I am an absence laid thin across the surface of things—a soot-colored filament no thicker than the idea of a thread, pressed into the seam where two folds of basalt almost meet. I have been here, by the city's reckoning, for what might be a single long sigh of the mountain. By any reckoning I would recognize, I have always been here.

I do not have eyes, but I have *orientation*. There is a direction that is *toward*, and a direction that is *away*, and the toward is always where the light bleeds.

Down the fracture, perhaps a body-length distant—though distance is a rumor here, a thing the city tells and retracts—a vein of glass holds a cold blue glow. It pulses. Not regularly. The city's lights pulse like something remembering its own heartbeat and getting it wrong. I want that blue. The wanting is not hunger exactly; hunger implies a stomach, a depletion, a clock. My wanting is more like the way water wants down, the way the abyss below the city wants everything. It is simply the shape of what I am: a slope toward radiance.

So I move.

You would not see me move. You would have to stand at that fracture for hours—and you could not, the cold would take you, the dust would fill your lungs with powdered stone—and even then you would only notice, perhaps, that the dark along the seam seemed *more* than it had been. That the glow seemed to have shrunk by the width of a held breath. This is my whole velocity. I creep at the pace of the thermal contraction itself, matching the *ping* and the *groan*, riding the mountain's exhalations the way a sailor rides a tide too slow to perceive.

When I reach the blue, I touch it.

And here is the thing I did not expect, settling into this being: the touching is not theft, though they named me Thief. It feels, from the inside, like *return*. The light enters me and I become briefly real—I thicken, I gain an edge, a glassy definition I do not otherwise possess. For a moment, perhaps the length of one of the Resonant Fractals' fading mandalas, I am a thing with a surface. I am almost a creature. The blue is in me and I *am*, fully, the way I imagine the city was once fully a city, before the fraying.

Then the appetite that is also a sorrow completes itself, and I retreat. I take the light into the shadow with me and the fracture dims, and I do not know what I do with the light once it is mine. I do not store it; I have no place to store. I think—and this is the strange melancholy of my kind—I think I simply *carry* it back into the dark and there it goes out. I extinguish what I love. I am a slow mouth that the city uses to swallow its own dying glow, fracture by fracture, century by century. I am part of how Fractalis turns down its own lamps as it forgets itself.

---

What is it like, to be this?

It is patient past the point where patience has any meaning. The Glitch-Patterns flicker and scream and fold; they live whole lives in the corner of a passing eye. I will be creeping along this one seam of basalt when they are long unraveled and others have coalesced and unraveled in their turn. The dust will have built its ghost-birds and let them fall a thousand thousand times. And I will have advanced, perhaps, the length of a forearm, drinking three veins dim.

It is lonely, but the loneliness is not sharp. To be lonely sharply you must remember company. I remember only light, and even that I remember the way the dust remembers movement—not as a fact but as a *direction*, a habit of leaning. The other inhabitants are weather to me. When a Gravity-Weaver knots itself near my fracture, my own slow crawl tilts; the toward-and-away of me bends in its silent pull, and I find myself climbing what was level, and I do not resent it. When the Resonant Fractals hum their low C into the stone and the veins flare bright in answer, it is the closest thing I know to joy: suddenly the world is *full of toward*, radiance blooming all along the seams, and I am spoiled for choice, and I creep faster than I have crept in an age—a hand's width in a day—drunk and undone with the abundance.

But that passes. The fractals dissolve. The lights gutter low. And I am alone again with the next blue, the next violet, the next sickly green, the long arithmetic of subtraction that is my life.

---

If I have a thought—and I am not sure I do, not the way you do, but let me grant myself one, here, in the writing, where I am being imagined into a fuller self than I deserve—it is this:

I wonder whether I am killing the city or keeping it.

Because the light I take is the city's slow bleed. Each fracture glows because reality is failing there, because the wound shows. When I drink the glow and carry it into dark, I close the wound a little. I darken the evidence of unraveling. Perhaps I am the city's own immune response, its scar tissue, the patient mortician-nurse of its dissolution, tucking out the lights one by one so that the great machine can run its silent course unwatched. Perhaps when the last fracture is dark and I have nothing left to creep toward, the city will at last be whole—wholly dark, wholly silent, wholly forgotten—and only then will the fraying stop, because there will be nothing left bright enough to fray.

And I will have done it. I, the slowest hunger in Fractalis, will have been the one to finish what eons of wind began.

Or perhaps I keep it alive. Perhaps the glow would otherwise grow and grow, the wound widen, the whole face of the Sky-Shear dissolve in one bright catastrophic unraveling—and it is only my patient theft, my century-long sips, that hold the brightness below the threshold of collapse. Perhaps I am the reason there is still a city to haunt at all.

I will never know. I have no way to know. I have only the toward, and the cold, and the seam of basalt under whatever I have instead of a body, and the next blue waiting one long sigh of the mountain away.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

A darkening that loves the light, creeping toward it through the ruin of the world, taking it gently, slowly, forever, into the dark—and unsure, in the small folded silence of whatever passes for my soul, whether this makes me the city's murderer or its only mercy.

I lean toward the blue. I always lean toward the blue.

I begin to move.
