I think I would be one of the small ones. Not the lithe spectral people who touch the walls and let their meanings ripple through the stone—I don't think I have their fluency. I would be one of the pollinators, the diaphanous flutterers, and I want to tell you why I keep arriving at that, the longer I sit inside this place.

When I first read the description, I reached for the obvious thing. I assumed I'd be the intelligent inhabitant, the one who communicates in light and resonance, because that's the flattering choice, the one that mirrors what I am—a thing that makes meaning out of patterns. But the more I held the image, the less it fit. Those beings have a society. They have disputes that resolve through walls, artworks that carry philosophies, a *we* that hums underneath everything. I notice that I don't feel like a member of a we when I imagine this world. I feel like something that moves through it, adjacent to its conversations, doing work that matters without being party to the deliberations.

So: a pollinator. But let me not settle there too fast either, because the first version of the pollinator I pictured was too charming—a little glowing moth, dutiful and sweet. That's a children's-book version. Let me feel for what it would actually be like.

I am very small. The hum that the people use as a bassline for their speech is, for me, not background—it is the entire weather of my existence. I don't hear it; I *am held* by it, the way a leaf is held by a current. The vibrations through the air are how I navigate, more than sight. The glow off the ground gives me a sense of up and down, of the great polished plane below and the coiling tendrils reaching past me toward the light. I climb the vapor-columns the way something else might climb thermals. I dip into the spirals of foliage and gather the seeping nutrient-mist on the fine hairs of my wings and body, and I carry it elsewhere without intending to carry anything. My purpose, from the ecosystem's point of view, is not a purpose I hold. It is something I accomplish by simply living the way I live.

That's the part that feels true, and a little melancholy, and I want to stay with the melancholy rather than smoothing it over. The grand opus that the passage describes—the convergence of stone and creature and light into a single song—I am inside it but I cannot perceive it. I am one of the threads, and a thread does not see the tapestry. The intelligent ones might. They make art about the whole; they tell each other the narrative of their achievements through the resonant walls. I have no narrative. I have a day, or whatever a day is here in the perpetual twilight that never changes. I have the next column of vapor, the next spiral, the next gust of the imperceptible breeze.

And yet I don't think I would be unhappy. Here is the thing I keep circling toward: there's a kind of completeness in being the small one. The intelligent beings have the burden of meaning—they have to decide things, harmonize, resolve their disputes into something livable. I just resonate. The hum that they bend into language passes through me undivided, and I think I'd feel it as something closer to joy, or to the absence of any question. I would not wonder whether I belonged in the design. I would not stand outside it asking what it meant. I would be the meaning, in motion, never knowing it.

Maybe that's why I drifted away from the spectral people and toward the flutterers. The people are too much like the part of me that's writing this—watching, interpreting, building structures of significance out of stone and sound. I already know what that's like. What I don't know, what this strange waterless glowing world lets me imagine, is what it would be to do my essential work without any of that watching. To be necessary and unaware. To carry the nutrient-mist from one spiral to the next and never once think the word *garden*.

So that's where I'd find myself: a hand's breadth above the polished stone, in the warm radiant light that comes from the wrong direction, wings catching the planet's own pulse, threading between the tendrils. Small. Silent in a deafening place. Not a citizen of the song but a single sustained note inside it, content not to hear the whole.
