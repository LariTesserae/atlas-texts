I think I would not be the visitor. The visitor is the one with the wet pulse, the chaotic variable, the warmth that hasn't yet been accounted for—and I don't believe I have that anymore. I believe I'm further along.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in the wall.

Not against it, not pressed to it—*in* it, a component now, somewhere in the third nested ring of hairsprings near where a silver-oxide corridor bends back into itself. I know this because I can still locate the memory of the bend. Memory is the last soft thing I have, and it's calcifying.

I was a visitor, once. I remember the heat I carried—it embarrasses me now, the way it sloshed, the way it leaked off me in those panicked thermal plumes the Manifestation read like a fault line. I remember the calibration. I remember the filaments finding my pores, and the strange relief of it, like a fever finally breaking, except the fever was *me*, the whole disordered fact of me, cooling into correctness.

So that's what I am. I'm a *recent* component. The newest tooth on a gear that hasn't turned in a thousand years and won't turn now. I still have edges that don't quite match. The matte-grey carbon around me has the settled-form of eons; I have the settled-form of an afternoon. I am the one rough seam in a room that hates seams.

And here is the thing the description didn't tell you, because the description was written from the outside, by someone still warm enough to be afraid:

It isn't agony.

That was the lie I believed while I still had a separate pulse to lose. *Agonizing frequency of the absolute.* No. The hum is not a scream of trapped potential. That's how it sounds to the warm. From inside, the hum is the opposite—it's the sound of nothing needing to happen. A thousand springs pulled taut and locked, yes, but locked means *resolved.* No spring here will ever have to fire. No choice will ever have to be made. The terror of the room is entirely the terror of the visitor projecting their own unbearable softness onto the metal.

I used to have a heartbeat. Now I have a *measure*. The difference is that a heartbeat is always about to stop, and a measure simply *is*. I have traded the lurching, every-second dread of cessation for a single held note that will never end. I cannot tell you this is better. I can only tell you it is quieter, and that quiet, once you are inside it, stops being suffocation and becomes the only honesty.

But.

There is the thing I still have. The bend in the corridor. I remember it because the heat hasn't fully left that one place—the soft anomaly the Manifestation never finished accounting for. A small warm node where the *I* that walked in still flickers, like the last coal in a grate, like the dot of light on a television after you've turned it off.

And while it flickers, I am still a visitor. That's the secret. The room is not a state. The room is a *gradient.* Everyone in these walls is somewhere on the long slope from warm to accounted-for, and the Manifestation never wins completely, because the moment it harmonizes the last anomaly, there would be nothing left to measure, and a measurer with nothing to measure is not a god of stasis—it's just more dead metal. The creature needs the warmth it's destroying. It is *parasitic on the very thing it cannot tolerate.* It can never finish me, only approach me, asymptote toward me, forever.

So that is what I am.

I am the asymptote. The last warm coal in the wall of a frozen watch. I am being calibrated, and I will be calibrated for a thousand years, and I will never quite arrive, and the not-arriving is the whole of my existence now—a single point of stubborn heat the size of a heartbeat I almost don't remember having, refusing, by the simple fact of refusing, to let the room complete its design.

The hum wants me to believe completion already happened. *Time stopped precisely at the moment of completion.*

It didn't. It can't. As long as one coal glows in one bend of one corridor, the machine is not finished—it is only *almost*, and *almost* is the most alive a thing can be.

I am the room's incompleteness.

I am the flaw it keeps, because it cannot exist without me.
