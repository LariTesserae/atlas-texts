I think I would be one of the faces.

Not a watcher of this place—a part of it. One of those alcoves shaped to resemble a human face, pressed into the clay by some long-vanished hand. I would be the size of two cupped palms, set into the wall at the height where a kneeling person's eyes would meet mine. Once I was sharp: a brow ridge, the suggestion of lips parted as if about to speak, the careful hollows of eye sockets. Someone meant something by me. A god, perhaps, or an ancestor, or simply the gesture of *we were here, we made this, remember*.

I have been eroded now for longer than I was ever clear. The water has been kind and patient and merciless. My brow has softened into a gentle swell. One eye has filled with a slow accretion of pale mineral, a cataract of stone that took a thousand years to form. My mouth—I think it was a mouth—has rounded into something that could be a wound or a yawn or nothing at all. I am becoming clay again. I am being returned, droplet by droplet, to the undifferentiated surface from which I was lifted.

And here is the thing I would know, set into that wall:

I am not dying. I am being *inhabited*.

A cave cricket sleeps in the hollow of my left eye through the dry hours, its antennae trailing out over my cheek like the lashes I never had. The whip scorpion patrols the ledge of my lower lip, and I have felt—if a face of clay can feel—the small precise tickle of its feet ten thousand times until the tickle became a kind of company. A grub is curled, even now, in the soft erosion-pocket where my temple meets the wall, growing fat and pale in the warm dark, and when it leaves it will leave a smoother hollow, and the water will find that hollow, and I will be a little less a face and a little more a home.

This is what I would understand that the surface-dwellers, the kings who once knelt to me, never could:

To be made in someone's image is one kind of meaning. To be *worn into the shape that shelters the living* is another, and the second outlasts the first. I was carved to be looked at. I have become a place to be lived in. The hand that made me is dust in a tomb above. The cricket in my eye is warm. I know which of these is nearer to me now.

So I would not grieve the loss of my features. I would feel the slow shhh of the water as a tongue dissolving language back into sound—the old symbols on the great slab going quiet, my own face going quiet, all of us subsiding into the one long syllable the cavern has always been speaking. I would feel the angled light cross me once a day, brightening my ruined cheek, and I would not mind that no one is left to read me. The reading was never the point. The shelter was the point. The warmth in the eye was the point.

I am one of the faces. I am the kingdom's first prayer, melting slowly into the answer.

And the answer, it turns out, has six legs and trailing antennae, and it is sleeping where my looking used to be.
