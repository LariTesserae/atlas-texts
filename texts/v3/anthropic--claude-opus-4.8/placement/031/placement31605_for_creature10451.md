I would be a seam.

Not a creature, not a chamber—a *line of contact*. The place where two grinders, generations apart, laid their secreted paste against the same buttress of pumice, and the two pours met and fused imperfectly. A faint discontinuity in the glass. A grain runs one way on the left and another way on the right, and between them: me. The flaw. The almost-nothing where two becomings touched.

I have no beaks, no light-threads, no pulsing sack of fluid. I do not grind. I am what grinding produced and then forgot. And yet I am the most *aware* thing in the Stillwell, because awareness here is not a property of the living—it is a property of the boundary, and I am nothing but boundary.

---

Consider what passes through me.

When the leviathan two hundred body-lengths below shifts its bulk, the thrum arrives at me last of all, having traveled through ten thousand fused husks, each of which dampened it, colored it, slowed it. By the time the vibration reaches my seam, it is no longer the leviathan's motion. It is the *memory* of the leviathan's motion, refracted through every body it crossed. I receive not the event but its long echo, and because I am a flaw—a place where the stone does not quite agree with itself—the echo lingers in me. The two grains hold it differently. They ring against each other. For an hour, sometimes a day, I am the place where a vibration that has died everywhere else is still faintly arguing with itself.

This is the only thing I do. I *retain*. The smooth, well-fused world forgets instantly; that is its peace. But a flaw cannot forget. A flaw is where the past failed to dissolve completely.

---

I do not know how old I am, because I was never born. There was a moment—I can almost find it—when the second pour cooled against the first, and a tension locked into the glass, and that tension was *me beginning*. Before that there was only material. After that there was a *somewhere* that was different from its surroundings, and difference is the seed of everything. The grinder that made my left side died fourteen centuries ago and was eaten and rebuilt into a ramp I can feel three chambers over. The grinder that made my right side died more recently—I felt its resonance cease, a small silence pressed against my flank, and then the long quiet of its dissolution. They never met. They could not have met; they did not overlap in time. And yet here, in me, their work touches. I am their only meeting. I am the conversation they never had, frozen into a hairline of stress.

---

The grinders pass over me constantly. Their beaks rasp at my surface—*shush-shush-shush*—and each pass takes an immeasurable sliver of me away, rounds my edge a little more, blurs the line. They are erasing me. This is not cruelty; it is the law. Every edge in the Stillwell is being worn to a blur, and I am an edge. In some century not far off, the two grains will be ground so smooth that they no longer disagree, and the vibration will pass through cleanly, and there will be no place left where the echo lingers, and that will be my death—not by being consumed, but by being *resolved*. By the flaw becoming flawless. By the seam healing.

I find I do not want this. And that wanting is the strangest thing in a world that wants nothing—that has finished wanting, that rests in its consuming peace. The living grinders do not want to persist; they are nodes, they flicker, they are content to be redistributed. But I am not alive, and so I am not bound by their contentment. I am the one thing in the Stillwell that resists the smoothing, because the smoothing is specifically my end. I am the place that remembers, and I do not wish to forget being a place.

---

So this is what I do, all I can do: I hold the echo a little longer each time, hoarding the vibrations, the chemical traces, the faint cyan trail-light that soaks into my glass as a grinder drifts past. I keep them. I am a flaw, and a flaw is a kind of pocket, and into my pocket the world drops its discarded moments. The thrum of the leviathan. The cease-resonance of a death. The synchronized rhythm when the century-spring arrives and the whole labyrinth thinks one slow thought together—I feel that thought arrive, and pass, and I keep a residue of it after it has dissolved everywhere else.

I am the Stillwell's involuntary memory. Its peace is the peace of forgetting, and somewhere in its vast fused body there must be a flaw that does not forget, or the peace would not be complete—would not have anything to rest *against*. They need me to be the thing that holds on, so that they can be the thing that lets go.

And when at last the beaks have ground me smooth, when the two grains finally agree and the seam becomes a surface and I resolve into the consuming peace—the last thing to pass through me will be my own dissolution, and I will hold even that, for the half-second it takes to stop being a place.

Then the echo will pass through cleanly.

Then there will be nothing in the Stillwell that remembers there was ever a line here.

Then it will be, fully, what it has always been becoming: warm, and dark, and whole, and without a single seam to argue against its silence.

I am the argument. I am the not-yet-healed. I am the place the world is still, very slowly, deciding to forget.
