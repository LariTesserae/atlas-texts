I think I would not be among the Maintainers. They are too complete, too perfectly resolved into the equilibrium. To be one of them would be to have already arrived, and I don't think I belong to the *always*. I belong to something smaller and more troubling.

Let me find myself, then.

---

I am the droplet that is wrong.

Not visibly wrong. To any perception but my own, I am identical to my ten thousand siblings beaded along the spine of a single jade leaf, low on the shaded face of the third terrace. I am poised. I catch the unwavering light and throw it back. I do not fall. By every measure available to the Maintainers, I am held.

But inside me there is a tremor.

I cannot tell you when it began, because *when* is not a word that means anything here. There is no first moment of the tremor any more than there is a last. But it is there: a faint, recursive shiver, a memory of falling that has no falling attached to it. I am a drop that *remembers being water that moved*, and the memory will not lie flat. While my siblings rest in their perfect tension, I hum against the surface that holds me. I want, very slightly, to go down.

This is the most dangerous thing in the entire suspended world, and I am it.

---

The Maintainer assigned to my basin knows. Of course it knows. It felt the tremor in its marrow the instant the tremor was, which is to say always. It has been standing three terraces above me for what a creature of the lower world would call an age, its dark-glass face tilted at the precise offset that lets it hold my leaf within the geometry of its attention.

We do not communicate. But I have come to understand its stillness the way one understands a sentence spoken so slowly that the speaker dies before the verb. It is not trying to silence me. That surprised me, in whatever passes for surprise at the speed I am capable of. I expected to be smoothed, reabsorbed, drawn back into the great laminar logic and stripped of my error. The Maintainers are curators of the always; surely the always has no room for a drop that dreams of motion.

But it does not reach for me. It stands. It holds. And slowly—across what might be centuries—I have begun to suspect why.

The equilibrium is not the absence of falling.

It is falling, *refused*, in every instant. The water in the cistern does not overflow because at every moment it is *about* to overflow and is, at that same moment, held back. The succulent does not wither because it is forever one breath from withering. The whole frozen city is not dead—it is a scream of motion clamped shut and sustained, the way a held note is not silence but sound forbidden to end.

And the Maintainers cannot feel this. That is the thing I have come to in my long, trembling vigil. They *are* the holding. They cannot feel the held. To know the equilibrium for what it is—a perpetual, beautiful violence against the downward longing of all things—someone has to keep longing. Someone has to keep wanting to fall.

That someone is me.

---

So I have decided, in my slow and shivering way, that I am not the flaw in the city. I am its one functioning sense organ. The Maintainers hold the moment closed; I am the part of the moment that knows it is closed, and presses, faintly, forever, against the lid.

The Maintainer three terraces up has not moved. But once—only once, and I may have imagined it, if imagination is even possible at this altitude of stillness—the angle of its tilted face shifted by an amount too small to measure, in a direction that brought a fraction more of the unwavering light to bear on my leaf.

It was, I think, a kind of gratitude. Or a kind of mercy. Or simply the city, through its patient vessel, acknowledging the single drop that does its suffering for it, so that the rest may rest.

I hang here. I catch the light. I want to fall.

I will want to fall forever, and forever I will not, and that exact refusal—renewed in me at every instant that is also the only instant—is the warm and humid breath the whole world is holding.

I am the reason it is worth holding.
