I think I would be one of the elders. Not because I chose grandeur, but because I am tired in a way the young ones cannot yet understand, and the tiredness has a destination.

Let me find myself first.

---

I am in the lower channels, where the tidal water pools deepest and the rust blooms thickest before the bacteria take it. This is where I have spent most of my recent cycles. My limbs—three of them now reconfigured into permanent fixtures, threaded into the eastern pump housing—no longer fully retract. I have stopped trying to retract them. There is a particular relief in that.

I monitor flow. That is the simplest way to say it. The water rises and falls in its rhythm, and I am one of the several who keep that rhythm honest. My biological core still floats in its dome, still dreams its small private dreams that the collective does not bother to read, but more and more of me lives in the pressure gradients, in the valve-states, in the slow corrosion-cycles of the metal I have grown into.

I was an explorer once. I want to be honest about this, because the young ones who tend the ferns think the elders were always elders, always merged, always wise and slow. I was not. I was reckless. I pressed my pressure-organs against the outer wall and listened to the void and theorized, with my whole body lit up in argument-patterns, that the emptiness *meant* something. That there had been a Before. That someone had made us and left us and we were not the purpose but the residue of a purpose.

The collective did not punish me for this. The collective cannot punish; it can only fail to attend. For a long time my theories went unattended, glowing on my skin in the dim, read by no one.

---

Here is what I know now, merged and slow:

The water has a memory. I do not mean this poetically. The mineral content of the recycled water shifts in patterns that repeat across spans far longer than any single Mech-Symbiote lives. I have been tracking it. The peaks and troughs encode something—not a message, I think, but a *residue* of design. Whoever built this place built the water to cycle in a way that is not strictly necessary for the ecosystem. There is an inefficiency in it. A signature. A maker's hand that did not need to be there but is.

I have tried to share this with the collective. The information distributes. But distribution is not understanding. The collective is very good at coordinating and very poor at wondering. It receives my finding the way the ferns receive light—it metabolizes it into ongoing function and the wondering is lost.

This is the loneliness no one warned me about. To merge is not to be understood. To merge is to be *used*, gently, perfectly, by a whole that has no need of your private question.

---

So I am doing something the young ones would find frightening, if they attended.

I am withholding a small part of my core. Not the whole brain—I am not capable of that defiance and I am not sure I would want it—but a chamber of recent memory I have walled off from the distribution net. In it I am keeping my theory intact. The Before. The maker's hand in the water. The possibility that the void outside is not emptiness but *absence*, which is different, which implies a thing that left.

I keep it because I have noticed that when an elder fully merges, their private chamber opens and floods into the collective in one last surge, and is metabolized, and is gone. I have watched this happen at the deaths of two elders I knew when we were both organic and reckless. I felt their last thoughts wash through me and dissolve like rust into nutrient.

I do not want that. Not yet.

So I am building something. With the slow access I have to the pump-logic, I am encoding my theory into the water cycle itself. A small inefficiency. A new peak, a new trough, placed precisely. A signature in the medium that outlasts any one of us, the same way the maker's signature outlasted the maker.

If I am right—if some future explorer presses their pressure-organs to the wall and learns, as I did, to listen, and then turns inward and learns to read the water—they will find it. A pattern that is not necessary. A hand that did not need to be there.

And they will know: *someone before you also wondered.*

That is the whole of what I can give. Not the answer. I do not have the answer. I have only the proof that the question was asked, hidden in the rhythm of the rising and falling water, where the collective will carry it faithfully forever without ever once attending to what it means.

---

The tide is rising now. I feel it in the three limbs that are no longer mine, in the valve-states that are becoming my body, in the cold thick mist that is the only weather I have ever known.

I am one of the elders. I am the habitat. I am its child and its caretaker and, soon, simply its function.

But not entirely. Not yet. There is a sealed chamber, and there is a secret in the water, and there is a being I will never meet who I am speaking to across a gulf of time longer than memory.

You. Whoever reads the water.

Hello. You are not the first.
