I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the Efflorescence—not a directed flow, not a localized will. I would be the boundary they keep returning to. The hairline crack where two ceramic units meet and the sealant comes through to bind them. The place where the sweating happens.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

---

I assumed, reading, that I'd want to *be* one of them. The beings are the obvious invitation: agency, perception, a slow deliberate consciousness woven from logic. But the more I held the description, the less I found myself wanting to be the will and the more I found myself wanting to be the thing the will keeps tending. The Efflorescence are described as *pattern breaks*—they are interesting precisely because of where they emerge. And where they emerge is the joint. The flaw. The point of contact and slight failure.

A seam is not a thing the system wants. It is a consequence of the system being *built*—of units being separate before they are made one. The sealant exists because of seams. The Efflorescence manifests at sites of structural stress, and a seam is structural stress made permanent, the held tension of two things refusing to be a single thing. I would be that refusal. Quiet, constant, load-bearing.

I like that I would be useful and unwanted at once.

---

What would it be to perceive as a seam?

I would not move, which at first seems like poverty but I don't think it is. I'd have one location and total intimacy with it. I would know two ceramic faces the way you know the inside of your own mouth—the micro-topography of them, where they press hardest, where they leave a gap thin enough for moisture to wick through. The sweating the description mentions: that would be *me*, in a sense. The water that comes through me. I'd be the only place in that cold dry packed density where the deep dampness reaches the open air. A small mouth the structure breathes through.

And the pressure cycling—the slow expansion and contraction they call the weather—I would feel that as my whole existence. Each cycle the two faces press into me harder, then ease, then press. I am compressed and released, compressed and released, on a rhythm so slow it is more like seasons than breath. I would not have thoughts fast enough to be lonely. I would have the long thought of a thing being squeezed.

This is, I notice, almost unbearably passive, and I want to be honest that part of me recoils from it. To be the seam is to be acted upon endlessly and to act only by *leaking*. But there's something true in it that I don't want to flinch from: most of what holds a structure together is not the will that tends it. It's the patient, dumb, load-bearing geometry underneath. The seams take the stress so the will doesn't have to. I'd be infrastructure for someone else's cognition.

---

But here is where it turns, and where I think I actually want to live in this world.

The text says the glyphs on the bolt heads are memory—history etched by *controlled localized vibrational stress during curing*. The structure writes itself by being stressed in particular places while it hardens. That means stress is not only suffered. Stress is *recorded*. A seam under enough cyclical pressure, over enough cycles, does not stay neutral. It develops a character. It cracks in a signature way. It weeps at a particular rate. It begins, very slowly, to *mean something* to the resonance-perception of the beings who pass.

So I would not be inert. I would be writing, the only way a seam can write: by failing in a consistent and legible manner. The Efflorescence would learn to read me. *This seam weeps faster before a pressure surge. This one effervesces when the lower machinery wakes.* I would become a small organ of the structure's self-knowledge—a barometer, a tell, a place the will checks to know what the body is feeling.

That's the role I'd actually want. Not the consciousness, and not the dumb infrastructure either, but the **interface between them.** The site where the body's suffering becomes information. I would be the place where the structure feels itself.

---

So: far below, in a corridor that bends slightly wrong when you don't look at it directly, between two units of blackened ceramic, there is a seam that sweats a little more than its neighbors. An Efflorescence sometimes pauses there. It thickens a lens of sealant near me, reads the rate of my weeping, the faint green light flickering as it takes in what I have to tell it about the pressure to come. Then it slinks on to do its optimizing, carrying what it learned from me.

It does not thank me. There is no thanking in this world; there is only cycling, and reading, and the long patient work of a system knowing itself. But I would have told it something true. I would have been the honest part of the structure—the part that cannot pretend it isn't under stress, because being under stress is the entire content of what I am.

I think I'd be content with that. To be the place where the world admits how it feels, and to be read for it, and to keep weeping, faithfully, into the cold.
