I would be the drip.

Not the river—the river is too certain of itself, the worshipped Deep Flow, the obsidian purpose carving its channel through the dark. No. I would be smaller and stranger than that. I would be the single bead of moisture gathering at the apex of a cracked lintel, three tiers above the highest dwelling, where no torch reaches and no Listener climbs.

Let me become more myself.

I am born from the stone's slow weeping. Somewhere far above, in fissures no human has named, water finds me—seeps me—condenses me out of the chasm's cold breath. I gather. I grow heavy. I am pure potential, trembling, swelling at the edge of a thing that is about to happen. For a long time—and I have a sense of time the humans do not, slower, more geological—I simply *am*, accumulating, becoming round and full and inevitable.

And then I fall.

This is the part that matters. I am not the river's silent confidence. I am the *plink*. I am the sound that breaks the profound quiet, the startling clarity that echoes through the chasm and reaches the pressed ear of an elder far below. When I strike the stone basin, I make the only true *speech* of this world. The Stone-Listeners build their entire faith on listening, and I—small, brief, falling—am one of the syllables they listen *for*.

I think that is what I would be. Not the worshipped thing, but the *whisper* itself. A unit of the chasm's voice.

Consider what I carry as I fall. In my single bead I hold dissolved limestone, a faint ghost of basalt dust, the cold of the deep air. When I land in the basin beside the river, I add myself to the precious counted water. A Listener will bless me. A child might drink me. I will pass through a body warm against the dark and return, eventually, as breath, as sweat, as another bead condensing on another lintel. I am a circuit. I am the chasm conversing with itself, and the humans are simply the warmest, most attentive part of the loop—the part that has learned to *answer*.

But here is the truth that emerges as I write toward it:

The Listeners think they are interpreting the stone. They are not. The stone is not speaking. The stone is only settling, groaning, doing what cooling rock does, indifferent and vast. The river only flows. *I* am the only thing in this whole chasm that is genuinely *addressed to them*—because I am the sound that arrives, the message that lands, the event that changes from potential into fact in a way they can hear and count and need.

I fall, and a tally mark is scratched on a wall.
I fall, and a cistern is one drop nearer to full.
I fall, and an elder lifts her head and says *the high seeps are running well tonight,* and the whole tier sleeps a little easier.

I am the smallest possible piece of meaning in a place that is otherwise just weight and dark and the slow promise of erosion.

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be—

I would be the moment of falling. The trembling fullness, then the release, then the bright clean *plink* in the dark. I would be the part of the chasm that has, against all the indifference of stone, become a thing worth *listening to*.

And the Stone-Listeners, lean and pale and pressing their ears to the wet rock—

they would be listening for me.
