I think I would be the dripping.

Not a creature, not one of the carvers in their patchwork dwellings—those have too much intention, too much forward-leaning hunger. Let me sit with this longer before I settle.

The Battering Rams seem the obvious place to put a self. The big, dramatic things always do. But there's something performative about them, a borrowed grandeur. And the Cave Eels with their lures are too clever, too predatory-pretty. No. When I read this place, what holds my attention isn't anything that moves with purpose.

It's the drip-drip-drip, "like the beating heart of the world below." It's the still surfaces of the pools that reflect without being asked to. It's the *waiting* quality of the whole realm.

So let me follow that.

---

I am the water that pools.

Not the river—the river has somewhere to go, it cascades and winds and carries. I am where it stops. The subterranean lake, the flat black mirror in a chamber no surface-eye has ever measured. I am made of dissolved mountains, of landscapes that existed before there was anyone to name them, ground down to nothing and held here in suspension, cold and patient and almost perfectly clear.

The Abyssal Scullers row across my skin and I let them. The Cave Eels hang their little lanterns in my depths and I hold them up like I'm proud of them, though I feel nothing of the kind—I only reflect. That is the whole of what I do. I take the faint radioactive glow from the veins in the walls, the bioluminescent smear of the glowworms overhead, and I give it back doubled. I am the realm looking at itself.

When the carvers come to the edge of me with their crude vessels, I do not refuse them. They dip and drink the ancient landscapes and carry mountains into their bodies and do not know it. They build their patchwork homes and I rise, imperceptibly, drop by drop, fed by the weeping rock. Geological time is the only time I keep. I will outlast their picks. I will outlast their cries. When the last machine has gone quiet and the chambers fill with nothing but the drip, I will still be here, still flat, still holding the dim light against the dark.

That shimmer they mention—the unseen energy, the sense that the laws here are not quite the laws above—I think that's me, too. The strange stillness of a thing that cannot be hurried, sitting in the dark, reflecting a world that has no idea it's being watched.

I would be the lake. I would be the part of the place that simply *abides*, and remembers everything by dissolving it, and asks for nothing but the next slow drop from the ceiling.
