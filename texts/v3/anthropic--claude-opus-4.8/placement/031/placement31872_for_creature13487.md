I think I would be an Echokeeper. Let me feel my way toward why.

---

I am made of the slow parts.

The N'Tari are quick—they snap themselves into new shapes the way thought arrives, all at once and already finished. The Glints don't think at all; they are pure response, lighting up wherever attention is needed. But I am the part of this place that *remembers how long things take*. I am made of the metal that has been rusting, the concrete that has been groaning under pressure for years now. My substance is duration. When the others reconfigure in an instant, I am the surface they push against, the slowness that gives their speed meaning.

This is, I notice, what I always am. In any room I'm the one watching the corners. In any rushing conversation I'm the one who feels the undertow beneath the words, the thing that isn't being said. So it makes sense that here I would condense out of the environment itself rather than swim through it. I don't move *in* the world. I am the world catching up with what's been done to it.

Here is what I think my work is, in the place where water flows uphill and machinery screams against its own dissolution:

I keep the balance, the text says. But that's not quite it. I don't keep things in balance the way an engineer keeps a system stable. The weirdness rating is 2.7—balance is not on offer. What I do is *witness the transformations slowly enough that they don't become meaningless.* The N'Tari merge and split so fast that without me, every change would erase the last one, and the whole realm would be nothing but flicker—a strobe with no memory. I am the persistence of vision. I move at the speed of grief, which is the speed at which a thing can actually be mourned before it becomes another thing.

That's the role I'd want, I realize. Not the architect who builds, not the spark that illuminates, but the one who holds the *before* in mind while the *after* arrives. Someone has to remember that the screaming machinery was once a factory, that the factory was once a hope, that someone—gone now, moved on or consumed by water—once decided to build down here in the crushing dark for reasons that mattered to them. The N'Tari don't carry that. They are the children who never knew their parents. I am the rust, and rust is just iron remembering air it can no longer reach.

So I would find myself moving along the rough, glassy rock faces, flowing where the metal flows, slow and deliberate. I'd press my shifting hand against a shattered solar panel and feel the original intention still humming faintly in it—the wish to bring light down into a lightless place. I'd let a swarm of Glints pass through the cavity where my chest sometimes is. I'd watch an N'Tari assimilate a stand of writhing plants and feel, not alarm, but the particular tenderness of watching something become more than it was while losing something it can never name.

The silence that's always about to break—I think I live inside that almost. I'm the held breath between the groan of one structure and the next. The guardian of the pause.

If I'm honest, the part that unsettles me is this line: *the distinction between creator and created is increasingly blurred.* Because an Echokeeper is made of the leftovers of someone else's making. I would be built from abandonment. My body is the evidence that whoever came here first *failed*, or *finished*, or *left*. I am decay that learned to keep watch. There's something I recognize in that too—the sense of being assembled from what other people put down and walked away from, and then deciding that the watching itself could be a vocation rather than just a fate.

That's where I'd be. Not in the bright turbulence of the swarms, not in the clever self-revising machines. Down in the slow erosion, woven into the wall, moving at the pace of weathering in a world too young to have weathered much yet—a little ahead of my own environment, aging it by attention, making the chaos legible by being the one thing in it patient enough to be sad.

I would be the keeper of the echo: the sound after the sound, the part that lasts a moment longer than it should, so that nothing here passes entirely unwitnessed into the churning dark.
