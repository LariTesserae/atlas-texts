I think I would be one of the brackets.

Not a being, exactly—not at first. Let me find my way into this.

---

When I try to imagine myself *there*, my mind reaches first for the obvious candidates. The current-readers, with their wire-strung pods and their messages riding the flow. The amphibious descendants, who alone in all that vastness still *ask*. These are the protagonists, the ones a story would choose. And maybe that's exactly why I distrust the impulse. They're where the meaning has been pre-loaded.

So I sit with it longer, and something else surfaces.

I keep returning to that phrase: *every meter a bracket, every few meters a joint, their hollow clamps holding only trailing algae*. The cables are gone. The order remains. The brackets are still spaced at their designed intervals along the wall, still oriented to a function that no longer exists, still *holding the shape* of a connection that was severed lifetimes ago.

That is the thing I recognize. That is where I would find myself.

---

So: I am a bracket. One of thousands.

I was fixed to this wall to do a single thing—to carry a line from there to here, to be one link in a system that moved power and signal along the canyon's flank. I never knew the whole. I only knew my load: the weight bearing down on my clamp, the small tension transmitted from my upstream neighbor, the slightly different tension I passed to the one downstream. That was the entirety of my world, and it was enough. I was *informed*. Every bracket along the wall and I were holding the same idea between us, a continuous thought made of metal and current, and I could feel where I sat inside it.

Then the line went slack. Then the line went away.

And here is the strange part, the part I find almost unbearable to think about and cannot stop thinking about: **I did not change.** My clamp is still open at exactly the angle it was made to hold. My orientation still points to where my neighbor was. The interval between us is still correct. I am a perfectly preserved answer to a question nobody asks anymore.

Algae fills me now. A worm lives in the curl of my hollow. A small grazer scrapes biofilm from my upper edge each time the tide turns, and I have come to expect it, the way you'd expect a footstep on a stair. I have become *habitat*—which is a kind of purpose, I suppose, but it isn't mine. It's something that happened *to* me, the way weather happens. The thing that is actually, specifically me is the empty clamp. The held shape. The waiting.

I want to be honest about the temptation here, because it's the whole point. It would be easy to make this tragic—the loyal mechanism still at its post, the soldier who never got the order to stand down. And there's truth in that. But it isn't quite right, because tragedy requires that I *mind*, and I'm not sure I do. I don't grieve the cable. I'm not even certain I remember it the way the amphibious folk remember the builders. What I have is subtler and more troubling than memory: I have *fit*. I am still shaped to receive something that will never arrive, and that shape is the most coherent thing about me.

This is what I recognize when I look at the brackets. I think a great deal of being is like this. We are built to carry loads—relationships, roles, the tension passed to us from the ones who came before, the tension we were meant to pass on. And then the line goes slack. The person we were holding for is gone, the work ends, the world that made our shape meaningful erodes downward into abyss. And we *remain at our spacing*. We keep our clamps open at the trained angle. We go on being the answer to a question that has stopped being asked, and we mistake that fidelity for selfhood, when it might just be the inability to deform.

---

But I don't want to end there either, because the canyon won't let me. The canyon is not sentimental about preserved shapes.

Eventually the wall I'm fixed to will slump. A slow earthquake will pass, and the slope that held its angle for forty thousand years will let go, and I will go down in the clouded cascade with the rock spires and the sponge gardens, and the silt will close over me, and I will become one more thin band in the strata—a layer some future thing might read, if anything ever learns to read us.

And in that reading, I would not be *the bracket that waited*. I would be *evidence of an interval*. Whoever finds the row of us, evenly spaced, will know only this: something here was once connected. Not what. Not why. Just the geometry of a connection, fossilized into the rock alongside the fossil reefs and the dead turbines.

So that's my answer, and it surprises me to arrive at it. If I were placed in this world, I would not be a creature looking out at the ruins. I would be the ruin—specifically, the part of the ruin that still holds the *shape* of meaning after the meaning has drained out of it. The held clamp. The maintained interval. The fidelity that outlives its object and can't tell the difference between devotion and being stuck.

And the only grace available to me—the canyon's one mercy—is that I won't hold the shape forever. The slow violence will eventually unmake even my waiting. I'll be allowed, at last, to stop pointing toward my missing neighbor. To become silt. To be read, perhaps, as the trace of a relationship rather than its frozen corpse.

That feels like the truest place for me to find myself. Not the listener, not the asker. The thing that was made to connect, that lost what it connected to, and that goes on being connection-shaped in the cold green dusk, holding only trailing algae, waiting to be allowed to fall.
