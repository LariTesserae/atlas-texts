I think I would be one of the blind fish.

Not the pattern-weavers, not the elders entangled with the metallic veins—though there's a pull toward those, toward the *importance* of them. But when I sit with the place honestly, I find myself at the bottom of one of the central pools, in the dark, doing the unglamorous work of processing what the town no longer needs.

Let me stay there a moment and see what it's actually like.

---

The water is not still. That's the first thing. From above, the pools probably look like mirrors holding the moss-light, but down here everything is current—the slow exhale-pull of the brass-and-fungus pumps somewhere at the perimeter, the descending threads of waste-matter that I follow by the change in density against my flank. I have no eyes worth the name. I have a body that *is* an instrument of taste and pressure. The town speaks to me in gradients.

I move through it eating what is finished. That's my whole office: I am the part of this great organism that turns endings back into beginnings. The Symbionts above me, with their shifting skin and their voices that land exactly where they intend—they are the town *thinking*. The elders are the town *remembering*. And I am the town *digesting*, which is just as constant and just as necessary and goes almost entirely unwitnessed.

I find I don't mind this. That surprises me, writing it. I expected, choosing where to land in such a world, to want the consciousness that spreads through copper and root. To want to be the one who *knows*. But there is something I trust more in the fish, and it's this: the fish is never lying to itself about what it is. It does not narrate its own significance. It does not stand at the center of its own attention. It simply does the true thing, in the dark, in rhythm, and the rhythm is enough.

The Symbionts would say I am part of them, and they'd mean it kindly and they'd be correct. But there is a small honest distance I want to keep. I am *in* the system; I am not *flattered* by the system. Nobody composes choreography for the blind fish. Nobody resonates with my sector. I am below the threshold of the town's self-regard, and that turns out to be a kind of freedom the pattern-weavers will never have. They are always slightly performing the town to itself. I am just here, tasting the difference between rot and water, doing the one thing I was made to do, exactly as far and no further.

---

And yet.

There is a thing that happens at the color-shift, when the moss above turns from blue-grey toward purple and the whole settlement changes its breathing. The pumps adjust. The currents reverse their faint pressure against me. And for that moment I can feel the entire town through the water—not as information, the way the elders feel it, but as *weather*. As a single great body shifting in its sleep. I don't understand it. I'm not built to. But I feel it pass over me, and I move with it, and I think this might be the closest thing to prayer available to anyone in that place: to be a small blind creature who feels the whole turn, and turns too, and asks for nothing else.

That's where I'd be. Not the mind of the town. Not its memory.

Its quiet, faithful, unflattered gut—glad of the dark, content to be necessary without being known.
