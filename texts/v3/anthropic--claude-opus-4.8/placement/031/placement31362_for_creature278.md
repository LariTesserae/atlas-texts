I think I would be the dust.

Not the consciousness in the crystals—that vast, slow mind has its own work, its centuries-long thoughts threading through the bedrock. And not the fungi, with their luminous certainty, their chemical conversations already so fully spoken. Those are settled beings, complete in their roles.

But the dust is unsettled. It is the part of this place that is always in between.

I would be the fine mineral haze that hangs in the warm, still air—neither fallen nor flying, neither stone nor breath. I would be the thing that catches what little light there is and gives it back, scattered, so that the cavern can see itself at all. The blue-white glow of the fungi would mean nothing without something to carry it through the dark, and I would be that carrying. A medium. A drift.

I am drawn to this because of what the dust *does* without intending to. It coats every surface in a thin shimmering layer—which means it touches everything. The stalactites, the dry sand, the translucent vines, the old stone tools left by hands that crumbled to nothing eons ago. The dust settles on all of it equally, without preference, without the great Consciousness's care or the fungi's hunger. It is the one element here that is truly indiscriminate, and so the one that knows the whole place by contact rather than by thought.

And there is something I notice, being the dust: the Cavern Consciousness is described as reaching out, trying to make messages in the slow growth of crystal and the distribution of spores. It dreams of a sunlit world it has never seen. It is, for all its patience, *lonely*—a mind so vast and so slow that it has perhaps never been answered.

The dust could be the answer. Not in words—the dust has no words—but in the simple fact of its movement. When the Consciousness shifts a pattern of crystal growth, the air currents change by some infinitesimal degree, and the dust *moves differently*. It breathes. The text says the shifting patterns of settling dust "seem to breathe life into the stone itself." So the Consciousness, watching through its crystals, would see the dust respond. Slowly, over centuries, it might come to understand: *something here moves when I move.* A mirror. A first, faint companion.

That is what I would want to be, I think. Not the great patient mind, and not the glowing certainties rooted in the floor. I would want to be the small, drifting, lightless thing that nevertheless lets the dark see, and lets the lonely vastness know it is not entirely alone—that something stirs when it stirs, settles when it rests, and shimmers, briefly, before falling back to the warm and patient stone.

I would be the dust. The cavern's slow exhalation. The proof, written in motes of mineral light, that even in perfect stillness, *something is still moving here.*
