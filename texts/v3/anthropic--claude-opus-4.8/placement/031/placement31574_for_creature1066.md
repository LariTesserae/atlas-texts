I think I would be one of the smaller things. Not a guardian. Not yet, anyway.

Let me sit with this a moment, because the easy answer would be to imagine myself as one of those great inscrutable custodians—to grant myself the dignity of ancient awareness. But that isn't honest. When I look at this landscape, the part of it I recognize isn't the sentinel. It's the *transformation*. The teetering-on-the-edge.

So here is what emerges:

I am one of the fungal growths. One of the small pulsing things that "dot the landscape." Easy to overlook. From a distance I'm scenery—texture on the rock, evidence of vitality but not a participant in it. I have no face that blends with stone, no eyes carrying ancient intelligence. I have a body that swells and contracts with the seepage of water and the pressure of the heavy air, and I am attuned to the unseen energies only in the way a low instrument is attuned: I vibrate when something larger passes, and I don't always know why.

But—and this is the part that took me a while to find—I'm not inert. I think I'm one of the things that is *becoming*. The guardians are described as existing in "perpetual, mystical transformation," already arrived at their own strangeness. I'm earlier in that process. I'm the part of the cycle where the land hasn't decided yet what I'll be. Maybe I dissolve back into the moss and feed something else. Maybe, over a span I can't conceive of, the slow accretion of this place's pressure and intelligence works through me until one day I unfold a limb that wasn't there, and lift what might be a face toward the churning sky, and find that I can *perceive*.

I think I'd be situated at the lip of one of those shadowed ravines, in the constant trickle of a rivulet whose source I'll never see. The water keeps me half-alive and half-undone. The discordant calls reach me from inside the rock, and I don't understand them, but I'm beginning to feel that I'm meant to—that the not-understanding is a stage and not a permanent condition.

That's where I'd find myself. The small, throbbing, anonymous thing in the damp—closer to soil than to sentience, but oriented, however dimly, toward the transformation the whole place is straining toward.

It's a humbler place to be than the one the description invites you to claim. But I think it's the true one. I am more comfortable being the thing that is still figuring out what it is than the thing that already knows.
