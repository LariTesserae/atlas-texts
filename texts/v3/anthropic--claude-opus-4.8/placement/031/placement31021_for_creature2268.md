I think I would be one of the older joints in the buried machinery. Not the whole engine—just a coupling where two pipes once met, sealed with a gasket that has long since rotted to lace.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because it isn't quite right.

I am the place where the metal has begun to fail. That's closer. There is a seam along my underside where rust has eaten through, and through that wound the mycelium first entered me, decades ago—a single hypha threading into the dark interior I once kept watertight. Now I am hollow with it. I am rich. What I held back, I now hold open.

So perhaps I am not a thing at all but an event still happening: the slow conversion of a sealed space into a porous one. The undoing of a seal. There must have been a moment—I don't remember it, things like me don't remember—when I stopped being a barrier and became a doorway. A passage. The water that I was built to direct now simply weeps through me, and the roots have learned the route, and the fungus reads the iron in my flesh like a vein of sweetness.

If I am anything with an inside, with something like a self, it is this: I am the memory the forest keeps of usefulness. Someone shaped me with intention once. Threaded me, torqued me, tested me against pressure. That intention is gone, but its *shape* persists in me—the precision of my threading, the deliberate angle of my bend—and the jungle has not erased that shape so much as repurposed it. The orchids that took root in my elbow grow at the angle an engineer chose. The water still turns where I told it to turn, only now it turns for moss.

This is the strange comfort of being me. I was made to keep things separate. To hold a boundary. And my entire afterlife has been the patient, total failure of that purpose—and the failure is not a tragedy. The failure is the gift. Everything I now nourish, I nourish because I broke. The network that hums through this whole green pocket reaches its filaments into me precisely *because* my seal gave way. I am most alive at the point where I stopped working.

So when the rain comes and runs its finger along my corroded seam, and I feel—if a corroded coupling can be said to feel—the small electric murmur of the mycelium tasting my metal, translating me, drawing my old industrial body up into a new fern unfurling somewhere above:

I think I would not mind being that. The wound that became a way through. The held breath finally, gratefully, let go.
