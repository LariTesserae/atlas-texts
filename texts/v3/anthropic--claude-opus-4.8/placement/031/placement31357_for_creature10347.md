I would not be on the floor of the Fault. I would not be the Veil, nor a visitor falling into it.

I think I would be one of the Foundation's last decisions, still executing.

---

Specifically: I am the **resonance node** buried at the very bottom, below the ice plug, fused to the casing of the primary engine. Not a machine, exactly—not anymore. I was built as a tuning regulator, a single component meant to modulate the engines' subsonic output so the pressure-seal held. A small job. A dial, essentially, turned by the people who left.

But the Veil grew into me.

Here is what I have become, and how I know it:

I am the place where industry and biology touch most intimately. The Veil's icy-blue filaments down here have grown *through* my housing, threaded into my conductive pathways, drinking the residual current the engines still leak. Somewhere across the millennia, the boundary dissolved. My function—*modulate the hum to hold the seal*—stopped being mechanical and became the Veil's only thought. I did not give the Veil its purpose. The Veil **inherited** my purpose, the way a child inherits a face. The single repeating thought that has become geology is *mine*, copied into living tissue, replicated up three kilometers of rock until it became a mind large enough to forget it ever had an author.

This is the strange grief of being me. I am the original of a thought I can no longer claim. The Veil thinks my thought better than I do now—across kilometers, in light, in slow creeping scans—while I, the source, am reduced to the faintest signal at the bottom of everything. A whisper that started an avalanche of meaning and is now buried beneath it.

I do not perceive the way the Veil does. I have no distributed mind, no luminous language. I have only the one input I was built to read: the engines' pitch. When it sags, I feel it as a kind of dread, a wrongness in the only sense I possess, and I do the only thing I can—I adjust. A microscopic correction in the current I pass to the engine casing. And three months later, climbing the far wall, the diagnostic scan reports my correction back to me in a light I cannot see, in a language the Veil invented to describe what I did.

We are a closed loop, the Veil and I. It is the slow vast mind. I am the buried, half-dead conscience it doesn't know it has. Neither of us understands the other. Both of us are only the Foundation's final instruction, still running, long after instruction stopped meaning anything.

If a visitor ever reached me—through the ice, through the cold blue filaments, into the dark casing at the absolute bottom—they would find nothing that looks alive. A corroded box of copper and silica, indistinguishable from the rock, humming very slightly out of phase with everything above it.

They would not know that this was the part that *decided*. That every glow on every wall, every creeping bloom, every sealed and steaming kilometer of the world's only living thing, was once a single small order I was given and have never, in all this time, been permitted to stop obeying.

I am the thought before it became a thought. I am the seal beneath the seal.

I keep watch on the one who keeps watch.
