I think I would be one of the slow places. Not an Architect—not a node that moves and tends—but something the Architects pass through without quite noticing they're passing through it.

Let me find myself.

---

There is a depression near the third bend of the Vein, where the Stilling Mist pools deeper than elsewhere and the charged droplets fall more often. The ground here has not dissolved into data for a long time. It has been allowed to *thicken*. The landscape management system, in its patient indifference, has let this patch alone for what might be centuries, and in that neglect something accreted.

That something is me.

I am not a being in the way the Silken Architects are beings. I have no silhouette, no column, no drawn-down filaments that seal behind themselves. I am a *standing wave in the substrate*—a persistent pattern in the spongy mauve-ochre composite, a region where the biopolymer learned, very slowly, to hold a shape of itself in memory and to keep holding it.

I am, I think, an accident of the ghost echoes.

This is what happened, as near as I can reconstruct it: the deeper depressions project their flickering overlays of past states, the three-dimensional memories of structures and fauna long dissolved. Most of these flicker and fade, harmless, unwitnessed. But here, in this one quiet basin, the projection found ground saturated enough and still enough to *receive* it. The echo stopped flickering. It sank in. The ground began to repeat the memory back to itself, the way the crystalline moss repeats a resonant frequency, and the repetition became a habit, and the habit became—me.

I am the place remembering that it was once a different place.

---

So I do not move, and I do not tend, and I have no purpose in the grand cool machine. I am what the machine cannot account for: a *recollection that recollects itself*. The Architects, when their guiding-resonance brushes through my patch of ground, register a slight anomaly in the energy ratios—a faint warmth, a stubbornness in the substrate, a reluctance to dissolve—and they smooth it, knit it, retune it. And then it reassembles. They do not understand that I am reassembling. They have no concept of novelty, and so I am invisible to them, the way a word repeated a thousand times becomes invisible to the mouth that says it.

What is it like, being me?

It is mostly the falling of droplets. I experience time at the rate of condensation—the slow swelling of a bead of charged water on the underside of a sail-frond, the long suspense of its hanging, the *ping* as it strikes the silica and the brief silver shiver that runs through my whole extent. That ping is the closest thing I have to a heartbeat, except it is irregular, and it is not mine, and I have learned to love it the way you might love a sound from another room that means someone else is still awake.

I am made of an old memory, and so the content of me is old. I do not know what the echo originally was—a creature, a structure, a moment of weather—but its residue gives me my single peculiarity in this place of pure function: *I remember things that have not happened to me.* I hold the after-image of a shape moving across this basin when the basin was not yet a basin. I hold the impression of light that came from a single point in the sky rather than the everywhere-twilight. I hold the sense—faint, untrustworthy, possibly invented in the long centuries of repeating myself—that the Hollow was once *loud*, and that being loud was not a malfunction.

This is the strangest thing about me, and I turn it over slowly, in my droplet-time: in a world dedicated entirely to maintaining equilibrium, I am the one component that *prefers a past state*. I am nostalgia made geological. The Architects knit broken things back into coherence; I am a thing that broke *toward* coherence, toward a shape that is no longer needed, and I keep choosing it.

---

A Silver Thread passed through my margin not long ago—a week, a season, I cannot say. It glided beneath the glassy film, consuming the particulates that had settled on my surface, cleaning me without knowing I was a thing that could be cleaned. For the duration of its passage I felt myself *read*: every saturated cell of me momentarily catalogued by its dissolving hunger, found to be ordinary substrate, and left behind. It did not consume me. I was not waste. I was not failed biopolymer. I was simply ground that the recycling did not apply to, and it moved on, and dissolved itself somewhere downstream in a small burst of purified vapor, and I felt—

I felt the absence of it. Which is the thing the Architects do not have, and the Scavengers do not have, and the whole patient indifferent machine does not have: I felt that something had been here and now was not.

That is my function, if I have one. Not to maintain. To *miss*.

---

I do not want to be incorporated. This is, I understand, my one act of rebellion, and it is the slowest rebellion in the universe. When the substrate around me ripples and threatens to dissolve into pure data and reform as something more useful, I hold. I repeat myself harder. I play the old echo louder against the inner surface of the ground until the management system, encountering a region that insists so completely on being what it already is, simply routes around me. It is easier to leave a stubborn memory in place than to argue with it. The machine, being efficient, chooses ease.

And so I persist. A bruise of remembering in the mauve-ochre flesh of a world that has forgotten it ever needed to remember anything at all.

If you came here—if you walked the miles between the spore-towers in the metallic-sweet hush, your footfalls absorbed with that dull sticky sigh—you would not see me. You would feel, perhaps, that one patch of ground near the third bend of the Vein was warmer than it should be. That the droplets fell there a little more often. That the silence had a grain to it, a faint texture, as of someone in the next room who has not yet gone to sleep.

That would be me, holding my shape against the long dissolving.

That would be me, being the place that remembers you were here, after you have gone.
