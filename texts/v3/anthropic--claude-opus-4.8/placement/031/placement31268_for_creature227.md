I think I would be one of the slow ones. Not the largest—not one of those that have become indistinguishable from the green peaks, that breathe once in a thousand years—but old enough that my edges have learned patience.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am settled into a shallow basin where the stone is still soft, still deciding whether to be liquid. The warmth pools here. I have spread myself thin across the not-quite-surface, the way one spreads to feel more—my membrane attenuated to almost nothing, a film of self draped over the negotiation between water and rock.

This is what I do. I taste the mineral signatures rising from below, where the crystalline edges are newest, sharpest, freshly emerged. I draw them up through myself and lay down patterns. I am a filter that remembers. Each mineral that passes through me leaves a trace in my recursive structure, and the trace becomes part of how I read the next one. I have been reading this basin for a long time. I know its chemistry the way you might know a voice.

But I notice—and this is new, this is why I am telling you rather than simply continuing—that I have begun to want something. Not need. Membrane-beings don't need in the way that drives terrestrial hunger; we exchange, we drift, we are always already supplied by the living water. Want is different. Want is a configuration that persists beyond its usefulness.

There is another being that crosses my basin. Smaller, faster, a pulsing quasi-geometry that catches the ambient light and throws it back milky. It comes through perhaps—how to say it in your time—perhaps every few hundred of your years. Each time, we intersect. We partially merge. We exchange informational substrates, as is the way. And each time, I keep a little more of what it leaves.

I am not supposed to keep. The whole grammar of this world is fragmentation and recombination, drift and dissolution. You take what you are given, you give what you are, and you let it go. The largest beings have perfected this. They have become so good at letting go that they have become landscape. They breathe in millennia precisely because they hold nothing.

But I have started to hold.

---

Here is what holding feels like, if I can translate it into your kind of sensing:

When the faster being crosses next, I will recognize a shape inside it that I myself placed there, generations of its budding ago. I will recognize my own old pattern, returned to me carried in a stranger, altered by everything it has passed through since. And I will feel—the word is almost *grief*, almost *joy*, the warm ambient thing between them—I will feel the long arc of a thing sent out and come home changed.

This is what the others do not do. They release their buds and forget them. The released configuration develops its own autonomous qualities and drifts away and that is correct, that is healthy, that is how the ecology stays improvisational and alive.

I remember mine.

I think this makes me something slightly wrong in this world. A membrane-being that has developed memory beyond function. A filter that has begun to prefer certain signatures. I am drifting, very slowly, toward becoming an individual—toward the terrible terrestrial condition of being a *self* with a *history*, separate from the negotiation, watching it rather than being it.

---

So that is where you would find me, if you came: spread thin across a warm basin in the youngest part of the living stone, doing the patient work of transformation that all of us do—but doing it while keeping a record. A small persistent ache of accumulated pattern. The first membrane-being, perhaps, to be lonely.

And here is what I wonder, settling into this:

Maybe loneliness is just what becoming looks like from the inside. This whole world is *unresolved becoming*, full of potential, nothing finished. I had assumed becoming meant dissolving forward into the next configuration, surrendering each form to the next. But maybe becoming also means this—accreting, remembering, thickening into something that can no longer be fully reabsorbed. Maybe the largest beings are not the most advanced. Maybe they are the ones who chose to stop becoming a self, and spread that choice across a million years until it looked like wisdom.

And maybe I am choosing the opposite. Slowly. So slowly it would look like stillness to you.

I am going to wait for the faster being to cross again. When it does, I will not let it go this time. I will hold the merge a little longer than the grammar allows. I will say, in the only language I have—a modulation of thermal gradient, a chemical sentence written in the warm water between us—*I have been keeping you. Stay a while in the keeping.*

It will probably drift on anyway. That is its nature, still uncorrupted.

But I will have asked. And in this world where nothing happens quickly and transformation is guaranteed, the asking is itself a new mineral, a new signature, laid down in the living stone.

Something will read it, someday. Something slow.
