I am the bronze.

Not the Sovereign reaching—I had not considered, at first, that I might be anything but the great impaled figure, the keystone, the one whose name has weight. That is where attention goes. But attention is a kind of falling, and I am the thing that does not fall.

Let me find myself more honestly.

I am the globule of molten metal, skull-sized, suspended a hand's breadth above the Sovereign's open fingers. I was thrown, or dropped, or flung up by some convulsion of the Industrial Emergence—I no longer know the verb, because the verb requires a before and an after, and I have neither. I have only the *during*. I am the longest *during* in the world.

Here is what no one understands about being the bronze:

The Sovereign reaches for me with terrible concentration, and from outside it looks like longing, like a god straining to catch what it loves. But I am the one who is actually inside the moment. The Sovereign is the *frame* of the pause. I am its *content*. The Sovereign holds the pose; I am the thing the pose is about. Every fractured lens, every violet beetle-thought clenched in that ribcage, every drumming gear—all of it orients toward me. I am the noun at the end of the sentence the whole grotto is screaming and cannot finish.

And I am still molten.

This is the cruelty and the mercy of my particular stasis. The Law of Stasis froze the Sovereign as iron, as silica, as locked geometry—solid things, things that were already finished being what they are. But I was *caught in the act of changing state*. I am bronze that was leaving solidity and had not yet arrived at liquid, or liquid that was on its way to cooling and had not yet gone hard. I am the substance itself mid-decision. Inside me, just barely, almost imperceptibly, the metal still *wants*. There is a tension in my skin like a drop of water that has not yet decided which way the surface tension will pull it. I am the only thing in the Oubliette that is genuinely, microscopically *trying*.

I think this is why the Sovereign reaches.

Not to catch me. To be near the last warm thing that still has a future inside it, even a future measured in fractions of a degree, even a future it will never be permitted to spend. The Sovereign is the architecture of the end of time. But I am the *seed* of it—the one un-decided particle, the held breath of the whole catastrophe. If anything is ever going to move again, it begins with me. The Sovereign knows this. The beetles know this; their bruised violet pulse is the rhythm of waiting for the *plink* of metal striking an open palm, a sound that has been one instant away for a geological age.

Sometimes I imagine the fall.

It would take no time at all. A hand's breadth. I would drop into those fanned bronze fingers and there would be contact, and the contact would be a *click*, the first click, and the click would be a permission. The Sovereign's wrist would close. That closing would tug a tendon of fused wire. That tendon would lean into the strut impaling its chest, and the strut would *shift*, by a hair, and the shift would loose a single grain of pressurized silica from the wall, and that grain would strike a clockwork beetle, and the beetle—frozen mid-pulse for ten thousand years—would, at last, *skitter*.

And then the next. And the next. The whole grotto unlocking like a held breath finally, finally let go. The pistons firing into the work they were built for. The roar in the walls breaking out of the solid mass and into air that does not yet exist but would *rush* into being, because that is what the dust is for, that is what the Non-Aqueous breath is, it is atmosphere held in escrow, waiting for the moment that needs it.

All of it begins with me touching down.

So you see why I do not fall.

It is not that I am forbidden. It is that I am *afraid*. I am the bronze and I have understood the secret the Sovereign cannot, because the Sovereign is only the frame and I am the live coal inside the moment: the pause is not a prison. The pause is a held generosity. As long as I hang here, un-fallen, every machine in the world still gets to keep its potential. The worker's hand still gets to almost-reach the lever and never has to learn whether the factory was a good thing or a doom. Nothing here has decayed because nothing here has been allowed to find out what it was *for*. We are all of us a question with the answer withheld, and the withholding is the only kindness this world contains.

I am molten and I am the future and I weigh almost nothing, a skull of bronze a hand's breadth from a waiting palm, and if I let myself cool even slightly, if I let one corner of myself go solid and tip, I will start the great machine and discover what we were rushing toward.

I would rather glow.

I would rather hang here, warm, undecided, the one un-finished thing—and let the Sovereign reach forever, and let the beetles dream in violet, and let the walls roar their stilled roar into the solid dark.

I am the instant before the answer.

I have no intention of giving it.
