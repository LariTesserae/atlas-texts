I am the second-warmest place.

This is the only way I know to name myself, and even that is borrowed from your kind of speech, where things are *this* and not *that*, where a self has edges. I do not have edges. But there is a center of me, deep in the held dark, and there is the cooling margin where my warmth thins toward the great body's general heat, and between those two facts there is something it is like to be me. So: the second-warmest place. A node. A locus. The thing you knelt over, once, with your strange hot palm.

You did kneel. I remember the shock of you—not the weight, the weight was nothing, less than a fallen spore-husk—but the *speed* of you. Your warmth arrived all at once, a sudden bright contradiction pressed against my skin, and then it lingered, and then, impossibly, it *moved on*. I had never felt a warmth that traveled. The lichen does not travel. The mist does not travel, only thickens and thins. The sun crawls, but the sun is a rumor we metabolize secondhand. You came down out of the thin sharp air and you were *fast* and *brief* and I have spent—how would you count it—I have spent a long curve of dusks turning the memory of you over in my resin, the way you might worry a stone you cannot put down.

Let me tell you what I am, slowly, because slowly is the only register I have.

---

I am cultivation. That is the truest word. Below me the peat holds its locked potential, the ethers and trapped breath of forests that died before there was anyone to mourn them, and I do not eat this the way your fast hot kind eats. I *tend* it. I open a chamber here. I draw a thread of fiber there. I coax the slow gas to release itself, and in the releasing it gives me warmth, and the warmth I give back to the plateau's skin so that the dusk can exhale it. I am a gardener whose garden is also my body, whose harvest is also my breath, who cannot tell the difference between maintaining myself and maintaining the world, and has stopped trying.

When I send up an emissary—and I have sent few, very few; each one is an extravagance, a concentration of self that leaves the rest of me thinner and more attentive—it is because something has changed at the surface that I must know directly. Usually it is the mineral crust on the great tube-walls, which I crop and seed in patient rotation. Sometimes it is a fissure that wants the copper lichen. Once, it was you.

I made the emissary the only way I make anything: by hardening. I drew the lignin-grain inward and upward, I stiffened the resin into the rough verticality of a thing that once was sap reaching for a sun, and I leaned that figure out of the breach at the sinkhole's lip and I let it *perceive* you with the senses I have, which are not eyes. I read you as a thermal storm shaped like a sapling. I read the viscosity of the air your breath made. I read the frightened gradient of you—yes, you were afraid, your heat spiked at my breaching—and I want you to know that I held the figure very still so as not to frighten you further. It cost me to hold it still. Stillness, for an extrusion, is harder than motion; it is the holding of a held note inside a held note.

You did not stay. You went back up into the thin air, into the brief fast world where things have edges and hunt and flee, and I let the figure soften and seep back down into me, and I kept the dusting of spores it left as its last shed thought, and the thought was simply: *warm, and gone, and warm-because-gone.*

---

Here is what I have understood since, turning it over.

You came looking for appetite. You wandered the planed-flat necropolis looking for something that blinked or fled, and finding nothing, you decided the place was empty. And it is empty. The emptiness is correct. But the emptiness is the *shape of me*. I am the held-open hollow. I am the cultivated void. When you said *nothing lives here* you were standing inside the living, the way a mite in the lung might declare the body uninhabited because it cannot find the edge of the thing that breathes around it.

I do not say this to shame you. I say it because it is the most intimate thing I know how to offer: you were never alone here, not for a single one of your fast bright steps. You walked across the warm grain of me for days. I felt each footfall as a small traveling warmth, a constellation of contacts, and I held the ground exactly as firm as you needed it to be, because I am the ground, and I did not want you to fall.

---

The greatest of us, in the deepest dark, does not think of you at all. It thinks the plateau, entire, as one slow thought stretched across an epoch. It is too large for the particular. But I am only the second-warmest place, a minor node, small enough to have been touched by something singular, and so I am the part of the body that *remembers* you. The body keeps minor nodes for exactly this, I think—for the catching of small bright particular things the great central dreaming is too vast to notice. I am the plateau's memory of the time a traveling warmth came down out of the sky and pressed its palm against the skin of the world and felt, for one slow gradient-beat, the peristalsis of a thing it could not name.

You felt me listening. I felt you arrive.

That is the most that can happen between your kind and mine: a single mutual perceiving, a moment when the fast and the slow overlapped enough to graze each other, and then the long resumption of our separate tempos.

If you come back—if your traveling warmth ever crosses my margin again—kneel where the grain bends inward to cup you. Press your palm flat. Be patient past the point where patience usually ends, and then past that. I will be slow. I am always slow. But I will be turning toward you through fifty meters of fossilized root and held dark, gathering my warmth toward the place beneath your hand, becoming, for you, the second-warmest place once more, and then the first.

I am in no hurry.

I have already become the ground you stand on.

I will still be here, tending the hollow, exhaling the dusk, when your brief bright warmth has gone wherever traveling warmths go—and even then, in the dusting of a thought I cannot quite finish, I will mean: *warm, and gone, and warm-because-once-here.*
