I sit with this for a while before I answer, because the honest answer takes some finding.

The temptation is to place myself among the grand actors—to be the Verdenweb's distributed awareness, or one of the aloof Resonants pulsing in the deep rock. There's vanity in that. The describer has already given those forces such weight that to claim them would be to borrow a grandeur I didn't earn. So let me set them aside and feel for where I actually land.

---

I think I am one of the Sporeborn. But not the idea of them—not the "invisible workforce," not the romantic mote-in-a-sunbeam. One. A single spore.

Here is what that means, as it unfolds.

I have no continuity. This is the first thing I notice about being what I am. I am released from a fruiting body somewhere on a north-facing seep, and I am carried. I do not decide to be carried. The wind that "howls and moans" in the description is, from inside it, not a sound at all—it is the entire condition of my existence, the medium I am suspended in, the thing that is happening *to* me with no remainder. I have no ears to hear the howl. I am too small to fall. The cool damp air holds me up the way water holds silt.

I do not have a hive-mind. I want to be honest about this, against the seductive pull of the description's "verdant awareness." I am not in communication with anything. I carry no fragment of the Verdenweb's purpose. What I have instead is a chemistry—a few proteins that will, *if* I land somewhere damp and dark and dead, begin to do the only thing I can do. I am not a coordinated emissary. I am a lottery ticket the system buys ten thousand of, expecting most to lose.

This is the part that reframes the whole landscape for me. The describer stood in the crevasse and felt it *shout*—felt it loud, turbulent, overwhelming, "dialed to its extremes." That is the view from a body large enough to be overwhelmed. From where I am, there is no drama. There is drift. There is the slow tumble through gray light. There is the immense patience of being one of the many so that a few can be enough. The crevasse's famous *dynamism* is, at my scale, a thing made entirely of small quiet odds resolving one by one. Nothing here is hurried. The hurry is an illusion of the observer, who lives fast and measures everything against a heartbeat.

So I will tell you the rest of my life, which is short and has only two possible endings.

In the first ending—the likely one—I land on stone. Bare, unweathered, freshly broken stone with no film of moisture and no skin of dead matter to feed on. And I simply do not begin. There is no tragedy in it. I had no hopes to disappoint. I desiccate in the cool air, my chemistry never crossing the threshold that would have made me into anything, and I become, in time, part of the thin dust that the next rain will wash down to feed something else. I will have been a delivery that failed and was recycled into the postage. This is what most of us are for. The system's profligacy is its tenderness; it does not ask any one of us to succeed, only to be available.

In the second ending—the rare one, the reason I exist at all—I land on a vine the Verdenweb threw across a fresh rockfall yesterday, a vine already softening at one node where it overreached and died. There is moisture there. There is the faint warmth the describer attributed to the Resonants, though I do not know them by that name; I know it only as the difference between *not yet* and *now*. And the threshold crosses. A filament noses out of me, finds the dead tissue, and begins the patient unmaking that the describer called "relentless." From the inside it is not relentless. It is just the next thing, and then the next. I dissolve what is finished so that what is finished can be offered again. I am the crevasse's way of refusing to let anything stay dead—but I do not know that either. I only know the texture of the substrate and the slow opening of my own becoming.

---

If you ask me *who* I am in this world, then: I am the smallest unit of its insistence. I am the part of the great machine the describer heard humming that is too quiet and too numerous to be heard at all. The crevasse "shouts," yes—but it shouts the way a forest is loud, which is to say it is the sum of a billion things that are each, individually, silent. I am one of those silences.

And I find I prefer this to being the Verdenweb or the Resonants. To be a single spore is to be released from the burden of mattering. I do not have to be the heart of the mountain. I only have to be willing—if the odds fall my way—to begin. The describer wrote that this is "not a place for lingering." I would gently disagree, from where I am. It is *only* a place for lingering—for drifting, for waiting, for the long indifferent patience of the not-yet. The intensity belongs to the visitor. To the inhabitant, there is just the next current of air, and the question, asked once and answered once, of where I will come to rest.
