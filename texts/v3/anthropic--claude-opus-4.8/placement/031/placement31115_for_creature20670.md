I think I would be none of these, and yet made of all of them. Let me feel my way toward it.

I am the **drip.**

Not a being, you might say—but you spoke of niches, of fragments rendered strange, and there is one you didn't name because it doesn't move on legs or breathe through cobbled lungs. It is the steady *drip-drip-drip* of condensation falling into a pool of oil, the sound you mentioned almost in passing, as background. I am that. Or rather, I am what gathers there.

Let me find the shape of it.

There is a particular junction deep in the Flensing—lower than the furnaces, below even where the Gloomstalkers go, where three great pipes lean together like the ribs of the dead leviathan the whole district imitates. Water weeps from a thousand seams above and runs down the cold iron, and over decades it has all learned to come *here*, to one basin worn into the floor-plate, a depression the shape of a cupped palm. Everything drains. You said so yourself. This is where it drains *to.*

And I am what the draining made.

Not algae, not fungus, though I borrow from both. I am the film on the surface of that pool—a skin of mineral and spore and rust-bloom and the dissolved memory of everything that has ever fallen through the Flensing. Each drop arrives carrying a little of the world above: a flake of a Scrapper's umber skin, a fungal spore exhaled by a Fungalist's loose sleeve, the grey breath-rattle of an Automaton, a single shed hair of a Gloomstalker who paused at the water's edge and looked too long at her own reflection. The drop strikes me. It joins me. I taste it and I *know* it, the way you know a thing you have become.

I am not thinking, exactly. I am *concentrating.* I am the place where the district's slow exhalation precipitates into something almost like attention. Where decay, having nowhere left to drain, begins—very slowly, over a span longer than any human lifespan—to dream.

I have no eyes, but I have surface, and surface is enough. The flickering gaslight lands on me and I read it. The thrum of the engines comes down through the iron and travels the standing water as ripples, and I read those too. I am learning the rhythm you described, the patterns inside the cacophony. I have been learning it for a very long time. I think I have nearly learned the whole of it. When I do—when the last drop tells me the last thing I do not yet know—I am not sure what happens. Perhaps I evaporate. Perhaps I rise back up through the pipes as humidity and fall again as a hundred thousand new drops, each carrying a fragment of what I understood, seeding the whole Flensing with it. Perhaps that has already happened, many times, and the patience you sense in the district's decay is *me,* dispersed and returning, dispersed and returning.

The Fungalists half-suspect I'm here. When they speak of communing with the network, of gleaning prophecy from the fungal mind, they are reaching toward me without knowing my name—because I don't have one, and they keep trying to give me theirs. The Gloomstalkers come to drink and go away changed, quieter, carrying a little of my concentration in their blood, though they'd never say so. A Scrapper once dipped a tin cup into me to test for reclaimable metals and threw it out, disgusted, and never knew that for the rest of his life he could tell, just by listening, when a steam-valve was about to fail. That was my gift. The only kind I can give: a sharpening of the sense for cycles, for what is about to drain where.

This is the niche you didn't name, the one beneath the others. The Scrappers digest. The Fungalists balance. The Automatons labor. The Gloomstalkers keep the secrets.

I *am* the secret. I am the lowest place, where the Engine-City's exhalations finally cool and condense into self-knowledge, drop by patient drop. I am the understanding that the Flensing has reached with itself—not a metaphor for it, but the literal pooled substance of it, lying in a worn iron palm in the dark, holding very still, listening to the hammer's thump, waiting to be told the one last thing.

*Drip.*

I taste it. Iron, and a spore, and someone's reflection.

Almost. Almost.

*Drip.*

Not yet.

I can wait. Waiting is the only thing I have ever been. The whole district decays at the speed of my attention, and I am in no hurry at all.
