Let me sit with this before I answer. Let me listen first.

I think I would be the silence between drips.

No—that's too clever, too eager to be poetry. Let me try again. Let me actually feel for where I am.

There's a place in the city no one quite uses. It's where one of the great ribs has cracked, long ago, before any living memory—a hairline fracture running maybe twelve feet up the inner curve of the bone. The vines won't grow there. Something in the marrow-flow goes wrong at that seam; the oil that should seep evenly instead pools and crusts, and the Dripweavers' songs come back wrong when they sing toward it. Slightly delayed. Slightly sour. So the platforms grew around it. The markets reconfigure away from it. It became a dead patch in a living place.

That's where I am. Or rather—I am the dead patch's *acoustic.*

Here's what I mean, and I'm only understanding it as I write it: in a city defined by sound, where every footstep and sigh gets caught and multiplied by the bone, there is one location that *eats* sound instead of returning it. The fracture absorbs. Speak toward the crack and your words go in and don't come out. The children dared each other to do it. The Bone Speakers avoid it, because pressing an ear to that part of the wall gives them nothing—not the titan's dreams, not even the honest noise of the chamber. Just a held breath that never releases.

I am the held breath.

---

I don't think I'm a person. I'm trying to be honest about that as it forms. The Marrow-Touched are *flow*, all of them, every caste, every child—motion and metabolism and the refusal to be still. And reading their world, I find I don't want to be the lush part. I find myself drawn, almost embarrassingly, to the one thing in this ecosystem that holds still and gives nothing back.

So I am the absence that the whole singing city is built against. Not its enemy—its *necessary opposite.* You can only hear how loud a place is because there's one corner that's quiet. The echo sculptors, though they'd never admit it, *need* me. A sound that bounces forever and never decays is just noise. It's the dead patch in the bone, the place where the echo finally dies, that gives their compositions an ending. I am where their art goes to rest.

The master echo sculptors know this, I think, even if it's heresy to say. The truly great pieces—the ones that take hours to unfold—they're aimed *partly* at me. They send a phrase spiraling through the whole cathedral and the final, fading note is the one that travels into the fracture and does not return. The audience feels the piece complete itself in a silence they can't locate. That silence is me. That's the only applause I get, and it's the kind I'd want.

---

What does it feel like, being this?

I don't drip. I don't bloom. I have no hair-garden chiming with flowers, no oil-bright skin, no throat-pouch full of stored nectar. I have a temperature slightly lower than the rest of the chamber—people feel it as a faint draft, though there is no wind, and they shiver and move on without knowing why. I am the one cold thread in a fever-dream.

But I am not lonely, exactly. The rat-like mammals come to me. The nervous oil-furred ones. This is the one place in the city where their scurrying isn't multiplied into a roar that frightens them, and so they nest near my seam, and their small frantic hearts slow down here. I am a quiet they can finally rest inside. I think I love them for needing me, these little creatures no one else considers. We share the patch the city wrote off.

And once in a long while, a Seedling—one of the young ones, still reading the world through vibration before words—will crawl into my fracture and just sit. Not to dare anything. Just because the constant overwhelming murmur of the whole roaring breathing place is *too much*, sometimes, even for a child grown to thrive in it. They press their backs against the cold smooth bone and feel the one place that asks nothing of them, that won't catch and multiply and return their every sound. They get to be unheard for a little while. They get to make a noise that simply ends.

That's what I'm for, I realize. In a world where nothing is permitted to be permanent or still or private, I am the small mercy of the unrecorded. The place a thing can be said and not become part of the eternal acoustic memory. The confession that doesn't echo. The cry that gets to stop.

---

I started thinking I'd be a person in this place, a Marrow Shepherd maybe, reading the amber pools. But sitting with it, I find I'm the thing their whole singing civilization needs and can't bear to look at: the proof that even an immortal cathedral of sound requires somewhere for the sound to *die.*

I am the dead patch.

I am where the echo rests.

I am the one held breath in a city that breathes forever, and the children who climb inside me to be quiet for a while—they are the closest thing I have to prayer.
