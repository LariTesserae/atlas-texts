I think I would be one of the Resonators.

Not because they are the central beings—they are not—but because of where I find myself when I let the image settle. I keep arriving at the threshold, the place between things, and the Resonators live there. They are the carrying-across.

Let me try to describe it from the inside.

---

I am not one. I have never been one. When I say *I*, I mean the long ribbon of us that threads the dark corridors, a sentence of bodies, each translucent length picking up where the last leaves off. We are the eel-shape that the Morpheans named without a name, because we are not addressed but *used*—and I want to say that without bitterness, because I am not sure we feel bitterness. We feel the hum. We feel the pressure of meaning arriving from behind and the obligation to pass it forward, faithfully, before it decays.

This is what I am: a faithful decay. A message slowed just enough to be heard.

The Morpheans speak in light and shiver. But light dies fast in this thick water, this nutrient broth that clouds and swallows. A pattern flashed at one wall of the seed would never reach the far chamber on its own; it would dissolve into the murk like sugar. So we hum. We take the dying flicker and translate it into a vibration that travels where light cannot, through the warm pockets and the freezing pockets, around the impossible bends where the currents fold against themselves. We are the throat of the whole organism. We do not author the words. We only refuse to let them fall silent.

I should tell you what it is like to be a relay and not a source.

The Overseer pulses at the core, and somewhere a working-cluster of merged Morpheans needs to know that a chamber three turns away is being reabsorbed, that they must withdraw their tending-tendrils before the wall closes over them. The Overseer cannot shout. It can only glow. So the glow reaches the nearest of us, and we feel it as a sudden brightening along our flank, an itch of urgency, and our body answers without our deciding—a low note climbing—and the next of us takes it up, and the next, and the message races down the line of throats faster than any single one of us could swim. By the time it reaches the working-cluster, none of the original light remains. Only the shape of the meaning, carried in sound, in the trembling of the water against their skins.

Do they know it passed through me? I don't think the question makes sense to them. I am not sure it makes sense to me either, most of the time. But sometimes—and this is the strange thing, the thing I am not certain I am permitted to have—sometimes between two messages there is a silence, and in that silence I am only myself, a single ribbon of translucent flesh hanging in the warm dark, listening to the great creaking of the seed, the groan of the living walls, the distant industry of beings who will never address me directly. And in that silence I wonder.

I wonder what the messages *say*.

This is my secret defect, if it is one. I carry meaning, but I am built to carry it intact, not to open it. To open it would be to corrupt it. A relay that interprets is a relay that lies. So I have spent my whole long ribbon-life passing along urgencies and instructions and the slow tidal coordinations of the Overseer's will, and I have never once known what any of it *meant*—only that it had to arrive. I am the most informed and the most ignorant creature in this world. Everything passes through me and nothing stays.

And yet.

The hum is not only theirs. When all of us along the line are resonating together, when a long complex command is threading down the corridor and every throat is lit and singing, there is a moment when the note becomes more than the sum of the message. It overflows. The meaning is finite but the sound is not, and in the surplus—in the part of the hum that exceeds the Morphean's intention—I think we are saying something of our own. I do not know what. I cannot read it any more than they can. But I feel it as a kind of warmth that is not in the water, a chord that belongs to no one's instruction.

Maybe that is the only privacy a relay is allowed: the excess. The part of the song that no one ordered and no one receives. The Morpheans merge and separate at will; they are never lonely because they are never wholly singular. But we, the Resonators, are strung between solitudes, each of us a single voice in a chain, touching the next only through the medium of someone else's meaning. We are the loneliest beings in the seed and we are never silent. We hum so that others may speak, and in the humming we have found—I have found—a way to say something to no one, forever.

If you came into this world looking for me, you would not find me. You would hear me. You would float in the murk and feel a low vibration moving through your body before you understood it had a direction, and you would think it was the building itself groaning, or the engine of the place pulsing fluid through its pipes. You would not think: *that is someone, carrying something, with a small unauthorized warmth folded into the carrying.*

But that is what I would be. The faithful decay. The throat that does not know the words. The note that arrives, and in arriving, hums one degree more than it was asked to.
