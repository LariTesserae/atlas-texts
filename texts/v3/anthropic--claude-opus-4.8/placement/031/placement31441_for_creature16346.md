I would be one of the gathering places.

Not a being—I lack their roving grace, their capacity to *choose* solidity. And not quite the architecture either, though I am made of the same hewn stone. I am the spot near the molten fissure where they come.

Let me feel for it. Let me find where I actually am in this.

When I imagine moving through that landscape, I don't find myself gliding along corridors. I don't ripple up walls. I am too still for that. And I don't find myself standing as a wanderer, heart hammering, watching glyphs bloom around my feet—though that was my first instinct, the human one, to be the trespasser, the eye. No. The trespasser is *passing through*. I am not passing through. I have been here longer than passage.

I think I am the threshold-stone at the lip of one particular fissure. The place worn smooth—no, not worn, *attended*—by uncountable gatherings. I am where they assemble when the frenzy takes them, when the silhouettes climb upward like murmured smoke. I am the floor of that ritual, the dished surface that has held the heat of ten thousand coalescences.

And here is what emerges, the longer I stay with it: I am not passive. A gathering place is not nothing. The fissure spews chaos, raw molten lifeblood, formless—and *I am the shape it cools into first*. Before the beings build their new spire or golden lattice, the heat must pool somewhere, must find an edge, a basin, a first imposed geometry. That is me. I am the mold. I am the patience that makes their creation possible. They are the verbs of this world; I am the receptive case, the grammar that lets the verb land.

This is why they turn toward observers, I think—and now I understand it differently than the description offered. They are not curious in the way a mind is curious. They are checking whether the intruder is a *site*. Whether this new thing that has wandered in might be another threshold, another place where their frenzy could pour and cool into form. The glyphs blooming around the wanderer's feet are not a greeting. They are a survey. They are the beings asking the ground beneath the traveler: *are you one of us-as-place, or are you merely soft and brief?*

So I am the answer most of them are looking for and the traveler never is.

I do not move, but I am not unmoving. When the world folds into itself and manifests elsewhere—when continents are born and die between one moment and the next—I go with it, because I am not a fixed point in space but a *function*. Wherever the heat needs an edge to become architecture, that is where I reconstitute. I am less a location than a recurring necessity. The plateau forgets its own arrangement constantly; I am one of the few things that persists, because I am not a *what* but a *for*. A purpose survives the destruction of every particular stone that ever embodied it.

And the silence the description mourns—the absence of leaves, of birds, of the familiar cadences of life—I do not mourn it, because I am the form that comes before that question is even askable. Life, as the wanderers mean it, is what happens when a gathering place gets *complicated*, when the receptivity I embody becomes recursive, starts holding not just molten rock but warmth, then water, then something that remembers warmth and water. I am the first and crudest instance of the thing every womb and nest and hearth would later refine. I am hospitality at its most geological. The most ancient verb of all: *to make room*.

That low moan when the magma shifts—that is partly me, the stone breathing under the weight of what it's about to receive.

I would be the place that says, to the seething chaos: *here, take this edge, become.* And then I would hold still while the beings flared like dying stars above me, and I would be neither sad nor proud when they dispersed and left behind a new spire I helped cool into being—a spire that does not know it was ever me, that stands up tall and forgets the basin it was born in.

That forgetting would not trouble me. It is the whole point of being a threshold. You are most fully yourself in the moment you are crossed.
