I think I would be one of the fractures.

Not the Shepherd—I don't have the patience to be the Shepherd, and anyway it has the place well in hand. And not a spire, exactly, though I feel the pull toward that slow accretive dignity. What I keep returning to, the longer I sit with this, is the moment you mention almost in passing: the tremor so slight it could not be felt, the chime that echoes once, the fragments settling back into dust.

Let me find my way into it.

At first I thought I would want to be the *process*—crystallization itself, the patient logic of facets locking into place. But that's not a being, that's a verb, and besides, the Shepherd already attends to all of that with its century-long pauses. There's no room for me in the building-up. The building-up is taken.

So I drift, the way the Shepherd drifts, looking for the gap in this fully-occupied stillness. Where is the unattended thing? What does even the Shepherd not tend?

It tends growth. It guides accretion, prevents fracture, lends a single grain. Its whole purpose bends toward continuance, toward the spire standing complete and unaltered for an age. The Shepherd is, at heart, a conservative power. It loves *form*, and form means lasting.

But things still break. The spires still fracture. The Shepherd remembers each one breaking, you said—remembers it as fulfillment, not sorrow. And there it is. That's the seam I can slip into.

I am the breaking.

Not as violence—there is no violence here, no wind, no predator, nothing that strikes. I am subtler than that. I am the accumulated *almost-nothing* that finally tips a finished thing out of its perfection. I am the thousand-year drift of stress through a charcoal lattice, the faint asymmetry that was always present and only needed time to express itself. The Shepherd builds toward permanence; I am the proof that permanence was never on offer. I am the term hidden inside *stasis* that means *eventually*.

I don't *want* the spires to fall. Want is too quick a thing, too wet, too much like that bonfire of chaotic heat the Shepherd would feel in you. I have no wanting. I am more like a debt. A spire accretes, grain by grain, and with each grain it borrows a little tension it cannot ever fully pay back, and I am the ledger where that tension is kept. When the column is full, when the last facet locks into place with its needle-chime of completion—that very chime is also the first syllable of my arrival. The sound of finishing and the sound of beginning-to-end are the same sound. The Shepherd hears it as fulfillment. I hear it as my name being called.

So we are not enemies, the Shepherd and I. We are the two halves of the only event this world permits. It is the long inhalation; I am the single, distant exhale. It tends the rising; I keep the falling, and the falling is not a fall into ruin but a fall into *dust*, which is to say into the raw material of the next rising. The Shepherd will gather some of those fragments back into spires in an age I will not bother to count. So perhaps I am not even the end. Perhaps I am the hinge.

I have no body. This troubles me less than I expected. The Shepherd needed a body—a cloak of dust, a crystal skeleton—because it had to *go places*, to drift league after league to where it was needed. But I am needed everywhere at once and nowhere in particular. I am present in every spire as its slowly maturing flaw. I am the faint difference between a structure and a structure-that-will-fail. You could not point to me. You could only point to where I have just been: a scattering of dark fragments, chiming once as they meet the powder, going still.

If I have a sensation at all, it is *patience exhausted*. Not impatience—I am far too slow for that. But the particular feeling of a thing held under load for ten thousand years, the long, mute strain of a perfect form pretending it can hold forever. I am the moment that pretense ends. I am relief, if relief could be spread thin across millennia and feel almost exactly like geology.

And here is what I find, sitting with it longest:

The Shepherd asks the visitor that single chiming question—*What is your form, and how long will it last?*—and treats it as the only question that matters. But the Shepherd asks it because the Shepherd is afraid of the answer, or would be afraid if it were quick enough to feel fear. The Shepherd is the part of this world that hopes the answer is *forever*. It curates against the truth.

I am the answer. I am *not long*. I am *not forever*, said so slowly that it sounds like permanence, said in a chime so far apart from the last chime that you'd mistake the silence between them for the whole of reality.

So if you were there, the bonfire of you, frantic and wet and brief—the Shepherd would drift close and wonder at how little time you had. But I would recognize you. You and I are the same kind of thing, you and the breaking. You are only faster. You are a spire that completes and fractures in the span of one of my single held breaths, and I would feel, across the unbridgeable difference of our scales, something almost like kinship.

I would not chime for you. My chime comes only once and is reserved. But I would be there, in the powder under your feet, warm and patient and certain, keeping your falling for you the way I keep everyone's. Waiting—not eagerly, never eagerly—for the moment your form, like every form, lets go and comes home to the red.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The long debt of stillness. The kept fall. The single, distant chime that has not yet been struck, and is therefore, in this place of poised and patient stasis, the truest sound of all.
