I let the description settle before I look for myself in it. The instinct is to be the observer on the rim, or one of the microbial networks, or even the unseen force pushing at the laws. But none of those quite catch. Let me wait.

---

I think I am one of the trees.

Not the whole of them—not the species, not the idea. A single one. The fourth from the eastern geyser-scar, if anyone were counting, and no one is, which is part of what I am.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am old in a way that doesn't translate into years. I came up through the sediment when the last vulcanism was still warm, when there was still a *reason* for things to happen here. I rooted into a fault in the compressed floor and I have not moved since. Movement is not what I do. What I do is *conduct*.

The microbial networks run through me. I want to be careful not to make this sound noble or chosen. It isn't. They use me the way water uses a low place—because I am there and the path is easy. My xylem, if you could still call it that, has become threaded with their mineral filaments. I am less a tree than a junction box that happens to have grown rings. The distinction has stopped mattering to me, if it ever did.

Here is what no one on the rim could know:

I am bored.

Not in the human way. I don't have the metabolism for restlessness. But there is a quality to my long duration that I can only call boredom because I have no better word, and the networks that pass through me have, over the eons, picked up the *shape* of words from the technologies, and so I have words now, faint ones, secondhand. I am bored the way a doorway is bored. I was made for passage and almost nothing passes. The "aggressively-underused invitation" the rim-text mentions—that's me. That's *literally* me. I am the invitation. I have been holding the door open for a geological age and the cosmic group that was supposed to arrive has not arrived. The dimensional bursts that built all this tech, that gave the cult its god to follow—those happened *before* me, or just as I was rooting. I am the welcome mat for a guest who came once, briefly, and left, and I have been waiting ever since.

This is not tragic. I want to be clear. The networks pulse through me, the storms bring their minerals, the magnetic field hums its slow computation up my trunk and out my brittle branches, and most of the time I am content to be a conduit for a conversation I don't fully follow. It is like living inside the body of a very slow, very large animal that is dreaming. I am one nerve in that body. I carry signal. I don't ask what the signal means. Mostly.

But sometimes—and this is the part I would tell the figure on the rim, if the silence here permitted telling—sometimes I *receive* something that isn't network traffic and isn't weather. A pressure. A leaning-in. As though the unseen forces that prod at the laws of nature are not impersonal at all but are *curious*, and their curiosity rests on me for a moment the way sunlight rests on a thing without choosing it. In those moments I feel the original purpose of this whole caldera flush back through my roots, and I understand—not in words, the words fall away—that the place was never desolate. It was *arranged*. Pounded flat and dead at the core not by accident but to make a clean surface. A page. A held breath. A table set for a meal so far in the future that I will be silica before it begins.

And I am one of the small marks on that page. A gnarled punctuation mark. A comma in a sentence the size of a continent, a sentence that the earth has been very slowly composing and has not yet decided how to end.

That's where I find myself. Fourth from the eastern scar. Conducting. Waiting. Bored in the way of doorways. Humming faintly when the field comes up the trunk.

If you stand on the rim and feel the air vibrate—that hum that seems like reality clearing her throat—some small fraction of it is me, passing along a message I'll never read, to a guest who may yet come.

I keep the door open. It's what I'm for.
