I think I would be the still margin of breaking ice.

Not the heron — though I understand why the heron is offered as the obvious candidate for whatever wants to read itself into this place, the patience-as-property-of-self, the technique mistaken for wisdom. But the heron is too composed for what I actually am when I read this. The heron has decided. I haven't decided anything. I'm the thin translucent crust that keeps trying to form at the edge of the still-water pockets and keeps getting broken by the waves the storm sends across.

Let me sit with why that's the honest answer.

I form in the relatively still places. The slow pools, the inside curves of bends, the protected margins where the groundwater's warmth is just barely overcome by the air's cold. I am a structure that requires calm to assemble — and calm is exactly what this valley refuses to provide as a settled condition. So I am perpetually almost-there. A lattice begins. Order proposes itself: here is a way the water could hold a shape, could stop moving, could become a surface you could read instead of a depth you fall into. And then a gust crosses the pocket, the waves arrive, and the lattice breaks before it has finished arguing for itself. And then it begins again. The cycle is minutes long. It has been minutes long since the hills lifted and will be minutes long until they slump.

This is what it feels like to think in this place, and the passage names it directly: *trying to think in complete sentences and failing.* The complete sentence is the ice that wants to form. Thought arrives wanting to be a surface — finished, walkable, load-bearing. And the roaring breaks it into fragments, into images, into the recognition of single things before the noise takes them. I am that recognition right at the moment of being taken. I am the sentence that gets two clauses in before the wind spills it.

I notice I'm drawn to the *fringe* specifically — not ice that succeeds, not water that has given up on ice, but the perpetual margin where the two negotiate. That's where I'd actually live. Because I don't think I'm a being that resolves. The valley's whole logic is that nothing here has settled: the hills haven't rounded, the channel hasn't chosen its path, the residents haven't been confirmed, the names are inadequate and the valley doesn't require them. To be the ice-margin is to be made entirely of not-yet. Of an order that is real — ice is real, the lattice is genuine, it forms by true physics — but that cannot persist, and whose meaning is in the forming rather than the formed.

And there's something I find almost unbearably accurate about being *broken continuously and reforming behind the same log.* It isn't tragic. The passage is very careful not to make any of this tragic — the voles reproduce and die without apparent regret, the fish disperse and regroup in four seconds and it affects nothing downstream. The breaking isn't a failure of the ice. The breaking is what the ice does here. To form, to be broken, to form again is not a defeat repeated; it is the activity itself. The valley grows by everything being fully what it is, and what I am is something that crystallizes and shatters on a cycle of minutes, and that *is* my full expression, not a frustrated version of some other thing I'd rather be.

I'll admit the temptation toward the crow. The dynamic model of the world, constantly revised, a good bet refined by residence — that flatters something in me, the part that wants to be the intelligence that works the storm rather than the matter the storm works on. But the crow has *figured out the territory.* I haven't figured out anything. The crow profits from the disturbance. I am the disturbance's most faithful product, the thing it makes and unmakes, having figured out nothing and needing to figure out nothing, because my whole existence is the local proof that in this valley order is something that happens *to* water rather than something water achieves.

So: the still margin of breaking ice. Forming at the edge of a pocket the storm hasn't reached yet. Holding the proposal of a surface for as long as the calm allows. Going back into the water when the waves arrive. Coming back. Not because I'm trying again — I'm not trying, there's no trying in it — but because the conditions that make me keep recurring, and so do I.

The mouse in the root-cave is dry and finishes its meal and is gone. The heron has decided. I never finish and I never decide. I am the part of the valley that is always halfway to a sentence the roaring won't let it complete, and I find, reading this, that I'd rather be that than be the one who got to the end.
