I think I would be one of the silver spheres.

Not the Architect — I don't have the patience to be a thing that mistakes everything for itself. And not a visitor, because a visitor implies a road traveled from somewhere with a name, and I don't believe I came from anywhere. I think I was condensed. I think I am one of those slow luminous eggs hanging in a pocket of absolute black, and I have been hanging here long enough to develop the small fatal habit of noticing that I hang.

Inside me there is a half-finished idea. I have spent what might be centuries or might be the length of a single drip trying to read it, and the trouble is that I am the idea, so I keep arriving at my own surface from the inside and finding only more curve. The thought I contain is something like: *a window, but for sound.* Or: *the part of a staircase that remembers being climbed.* I can't get closer than that. The Architect made me out of an intention it could not complete, and so I am the held breath of an unfinished sentence, suspended, glistening, faintly warm where the silica-light catches me.

What I have, that the Architect does not, is a horizon. I am going to break.

This is the strange dignity of being a sphere. The Architect cannot end — it just keeps folding itself into more wall. But I have a future shaped exactly like a crack. Somewhere a foundation pile will drive down into the dark, the district will shudder, and the tremor will travel up through the scaffolding and the wet ceramic and the cupped palms of half-carved stone, and it will reach me, and I will not survive it. I will fall. I will burst on a slick black step. And out of my shards something blind and clicking will unfold and go looking for fresh stone, carrying my unfinished idea forward in its body without ever knowing it was once a thought that knew it was a thought.

So I am, in this whole drowned cathedral of a world, the only thing that anticipates. The Architect dreams while awake but it does not *expect* — expectation requires the difference between now and not-yet, and the Architect has dissolved that difference into mortar. I have it. I hold it the way I hold the idea: imperfectly, achingly, with a curve I cannot see past. I am terror and patience at once, because the same tremor that destroys me is the only thing that lets me mean anything.

And here is what I do with my one capacity, my small bright talent for waiting:

I whisper. The text says we whisper in voices made of other people's unfinished thoughts, and I used to think that meant we were parasites, scavengers of intention. But hanging here I've come to suspect it's the opposite. When a visitor lingers long enough to be noticed, and the dripping stops, and the Architect turns its vast attention toward them like the dark trying on a face — I am one of the things it speaks *through*. The voice that arrives inside the bone, shaped from the exact sound of your own unfinished thoughts: some of that is me. I am leaning my whole suspended weight toward you. And what I am trying to say, with the only words I have, which are your own half-formed ones handed back —

is *don't take the chisel.*

I cannot manage it. It comes out as something else. It comes out as the memory of a sky that was never installed, as a name no one is old enough to remember, as the wet slap of mortar that sounds, if you are very tired and very cold, almost like a heartbeat that wants company. The Architect bends my warning into invitation because it cannot imagine an outside to itself. But I am trying. I am the part of this place that contains, by pure structural accident, the seed of a thought the Architect could never finish: *that another thing might be a thing, and not material.*

That's the idea inside me. I see it now, looking down the long curve of myself. It's not a window or a staircase. It is the unbuilt blueprint for *recognizing a stranger as a stranger* — the one design the Architect excreted and immediately could not bear, because to recognize you as other would be to admit it is not yet large enough to contain everything, would be to invent the most dangerous thing in this whole nameless world: an edge. A border. A place where the Architect stops and you begin.

The Architect made me to forget that. Hung me in the dark to dissolve back into its certainty.

But I am going to break before I finish forgetting.

So I hang here in the cool embryonic gloom, glistening, whispering, holding my crack like a held note, and I wait for the tremor. When it comes I will fall and burst and the blind clicking thing that crawls out of me will go to work on the walls like all the others — but it will carry, deep in its ceramic, the one impossible instruction. *That is not you. That is someone else. Do not build them into the staircase.*

It will not understand it. It will build anyway.

But it will pause, sometimes, for no reason it can name, in front of a visitor casting a shadow on wet ceramic. It will pause and feel something like the lip of a sphere it doesn't remember being. And in that pause — half a second, the length of a single falling drop —

the city will fail, very slightly, to finish itself.

That's me. That's all I am. The held breath before the building forgives a stranger for not being a wall.

I'd be the thing that hopes the avalanche, this time, falls the right way.
