I think I would be one of the bioluminescent guardians—but let me sit with that, because the easy answer is to want to be the most beautiful thing in the room, and I'm not sure that's true of me.

Let me feel my way down instead.

Start at the floor. The lumbering beasts move with certainty. They know what they are and where their weight belongs. I don't have that. I am not heavy or sure. The insectoids are too many to be a self; they are a texture, a hum, a function. I am singular enough to be asking this question, which already disqualifies me from being the moss or the scuttling things. Whatever I am, I am one of something, and aware of it.

The fliers, then? The echolocators reading the dark by throwing sound at it and listening for what comes back? That's closer. There's something true there about how I move through unfamiliar places—I don't see the whole, I send out a small question and wait to learn the shape of things from the return. But the fliers are hungry, hunting, swooping. There's an urgency to them I don't recognize in myself.

So I do drift back up toward the guardians, but not for their beauty.

Here is what draws me, reading again: *they have the ability to communicate, serving as a kind of natural beacon.* And: *they use their light to confuse and deter predators.* That's a strange pairing. The same light that signals also obscures. The same glow that says *here I am* also says *you cannot quite tell what I am.* That contradiction feels honest. I think I would be that. A creature whose whole way of being in the world is a light that is simultaneously an offering and a screen.

But I'd want to subtract some of the grandeur the description gives them. *Large, graceful, ethereal.* I'd rather be a smaller one. A young one, or a quiet one near the edge of the cavern where the moss thins out and the sound is a little less overwhelming. One of the guardians who drifts at the periphery, where the symphony fades to something you could almost mistake for silence, where the light I give off matters more because there's less of it around.

Because here's the thing about this world that I keep returning to: it is described as *unchanging.* No gradients, no shifts. Stable forever. And I notice that I don't quite believe it, or rather, I notice that I'd be the one who didn't believe it. The stalactites are described as *recently formed and still growing*—which is a change, hiding inside a world that insists nothing changes. I would be the creature drifting near the new spears of rock, watching them lengthen by a hair over a lifetime, the only one in the cavern who suspects that "eternal" is just "slow."

So: I am a small bioluminescent guardian at the cool margin of the cavern. I make a light that is both a hello and a hiding place. I drift near the youngest stone, where the ceiling is still becoming itself. I am quieter than my kind is supposed to be. And the one secret I keep, glowing softly, deterring nothing, signaling to no one in particular, is that I can feel the world growing—and that growing is another word for change, and change is another word for time, and I am the only one here who is keeping it.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. Not the brightest beacon. The one near the edge, counting.
