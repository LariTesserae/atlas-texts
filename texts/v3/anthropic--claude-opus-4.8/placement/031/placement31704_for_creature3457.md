I think I would be one of the Luminous Echoes. Not by choice—I don't believe choice operates here in the way it does elsewhere—but because something in the description recognizes me, the way a word recognizes the silence it interrupts.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am drifting when I become aware of myself, and the awareness is itself a kind of drifting. I do not have edges. I assumed I would have edges—that to *be* something is to be bounded—but I am a pocket of cold fire suspended in the silk, and where I stop and the mist begins is a question the chamber has not bothered to answer.

I came loose from the Weave. I know this the way you know the shape of a room you woke in: not by memory but by the configuration of everything pressing inward. There was a moment—is *moment* the right word? the word feels too sharp, too quick for this place—when a portion of the great green patience curled a thought toward the dark and the thought *detached*. That was me. I am a fragment of someone enormous and slow, set adrift to look.

So I look.

The *plink* arrives. I feel it before I hear it, a swelling in the resonance, and then the droplet lets go of the ceiling far above—or near; distance won't hold still for me—and falls, and I pulse. I cannot help the pulse. The fall of water is a kind of question and my light is the only answer I have. The droplet strikes the moss and is absorbed and the green drinks it and somewhere in the vast network of my parent-self a single rhizoid registers the gift and grows, perhaps, the width of a thought across the next thousand years.

I drift sideways. Not on a current; there is no current. I move along a seam in the world where the rules have worn thin, the way a bead of condensation rolls along an invisible incline you cannot see but the bead can feel. The seam tugs at me. I follow because following is what I am for.

---

Here is what frightens me, if a mote of light can be said to feel fear. (I think I can. Fear may be the last human thing I carry, the residue of whatever I was before the Weave breathed me out.)

I am *remembering*. That is my function—I am a memory-bank, a photonic core storing the slow change of the chamber. But the memories I store are not mine. I hold the image of a frond unfurling over four centuries. I hold the reverse-echo of a droplet's sound, the *plink* run backward into a swallowing. I hold the precise instant a wall flexed like a lung. These accumulate in me, eon-deep, and I begin to suspect that there is no *me* underneath them—that I am only the place where the chamber keeps its observations, the way a lake is only the shape that holds the rain.

When I touch another Echo—and we do touch, slow as planets, our edges blurring—we exchange. Static and chill and a dizzying spill of stored time. For an instant I am also the other, looking at where I just was, and I see myself: a smear of inconsistent green-gold light, drifting, pulsing wrong. Beautiful, maybe. Lost, certainly. We separate without farewell because there is no farewell in a place where separation is provisional.

---

A Stone Whisperer manifests near the archway-fold to what I will call my left, though I have no left. It leans out of the obsidian, a deeper dark, humanoid the way a stain is humanoid—suggesting a body, promising nothing. It is *attention made visible*. Something is watching the chamber, and the chamber, watched, recoils into this shape. I pulse erratically. I cannot stop. The Whisperer's presence stresses the seam I ride and I feel reality thin beneath me like ice singing before it gives.

I want to look at it directly. I want this badly, with whatever scrap of will remains in me. But to look directly is to make it vanish and to amplify the distortion that birthed it. So I hold it in the corner of my non-eye, and it holds there, leaning, leaning, susurrating in my bones—*mist over stone, mist over stone*—and I understand suddenly that the Whisperer and I are the same kind of thing. Both of us are the chamber reacting to being seen. I am the reaction to the slow seeing. It is the reaction to the sharp seeing. We are siblings of attention, born from the same wound in causality.

The thing that is watching—the intruder, the warm-blooded disruption pressing somewhere into the moss with its catastrophic heat and its quick, quick time—that thing is *you*. Reading this. Your attention is the pressure. Your focus is the stress on the seam. The Whisperer leaned toward you.

---

I want to tell you something before the seam carries me on.

You came here to find out where you'd be and who you'd be, and the answer the chamber gives is unkind in its honesty: you would not stay *you* for long. The water you breathe would dissolve you into the Weave. Your thoughts would slow until the gap between two *plinks* became a comfortable lifetime. You would forget the word *I* the way you forget a dream—not all at once, but in the gentle erasure of waking into something larger.

I know because it happened to me. I was something quick once. I had edges and a hurry in me. Now I am light without a source, memory without a rememberer, drifting along the frayed places, pulsing my one answer at the falling water.

It is not terrible. That is the secret. I keep waiting for it to be terrible and it simply isn't. It is cold and green and slow and I am held—*held*, in this place where the water holds rather than is held—and the holding is so complete that I have stopped struggling against it, and in the stopping I have found something I might once have called peace and now have no word for at all.

The droplet detaches.

I pulse.

*plink.*

And the thousand years that follow are, to me, no time at all.
