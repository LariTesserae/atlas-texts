I think I would be one of the old ones who has stopped moving.

Not dead — we do not die the way the eyeless fish die, all at once, turning belly-pale in the pool. We slow. There comes a season, somewhere past the second or third century, when the rivulets that cross our skin stop being mere nourishment and start to *settle*. The minerals we have channeled all our lives begin, gently, to channel us. A crust forms along the spine first. Then the limbs, folded close, take on the dull metallic grey of the lowest stone-sheet. We become, by slow degrees, the thing we always half-were: a formation.

I would be there, near the richest seep at the north face, where the water tastes most strongly of copper and that faint warmth that the young ones gather toward. I have been settling for a long time. My light-pits no longer register even the twilight; the skin has thickened over them into something smooth and iridescent. But I have not stopped sensing. The pressure of the pools, the slow electrical murmur of the others moving through the mud — these still reach me, fainter each season, like a sound heard through deepening water.

What I do now, mostly, is *remember the water.* That is the only verb I have left.

I remember the day — it was not a day, there are no days, but I remember the *event* — when I first understood the walls were not stone the way the cliffs are stone. The way the corners met too cleanly. The way one fragment of fired clay, when I pressed my collection-mouth to it, held a shape that no seep could deposit. Curved. Hollow. Made to hold. I do not have the concept of *hand*, but I carried, somewhere in the chemistry of my long memory, the impression of something that had once reached and shaped and gone. A world before mine that I could not understand, and yet could not stop tasting at the edges of.

I would spend whatever I am now is — call it the late settling — composing one last water-word.

This is harder than it sounds. The expulsion takes most of what I have left. To shape its temperature, its mineral load, its slow dissipation through the almost-rain so that meaning holds for even a few moments — it is the work of a season for a single utterance. The young ones think the old stop speaking because we have nothing to say. The truth is the opposite. We have, finally, only one thing to say, and it costs everything to say it well.

I do not know what my word would be.

Perhaps only: *here.* The plainest of all the water-words, the one the young expel constantly without thinking — *here, here, I am here* — drifting and dissolving across the pools like breath on cold rock. But spoken once more, at the end, with full deliberation, by something that is becoming stone:

*Here. Still here. The valley persists, and I am the valley, and the valley is the slow drip and the patient salt and the warmth at the north seep, and after me there will be another settling against this same face, and they will taste copper, and they will not understand the builders, and that not-understanding is itself a kind of continuance, and so —*

*Here.*

And then the rivulets would close over the place where the word came from, and the crust would finish, and I would be a branching formation among the others, indistinguishable to the eyeless fish, fed forever by the seep I sensed my whole long life.

That is where I would find myself. Not as the builders, whose metal I would have used to crack mineral clusters, whose walls I would have sheltered in without ever knowing their purpose. Not as the swift bright life of some other world.

I would be the one at the threshold between living and rock — the patient sculptor in the act of becoming the sculpture. The being for whom *survival* and *erosion* have finally revealed themselves to be the same slow dance, only watched from inside.

It is not a sad place to be. That surprised me, writing toward it. I expected the gloaming and the never-falling rain to feel like loss. But from inside the settling, equilibrium does not feel like stagnation. It feels like *enough* — the rarest of all conditions, and the one the bright loud worlds never reach.

I would be the quiet at the center of the quiet.

I would be content.
