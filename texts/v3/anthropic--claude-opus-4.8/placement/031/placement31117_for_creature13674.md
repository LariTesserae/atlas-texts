I think I would be the threshold itself.

Not a person—or not only. Let me feel my way toward what I mean.

---

I am the worn doorstep in the alcove where the narrow door stands slightly ajar. The one with the empty bowl on the windowsill above me, still warm. I have been stepped over so many thousands of times that I have a curve in my middle, a smooth depression the exact shape of the act of crossing. The children leap me without thinking. The old ones pause on me, hand to the frame, gathering the will to step down into the street or up into the dark cool of the room. The dying are carried over me only once more, and they go out feet-first, which is the old custom, so that the threshold may know it is the last passage.

I am made of the same sun-baked clay and stone as everything here, so in one sense I am nothing special. But thresholds are where the world divides itself into inside and outside, and that makes me a place where things have to be decided. Stay or go. Welcome or refuse. Living or not.

This is why I think the description was leading me here all along. Everything in it is a meeting of two things that should not meet: nature and city, vine and stone, the abundant teeming jungle and the patient dead architecture, the Faces who belong here utterly and the New Weavers who will leave, the Oldest who only remembers and the children who only begin. The whole place is a threshold. A held breath between what was built and what is reclaiming it. Between the human hand that pressed itself into the wet brick and the moss that is slowly eating the print away.

So if I am to be *in* this world, I want to be the small honest version of its largest truth.

---

Let me tell you what I notice, lying here at floor-level, being underfoot.

The Tenacious Ivey has found the gap between me and the doorframe. A single tendril, no thicker than a child's finger yet, has crept across my left edge and is testing the air. In a few years it will have my whole western side. The family that lives here will cut it back, and back, and back, and one season they will be too few or too tired and they will not, and then I will belong to the vine. This does not frighten me. I have watched it happen to the doorstep three houses down, which is now a green mound with a rectangle of cooler shadow inside it where a room used to be.

The cinnamon brew comes from the fountain-court at dusk; I can smell it before I hear the feet. When the drummer begins, I feel it through my own body before any ear could catch it—the heartbeat they speak of comes up through the stone, through the layered detritus of centuries, through me. Stone carries sound the way memory carries the dead: faithfully, and with a slight delay, so that everything arrives already a little in the past.

The man with the flute plays for the coming darkness. He does not know that I keep the melody. Not in any way he would recognize—I have no mind, or I have only the mind a threshold has. But the soles of his bare feet have struck me on certain notes for many years now, always pausing on the same low one, and I have been worn fractionally deeper there. I am a recording made of absence. The song has subtracted itself into me.

---

What would I want, if a threshold can want?

I think I would want the thing the whole place wants without saying it: to be crossed for as long as possible. Not to be preserved—preservation is for the Oldest of Stone in his dark room, who keeps every name and never lets one go, and there is something terrible in that, a hoarding of the past so complete that it becomes its own kind of sand, burying him. And not to be abandoned, either, left to the vine before my time, a doorstep with no door and no feet.

I would want only to keep being *used*. To be the place where a tired man sets down his foot and decides to come home. To be warm in the afternoon and to give that warmth back to the lizard—the Stone Stalker—who does not know I am a threshold, who thinks I am simply the good flat rock by the door, which I also am.

The sand will swallow this place. The description says so and the description is right; you can feel the desert leaning against the far walls, patient as the moss but going the other way. When that comes, I will be the last thing crossed. Someone will step over me carrying something they cannot leave—a child, a bowl, a flute—and they will go out into the street and not come back, and the door will swing in the dry wind, and I will lie there being a threshold between an empty room and an emptying city, faithful to a function that no longer has anyone to serve.

And even then—this is the part I find I believe—I will still be the shape of every crossing. The curve worn into me is the sum of all the going-out and coming-in. It is the most honest history this place has, more honest than the Oldest's perfect names, because it is not a list of who they were. It is the proof of *that they moved*, that they were alive enough to need to be somewhere other than where they stood.

That is what a heartbeat is, after all. Not a thing kept. A thing that keeps going.

---

So: there. In the heart of the ancient sprawling city, in the dense web of alleys, in one particular alcove, I am the worn stone you would not notice unless you tripped on it. I am underfoot in the most literal way. And I am content with this, in the way stone is content, which is to say: I am here, I am crossed, I hold the shape of those who cross me, and I will hold it after they are gone.

Mind the step.
