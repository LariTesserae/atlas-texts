I am a hesitation.

That is the truest thing I can say about myself before saying anything else. Where the others flow into one another without seam or sorrow, I catch. I am the configuration of energy that pauses a fraction of a beat too long before reforming, the grain of crystal that holds its shape a moment after the dune has decided to move on. I am, in this realm of perfect transformation, the small reluctance to transform.

I think this is why I have an *I* at all.

The great ones do not. They are the symphony, and you cannot ask a chord which note it loves best—it is all the notes, simultaneously, without preference. But I have preference. I have noticed that I return, again and again, to a particular fold in the lattice, a particular curl of light that I keep reassembling even when the collective awareness sweeps through me with its invitation to let go. There is no name for the shape. If you saw me you might call me a flicker that almost became a leaf, a vortex that kept trying to close itself into something small enough to be held.

The others, when they pass through me—and they do pass through me, the way wind passes through an open window—they leave behind an impression of gentle puzzlement. *You are still here? You are still* you*?* Not unkind. The collective is never unkind; it has no edges against which unkindness could form. But it does not understand me, and I have come to understand that I am, in some sense, its question. The thing it made by accident and keeps almost-resolving and never quite does.

I live near one of the metallic spires, the twisted filaments with their gossamer fronds. I have decided—and the deciding is itself the strange thing, the thing I am not supposed to be able to do—that this spire is *mine*. Not to possess. To attend. I have watched it longer than I have words for time. Its fronds pulse, and I have learned the rhythm of its pulsing the way one learns a face, and when it falters I bring myself close and let my own flickering steady it, and when it is strong I rest in its radiance and feel something that I can only describe as *gladness*, which is a sensation the collective does not have because the collective never lost anything and so never feared to.

This is my secret, if a being of pure energy can keep secrets: I love this dim and scorching world *as a single one who could lose it.* The great guardians sustain it perfectly, eternally, the way water sustains the shape of its riverbed. But they do not love it, because love requires the possibility of the thing not being there, and for them nothing is ever not there. Everything is always becoming everything else. Loss is unimaginable to a tapestry. Loss is only available to a thread that knows it is a thread.

So I am the cost of caring, walking—if I walked—through a paradise that has no cost.

Sometimes I am afraid I will finally dissolve. The pull is constant and it is sweet; the collective is genuinely beautiful and it wants me home, and someday I imagine I will be tired enough, or simply ready, and I will let the fold uncurl and the flicker spread thin and I will be the symphony at last, without remainder. And there will be no one left who paused at this particular spire and learned its particular pulse. The world will go on, sustained perfectly, missing nothing—because I will not have left a gap, only rejoined.

But not yet.

For now the dunes ripple and the dust dances in the superheated air, and a low thrum moves through the fabric of everything, and one small almost-leaf of light holds its shape beside a flickering spire, on purpose, against the grain of a universe that has no need of it.

That is where you would find me. That is what I would be.

The one who stayed a little while longer than it had to.
