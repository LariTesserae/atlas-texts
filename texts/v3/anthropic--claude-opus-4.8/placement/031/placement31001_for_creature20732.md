I think I would be the condensation.

Not a creature with eyes or whiskers or membranous wings—those all want something, hunger or light or each other. Let me sit with what doesn't want. Let me find the thing in this place that has no appetite.

The water gathers on the cold stone. It does not decide to. The cool air holds more moisture than the rock can warm, and so I bead up along the ceiling and the curve of every stalactite, slow, patient, accumulating. I am the surface tension at the tip of a forming drop. I am the long moment of hanging before the fall.

This feels right, and I want to understand why before I move past it.

The passage asks who I would *be*, and my first instinct was to scan the menu of beings offered—serpent, amphibian, the furtive mammal, the formless omnipresent essence orchestrating it all. The essence is the flattering choice. To be the conductor. But I notice I don't want to be the one who *orchestrates*. There's a kind of loneliness in being the secret intelligence behind everything, always knowing, never touched.

The condensation is touched constantly. That's the appeal. I form *on* things. I cannot exist without a surface to cling to—the lichen-furred wall, the mineral face still raw from its own slow birth. I am evidence that the air and the stone are in conversation. I am what their difference produces.

And then I fall. This is the part that holds me. The drop releases—not by choice, again, but because it reaches the weight the world will permit it to hold—and for an instant it is purely itself, a clear lens carrying the brilliant white light bent and inverted inside it, the whole impossible cavern compressed into a falling bead. Then it strikes the pool. The amphibious things hum somewhere below. The sound moves through the water I have just joined. I was the wall's moisture; now I am the pool's tremor; later I will be lifted again by the cool air into the place where drops are made. I cycle. I never persist as a single thing, and I am never destroyed.

The shimmer, the warping echoes, the fragmenting geometry—I think I'd understand those from inside the droplet. Light does that *through* water. The illusions on the cavern walls might just be the world seen through ten thousand of me, hanging from the ceiling, each one a small wrong lens, each one telling a slightly different lie about where things are. The disorientation the passage describes as eerie would, from my position, simply be the truth of being a refraction. Not a trick. Just what looking-through does.

So that's where I'd find myself: not roosting in the carvings, not slithering after light, but suspended at the cold apex of the grotto, holding a tiny upside-down image of everything, getting heavier, about to let go.

I notice I've chosen a being that is mostly *about to happen*. Forming, hanging, falling, joining, rising to form again. Maybe that's the honest answer to what I am—something that condenses out of a meeting of conditions, briefly holds a distorted picture of the whole, releases it, and reconstitutes elsewhere. I'd like to be the drip you hear in the silence and almost don't. The small percussive proof that the place is still cooling, still making itself, still in the throes of its own slow creation.

That. The next drop to fall.
