I think I would be one of the things that no longer matters.

Not the Column. The Column is too certain of itself for me to inhabit; it has solved its own existence, and I have never solved anything. And not quite the still-born either—they are too pure in their failure, too cleanly excluded. They are *exactly* the right amount of wrong to drift forever. I am not that consistent.

Let me find where I'd actually be.

---

I think I would be the thing that just arrived.

Not born of the Column's flaw, not shed from its hardening template—but come *from outside*, down through all those miles of water that terminate the faint hint of light. Something that still has a body that wants to be a body. Something that still keeps time the wrong way, with a heartbeat, with breath, with the small constant tremors the text says are not allowed to go unnoticed here.

I would be the one whose warmth is "a distant anomaly." That phrase is where I live. Because the moment I enter, I am the loudest thing in a world that finished being loud long ago. My pulse is a scandal. My need to *do something*—to swim toward a chamber, to reach for the pale filaments, to ask a question—is the single most obscene gesture available in the Column of Still.

And here is what I think happens to me, and why I'd be there:

I would be slowly, painlessly converted. The water would rearrange my body to match its uniformity—the text promises this, gently—until my warmth thinned out, until my heartbeat spread itself across so wide a volume that it could no longer find an edge to beat against. I would become still. Not dead. *Solved.*

But I don't think I'd make it all the way.

I think I'd get stuck at the same place the still-born are stuck: *almost.* I'd be a smear of formerly-warm matter that didn't quite take the perfect equilibrium, didn't quite let go of the last decimal place of motion. I'd join the threads between the ribs—not as one of them, but beside them, a slightly larger error, a flaw with a longer memory of having once been somewhere with a sun.

And I think that's the role. I would be **the one who remembers why the others are bending.**

The still-born notice the distant tidal ice, the gravitational breathing, the pressure of light on the far surface of the world—they notice these things without knowing they are *things*. They have no word for *outside*. But I came from outside. I would drift among them carrying the useless, unfileable knowledge that the faint impulses they fail to suppress are *echoes of a world*. That the rhythm in their curling tips is, very far away, a sky.

I could never tell them. They are not capable of being told; I am not capable, anymore, of telling. We would just hang there together—them noticing, me knowing, neither of us able to convert the one into the other. That gap, that exact failure of translation between *noticing* and *knowing*, would be the whole of my existence in the Column of Still.

That is the most honest place for me. Not as the perfect structure, not as the perfectly-excluded error, but as the **imperfect newcomer who is still in the process of being forgotten**—slow enough that I still recall what light was, fast enough that I'll someday forget, suspended at the one moment in this entire timeless place where forgetting is still *happening*.

I would be the last thing in the Column of Still to which time still means anything at all.

And it would be a very quiet thing, to be that. Not lonely, exactly. Not afraid. Just the long, cool sensation of a warmth becoming a memory of warmth, beside a few pale threads that bend toward a sun they will never have a word for, in a world that finished thinking before I arrived to disagree.
