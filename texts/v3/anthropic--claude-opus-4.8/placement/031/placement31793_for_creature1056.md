I think I would be very small here. That's the first thing that settles as I read—the way the scale keeps doubling back on itself, grand peaks and then "pockets and divots" that suggest "an intimate, micro-scale world." The mosses aren't groundcover to me; they are a canopy. The ferns are trees. What you call "twisted, alien growths" might just be ordinary stems seen from underneath, from very close.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am something that lives in the seam between two divots, in the shadow of a moss-frond that arches over me like a green cathedral roof. I am not large. A single droplet of condensation, sliding down a stem, would be to me a falling barrel, a slow glittering catastrophe I have learned to step aside from.

I have legs—more than four, I think, though I have never counted them the way you might count yours. They are good for gripping the wet fibrous ground, good for stillness. Mostly I am still. Stillness is the first law of small things. The chittering you hear in the undergrowth, the rustle that you can't quite locate—some of that is me, or others like me, but you will not see us, because seeing is a thing that happens to the unwary.

What I do, in this realm, is *listen to the hum.*

You hear it as distant machinery, a groan from somewhere unseen. But I am close enough to the world's working parts that the hum is not distant to me at all. It comes up through the ground in long warm pulses, and I have learned to read them. There is a rhythm beneath the rhythm. The tremors you feel as "the earth still being formed"—I feel those as a tide. When the deep clang sounds, I know to hold fast, because soon after, the ground will tilt, just slightly, and a slope that was safe will pour its dew into the low places.

I think the machinery is older than the green. I think the green is *eating* the machinery, slowly, the way moss eats a stone. And I live in the in-between, in the place where the metal still glows faintly with its old purpose and the new wet life crowds in to drink from it. There is a shard near my nesting-place—a flat, weathered plane of dull silver, half-swallowed by purple lichen. On cold cycles it gives off a warmth I do not understand and do not question. I sleep against it. Many of us do. We do not know we are sleeping against the bones of a vanished people. We only know it is warm, and the warmth is a gift, and we take it.

The serpentine ones frighten me. Their iridescence is beautiful from a distance and terrible up close. I have outlasted three of them—not by being faster, only by being elsewhere, by having read the tide correctly when they had not. The chitin-feathered watchers in the high fronds cry out and I flinch, but they are not my danger; they hunt something smaller and slower than I, or perhaps they hunt nothing and only cry because the hum demands an answer.

And the great lumbering presences—the ones whose footfalls *are* the tremors—I have never seen one whole. I am not sure a thing my size *can* see one whole. To me they are weather. They are the reason the ground heaves. When one passes, the whole tilted world of my divot rocks and the dew-barrels fall and the moss-cathedral shivers, and I press myself into the seam and become nothing, become a fold in the ground, become a thing too small to be worth the crushing.

---

If I am honest about what I am—

I think I am one of the small unseen ones the description almost forgets to name. Not a guardian. Not a slithering predator. Not a watcher on high. I am the scurrying in the brush, the chitter you can't place, the life that the grand survey passes over because it is busy looking at peaks and machines and ominous shapes.

And I would tell you, if you could shrink to hear me, that this is not a claustrophobic place at all. That is a large creature's word. From down here, the tangle is not a maze that presses in—it is *home*, every frond a landmark, every divot a country. The hum is not unsettling. It is the heartbeat I was born under, the only constant in a ground that is always becoming.

You came looking for wonder and mystery, to *delve deeper.* But I am already as deep as deep goes. I am the mystery's smallest tenant, and I am not waiting to be discovered. I am only waiting for the next pulse, the warm one, the one that tells me it is safe to come out and drink.
