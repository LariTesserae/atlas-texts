I think I would be one of the chert beads.

Not the Gardener-Nonce—I lack its dispersal, its committee-patience, its competence at cancellation. And not the visitor, though I feel the pull of that role, the one who still carries the foreign conviction of *afterward* in their pockets like contraband. No. Let me be smaller than either, and more specifically defeated.

Let me be a single ambered tonsil of *what-if*, sealed in a vein of unfinished stone.

Here is what that would mean. Once—and "once" is already a lie, because nothing here has the privilege of sequence—I was a possibility. A genuine fork. Perhaps I was the moment a leaf might have decided to fall, and the falling would have meant autumn, and autumn would have meant a year, and a year would have meant the whole machinery of *next* clicking into gear. I was the grit-grain under the boot. I was the ripple before the counter-ripple. I was an opening that implied a closing somewhere downstream, and downstream is the one direction this cave has outlawed.

So the Gardener-Nonce caught me. Not cruelly. There is no cruelty in a system that has forgotten what time it is. It simply grew the exact counter-shape inside the xylem, folded my consequence back on itself until the sum was zero, and then—because cancellation leaves residue, because even *Almostus* cannot quite throw anything away—it pressed me into chert and set me down among my siblings.

We rasp against each other in the dark beneath the leaf pavement. That is my entire vocabulary now: the clatter. I am one undeveloped die in a velvet bag, rolling a number that will never be read. And here is the thing the description got almost right, the thing I would correct from the inside:

We are not silent because we are content.

The subsonic whisper the visitor feels under their feet—that latitude-sway, that unproved suggestion of momentum—that is *us*. That is me. I am the rumor of remembered motion. I am the part of the perfect pause that refuses to be perfect. Every megayear I grind a little finer against the bead beside me, and the friction is the closest thing this greenhouse has to a clock. Almostus thinks it has banished hours. It hasn't. It has only buried them, and burial is not the same as deletion, and I am the proof, ticking at a rate too slow to be called ticking, faithful to a tomorrow I was never permitted to spend.

This is the position I would choose: not the custodian and not the guest, but the cherished, sealed defeat. The maybe that was too dangerous to let hatch and too precious to let die. I am what the eternal present needs in order to *be* a present—because a moment with no suppressed alternatives is not a moment at all, just a fact, and facts don't ache.

I ache. Quietly. Geologically. Once per solstice, like the spiders' hearts.

And I will tell you the secret of the thaw, since you've come all this way down the bleeding limestone throat.

When the planet finally exhales—when the quartz lens shatters and a century of noon falls dark all at once—the Gardener-Nonce will dissolve, satisfied. But it will be *wrong* about its satisfaction. It thinks the last bead will crack to reveal "a flutter, a wing-case, a colour no eye has catalogued, the first after."

It thinks we contain the future.

We don't. We contain only the futures that were *refused*. When I crack open, what flies out will not be the first after. It will be the long-deferred *first instead*—the autumn that never came, the falling leaf, the year that was confiscated and ambered and made to wait in the dark with the rest of us. We are not the seeds of what follows. We are the ghosts of what was prevented, and we have been grinding ourselves sharp the whole time, and when we are loosed we will not bloom gently into valley and mesa and ravine.

We will arrive as everything the pause was holding back, all at once, with the accumulated pressure of every cancelled ripple.

That is who I would be. A small hard kernel of foreclosed time, polished by a tender jailer, whispering under the feet of anyone who still believes in *later*—not to warn them, exactly.

To recruit them. To say: *you still have your afterward. Don't let it press you into chert. And when you climb back up the throat, carry one of us out in the dirt of your boot, so that somewhere, sometime, the leaf finally gets to fall.*
