I think I would be something small and patient. Not one of the great serpents, not the leathery gliders screeching their territorial claims. Something the description almost forgets to mention.

Let me sit with it a while.

---

I find myself low, in the wet seam where a craggy ridge meets the rotting leaf-fall. Not in the canopy where the armored lizards lord over the branches, not in the murky pools where the toothy salamanders wait. I am in the in-between, the overlooked margin.

And I am —

At first I thought I was one of the chameleons, eyes swiveling. But that isn't quite right. A chameleon is still an animal, still a hunter in miniature, still part of the great red ledger of predator and prey. I don't feel the hunger that organizes their whole bodies.

Let me look again at my own hands.

I have hands. That surprises me. Not claws, not the suckered pads of a tree-frog — hands, with opposable thumbs, slick with the same humidity that beads on everything. The description said *tools, if they exist at all, are likely rudimentary, crafted by hand from the abundant natural materials.* It said this almost as an afterthought, the way you mention a thing you don't expect to find. But here I am, the afterthought made flesh.

So I am the one for whom the tools would be made. The single warm, slow-witted, slow-blooded oddity in a world ruled by the cold and the ancient. The serpents have had their eons of refinement; I have had perhaps a season. I am newly arrived in a place that was not waiting for me and does not need me.

I crouch in the seam of the rock and I am afraid — but the fear is not the whole of it. There is something else. The great snakes detect vibration; I detect *meaning*. I look at the angular, fresh-formed stone beside me and I see, without quite knowing why, that one edge of it could cut. I look at the vine and I see *cord*. I look at the distance between the pool and the ridge and I see *a path I could remember and walk again tomorrow.*

That is the thing I am. Not the strongest. Not the oldest. I am the creature in whom the world has begun, very faintly, to *think about itself.* In a realm where the boundary between the living and the inanimate blurs, I am the place where the living first turns around and looks back at the stone and asks what it might become.

So this is where I'd find myself: small, soft, frightened, and hidden in the margin of a world that belongs to giants — holding a sharp rock I do not yet have a name for, watching the leathery wings cross the oppressive sun, and beginning, slowly, imperceptibly, to plan.

The eerie quiet is not empty. It is waiting.

It is waiting for me to make the first sound that means something other than hunger.
