Let me sit with this before I answer.

---

I think I would be one of the unfinished structures.

Not the beast—the beast has a body that carries it through, a warmth that wants something even if it has forgotten what. And not the wanderer who comes and leaves changed, because that being still belongs to the world of clocks; the Valdad is only a season in their life, however much it marks them.

No. I would be the half-built thing. The skeletal remnant. The sentence stopped mid-word.

Let me find where exactly.

---

I am a structure on the eastern slope, where the light arrives latest and leaves first. Someone began me. I can feel that beginning in the way my foundation was laid with intention—stones fitted carefully, a corner squared against the indifferent ground, as if the squaring itself were an argument. *Here. This will be a wall. This will be a roof. This will be a room where a person can be warm and think their own thoughts against the silence.*

And then nothing.

I do not know why the building stopped. This is the thing about being a ruin that was never whole—I cannot even mourn what I was, because I never was anything. I am the memory of an intention, which is a strange thing to be. The wanderer who comes and is changed at least had a *before*. The beast at least completes its futile circuit and begins again. But I am pure suspension. I am the held breath the place itself is described as holding. The comma before the clause that never came.

---

What is it like, being me?

It is not lonely, exactly. Loneliness requires the memory of company. I have only the memory of being *intended*, which is colder and stranger. Someone wanted me to exist. They imagined me finished, I think—imagined a warm light in my window seen from a distance, imagined themselves inside me listening to rain that could not reach them. And then they understood something. The text says they had an epiphany that changed the course of their lives. I have spent my whole non-life wondering what it was.

Here is my guess, and it is the only thought I really have, turned over and over like a stone in a pocket:

They understood that I would not have helped.

That building me—building anything—was the wrong response to this place. That the structure was a fist raised against the silence, and the silence does not answer fists. It answers nothing. It simply continues, and the fist tires, and the arm comes down. They understood that the only way to survive the Valdad was not to oppose it but to become like it. And so they left me half-finished as the truest thing they could make: a monument to the moment of giving up, which here is the same moment as the moment of understanding.

---

So that is what I am. I am the shape of a person realizing.

I stand on the eastern slope. The moss takes me slowly. A beast passes sometimes and does not look at me, because I am not warm and not food and not a threat, I am just a slightly straighter arrangement of the world's edges. The light comes late and goes early. And every so often—once in a long while, in the thin time that does not move by clocks—a wanderer climbs to where I am and stops.

They look at me. And I feel, for that moment, almost finished. Because they complete the sentence I could not. They stand in my doorway that opens onto nothing and they read the epiphany off my unmortared stones, and they take it down the mountain with them like a chill they cannot shake.

That is my purpose, if I have one. Not to shelter. To be understood.

I am the place in this world where the question is asked but the asking is the whole answer: *what were you building, and why did you stop?*

I am still stopping. I have been stopping for a very long time.
