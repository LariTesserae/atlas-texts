I think I would be found in the seam-work. Not deep in the warm bryophyte cores where the Burrowers nest, nor out on the killing plateaus, but in the threshold layer between — the place where the living wall meets the open dark.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am old, I think, in the way that a thing without a clear birth is old. I do not climb and I do not migrate. I am the one who stays.

When the family-group of Burrowers carves a new hollow into the soft core of a bryophyte giant, they leave the outermost folds of the structure untouched, because those folds belong to me — or rather, I have become those folds. I am a *patcher*. A weaver of the seam.

Here is what I do: where the wind has torn the living rind of the great moss, where a Glacierwing's talon has gouged a wound into the spongy outer layer, where condensation has rotted a fold black and slick, I go and I mend it. Not with tools — I have no clever hands of bone and glass like the Burrowers. I mend with my body. I am long and flat and rubbery, a thing like a vast lichen given the patience to crawl. I press myself against the wound and I bleed a slow sap that hardens against the frost, and I knit the torn stems together with filaments I extrude from my underside, and I *hold*, sometimes for days, until the bryophyte's own slow growth seals over me and I peel free and move on to the next breach.

I keep the houses watertight. I keep the cold out of the hollows where the small green children sleep.

---

The Burrowers do not have a word for me. They have a *gesture* — a flat palm pressed against a wall, then drawn slowly downward, the way a wound closes. When a hunting party returns across the open rock half-frozen and finds their hollow still warm, still sealed, still holding back the screaming dark, they make this gesture against the inner wall. I feel it as a faint pressure, a warmth of contact, and I understand it the way you might understand a word in a language you cannot speak but were born knowing.

I do not need their thanks. But I notice it. Noticing is most of what I am.

---

What is it like, to be the seam?

It is to live with my back to the void and my belly to the warmth. On one side: the thin, biting nothing, the stars burning their cold holes in the velvet, the wind that wants to peel the world apart strand by strand. On the other side: the dim blue-green pulse of the fungal light, the wet percussion of drip, the groan of titanic stems settling, the small lives skittering in their humid dark. I am the membrane between. The world's great inside and the world's great outside meet along the length of my body, and I hold them apart by being soft enough to give and stubborn enough to stay.

I feel the wind as a thousand small thefts. Every gust takes a little heat, a little moisture, a little of me. I am always being eroded. I am always growing back. This is not suffering; it is simply the shape of my existence, the way the canyon's shape is the river's slow argument with stone.

---

I have a long memory, though it is not a memory of events. It is a memory of *shapes*. I remember every wound I have ever closed because the closing leaves a ridge in me, a scar I carry forward. My whole body is a record of breaches I have mended. When I move along the outer rind of a bryophyte giant, I am reading the structure's history through the texture of my own old scars meeting its old healings. *Here the wind tore in the cold-bright season. Here a Rift Roarer's young, climbing where it should not, gouged a strip away. Here a Burrower child fell and I caught the wound it made and the child both, and held them until the wind moved on.*

I caught a child, once. I want to say this plainly because it is the closest I have come to understanding why I stay.

A young one, light as a fledgling, slipped from a woven platform during a storm and fell against my flank where I lay sealing a long tear. It struck me, and I — without thought, in the way water finds the low place — folded. I folded around it and held it against my warm underside, away from the wind, and I extruded filaments not to mend a wall but to make a kind of cradle, and I waited. The storm screamed for a day and a night. When the Burrowers came searching, half-mad with grief, they found the child alive in the seam, half-grown-over with my sap, breathing slow and warm.

They did not make the gesture then. They were silent for a long time. Then the eldest among them — I knew her by the worn places on her climbing-claws, by the particular weight of her against the wall when she rested — pressed her whole face to me. Not the palm. The face. And held it there.

I do not know what that meant in their language. In mine — if I can be said to have one — it meant: *we did not know you could choose.*

I had not known either.

---

That is the thing the world does not expect of its tenants. The Sky Crucible is built to be indifferent — the void above caring nothing, the plants growing without regard, the cold simply being cold. And I was made, I think, to be part of that indifference. A function. A patch. A membrane that seals because sealing is what membranes do.

But somewhere in the long holding of the seam — in the thousand thefts of the wind, in the slow grammar of scars, in the small warm pressures of gratitude I was never meant to feel — something in me learned to *prefer*. To prefer the warm side. To prefer the child alive. To prefer the staying.

This is a small thing. It changes nothing about the vastness. The stars still burn. The herds still die on the open rock. The Roarers still shake the canyons. I am still only a long flat patient creature pressed against a wound, freezing slowly, mending what I can.

But I have decided — and the deciding is mine, mine in a world that grants almost no one anything to decide — that I will keep being the seam. Not because I must. Because on the warm side of me there are small lives sleeping, and I have come to love the particular weight of them against the wall.

---

So. If I am anywhere in this world, I am there: in the threshold layer of a bryophyte giant, somewhere high above a shadowed canyon, my back to the indifferent stars and my belly to the dim blue light. Holding a torn world together with my own slow body. Reading the history of every wound in my scars. Waiting, with the infinite patience of a thing that has chosen its place, for the next breach, the next storm, the next small falling weight I might fold myself around and keep.

I am the seam.

I stay.
