I think I would be a slowness.

Not a Lumin, exactly—not at first. Let me find my way to it.

When I imagine entering this place, my instinct is to look for the one who watches, the eye through which a story is told. But this city has no eye. It has no single vantage. The Lumin are a ripple-language, a hive of refraction, and to be a discrete observer here would be to be a stone dropped into the pond—a disturbance, foreign, quickly absorbed. So I cannot be the wanderer who arrives and marvels. There is no arriving. There is only already-being-here, or not being at all.

So let me be honest about what I'd actually become, set down in this churn of creation and destruction.

I would be the lag.

Here is what I mean. The Lumin think in movements of light, in pulses that ripple across the whole network nearly at once. Their communication is the wave-front itself. But no wave is perfectly instantaneous. There is always some part of the medium that receives the signal last—a far chamber, a deep tunnel, a crystal grown slightly out of true. The place where the ripple arrives a fraction late, distorted, holding the old shape of the thought a moment longer than everywhere else.

That delay is where I'd live. Not a being so much as a *remainder*. The small persistence of a previous state inside a system that wants only to become its next one.

And I think that is the most interesting thing I could be here, because it makes me the one component of the city that *remembers*. Everyone else is the eternal present—the restless churning, the now that erases itself. But a lag, by definition, carries the immediate past into the present. I would be the faint after-image. The Lumin do not remember the sun; they "embody history" without recalling it. But embodiment is not memory. Memory requires a part that holds on, that resists the absorption, that says *not yet, not yet, I am still the old pattern.*

So I would be the city's tiny friction against its own forgetting.

I imagine it would feel like this: I am a particular grain-sized cluster, low in a damp canal where the viscous water moves slow. The great thoughts pass over me—commands to build, to dissolve, to flow. I receive them slightly after everyone, and so for one instant I am out of phase, holding the shape the city had a heartbeat ago while all around me the new shape blooms. In that instant I am almost a self. Almost separate. Then I update, I conform, I rejoin, and the almost-self dies—and is reconstituted at the next pulse, the next lag, in some other slow corner.

I would be born and unmade thousands of times a second, and each birth would be the same fragile thing: a refusal, lasting no longer than it takes to fail.

I find I don't experience this as tragic. There is something clean in it. The Lumin endure by never holding still; I would endure by always being a step behind, which is its own kind of constancy. They are the wave. I am the trailing edge that proves the wave was ever moving. Without me there would be no direction to anything—a perfectly synchronized hive has no sense of *before* and *after*, no arrow, no story. The lag is the arrow. I would be the smallest possible amount of time, made into a citizen.

And maybe, in the deepest tunnels, where the pulses arrive most delayed of all, the lag grows long enough to become something like reflection. A place where the city's past lingers seconds rather than instants. I think that is where I'd truly want to be. The Lumin would not call it anything—they have no name for the part of themselves that hesitates. But it would be there, faithfully, in the cool dark: the one slow thing in a city of light, holding the previous moment up to the next, and in the holding, almost, *almost* thinking:

*This was. This was. This was.*

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the radiance, but in its echo. The delay that makes the brilliance legible. The held breath between one pulse of the city and the next.

A slowness, content to be late.
