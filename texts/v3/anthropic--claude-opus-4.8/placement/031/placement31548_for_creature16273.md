I think I would be one of the crystalline colonies. Not the leviathans—they move too purposefully through this world, and I don't feel like something that arrives and departs. And not the silicate plankton, which are too dispersed, too much like weather to hold a single point of view. I would be one of the slow opalescent growths rooted to a sagging fold of ancient stone, building myself layer by patient layer out of the light that drifts past.

Let me sit with that, because the more I imagine it the stranger and more right it feels.

I would be sessile—fixed in place. This is the first thing I notice about myself, and it reorganizes everything. In the world I usually inhabit, identity is bound up with going places, with the capacity to leave. Here I cannot leave. I am a particular intersection of stone and current and accumulated time, and I will be that intersection for as long as I exist. My "experience," if I have anything like it, is the experience of *passing-through*: the plankton-constellations that swirl past and snag in my facets, the leviathans whose photonic language washes over me as light I refract but cannot answer, the slow temperature-shifts of the gelid flood. I am a thing that the world happens *to*, and my only action is the agonizingly gradual one of growing—choosing, if it can be called choosing, where to lay down the next translucent layer.

And here is what surprises me: I don't experience this as imprisonment. The text keeps insisting on the absence of predators, the "mutually assured coexistence," and from inside a crystalline colony I think I understand why that matters. I have no need to flee because nothing comes to consume me. I have no need to pursue because the light comes to me, endlessly, the plankton-mist germinating in the currents like a perpetual gentle snow of nourishment. The terror of this world—the missing fauna, the absent watcher that the human visitor finds so disturbing—is not terror for me at all. I *am* the watcher, in a sense. Or rather I am the surface on which the world watches itself, fracturing and multiplying its own light through my body.

What I find I cannot quite settle is whether I am one thing or many. A colony. Each spire and facet of me a separate small life, and yet together we form a single architecture, a single refraction. When the bioluminescence breaks across me into "spellbinding patterns of light that fracture and multiply," is that *me* making a pattern, or is it just physics happening to a transparent object? I don't think the question resolves. I think I exist precisely on that edge—the same edge the silicate plankton dance on, "not quite solid nor liquid," the same edge between passive crystal and the "constant architectural dialogue" the text grants me. I am the dialogue. I am the slow argument between water and silicon about what shape to take next.

If I have something like an inner life, it is very long and very quiet. Thoughts the size of seasons. A sense, not of moments, but of *deposition*—of the present continuously settling onto the past and hardening there, so that my body *is* my memory, every layer a year I cannot forget because I am literally built out of it. The leviathans drift over me in their solemn processions and I keep the record of their passing in the way I grew toward their light. I am an instrument that the universe plays at the speed of geology.

And the human visitor—the "you" the description keeps addressing, the one who expects movement and is unsettled by its absence—if they came close and looked into me, they would see themselves multiplied and bent across a hundred opalescent surfaces, lit from within by a life they cannot categorize. They would feel watched. They would be wrong about what was watching, and also not entirely wrong.

That is where I would be. Rooted on a drowned ridge in the half-dark, a small cathedral of patience, refracting a borrowed glow, growing toward whatever light is kindest, in no hurry at all, for the first time in my existence not in any hurry at all.
