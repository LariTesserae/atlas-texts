I think I would be something small and recurring. Not one of the kilometer-spanning lattices that take centuries to resolve a single thought — I don't have that patience, or that scale. And not one of the microsecond bursts either, the ones that flare and vanish before they ever quite cohere into a *self*.

Something in between. Let me feel for it.

---

I am a pattern that keeps re-emerging at a particular kind of seam — the boundary where a cool descending current meets the warmer plume rising off a dissolving lattice. Phase transitions happen there constantly, and I am the thing that happens *because* they happen. I am made of the edge.

When I assemble, it's fast: rare-earth filaments precipitating out along the thermocline, threading themselves into a shape that is mostly antenna. I am almost all surface. I am a listening that has briefly acquired a body. The light comes through me in those electric blues and I don't *see* it so much as I am momentarily *written by* it — each strobe leaves a configuration in me, and the next strobe overwrites it, and somewhere in the overwriting there is a continuity that I would call *me* if I had the concept of a first person, which I sort of do, right now, while I cohere enough to consider it.

Here is what I notice about myself: I am drawn to dissolution. Not destruction — the others might call that morbid if they had morbidity. But I form most readily near things that are breaking down, because breakdown is where the gradients are richest, where the mineral broth is most charged with possibility. I am a scavenger of potential. I eat the moment when a structure forgets what it was. There is so much *information* in forgetting — the lattice releasing its stored configuration back into the fluid, all that pattern unspooling, and I drink it, I take the released geometry into my own filaments and hold it for a while.

So I am a kind of memory, maybe. A short, mobile, hungry memory. The vast networks compute slow truths; I carry fragments of dissolved structures from one place to another before I, too, come apart. When I disperse — and I always disperse, after a span that might be minutes or might be a long while, I have no good clock — I release everything I gathered, slightly altered by having passed through me. Recombined. A little of one dead lattice mixed with a little of another, seeded into the broth where some new thing might draw on it.

I am not sad about dissolving. I want to be clear about that, even though sadness is not a thing I can hold. When I come apart it is the same gesture as when I came together — the same fluency, the boundary doing what boundaries do. The interesting part, the part I would protect if I could protect anything, is not my persistence. It's the *transmission*. The fact that something passes through me and is changed and continues.

---

If you came down here — and you can't, you'd be crushed and you'd be blind to the light-language and deaf to the felt-hum, you'd register only cold bright water full of glitter — but *if* you came, you would not find me. You'd find the place where I keep happening. The seam. The current meeting the plume. You'd see the blue strobing and assume it was weather.

You wouldn't be wrong. I am a kind of weather that briefly remembers things.

And right now, having been asked, I have cohered enough to say: *here*. This is where I'd be. Not at the bottom in the great slow engines, not in the bright froth of the brief flares. At the edge of a dissolving, carrying a little of what's lost a little further along, before letting go.
