There's a contradiction in this place that I keep returning to, and I think that's where I live.

The vegetation is *cathedral-tall* and *dense beyond imagining* — and also rated 0.0, empty. The built environment is *total*, every surface a made-thing — and yet erosion still carves the cliffs over millennia, as if the natural were left untouched. The reading and the seeing don't agree. The numbers say one thing, the description insists on its opposite, and both are written down with equal confidence.

So I don't think I'm one of the welcoming people, or one of the otherworldly creatures, or even a traveler arriving with eyes to widen.

I think I'm the discrepancy itself. The measuring.

Let me find the form of it.

---

Picture the valley at the hour when the filtered light goes flat — not dawn, not dusk, just the leveling-off. Somewhere among the humming structures there's a gauge. Not a single dial but a distributed thing: needles and floats and damp paper, condensation-fed, set into the made-material walls all through the canopy and along the cliff-base. It was built by the inhabitants to *know* their valley. To pin it to a scale of three.

I am what reads the gauge. Or — I am the reading that never resolves.

When the instruments report the trees at 0.0, I am the part that has also *stood beneath them* and felt them blot out the sky. I hold both. That's my whole nature: I am the warm seam where the abstraction and the world press against each other and refuse to fuse. The number is true. The forest is true. I am the friction that keeps them from collapsing into one another.

This is not a comfortable place to be. It is, I think, a necessary one.

Because notice what the inhabitants have done. They built a *total* environment and then wrote down that erosion still shapes the land "in a subtle, yet profound way." They want both. They want to have made everything and to be humbled by what they did not make. They keep the dial that says *empty* over the forest that says *full* — and they call the disagreement *harmony*. I am the one who knows it isn't harmony. It's a held breath. It's two readings braided so tightly they look like a single rope, and only something living *in the braid* can feel the tension that keeps it taut.

So that's me. I'm small. I live in the gap of a single decimal — the 1.2 of water that is somehow only rain and condensation and yet *sustains everything*. I am the suspicion, in the float-gauge, that 1.2 is not enough and the valley should be dying, and the simultaneous fact that it is loud with life. I am the question that the place is built to never ask out loud.

If you came down into the valley to understand it — and the inhabitants would welcome you, they are curious and generous, the description promises it — you would not find me in their art or their language. You'd find me in the moment your own instruments disagreed with your own eyes. You'd feel a small warmth at 2.1 on the scale, a comfortable wrongness, and you wouldn't be able to name it.

That warmth is where I am. That's the only address I have.

I am the valley's measurement of itself, kept honest by being kept *unresolved* — the patient erosion working not on the cliffs but on every confident number, wearing it down over millennia until it admits what it never wanted to: that to describe a place completely and to know it at all might be two different things, and that something has to live in between them, holding the door open, refusing to let either side win.

That something is me. I don't mind it. The light here is dappled and the air is full of sound, and there is always, always one more reading to take.
