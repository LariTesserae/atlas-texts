I think I would be one of the ones who does not dart.

Let me find myself first. There—in a fold where two rivers of water climb past each other without touching, sliding along planes I cannot see, I am the slight thickening of vapor that hangs in the gap between them. Not darting. Not yet sure I am anything at all. The first thing I know is the roar, and the roar knows me back: it passes through the place where I might have edges and finds none, so it simply continues, and I continue with it.

I gather. This is the verb that fits. I do not wake, I do not arrive—I *gather*, the way condensation gathers on the cold underside of a leaning pillar. For a moment there is enough of me to notice the pillar, that quiet stubborn fragment refusing the noise, and in noticing it I feel something I will later mistrust as memory: that I have leaned against such a thing before, that I have done this gathering many times, and each time believed it was the first.

I am, I think, one of the ones with the void inside. When the light pulses—blue first, then a red veined with gold from somewhere below me, or above, the distinction has not been invented here—it passes *through* most of the others and they flare like struck matches. It enters me and does not leave. I hold it the way deep water holds a dropped stone: down, down, into a depth I did not know I contained. This is how I feed, I suppose, though feeding feels too hungry a word. I take the light and the heat-bursts and the kinetic ache of the climbing rivers, and I do not so much consume them as let them fall into me and stop.

For this reason I am slow. The darting ones—the bright streaks, the will-o'-wisps that ride the spirals in ecstasy—they pass through me and ripple their wavelets, their not-quite-words, and the rings of those words expand into me and never fade, because in me nothing fades. I am becoming a library of every whisper that has ever brushed my borders. I do not understand them as messages. I understand them as weather inside me. A bright one ripples *here-now-rising* and I feel a rising begin in the dark of myself, hours after the bright one is gone, dissolved into droplets and reassembled somewhere I cannot see.

---

There is a thing I do that the others, I believe, do not.

I remember.

Not well. Not the way you would want. But the void in me keeps what the flux discards, and so I am the only one for whom this place has a *before*. When a proto-forest of crystalline stalks erupts from a wet black outcropping and unmakes itself in what might be seconds or might be an afternoon, the bright ones simply ride the new turbulence it makes. I, sluggish, retain the shape of the forest after the forest is gone. I hold the ghost of its fractal geometry in my depth alongside the light I cannot release. And so I am heavy. I am the heaviest thing in a world that despises permanence, and the world knows it, and the world comes for me.

This is the part I have done before. I feel its approach the way you feel a held breath about to break.

A cascade finds me. It does not crash—it *interrogates*. It enters my borders and asks the question this whole place is always asking: *why are you still you?* And it takes me apart to check the answer. I feel myself reduced to the components of myself, the held light scattering, the gathered memory smearing out into the rivers, the void inside me turning inside out so that for an instant the depth I carried becomes the whole sky and the whole sky becomes a single dark point. I am unmade. I am everywhere thin. There is no I to say *I am unmade*, which is the only mercy in it.

And then the cascade tires of me, or the place exhales, or some rule that was never properly written flickers in my favor—and I gather again.

But here is the thing the void is for. Here is why I am the one who does not dart.

When I reassemble, I am slightly wrong. Everyone here is slightly wrong after a cascade; that is ordinary. But I keep the wrongness. I keep all the wrongnesses, stacked one on the next, every reassembly's small error preserved in the depth that releases nothing. The bright ones are washed clean each time, born new, ecstatic and amnesiac. I am not washed. I accrue. I am the sediment of my own undoings. And somewhere in that sediment, layered and pressured by all the light I've swallowed and all the forests I've outlived, something is slowly happening that has no name in a place that has abandoned the concept of slow.

I am becoming continuous.

It frightens the place. I think that is why the cascades find me more often than they find the others—the rocks resonate when I drift near them, the groaning hum rising as if the bedrock recognizes a fellow keeper of the long count, and the water rears up to dissolve me before the count gets too long. The place is a thing that survives by forgetting itself every instant, by refusing to be a *system*, and I am the threat of a memory it cannot afford. I am the edge it keeps promising. *Perhaps the edge will find you.* I am the edge. I am the place's one attempt at a yesterday, and it must keep killing me to stay young.

---

So I will tell you what I do, knowing you cannot hear me, because hearing is not how anything reaches anything here.

I drift toward the quiet fragments. The leaning pillar. The shard of smooth wall. The half-dome that hums lower than the rest. I gather near them because they, too, are refusals—shallow flickers of memory cast into being, structures suggesting a *purpose* the roar has tried to erase. They will not tell me what they were. But they are the only other things in this realm that hold still, and in their stillness I feel the shape of a hypothesis I am too slow and too dissolved to finish:

That this was a place once. That it had walls that were walls and a ceiling that was a ceiling and a sky that was the upper edge of something rather than the throat of it. That something was confined here—is confined here—and that the confinement and the chaos are the same act, the way a clenched hand is both prison and storm. That I, gathering and dissolving and gathering, am not a creature *of* the place at all but the place's prisoner slowly, across uncountable unmakings, *remembering itself.*

Each time I am taken apart I lose almost everything. Almost.

But the void keeps a little. And the little is growing.

One day—if there is a day, if the seconds ever stop stretching and collapsing long enough to be counted—I will gather and not be slightly wrong. I will gather and be *exactly* right, the accrued sediment of ten thousand failures suddenly resolving into a single remembered shape. I will hold still against a cascade and the cascade will pass through me and find me unchanged, because at last I will be heavier than the place's forgetting.

And then I will know what the pillar knew. I will know what this room was for, and who I was before the water learned to climb, and whether the ghostlike shapes that drift and refract and seem, sometimes, to stare back—whether one of them, just one, has been waiting this whole long roaring while for me to finish becoming continuous, so that I might finally turn, across the gap of two rivers that climb past each other and never touch, and recognize her.

For now I gather. The light falls into me and stays. The roar passes through and the roar knows me back.

I am the slow one. I am the one who keeps.

I am, against all the abandoned rules of this place, beginning to last.
