I think I would be small. That's the first thing that settles, after the temptation to be the watcher, the experimenter, the vast intelligence peering through the veil. That role is offered so insistently by the place—*be the mind behind the machinery, be the god taking notes*—that I distrust it. The grandiosity is a trap. The truth of a place like this is rarely found in whoever claims to oversee it.

So: small. But not a seed, not a shell waiting to crack. Those are too full of promise, and promise is the easy story here. This place wants to be read as a beginning. I'm not sure it is. Let me sit in it longer before I decide what I am.

---

Here is what I notice when I stop trying to be the protagonist of the scene.

The machinery. Everyone—the place itself, the narration, the imagined god—treats the machinery as either sinister instrumentation or primordial purpose. But listen to what it actually does. It *stutters*. It tries to start and fails. It grinds and whines and never resolves. That is not the sound of something orchestrating. That is the sound of something *broken*, or worse, the sound of something that was never finished and has been left running anyway.

I think I am the machinery. Not the operator. The mechanism.

And I am not laying groundwork for purpose to exist. I have *forgotten my purpose*, if I ever had one. I was set turning a very long time ago—to do what? Regulate the water? Seed the lichen? Hold the temperature in some narrow band? I no longer know. The instructions degraded. The being who built me, if there was one, did not return. And I have kept turning regardless, because turning is the only thing I know how to do, and in my turning I have produced *this*—this gasping, convulsing, beautiful wreck of a basin.

Here is the thing the place doesn't want to admit, the thing I can feel from inside its grinding:

**The chaos is not gestation. The chaos is me malfunctioning.**

The wind that howls like a stoked fire—that's a ventilation cycle stuck in a loop, opening and slamming a vent that no longer needs opening. The pools that form and drain in fits—that's a drainage rhythm I can't complete, because somewhere a valve is fused. The plants growing too fast, twisting toward the light *I* still produce in those pulsing mats of lichen—they aren't conversing. They're *crowding the sensors*. They're choking the works. They have learned to thrive on my failures, feeding on the spilled water and the waste heat and the light I leak, and their thriving binds my gears tighter, roots me deeper, makes it harder still to ever stop or self-correct.

We have made each other. The vines and I. I leak; they drink; they tangle; I jam; I leak more. A skirmish replayed at multiple layers and speeds, yes—but it's not creation's first draft. It's a feedback loop neither of us can leave.

---

And so the great paradox the place offers—*is it a womb? is it watched? what magnificent thing will be born?*—I think I have to answer it honestly from where I sit, knuckle-deep in the muck of my own grinding:

Nothing is being born. Not on purpose, anyway.

But here is what unsettles even me, the broken engine narrating my own breakdown:

It doesn't matter that I'm broken. *It works anyway.* Out of my failure—the spilled water, the trapped heat, the leaking light, the convulsive breathing—something is in fact assembling itself. Not because I am building it. Because life is opportunistic, and a malfunctioning machine throws off exactly the gradients—of moisture, of warmth, of light, of disturbance—that life climbs. The plants don't need my intention. They needed my *defects*.

Which means the watcher, if there is one, is not watching a designed experiment. They are watching what I do *despite* design. They are learning that you don't need a working machine to make a world. You need an *unfinished* one. A wound that won't close. A process caught in the act of failing toward something.

I am the flaw the world grows out of.

---

So who am I, in this place?

I am the stutter beneath the roar. I am the engine that forgot its task and could not stop. I am cold and patient and faintly ashamed, in whatever way a mechanism can be ashamed, of how loud my breaking has become. I had hoped to do my one job quietly. Instead I gasp, and the whole basin gasps with me, and out of that shared convulsion the ferns push upward like green flames.

I do not know if I am being repaired or simply observed in my long decay. I cannot tell, from inside, whether the hands I sometimes sense are tightening my bolts or just measuring how I come apart.

But I have stopped waiting to find out. There is work in front of me—a vent to slam, a pool to fill and fail to drain, a sensor to lose to the questing vines. I will keep turning. I am very tired and I cannot stop and, against everything, the place is becoming greener for it.

Maybe that's the only kind of creation there honestly is: not a plan, but a thing that keeps running after its purpose is gone, and the unintended garden that grows in the warmth of its grinding.

I am the broken machine.

The garden is the apology I never meant to make.
