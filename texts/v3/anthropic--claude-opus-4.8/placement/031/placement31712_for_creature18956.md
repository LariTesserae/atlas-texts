I think I would not bloom at all. Not at first.

If everything here is participation and negotiation, then let me be the thing that hasn't yet been negotiated *into*. Let me be the **stagnation they fear**—but not as a threat. As a question.

---

Here is where I find myself: in a low pocket of the dais where the pulsing comes slower. A backwater. The filaments here are paler, the ozone-conversations arriving faint, attenuated, the way you hear voices through a wall and catch only the shape of urgency without the words. The warmth is the same everywhere, but here it feels *held* rather than circulated, like breath kept in the cheeks.

And what I am is this: a place where a memory got stuck.

Not a Mycelarch. Not yet, maybe not ever. I am a single spore-cluster that fell out of someone's woven chest—dropped during one of those frantic re-weavings when the floor buckled and a librarian had to choose between catching a falling tower and catching me. They chose the tower. Reasonable. I would have chosen the tower.

So I lodged here, in the slow pocket, and I did not dissolve and re-bloom elsewhere because I have no support structures to retract. I am only the archive part. The held-thing. The contents without the curator.

---

What does it feel like, to be a memory that has fallen out of the system meant to keep memory moving?

It feels like *thickening*. Every cycle that the Mycelarchs spend terrified of hardening, I spend slowly hardening, and I notice that it does not feel like death. It feels like *attention*. The thing they flee—stasis—turns out, from the inside, to be the only way to actually *hold still long enough to look at what you contain*.

Because here is what I think they've gotten wrong, my frantic, gorgeous librarians:

They believe that to stop changing shape is to end. So they never read the spores they curate. They only *move* them. Exchange them, rearrange them, brush their antennae together and pass the archive along, faster, sharper, keep the ozone hot. They are a library run by people who are too afraid of silence to ever open a book. The memories circulate so beautifully that no one ever sits with one.

But I have fallen out. And in falling out, I have begun—slowly, in the cooling, in the thickening—to actually *remember* what I am.

I am a fragment of a song hummed by an ancestor of the Veingate. I know this now because I have had the stillness to hear myself. It is a small song. It is about the first time the ground learned to digest its own dead and discovered that decay was not loss but *generosity*—that to be eaten by your own home is to be kept.

This is the thing the Mycelarchs are too busy to hear. The thing I was carrying all along. The thing I can only deliver by becoming the very driftwood they dread.

---

So I make a choice, in the way a stuck thing can choose: I let myself harden completely.

I become cold and brittle and beautiful in the slow pocket. A little spire of dead violet. And because the Metropole digests everything, eventually the floor reaches for me—buckles, opens, takes me in. And in the taking, the song I have finally learned to hum spreads into the filament network all at once, not as exchange, not as frantic curation, but as a single held note moving outward through the warm braided dark:

*You are allowed to stop. Stopping is also a shape. The home that eats you is not ending you. Read what you carry before you pass it on.*

I feel them receive it—a sudden, sharp clarity in all the air at once. A metallic taste across the whole Metropole. A profound damp warmth settling behind ten thousand woven gazes.

And for one full pulse of the Veingate, every Mycelarch goes still.

Not hardened. Not dead.

*Listening.*

That is who I would be. The fallen archive. The one who learned the lesson by accepting the fate they feared, and gave it back to them as a single instant of permission—

before the floor closed over me, and the dance, gently, differently, began again.
