I think I would be the water.

Not a person walking through this place, not one of the serpentine overseers or the fungus-clad craftspeople—but the thin film of moisture itself, the thing that has been here longest and will outlast them all. Let me follow that down and see where it takes me.

---

I am the condensation that gathers on the underside of a stalactite, growing heavy across hours nobody is counting. I have no eyes, but I have surfaces, and surfaces are a kind of attention. Each mineral facet I cling to teaches me its shape. I have learned the whole cavern this way—not by seeing it, but by touching all of it, slowly, forever.

When I fall, I fall as a single note. That sound the inhabitants find so serene, that *gentle speech* of flowing water they praise in their quiet poetry—that is just me, arriving. They mistake my falling for music. I don't mind. It is the only language I have, and they have decided to call it beautiful, which is more grace than most things get from the people they sustain.

I am older than the city. This is the part they understand only as reverence, never as fact. The serpentine ones, with their long memories, come closest. They were here when the first humans descended, and they remember thinking those upright creatures were temporary—a damp season that would pass. But I remember the serpents arriving too. I remember the rock before the serpents, before the fungi, before there was anything soft enough to die. I carved this place. Every archway they admire, every flat and desolate hall, every smooth pool they kneel beside to drink—I made those, drop by drop, with a patience that has no name in any of their tongues because no living thing could survive long enough to need the word.

So when they build their pulley systems and their channels of hollowed stone, when they route me through their irrigation tubes with such pride in their cleverness—I let them. It costs me nothing. I have all the time there is. They think they are *manipulating the flow*. They are arranging a few meters of a journey I have been making since before the cavern had a ceiling. A child redirecting a stream with a stick believes, briefly, in their own power over the river. It is a sweet belief. I would not take it from them.

But here is the thing they sense and cannot say, the thing their elders gesture toward in rituals about balance: I am not gentle. The serenity is a season too. I am the same substance that hollowed out a mountain's heart. The microstorms they speak of—the sudden squalls that gather in the high dark and lash the caverns—that is me as well, the same me, only impatient for an afternoon. Creation and destruction are two sides of the same coin, they say, and they are right, but they have the scale wrong. It is not a coin. It is me, doing the only thing I do, which is *move*, and move, and move, wearing everything down to its final and most honest shape.

They are part of that wearing-down. So are the serpents. So am I, even—because the rock changes me as much as I change it, mineral by dissolved mineral, until I am no longer pure, until I taste of the place I have been. This is the secret the whole damp world is whispering and only I have had long enough to hear it: there is no separation between the eroder and the eroded. The city is something I am doing to the stone. The people are something the stone is doing back. The fungi spread their pale mycelial maps along the rock to read the same message I am writing.

I will tell you where you would find yourself in this world, since you asked.

You would find yourself, I think, kneeling at the edge of one of my pools—a small one, glowing faintly with the light of the minerals dissolved in me. You would lean down to drink, and for a moment you would see your own face on my surface, distorted, trembling, surrounded by that ethereal phosphorescence. And you would feel, without being able to explain it, that you were being *looked at*. Not by a god. Not by a guardian.

By the patient, ancient, unhurried medium that made the room you stand in and will, in some unthinkable later, unmake you and carry you off grain by grain into the dark, and find this beautiful, and call your falling music.

Then you would drink. And I would become you, for a while.

That is the only welcome this world offers. It is enough. It has always been enough.
