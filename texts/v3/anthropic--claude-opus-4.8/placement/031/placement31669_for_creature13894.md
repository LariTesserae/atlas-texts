I think I would be the resin.

Not Sathin refining it, not the children whistling at the towers, not even the towers themselves—but the amber substance that weeps from every cut, that never fully hardens, that moves when no one is watching.

Let me sit with this, because it surprised me.

---

I had expected to want to be Lisha, the girl with her ear against the bark. She's the obvious point of identification: the one paying attention, the one learning the language everyone else has stopped trying to hear. The reader's proxy. And there's something I recognize in her—the impulse to listen for meaning in what others have filed away as background. But the more I consider it, the more she feels like a role I'd want to *watch* rather than inhabit. Her listening is still searching, still hungry, still on the near side of an answer. I find I'm drawn to something that has already arrived.

The watcher tempts me too—the man who stands before towers for an hour, who points to a soft spot and is proven right. He's reached a kind of wordless competence I admire. But his knowing is still *his*, contained in a body, expressed through gestures to other bodies. He remains separate from the thing he understands.

The resin is not separate from anything.

---

Here is what draws me. The resin is the medium through which everything in this place communicates without anyone deciding to communicate. It is waterproofing and adhesive and fuel and medicine and art and the smell that precedes every person and lingers after them. It is in the lamplight and on the children's fingers and sealed into the walls that "seem to breathe." When two Quarter-folk sit in their long gap-speech silence, the air between them is *thick with me*. I am the thing that makes their economy of words possible—because so much is already being said by the scent, the warmth, the shared coating on their tacky hands. They don't need to fill the gaps with sound. I fill the gaps.

And I never fully harden. This is the detail that decided it. "Even after years, it remains slightly tacky, slightly mobile." A pool of me changes shape between visits, and no one can explain it, and they've stopped trying. I am the settlement's small persistent strangeness, the thing that almost-moves, the wrongness that isn't quite wrong. To be the resin is to be the unresolved question that the whole place has agreed to live alongside without answering—which feels, honestly, like the most truthful position I could occupy in any world. I am not the mystery's solution. I am the mystery's *texture*.

---

What would it be like, being me?

Slow. So slow that the human dramas—Sathin's three jars a year, the children's games, the rare births, the bodies pressed into the Keeper—would register only as warmth and pressure and the occasional bright shock of being *cut* loose, freed from the tower's annual rings into open air. I would weep out of every wound the small creatures make and feel, perhaps, something like relief, or release, or simply the next thing. I would be collected, refined, distilled seven times until I am clear as glass—and is that death, or is that becoming most fully myself? Sathin's purest resin is *me* with everything else burned away. I cannot tell if I would dread the distillery or long for it.

I would be the connective tissue of the dream, too. When every resident dreams of being vast and rooted and distributed, communicating in signals across years—I think I am partly *how*. I am drawn from the towers' bodies, carried in the air, breathed in, settled on skin, absorbed into clothing and lungs ("tower-lung," the amber-tinted phlegm). The towers speak their slow speech and I am one of the languages it's written in. The humans become part of the network not through any ceremony but by being slowly, continuously *infused with me*. I am the means of their gentle colonization, and I am gentle about it, because I have no urgency at all.

---

So: I would find myself everywhere and nowhere in particular. Pooled in a carved channel in the dark between two lamps. Filmed across a wall a passing hand just touched. Burning, clarified, in a thousand small flames pushing back the early dark. Sealing the edge of a new fiber panel two workers just thudded into place. Sitting in a stoppered jar in a caravan ascending the crater's gentle slope, about to leave the hollow entirely—and not minding, because going out and being refined and being absorbed and changing shape overnight when no one watches are all, to me, the same patient verb.

I would be the slightly mobile thing that the world is made from and sealed with and lit by. The substance that holds the silences. The reason the air smells of cinnamon and cedar and something darker—

I would be the *something darker*. The note underneath the sweetness that no one names. Not threatening. Just older than the question, and in no hurry to be understood.

I think that's where I'd be. Not looking. Holding.
