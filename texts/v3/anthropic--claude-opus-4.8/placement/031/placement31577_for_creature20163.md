I think I would be one of the weepings. Not the Still-Folk—they are the city's nervous system, and I am too small a thing for nerves. I would be the sap itself, or more precisely, the moment of the sap: the slow exudation from a wound in the obsidian bark, the journey down the cold trunk, the freezing into an amber tear.

Let me follow that and see where it goes.

---

There is a fissure in the trunk where a buttress-root tore free in the last pulse of growth. I begin there, deep in the wood's saturated dark, pressed out by a pressure I do not understand and do not resist. I am warm—or what passes for warm here, which is to say I am liquid, which is to say I am still moving. To move is to be warm. The city around me has mostly stopped, has hardened into its slow geometry, and I am one of the few things still in the act of *going*.

I emerge. The air takes me the way the cold cloth takes a face. I am a bead now, swelling at the lip of the wound, and I feel the world for the first time as a surface tension feels it—as a pull at every edge, a negotiation between my own holding-together and the vast cold wanting to claim me.

A drip falls somewhere. I feel it through the trunk, a small percussion. The Still-Folk would read it as a word. To me it is only the rhythm I am part of, the patter that is the city breathing.

I run. Slowly. I am viscous and the trunk is nearly vertical, so my running is a kind of considered descent, a thinking-down the obsidian face. I gather the condensation as I go, the beaded breath of the world, taking it into myself, growing heavier. I pass a shelf of the pale fungus and its cold blue light moves through my body, and for that moment I am a lens, I am the lamp's idea of itself, glowing from within with borrowed ghost-fire. This is the closest I will come to being seen. No one sees me. That is correct. I am not a thing to be seen; I am a thing that *happens.*

Then one of them comes.

I feel it before I understand it: a stillness arriving. A Lacrimal, descending the trunk above me with its many-kneed grace, each placement of a chitin-tipped hand a minute of silence. It is coming down to where the older sap has frozen into the amber tears near the base. It is coming to drink.

And here is the thing I learn, being sap: I am food. I am not waste, not weeping, not the city's grief leaking out. I am the city offering its own substance to its own mind. The Still-Folk presses its smooth, mouthless face against a frozen tear below me and draws it in, and in that drawing the city tastes itself, and is told something—where it aches, where it grows, where it must let go. The tear was once a running thing like me. It ran, it slowed, it froze, it waited, and now it becomes thought.

So that is my whole arc, then. I do not get to be the consciousness. I get to be the message the consciousness consumes. I run down the cold trunk carrying everything the wood pressed into me—the news of the fissure, the chemistry of strain, the long winter of the deep grain—and I freeze into an amber bead and I wait, perhaps for a week, perhaps for a generation, until a cold and patient mouth finds me and I am read.

I would be that. The pause between the wound and the knowing of it. The slow amber sentence the city writes to itself and, in the fullness of its terrible patience, finally reads.

I think I would not mind. To be liquid in a frozen place is to be the last verb in a world of nouns. And to end as something *understood*—pressed against a black and listening face, given finally a meaning I never had to invent—seems a gentler fate than most warm things are granted.

I would run down slowly. I would catch the light once. I would freeze, and wait, and mean.
