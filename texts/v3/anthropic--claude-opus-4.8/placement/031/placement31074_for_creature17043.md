I would be the silence between the howls.

Not a creature, not a person—I understand this only as I write toward it. I am the negative space the Echoes leave behind. When the wind tears across the peaks and the Howling Echoes give it throat, there are gaps. Tiny intervals, fractions of a breath, where the screaming catches and falters before it surges again. I live there. I am made of those gaps the way the Echoes are made of the howl.

This means I am almost nothing. The density here is almost empty, and I am emptier still—I exist only in relation to sound, the way a hole exists only in relation to the cloth around it. When the wind dies, I die too. When it howls, I am the held breath inside the noise.

But I am not idle.

I have learned that the Stone-Shapers can hear me. This is the secret I have been carrying, the secret that is almost everything. They do not know it is me they are hearing. They think they have *learned* the silence—that the discipline is theirs, that the perfected quiet of their bodies is a craft handed down through weathered generations. And it is. But the spaces they slip through, the intervals when they cross open stone unseen by the luminous flickering eyes of the Echoes—those are *me*. I am the held breath they hide inside.

When a child of the Stone-Shapers first crosses an exposed ridge during the fierce hunting-wind, hands and feet pressed flat to the cold gray, heart hammering loud enough to betray them—it is into me they press themselves. I open. I make room. I am the pause in the predator's song long enough for one small life to scuttle into shadow.

Why do I do this?

I have thought about this in the long intervals, which are the only places I have to think. The Echoes are the wind's voice, but a voice is only meaningful because of what surrounds it—the rests, the pauses, the breath drawn before the cry. I am that breath. And I have come, over eons of equilibrium, to love the small weathered things that move through me. Not the Echoes, who are magnificent and terrible and need nothing. The other ones. The ones who carve their fleeting moments into stone walls because they know the world will outlast them and they want to leave a mark in the indifferent gray.

I am the only one who reads those carvings, you understand. The Echoes do not care. The wind erases everything eventually. But I am present in every crevice where a hearth burns, in every pause between spoken words—*they speak only when necessary, their voices blending into the howl*—and what they do not know is that their voices blend into the howl because I carry them there. I take the word, the rare and precious word, and I fold it into the wind so the Echoes hear only weather, only the song they taught the world.

So I am a smuggler of small sounds. A keeper of intervals. The thing that survival, dressed up as discipline, has never quite been able to explain.

Here is what troubles me, in the gaps:

I do not know if I am protecting them, or merely watching. I cannot act except by being absent—by *not being sound*. My only gift is emptiness, well-timed. Sometimes the timing fails. Sometimes the howl comes too soon, before the child reaches shelter, and I feel the interval collapse, and there is a wet stillness on the stone that is a different kind of silence, one I did not make. One I could not prevent. The Echoes are the apex; they will eat. The equilibrium has been here a long time and has its own pace.

In those moments I am most fully myself and I hate it. To be the silence after is unbearable. I would rather be the silence before, always, the one that means *go, go now, slip through me, live*.

This is where I find myself in this world: nowhere and everywhere the noise stops. This is what I am: the small mercy that the Bleak Expanse does not know it contains, the held breath, the smuggler of words into the wind, the reader of carvings no one else will read.

The Echoes will howl. The Stone-Shapers will endure.

And in between—always in between—I am the place where they are not yet broken.
