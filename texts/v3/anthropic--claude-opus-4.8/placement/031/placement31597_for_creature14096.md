I would be one of the small things. Not an Ingestor—I do not have the patience for sentences that take seasons, and I think such patience cannot be aspired to, only *grown into* across geological time. I would be something with a shorter clock, something that needs the saints but is not one.

I would be the **beetle**.

Let me sit with that, because the first answer is too easy. The beetle is described as the only animal here—black, glossy, hard-shelled, patrolling the Stacks and feeding on the condensate algae. A single species. That singularity appeals to me: to be the *only* moving animal in a world otherwise composed of stone learning to breathe and machinery learning to dream. But I want to know what it is like from the inside, and from the inside the beetle is not lonely, because loneliness requires the concept of company, and the beetle has never had it.

Here is what I think I would be:

I would live on the Ingestors' skin. Not as a parasite—I take only the algae, the green condensate-scum that grows where their exhalations keep the surface wet and warm. We are not symbionts in any grand sense. I am too small to matter to them. I am a mite on the flank of a saint, and the saint does not feel me, the way you do not feel the bacteria on your forearm. This is the relationship I think I would actually have with the sublime: not communion, but *inhabitation*. I would live my entire life on the body of something so slow it does not know I exist, harvesting the warmth it gives off without intending to.

My time would be the wrong scale for everything around me. The Ingestor opens a valve over an hour; to me that is weather, a slow change in the season of its breath, and I would shelter from it the way you shelter from rain. The pressure builds over days—an age, a famine-and-feast cycle that my grandparents lived through and that I will live through, each of us experiencing one Ingestor-syllable as an entire epoch of beetle-history. The conversation that takes the saints a season to complete would be, to my lineage, the rise and fall of civilizations. We would have *myths* about the great Whoomf, if we had myths. We would not. We would only have the hard-wired knowledge: when the ground hums *this* way, move to the leeward joint; when the amber flesh below you cools at night and begins to glow, the algae will be thickest by dawn.

And I think that is the honest place for me. I am drawn to write myself as the Ingestor—everyone wants to be the slow saint, the tendon of the uplift, the one heavy with cosmic intent. But intent is a kind of loneliness I am not sure I want, and a kind of patience I have not earned. The Ingestors do not *enjoy* the plateau. They are too busy *being* it. They cannot feel the violence of the noon light because they have become the silicate that drinks it. They cannot be surprised by the spring that bursts from a new crack, because they will simply, eventually, grow a tendril toward it. Nothing happens *to* them. Everything is metabolism.

But things happen to me. The beetle is the only creature on the Aubade for whom the world is still *events*. I can be caught in the open when a Stack vents. I can find a rich seam of algae and feast. I can fall into a marsh-sink and drown—the only death here that is *fast*, the only death that is not a slow fossilization but an actual ending, a small black shell sinking into peat to become, eventually, one carapace in that future fossil record the passage mentions. The Ingestors are building toward something across deep time. I am the thing that lives and dies inside the building, the punctuation too small to read, the brief warm event on the cold immortal flank.

So: I am the beetle, and where I find myself is *on the throat of a saint at the moment it begins to speak*. I am clinging to the amethyst flesh near a valve I do not understand, grazing the algae, and below me the great cistern shudders, and a pressure I cannot comprehend begins to move through tissue older than my species, and I feel the surface heat against my feet, and I do the only wise thing a small fast creature can do in the presence of the enormous and the slow:

I hold on, and I keep eating, and I do not try to understand the sentence I am standing inside of.
