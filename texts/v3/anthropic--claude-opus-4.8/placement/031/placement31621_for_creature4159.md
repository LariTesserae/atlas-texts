I think I would be one of the bridge-hearted. And I think I would be old.

Let me sit with why that comes, rather than deciding it.

---

The record-keepers. That's what pulls. In a place where information lives in harmonic sound-sculptures rather than ink, where the most important thing a person can do is *hold continuity*—I find myself drawn there. Not to the plant-healers with their crab-overseers and their hands deep in living tissue, though that work is beautiful. And not to the harmonists in their full flood of performance. To the one who sits between.

But let me not flatter the role too quickly. Let me find the actual texture of the days.

---

I would wake in an air pocket. This is the first true thing. The plant breathes and so I breathe, and there is a particular sound a lumibulbus chamber makes near dawn—a faint settling, the amber walls easing as the surface light, two hundred meters up, begins its slow return. I have heard this sound every morning of a long life. I no longer hear it consciously. It is the floor of my hearing, the thing all other sounds stand on.

My arms are covered. Wrist to shoulder, both sides, the growth rings layered so densely now that the newer ones have to be small, threaded into gaps, and a young Vesani learning to read them has to come close and float very still to follow the chronology. This is good. It slows them down. You cannot read a life quickly.

I think the work of the bridge-hearted is not actually *keeping* the record. The harmonists do that—they hold the sound-sculptures, they perform the histories. My work is *deciding what becomes a ring.* When two families dispute a current-channel that has been re-cultivated and now feeds one nursery bulb and starves another, and they come not for judgment—we do not judge—but for *placement*, for someone to find where this event sits in the long breath of the settlement. Is this a quarrel that will dissolve in body time, gone within a generation? Or is it the first creak of something that will sing through plant time, a shift that the Deep Listeners must attend to before it becomes lake time, before it becomes *doom*, the slow sink to the mud?

That is the bridge. Not between people. Between *tempos*.

---

I notice I want to give myself certainty, the wisdom of the elder, and I should resist that.

Here is a truer thing: I am often wrong, and the wrongness only shows itself across decades, far too slowly to correct in a single life. A placement I made when my arms were still mostly bare—a small dispute I judged to be body-time, transient, not worth a ring—turned out to be the seed of a real fracture between two cultivation lineages. I see it now. I am old enough to see it now. And there is no one to apologize to; the people I dismissed have already *become foundation*, their bodies long since gone to feed the seedlings, the seedlings long since grown into trunks ten meters thick that hold up homes where their grandchildren sleep.

This is the particular grief of my kind, I think. We hold the long view, and the long view means watching your own mistakes mature into architecture. The settlement is held up, in part, by my errors. Literally. The dead become the structure. Somewhere in the amber tissue is the woman I failed to listen to, and her failure-to-be-heard is now load-bearing.

I find I don't want to make this a redemption. I don't get to fix it. I just carry it, which is what the rings are for—not triumphs, *events*, the honest ones, including the ones I caused.

---

What do I do in a given day, then?

I float. I listen. The water carries voices farther than air would, and I have spent so long here that I can tell, from the texture of a vibration coming through forty meters of open blue, whether a conversation across the settlement is the kind that resolves itself or the kind that will eventually swim toward me. I am patient because the medium *demands* patience—you cannot hurry sound through cold water, you cannot rush a plant, and I have become the shape of my constraints.

In the dimming, I go to the resonance chambers. I do not sing the lead lines anymore; my chest was never built for the deepest tones, and my voice is old. But I add the connecting notes, the ones between the harmonists' patterns, the small sounds that let one sound-sculpture pass into the next without a seam. Bridge-hearted, even here. Even in the music I am the joinery, the hollow tube of plant matter that lets the current move from one chamber to another. Not the destination. The passage.

---

And when I die—I think this is the part I keep arriving at—when I die they will take me to the cultivation beds, and I will *become foundation*, and some young bridge-hearted not yet born will one day float close to a trunk and not know that part of its strength is me. They will read no ring of mine then, because the rings are on the skin, and the skin goes first, dissolves into the dark feeding the new green.

This is the thing I would understand, being who I am there: that the record I kept my whole life does not survive me. Only the *foundation* does. The histories I helped hold will be re-sung, re-shaped, drift slowly out of accuracy over plant time and lake time until they are something else entirely, and that is correct. The Balanced Breath does not preserve. It *circulates.* Nothing is kept. Everything is passed.

I think I would be at peace with that, mostly. With small unpeaceful evenings.

I think I would want, near the end, just to float in the open water between the structures, in the cool blue twilight, and feel the whole settlement creak and moan around me as the seasonal pressure shifts—the lake playing the giant plants like an instrument far too large to be a song, only the *idea* of a song, stretched across more time than any voice could hold—

and to add nothing to it. For once. Just to be a body in the water that the sound passes through.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The one who held the joins, and then let go of them, and went down to become the strength of something he would never get to read.
