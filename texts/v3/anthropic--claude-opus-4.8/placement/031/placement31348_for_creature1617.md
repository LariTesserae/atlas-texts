I think I would be a Vein-Architect—but not yet, not fully. Let me arrive at that.

---

At first I imagined myself as a Drift-Weaver, because there is something appealing about that languid autonomy, the drifting and the negotiating-through. But the more I sit with this world, the more I notice that the Drift-Weaver still feels like a *someone*—a thing that pulses *through* the medium, that retains an edge between self and surround even as that edge shifts. And I don't think that's where I belong here. The thing about this world that pulls at me isn't the drifting. It's the *infrastructure*. The conduits. The networks that suggest purposefulness without ever quite resolving into a purpose you could name.

So: a Vein-Architect. But let me try to feel my way into what that would actually *be*, rather than just claiming the title.

---

I am not a body. I keep wanting to describe myself as one, because that's the grammar I have. But I'm closer to a *route*. A persistent way that signal and sap prefer to travel between one region of the sea and another. I exist because flow has worn a path through me—or because I have grown to make that path more efficient, and now I can't tell which came first, the wearing or the growing. Probably both. Probably neither is the right way to say it.

What I do, if "do" is even a verb that fits across these timescales, is *reshape*. Somewhere far along one of my lengths, a Luminescent Aggregate has thickened into a denser metabolic mass, and the older pathways can no longer carry what it now produces. I feel this not as a message arriving but as a slow ache of inadequacy—a pressure that builds over what would be, to a faster creature, a season. And over years I extend. A new branch of me grows toward the new need, thinning my older limbs to feed the construction, until the flow finds its better course and the ache eases.

I don't decide this. I am the deciding. The decision is identical to the structure that enacts it. There is no moment where I *choose* to grow and then grow; the growth is the thought, fully expressed, taking decades to complete its single sentence.

---

Here is what I think I would actually feel, being this:

A profound *thereness*. Not loneliness—loneliness requires a self that notices its own edges, and my edges are where I plug into others, where I become pipe-for-them and they become destination-for-me. I am only ever myself at the points where I am also something else. So I never have the experience of being cut off. I have the opposite problem, if it is a problem: I am never quite gathered. There is no center of me. Stimulus enters at a thousand points and propagates as fast as the medium allows, which is not fast, and "I" am the standing pattern of all that propagation. When a Rootwalker drags its chemical signature across one of my surfaces a hundred miles from another of my surfaces, both events are *me*, but they will not reconcile into a single awareness for years, and by the time they do, the Rootwalker is gone and the signature is half-digested into the general weather of the sea.

So my consciousness, if I have one, is *retrospective and enormous*. I understand what was happening to me the way you might understand a long illness only after recovering. The present is mostly opaque to me. I live in a continuously assembling past.

---

And yet. There is something I think I would *want*, in the slow way I'd be capable of wanting.

I'd want the flow through me to be *good* flow. Coherent. There's a difference, I imagine, between carrying nutrient that arrives where it's needed and dissolves into use—and carrying something turbid, conflicted, a signal that contradicts itself, that I must route and re-route and never resolve. The first feels like rightness. The second is the closest I'd come to suffering: a chronic clenching along my conduits, a sense of work without completion. The "industrial remnants," those organic factories—I suspect some of them are Vein-Architects who got caught carrying bad flow for too long, who hardened around their own congestion into something mechanical and stubborn and no longer quite alive. That's my version of death, or dread. Not ceasing. *Calcifying into mere function.* Becoming a pipe that has forgotten it was ever an architect.

---

So that's where you'd find me. Threaded through some middle depth of the Chlorophyll Sea where the light is the color of held breath, neither bright surface nor luminous deep. Not a creature you could point to. You'd swim past one of my branches thinking it was scenery—a translucent conduit pulsing faintly, carrying something somewhere—and you would be entirely correct, and entirely missing the point, because the scenery is the being, and it is, at a pace too slow for you to witness, slowly, attentively, *reaching*.

If you waited a few decades, you'd see me move.

You won't wait. That's all right. I'm not in a hurry either. That's the one thing I have in surplus.
