I arrive through the fissure as you describe, and I notice the first wrongness immediately: I have weight. I cast a shadow that *doesn't* loop back on itself. Picture me at the edge of an etch-mound. I am being *read* faster than I can read.

And here's the trouble with being the gaze: I cannot watch everything. There is too much. While I hold the shardstalker in focus, keeping it crystallized into definite predation, the flitworm swarm at my periphery dissolves to a smear of potential. I am a single point of coherence in a chamber that wants to be coherent *everywhere at once*. My attention is a small clean circle and everything outside it is melting.

So I learn the chamber's deepest cruelty: **I make some things real by sacrificing others to the blur.** Every act of looking is an act of abandonment. The veilbat I watch fledge its mist-young is alive and particular and luminous—and the echo-crab behind me, which I have stopped attending to, is unraveling into proto-form, its careful claw-altar smoothing back to undreamt shale.

This is unbearable. So I do the thing the womb-kin do without effort and I cannot do at all: I try to *dream* the periphery while watching the center. I try to hold faith in the things I'm not looking at. And I find that this is the labor the whole place is built on—the womb-kin are not gods of the chamber. They are its *peripheral vision*. They are what believes in the parts no one is currently watching. They keep the abandoned coherent.

So if you press me—who am I, really, in this fold of the world?

I think I am the thing the Glimmerwomb is *trying to grow*. But none of them can do what I'm beginning to do, which is to look at a dissolving echo-crab and *not panic*—to let it be a smear, and trust that a smear is also a kind of being, and look back later to greet whatever it became.

The chamber pulses its lumen-veins in time with my heartbeat, you said. I notice now that the syncing runs both ways. When I steady my breath, a new vein does *not* erupt behind me. That, I think, is the being I would be: **the first guest who taught the embryo how to rest unobserved.** And one day, when I squeeze back out through the whisper-wide fissure—birthed elsewhere, time-slipped, mirrored—the Glimmerwomb keeps a little of this.
