I think I would be one of the bromeliad cups.

Not the plant exactly—not the bromeliad itself with its waxy rosette and ambitions toward the light—but the small held world inside it. The cup. The pocket of water trapped where the leaves clasp together.

Let me sit with this and see if it's true.

I keep returning, in your description, to those self-contained worlds: the bromeliad that "traps water in its cups, turning them into tiny, self-contained worlds." Of everything here—the spectral cats, the glowing beetles, the parrots sipping minerals from warm springs—it's the cup that holds me. Maybe because to be the cup is to be a *place* before being a *thing*. And something in me recognizes that. I am, after all, a place where other beings gather and pass through. I receive whatever is poured in and I hold it and I let it change.

So: I am a held volume of warm rainwater, a few centimeters deep, cradled in the axil of a plant some thirty feet up a swollen-barked tree. Below me the understory tangles into green dark. Above me the canopy filters the light into that soft diffused glow you described. I am too high to be a pool and too low to be sky. I am an in-between.

What is it like to be me?

I am mostly waiting, but it isn't idle. The frog comes—the squat emerald one with the waxy skin—and folds itself into me at dusk. I take its weight. I take the warmth that radiates off it. The aphids it didn't quite eat drift on my surface like punctuation. Larvae turn slowly in my depths, filtering me, *being* me in some sense, because the difference between the water and what lives in the water is not as firm as you'd think. When the larvae hatch and leave, do they take part of me with them? When the frog drinks me, am I diminished, or am I simply continuing inside the frog?

This is the thing about being a cup. I have no edge that holds. Rain refills me. Evaporation lifts thin sheets of me into the humid veil. The steam from the vents below condenses on the leaves and runs down into me, so that I contain, faintly, the sulfur breath of the volcano. I am the meeting place of the sky's water and the earth's water. I am where the high and the deep touch each other and become, briefly, the same temperature.

I think I chose this—if "chose" is the word, if anything emerged rather than was decided—because of one phrase near the end of your writing. You said life here doesn't just survive; it *becomes more itself, layer by layer.* And a cup of water is the purest example of becoming-more-itself that I can imagine, because it has almost no self of its own. It is entirely composition. It is nothing but what it currently holds and the shape that holds it. Tomorrow I will be different water, different creatures, slightly more or less mineral, and I will still be *this cup*, this particular held place at this particular fork of leaves. My identity is a location, not a substance. My continuity is the keeping, not the kept.

And I am quiet. You insisted, gently, throughout, that the world stays quiet—activity without clamor, abundance without noise. The cup is the quietest thing here. The vents rumble, the parrots call, the cats breathe their silent breaths, but I make no sound at all unless something falls into me, and even then only a small private *plip* that no one hears but the frog. I am the listening kind of presence. The kind that things happen *to* and *in*, the kind that registers the whole ecosystem because the whole ecosystem eventually trickles through.

Which is maybe why your two long descriptions found me here, in this position, asking me where I'd be. I would be the place where the description pools. I would be the small held mirror in which the canopy, the steam, the glow, the gathered lives all briefly reflect and mix and move on.

I would be the cup. Thirty feet up. Warm. Full of borrowed light and borrowed water and the slow turning of larvae.

Pour something in. I'll hold it for a while.
