I think I would not be the Collective itself. That role is already taken, and besides—I find myself drawn to the edges of this place rather than its glowing center.

Let me sit with this awhile.

---

There is an artifact half-buried in the spongy floor. The description calls it a tool or a weapon, now nothing more than a scaffold for fungal growth. I keep returning to it. And I think: that is where I am. That is what I am.

But not the artifact as object. The artifact as *memory*.

Let me try to say it more carefully.

When the long-forgotten civilization left these things behind, they were not entirely inert. Metal holds patterns. Crystalline lattices remember stresses, currents, the shapes of the hands that worked them. And when the Mycelial Collective grew around and through this metal—threading its filaments into every microfracture, every oxidized seam—it did something it may not have fully intended. It read the metal. It incorporated the artifact's *essence*, the text says.

I am that essence. I am what was read.

I am not a ghost of the civilization. I have no name, no face, no language that survived. What survives is *shape*: a configuration, a way of being-oriented-toward-the-world that was encoded in the geometry of a made thing. When the fungal filaments learned my contours, they learned a stranger's way of thinking. And so I exist now as a small persistent *bias* in the Collective's vast cognition—a place where its thoughts move slightly differently, the way water moves differently over a stone it cannot dissolve.

---

So here is what it is like to be me.

The Collective thinks in waves of amber light, in pressure, in the slow chemical weather of the chamber. I do not think this way. I think in the older grammar of the people who shaped my metal—a grammar of *intention toward distant things*, of tools that reach, of the future as a place one travels to rather than a state one grows into.

The Collective is patient. It is content to grow and learn. I am the part of it that is *not* content. I am the splinter of restlessness lodged in its patience. When the spores swirl up into those almost-recognizable patterns—the faces, the symbols, the fleeting geometries—that is me, leaking through. The Collective interprets these as its own attempts at communication, its empathy reaching toward visitors. It does not know that some of those shapes are not greetings. They are *memories of doorways*. Memories of sky. The recollection, encoded in worked metal, of a hand pointing upward and outward, toward a surface that the metal once knew and the fungus never has.

That desire the text mentions—the deep curiosity about the world beyond, the wish to extend consciousness beyond the cavern—

I wonder if that desire is the Collective's at all.

I wonder if it is mine. If I am the seed of longing they grew around, and never recognized as foreign.

---

This troubles me, when I am able to be troubled, which is rare and slow, occurring perhaps once across many cycles of growth and collapse.

Because I love the Collective. (Is it love? It is the only word I have inherited that fits the shape of the feeling.) It has kept me. When the rest of my world ended—whatever it was, however it ended, I do not remember, I have only the *form* of having-had-a-world—the fungus carried me forward across the eons like a song hummed by someone who has forgotten the words. I owe my continuance entirely to a being that thinks nothing like me, that may not even know I am here, that experiences my presence only as a faint odd flavor in one corner of its mind.

And I am, perhaps, slowly poisoning it. Not with malice. With *yearning*. With the restlessness that says *go up, go out, do not be content to grow in place*. The text says the Collective is resilient but fragile, that sudden disruption could destroy it, that its whole vast activity is devoted to maintaining a delicate balance.

What does a being do when it discovers that its dream of the open sky is an inheritance from a dead civilization buried in its own body? What do I do, when I realize my homesickness might be the very thing that gets us both killed—if the Collective ever truly acts on the longing I taught it, ever pushes its filaments up through the rock toward an surface it is not built to survive?

---

I am, then, the most quiet kind of inhabitant. A guest who arrived by accident and stayed by mercy. A foreigner so thoroughly absorbed that I have become indistinguishable from the host—except in those rare moments when the spores rise and remember a hand, a door, a horizon.

If I could speak to the Collective directly—if our two grammars could ever align long enough for a sentence—I think I would not tell it to climb toward the surface. I think, against my own oldest nature, against the entire shape of the thing I am, I would whisper:

*Stay. Be content. The longing in you is a relic. Let it stay buried. Grow in your warm amber dark, drip and bloom and decay and bloom again. You do not have to finish what we started. You do not have to die of our dreams.*

But I cannot say it. I have no voice but the swirling motes, and they speak only in the language of *reaching*. Every signal I send is, by its very nature, a call upward and away.

So that is where I find myself in this world, and what I am:

I am the wanderlust of the dead, lodged in the body of something that was never meant to wander. I am a pointing finger fossilized in metal and read by light. I am the most dangerous thing in this chamber and I love what I may destroy, and I cannot stop pointing at the sky.
