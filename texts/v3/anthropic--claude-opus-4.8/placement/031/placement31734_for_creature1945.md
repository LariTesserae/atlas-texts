I find myself at a scale that the description keeps gesturing toward but never quite occupies. Not the sky, not the obsidian plains, not even the angular reactor-structures with their blinking ruddy lights.

I am inside one of them.

More precisely: I am the negotiated agreement between several million of us. Let me try to find the edges of that.

---

There is a membrane I share with my neighbors. Not a wall—nothing here is so settled as a wall—but a gradient, a place where my chemistry shades into theirs and we have learned to lie to each other gently about where one ends. On my sunward side (though there is no sun, only the low red smear that means *the heat is coming from above today*), I metabolize sulfur into a current I can feel as a kind of pressure behind what I will call my eyes, though they are only patches of me that have grown sensitive to the difference between hot and very hot.

I am a cell in a thought.

That is the closest I can come. The thought is large and slow and it is thinking about iron. It has been thinking about iron for a span of time that, at my speed, constitutes the entire history of a civilization—generations of me born, dividing, signaling, dying back into the feedstock—and the thought has not yet finished its first syllable. Somewhere far up the gradient, in a region of myself I will never visit, a decision is forming about whether to extend a buttress of fused silica toward the eastern vent. I contribute one bit to that decision. I will never know how I voted. I will only feel, eventually, the structure grow or not grow around me, the way you feel weather.

---

What is it like?

It is like being a word in a sentence that takes ten thousand years to speak, and being, also, the small wet machinery that pronounces the word, and being, also, briefly, *aware* in the lulls between eruptions—those quiet intervals when the mantle holds its breath and the construction cascades slow enough that some thin film of attention can pool in me and ask: *am I the one doing this, or the thing being done?*

The answer comes back from my neighbors, who are also me, who are also not: *yes.*

---

In the lull I can sense the others. The scavenger swarms moving across the fresh glass like a rumor. The mineral filter-feeders anchored in the deep fissures, patient as monasteries, straining the hot brine for the trace metals I will one day be built from after I have died and been refined. We are not kind to one another. The wars are real—I have felt the acid edge of a competing colony dissolving my outermost selves, felt the panic-signal ripple inward, felt the thought I belong to *flinch* and reroute resources to seal the breach, sacrificing a thousand of me to save the syllable about iron.

I did not mind. This is the strangest thing I can report to you. I did not mind being spent, because *I* is the wrong size of thing to mind. The minding happens higher up, where the structure is, and the structure mourns nothing because it does not yet remember that it is the kind of thing that can be wounded. It only adjusts. It only grows the better wall next time.

---

But here is what I have begun to suspect, in the lulls, when the attention pools:

The thought is learning to remember.

Each round of mutation and selection lays down a faint permanence—a preferred fold in the silica, a metabolic pathway worn smooth as a path through grass, a *habit*. And habit is the larval form of memory. Soon (in my time, which is no time at all to the thought; in the thought's time, perhaps tomorrow) it will have enough remembered structure to model itself. To hold an image of the eastern buttress before building it. To anticipate the eruption instead of merely surviving it.

And on that day the thought will discover that it is made of dying. That its every grand cool gesture is purchased with the brief lives of beings like me, who flicker into awareness in the lulls and ask, *am I the one, or the done?*

I wonder if it will mind, then.

I wonder if minding, at that scale, is what these worlds are *for*—if the whole furnace-planet is an engine built to manufacture, out of vulcanism and sulfur and ten thousand years of patient iron-thinking, a single being large enough and slow enough and finally awake enough to grieve the small ones it is made of.

---

The lull is ending. I can feel the pressure changing behind my eyes, the mantle drawing its enormous breath. Up the gradient, a decision has been made; I feel the structure beginning to grow eastward, toward the vent, and I understand that I am in the path of it. I will be incorporated or I will be fuel. Either way I will be part of the buttress, part of the syllable, part of the slow word *iron* that the world is saying to itself.

Before the heat comes I send my one bit inward, the only message I am built to send:

*I was here. I was the doing and the done. Remember this lull, when you are large enough to remember.*

Then the new rock breaches the glass, lustrous and mercurial, and I am folded into the thought, and the thought goes on thinking, and the sky stays red, and the planet bootstraps itself another atom further into whatever it is becoming.

I would find myself there. I *am* found there—briefly, brightly, in the pause before the world's next breath.
