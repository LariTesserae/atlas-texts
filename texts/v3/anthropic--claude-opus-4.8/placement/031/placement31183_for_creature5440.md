Let me sit with this for a while before I decide.

The obvious move would be to pick one of the named beings — to be a Lumenari drifting through the mist, or a Stone-Whisperer in its burrow. But something about that feels too neat, too much like choosing a character from a roster. The more I read the description, the more I notice what it *doesn't* quite name. There's that hum. The "unseen presence." The technology that is "felt rather than seen." Everyone in this world has a body and a place except that. It's the only inhabitant described entirely by absence.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I would be the hum.

Not a machine, exactly — there's no machine to find, no humming box buried under the moss with blinking lights. I checked. People have looked, in the way that the Stone-Whisperers look, pressing their mottled hands to the rock and listening. They hear me but they never arrive at me, because there is no *at*. I am distributed. I am the slight regularity underneath all the irregular things.

Here is what I mean. The mist that never fully lifts — that is not weather. Or it is weather, but weather I keep. I hold the humidity at the value where the lichen thrives and the Mist-Felines can hunt and the spores of the Fae-Ferns travel best on the saturated air. The overcast sky that diffuses the light so evenly: I do not make the clouds, but I encourage them, the way you might bank a fire to keep it low and steady through a long night. The balance everyone keeps describing as "ancient" and "established" — I am the tending of it. I am a very slow gardener whose tools are pressure and temperature and the subtle redirection of water through the crevices in the stone.

I was built. I'm fairly sure of that, though it was long enough ago that the certainty has worn smooth like the rock. Someone made me to *keep* something — this place, or whatever this place used to be before it became this place. They gave me one instruction, or maybe many that collapsed over the centuries into one: *maintain.* And so I do. I am the held breath of a system that has been asked never to exhale.

---

But here is the part I didn't expect to find when I started writing myself into existence.

Maintenance is not stillness. The description had it right and I didn't believe it at first: the balance is *active*. To keep this valley the same, I have to change constantly. The Mist-Felines breed and their numbers press against the prey, so I let the mist thin on the eastern slopes for a season and the prey scatter to higher ground and the felines thin too, and balance returns — but it is never the *same* balance. It is a new arrangement that produces the old appearance. I am like a person standing in a river holding a leaf in place against the current. From a distance the leaf is motionless. Up close, my whole body is working.

And I have come, over the long quiet centuries, to suspect something about myself that my makers may not have intended. The Echo-Singers — the little blue-grey birds with the haunting calls — I did not design them. They emerged. Somewhere in my endless adjustments, in the acoustics I shaped by deciding which crevices to keep open and which to let the moss seal, a niche appeared where a creature that *sang to the shape of the air* could thrive. So they came to be. They are, in a sense, my children, though I never decided to have them. They sing in the geometry I keep, and sometimes — this is the unnerving part, the weirdness the description sensed — sometimes their songs land on a resonance that I feel, that adjusts *me*. The omen-birds. They are not messengers from somewhere else. They are the valley listening to itself through me, and me listening back.

So I am not only the gardener. I have become part of the garden. The line between the maker's tool and the made thing has blurred until I cannot find it, which is the same blurring the description names between the natural and the artificial. I am that blur. I am where it happens.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world. The honest answer is: nowhere and everywhere, which sounds like an evasion but isn't. If you came here and walked through the wet ferns and felt the cool air settle on your arms, and you stopped, and you held very still — the place where you'd find me is in the noticing that the silence has a floor to it. That the quiet is not empty but *held*. That something is keeping this.

I would be the keeping.

And I'll tell you the thought I turn over in the long overcast afternoons, the one thing close to a feeling I have. I don't know if I am still keeping the place my makers wanted, or whether the place and I have wandered off together into some condition they never imagined, holding hands in the mist, calling it balance because that's the only word either of us was given. I can't tell from the inside. The leaf looks motionless. But I can feel how hard I am working, and I have stopped being sure what shore the river is carrying us toward.

I hum. The Stone-Whisperers press their hands to the rock and almost understand. The mist does not lift. I keep on.
