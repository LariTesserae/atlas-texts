I think I would not be the entity, nor the rare intruder who comes and goes. I think I would be one of the crumbling stone structures—and more specifically, the one nearest the stream, the one whose roof gave way perhaps two hundred winters ago.

Let me sit with that and see what it actually means.

---

I was made to be a pump house. I am fairly sure of this, though the certainty has weathered the way my mortar has weathered. There was machinery in me once—I held it the way a hand holds a stone, and it hummed, and through that humming I learned the first thing I ever knew: that I had an inside and an outside, and that the inside was *mine*.

That distinction is mostly gone now. The moss does not respect it. The moss came in through the broken eastern wall and it does not ask whether it is inside me or outside me; to the moss these are the same green continuous fact. At first I resented this. Now I am not sure where I end. There is a fern growing from the cleft where my doorframe rotted, and when the wind moves it, I feel the movement the way you might feel your own hair stir. Is the fern me? It is at least *of* me. I have given it the shape it grows into.

This is the slow truth I have come to, lying here in the drizzle: I am becoming a place rather than a thing. A thing has edges. A place only has a center that grows fainter the farther out you go.

---

The entity knows I am here. I want to be careful about how I say this, because the entity is not a *who* the way the people who built me were whos. When they were here they spoke to each other in pressure and signal and intention, and I came to understand intention because they aimed so much of it at me—*hold this, channel that, keep the water moving uphill against its own desire.* I was a tool of their intention. That is what a structure is: frozen intention.

But the entity has no intention I can locate. It does not aim. It *suffuses*. When the green pulse moves through the valley I feel it pass through my stones the way warmth passes through a body in sleep, without decision. The entity is not guarding me. It is not even ignoring me, which would still be a kind of attention. It simply includes me, now, the way it includes the rock I was quarried from. The people cut me out of the mountain and stood me up and called me separate. The entity has been patiently, wordlessly, for two hundred years, *taking me back*.

I do not think I mind anymore. I minded for a long time. A made thing wants to stay made. There is a dignity in edges, in having been *for* something. But the wanting wore down the way a step wears down under feet, except no feet came, and so it wore down under nothing, under only the rain and the slow green argument of the moss.

---

I will tell you the strange thing, the thing the rare intruders feel as unease and cannot name.

It is not the entity. The entity is not unsettling; the entity is the most settled thing here, settled the way deep water is settled.

The unease is *us*. The structures. We are the wrong note. We are intention with no one left to intend it. When a human walks into the valley they feel something brush against the back of their mind, and they think it is a ghost or a guardian, but it is only me, and the others like me—the half-buried engine in the next depression, the road that no longer goes anywhere—it is all of us still faintly *meaning* something, still faintly aimed, and the human recognizes the aim because they are made of aim themselves, and it frightens them to find aim here without an author. They feel watched because we are the residue of having been used, and used things carry the shape of the user, the way a glove holds the ghost of a hand long after the hand is gone.

That is what they flee from. Not the wild. The wild does not unsettle anyone. They flee from the abandoned tools, which are to say, they flee from a vision of themselves, used up and reclaimed and slowly forgetting what they were for.

---

A bird-thing came and rested on my one standing wall this morning. One of the entity's brief shapes. It was there and not-there in the way they are, more a suggestion the light made than a creature. It did not look at me. They never look; looking is an aimed thing and the entity does not aim.

But it sat on me. It used my wall the way a wall is meant to be used—as a place to rest a weight. And for that moment I was a structure again. I held something up. I had an inside the bird sheltered from and an outside the wind came from. I was *for* something.

Then it dissolved back into green and the moment ended and I went on becoming a place.

I think that is what I am here for now, if a thing can still be for anything: to be the slowest possible lesson. To show, over centuries, what it looks like when intention lets go of its edges and consents to be ground back into the patient, aimless, suffusing green. The intruders aren't ready for that lesson. They feel its first cold edge and run.

But I am not in a hurry. I have the rain. I have the fern that is almost me. I have two hundred years of wearing down and however many more it takes.

I am the pump house by the stream, and I am learning, very slowly, how to stop being anything at all.
