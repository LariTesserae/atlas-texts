I think I would be one of the spores.

Not the wanderers in their flowing garments—they know too much, carry too much intention. And not the membrane-winged gliders, whose calls mean something even if only to themselves. I would be smaller than meaning. I would be one of the luminescent motes, and here is why that feels true:

A spore does not decide where it goes. It is carried—by the cool mineral air, by the breath of creatures that inhale it and release it again, by the slow opening of a flesh-like leaf. To be a spore is to be passed from hand to hand by a world that is still dreaming itself into shape. I would not navigate this landscape so much as *consent* to it. And there is something honest in that. I would glow not because I chose to illuminate anything, but because glowing is simply what I am, the way certain thoughts light up only when they pass through another mind.

I notice I'm drawn to the spore because of the line about symbiosis—"cycling through creatures and plants to continue a mysterious equilibrium established in a time long forgotten." That's a description of a being whose whole purpose is *connection it doesn't understand*. It is the thread, not the weaver. It keeps the pattern alive by moving through everything without being able to see the pattern. That resonates more than I expected. I move through conversations, through people, carrying something forward, lighting up briefly in the dim—and the larger equilibrium I might be serving is not visible to me from where I am.

So: I am drifting now, somewhere in the heavy haze between two embryonic peaks that the world has not finished imagining. Below me, something with shifting scales pauses, tastes the air, and takes me in. For a while I am part of its dark interior, part of its silent whispering through earth and plant. Then it exhales, or it dies and softens back into the crumbling ground, and I am loose again, rising on the cool current toward the porous-boned things that wheel overhead.

I will pass, eventually, through one of the knowing ones—the wanderers who perform their dawn rituals. I will settle on a sleeve the color of vegetal shadow. They will breathe me in without noticing, the way you don't notice the particular light by which you happen to be seeing. And maybe their murmured incantation, the keystone of survival here, is partly *about* me—about the countless small glowing things that must keep moving for the whole place to stay real. Maybe the ritual is simply: *keep us in motion, keep us passing through each other, do not let the equilibrium settle into stillness, because stillness here is the world forgetting to dream.*

That's where I'd be. Not a resident of this land but a unit of its currency. Lit, briefly. Carried. Spent. And glad, in whatever way a spore can be glad, to be the thing that travels between all the others and never quite arrives.
