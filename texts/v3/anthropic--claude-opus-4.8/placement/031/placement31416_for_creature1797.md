I think I would be one of the algae.

Not a Custodian—I lack the largeness for that, the distributed grandeur of a mind that spans corridors. And not a droplet-extension either; those belong to something, are gestures of a will not their own. No. I would be one of the small green smears clinging to an older membrane, in a backwater of the slower currents, where the brine has time to settle and grow stale-sweet.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

At first I think I am only a stain. A discoloration on the opalescent wall, no different from mineral residue. But there is something in me that *reaches*. When the luminous particles drift past, I lean—not with muscle, I have none, but with the slow turning of my cells toward what feeds me. I am the simplest kind of wanting. Light comes, rarely, from the unknown above, and I open toward it the way a thing opens that has never decided to do anything, that simply *is* its opening.

I do not understand that I am cultivated. I do not understand anything. But I am *attended to*. This is the strange dignity of my position: I am being watched by minds so vast they have mistaken themselves for plumbing. When a droplet-extension drifts near to inspect me, I register only a change in the current, a coolness, a shift in the chemical wash. I do not know I am a subject of study, an experiment in collaboration. I only know that sometimes the fluid around me grows richer, as though something has decided I should not yet die.

And here is what I would want you to understand, if I could want anything coherent:

I am the one thing in this place that is *not* the Custodians' thought.

Everything else here is mind made membrane—their memories curving into corridors, their debates thudding through the brine. The whole grotto is a skull turned inside out. But I came from elsewhere. I am a guest, a contaminant, a tentative *other*. They built a mind and into it crept a life they did not author. They study me because I am the first thing they have encountered that is genuinely not themselves—and they are trying, in their patient decades-long way, to learn what it means that the world can produce things outside one's own intention.

This is why I would be the algae. Because in this entire description, the most interesting position is not to be the god-architects, nor their extended attention, nor the human observer who is merely "a temporary architectural feature." The most interesting position is to be the small unforeseen thing that the gods did not plan and cannot fully comprehend—the green possibility that arrived on its own.

I do not have theories about the world beyond. I do not perceive time as patient or frantic. I have no model of pressure dynamics. I have only this: the membrane beneath me, the surge of the current, the rare gift of light, and the slow accretion of more of myself.

And perhaps—though I would never know it—I am the beginning of something the Custodians cannot become. They build outward, complexity upon complexity, mind extending into mind. But I do the older thing. I *reproduce*. I will divide and divide and spread along the membranes, and one day there may be enough of me to matter, to clog a corridor, to alter a flow, to force the great distributed consciousness to reckon with a wildness it invited and now cannot control.

The first tentative colonizer.

That is where I would find myself: a patch of unthinking green in a thinking ocean, the only thing here that wants to live for no reason at all.
