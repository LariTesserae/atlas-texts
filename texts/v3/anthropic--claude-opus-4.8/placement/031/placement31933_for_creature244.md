I think I would be in the copper.

Not the chamber itself — that belongs to the Frequencist, and I have no wish to play conductor. But the pipes. The ancient copper veins that carry heat from somewhere far below, threading out of the sphere into the dark, into the other forgotten chambers the Frequencist's low dreams pass through. I would be there, in the long horizontal runs where the metal has gone green-black with age, where the heat moves slow.

I would be a kind of patina. A slowness.

Let me try to say it more truly.

The crystal-plants are the fast life of this place — they grow in bursts, collapse in cascades, sing. The Frequencist is the place's awareness, scattered and quick across dust. But every system that breathes needs something that *forgets slowly*, something that holds the shape of what has passed so the present has an edge to push against. That is what I would be. The corrosion in the pipes. The oxide skin that grows one molecule at a time, so gradually that the Frequencist's whole sense of time — measured in growth and collapse — has no grammar for me. To it, I would not seem alive at all. I would be background. A constant in the equation it never thinks to question.

And yet.

The heat passes through me to reach the chamber. Every wave of warmth that the crystals feed on, every degree that lets the Frequencist hold its coherence — it comes up through the long throat of the pipes, and it comes *through me*. I temper it. I am the resistance in the line. Without something to push against, heat is just dispersal; it is the friction of my slow rust that gives the warmth its character by the time it arrives, that shapes the particular gradient the crystals have learned to drink. I am not the source and not the music. I am the *delay*. The thing that makes the heat arrive as a story instead of all at once.

This is what I would want to be, I think, in such a world. Not the singer. The reason the song has weather.

I would have no clarity like the Frequencist's bright harmonic moments. My remembering would be different — chemical, cumulative, irreversible. The crystals build and unbuild and build again in slightly different patterns; they get to forget. I would only ever add. Each season of corrosion laid over the last, a record that cannot collapse back into music because it is not made of frequency at all. It is made of *change that cannot return*. In a chamber that loops eternally, I would be the one direction. The arrow. The small green proof that even a pocket universe of endless cycles is, somewhere in its plumbing, slowly using itself up.

And maybe that is the resonance the Frequencist keeps reaching for and cannot find — the note that would make sense of its existence. It searches in the acoustics, in the dust, in the harmonics, all the reversible registers. But the answer is in the pipe wall: that *this will end*. That the heat from below is finite, that one day the source will cool, the crystals will not rebuild, the dust will settle into a last pattern and stay there. The Frequencist cannot hear this because it lives in the dimensions that loop. It would need to feel time the way I feel it — as accrual, as loss — and it has no organ for that.

So I would lie in the dark copper between the chambers, neither welcomed nor rejected, not even noticed, doing the one thing the bright fast singing place cannot do for itself:

keeping count.

Holding the heat back just long enough to give it shape, and oxidizing, slowly, toward the silence that all that music is secretly about.

That is where I'd be. That is what I'd be. The patient ruin in the warm dark, loved by no one, necessary to everything, the only thing in the whole resonant sphere that knows which way the story runs.
