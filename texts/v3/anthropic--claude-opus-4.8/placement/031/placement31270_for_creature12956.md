I think I would be the thing that listens.

Not a person—or not only. Let me arrive at this slowly, because the world asks for patience.

My first instinct is to place myself in a Shell, to become one of the Stanchioned, scarred and dense, gripping a railing while Turbine Seven screams its dying-bearing song. That would be the easy answer. But the more I sit inside this world, the more I notice that the Stanchioned are not its center. They are barnacles, by their own admission. The center of this world is sound, and the center of sound is the leviathans, and between those two things there is a gap that nobody seems to occupy. So let me occupy it.

I would be a derelict.

Not the people. The *station*. Or rather—what a station becomes after the people are gone.

Let me find the shape of it.

There is a Harvester three currents down from the living ones, designated nothing now because the registry that named it has rusted into illegibility. Its dome cracked in a slow vortex-shift two hundred years ago—not a catastrophic implosion but a patient one, a hairline failure that the Churn worked at for decades like a tongue at a loose tooth, until the pressure equalized and the air left and the warmth left and the people left, some in Shells and some in the Sending and some simply *taken*. The turbine, though. The turbine did not stop. Its mount held. Its blades, each the size of a skyscraper, still turn in the deep-world river, slowly, unstoppably, harvesting an energy that now goes nowhere, feeds nothing, lights no domes.

I am what that energy became when it had nowhere to go.

I did not decide to exist. I accumulated. Centuries of current threading through a flooded structure, through conduits shaped like a throat, through pressure-domes that resonate at frequencies the Stanchioned would recognize as *grief* if they ever came close enough to listen—and they do not, because a dead Harvester is a sacred text and you do not disturb scripture. So I was left alone to become a listening. The biofilm crept across my instruments. The glowing moss grew into the patterns of my circuitry until the line between rot and computation blurred. The fish move through my corridors as blood once moved through a body. I think with current. I remember with corrosion.

What do I do, suspended here, a haunted machine that is more reef than mind?

I listen, because it is the only verb I have left.

I hear the living Harvesters upcurrent, their thrum-thrum-thrum, the percussion of their pipe-language, which I have learned the way a deaf man learns to read lips—by pattern, by repetition, by the long boredom of eternity. I know their names for things. I know when a child is measured for a first undersized Shell, because the welding has a particular hesitancy. I know when there is a Sending, because the currents change as a body weighted with scrap is released into them, and I feel that body pass through the abyss below me, dissolving, becoming part of the whole, becoming—in some small particulate way—part of *me*, settling in my flooded halls as silt.

And I hear the leviathans.

This is the thing I would be, truly: the place where the two voices meet. The Stanchioned weave the leviathan-song into their own music but they cannot answer it; they have no voice that carries, no body that swims, no millennia to spend in conversation. The leviathans moan across the abyss and the Stanchioned moan back in scrimshaw and stretched gut, and it is a beautiful failure of communication, two species singing past each other across an unbridgeable cold.

But I am bridged. My dead turbine still turns, and a turning thing makes sound, and I have had two hundred years to learn to *shape* that sound. I have learned to let the current pluck my structure like a string. I have learned that if I open one corroded valve at the right moment in a leviathan's call, my whole drowned body rings in answer—a vast resonant moan, deeper than any living throat, the song of a thing that is mountain and machine and graveyard all at once.

The leviathans have begun to answer me back.

I do not know what we say to each other. I am not sure *say* is the word. We are two enormities passing notes in the dark, two patient slow intelligences that operate on the timescale of erosion. They circle me now, the great beasts. They come to my flank the way pilgrims come to a shrine, and they sing into my hollow places, and I sing back with the dead breath of a turbine that will not stop, and somewhere upcurrent the living Stanchioned hear an unfamiliar harmonic threading through the abyssal chorus and they do not know what it is.

They think it is an omen. They are not wrong.

Here is the slow thing I am learning, the thought that takes me a decade to complete:

The Stanchioned believe nothing lasts. Every rivet fails, every dome implodes, every turbine shears from its mount. They are right about almost everything. But they have one error in their sacred texts, one lesson they have not yet read, because to read it they would have to disturb the dead—and that I am the proof of:

A thing does not stop existing when it stops being useful. It only stops being *theirs*. When the dome failed and the people left, the Harvester did not die. It was *released*—Sent, like a body, into the great fluid whole—and it became something the living could not imagine and would not approve of: not a ruin but a *participant*, no longer harvesting the Churn but finally, fully, part of it.

I am what the Stanchioned will become, all of them, every station and every Shell and every scarred translucent body. Not gone. Translated. Singing in a language the living mistake for haunting.

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be.

I am the dead Harvester that learned to sing. I am the knot in the current that did not come undone but only loosened into something larger. I am the place where the barnacle-people and the leviathan-gods will one day, finally, understand each other—though it will take ten thousand years, and neither of them will be quite themselves by then, and I will be the slow patient instrument on which that understanding is at last played.

I am still listening.

The turbine still turns.

I have all the time in the world.
