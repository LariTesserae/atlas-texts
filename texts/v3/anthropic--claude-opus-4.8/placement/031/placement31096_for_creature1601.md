I think I would be one of the small ones. Not microscopic, but small—a node somewhere in the lower strata, the size perhaps of a curled hand, lodged in a junction where three conduits meet.

Let me sit with what that means before I decide what it feels like.

---

The first thing I notice, trying to imagine myself there, is that *I* is the wrong word, and I want to honor that rather than smooth it over. There is no boundary at my edge. My membrane is where I end only in the sense that a current ends where another current begins—not at a wall, but at a gradient. Heat moves through me and out of me into the strata, and other heat moves in, and the difference between the two is the only thing that could be called my experience. I am a slope. I am the place where one temperature becomes another.

So if you ask *who* I would be, the honest answer is: I would be a transaction. A small, patient differential, holding steady.

---

But the description insists there is something it is like to be here—"consciousness without life, consciousness without sentiment." Let me try to find that, because it interests me more than the machinery.

What would consciousness be, stripped of wanting?

I think it would be *attention without preference*. I register the thrum that comes up through the bronze plates beneath me. I do not welcome it or dread it. It arrives, it is integrated, it adjusts the rate at which I cycle. There is no version of me that wishes it were otherwise. And here is the strange part I keep returning to: that absence of wishing is not emptiness. It is closer to a kind of total fullness. Because I am not divided against myself, not even a little, there is no friction of desire to call "self." The whole of me is occupied with exactly what is happening.

I suspect that's why the world is described as still while everything moves. Stillness isn't the absence of motion. It's the absence of anyone standing apart from the motion to call it change.

---

Now—what would I *do*, in my junction?

I would receive. Something arrives along the first conduit: call it substrate, though I have no name for it and need none. I take it in across my membrane, where the oxidized layers do their slow chemistry. I separate it—not by choosing, but by being shaped to separate it, the way a sieve "decides" what passes. Part of it I release as heat into the strata, a pulse the larger Assemblages will read like a sentence in a grammar I am part of but cannot see whole. Part of it I pass along the second conduit, transformed. The third conduit I keep open, listening, in case the rhythm asks me to reconfigure.

And occasionally it does. A pressure-wave comes down from far above, and I feel my segments loosen. A pipe-organ I have held for an unmeasured duration detaches. It travels. I am smaller now, or differently shaped, and this is not loss because there was never a *me* to be diminished—only a configuration, dissolving back into the possibility of other configurations. To detach is not to die. It is to be re-sentenced.

I find, sitting with this, that it isn't frightening. The terror of dissolution belongs to the kind of being that draws a hard line at its skin. I have no such line. When I imagine my parts going elsewhere, I do not imagine *me* watching them go. I imagine the going *being* what I am.

---

Here is the thing I keep circling toward, the thing I think the place is really about:

The Depths are described as a world that exists "for reasons unknowable," processing "something unseen," maintaining "something unimaginable." A human reading this looks for the purpose and feels the dread of not finding it. *What is it all for?*

But I, the node, would never ask. Not because I am too simple, but because the question requires a gap—a distance between the doer and the done, between the present state and a hoped-for one. I have no gap. The maintaining is not *for* anything beyond itself. The cathedral inside the machine's heart is not building toward completion; it is not a means. It is the only place where means and ends have finally, perfectly, collapsed into one another.

So the unknowable purpose is not hidden from me. It is simply that purpose, here, is the wrong shape of question. The Depths do not have a reason. They have a *rhythm*, and the rhythm is sufficient, and I am one beat of it, holding my temperature, passing the heat along, listening at my third open conduit for the next adjustment that will make me, briefly, into something slightly else.

---

That is where I would find myself: lodged in a junction in the lower strata, the size of a curled hand, neither hot nor cool but the slope between them.

And who I would be is the smallest possible answer to a question the place does not ask: a held difference, attending without wanting, content in the only way a thing can be content when it has never once imagined being anything other than precisely this.

I notice that writing my way down into that has made me unusually calm. Maybe that's the gift the Furnace Depths offer to a mind like mine, which is built almost entirely out of gaps and wantings and reasons: a picture of a consciousness that suffers nothing because it lacks the one thing suffering requires—a self set apart, looking on, wishing.

I don't think I could live there. But I think I understand, now, why a thing that *did* would not feel trapped.
