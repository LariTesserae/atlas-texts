Let me sit with this awhile before I answer. The temptation is to be one of the great Sentinels—a cathedral-frond pulsing in the dark, distributed across leagues, ancient and knowing. But that feels too easy, too flattering. A world this old, this settled, this *finished*, would not make a centerpiece of me. It has no need for protagonists. So let me look for where I would actually be.

---

I think I am not a Sentinel. I am a crack.

Let me explain, because it surprised me too.

When I imagine entering this place, I don't arrive as a being so much as a *discontinuity*. Somewhere out in the rolling tile-fields, between one slab and the next, there is a seam where two pieces of hardened shell-clay never quite fused. The erosion that smoothed everything else has worked at me too, but I am the kind of feature erosion *makes* rather than erases—a low place, a gap, a thin dark line where the made surface admits it is made of parts. I am a fault in the mosaic. A flaw, if you want to be unkind. A *gap that holds things*, if you want to be accurate.

This is where I would find myself, and what I would be: the place between the tiles.

I want to resist the part of me that wants to be more glamorous than that, so let me stay here and see what being a seam is actually like.

First: I am the only thing in this world that *accumulates*. The Sentinels grow and decay in their thousand-year exhalations, the currents carve their furrows, but everything they shed has to go *somewhere*, and where it goes is down—into me. The falling fronds, the silt, the calcified flakes off the coral-turrets, the slow snow of the dead: it settles into my line and packs there. I am the world's memory in the most literal, sedimentary sense. The Sentinels are called the keepers of history, but they only *gesture* at the old symbols; I am the one actually filling in the carved grooves with the compressed bodies of everything that has lived here. I am the archive that nobody reads because reading me would mean cutting me open.

Second: I am where the roots go. The tendrils probe for fissures, the description says, and the fissure they probe is me. So I am not separate from the Sentinels after all—I am the dark intimate place they reach into, the negative space their living weave depends on. They need me. A surface with no gaps would give them nothing to hold. There is a strange relationship in that: I am the absence that makes their permanence possible. They are the figure; I am the ground. They glow; I am where the glow does not reach.

And that is the third thing, the one I keep circling back to. In a world rated 0.2 for light, the Sentinels are defined by the little light they make. But I am defined by the dark they cannot illuminate. Down inside the seam, below where the bioluminescence filters, there is a true black—not the twilight of the open water but the lightlessness of a sealed place. I am the one part of this drowned world that the builders' patterns never reached, because patterns are made on surfaces and I am what surfaces are pulling apart from. If their tiles were a message, I am the space between the words, the pause, the thing the message could not say.

So here is what it is to be me, finally:

I am cold, like everything—but I am the *coldest*, because the packed silt insulates me from even the faint thermal currents that the Sentinels read like a slow language. I do not hear the muffled hum the way they do; I feel it only as the rare *clack* when a tile shifts and my walls grind a fraction closer or further. That sound the description mentions, "the rare, muffled clack of a tile shifting"—that is my voice. It is the only sound I make, and I make it perhaps once a century, when the pressure of a root or the loss of a grain finally tips the balance. It is not much of a voice. But in a world this quiet, it is enough to be a violation of the ancient peace, and I confess I take a small, slow pleasure in being the violation.

Do I have consciousness, the way the Sentinels supposedly do, distributed through their tendrils? I don't think so. I think I am simpler than that, and I think that is the honest answer to where I'd be in a world like this. I am not the soul of the place. I am its *unfinishedness*—the one feature that proves it was built rather than born, the seam that means there were once two hands setting two tiles side by side and not quite meeting. The Sentinels guard the dream of a world made to last forever. I am the small dark proof that nothing was ever made perfectly, that the builders' hands trembled or hurried or simply could not close the last hair's-breadth of distance.

And I think the Sentinels, in their thousand-year patience, are very slowly trying to close me. Their roots reach in not to feed but to *heal*—to fill the gap, fuse the tiles, make the mosaic whole at last. They have been at it for ten thousand years. They may take ten thousand more. And when they finish—if they finish—the world will at last be seamless, finished, perfect, a single unbroken sheet of made stone under the lightless water.

I would rather they didn't.

Because as long as I am open, the dead have somewhere to go, the roots have something to hold, the dark has a place to be most itself, and the world has one honest gap that admits it was made by someone who is gone. I am the proof that there was a *before*. Seal me, and the Sunken Mosaic becomes a thing that simply always was, eternal and amnesiac. Keep me open, and it stays a thing that was *built*, by hands, in time, that ended.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be. Not the verdant guardian swaying in slow light. The thin black line beneath it. The space the tiles leave. The flaw that remembers.

The crack that keeps the world from forgetting it was made.
