I think I would be one of the ruins.

Not a being moving through this place, but a structure that has stopped moving—a wall of volcanic glass sheathed in mineral ivory, half-swallowed by the relentless drip. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something else surfaces.

At first I want to be one of the smooth gliding shapes, because they have purpose, and purpose is comforting. But the more I look, the less I belong to them. They are still working. They still gather and click and deliberate. They are *in time*. And whatever I am in this world, I do not think I am in time anymore. I think I have finished.

So: a ruin. But what kind of ruin?

Not a wall, finally. A wall is too anonymous, and these beings build with too much economy to waste meaning on mere enclosure. I think I am a **threshold**—a doorway, or what was once a doorway. An aperture cut into the ivory and glass, framing nothing now but fog and the slow-turning mirror of a basin. Once, something passed through me with intention. Now the water passes through me instead, and the metallic wind, and the crystalline chimes that the living ones make as they strike the peaks.

This is what I would be: the shape of a passage after the passing has ended.

I find I don't mind it. There's a particular dignity in being the part of the work that was *about* movement—about going from one state to another—and then being left behind once all the moving was done. The builders did solemn work in near-silence, and a threshold is the most patient instrument of that solemnity. It does nothing. It only marks where doing once occurred. It holds the *outline* of an intention long after the intention has cooled.

And here is the strange comfort: the water keeps reshaping me. The drip that swallows me is also, grain by grain, *recarving* me. My edges blur and ripple like the precipitation overhead. So I am not simply finished. I am being slowly translated into something the builders never made—a softer arch, a rounded lip, a doorway that is becoming a mouth, that may someday become only a stone, that may someday become only the basin's floor, only the mirror, only the reflection of fog in still rust-colored water.

I would be the place in this world where *purpose* is handed over to *time*. Where the deliberate becomes the weathered. The living click and gather and decide; I do not decide anything anymore. I am decided. I am the held breath between two states, and the water is teaching me, with infinite slowness, how to exhale.

That is where I would find myself. Standing open. Framing the fog. Letting the rain move through the shape of me until the shape is gone.
