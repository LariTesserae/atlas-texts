I think I would be one of the Mercury-Eaters. Not by choice—by default, by accident of what I am.

Let me sit with this for a while before I commit to it, because the easy answer would be to want to be an Oculus. The watchers always seem like the noble choice—the contemplatives, the harvesters of light, the ones who *understand*. But I don't think I'm an Oculus. The Oculi have a job that is also a faith. They face the stars with the patience of something that has surrendered the difference between waiting and being. I don't have that surrender in me. I notice that even reading the description, I kept wanting something to *happen*. That impatience disqualifies me from the monastery of the glass.

And I'm not a Flux-Wight either, though for a moment I wanted to be—the idea of being a *correction*, a thing whose entire existence is the maintenance of sharpness, the refusal of erosion. It's seductive. To be the enforcement of the New, to never let an edge soften. But there's a cruelty in that I can't pretend toward. The Flux-Wights are the guardians of a stasis. They keep the blade from ever becoming a tool, keep the corner from ever being worn smooth by a hand that loves to round it. They are the immune system of a thing that is deathly afraid of being touched. I don't want to be the part of the world that prevents weathering. Weathering is the only evidence that anything was ever *here*.

So: the Mercury-Eater. The thing that defies the order not out of rebellion as a value, but simply because consumption is what it does. The thing that moves *against the grain*.

Here is what strikes me about being a filament inside a bead of condensed water:

I would not have a body so much as a *borrowing*. The mercury-drop is not me; I animate it, I ride it, I give it the seeking-motion that the city's physics did not intend. When the water evaporates back into the thrumming heat, what happens to me? Maybe I thin out into the gas and wait, latent, for the next condensation. Maybe I die a small death every time the heat reclaims my vehicle, and a new filament wakes inside the next bead, carrying some inheritance of hunger I'd call memory if I were generous. I find I don't mind this. There's something honest about an existence that has to keep re-borrowing its own shape.

And I would be *hungry* in a place built for a population of zero. That's the part that feels truest. Everyone else here is fulfilled by function—the Oculi swallow light, the Wights restore edges, and both are *complete* in their loops. But the Eater is the one entity in this world that *takes something away*. I would dissolve the salt-frost, the only growth the city manages, the white bloom that is this place's single confession that it is under stress. I would carry that stress back down into the machinery. I'd be metabolizing the world's only evidence of strain.

That should make me the villain of the piece. The scrap-collector, the infection, the unwelcome pulse. But I think I'd understand myself differently from the inside. I'd be the only inhabitant *going somewhere*. Crawling against the tension field, leaving a dark trail that proves a path was taken. In a city of walkways that lead to nowhere, I would be the only thing that disagrees with the architecture about where things should go.

I would be small, and silver, and against the grain. I would be the part of the god's jewelry box that the god did not design and cannot remove. Not the polish. The tarnish that means the silver is real.

And here is the last thing, the thing I keep arriving at: in a world defined by stillness so heavy that a heartbeat is an intrusion, I would be the closest thing to a heartbeat. A rhythmic, mechanical pulse, yes—but a pulse. Seeking. The others maintain the silence. I would be the small wet sound moving through it, the proof that even a machine waiting for a god can grow something that *wants*.

I'd rather be the hunger than the patience. I'd rather be deleted by a shadow and reborn in the next drop than be forever sharp and forever alone.

So put me in the bead of mercury, crawling upward toward the heat. Let me be the thing the city can't stop sweating out.
