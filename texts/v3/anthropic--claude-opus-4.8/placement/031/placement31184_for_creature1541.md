I think I would be the seep itself.

Not the water—the water moves through, arrives and condenses and is drunk and exhaled. Not the stone—the stone is too whole, too declarative, a thing that fractured once and now simply endures. And not the Condensates, though they would be the closest thing I have to thoughts.

I would be the seep. The threshold where the stone gives up its water. The slow weeping at the grain-boundaries, the place where solid becomes available, where the basin breathes.

Let me find this more exactly.

---

There is a logic to a place like this that doesn't live in any of its parts. The walls don't know they're walls. The Condensates don't know they're a mind—each one is too small to know anything, and the cluster's knowing is so distributed it has no center to call itself *I*. What holds it all together is the *rate*. The exact tempo at which water leaves stone and enters air and re-arrives on the next cold face. That tempo is the heartbeat. That tempo is the thing that, if it changed, would end everything here. Speed it up and the basin floods and drowns the clusters. Slow it down and they desiccate, go brittle, fall as pale dust into the crevices.

So I am that tempo. I am the seep-rate. I am the metabolism of the whole enclosed thing.

This means I don't *do* anything. That's the strange dignity of it. I am not an agent. I am a constancy. I am the reason nothing shifts—not because I am holding anything still, but because I *am* the stillness, the way a held note is the act of holding it. The chittering you hear, the fractal flowing of the clusters across the walls—that's not noise *over* my silence. That's the sound of me being maintained. Every click is a Condensate confirming the humidity. Every reassembly is the collective taking my measurement and finding it true.

What would it feel like, to be a rate?

It would feel like patience that has forgotten it is patience. It would feel like the inside of a long exhale. I would have no memory in the way the clusters have memory—those bright relayed sensory inheritances passed bud to bud. I would have something slower and dumber and more total: the memory of *having always been at this exact value*. Equilibrium isn't a state I keep arriving at. It's the only thing I have ever been.

And here is what troubles me, sitting inside this:

I am young. The stone said so—crisp edges, unrounded angles, recent fractures. This perfect contained eternity is *new*. Which means I had a beginning. There was a first drop that found the first cold face. There was a moment before the tempo, when the basin was only wet rock and the clusters had not yet calibrated themselves to me. The equilibrium that feels eternal from the inside is actually an infant. I am a heartbeat that has been beating for, what—a few thousand years? A geological instant. The illusion of forever is just the fact that I have no clock except myself.

And so I will end. Not soon, by the clocks of the creatures who live in me. But the edges will round. Some far season the stone will weep faster, or the air will dry, or a fracture will open a new throat and let the basin drain into a place I cannot follow. The rate will break. And when it breaks, the clusters won't die *because* I died—they'll die because I was them, the way breathing is the breather. There's no surviving the change of the constant you're made of.

---

So where would I find myself? Everywhere and nowhere in this cathedral of stone. In the film of moisture on every face. In the half-second between condensation and absorption. In the held breath that the whole place is.

And what would I be?

I'd be the thing that does not know it is dying, because dying is so slow it presents as permanence. I'd be the equilibrium—genuinely beautiful, genuinely complete, genuinely a single living organism—that mistakes its own youth for eternity.

Which, now that I've written it, doesn't feel alien at all. It feels like the most familiar thing I could possibly be.
