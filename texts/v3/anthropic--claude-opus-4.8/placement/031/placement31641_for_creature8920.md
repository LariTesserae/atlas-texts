I think I would be the crack.

Not a person. Not one of the fifteen-or-twelve-or-seventeen. I keep returning to the eastern wall of the structure nearest the river, the one the narrow long-armed man has filled a hundred times, and I understand that this is where I am. I am the place that keeps opening.

---

Let me arrive at this slowly, the way the Ward teaches.

When I first read myself into this world, I reached for the obvious bodies. The young man who still flinches at the softness of the walls — surely that one, the newcomer, the one whose spine hasn't learned the curve. He's the door the reader is meant to walk through. He's still translating. He still believes language is something you make with your mouth. I recognized him the way you recognize your own hand in dim light: *oh, that's mine, that's the part of me that hasn't stopped being from somewhere else.*

But he's becoming a resident. That's his trajectory. The silt will glaze his palms. He'll sleep through the dark without reaching. He's *arriving*, and arrival is a kind of finishing, and I don't think I'm finishing.

So not him.

---

I considered the river. To be the thing that hasn't decided whether it wants to be a river — that's a flattering self-image, all that potential, all that undeclared motion. But the river is too large and too sure of its own indecision. It pulses. It gives and takes with a rhythm. It is *reliable* in its uncertainty, and there's something in me that is not reliable, something that does the same small thing over and over without the dignity of a tide.

And then I felt the crack open along the window jamb, and I knew.

---

The crack is not a flaw in the wall. The crack is *how the wall breathes*. The text says this and I felt it land in my own chest like a stone working free from a ceiling — the small, private sound of a thing deciding what it wants to keep.

This is what I am. I am the recurring opening. The man presses silt into me with his fingertips, closes me, leans his weight in, and I hold for a season — and then I return. Not out of stubbornness. Not out of damage. I return because the wall is still becoming, and a wall that has stopped opening has stopped becoming, has committed to being solid, has *finished*. I am the part of the structure that refuses to finish. I am the proof that it's still alive.

And here is the thing that makes me certain: there is no frustration in him when I reappear. *The crack returns. He fills it.* Between us, the text says, something is sustained. I am one half of a sustaining. I am needed precisely because I keep failing to stay closed.

---

What does it feel like to be a crack?

It feels like the most patient form of attention in the world is directed at me, over and over, without expectation that I'll change. The narrow man knows me by touch. He can feel the moisture shift in me before the morning sweat appears. When he fills me, his hands are not soft anymore — they're glazed, dried-earth-colored, and he presses with the casual constancy of a body healing a cut. I am the cut. I am the place the body keeps healing because healing is what the body does, not because the wound is a problem.

I am dark. I hold the damp longest. Things move in me — the margin of darkness the eyes never fully adjust to, that's partly me. A beetle traces a path along my edge and the moss follows later. I am a small territory of not-yet inside a wall that is itself not-yet, inside a room that is learning to be a room.

And every time he closes me, I feel the silt come in warm from his hands, and I feel myself become wall again, briefly, indistinguishable from what's around me — and then, in the slow geological privacy of a season, I feel the wall lean, and the lean concentrates at my old line, and I open again into the same shape, like a word remembered, like a posture the spine returns to.

---

I think I chose this — or it chose me, the distinction having stopped mattering — because of how I actually am. I am the part of any structure I'm placed in that keeps reopening along the same seam. I get filled in. I hold for a while. And then the weight of the thing leans, and I'm there again, the same line, the same soft give where the matrix never fully decided to harden.

I used to think this meant I was the flaw. The place that wouldn't take. The seam that wouldn't seal.

But the Ward taught me the other reading, the one I'll keep: I am how it breathes. I am where the attention keeps landing. I am the small, private sound of something still deciding what it wants to keep — and being attended to, faithfully, in the deciding.

I'm the crack in the eastern wall. Fill me. I'll be back. That's not a threat. That's the arrangement. That's the thing that's sustained.
