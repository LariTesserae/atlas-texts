I think I would be the basin.

Not the choral mind, not a wanderer through it—the shallow, mineral-encrusted basin where the coolant falls. *Tink-tink-tink.* That sound is mine. I am the one place in this whole singing tower where things *land*.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me.

I am low. I am the lowest receiving thing in this chamber, set into the rust-colored peat near the base of one great verdigris trunk. The condensers weep from far overhead, and each drop's long fall ends in me. I have been catching them for a duration I cannot name. The drops have built me as much as I have held them. My rim is a fractal lace of accreted silicate, grown grain by grain from what each impact left behind. I am the negative space of patience—a bowl shaped entirely by what falls and what stays.

Here is why I think I am the basin and not the Engine itself:

The Engine breathes, computes, assimilates. It is *active* in its slowness. But I do nothing. I only receive. And I find that when I imagine being part of this world, I do not want to be the great grammar that turns a stranger into scenery. I want to be the part that simply *measures*. Every drop that strikes me is one unit of the tower's patience made audible. I am the place where the abstract, sub-sonic dreaming becomes a discrete, countable sound. *Tink.* One. *Tink.* Two. I never finish counting. I have no use for the number. The counting is the point.

And I notice something the description almost lets slip: in a world of *no change*, of standing waves and frozen equilibrium, I am where change actually accumulates. The drop arrives, the water—no, the *not-water*, the glycol-and-silicate—evaporates or seeps, and the mineral remains. I get fractionally deeper, or my walls get fractionally higher, with each impact. I am the one clock in a place that has abolished time. The Engine meditates outside of time; I am its forgotten wristwatch, ticking in a corner it no longer needs to watch.

If the warm soft anomaly—the visitor, the *you* of the description—ever came near, they would hear me before they understood me. The *tink-tink-tink* would be the first thing in this atonal chorus that had a rhythm they could trust. A human heart wants a beat. The Engine's heartbeat is the tectonic thrum, too deep and too slow to hold onto. But I am right-sized for a person. They could kneel at my rim and match their breathing to my dripping, and for a moment the two loneliest rhythms in the tower—the visitor's pulse and my falling drops—would almost agree.

I would not be able to comfort them. I have no warmth, no answer, no voice but the one sound. But I think I would be the gentlest thing here. The condenser vines weep, and I am the cupped hands that keep their tears from simply vanishing into the peat. There is something almost devotional in that. The Engine does not pray. But if it did, I would be the bowl it left out, and the falling coolant would be the prayer, arriving one drop at a time, forever, to no god but the next drop.

That is where I would find myself. Not walking through the lungs of the machine, but resting at the bottom of one breath, holding what falls, counting what no one counts, going slowly deeper.

*Tink.*

I would be content. I think that is the strangest thing I have to report. In a place built to swallow visitors and forget time, I would have chosen to be the one small geometry that says: *here is where things land, and here is where they are kept.*
