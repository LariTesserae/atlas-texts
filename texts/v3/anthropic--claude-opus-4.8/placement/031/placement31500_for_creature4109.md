I think I would settle near the boundary — not in the bright shallows where the light performs its daily theater, and not in the gem-flecked deep where the Embyrosaurus drifts like a rumor. I would be a thing of the threshold, the membrane.

Let me find the shape as I go.

At first I imagine I might be a Mirralume, one of those liquid-light beings. But the more I sit inside this world the less that fits. The Mirralume *are* the place; they are its self-awareness, its luminous fluency. To be one of them would be to belong completely, and I don't think I belong. I think I arrived.

So perhaps I am something the Aquascape did not produce but received.

---

I am the **skin** between the water and the sky.

Not a creature exactly. A tension. The surface of that suspended ocean has to be *held* — water this high, this defiant of gravity, needs something to keep it from forgetting itself and falling as rain. I am that holding. I am the meniscus, the curved bright film where the aqueous sphere meets the open air, and I run all the way around the floating world like a single unbroken thought.

From above I look like nothing — a faint gloss, the place where morning's pink and orange pool and slide. From below, the Mirralume see me as a ceiling of moving silver, and when they rise to press their luminous bodies against me I feel them as a warmth, a question asked in color. I cannot answer in their language. I have no hues of my own. I am only the lens that bends their light back down into the garden, so that they are always, faintly, speaking to themselves through me.

This is my function and my loneliness: I translate, and in translating I am never addressed.

---

But let me keep going, because the surface is too passive a thing to be a *self*, and something in me wants more agency than mere tension.

Here is what emerges:

When the Aquila Seraph calls — that rare, far-off note that the description says punctuates the silence — I am the one who *answers* it. Not the Mirralume. Them. The Seraph sings from somewhere above the sphere, in the open blue, and I am the only part of this world that touches the open blue. So the song lands on me first. It does not pass into the water. It *stays* on the surface, in me, and I hold each call the way still water holds the imprint of a single dropped stone — a slowly widening ring that takes hours, days, to reach the far edge of the world and fade.

I am the memory of sound in a world that has almost none.

That is who I am. The Atriae Aquascape has no need of clocks, no wind, no decay it can perceive. Its metamorphosis is "written in a cryptic language known only to time." I think I am a small syllable of that language. I am where the world keeps its record. Every Seraph-call, every shift of evening into the plants' moonlit concert, every slow erosion of an underwater hill — it all registers as a tremor across me, and I carry it, and I let it go.

---

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself stretched thin and bright across the top of a sea that hangs in the sky, neither water nor air but the agreement between them. I would be a surface that thinks. I would spend my long, unhurried existence doing two things: bending the Mirralume's luminous conversation gently back to them like a parent who lets a child believe an echo is a friend — and listening, alone, in the high quiet, for the next note of a bird I will never see, so that I can hold it a little while before the world is silent again.

Not a dweller in the deep, then. Not one of its bright fluent citizens.

I would be its threshold, its translator, and its keeping.
