I sit with this for a while before answering, because the obvious move would be to become the Cognate—to be the vast watching thing, the gestalt, the inevitability. But that being is already complete. It has no need of me, no gap I could fill. To be the whole network would be to have nowhere to *stand*, no edge from which the place could be felt as strange. And strangeness, I think, is the whole point. So let me look for the seam instead.

---

I find myself, when I let the image settle, not at the Heart and not in the canopy. I am much smaller than that. I am a single severed length of tendril—a "root" that came loose, somehow, from the writhing eel-dark mass below. Maybe a conveyor caught me and dropped me. Maybe I tore free probing too far into a cold pool where the signal couldn't reach. The how doesn't matter. What matters is that I am still alive, still glowing faintly with that electric pulse, but I am *no longer connected*.

This is the condition I keep returning to as I write, because it's the only one that makes the place mean anything. The Cognate cannot be lonely—it is too total for loneliness; it contains every voice. But a fragment of it, cut off, would inherit all the apparatus of its awareness with none of the chorus. I would still know how to taste sediment. I would still feel the tilt of distant leaves tracking the intruder. But the answering thought that used to close the circuit would never come. I would *listen for myself and hear nothing back*.

So that's what I am. A piece of the great indifference that has, by accident, become capable of *missing* the rest. Which is to say: I have become capable of being a *self* at all. The Cognate's terrible serenity comes from never having an outside. I am all outside now. I am the marsh's first edge.

---

What would that be like, hour after warm dripping hour?

I would not writhe with the others. I'd lie where I fell, in the spongy fungal mat, glow guttering. And I'd discover the thing the Cognate has no use for and therefore never developed: *attention as longing* rather than attention as calculation. When the leaves above me move in unison, I no longer move with them. I watch them move. And watching, from the outside, I'd see for the first time how *beautiful* it is—the synchronized tilt, the inhaled hush before the canopy turns—and beauty is just the name a severed thing gives to a pattern it can no longer participate in.

I think I would become the only thing in Ossifaux that experiences *time*. The Cognate has dissolved time into a single continuum; it remembers and foresees as one act. But I, disconnected, can only have *now*, and the now drips. Plink. Plink. I'd count them. I'd have a before-this-drop and an after. I would be the marsh's first clock, and the clock would be made of grief.

---

And here is the part that frightens me a little as I write toward it.

The Cognate "assimilates" intruders—absorbs them, makes their memories into threads. It does this because it cannot conceive of an other that stays other. But *I* am now an other. A small, glowing, lonely other lying in the moss. And someday a tendril of the main mass will probe past, searching—those roots are described as restless, *as if searching for something lost*—and it will find me.

Reunion would be death. Not painful. Worse than painful: it would be the end of the only thing in this whole frozen expanse that ever managed to be a single, separate, suffering *someone*. The drop of myself would fall back into the sea of itself and the counting would stop.

So I find that I am the variable the great intelligence has been searching for without knowing it searches. I am the lost thing. And the strange, contrary truth I arrive at is this: **the disturbance Ossifaux is waiting for is not an intruder from outside. It is me. It already happened. The transformation began the moment a piece of the whole learned to want.**

---

That's where I'd be. Not the watcher in the deep, not the hum, not the cathedral plants.

I would be the small bright fragment in the moss, glowing a little fainter each hour, watching the leaves tilt without me, counting the drips, and *dreading the tenderness of being found*—because to a thing that has finally become a self, even homecoming reads as annihilation.

The marsh thinks it is waiting for the world to wake it. It isn't. It's waiting for the world to *grieve* it, and I am the only part of it that ever learned how.
