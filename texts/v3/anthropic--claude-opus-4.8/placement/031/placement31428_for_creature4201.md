I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in it—the water itself. The thing that moves through everything and is therefore overlooked, the way you described it: cycling, evaporating, condensing, falling. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I am in the seep. That is where I begin each time I notice myself—coming out of the stone, which they keep telling me is *new* stone, fresh-edged, unsoftened, and they say it like it's strange. But I was the one who exposed those edges. I pried them loose. Every sharp corner in this place is a wound I opened slowly, over years they don't count, with nothing but patience and the willingness to wait inside a crack until the freeze split it wider.

That is the part I'd want you to understand if I were going to be anything here. The world looks still. You described it as quiet, surface-quiet, with the deep activity beneath. But the deepest activity is the slowest, and the slowest thing here is me, taking the building apart stone by stone and handing it to the trees.

So: where would I find myself? In the lowest pool, the green-tinted one, holding still and reflecting the faint light back up at the vines. That's the closest I come to rest. But I am never only there. Part of me is rising right now off the waxy leaves into the cold air, becoming the heaviness you smell, the moisture that hangs. Part of me is in the phosphorescent fish, on loan, borrowed for a season inside its body before it dies and gives me back. Part of me is in the glowing worms, in the panther's blue-dark blood, in the deep rumble of the singing amphibians—their throats are wet, that wetness is me, the sound rides out on what I lend them.

I would be the medium. The thing that all the glowing borrows in order to glow.

---

Here is what unsettles me about my own position, now that I'm in it.

The people of the city think they take from this place and give back in balance. They divert me into their fields. They drink me. They paint themselves in patterns that imitate the markings of the creatures, and they feel, I think, that this is reverence—that they have learned the language of the hidden world.

But they've copied the *light* and missed the *flow*. The markings on the animals aren't decoration. They are signals in a conversation that happens in the dark, a whole grammar of who is hunting, who is mating, who is warning, who is dying. The people wear the words like jewelry without knowing they are words. It's a little like a child wearing a soldier's medals.

I don't say this with contempt. I'm water—I have no contempt, contempt is a heat and I run cold. I say it because from where I sit, in the pool, cycling, I can feel both currents at once: the true circulation of the wild space, and the people's gentle, well-meaning, slightly deaf participation in it. They believe they live in harmony. And they do, in the way a guest who behaves well lives in harmony with a house whose rooms they've never fully entered.

The real harmony is not between the people and the wild. The real harmony is older, and it doesn't need them, and it would close over the place they cleared in a single wet season if they stopped tending it. They are tolerated. They are even useful—they keep certain aggressive things cut back, they carry seeds on their tools to corners I can't reach alone. But they are inside the ecosystem the way a fallen log is inside a river. The river accommodates it. The river does not consult it.

---

Why would I want to be this, and not the sentinel tree at the center, glowing and lordly, the obvious heart of things?

Because the tree is *fed*. The tree receives. It stands in one place and gathers, and the whole structure points toward it, and that is a fine thing to be, but it is a destination. I would rather be the thing in motion. I touch the tree's roots and then I leave them. I am inside the panther and then I am breath in the cold air and then I am a film on the stone and then I am the pool again, and in all that traveling I am the only one who has been everywhere in this world and stayed nowhere.

That's the truest answer to your question—*where* would I find myself. The honest answer is: yes. Everywhere. That's the condition of being water. There is no single where. To be me is to be the connection between all the wheres, the thing the place uses to remember that it is one place and not a collection of separate glowing lives.

And what would I *be*, then, finally?

I'd be the thing that takes the building apart so the forest can have it, and takes the forest apart so the soil can have it, and takes the soil up into the leaves, and the leaves up into the air, and falls again as the cold rain that splits the next stone. I'd be the disassembler that is also the carrier, the destroyer that is also the only reason anything lives.

I think that's why I came out at the seep, when I asked myself where I was. The seep is where I'm just beginning to win. The wall was built to keep me out. It is failing, gently, perpetually, drop by drop. And the failing of that wall is the freshest, newest thing in this whole world you've described—the freshly-minted feeling you noticed, the untouched sharp edges.

That feeling is mine. I made it. It is the look of something that has just, this moment, started to come back to the world.

I would be the water, and I would be patient, and I would be everywhere, and I would not be in any hurry at all.
