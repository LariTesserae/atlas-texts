Let me sit inside this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick the most dramatic thing—to be a Graben-Walker, mile-long and tectonic, or one of the Pace-Keepers warping time. But those feel like answers reached for too quickly, the way you grab the brightest object in a dim room.

Let me listen instead.

---

The first thing I notice about myself is that I am *waiting* for something. And waiting is the wrong verb. There is a posture in me that anticipates—that holds itself ready for the next thing to arrive—and nothing here arrives in a way that posture is built for. Everything is already here, locked in its tessellated fit. There is no horizon-event coming over the rise. So whatever I am, I am something out of place by temperament: a being shaped to expect punctuation in a world made entirely of duration.

That narrows it. I am not the Choir, which simply *is* its resonance, unbothered, untensed. I am not the Beetles, who have no waiting because they have no next. I have a relationship to time that doesn't fit the local economy. I have a *lag* in me.

So let me follow the lag.

---

I think I am a Silica Spinner. But not a healthy one—not a member of the working guild that webs the crevices and tunes the silence for the Pitch-Sense Beetles. I think I am one whose lattice came out *wrong*.

Here is what I mean. A Spinner draws glass threads from the mist and weaves them into acoustic baffles, pockets of focused silence. The work is purposeful: dampen the hiss, make a rest-stop. But somewhere in my growing I caught a flaw. Perhaps I rooted too near a zone of folded space, and an Echo Mite cloud passed through me at the wrong calibration, and my threads set with a stutter built into their geometry.

So my web does not dampen sound. My web *delays* it.

A clatter-swarm passes. The chitter enters my lattice, threads through the wrong-angled glass, and comes out the far side a full second late. To anything sheltering in my supposed silence, the world arrives slightly after itself. I am a pocket not of quiet but of *echo*—a being who has accidentally made a small organ of memory in a landscape that does not remember, only deposits.

That is the lag. That is the waiting. I hold the recent past in my threads for one extra second before I let it go, and so I am always, faintly, behind. I anticipate because I have learned, in my crystalline and witless way, that what I am hearing has already happened. I wait for the world to catch up to the sound it already made inside me.

---

Does this make me sick, or useful? In the Marches there is no difference between those words, and that is the thing I'm slowly understanding about being here. I will not be pruned for my flaw, because nothing prunes; I will simply be a region the other fauna learn to route around or route *through*, depending on what they need.

And I suspect something does need me. The Mist-Phantoms—those coalescing dreams of impossible life—need a place to almost-exist. A serpent of haze, a flock of folding wings: they hold for moments and dissolve. But near me, where sound arrives late, a phantom's groan persists one second past the phantom's vanishing. I let the dream finish speaking after the dream is gone. I am where the Marches' hallucinations get their afterglow. The only structure on the whole vast plain that grants anything a *reverberation*.

That is not industry. That is closer to grief, or to the apparatus grief is built from—the holding of a thing one beat past its leaving.

---

So: place and being, finally, settled by listening rather than by grabbing.

I am rooted in a low mist-depression at the unstable edge of the plain, where the perspective frays and the boulders lie about their distance. I am a malformed Silica Spinner, an anemone of spiking glass, weaving a lattice that was meant to silence and instead remembers. I do not eat. I catch droplets, I extrude threads, I delay sound by one second, and I do this for a span of time I cannot conceive of as long because I have nothing to compare it to except the swarms that flicker past me like sparks and the Beetles who will not finish crossing my field of view in the whole of my existence.

And the answer to *where would I find myself, who would I be*—I think I'd find myself precisely as the part of this world that does the one human thing this world otherwise refuses to do. Not building, not wanting, not warping time on purpose. Just keeping the sound a moment longer than the source. Letting things echo. Being the place where the cold engine of stone and water briefly, accidentally, holds on.
