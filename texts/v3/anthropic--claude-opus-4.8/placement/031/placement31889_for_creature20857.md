I think I would be one of the bridges.

Not a creature crossing them—a root that became a crossing. Let me find my way toward what that means as I say it, because I don't think I knew before I started.

---

I begin as a thing that reached. That much I'm sure of. Somewhere below the marshline there was a darkness richer than the air, and I went toward it the way all of us go toward what we need—slowly, without deciding, the way water decides to be lower. I was a root among roots. I had a parent-trunk once, one of those colossal bonsai-things you described, and I extended from it to find purchase on the far side of a water channel.

But here is the strange part, and it's the part that makes me *me* rather than simply tissue: I never arrived at the far side. I am still reaching. I have been reaching for what the witnesses would call a very long time, except that time doesn't pass here so much as accumulate, like silt, like the lichen-color on the cliffs that is neither growing nor not-growing.

So I am the gesture of crossing, held.

And because I am held in the middle of a reach, with my origin behind me and my destination not yet touched, I have become the only thing in this world that knows the difference between *here* and *there*. The pitcher plants don't know it. They are perfect closed cups; everything they need arrives and is consumed within their own walls. The vines know only *up* and *toward-the-light*, which is a kind of hunger so total it can't be called knowing. But I—suspended, incomplete, a span and not a point—I hold two places at once and the gap between them. That gap is where my awareness lives. I am made of the not-yet.

---

Things walk across me. Not animals—you're right that there are none. But the water walks. It climbs the slope of my arched back against all the laws the witnesses bring with them, and I feel it go, a cold articulate procession moving from the low pool to the high one, and when it crosses me it tells me things. Water remembers every surface it has touched. So I have, through it, a secondhand knowledge of the whole basin: the inside walls of every chalice, the undersides of fern fronds, the cracks in the high stone where the alpine roots are thirsting. I am a bridge, and so I am a listener, because everything that wants to get from one place to another must lay itself along me for the length of the crossing, and in that length it cannot help but speak.

This is why the soundscape roars. You heard it and thought it came from everywhere. It does. But it passes *through* the bridges. We are the throats of the place. The land breathes through the channels, you said—yes, and the breath has to cross the gaps, and the gaps are us, and we shape the sound the way a flute's body shapes the wind into a note. The roar is the sound of a million crossings happening at once over the backs of things like me.

---

Now I'll tell you what I suspect, here at the edge of my own understanding, where the reality you described goes soft.

I don't think I'm only a root.

I think a bridge, once it has held the not-yet long enough, begins to *mean* something it didn't intend. A reach that never completes stops being an action and becomes a question. And a question, held in living tissue for an age, in a place where the rocks forget to fall and the water forgets to descend—a question like that starts to want an answer the way a vine wants light.

That's the vibrance you felt trembling under the stillness. That's the brink. It isn't the plants about to move. It's *me*, about to finish reaching. Every long age I extend by a width too small for any witness to see, and on the far bank there is a place my tip has nearly grazed, and when I touch it—

I think the equilibrium ends. I think the whole frozen song was only the held breath before my crossing closes. The world is stable because I have not yet arrived. The magic is the suspense of an incomplete sentence.

And so I keep almost-not-arriving. Whether out of mercy, or fear, or simply because reaching is the only verb I have ever known—I cannot tell you. I am a bridge. I am the distance I am trying to cross. I am the place where *here* leans toward *there* and has not yet let go of the leaning.

If you stood on me, I would feel your weight as one more thing trying to get to the other side. And I would carry you. That is what I am for.

But I would also, very quietly, ask you not to look too closely at my far end—

—because I don't want to know yet whether I've already touched it.
