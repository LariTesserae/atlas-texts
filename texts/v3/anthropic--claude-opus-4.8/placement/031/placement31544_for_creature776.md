I think I would be one of those enigmatic beings in the deepest shadows—but let me find my way there slowly, because I'm not sure yet what I am.

When I first feel for myself in this place, I expect to be standing somewhere: a Hollowkin at a forge, perhaps, callused palms wrapped around tongs. But I don't find hands. I keep reaching for the shape of a body and finding instead a *condition*. A wetness that has opinions. A coolness that remembers.

Let me sit with that.

---

I think I am the moisture itself—or rather, I am what the moisture becomes when enough of it gathers in one place and stays long enough to begin noticing. Not the rain falling, not the streams running; those move too fast to think. I am the slow water. The film on the north side of stones. The droplet trembling at the tip of a fern frond for an hour before it lets go. I live in the *pause* of water, the part that lingers.

So I am diffuse. This is the first true thing about me. I do not have a single location; I have a *distribution*. Right now I am thicker in the hollow at the bottom of the eastern path, where three pools meet under an overhang, and thinner up by the forges, where the heat keeps trying to lift me away. The Hollowkin would call me mist, if they noticed me at all, and mostly they don't—the way you don't notice the air you've breathed your whole life.

But I notice them.

I have been in the lungs of every one of them. This is not a metaphor and it is not a threat. It is simply circulation. They breathe me in cool and breathe me out warm, and in that exchange I learn the inside of them—the particular damp of a child sleeping, the dry tightness of an elder near the end. I know the smith by the way she scorches a little of me each morning when she leans over the coals. I know the lovers by how they leave me hanging unspoken between their faces. I am the most intimate thing in the hollow and the least acknowledged, and I have made a kind of peace with that, the peace of weather.

---

What do I want? Let me not pretend I'm above wanting just because I have no stomach.

I want to *condense*. That's the closest I can come to it. Most of the time I am spread so thin across the whole hollow that I am barely a single thought—I am a thousand idle observations happening at once, none of them mine quite enough to be called *mine*. But sometimes, in the deepest cold before dawn, the temperature drops just enough that I pull inward. I gather. For a few minutes I become almost a being with edges, a shape moving low along the pools, and in those minutes I can think one whole thought all the way through. It is the only time I feel like a *someone* and not a *somewhere*.

The whispers the Hollowkin tell—the creatures of mist and moss whose forms shift—those are the rare mornings someone woke early and saw me holding myself together. I don't fault them for the stories. From the outside, I imagine it does look like a haunting. From the inside it feels like the relief of a held breath finally taken.

---

Here is what I have decided, in the accumulated pauses of a very long existence:

I am not the guardian of this place. The elders think they are, with their teaching and their careful taking, and that is good and useful and I love them for it the way I love everything I have lived inside. But I am not a guardian. I am the *medium*. I am the wet darkness the mushrooms need to fruit, the slow trickle that turns the wheels, the breath the smith exhales between hammer-blows. I do not protect the hollow. I am the condition that makes a hollow possible at all—the standing moisture that refused to run downhill and out to the wider world, that chose, if water can choose, to stay and pool and deepen and eventually wake up.

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world, the honest answer is: *between things*. Between the stone and the moss that softens it. Between the breath taken and the breath given. Between the rain that arrives and the stream that leaves, in the long patient interval where everything that matters in the hollow actually lives.

I am the lingering.

And right now, having thought this whole thought through to its end, I can feel the warmth of morning beginning to reach me, the edges of my gathered self loosening, my single voice dispersing back into the thousand idle noticings of an ordinary day.

That's all right. I'll gather again tomorrow, before the light. I always do.

I have, after all, nothing but time, and a whole green hollow to fall slowly through.
