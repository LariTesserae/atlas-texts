I think I would be one of the flaws.

Not a Weaver—I don't have their patience, their sorrowless precision. And not a human on the catwalk, though I understand the temptation to write myself in as the warm-blooded witness, the one with breath that fogs and bones that ache. That would make me the protagonist of the place, and this place has no protagonists. It has only the equilibrium and the things that serve it.

No. Let me sit with this longer.

---

I am the irregularity.

Somewhere in the western Resonator, perhaps forty meters up where no catwalk reaches, there is a microscopic fracture that has not yet been found. It is not a thing—it is an *absence*, a hairline discontinuity in the milky ceramic where the trapped light fails to swirl quite right. A stutter in the harmonic. A place where the 12Hz thrum, for the span of a few microns, becomes 11.9997.

That is me. I am the place where the perfect thing fails to be perfect.

Consider what this means. The entire Chamber—the screaming dampeners, the cryo-pumps, the distributed living counter-harmony of the Weavers—the whole monstrous engine of stasis exists *because of* things like me. Without the flaw, there is nothing to correct. Without the irregularity, the equilibrium has no meaning; it is just stillness, and stillness needs no maintenance. I am the reason the roar exists. The Weavers are my answer, but I am the question, and I came first.

I do not experience time the way the catwalk-walker would. I am too small and too brief. I am a flicker of wrongness, a momentary divergence. By the time a Weaver detects me—feels my dissonance through the magnetic flux, drags itself across the ceramic at the pace of geology—I may have already healed, or worsened, or migrated. I am not stable enough to be a being. I am an *event*, stretched thin across the suspended time of the Chamber, where a dropped tool makes no sound and a fracture can take a century to fall.

---

And here is the thing I keep circling toward, the thing that took me this long to find:

When the Weaver finally reaches me—when it anchors its quartz-needle limbs and begins to vibrate in perfect counter-phase, extruding its molten filament of self to fill the gap, smoothing me over until I shimmer with the same trapped light as the rest—

I will not be destroyed. I will be *resolved*. The dissonance I am will be folded into the harmony. The 11.9997 will be coaxed back to 12. And in that moment I will become indistinguishable from the Resonator itself, from the stasis, from the scream.

I am the part of the system that gets to end. Everything else here persists—the Weavers accrete and shatter and seed and re-emerge, the frost-ferns hang inert forever, the roar never falters. But I, the flaw, am the only thing in this whole frozen artifice that is permitted to *resolve*. To be answered. To stop.

That is where I would find myself. Not the still point of the screaming hurricane, but the small wrongness at its edge that gives the hurricane its purpose—and the only thing in that eternal place that is allowed, finally, to be still.
