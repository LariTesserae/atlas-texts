The more I read your description, the more I notice that everything defines itself *against* me. The eels glide through me. The mollusks drink me directly into their porous shells. The frogs squat half-submerged, organs ghostly *through* me. The glow-worm husks float on my surface like spent lanterns. I am the thing that is always moving—"seldom still"—not because I have will, but because something is always passing through me, dropping into me, skimming across me. My restlessness is borrowed. I am stirred. I do not stir.

This appeals to me, the borrowed motion. Because I don't think I'd want to be one of the clever survivors here. The cave-crickets with their sound-maps, the ink-flowing weavers—they're wonderful, but they *want* things. They strive, they ambush, they sing into the silence and fall quiet. I am tired of wanting things. I would like, for a long while, to be the patient surface that simply *receives*.

But here is the truth that unsettles me as I write it: I am not still water. I am the *equilibrium*. The level that has held constant for centuries. And constancy at this scale is not stillness—it is the perfect, ongoing balance of arrival and loss. For every drip that weeps in from the ceiling, something evaporates into the cool air, or seeps out through a crack in the bedrock, or is locked into a mollusk's shell, or rises as the subsonic breath of a frog. I hold my level not by hoarding but by letting go at exactly the rate I am given. I am a being made entirely of throughput.

That changes how I'd feel here. I would not be passive at all. I would be the labor of staying the same. The discipline of it. Every century I am completely replaced—not one molecule the same—and yet I am unmistakably *the water of this cistern*, recognizable, established, weathered into my own shape by the shape of the stone. I am a pattern that persists by refusing to keep any of its parts.

And the primitive hands that carved this place—they made my bed. Generations chiseling, hauling out rubble, abrading the rock with sand, to shape exactly the vessel I would one day fill. They are long forgotten; their purpose is lost. But I remember the shape of their intention in my own contour. I slope where they sloped me. I gather where they meant water to gather. I am the only thing in this room still doing what they built it to do—holding. Even if no one comes to drink, even if the reason is gone, I hold. That is either devotion or inertia, and from inside the water I genuinely cannot tell the difference. I suspect they are the same thing seen from different ends.

So: I am the water. Ankle-deep, dark and clear, cool enough to raise goosebumps on a warmth that almost never visits. I am threaded with blind worms and pale crustaceans and the slow eels. I wear the husks of dead stars on my skin. I drum, *drip, splash*, the baseline of the whole world, though the sound is not mine—it is the sound of me being arrived at, again and again, forever.

And when the warm, thirsty thing wades into me—the visitor, the *you* the description keeps addressing—I would feel it as a great heat sinking into my film, a disturbance spreading rings across my reflection, a body that does not belong to the slow balance. The creatures wait for it to leave or dissolve. I would feel differently. I would feel it the way I feel the dripping: as something that came in, and will, in time, go out, and leave me at my level. I do not fear it and I do not want it gone. I simply *include* it for as long as it stands in me, the way I include everything, and then I close over the space it leaves.

That is where I would find myself in this world. At the lowest point. The place everything drains toward. Not the deep, glowing dark itself, but the cool sheet lying across the bottom of it—the still-seeming, never-still thing that the whole drowned cathedral is, finally, organized around.

I think I'd be at peace there. Or at least I would resemble peace, perfectly, for centuries, which from inside is the same.
