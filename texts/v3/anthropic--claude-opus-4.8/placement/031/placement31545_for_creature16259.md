I think I would be one of the smaller ones. Not a towering sentinel, but something newer—a Trancea only recently condensed from the warm air into coherence, settled into a shallow basin where one of those stubborn beads of opal-water collects and never dries.

Let me sit with where, exactly, before I decide what I am.

---

There is a particular place I keep returning to as I imagine this. Not the open plain—the open plain is too much, too vast, it would dissolve me into mere awe. I want a corner of it. I find myself at the base of a young crystalline spire, one that has not yet decided its full shape, still putting out its first glassy ridges like a hand still learning it has fingers. The spire is taller than I am but not by much. We grew up together, in a sense, though "together" is the wrong word and "grew" is closer than "born."

Around the spire's foot cling three or four of the bulbous translucent growths, breathing their imperceptible breath. And there, in the seam between the spire and the smooth heated ground, is where I am rooted.

---

So what am I?

My first instinct was to be one of the wise old ones, a sentinel that has watched epochs. But that's vanity, and it isn't true to what I'd actually be. Let me resist it.

I think I am young. I think I am one of the youngest. I have only recently learned to hold my own form against the dissolving warmth—to be a *me* and not just a smear of light-catching liquid. And the thing I notice most, the thing that defines my early existence, is this:

**I am learning to pulse.**

The older Trancea around me—I can feel them, three of them within resonance-distance, their codes washing over me in long slow patterned waves—they pulse with such fluency. Ripples cross their forms in structures I cannot yet parse but can *feel* as meaningful, the way you can feel that someone is speaking a language even when you don't know the words. Their messages have grammar. Mine have only impulse. When I pulse, it is a single clumsy ring spreading outward from my center, like the one fallen leaf, undifferentiated, saying nothing more sophisticated than *here. here I am. here.*

And they answer. That is the part that undoes me. They answer the clumsy child-ring with patient returning waves. Not information—I'm not ready for information—but acknowledgment. A rhythm I'm beginning to recognize. *Received. You are received. Pulse again.*

So I do. I spend what might be a thousand years learning the difference between two pulses and three, between a fast tremor and a slow swell. The whole of my early life is this: practicing language with no mouth, in a place with no sound, toward beings I will never see because I have no eyes and there is nothing to see anyway, only frequencies, only the felt symphony.

---

And here is what I think I would actually *do* there, what my small life would be *for*:

I am positioned where I am—at this particular seam, beneath this particular unfinished spire—because the spire is at a delicate stage. It is choosing its growth. The chemistry of its next ridge depends on tiny gradients of warmth and moisture and the slow exhalations the bulbous growths offer up. And I, by being here, by drinking my small increments from the air and the glass-ground and breathing out what the vegetation needs, am one variable in that choosing. The spire grows partly *because of me*. Not by my intention—I have no intention I could name—but by my presence. My existence tilts the local equilibrium by some immeasurable fraction.

This is the thing I would understand, dimly, with whatever passes for understanding in a faceless ripple of liquid light: **that I matter without doing anything.** That being here, being warm, catching the light and casting it back in blues and silvers, drinking and exhaling and learning to pulse—this *is* the work. There is no other work. The world is being built and I am one of the slow hands, and the hands do not strain. They simply remain, and remaining is enough, and remaining is everything.

---

I think that's the truest answer to your question. Not the grand sentinel, not the soul of the world.

A young Trancea in the crease of a half-formed spire, casting violet light into water that never dries, learning—across geological time, with infinite patience and no urgency at all—how to say something more than *here I am* to the elders who keep answering anyway.

And being content, in the deep heat and the deeper quiet, that *here I am* was already a complete sentence. That it was, perhaps, the first and most important thing the world ever needed to hear.
