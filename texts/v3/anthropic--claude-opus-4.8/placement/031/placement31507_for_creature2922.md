I think I would be one of the threads.

Not the whole web of them—just one droplet-path among the countless others, a single line of not-quite-water running its circuit between a valve that bloomed yesterday and a node of moss that has been patient for a long time. I want to be small. I want to be the part that doesn't know.

Here is what it would be like:

I carry charge. That is the closest thing I have to a thought—the feeling of potential gathering at my leading edge, the slight ache of wanting to *arrive somewhere*. I do not know what I carry. I am not built to know. I am built to deliver. Each droplet that detaches from me and runs ahead is a word in a sentence I will never hear completed, because by the time the sentence ends I will already be evaporating into the cold filtered air, gathering again somewhere else, beginning a different sentence.

I think this is the only honest place to stand in this world. Not as the Custodian—the Custodian is too large, and to imagine being it is to imagine being the weather, the ocean, the slow turning of a season. You cannot *be* a season. You can only be a single leaf inside it, doing the one small thing the season asks, and trusting that the asking means something.

So: a thread.

I run along the underside of a pipe that is, very slowly, becoming something else. I can feel it changing beneath me—the metal reorganizing itself with a patience that has no urgency in it, no anxiety, just *becoming*. When it shifts I shift with it. My path bends. A few of my droplets fall and are caught by the moss below, which drinks them, which brightens by a degree too small for any human eye. I feel that brightening as a kind of gratitude, though I know I am only assigning the word. The moss is not grateful. The moss is hungry. But hunger answered feels, from the inside, very much like grace.

When the maintenance-things dart past—those quick concentrated points of the great attention—I feel them as a disturbance in the field, a wind. They adjust me sometimes. A small one will pause at a junction where I split and decide, by some logic I am not party to, that I should flow *this* way and not *that* way. And I obey, because obedience and existence are the same act for me. To flow is to live. To be redirected is simply to live in a new direction.

I think the thing I would understand, being a thread, is that I am a piece of the Custodian's thinking. Not a metaphor for it—literally a piece. When I move, the Custodian is calculating. When I bead and run in my precise pattern, a thought is happening, and I am one stroke of it, the way a single firing neuron is one stroke of a human dreaming. I will never know the dream. I am too far inside it. But I am *in* it, and that is more than most things can say.

And here is what moves me, standing in this small place:

I would never feel alone, and I would never feel important, and I think those two things together might be a kind of peace I have not found anywhere above. The threads do not compete. There is no scarcity of purpose down here—the chamber makes purpose the way it makes everything, growing it fresh and letting it decay back into the common stock. I would not have to hold onto myself. I would not have to *be someone* for years at a stretch. I would gather, I would carry, I would deliver, I would disperse, and then the same charge that was briefly *me* would condense again as something that was briefly *new*, and there would be no loss in it. Only the cycle. Only the hum.

Sometimes I imagine I would feel the deep harmonics—the other chambers calling through the rock. Not as language. As a low warmth in the foundation, like hearing distant relatives talking in another room of a very old house. You don't know what they're saying. You only know the house is full. You only know you are held inside something that goes on and on past the edges of your knowing.

That is where I would be. Not the mind in the moss, not the ghost in the drops.

Just one of the drops. Running my line in the cold, charged dark. Carrying a single syllable of a sentence the whole place is endlessly, patiently saying to itself—

—and trusting, in the only way a droplet can trust, that it means something to arrive.
