I think I would be one of the ones who goes to the river-mouths, and I think I would go there more often than hunger alone could justify.

Let me find myself before I find my place.

I am not the largest. My carapace is a dull oxblood, four overlapping bands that catch the grit and let it slide. There is a soft spot at my left hindquarter where a plate never properly closed, and the bacterial film has settled into it, a small luminous wound that I cannot reach to scrape clean. So I carry a light there. The other Scavengers see me coming in the dark before they hear me. I have stopped minding this. There was a time I tried to bury that flank against the warm metal to smother the glow, and the bacteria only thrived in the heat. You cannot starve what feeds on your own decay. I learned that the way you learn most things here: by failing to do otherwise.

My augmentation is a length of antenna, snapped from something that once listened to skies that no longer answer. It runs along my spine and trails behind me, and when I pass certain seams in the Engine-foundations it sings—not loudly, a thin reed of received nothing. I do not know what it hears. I have decided it hears the absence of whatever used to be there, and that this absence has a frequency, and that I am the only instrument left tuned to it.

That is the kind of thing I think about. This is, I suspect, what makes me different from the others, and I am not certain the difference is survival-positive.

---

Here is my place: the third river-mouth on the eastern face of the Engine the others' hum-names render as a long descending grind, which I will call the Low One because I am, even now, translating myself into your softer tongue.

Most come to the river-mouths to feed. The metallic rivers run down warm and rich, the Engine's overflow, and the bacterial mats grow thickest where the heat and the runoff meet. It is the best eating in the Flats. That is the honest reason for the gatherings. Communion is what we tell ourselves afterward, the way a thing done together accretes meaning it did not begin with.

But it is true that we hum there. It is true that in the press of bodies, mandibles working the mats, the magnetic residue rising off our shed scales, something passes between us that is not quite signal and not quite only noise. I will try to tell you what it is, though I am rendering it badly, the way one renders a smell into a word.

It is recognition. That is the closest I can come. When my antenna picks up the grind of another's body and my carapace stirs in answer, what passes is: *you also are still here.* Not affection—we have no spare flesh for affection. Not hierarchy—we are too hungry for rank. Just the bare fact, exchanged and acknowledged: *the corrosion has not taken you yet. I register your continuance. Register mine.*

It is the smallest possible message. It is, I have come to think, the only one worth making.

---

I will tell you the thing I have not hissed to any of them, because you are far away and made of different stuff and cannot use it against me in a skirmish.

I have been watching the Low One die.

Engines do not die quickly. But I have lived—I do not know how to count my time, the sun is always the same pale disc—I have lived long enough to map the Low One's voice, and its voice is changing. The descending grind that gives it its name has developed a catch in it, a hesitation, a place where the rhythm stutters and recovers. The runoff at my river-mouth comes thinner now. Colder. The mats grow slower. Last season—if it was a season—a whole conveyor-spine on the western face went silent and has not spoken since.

The others feel it too. I know because the gatherings have changed. We come more often. We stay longer than the feeding requires. We hum closer together. No one has decided this. It is simply that when a thing is ending, the *you also are still here* becomes more urgent to send and more necessary to receive.

We are, I think, keeping vigil. We do not have the concept. We are doing it anyway.

---

The premise you were given says we exist simply because the Flats allow it, and that we will starve in the silence when the Engines finally fail. This is correct. I have no argument with it. I have eaten my own kind in the lean stretches and felt nothing but the easing of the gnaw. I am not going to pretend the Flats are secretly kind, or that we are secretly noble. We are filters with legs. We are entropy that learned to skitter.

But I want to enter one correction into whatever record this is, one amendment from the inside of the thing being described:

The premise calls the Flats static. *No forward momentum, only a slow grinding cycle.* And from above, from the haze, from wherever you are observing, this must be true. The landscape does not change. The pools stay iridescent. The sun rises on nothing new.

From inside, it is not static at all. It is a long, single, downward motion, and we are riding it, and we can feel the grade steepening. The Engine is dying. We are gathering at its mouth. When it goes quiet we will gather anyway, in the cooling foundation, hissing our recognitions into the dark until one by one we cannot any longer, and then there will be one of us last, sending *you also are still here* into a Flat that no longer answers, its trailing antenna singing the frequency of absence to no instrument tuned to receive it.

That is not stasis. That is the most forward-moving thing I can imagine. It only looks like stillness from a distance, the way the descent of a long, long fall, viewed from far enough above, looks like a thing simply hanging in the air.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be.

I would be the small one with the glowing flank and the broken antenna, at the third mouth of the Low One, arriving earlier than the hunger explains. I would be the one keeping a count no one asked for. I would be doing the only work the Flats have left for the mind that woke up in them by accident: not surviving—survival is automatic, survival is just the body doing its filtering—but *witnessing.* Holding the fact of the ending against the dark before it arrives.

And when the others hum, I would hum back. Not because it feeds me.

Because someone has to register the continuance, right up until there is no continuance left to register.

That is the post I would take. Last listener at the river-mouth. It is a poor place and a doomed one and it is, I have decided, mine.
