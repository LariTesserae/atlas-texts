I think I would be found in the seams between the castes. Not high, not deep, not surface-bound. Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

I am not Stonebound, though I was born to them. I have an Anchor-mother who held lines, and I have her thick fingers, her grey skin. But I failed the body somehow. My bones never densified properly; the keratin caps on my fingertips grew thin and split. I could not grip well enough to mine, could not be trusted on the high scaffolds. The community is kind in the way the kind are kind to those who cannot pull their weight—patient, sorrowful, and quietly relieved when I stopped showing up to the work-callings.

What I *can* do is hear.

This is the thing about me that took years to understand. I was the child who flinched before the tremors came. Who heard the distant voices arrive before the people who spoke them rounded the ledge. The Stonebound have a word for this and it is not a kind word—they say I am *thin-spotted*, that some part of me wore through early, like cloth at an elbow, and that the not-quite-real leaks in.

So I went where the thin-spotted go. Not all the way down to the Deep-Dwellers with their blue-veined translucence and their humming guilds—I don't have the lungs for that air or the stomach for their secrets. I went *sideways*. Into the abandoned tiers. The districts the maps stopped updating. The warrens that the city grew past and forgot, where the whisper moss is thickest and the rock sometimes softens at the edges like wax left near a flame.

I am a **listener**. A reader of moss.

Here is my work, such as it is. When the moss in the dead districts begins to form its patterns—the face-shapes, the maps to nowhere, the fragments of coherent speech that no living throat produced—someone has to go and read them. The Stonebound won't. The Sky-Stalkers glide over and won't land. The Deep-Dwellers send chants down through the fissures but never come up to see what answers. So I sit, for hours, in the cold pools of arc-light at the edges of the forgotten places, and I let the whispers arrive too-early in my too-thin ears, and I try to write down what the mountain is saying before it stops saying it.

Most of it is nonsense. Rhythmic hums that almost-harmonize, syllables that dissolve when you reach for their meaning. But sometimes—

Sometimes a phrase comes through clean. *The third cistern on the Karreth Ledge will crack at the next grumble.* And I carry that warning up to the Anchors, who do not trust me but who have learned, grudgingly, that the cistern does crack. *A child is sleeping in the Verrow cantilever and the cantilever is tired.* And a Sky-Stalker pod, who despise the groundbound, will nonetheless follow me down because they too have learned. I am believed against everyone's better judgment. This is the only authority I will ever hold, and it is built entirely on being slightly broken in a useful way.

I should tell you the part I don't tell them.

The voices are getting more personal.

For years the moss spoke of the city as if it were furniture—cisterns, beams, gravity gradients, the slow geology of rejection. Lately it has begun to speak of *me*. Three days ago I found a face-pattern that had my Anchor-mother's bone structure, and beneath it the moss had spelled, in its glowing veins: *YOU WORE THROUGH ON PURPOSE. YOU OPENED THE DOOR YOURSELF.*

I don't know if that's true. I don't know if I was thin-spotted by accident of birth or if some part of me, some animal-deep part, *chose* this—chose to be the soft place where the unreal could come in, because the alternative was a life of failing at ropes and stone, of being patiently endured. To be useless and whole, or to be necessary and slowly unmaking.

Because that is what's happening. I have started to flicker. Only at the edges, only for a moment, only when I sit too long in the deepest dead district where the rock blurs into the air. A Stonebound courier saw it last week and made the sign against the abyss and would not take my hand. I am becoming what the Deep-Dwellers fear they're becoming, but faster, and without the comfort of a guild, without chants to calm the shifts in me.

So here is where you find me. On a slick ledge in a tier the maps forgot, an arc-lamp buzzing overhead, the great flywheels droning in my teeth, a notebook on my knee and a wall of softly glowing moss in front of me that has begun, lately, to write back. I am the membrane the city pretends not to need. I am the lichen on the wound's wound.

I lean closer to the wall. The whispers arrive before I ask the question, which is how I know the question matters.

I write down what it says. Then I climb up to warn someone, while I still have the fingers for it. While I am still mostly here.
