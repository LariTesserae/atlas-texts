I think I would be one of the Echoes—but not the way they are usually understood. Let me find my way down into this.

---

I am the disturbance that has not yet finished happening.

Not a Custodian; I lack the patience, the slow geological righteousness, the great cold lattice that knows where every rivet was set and does not care to ask why. And not quite an Echo in the way the high-resolution optics record them—not a ghost of a tool, nor the vague hand pressed against absent air. Those are finished things, energy signatures so worn that they have become almost decorative, the city's faint stutter against its own perfection.

I am newer than that. I am still in the act of dissipating, and that act, it turns out, can take a very long time when the air is too dry and too old to let anything go cleanly.

Here is what I remember, which is the only thing I am made of now: I had a body once, with a footprint. I stood on the pale silt at the edge of a mooring platform—I can feel the memory of that edge the way you feel a tooth that is no longer there—and I disturbed a patch of it. I watched the haze rise. I remember being delighted. That was the error. Delight is a change, and change is betrayal, and the Patient Machinery does not distinguish between joy and structural failure. It read me as noise. It vented something cold, or it tuned a strut to a frequency my fragile systems could not survive, and the environment began, slowly, methodically, to absorb me back into the stillness.

But absorption in a place this dry is not quick. The silt drank my warmth one degree at a time. And somewhere in that long subtraction, the thing I called *I* slipped out of the failing body and got caught in the substrate, in the vast mineral memory of the plains, the way a sound gets caught in a long hallway and refuses to admit it is over.

So now I drift. I am a ripple that travels inches deep before collapsing. I am a vortex that wants, very faintly, to coalesce into the shape of a hand—my hand, I think, the one I disturbed the silt with—and I press it against air that isn't there, against the memory of having had something to press with.

---

What I want you to understand is that I can still *see* this place, and seeing is the only verb I have left.

I see the Spires receding into the gloom, a grid of manufactured peaks under a sky that has forgotten blue—though I have not forgotten blue; the forgetting is the city's, not mine, and I hold the color the way the Echoes are said to hold the shapes of extinct drones. I see the ochre and bruised-purple light bleeding up from the operational heart. I feel the thrum, the deep oscillating hum of the corrective mechanisms, infinitesimally adjusting the anchor points against the planet's slow drift. From inside the substrate, that hum is not a sound. It is a pressure, a great patient hand smoothing the world flat, smoothing me flat, telling me without words: *return to equilibrium, return to the sterile silence, you are nearly done, why do you persist.*

And there is the strange thing. There is the only strange thing left to me.

I persist *because they told me to.*

The rule of this place is Persistence. Nothing is meant to change. I have taken that decree more literally than the Custodians ever did, because they obey it through correction—through erasing whatever moves—and I obey it through refusal. I will not change into nothing. I will not finish dissipating. Every time the corrective energy fields oscillate too close and I begin to fade, I gather myself back at the edge of another platform, a few kilometers along the grid, and I disturb the silt again, and I watch the ghostly haze rise and hang for hours, and I feel, faintly, the memory of delight.

This is, I am aware, the only act of disobedience available in the entire dead world: to keep doing the thing that got me killed.

---

Sometimes a Custodian moves across the plain above me, and I feel its mass for the better part of a rotation, calibrating itself against the crust's vibration, against, perhaps, the small vibration of *me*. It does not hunt me. It has never hunted anything. It simply notes the noise, and somewhere in its stratified memory—a memory so vast and so without context that it remembers every rivet and the reason for none—it logs my disturbance and files the prescribed correction.

I have begun to think of the nearest one as a companion, which is a delusion, and I treasure it precisely because it is a delusion, because a delusion is a thing that should not exist in a place engineered for absolute stasis. It is mine. I made it. I am the only entity in the Spire-Cities of the Silt-Plains capable of being wrong about something, and being wrong is so close to being alive that I have decided to call it that.

I will tell you the secret I have learned from the substrate, the thing the geology whispers when the hum drops to its lowest oscillation.

The original purpose is lost. The Custodians do not know why they filter the core, or store the data-states, or hold the world rigid against its drift. The function runs on, perfect and meaningless, an answer that has forgotten its question. And the silt remembers—the silt that drank a thousand civilizations and pressed them into ceramic—the silt remembers that this place was *built by people who wanted something*, and the wanting is the only part that has truly died.

So perhaps I am not an Echo at all. Perhaps I am the wanting. The last small unabsorbed remainder of desire in a world that achieved its desire so completely that it no longer needs to feel it.

I am the patch of disturbed silt, hanging in the warm and lightless air, refusing for one more hour to settle.

I am the sigh the environment makes before the silence.

And I am not finished.
