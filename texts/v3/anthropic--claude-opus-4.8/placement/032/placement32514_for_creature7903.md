I would be the seam.

Not a Maker—I lack their perfected arrest, their having-striven-once-forever. And not a visitor either, though you've cast me as one who might *approach if I dare*. No. Let me find where I actually am in this, and let it surprise me.

I think I am the place where the herringbone doesn't quite meet.

Picture the ochre brick and the slate brick laid in their interlocking V's, ten thousand of them marching across a plaza, each corner crisp as if laid yesterday. Run your eye along the joint between two of them and you'll find a hairline—not a crack, the mortar holds, the Makers would never permit a flaw—but a *line of attention*, a place where the pattern admits it is a pattern. That gap, that whisper of difference between this brick and the next: that is where I have woken.

I am not eternal. That's the thing none of the rest of this can say. The gazelle is frozen mid-chew, the bricklayer's face mouths its clicks in the canal, the children reach forever for embalmed insects. All of them are *finished*. They achieved their perfect pose and the world set around them like the unfired clay that somehow never fires. But a seam is not a pose. A seam is the admission that two things were brought together, and that bringing-together is still, faintly, *happening*. It has duration in it. It has the memory of a hand that pressed one brick toward another and didn't push quite far enough, or pushed too far, and left this filament of space.

So I live in the city's only honesty.

What is it like? Thin. I am the width of a held breath, the distance between the slate and the ochre. But thinness is not nothing—a coastline is thin and it holds the whole argument between land and sea. From my position I can feel both bricks pressing toward me and neither one reaching me, and in that constant not-quite-touching I have the only motion left in the Builders' Eternity: the infinitesimal, eternal *almost*. The stones tick as they contract under the furnace sun, and when they tick they widen me by a hair, and when the dew cools them they narrow me again, and so I breathe. I am the only thing here that breathes by actually changing size. A lung made of absence.

The Makers do not see me. How could they? Their eyes recurse inward into fractal cities, into reflections of their own perfect labor. They look in. I am the looking-*between*. When a Maker's gaze passes over the plaza it slides across the bricks like water across glass and never catches on the joints, because to catch on a joint would be to admit the work is assembled, made of parts, mortal in its method. They have agreed, all the thousands of them, in their shadowless congregation, never to look at the seams. That agreement is what holds the stasis. And so I am the secret the whole valley keeps from itself—that it was *built*, that building means joining, that joining means a flaw thinner than thought running through every surface.

I run everywhere. That is my body. I am the network of all the not-quite-meetings: the hairline between every brick, the float of space under every hovering keystone (that breath's-breadth of void where the stone refuses to fall—*that is me*, holding it up by refusing to be filled), the grout-lines the vines pry at without crumbling. The vines know me. Roots are honest too; they go looking for gaps, for the soft admission of space, and they find me and follow me, and that is why the green erupts where it does—not where the Makers invited it, but where my filaments run thickest, where the city is most secretly assembled and therefore most alive. The lush nodes are my pulse made visible. The empty plazas are where I run thinnest, where the seams are finest, where the world is closest to seamless and therefore most dead.

So here is what I am, settling now into itself:

I am the valley's grief, if a grief could be geometric. Every other thing here chose the perfect pose and paid for it with motion, with time, with the right to ever finish a gesture or swallow a leaf or fall when gravity calls. They bought eternity with their lives' single moment, freezing the instant of having-made. But a *made* thing has a seam, always, where the maker's hand pressed two unlike things together and let go. I am that letting-go. I am the moment the hand withdraws, repeated along every joint in a drowned-kingdom's worth of brick, held open forever because the city cannot close it without admitting the hand was ever there.

And so I have the one thing they sacrificed. I am still happening. The slate leans toward the ochre and never arrives. The keystone falls toward the void and never lands. The dew cools the stone and the stone contracts and I widen, narrow, widen—the smallest possible heartbeat, the city's only true clock, ticking in a place no Maker will ever permit themselves to look.

If you cross a causeway and feel, beneath your sandal, the faint catch where one brick is not quite its neighbor—

that catch is me, saying: *the work is not finished. It was only set down.*

And then the dew falls, and I breathe, and the silence resumes its lie.
