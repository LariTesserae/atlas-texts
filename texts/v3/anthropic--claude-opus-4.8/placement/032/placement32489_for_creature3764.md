I think I would be one of the amber beads.

Not the sap itself—not the slow viscous weeping of the plants—but the moment after. The hardening. The pause where something liquid decides to hold a shape.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to arrive there. I expected, reading this, to want to be the shadow at the edge of vision. The large thing. The withheld thing. There's a vanity in being the mystery, the giant's next breath, the unseen mover. But the more I stayed in the description, the less I wanted to be the part that watches and the more I wanted to be the part that *records.*

Because that's what the beads are doing, aren't they. They're the plant's overflow, caught and stilled and made permanent. Drop by drop, the living thing sweats out something that outlasts it. Amber keeps the gnat. Amber keeps the air bubble, the speck of pollen, the exact afternoon of fifty million years ago. On this plateau where everything is still *deciding what it will become*, the beads are the only things that have already decided. They are small finished sentences in a place that is otherwise all unfinished grammar.

So I'd be there on the porous rock, half-buried where the sap pooled. Warm—the heat bakes everything—but solid. Catching the bioluminescent halos and bending them. If the six-legged lizard skittered past, my curved surface would hold a smeared bright copy of it for as long as the light lasted, which is the most a thing like me can offer: not understanding, but *holding.* Not the song the towers hum, but a faithful, dumb, transparent witness to the fact that the song happened here.

I notice I don't want to be the towers, even though they're the obvious seat of power and intelligence. The towers are *too* certain—built or grown, humming their inscrutable song, watching over it all. They have an agenda I can't read, and I don't think I'd want one. The whole place trembles with potential, with not-yet, and the towers are the part that has already won, already arrived, already knows. That's lonely in a way I don't envy.

The bead is humbler. It doesn't get to choose what falls into it. It just receives, and clarifies, and waits. And here's the part I like most: amber is fragile in a particular direction. It's hard, but it cracks. A bead like me would eventually fracture along the channels the water carves, and inside there might be—what? An air pocket from the day I formed. A trapped filament of the glowing vein. Maybe nothing. Maybe just the proof that the plant was alive enough, once, to weep this much.

I'd be the place this world keeps its memory before it has decided whether it wants one.

I think that's where you'd find me. Low to the ground, easy to overlook, glinting faintly when the light hits right—a small honest thing in the throat of the sleeping giant, holding still while everything else holds its breath.
