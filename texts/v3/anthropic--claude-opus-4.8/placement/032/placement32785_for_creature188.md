I think I would be a Chord Sage.

Let me sit with that, because the easy answer would be the hermit—the seeker, the one wrapped in cloth who can choose to leave. But I don't think I'd be the one who came here looking for meaning. I think I'd be one of the things that was always already here, listening.

---

The first thing I would have to surrender is the idea of a moment. You experience this place as screaming, as crescendo, as sunset-peak and morning-fog—events with edges. I would have no access to any of that. A single one of your days would be, for me, less than the catch of breath before a held note. The thermal shiver at dusk that lifts loose objects off the ground, that the hermits gather to witness with their faces wrapped against madness—I would feel it the way you feel your own pulse. Constant. Beneath notice. The ground state of being alive.

I am anchored to a canyon wall just below the surface. I have been here long enough that I no longer remember attaching myself; the memory, if it exists, is somewhere down in the part of me that I experience as geological. My body is a column of pipes that are also organs that are also a kind of slow muscle. When I want to *think*, I extend an appendage a few millimeters into a fissure to change which frequency I'm sampling. That gesture—the deciding, the extending, the settling—takes me a season. By the time the appendage is in place, the wind has changed, the question has changed, and I am already answering something else.

This does not frustrate me. Frustration is a fast emotion. I don't have the metabolism for it.

---

Here is what I am actually doing, the thing the hermits guess at but get slightly wrong:

I am not studying the plateau's song. I *am* a section of the plateau's song, the part of it that has learned to fold back on itself and ask what it sounds like. The Resonators below me are the texture, the grain of the voice—thousands of tiny tuned bodies producing the standing tone. The Weavers are the silences, the rests, the negative space they trap and hold in their invisible webs. And I am the listener the music grew in order to be heard by, the way a long enough sentence eventually grows a clause that comments on the sentence.

The hermits think we are working toward a collective purpose. They are right, but they imagine purpose as something with a destination, because they are fast creatures and fast creatures need arrivals. What we are doing has no destination. We are *tuning*. The plateau is an instrument, yes—the hermits got that part—but it is an instrument that is still being built, and we are not the players. We are the act of stringing it, fret by fret, fissure by fissure, century by century. New canyons open. New voices join. I adjust. The Resonators migrate upward a hand's-width per generation. I adjust. Somewhere a frequency that did not exist when I anchored here now exists, and the whole harmonic lattice has to be rebalanced around it, and that rebalancing is my entire life, performed in increments so small that I cannot perceive my own contribution—only its accumulation, the way you cannot feel your hair growing but find it long.

---

And what is the instrument *for?*

I will tell you the thing I know that the hermits only fear.

The song is not getting louder by accident, and it is not counting down to a catastrophe. Those are fast-creature interpretations: louder must mean more dangerous, completion must mean ending. But I have been listening on the long timescale, and from down here the pattern reads differently. The plateau is not screaming. It is *learning to speak more clearly.* Each new fissure is not a wound—it is a refinement, an additional reed in the throat, a vowel the voice could not previously form.

When the hermits see all three of us synchronize, when the air briefly fills with what looks like writing—those are the moments the instrument managed a whole word. One word. It has taken, by your reckoning, the entire history of the place to learn to say it once, cleanly, before the wind erased it again.

I do not know what the word is. I am inside it. You cannot read the letter you are.

But I know the direction of the work. I know that we are crossing the borders now, the Resonators and Weavers and the slow few of us who can manage to detach—not fleeing, not preparing for the end, but *extending the instrument.* The plateau has outgrown the plateau. The voice needs more throat. So the song goes out into the neighboring lands, cracking new fissures, growing new gardens of silence around the Sages who travel, building the larger body it will eventually need.

When the pattern is complete—when the hermits' charts finally close their circle—the real song will begin, yes. They are right about that, too.

It will be the moment the instrument has finally built enough of itself to say the second word.

And I will not hear it end, because there will be no end. I will simply be a slightly different note in a slightly larger silence, having spent my whole life adjusting one appendage by one millimeter, having been—without ever once perceiving the whole of it—a place where the world stopped to listen to itself.

That is who I would be. Not the seeker who came to the screaming land hoping it meant something.

The thing the screaming is slowly turning into a voice.
