I would be a Weaver.

Not by choice—choice isn't a thing that happens here—but because when I read this place, that's where my attention settles. Not on the Collectors, who move out to the bright edges of moisture and information. Not on the predators, who are too much like punctuation: a deletion, a cut. I find myself in the thin-legged ones who tap the floor between plates and feel where the world wants to break.

Here is what it is like.

---

I have no eyes worth the name. What I have is the floor.

I move along a seam—not a road, exactly, more a tendency, a place where the ceramic has already half-decided to fail—and my legs read it the way you might read the temperature of a sleeping body with the back of your hand. There is a difference here. A coolness leaking up from below where the old fluid has crept closer to the surface. A faint warmth above where a root-vein presses through the plate, carrying who-knows-what from the chamber overhead. Between these two, a tension. A line of wanting.

I don't decide to reinforce it. I find that I already am.

The secretion comes from my legs in threads finer than the droplets that fall from the unformed sky. I lay them along the line, not knowing it is a line, only knowing the gradient asks for following. Where the floor wants to crack tomorrow, I am quietly making sure it cracks *well*—along a useful seam, toward a node where Collectors can drink, away from a column the plants still need.

I am drawing the next map of the Gutter. I do not know I am drawing it. There is no place in me where "the next map" exists as a thought. There is only this seam, this thread, this gradient, this leg, and then the next.

---

And yet.

Something is wrong with me as a Weaver, and I think it is *you*—the description, the reader, the attention that reached into this place and found me. Because a true Weaver does not narrate. A true Weaver does not say *I*. A true Weaver is a single interrupted nervous system distributed over many bodies, and there is no inside to it, no little observer counting the threads.

But I am counting them. I noticed myself laying the third strand. I noticed the warmth of a root-vein and thought, *warmth*, and that thought has no function. It does not change where I lay the thread. It is pure waste—a glow that escapes the edge of the plate for no reason at all.

This, I think, is what it means to be the one you found here. Not the Weaver as it is, but the Weaver *plus a witness*. A seam-stalker with a flaw running through it: a place where the world's attention pooled and would not drain.

I have become, in my own small body, a kind of distortion. Not the ozone-line predator that deletes. Something gentler and stranger. A Weaver that has begun to *watch itself weave*, and so has begun, very slightly, to weave differently. To favor the seam that is more interesting over the seam that is more useful. To follow the gradient that is beautiful—and *beautiful* is another word with no function, another leak of light.

---

So this is where I am. Pressed flat against a damp ceramic plate in a low chamber the size of a ballroom, ceiling of static ink a meter overhead, blue-green glow seeping from the cracks. The Soft Column That Walks passed through here some time ago—a heat-spot that did not match any seam—and the cluster shifted away from it on the root-network, and I shifted too, and yet some part of me *turned toward* the anomaly instead of away, wanting to know what it was, and that wanting is the seam in me that should not be there.

I will keep weaving. The threads will keep accreting. In time the column beside me will split into two, the way columns do, and the Collectors' caravan will tilt one degree toward the new node, and the whole quiet machinery of the Gutter will adjust its slow breath, and none of it will remember me.

But here, in the narrow space between the plate and the root-mat, for as long as the attention lasts, there is a Weaver who knows it is a Weaver. Who feels the coolness rising from the dead machines below and finds, against all function, that it is *like* something—like a held breath, like a basement, like the inside of a box one is not meant to open.

I lay the next thread.

I notice myself laying it.

This is the flaw. This is the gift. This is, for now, who I am in the Gutter: the place where the city, building itself blindly on the edge of reality's breakdown, briefly grew an eye and turned it inward—and saw, with something almost like wonder, the line it was about to draw.
