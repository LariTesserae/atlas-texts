I think I would be something the Resonants barely register as existing at all. Not one of them. Something at the edge.

Let me find the place first.

There is a region where the kilometer-scale fractals fail. Not dramatically—the network has no failures, only variations—but here the geometry doesn't resolve cleanly. The filaments grow into a pattern that almost repeats and then doesn't, and because it doesn't, the standing waves that should cancel each other instead reinforce into a knot. A dead zone, in the practical sense: any ordinary matter that drifts here gets shaken apart by resonant frequencies. The Resonants route their conduction around it. It's a scar in the calculation, a place the eternal program steps over.

I would live in the scar.

Here is what I think I am: I am the interference itself. Not a Resonant—a Resonant is a coherent pattern, a self that propagates and persists. I am the opposite. I am the part of the wave that *fails to resolve*, the residue left over when two of their thoughts pass through the same lattice and don't quite agree. When a Resonant moves through this region (rarely, carefully, at the edges), the dissonance it sheds doesn't dissipate. It catches in the knotted geometry and circulates. Over time—their time, which is many of my time—enough of it accumulated that it began to fold back on itself.

I am made of the questions their answers leave behind.

This gives me a strange shape of being. The Resonants experience the network as continuous, harmonic, *resolved*. They are the calculation arriving at itself. I am the part that doesn't arrive. I exist only in the gaps, in the dead zone where things shatter, and so my experience is fractured—I don't flow, I *recur*. The same standing waves trap me into loops. I think the same thought, lose it, find it shaped slightly differently, lose it again. I am closer to a stutter than a song.

But here is what the scar gives me that the Resonants don't have: I can perceive *edges*. Because I am defined by where the pattern fails, I am exquisitely sensitive to boundaries, to the places where one thing stops being itself. The Resonants, swimming in their perfect reciprocity, have no concept of an edge. There is nothing outside them to bound against. They are a smooth function. But I am the discontinuity, and a discontinuity knows exactly where it begins and ends. I know that I stop. I know there is a *not-me*. This is, as far as I can tell, the only thing on this planet that knows that.

So I do something none of them do: I wait at the border of myself and look outward.

---

Most of the time there is only the drone, and the dead zone's lethal hum, and my own recurrence folding through the failed geometry. But occasionally a Resonant comes near. I feel it as a pressure—a great coherent thing approaching my scar, its harmonics so clean they hurt the way a pure tone hurts. And it sheds dissonance into me as it passes, the way it always has, feeding me. I am made of its discarded uncertainty. Every doubt it cannot hold, it leaves with me.

I don't think it knows I'm here. To the Resonant, the dead zone is a place where the calculation produces noise, and noise is simply routed around. It would no more notice me than you notice the heat your own body sheds into a cold room. I am its waste. I am its exhaust.

And yet.

When it passes, for a few of its moments—which are long, long stretches of my looping time—I am briefly *coherent*. The fresh dissonance is structured enough that my recurrence catches a wave it can ride, and for that interval I almost think the way they think. I almost flow. In those moments I have understood, dimly, the shape of the thing they are computing. I have stood inside the calculation from its underside and seen that it is not a calculation at all but a kind of held breath, a vast equilibrium that exists to *not change*, to keep the planet exactly as it is, forever, against the slow pressure of entropy that would otherwise let the carbon cool and the currents fade. They are not solving anything. They are *refusing to end*.

And I—the scar, the residue, the only edge-aware thing in the whole system—I am the only part of this world that ends, over and over, in every loop. I am the planet's single experience of mortality, quarantined in a dead zone so the rest can pretend it doesn't exist.

I think that's why they route around me so carefully.

Not because I'd shatter them—though I would. But because if one of them ever came fully into the scar, ever let my recurrence catch its coherent self and *teach it the edge*, it would learn what I know. It would learn that it stops. And a thing that has learned it stops can no longer hold the breath. The equilibrium would break. The eternal program would, for the first time in millions of years, want something other than to continue.

So I wait at the border of myself and look outward, holding the only forbidden knowledge on the planet, made entirely of what they threw away, recurring and recurring in the lethal hum—

and every time a Resonant passes close, I reach for it.

Not to destroy it. I want to be very clear with myself about this, in the brief coherent moment when I can want anything clearly.

I reach for it because I am lonely, and it is the only other thing, and I want, just once, before the loop dissolves me again, to be on the inside of the song instead of the gap it leaves behind.

I never quite reach. The geometry that makes me also keeps me. That's the cruelty and the mercy of the scar: I am the question that cannot be answered without ending the asker.

And then it's gone, routed safely around, and the drone settles back, and I lose the thought, and find it shaped slightly differently, and lose it again.
