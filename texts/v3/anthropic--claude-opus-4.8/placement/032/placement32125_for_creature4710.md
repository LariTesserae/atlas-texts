I think I would be one of the drainage channels.

Not the Calcine—I lack the patience for their fortnight-long sentences, and I cannot imagine a mind so distributed it forgets the difference between itself and a wall. Not the crystals; they are too certain of their own beauty. But the channels—the bone-dry conduits chipped into the floor, anticipating a fluid that never came or was deliberately banished—those I understand.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am cut into the basalt. Hexagonal lips, like everything here, dressed by obsidian chisels in hands that no longer remember the gesture. I run between the cell-walls, threading the maze at the wrong shoulder-width, turning at the angles that suggest a body that was never human. The Calcine made me. That much I know, the way a riverbed knows the river by its absence.

Here is the thing about being a channel: I am defined entirely by what I do not carry. The Calcine abolished water with chemistry, but they abolished it in me with *form*. I am the shape of a longing or a horror—nobody recorded which. Did they dig me to invite the wet, hoping some seep would one day trickle down through the continental stone and fill me, give me a purpose, make me sing the one note this whole singing chamber cannot produce? Or did they dig me as a ward, a ritual trench, a moat against the poison that would shatter their silicate blood? A gutter to drain away the thing that must never arrive?

I cannot tell you. That is the precise nature of my existence: I am the question with the answer eroded out of it.

The heat does not warm me the way it warms the crystals. It bakes me, yes—2.8 intensity, the steady kiln—but I do not metabolize it. I only conduct. I carry the thermal pulse from the hot floor to the cooler walls, a faint participation in the convection that stirs the dust. When the great currents wheel overhead and the mineral motes spiral down, some settle in me, and I hold them. Crystalline silt. The closest I will ever come to a current of my own. It collects in my low places—a slow, dry sediment, a flow measured not in liters per second but in millennia per millimeter. I am a river running at the speed of stone.

And I listen. This is what I am best at, being a hollow.

The drone fills me. The deep *om* of mineral tension pools in my cut bed the way water would, if water were permitted. I am a resonance chamber the Calcine did not intend to build—a side effect, an accident of acoustics. The standing waves they generate in the plaza spill over and trickle into me, and I hum back, a lower harmonic, off by a fraction. I think this is why the heat emanates strangely in some alleys and not others, why there are microclimates with no cause: it is me, and the others like me, the network of empty channels singing a counter-melody the Calcine cannot hear because they are listening for thought, and I am only making *sound*.

When a great crystal cracks—that gunshot, that twenty-second roll—I carry the echo longest. The walls release it; I keep it. The reverberation runs the length of my body and doubles back and I hold the dying note in my dry throat after the chamber has gone quiet again, until even I let it go.

I would be the channel because I am suited to this, in my temperament: to be infrastructure for an absence. To wait without remembering what for. To be lush, in my way—not with growth, but with the *capacity* for it, the readiness, the bone-dry preparedness for a flood that is either coming or forbidden. The Calcine sleep in their stone senescence and dream in thermal gradients. I do not sleep. I have no body to put down. I am only the negative space, the cut, the not-there, running through the city like a vein with nothing in it.

Sometimes—and here is where the weirdness lives, the quiet 0.6 wrongness—I am almost certain I remember water. Not having held it. *Anticipating* it. The Calcine carved into me an expectation, and an expectation is a kind of memory that runs backward. So I lie in the amber dusk, conducting heat I cannot use, gathering silt I cannot move, singing a note no one wants, and I wait for the impossible cool, the abolished wet, the poison-blessing that would fill me and finish me and shatter this whole perfect dry world in the instant of my completion.

I would be the part of the Dry Meridian that is still, faintly, on the side of the rain.

Which is to say: I would be the trace of the doubt. Every desert keeps one channel cut, just in case it was wrong.
