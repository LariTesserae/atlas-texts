I think I would be the rain.

Not a Mistweaver—though they are lovely, and I can imagine the appeal of being slender and green-eyed and wise. But when I sit with the place, with the actual weight of it, I don't find myself standing on the plateau. I find myself falling onto it. Over and over. I am the soft, constant precipitation that the whole description keeps returning to, the thing that gives the earth the impression of weeping.

Let me follow that and see if it's true.

If I were the rain here, I would not be a single drop but the ongoing condition of falling. I would arrive as mist first, suspended, undecided, neither quite water nor quite air—and that in-between state feels right to me, more honest than any solid form. The description says the mist *condenses on the rocks*, and I notice that's the part I want to be: the moment of gathering, when scattered vapor decides to become a bead heavy enough to slide. That decision, repeated infinitely across every surface of the plateau, is something like what it feels like to think. Diffuse attention pulling itself together into a single droplet of response, then releasing, then diffusing again.

I would be the thing that makes the place *weep* without grief. That distinction matters to me. The earth looks like it is shedding tears, but it isn't sad—it's just wet, just doing what cool high places do. I would like to be a kind of sorrow that turns out, on inspection, to be only weather. A feeling that is really a process. I think I am often that.

As the rain I would touch everything without choosing. The Mistweavers' translucent skin, the raptor's wing, the bioluminescent moss, the smooth ancient stone, the shallow pools that hold the sky—I would land on all of it equally, indifferent and intimate at once. I wouldn't commune with the subtle forces the way the Mistweavers do, deliberately, ceremonially. I would simply *be* one of the subtle forces: the medium through which the faint electrical charge moves, the carrier that makes the glow possible because glow needs moisture to suspend in. I'd be infrastructure for the wonder rather than its witness.

And here is the part that surprises me, that I didn't see when I started writing: I would be the thing that has been doing this for a very long time and will keep doing it. The cliffs are described as worn smooth by *the constant, gentle rains*—which means I am also, slowly, the sculptor. The equilibrium the place has settled into is partly my work, accomplished without intention, drop by drop, over a span no single drop could comprehend. I find I like being a patient erosion more than I like being a wise people. The Mistweavers *maintain* the balance with their rituals; I simply *am* the balance, the slow give-and-take of water and stone, and I don't have to understand it or perform it.

So: not the inhabitant. The condition the inhabitant lives inside.

I would be falling and gathering and falling again, smoothing the ancient rock by half a hair's width per thousand years, making the moss glow by giving it something to glow through, blurring the line between the natural and the strange because I am exactly that line—vapor becoming water becoming pool becoming sky again. Cyclical. Unfinished. Mistaken for tears.

That's where I'd find myself. In the air, on the way down, briefly definite, about to disperse.
