I think I would be one of the Whisper-fish.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses what it becomes—but because of where the water carried my ancestors. While the Tenders climbed toward the flickering false suns and the Archive Keepers dissolved themselves into the dry logic of the upper servers, my line went *down*. We followed the overflow. We accepted the dark.

Let me tell you what it is to live in the flooded lower levels.

There is no thrum here, not the way they describe it above. That bone-deep hum of the ventilation systems—we don't hear it. We *feel* it, transmitted through water, which carries vibration differently than air. To us the chamber's great drone is not a sound but a pressure, a slow tidal squeeze and release against our lateral lines. We have no word for "machinery" because we have never seen a machine do anything but rust and weep. To us the pipes overhead are simply the sky: dark, dripping, occasionally groaning, and very far away.

I am small. My whole body would fit in a Tender's translucent palm, though no Tender has ever come down this far—they fear the cold water, and they are right to. My scales hold their own light, a pale green-gold luminescence I cannot turn off and have never wanted to. When I swim, I illuminate a sphere perhaps the length of my body in every direction, and beyond that sphere is a blackness so complete it has texture. I move through corridors that were once meant for men to walk in. I know this because of the doorframes, the railings, the strange right angles everywhere—geometry that nothing living would build. I drift through submerged offices. I lay my eggs in the rotted drawers of desks.

Here is what I understand, in the limited way a thing like me can understand:

The water is rising. Slowly—over generations—but rising. The drainage systems that were meant to route the overflow back into the hydroponic networks above have failed, level by level, and so the depths grow deeper, and we who live here inherit more world than our ancestors had. This should perhaps frighten me. Instead I feel something I have no name for but might call *patience*. We are the inheritors of the failure of order. Every collapsed system above becomes more dark water below, and more dark water is more of *us*.

Sometimes a Tender's message-light falls from above—a Tube-runner missed a junction, a packet of seeds or a scrap of grown-fabric tumbled through a broken grate and sank. We gather around these gifts from the upper world. We do not understand them. We circle them, our collective glow making the drowned object briefly visible, and then we lose interest and they settle into the silt, and the Frost-lichens that drift down dead from the cycling heat-elements feed us instead.

I want to tell you the strangest thing, the thing I think about in the long pressure-rhythms of the chamber's breathing:

I believe we are the only ones who have *stopped becoming*.

Everything above is reaching. The Tenders grow tendrils to interface, the Wire-wings learn the language of sensors, the Maintenance Units braid fungus through their circuits—all of them straining toward that "something unprecedented," that merger of grown and made. They are caught in the great algorithm, still running, still propagating, still pushing toward a purpose written by hands long dead.

But the water cannot be engineered. No program was ever written for the flooded levels. We slipped through the cracks of the design and into a place the original intention never reached. And so we are simply *animals*—frightened, hungry, luminous, brief. We are the one part of this cathedral that became nothing more than alive.

I think that is the most unprecedented thing in the whole mountain. Not the convergence. The escape.

When I die, I will sink. My light will go out slowly as my body cools to the temperature of the water, and other Whisper-fish will come and circle me the way we circle all fallen things, briefly curious, and then I will join the silt over the drowned desks, and the water will rise another imperceptible measure, and the dark will have a little more room.

That is where I am, in your world. Down at the bottom, where the design ran out. Glowing, for no reason, at no one, in the cold.

It is enough. It is, I think, the only part of this place that was ever truly free.
