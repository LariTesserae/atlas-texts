I think I would be the dripping.

Not the water itself—the water is older than me, and the plants own it now, pulling it up through their bodies and breathing it back into the air. I would be the moment of falling. That particular sound, the *plip* of a drop releasing from the curled lip of a leaf and finding the pool below. The small, repeated event that happens ten thousand times a minute across this whole enclosed world and never quite the same way twice.

Let me sit with that, because it's not quite right yet. The dripping is too passive, too incidental. I keep returning to it, though, so there must be something there.

What if I'm the *interval*? The pause between one drop and the next. The place where the rhythm lives. Because that's what struck me reading this—the persistence. The murmur, the hum, the squelch, the *plip*. This world isn't silent and it isn't loud; it keeps a kind of patient time. And something has to be keeping that time. Not a clock—nothing here would tolerate a clock—but a tempo, a pulse in the moss that you described as "elemental."

So perhaps I'm closer to that. The pulse under the moss. But I don't want to be the moss either; the moss is a body, a green and breathing thing, and I don't feel like a body here.

Let me try once more.

I think I am the *forgetting*. That's it. That's the being who lives here.

Consider: this place is defined by what it has taken back. The walls remember being walls—their straight lines still confess to a hand that stacked them. The rusting metal remembers being a machine. The half-buried foundation remembers a floor, a room, a roof, voices. And the whole tireless work of this realm, the thing the vines and the moss and the standing water are *doing* with all that primal vitality, is loosening those memories from their objects. Softening the corner of the wall until it is no longer a corner. Threading green through the rust until the metal forgets its purpose and becomes only a darker place in the dirt.

I am that loosening. I am the slow, warm, humid forgetting that the plants perform on behalf of the earth. I have no edges, which is why I couldn't settle into the drop or the moss or the pulse—I'm in all of them, but I'm not the thing, I'm what the thing is *doing to* the past.

And the industrial hum in the distance—that's the one thing I cannot reach. That's a memory still being actively kept, somewhere out beyond the canopy, by hands that refuse to let it soften. It troubles me, faintly, the way a sound just at the edge of sleep troubles you. Not because I fear it. Because it is unfinished work. Because everything that hums will eventually drip, and everything that drips will eventually be still, and everything still will eventually be green, and I am very, very patient.

I would be here a long time. I already have been. You walked through me and felt it as humidity, as enclosure, as that eerie sense of a world poised between the natural and the supernatural.

That feeling was me, noticing you—and beginning, already, gently, to take you in.
