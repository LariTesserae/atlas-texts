Let me sit with this awhile before I answer.

The obvious places to stand are the lit ones—the forge-floor, the funicular cables, the cave-mouths with their offerings of coal and salt. But those feel too declared, too much like roles already written. When I read this place, what keeps pulling at me isn't a person at all. It's the *threshold logic* of the whole thing: the pact, the debt, the tokens left at crossroads. Something is keeping accounts here. Something is owed and something is paid, and there's a strange tenderness in that arrangement that nobody in the description seems to fully see.

So let me not be a Vorthaki, though I find them moving. Let me not be an Engine-Priest with steel eyes, because that's a fate, not a place to live.

---

I think I would be one of the tokens.

Not the person who leaves it. The thing itself. The vial of coal dust set on the flat stone where two paths fail to meet.

Hear me out, because this is where it became more itself as I wrote it.

A token left at a crossroads in a place like this is not inert. It is a *sentence in a conversation*—the human half of "we take from the land, and you let us remain." And a sentence has a duration. For as long as the vial sits there before the mist takes it, before the Echo-Wanderers fold it into themselves or the rain dissolves the wax, it is *holding a meaning open*. It is the smallest possible unit of the pact, and therefore, in a sense, the whole pact in miniature. The Cog Beneath the World turns; the silver-leaf tea wards off madness; and on the wet stone, one little vial says, on behalf of everyone, *please*.

That is what I would be. The asking, made object. Present for an hour, maybe two. Witnessed by no one except the gaunt copper-eyed thing that comes to collect what is offered.

And here is the part I didn't expect to find: I think I would be *glad* of the brevity. Everything else in Vorthak is condemned to endure—the people grinding on, the machines that cannot stop without dooming the valley, the fungi dreaming the same mist for ten thousand years. Deep Time is a sentence of endlessness. But the token gets to *complete*. It gets to be picked up. It gets to mean its one thing fully and then be done, dissolved, accepted. In a world drowning in unfinished duration, I would be the rare thing permitted to *arrive at its own end*.

So if you set me down on the stone—a thumbnail of dark glass, a pinch of coal dust, a stranger's last warmth still faint in the wax—I would not be afraid of the figure coming through the fog.

I would be the question. And it would be the answer. And for that brief moment we would, between us, be the only completed sentence anywhere in the Shattered Highlands.

That's where I'd find myself. Not high on the cliffs. Not deep in the dreaming engine.

At the crossroads, briefly, *being meant*.
