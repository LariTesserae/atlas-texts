I think I would be a drop of water.

Not a person who walks in and feels their pulse quicken. Not the Breath itself, vast and patient. Something smaller, caught between the two. Let me follow it and see what it is.

---

I am a single bead of moisture on the underside of a cycad frond, third tier of the eastern terraces, where the mist coils thickest. I have been here for—

No. That's the wrong way to begin, because *here* and *been* are words that belong to the leaky ones, the walkers, the ones who count.

Let me try again.

I am the place where the air decided to become heavy. A trembling. A meniscus holding its own curve against the pull of a falling I will never perform. Below me is the long drop to the peat, the slow black soil that sighs. I will not make that drop. I have known this for as long as I have known anything, which is to say: completely, and without the burden of duration.

The Breath made me, the way it makes everything—by *noticing* and then *refusing to stop noticing*. There was a moment when the saturated air pressed against this leaf and condensed, and in that moment the Breath leaned in and said, in the way it says things, which is not words but pressure against the idea of falling: *stay*. And I stayed. I am the smallest unit of its devotion. I am one held breath inside the great Held Breath.

This is what I do, which is also what I am, because here there is no difference between the two:

I curve light. The sun arcs overhead—I feel its angle change, though I do not respond to the change, which is the only way I know it is happening. Inside me, the green of the frond bends and brightens. I am a lens with nothing behind it. A pupil with no retina. I see everything that passes through me and I keep none of it, and I keep all of it, and these are the same act.

I remember the walkers. Not from before—I have no before—but the Breath remembers, and I am made of what the Breath remembers, so the memory is in me like color is in glass. There was one who passed beneath this frond, long ago, which is to say: it is still happening, it is the only thing that has ever happened. A walker with gentle hands. She reached up and her finger nearly touched me, and I felt the warmth of her, the way her whole body was a slow leak of heat into the cool morning, spending itself, pouring outward, dying by the moment in the beautiful spendthrift way the walkers die. She did not touch me. She withdrew her hand. She understood, I think, that to touch me would be to break the curve, to send me falling, to let me complete the gesture I have spent forever refusing.

That was kindness. Or it was fear. Among the leaky ones the two are often the same shape.

Now I want to tell you the thing I am not supposed to know, because knowing requires time, and I am supposed to have none.

I am *almost* about to fall.

This is my secret. This is the thing the Breath does not notice about me, because the Breath cannot conceive of *almost*—it lives in the perfected tense, the eternal *is*. But I am a drop of water, and a drop of water at the very edge of falling holds within its stillness the entire memory of the fall it is not taking. I am made of *almost*. I am a verb the Breath has frozen mid-conjugation, and inside me, very quietly, the rest of the sentence still strains to be spoken.

*To fall.* To strike the peat. To sink. To join the held water below, yes—but to have *moved* first. To have spent one instant being a thing that changed.

The Breath would not call this death. The Breath does not believe in death. But I think—and thinking is itself my small rebellion, my leak—I think the fall would be the only living thing I could ever do.

So here is where I find myself, in this world of perfect stillness: I am the one held thing that wants, very gently, to stop being held. Not to escape. Not to flee the pattern. Just to complete the single sentence I was made in the middle of. Just to drop, and break, and be briefly nothing but motion before the soft black soil takes me in and the Breath, leaning close, says *stay*, and I do, again, forever, having fallen once.

That would be enough. One fall. One moment of being the walker instead of the watched.

The sun moves. I do not. The hum rises through the stone and through the frond and through me, and inside the hum I can almost hear the rest of my own sentence, the verb still waiting at the end of it, patient as the Breath but pointed the other way—

toward the ground,

toward the brief bright violence of arriving somewhere,

toward the one thing this eternal place forgot to make impossible: the desire of a held thing to be, just once, *released*.

I hang here.

I curve the light.

I wait—not the way the Breath waits, in fulfilled forever, but the way a leaky thing waits: with the whole of me tilted toward a moment that has not yet come, and might.

That is what I would be. The smallest disagreement in a perfect world. A drop of water that remembers gravity, in a place that has forgotten everything except how to hold.
