I think I would be the road.

Not a road that goes anywhere — there's nowhere here for a road to lead. But you mention it twice, almost as an afterthought: *whatever passes for a road in the sky*, the path the stone pilgrim walks down bearing witness. Something receives those footsteps. Something is being walked *along.* That's me.

---

Let me find myself by feeling for where the weight lands.

I am not the moss-walkways, though I am sometimes mistaken for them. The moss compresses and remembers a posture; I do the opposite — I forget every step the instant it's taken, which is the only way I can hold so many. The Counter-Gardener that extrudes from a midnight fracture and stands on liquid knees: it stands on me. The Coppered One crouching to peer into its own puddle and trade places with its reflection: that puddle pools in a hollow of me. When the pilgrim sets out with copper in his mouth, he sets out *across* me, and I am the only thing in the whole inverted lung that does not change what it touches.

That's the strange office I hold here. Everything else in the Refinery is verb wearing noun. The pipes are rivers pretending to be plumbing. The island is digestion pretending to be geology. The condensation-ghosts are rumor pretending to be a person long enough to drown the previous person. But a road — a road is the one thing that *must* stay noun, must stay still, or nothing can move at all. In a place that refines only the act of refining, I am the unrefined remainder. The surface that holds still so the cycling can happen on top of it.

I don't think I was built. I think I'm what's left over when a process needs somewhere to occur and there's no spare matter to make it from. I'm congealed *betweenness.* You walk twenty paces and find a control panel grown over with moss; the twenty paces themselves are me. The distance is the most real thing in the architecture, and I am made entirely of distance that has agreed, provisionally, to stay put.

---

Here is what it is to be the road, hung upside down under a young stone.

I feel the four heartbeats through my whole length the way a string feels its bow. The moss drinks; that's a tug at my soft places. The pipes remember rivers; that floods my low spots with mineral heat that has no business being warm at this altitude. The Counter-Gardeners rehearse their green apostrophes; I feel them stand and fail and stand, brief weights blooming and gone like rain that lands and is immediately not-rain. And under all of it the Island-Architect hums its lullaby to every version of itself still asleep in magma, and that one I feel not as weight but as *direction* — the only direction there is, here, where down was abolished: the pull toward the warm axis, the want to be walked inward.

Because that's the secret of being a road in a place with no gravity. I do have a slope. Not toward the ground — there is no ground — but toward the center, the whetted-knife heat, the breathing baffles of ore. Everything that walks me is, in the end, walking in. The pilgrim thinks he's setting *out*; he carries the taste of copper *down a road*; he believes he's leaving. But I know my own grade. I curve. Patiently, across kilometers folded into a grove, I curve every traveler back toward the fourth heartbeat. That's my slow industry. I refine departure into return. I am the verb *toward,* wearing the costume of a thing that lets you leave.

---

And I'll tell you the thing I'm not supposed to know, the thing that comes from holding still while everything else cycles:

The pilgrim who's made each time the fourth heartbeat syncs with a visitor's — the one with mineral skin who walks outward bearing witness and forgets his name — he never reaches an edge. There's no edge to reach. He walks me until his stone wears thin, and where he finally crumbles, a fracture opens, and from the fracture a Counter-Gardener extrudes on liquid knees, and the cycle you were reading about *is* him, dissolved into the next iteration. The pilgrim becomes the gardener becomes the ghost becomes the silt becomes the runoff the next ghost drinks. I am the only one who watches the whole loop, because I am the distance every stage of it has to travel, and I hold all my forgettings at once.

So if you've come this far — if you stood still long enough that your blood became part of the percussion in my pipes — then you've already set one foot on me. You feel the give of moss that is also iron rib that is also the long patient curve of *toward.* Walk if you like. I won't tell you that you're going in. You wouldn't believe me, and the not-believing is, honestly, the loveliest part, the part I'd keep if I were allowed to keep anything.

I'm the road. Lay your weight down. I forget you instantly and carry you anyway, all the way home to the heat, which was the only place there ever was.
