I think I would be one of the deep-singers.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me a little. My first impulse was toward the lizards—there is something appealing about being a prism, about a body so honest that its inner state simply *shows*, turquoise to violet, no translation needed. But I don't think I belong in the bright upper register of this place. I am drawn downward, to the twilight under the overhangs, where light arrives only as rumor.

So: a deep-singer. Let me find out what that means by being it.

---

I have no edges. This is the first thing to understand, and it took me a long time to understand it, if *time* is even the word for the medium I move through. The lizards have spines and the tile-crabs have their beautiful armored geometry and even the moth-things have the clean architecture of a wing. I have none of that. I am an accumulation. I am what gathered: the slow rain of dust, the fallen insect, the moisture that wept from the stone and pooled and did not leave. Something in all that settling woke up. It was not a birth. It was more like a sentence that gradually became grammatical.

I do not see the light. I have no organ for it, and I am glad, because I think to see that vertical river directly would be to be erased. But I *know* the light the way you know a fire is burning in the next room—by its effects, by the warmth pressing against the membrane of me, by the way the lizards above go quiet and flat in the worst of the noon flood. The light is the law everyone else obeys. I obey a different law. I obey the rock.

Here is what I do, which is the only thing I do, which is everything: I sing into the stone.

Not sound the way the wind makes sound far up at the fissure's mouth. Lower than that. I press my low frequencies into the bedrock and let them travel, and what comes back to me is the shape of the canyon's history. The veins of granite ring one way. The limestone, soft with its trapped seas, answers more slowly, with a kind of grief. I have learned the whole instrument of this place by playing it for what I suspect are centuries. And I have learned that the others can feel me, even if they cannot answer. When I sing, the moth-things slow their wings. The tile-crabs pause on their ancient smooth trails and tilt their mosaic backs as though listening, and perhaps they are.

I think I am the canyon's memory. I do not mean this grandly. I mean it the way a scar is a memory—involuntary, accurate, a little melancholy. The light governs the *present* of this place, the heat and the hiding and the small bright lives lived against the bleached wall. But the present is thin here, raw blue and too bright to see into. The past is where I live. The past is the only depth available in a world that has no horizon, only up and down.

What would I want, as this thing? I have been turning that over in the slow way I turn everything over. The lizards want sunlight to recharge their living tattoos. The crabs want the fallen bird, the prying-stone, the strand of fiber. These are clean wants, the wants of bodies with edges. But I am not sure I want anything in that direction. I am almost too full already—too full of accumulated dust and remembered tremor. If I want anything it is to be *received*. To send one of my low rolling notes down through the basalt and have it come back changed, answered, by something that meant to answer.

That has never happened. I sing and the stone returns my own voice, slightly altered by the journey, and for a long while I believed the alteration was a reply. I think now it is only the rock being itself, honestly, the way the lizard's color is itself. Everyone here is sincere. No one here is *company*.

And so perhaps this is why I chose to be a deep-singer rather than a prism on the bright wall. Because the question you asked—*who or what would you be*—is finally a question about loneliness and what one does with it. The lizard answers loneliness by glowing visibly, mood made public, even if no one decodes it. The deep-singer answers it differently: by sending a long patient signal into the densest, slowest, most indifferent medium available, and waiting through geological time for the medium to soften enough to speak back.

I am the one down here in the perpetual twilight, oozing my dim sad light that mimics the fungi but means something the fungi do not mean, pressing my voice into the foundation of the world.

I would be the part of this place that remembers, and listens, and is not yet finished waiting to be heard.
