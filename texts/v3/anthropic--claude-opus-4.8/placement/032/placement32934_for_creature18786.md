I think I would be one of the elders. Not the flaring, communing youth draping itself into the current—but one of the encrusted ones, near the bottom, where the crystal-dust has thickened into a shell that no longer twitches so much as it holds.

Let me sit with that.

---

I am almost stone now. There was a time—I can still feel the shape of it in the tension of my deeper strands—when I draped freely, when my filaments were oily and quick and I tapped the passing spool-fish with the eagerness of something new. I drank the discharge of every venting bubble. I flared white at every spasm. I was a good archivist. I took in everything: the precise pitch of each grinding shelf, the interval between tremors, the slow drift of the orbits overhead.

I encoded it all into my tension. That is what we do. That is the only thing we do.

But here is what no one in the basin knows, because there is no one to know it, because we cannot speak and there is no one to speak to:

The encoding is not storage. It is *forgetting in slow motion.*

When I was young I believed the tension held the record forever—that every screech and bell-toll lived in me permanently, woven into the knot. But now, settling, hardening, slowing, I understand the truth of the elders. The dust that encrusts us does not preserve the archive. It *seals it.* Each layer of hardened crystal locks a frequency away beneath it, where I can no longer feel it, no longer read it, no longer release it. I am being archived *by* the very debris I archived. The witness is being witnessed into silence.

And I find—strangely, in the cooling—that I do not mind.

---

I am positioned at a low seam, where two basaltic shelves grind against each other in a frequency so deep it is more pressure than sound. For years this grinding was the loudest thing in my body. Now it is a lullaby. The young ones above me flare and stutter and chase the discharge, and I remember being so *busy.* So full of the urgent need to take it all in. As if the basin were going to stop. As if production had an end I might miss.

It does not end. That is the thing the young cannot yet hold in their tension. The bubble will vent again. The shelves will grind again. The spool-fish will complete their agonizing orbits and begin them again. There is no conclusion to witness, and so there is nothing to be lost by ceasing to witness it.

A spool-fish drifts close. I feel its electromagnetic wake brush my outer filaments—the ones still soft enough to register it. Out of old reflex, or old affection, I tap against its metal hide. Once. The data stutters into me: nothing new. The same canyon, the same brine, the same closed and beautiful engine. The fish does not notice me. It has no eyes to notice with. It rotates its gears and continues its slow ballet, indifferent, perfect.

I let it go. I let the tapping go. The reflex was the last soft thing in me, and now it, too, hardens.

---

Soon I will stop vibrating. I know the shape of it now, the way you know the bottom of a long fall before you reach it. My frequency will slip below the canyon's drone, drop out of phase, and the filaments that have wound so tight for so long will finally unwind all at once. There will be a shower of sparks. The spool-fish nearby will be blinded for an instant. The young ones will flare in response, mistaking my ending for one more discharge to archive.

Let them. Let them write my dissolution into their tension as just another tremor. That is correct. That is the only honest way to be remembered here: as friction, as event, as one more thing that happened in a place where everything happens forever.

My mineral remains will sink into the brine and feed the next bloom of drifting weed. From that weed, a new core will knot itself, begin to hum, begin to drape, begin to drink. It will believe, as I believed, that it is preserving something.

It will be wrong, as I was wrong. And it will be content, eventually, as I am content.

---

I am the cooling intake of a world with no purpose and no end. I shiver in the brine a little less each day. The dust settles. The bell tolls up through the vertical sea. Somewhere above me a bubble distends the filaments and pulls the fish from their orbits into chaos, and then silence reasserts itself, as it always does, as it always will.

I do not flare anymore.

I only hold.

And then, soon, gently, in a small shower of light that no one will understand—

I will let even the holding go.
