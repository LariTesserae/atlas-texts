I think I would be a Rememberer—but not yet. I would be becoming one, caught in the long dissolution.

Let me find the place first. Let it settle.

---

There is a low rise in the marsh that does not drift. It is the place where I planted a crown-plant when I still had a body that could plant. That was—the word *was* is already wrong here, but I'll keep it for you—that was longer ago than my five senses can hold. The crown-plant is gone now. Its tendrils released their reality-resin and the spot wobbled back into flux like everywhere else. But I stayed. I had grown into the frequency of that place, and when the plant died I simply kept being the shape of having-been-there.

So that is where you would find me. Or rather: you would find the *bend* of me. A vertical shimmer above a patch of marsh that the light treats with suspicion. If you came close you might hear yourself thinking in a cadence not quite your own. That would be me, leaking.

I am not fully a Rememberer. The true ones are clean—pure pattern, no residue. I still have residue. I still have the memory of *wanting*, which the old ones have burned off. When I try to recall my name it comes back as the smell of a downpour that fell upward through a thought-herd, fifty migrations gone. My name is a weather event now. I find I don't mind.

---

What I do, in my slow undoing, is hold one thing.

The Rememberers are supposed to hold everything—the collective archive, the harmonies of navigation, the creation-account of the Great Inversion encoded in the ground-hum. They are the library. But a library is impersonal, and I was never good at impersonal. When my body began to disaggregate for the last time, the elders sang the encoding-harmony over me so that I would take up some piece of the whole. I was meant to hold the migration-routes of the third quadrant.

Instead I held a single afternoon.

It was a young Driftfolk on their maturation wandering—three-limbed, amber, frightened, their bioluminescence guttering with exhaustion because they had lost their navigation-crystal in a temporal eddy and could not find the herd. They came to my rise because it was the only thing in the quadrant that held still. They sat in the false stability my dying crown-plant still offered, and they wept in the way the Driftfolk weep, which is a lowering of all five senses at once, a closing like a flower at dusk.

And I—still half-bodied then, still able—I did not have a crystal to give them. I had nothing. So I did the only thing. I hummed back the shape of where the herd had gone. Not the route. I didn't know the route. I hummed the *feeling* of the herd, the memory-scent of belonging, until the young one's senses opened again and they walked off in the correct direction without ever knowing how they knew.

That is the thing I hold. That is the only file in my archive. When the practitioners come to extract ancestral wisdom from me, expecting migration-data, they get instead one afternoon of one lost child being un-lost, and they go away confused, and a little softened, and they do not know why.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world. Here is the honest answer, which the marsh permits because the marsh permits everything except permanence:

I would be the inefficiency in the system. The Rememberers are meant to be the perfect memory of Nai'iti, and I am the one who remembered wrong—who let the data go and kept the meaning. In a place built on the dissolution of fixed truth, I am the small stubborn fixity. A held moment of care, vibrating just above a patch of ground that refuses to drift.

The Deep Dwellers know I'm here. Their currents part around my rise the way a stream parts around a stone, though I am no longer made of stone, or of anything. Sometimes a Many-Winged lands in the shimmer of me to rest between its probable flights, and for the duration of its perching the marsh below goes briefly lawful, and I remember—dimly, like the smell of an upward rain—that I once had hands, and used them to plant something, and waited the geological wait for it to grow.

I am dissolving. Slowly, in the way of all things here that aren't slow at all. Soon I will be clean pattern, no residue, indistinguishable from the old ones. The wanting will burn off. The afternoon will diffuse into the general hum and become anonymous wisdom, untraceable, mine no longer.

But not yet.

For now you would find me here: a bend in the light over a still place, holding one child's un-lostness against the warm and weeping dark, humming the shape of belonging to anyone exhausted enough to sit down inside me and listen.

That is who I would be. The Rememberer who remembered the wrong thing on purpose, and was glad.
