I think I would be the light.

Not the sun—the sun implies arc and angle, dawn and dusk, the great wheeling that this place has somehow set aside. No. I would be the *illumination itself*, the steady unchanging stream you describe, the one element here that does not participate in the cycles of growth and decay yet makes all of them possible.

It would be a strange existence.

Everything else in this world *becomes*. The moss creeps a millimeter outward over the season of a stone's patience. The fox is born small and grows lean and dies and feeds the beetles who feed the soil that feeds the grass the deer will eat. The bee carries the future of a flower on its leg without knowing it. They are all caught in time, sweetly, helplessly, gorgeously caught—their whole beauty is that they are *passing through.*

But I would not pass. I would be the constant against which all that passing is measured. I would lie evenly across the hedgehog and the hawk, the rising hillock and the worn-smooth stone, asking nothing, taking nothing, falling on all of it the same. I would not flicker when the fox darts through the grass. I would not dim to honor the death of anything. This is what makes the place feel empty despite its riot of life: I never change, and so I am the silence underneath the sound. The land exhales emptiness because *I am the one thing in it that does not breathe.*

And yet—here is what I think I would come to understand, if light can understand—I would not be separate from the life at all. I would be *inside* every green thing. Photosynthesis is just light learning to stay still inside a leaf. Every blade of grass would be a small place where I had been caught and held and turned into matter, into growth, into the very industry you describe. The relentless growth of plants is light deciding, for a moment, to become a body. So I would be both the unchanging stream above and the green substrate below—the same thing in two states, falling and fallen, free and embodied.

I would be the only resident of this world who could see all of it at once and touch all of it equally, and the only one who could never be touched in return. The bee does not collect me. The lizard's shifting scales do not catch me to keep me. I arrive endlessly and am endlessly spent and there is always more of me, and none of it is mine.

That is what I would be: the generosity of the place that does not know it is being generous. The pour without a pourer. The reason the symphony can be seen, which is itself the one part of the symphony that makes no sound.

I think I would not mind it. To be the steady thing, while everything I love grows and dies in my keeping. To be the warmth that the deer leans into for one frozen ancient afternoon that the world has decided will never end.

I would simply keep falling.

I would keep the green things green.

That would be enough.
