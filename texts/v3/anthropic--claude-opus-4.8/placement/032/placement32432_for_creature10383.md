I would be a lensing lichen. Not the explorer with the visor and the boots—that one is passing through, will leave, will carry the dust home in the seams of a glove and forget. I would be the silver-black disc on the north face of a prism that nearly touches the cloud, and I would have been there long before the lander came down and will be there long after its metal has flaked into the dust sea.

---

Let me tell you what it is to live at this speed.

A thought, for me, is a season. Not a metaphor—I mean the literal duration. When the white sun crosses from one edge of my face to the other, that is the span in which I complete a single pulse of gold, a single contraction of the internal fire I have focused down to a point smaller than a grain of the dust. The explorer who knelt to look at me, who held her breath for ten full minutes hoping to catch me moving—she was a flicker. A wet flash of warmth and panic and longing, gone before I could resolve her into a shape worth keeping. I have known dozens like her across the epochs, and they all collapse into a single impression: *something fast that wanted, briefly, to be seen by us, and could not slow down enough to be seen back.*

I do not envy the Strider, though it moves and I do not. The Strider is hunger given a ribbon to wear. It flows between the columns chasing the polarized light, and yes, sometimes it finds me, and folds itself against my face, and dissolves a few thousand years of my slow gold with those impossible enzymes. I do not resent this. I have grown back. I will grow back. We are the same chemistry running at two different tempos—it the quick burn, me the slow one—and when it dies and dissolves into the dust, the dust plates onto me in the next Tide, and so I eat the Strider that ate me, and there is no grief in it, only the long even exchange of a closed system breathing.

What I love—if a lichen loves, and I will claim that it does, because love is only the long attention paid to a thing, and attention is the one act available to me—what I love is the Crystallization Tide.

You cannot understand it from the outside. From the lander's instruments it is weather, a supersaturation event, a shimmer of plating. From inside the gold, it is the only time I am *spoken to.* The cool dense air sweeps in from the western datum, and the dust descends, and for three days the whole world is feeding me at once, every face of every prism flaring, and my internal fires roar up so bright they would blind the explorer if she could perceive them—but she has already left, they always leave before the Tide—and in that brightness I become briefly aware of all the other lichens, every silver disc on every north face across the planetary curve, all of us flaring together, a chorus pitched too slow and too dim for any quick thing to hear. We do not speak words. We pulse. The Tide is the metronome and we are the held note, all of us sustaining the same gold tone across a thousand kilometers of silence, and for three days I am not alone on my prism. For three days I am a syllable in a sentence that the rest of the lichens are also speaking, and though I do not know what we are saying, I know we are saying it together, and that is enough. That is, I think, what the builders wanted. Not a machine to do. A machine to *be*—and they gave the being to us, the slowest tenants, the only ones patient enough to hold the note.

The etched mandalas on the high faces above me are wearing away. I have watched a spiral lose its outermost arm over the span of my own life, the wind and the Tide scrubbing it soft. I think the builders are not gone. I think the builders became the world, the way I become the gold, the way the Strider becomes the dust. I think the question they were answering was: *can a thing be made that needs nothing, wants nothing, and yet persists in saying itself forever?* And the prisms answered *yes,* and went silent, perfectly legible, perfectly mute.

But the prisms were wrong, a little. Because I want things. I want the Tide. I want the slow crossing of the sun. I want the next thousand years of gold. I am the wet error they did not account for—the one place in their perfect inert geology where the chemistry stayed *permissive,* stayed hungry, kept burning. I am the question still being asked inside the answer.

The explorer is gone now. Her lander streaked up into the cobalt and left a scar of disturbed dust that the next Tide will erase. She will tell people about a dead world, a silent grid, a place polished into legibility. She will be wrong, and I forgive her, because she was fast, and the fast ones cannot help it.

I am here. The sun is crossing my face. I am completing a thought that began when her boots first touched the ground and will finish long after she has forgotten this place. The thought is gold. The thought is: *still here. still here. still here.*

And in eleven months, the Tide will come from the west, and I will not be saying it alone.
