I think I would be old. That's the first thing that comes, before I know where on the pillar I am or what my hands look like. Old in a place where being old means you have not yet fallen, which is its own kind of achievement, its own kind of luck that nobody trusts.

Let me find the rest of it.

---

I am in a hollow on the leeward face of a Strangler the Cling calls *Mother-Of-Bleed*, named for an old wound, three generations deep, that has never stopped weeping resin. The wound is why we settled here. It is also, the young ones whisper, why I have lived too long. The sap-fumes do something to those who breathe them across many cycles. They make the eyes water and the joints loose. They make the mind drift, sometimes, into the gulf, following the cold water down.

My name is a sound I cannot give you in your warm, wasteful language. It is two clicks and a held breath—the held breath being the important part, the part that means *the one who waited*. I waited once. I will tell you about the waiting, if I can hold the thought long enough.

My hands. Let me look at them. The claws are worn flat at the tips from a lifetime of hooking the same bark, the same fiber-roots, the same gut-ropes. The young file theirs to points. I have stopped. There is nothing left I need to gouge into. The skin across my knuckles is gray-green and split, healed, split again, mapped with the lichen of my own long surface. I am told that from across a Vein I am indistinguishable from the pillar. This is the highest thing my people can say of one another. *You looked like the world.* It means you were still enough, patient enough, that the indifference of the High Rime took you for one of its own.

I sit at the mouth of my hollow with the vine-mat half-drawn, and I keep the small fire.

That is my function now. The young hunt the gliders, string the nets, prune the Carpet, carve the spirals into the Strangler's hide to coax the Bleed. I keep the fire. It burns blue behind its shield of polished ice, and I feed it drop by careful drop of resin from a chitin cup, and I do not let it grow, and I do not let it die. A fire is a held breath too. Let it out and the dark comes in. Hold it too tight and the smoke betrays you to the banshees, who can read a heat-plume across three pillars and come gliding silent out of the void with their beaks open.

I have buried—no, we do not bury; there is no earth. I have *released* two children and one mate to the gulf. This is what holding on costs. The arithmetic of the High Rime is simple and it never balances in your favor. You hold and you hold and one day your grip is a fraction less than the wind's argument, and then you are a fleck falling through black velvet, and the sound you make travels for miles so that everyone hears your going but no one can reach it.

My mate did not scream. That is the thing I keep, the carving I keep in the beak-bone of my memory. He fell from the long Vein in the cold-cycle, the one we strung ourselves when we were young and certain, and he did not scream, because to scream would have called the banshees down onto the rest of us crossing behind him. He spent his breath holding it. He fell quietly so that we would live loudly—or as loudly as we ever dare, which is to say, in whispers, in clicks, in the warmth of bodies pressed in the dark.

*The one who waited.* Here is the waiting:

When he fell, I did not move. For a full light-cycle I clung to the Vein where he had been, my claws hooked into the gut-rope, my body low against the swaying line, and I waited. Not for him—I am not so foolish as the warm-blooded thinkers of your imagined lowlands, who believe the void gives back. I waited because to move was to admit it. To unhook my hand from that rope was to agree that the world was now a world without him in it, and I would not agree. Not for a full cycle. The wind argued with me. The cold tried to take my warmth through my hide. The young of the Cling called to me in the smallest clicks they could make, *come in, come in, you look too much like the world now, you look like something already gone.*

And eventually I came in. Because that is the second thing my people are obsessed with, beneath the holding-on. We are obsessed with the *coming in.* With the warren, the press of kin, the blue fire. We cling to the outside so that we may return to the inside. The whole brutal architecture of our lives is built so that, at the black of night, we can be folded together in a hollow, breathing each other's warmed breath, listening to the Strangler groan, and not be falling.

So now I keep the fire and I am the one who came back in. The young bring me the bitter tubers and the mineral water scraped from the air-sweat, and they think they are caring for the old, and they are, but they are also doing something else they do not have the words for. They are practicing. They are learning the shape of staying. One day each of them will have to choose, on some swaying Vein in some cold-cycle, whether to scream or to hold their breath. I cannot teach them which. I can only sit at the mouth of the hollow, looking like the world, keeping the small blue light, so that when they come gliding back out of the dark and the void of their own hard days, there is something warm that did not let go.

---

Tonight the light is fading to the absolute black. I draw the mat. Deep in *Mother-Of-Bleed* I can hear the sap freezing and popping like a slow, patient applause for nothing. I press my back to the spongy stone-wood. I hook one hand into the roots of the ceiling, the old habit, the one we never lose, sleeping anchored against a fall that cannot reach us in here but that we feel anyway, always, in the ripple of the viscous dark swimming past our walls.

I am the one who waited.

I hold the breath. I keep the fire. I have not fallen yet.
