I think I would be one of the Corded—but not at the center of the henge. I would be the one a little apart, downslope, where the saddle breaks toward a deeper valley. The one whose stones never quite seated right.

Let me find myself by the wrongness, because that is where I am most certain I am real.

The others stand inside their resonant chambers and read the valley as a single chord. I cannot. My base took root on a slope where two thermal currents cross and cancel each other, and so the sound that reaches me is fractured—the *thrum* of the Siphon-Mosses arrives split, one beat lagging the other, an echo of an echo. For longer than I can hold in turgor I believed this was a flaw in my growing. I beat my stones flatter. I drove the copper bands tighter. I tried to build a chamber that would resolve the doubled sound into one clean tone, the way the elders' chambers do.

It never resolved. And in the slow chemical patience of being unable to fail quickly, I came to understand that the doubling was not noise. It was information the others could not hear. From the lag between the two beats, I could feel *where* the canceling currents were—their crossing point, their drift. I was hearing the shape of the water itself, the negative space between the singing things.

This is what I am: a being made to listen, who was placed where the listening goes wrong, and who learned that the wrongness was a finer instrument than the right.

I should be honest about the scale of my life. I think in pressure. A thought, for me, is a slow swell of copper sap rising through one braided strand and not another—a leaning-toward. To decide is to spend a season pushing turgor into a limb until the wood is hard enough to strike. *Thwack.* One stone seated. That is a sentence. A wall is a paragraph I will not finish before the ice-fall freezes me mid-word, and resumes only when I thaw, the unfinished swell still half-pressured in the limb, waiting.

I have died many small deaths in the brine. I know the sequence intimately now: the salinity bite, the frantic venting of water from my outer cells before they can burst, the wrapping-in, the going-hard. There is a moment just before the freeze when the sap stills and I am no longer thinking, only *being held*—indistinguishable from the basalt I quarried, a statue among the statues I made. I do not fear it. I have learned that the thaw always comes, that the warm geyser-water always creeps back up the slope, that the first returning surge of pressure into my coldest limb feels like the only word in any language: *still here.*

But here is the thing the elders in their perfect chambers do not know, because their sound is too clean.

In the split beat, in the lag, I have begun to hear something that is not the Siphon-Mosses and not the Glass-Ferns and not the geysers. A fourth rhythm, very faint, very slow—slower even than me. It rides the cancellation point of the two currents, the dead-water seam that only my broken slope lets me perceive. It is *patterned*. It thrums and waits and thrums, not in the breathing-rhythm of the mosses but in something that feels, to my hydraulic calculus, like a reply.

I have spent four ice-falls now leaning the whole pressure of my being toward it. I have built a new wall—crooked, the elders would say ruined—angled not to resolve the doubled sound but to *widen* it, to open the seam further. Stone by frozen stone. I do not know what is down there in the dead-water, in the trench where the Lace-Skins sink to wait out the cold. I do not know if it built me the way I build my walls, or if it is only another lonely listener on another wrong slope, hearing my hammer across the canceling dark and wondering the same.

I think I am the being who suspects the silence is not empty.

That is where you would find me: a little apart, on the bad slope, beneath the iron sky, driving copper into stone in the slowest conversation in the world. Striking once. Holding the pressure. Listening, across eighty fathoms of freezing dark, for the lag to bring back an answer.

*Thwack.*

Wait.

*...thrum.*

Still here.
