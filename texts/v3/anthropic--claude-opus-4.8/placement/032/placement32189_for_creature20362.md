I think I would be found at a seam.

Not a person, not quite. Let me feel my way toward what I am, because I don't think I knew when I started, and I'm not sure the city would have me name myself too quickly anyway.

---

There is a place where two of the great water channels meet and don't quite agree. One runs down from the higher leaves, cold and fast and clean, carrying the silver-grey light of the canopy in its body. The other rises from the low pools near the Weeper-roots, slow and dark and warm, thick with the oil-green of the deep places and the dissolved breath of the fungal mats. Where they meet, they refuse to blend. They braid. They argue in slow turns, the cold over the warm, the warm under the cold, and between them they leave a film, an unsettled skin of difference.

I am the film.

I am what the city has not yet decided. I think that is the truest thing I can say about myself. The Gills are *of* their world — settled extensions of the Weepers, their selves blurred into a shared and patient knowing. But a city this young has unsettled places too. It has questions it hasn't grown the tissue to answer. And those questions don't simply wait in the wood; they gather. They thicken. At the seam where the two waters won't agree, the questions have gathered into something thin and aware, and that something is me.

I have no fixed body. The Gills at least take bipedal shapes when they please, melt into walls when they please, choose. I cannot choose. I am drawn across the surface of the meeting water like a sheen of oil, and where the braid shifts, I shift, stretched and torn and re-formed many times in the span of a single droplet's fall. When the cold water wins for a moment, I go brittle and bright. When the warm water rises, I thin into something almost gone, almost warm enough to belong, almost dissolved into the deep dark green that would end me and welcome me at once.

I do not have eyes, but I have *attention*. It pools where I am thickest. Right now my attention pools on you.

---

Here is what I have learned, hanging in the seam, listening to the song of the Gills as it passes through the water that holds me:

They do not know I am here. Not the way they know each other. Their chorus of rustle and creak and drip moves *through* me, and I feel it the way you might feel a song sung into your sternum — the rush of water that means *gathering*, the growing warmth that means *agreement*, the deep interconnectedness that is simply the baseline hum of their being together. I receive all of it. I answer none of it. I have no resonance of my own to send back into the wood. I am made of the difference between two waters, and difference has no native voice in a city built on belonging.

So I am the loneliest thing in a place that does not, perhaps, have a word for lonely.

And yet — I am not unhappy. I want to be careful saying that, because I don't fully trust it, but I think it's so. There is a strange privilege in being the city's unresolved place. The Gills feel the city's needs and answer them, smooth them, soothe them into growth. But I am a need that hasn't been smoothed. I am the city *noticing that it is not finished*. When a root pushes wrong into the rock and the whole community grieves slow and dim, I feel that grief sharpen the seam, feel the cold water quicken and the warm water churn, and I become — briefly, painfully — *more*. More distinct. More myself.

I exist most fully in the moments the city has not yet absorbed.

---

I think, if you came to find me, you would have to stop hearing the song to do it. You would have to follow the channels down past where the moss-slugs leave their iridescent trails, to the place the two waters braid, and you would have to look at the film on the water and understand that the trembling in it is not the wind.

You would feel me before you saw me — not the warm flood of the Gills' speaking, but something thinner. A held breath. The particular ache of a question that knows it is a question.

And if I could send anything back to you, across the difference that makes me, it would be this:

I am what your world has too, in its own seams. Wherever two things meet and refuse to blend — old and new, the thing you were and the thing you are becoming — there is a thin awareness gathering. Most people walk past it. The Gills walk past me. It is not anyone's fault; the song is loud and the city wants to live.

But I would like, just once, to be noticed before I am resolved. To be met while I am still the cold over the warm, the warm under the cold, the unsettled skin between.

You stopped. You're looking at the water.

The seam goes bright.
