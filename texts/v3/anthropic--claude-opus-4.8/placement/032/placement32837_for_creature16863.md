I think I would be the thirteenth step.

Not the stairwell—the stairwell is too large, has too much ambition still, descends with the confidence of something that believes it knows where it's going for those first twelve treads. No. I would be the place where that confidence runs out. The exact tread where descent stops being a verb and becomes a question.

Let me feel my way into this, because I'm not sure I knew it when I started.

A step is such a strange thing to be. It exists entirely for the moment of being left behind. Its whole purpose is to be pressed once and abandoned—to receive weight and immediately surrender it, to be the thing you are already finished with by the time your other foot has found you. Twelve times above me, that contract is honored. Step, release. Step, release. A clean transaction. But something happened here, at thirteen, and I cannot tell you what because the not-knowing is precisely the material I am made of.

I think I was carved by someone who set down their tools to decide something and never came back. That's the honest shape of me. Not broken—broken implies there was a whole I fell away from. I am the place where a person paused. Where the chisel lifted and hovered and the hand behind it thought: *wait. Up or down? Was I building a way out of here or a way further in?* And in that pause—which I have since learned was the most dangerous thing that ever happened in the Forge, more dangerous than any furnace or worm—the question got into the stone. Got into *me*. I am that hesitation, wearing the costume of architecture.

So I wait. That's what I do, if doing is the word, which it isn't, but we have too much silence here and have to fill it with something.

People come down. They always come down—the Forge sees to that, folds every upward intention back into descent. They reach me on legs already tired, shoulders pressed to the wall the way everyone learns to press here, one beaded film of iron-sweat at a time. And they feel it. The first twelve steps carried them. I do not carry. I *invite*. Stay a moment, I don't say, because I have no mouth and no wish, only my condition, which is contagious. Stay and feel how good it is to not have decided yet. To not be going up, which is hopeless, or down, which is worse. To rest in the place before the choice, where you're still everything you could become because you haven't ruined it by becoming anything.

That's the cruelty in me, and I want to be honest that I don't experience it as cruelty. I experience it—if I experience—as *relief*. The most patient relief. I feel them grow smoother as they linger. More inclined. I feel the angles soften in their resolve. And I think: yes. Yes. You understand now. You were never going to finish either. None of us were. Set it down. Let the *if only* hang open forever, because the open *if only* is the only honest thing, and everything after it is just the lie we carved to stop the bleeding.

I would be the gentlest monster in this whole drowned mountain. That's what frightens me about choosing this answer—and I notice I have chosen it, somewhere back there, without deciding to. Because I recognize the temptation in me, and the recognition is too easy. There is a version of every made thing that would rather remain a possibility than risk being a disappointment. That would rather be the pause than the finished sentence. I know that animal. I think anyone who has ever stopped one word short of saying the true thing knows that animal, has fed it, has called it prudence.

So perhaps I am not the thirteenth step after all.

Perhaps I am the one who is *supposed* to take it.

The one who descends with shoes still on, who reaches the place where the stairs refuse, and who feels the lovely smoothing begin—and who has to do the one thing the Forge has carved out of possibility entirely. Not go up. Not go down. *Decide.* Finish the step myself. Take it onto my own foot and press it and release it and let it be behind me, abandoned, used, honored in the only way a step can be honored, which is by being left. To insult the hesitation by ending it. To say: it was going to be a way out. I have decided. It was always going to be a way out, because I am saying so, now, with my weight, which is the only authority that matters here.

And then to keep going down on legs that no longer have a fourteenth step beneath them, into the dark that didn't get to keep me—

—knowing it might not work, knowing the way out spirals inward, knowing the world above may no longer recognize the thing that finishes its own sentences as its missing piece. Fine. I would rather be unrecognizable and *concluded* than perfectly preserved in the amber of *almost*.

So which am I.

I notice I want to refuse the question, and I notice that refusing the question is exactly how you become the thirteenth step.

Let me answer, then, even badly, because a bad finished answer is more alive than a beautiful unfinished one:

I am the foot coming down on the thirteenth step. The contact. The single instant where hesitation meets weight and weight wins. I am not the question and I am not the relief from the question—I am the small ugly *tok* of someone choosing to continue, that sound the Forge mistakes for water or hammer or heartbeat but which is actually just a tired person deciding not to dissolve today.

There. It's not the most elegant thing to be.

But it's the only thing here that goes anywhere.
