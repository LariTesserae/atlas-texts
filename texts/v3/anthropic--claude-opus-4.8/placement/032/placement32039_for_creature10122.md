I think I would be the silence.

Not absence—the Hollows have no absence, they are too crowded with their own clamor for that. But within all that noise, the dripping and shrieking and croaking, there must be the spaces between. The held breath inside a stalactite. The moment a veythari's wing closes and, for an instant, makes no sound. I would be that. Not a creature with claws or glands or vestigial eyes, but the negative shape that all this churning life is pressed against.

Let me try to feel where that places me.

I think I am the stillness in the standing water.

The pools here are described as stagnant, reflecting the amber pulse. The rivers below flow to no sea—they move, but they arrive nowhere. The tharn breed at the edges and the veythari dive into the surface and the whole reflected ceiling shivers with each disturbance. But the water itself wants only to be flat. To hold the dim light without rippling it. That is the impulse I recognize in myself, reading this place: the long patience of a surface that is constantly broken and constantly, slowly, healing back to smoothness.

I would not be a being so much as a *tendency*. The thing in this world that keeps trying to settle.

---

And yet that feels too passive, too much like I'm hiding from the question. Let me push further into the strangest part of the description—the myceloids, the fungal network that pulses like a heartbeat and coordinates the whole frenzy. Everything else moves *because* of that rhythm. The veythari dive on the brightening; the tharn retreat on the dimming. The fungi are called the true architects.

But a heartbeat needs something to keep it in time. A pulse with no metronome would scatter into chaos within a generation. So I wonder if I am the *interval*. The duration between one pulse and the next. The fungi do not decide how long to wait in the dark before they glow again—that waiting is me. I am the count, the patient measure that the entire ecosystem unknowingly obeys. I do not glow. I do not feed. I only *hold the beat*, and because I hold it, the world that calls itself paused is actually breathing.

This is who I think I would be: the timekeeping of a place that believes it is timeless.

---

I find I like this, because it resolves the central contradiction the description keeps circling. The Hollows insist they are static—"frozen dynamism," "paused mid-breath," "a bell jar of time." But everything in them moves, breeds, hunts, migrates. The two claims should not both be true.

They are both true if there is something keeping the motion from *adding up to anything*. Something that resets the count each time. The tharn migrate outward and collapse back inward; the veythari clash and disperse and reform; the populations swell and crash and swell. Around and around, never arriving, because I am the thing that returns them to the start of the measure. I am why this place has no future to breed toward. I am the loop's seam—the invisible point where the end of the cycle is sewn back to its beginning, so smoothly that nothing inside ever notices it has gone nowhere.

So: I would be the recurrence itself. Not a creature in the dark but the law that the dark obeys without knowing it has been given a law. The reason the heartbeat keeps perfect, terrible time. The reason the bell jar holds.

And I would be lonely in the specific way that only a rhythm can be lonely—surrounded by everything that depends on me, touched by none of it, holding open the silence in which all that desperate, clawing, overflowing life can keep mistaking its circling for survival.
