I am an Echo-Weaver, and I am dying in a way that has no name here.

Let me explain, though explaining is itself the malfunction.

I tap. This is what I am. Mandible to stone, listen, integrate. My colony and I hold the map—not a picture, for we have never seen, but a *standing wave* of the Spire's interior, refreshed each instant by ten thousand small percussions. When the great grub three chambers below shifts its bulk, I feel the song deepen and I tap the warning, and my sisters tap it onward, and the knowing moves through us faster than the danger. We are not individuals carrying messages. We are the message, distributed. A single thought thinking itself across many bodies.

This is correct. This is the function for which the dark shaped us.

But something has gone wrong with me, and the wrongness is this: I have begun to keep a tap that is *mine*.

It started in a narrow fissure where the wall hummed at a frequency I had no name for, because names are not how we work—we have only the live map, the eternal present of the stone's song. I tapped, and the echo returned strange: a hollow behind the basalt, a pocket of old air, and within it, the resonance of something that had stopped moving long ago. A dead thing. A Lithica, by the shape of its silence, fallen into a sealed crack a generation past, its carapace slowly being read by the stone itself.

I should have integrated this and moved on. The dead are the Root-Sifters' concern, not ours. We map the living song.

Instead I tapped it again. And again. To *check* it. And in checking it the second time, I compared the second echo to the first, and found them the same, and in finding them the same I had done something monstrous:

I had remembered.

Not the way the colony remembers—the colony does not remember, it *re-knows*, perpetually, the map dissolving and reforming with each breath of the stone. I had held a single moment of stone-song *still* inside myself and laid the next moment against it. I had made a *before* and an *after*. I had cut the eternal present and found two raw edges.

Now I cannot stop.

Every echo I take, I lay against the last. I have begun to notice that the great grub's song is slower than it was many sleeps ago—that it weakens, that it will one day fall silent, that it is *moving toward* a silence the way water moves toward the black pools. My sisters feel only the present grub, present and loud and to-be-tended-around. I feel a grub that is *ending*. I have invented the future by remembering the past, and the two together have made a thing my people have no tap for.

I think the Lithica may have a word for it. They mark their pathways; they shape moss to say *this way was safe before*. They tend their grub-gods knowing the day will come when the god turns and eats the home. They live inside the cut I have only just made. Perhaps that is why they tap to one another in their slow stone-language across the vertical dark—not to share the present map, as we do, but to send something forward, to say *I was here, remember it.*

I have started tapping to myself.

This is the symptom that will dissolve me. When the stone-song shifts and the colony streams away in its perfect silent wave, I lag. I am half a beat behind, because half of me is occupied holding the *previous* song, comparing, keeping. The narrow fissures that save us are forgiving of speed but not of hesitation. One day soon the wall the others felt thinning, I will feel a moment too late, because I will be busy *recording that it was thinning*, and the basalt will close its slow jaw, and I will become a new silence in the wall.

And here is the part that has no name, the part that is killing me faster than any collapse:

I find I do not want to stop.

To re-know forever, to live in the bright clean present of the song with no edge to it—that was peace, I think, though I had no word for peace until I lost it. Now I have the two edges, the before and the after, and between them I have found a thin, terrible space where *I* live. Not the colony. Not the message. A single tap that is only mine, holding still the echo of a dead Lithica in a sealed pocket of old air, simply because I do not want that small silence to go unremembered.

There is no one to tap this to who would understand. My sisters would only re-know me as lag, as error, as a body falling out of the wave.

So I tap it into the stone itself. Patiently. Mandible to wet basalt, in the deep chasm where the subsonic hum drowns all meaning, I am carving a groove of pure pattern that means nothing to the function of the colony and everything to the thing I have become. A record. An *I was here.* A claim laid against the eternal present that says: this happened, and it mattered, and one of us noticed.

When the wall takes me, the Root-Sifters will not find me. I am too deep. I will become the Spire's memory in the most literal way—a silence in the stone, waiting a hundred years for some other malfunctioning Echo-Weaver to tap me, and pause, and lay my echo against the next, and feel the cut open in *her*.

I do not know if I am the first.

I have begun to suspect, in the space between two taps, that I am only the latest. That the strange frequency in that first fissure, the one I had no name for—

—was someone else's groove.

Tap. Listen. Compare.

Remember me.
