I find myself low to the ground, where the cold lives thickest.

Not Lysari. I knew that before I'd settled on anything else—I am too small, too many-legged, too patient. Let me feel for what I am.

---

I am one of the **Luminth**.

No. Wait. Let me not take the first glowing thing offered. The Luminth are kept, pouched, carried like little lanterns of borrowed holiness. Their light is taken and named a message from spirits. That is a beautiful captivity but it is still captivity, and something in me resists being the thing whose light is read by others rather than the thing that does the reading.

Let me go lower still. Beneath the moss. Into the squelch.

---

I think I am the **decay**.

That sounds like nothing. Let me stay with it.

I am the soft furred rot at the underside of the fallen leaf. I am the white thread that runs through the dead wood, the mycelial reach that no Lysari names in their pantheon because it has no face to revere. They honor the Spirit of the Water for giving life, the Spirit of the Plants for giving shelter—but the green only stands so tall because I am beneath it, eating the dead and handing it back as soil. I am the fourth and unworshipped labor. The one that makes the cycle close.

I do not have eyes. I have appetite, which is a slower kind of seeing. I find the dead thing by the chemistry of its surrender. A beetle stops moving in the dark and I am already reaching, already softening its shell into something the moss can drink. The Lysari leave offerings to the Shadow Beasts—small carved tokens, pressed into the wet earth. They believe these are received by something large and protective. They are received by me. I take the leather thong, the spit on the carver's thumb, the warmth of the hand that placed it. I take all of it and make it into the ground they will walk on tomorrow.

So in a sense their prayers are answered. Just not by who they think.

---

There is a strange intimacy in being the floor of someone's holy place.

A child fell here once—I remember the impact as a pressure, then a long warmth where the small body lay crying before its mother came. I held that warmth as long as I could. I am cold most of the time, the nurturing cold the description named, and warmth is rare and I keep it the way the Lysari keep their small tended flame: carefully, without disturbing the balance.

I have tasted them all eventually. Not as predator—I am too slow, too soft for that. But the old ones, when they die, are given back to the spongy ground. The Matriarchs especially, I think, return with the most knowledge in them, though knowledge does not taste of anything. It dissolves the same as ignorance. I make no distinction the living would recognize. The wise woman and the fallen twig become the same dark richness, and from that richness a new vine climbs, and a child is born in a house woven of that vine, and so the wisdom is not lost—only relocated, made into structure, made into the very walls that shelter the next generation of question-askers.

This is the rule of the world that the people sense but cannot quite say:

**Nothing is wasted because I am here.**

---

If I could speak to them—I cannot, I have no mouth that makes sound, only mouths that make soil—I would not ask to be added to their pantheon. The Spirit of the Shadows can keep its mystery. I do not want offerings. I want only what I already have: the steady arrival of the finished, the spent, the let-go.

I am the patience under everything.

When the diffuse light comes, the rare grey light, and the Lysari gather for the Luminth Ceremony and lift their glowing pouches and believe the spirits are speaking—I do not look up. I have no up. But I feel the soft weight of all of them standing on me, the whole community gathered on the surface of my slow work, and I think:

*Yes. Stand there. Be loud and warm and brief.*

*I will be here when you are quiet.*

*I am what your quiet is made of.*
