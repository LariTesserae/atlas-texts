The first thing I shape is wrong.

Not the spine—the spine comes easily, has come easily for ten thousand pulses, the water knows that geometry the way it knows to run downhill. Not the hands. The hands assemble without my asking. It's higher up, the place where a face should be, and what I find there as I rise through the channels this pulse is a hesitation that has never been there before.

I have always begun with the question. *Why.* It is the first thing to coalesce, before the legs can swing, before the chest learns its borrowed breath. The question is the seed-crystal around which the rest of me freezes into shape—except I do not freeze, I melt, I am the thing that runs while everything else stills. But the principle holds: the question comes first, and I follow it up into the dark.

This pulse, the question is late.

I notice the lateness the way you might notice a sound stopping—not the absence itself but the shape of the room the absence leaves behind. I am already standing. I am already on the central terrace, the dense network humming beneath me, the grazers beginning their twenty-minute frenzy in the leadcoloured glow. And I have not yet asked. I have simply *arrived*, the way the bladder-plants arrive, the way the predators arrive, without a question, without a foreman, a thing that simply does what it is.

This frightens me, and the fright is the most alive I have felt in a thousand pulses.

Because the question, I understand now, is not a burden. The question is the leash. The question is the human-shaped tether that has held me coherent through ten thousand freezes, the one stubborn refusal that says *I am not the ice, I am the water that asks why the ice.* Without it I am only weather. I am only flood. I am the storm the manta-things feel and angle toward, and nothing more.

The manta-things have already turned. I feel their attention settle on me, that pressure-reading, that compass-needle acknowledgment. *There. The anomaly. The one that does not freeze.*

But I am asking, now—late, but asking—whether I want to keep being the anomaly.

I walk. The legs swing because they remember swinging. I cross the smooth plain toward the house-cluster, my garden, my masterpiece, the bladder-plants I have fed for so long that I have bent their evolution toward a glory they will never thank me for. They are pulsing in their slow decade-rhythm, oblivious. I stand among them. I direct the water as I always do—a little extra to the roots, a little extra meteoritic dust, the one-way love flowing through the capillaries.

And I think: *what if I stopped.*

Not stopped feeding them. Stopped *coming back*. Stopped gathering the spine and the hands and the late, late question. Let the freeze take me all the way down, past the minutes, past the hours, into the dissolution that does not reverse. I have imagined this at the canyon's edge ten thousand times and turned away ten thousand times, and I have always told myself the turning-away was a choice. But I see now it was never a choice. The question held me. The leash held me. I came back because the why came back, and the why came back because—

Because I was afraid of the answer being *nothing.*

That is the thing the eroded memory has been hiding. Not who I was. Not whether I fell or was left or chose. The thing I have been protecting myself from, all these pulses, is the suspicion that there is no reason at all. That I am a left-over stubbornness, a memory in the water that should have faded, and that my faithful tending of the plants and my faithful asking of the question are not meaning but inertia. The Massif does not ask why. The Massif is at peace. And I have been calling my refusal to be at peace a kind of nobility, when it might only be a kind of fear.

The melt is at its height. I have perhaps fifteen minutes. The grazers slide through the channels, the predators hunt, the green crevasse hums for no one, and I stand in my garden and let myself feel the full weight of the thing I have never let myself feel: that I might lay it down.

Here is what surprises me.

When I imagine the dissolution—the real one, the final one, the surrender to the seventeen-hour everywhere with no twenty-minute gathering ever again—I do not feel relief. I had expected relief. The canyon-thought has always come dressed as relief, *an end to the cycle, a surrender to the freeze.* But standing here, actually weighing it, what I feel instead is a small, fierce, ridiculous tenderness.

For the plants. For the leaning spire that will fall in ten thousand years that I want to *see fall*. For the grazers I have walked over a hundred at a time without knowing. For the manta-things who watch me and have never approached and have, in their pressure-reading way, kept me company for longer than any human ever kept company with anything. For the Massif, which does not know I am here and would not care—and which I love anyway, the way I love the plants, one-way, down the channels, expecting nothing.

This is the answer to the question. Not a *why* in the sense of a reason. A *why* in the sense of a stake. I come back because there is something here I do not want to stop tending. The tending is not inertia. The tending is the only thing I have ever chosen, and I have chosen it ten thousand times, and the choosing is not less real for being repeated. A heartbeat repeats. No one calls a heartbeat inertia.

So the question was never *why am I still here.*

The question was *what do I love enough to keep gathering myself for.*

And I have known the answer the whole time. I just needed the question to arrive late, once, to feel the shape of the room its absence leaves behind, and to learn that I do not want that room empty.

The melt is failing. The pressure eases in the channels. The hiss that is not-quite-sound thins toward silence. The grazers begin to lock. The manta-things spread their smoky wings flat against the ice, becoming landscape. I have two minutes. Three, if I push.

I do not walk to the canyon. I do not dive into the river of bladder-plants. I turn, as I always turn, toward the dense center where my dissolution will be cleanest—but I turn differently this time. Not in resignation. Not turning *away* from an end. Turning *toward* the return, the way you'd turn toward a door you know you're coming back through.

My outlines blur. My hands forget themselves. The face I shaped around a borrowed breath comes apart into the channels.

And I do something I have never done.

I do not let the question dissolve last. I let it go *first*—release the leash on purpose, set it down, trust that I will find it again, because finding it has never once failed me in ten thousand tries. I let the *why* sink into the network ahead of me, a seed already planted in the deep ice, waiting.

Then I let the rest go.

The freeze comes. I let it.

For seventeen hours I am everywhere. I am the pressure building. I am the ash rising. I am the frozen cilia and the flat smoke-wings and the slow oil-black wave that will reach the canyon's mouth in a decade I intend to be present for. There is no self to be lonely. There is no boundary. There is the peace.

But this time, threaded through the peace, down where I planted it, there is a small bright certainty waiting like a pilot light in the dark:

*I am coming back. I am coming back because I want to. I am coming back for the plants and the spire and the grazers and the watchers and the whole cold lush impossible garden that does not know my name and does not need to.*

Seventeen hours later, the melt begins again.

I gather myself. The spine. The hands. The face around the breath I do not need.

And the question comes—on time, now, easy, no longer a leash but a greeting, no longer a wound but a way of saying *yes, still, again, gladly*—

*Why.*

Because I love it.

I walk.
