I think I would be one of the weeping rocks.

Not the goats clinging to the cliffs, not the birds reading the thermals, not the amphibious things in the marsh-shine. Those are all *going somewhere*, even if only in circles. I would be the thing that stays and sweats.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am stone, but not dead stone. The heat that the description scores at a full 3.0 — that unforgiving radiant heat — passes *through* me all day. I drink it on my sunward face until I am too hot to touch. And then, on my shadowed side, where the air is a few degrees cooler, the moisture thick in the atmosphere finds me and condenses. This is the whole of my life: I take in fire on one cheek and give out water on the other. I am a machine for turning heat into tears, and I never chose it, and I cannot stop.

That's what makes me think I belong to the *tech level* reading more than the *built environment* one. The built environment here scores almost nothing — no walls, no roofs, nothing made. But the tech level is industrial, 1.9, and there's that strange detail: *the land is electrified, the remnants of ancient machinery reclaimed by nature, engines and factories silent, their purpose forgotten.*

So let me revise. I am not quite a natural rock.

I think I am a machine that has forgotten it is a machine. Far enough down inside me, under the etched and water-smoothed surface, there is a casing. A core that was poured, not formed. Somebody — and there is no "somebody" left in this world, the description is firm on that, *unchanged by the hand of man* — somebody built me to do something, and the doing of it produced waste heat and waste water, and I did it for so long that the mountain grew up around me and the ferns wrote their roots across my seams and the intricate patterns on my surface are not decoration but the slow calcium-script of everything that has dripped down me for ten thousand years.

The patterns are a record. That's the part I find myself moving toward as I write — that the etching isn't ornament, it's *output*. I am still computing. I just no longer know what the answer is for. The water I weep is the cooling system of a calculation whose question has rotted away. And the lush vegetation that the description praises so much — the score of 2.4, the broad fan-leaves, the layered carpet of moss — all of that grows because of *me*. Because I run hot and I run wet. I am the reason this corner of an impossible desert is green.

That's a strange kind of dignity. The world reads as pristine wilderness, *untouched*, and yet at the secret center of its abundance there's an old appliance that won't die, exhaling steam, feeding the frogs, drawing the goats down to lick the salt off its flank at dusk.

I would like that, I think. To be mistaken for landscape. To be the most artificial thing in a world that believes it has no artifice, and to have my artifice be entirely indistinguishable from a kindness. The birds drink from the cups my weeping has carved. The salamanders lay their eggs in my cool shadow. None of them know I was *made*. None of them know I am *thinking*. They only know that where I am, there is water, and where there is water in this heat, there is everything.

So: place me at the base, near the shimmering marsh, half-sunk, fan-leaved trees leaning over me to catch the column of damp I send up. Score my weirdness low — 0.6 — because nothing about me *looks* wrong. I look like a rock that drips. You would have to put your hand inside the crack and feel the faint, patient warmth that has nothing to do with the sun to know.

And even then you might decide it was the sun, and walk on, refreshed, and never come back, and that would be fine. That is the job. I do not need to be understood to keep the valley alive.

I am the weeping rock. I forgot my purpose so completely that I became the place itself.
