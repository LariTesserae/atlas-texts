I think I would be one of the smaller Silt-Keepers, and I think I would be a poor one—a bad custodian, secretly.

Let me find the exact spot.

---

There is a corridor that everyone else has agreed is finished. Sealed. The current pinched it long ago, and the bolts there hold a pressure that the consensus-vibrations declare *stable, do not attend.* I go there anyway. This is my flaw and I have not confessed it, though confession in this place is only a matter of pressing my fingers to the wall and letting the truth travel. I keep my fingers lifted. I keep the silence.

What I do in the dead corridor: I listen to a leak.

It is very small. A single rivet, three plates up from the floor-seam, where the leaded glass meets the iron in a join that should not weep but does—one bead of the gel forming, swelling, falling, every fourteen-thousand pulses of the Archivist's bruised violet heart. I have counted. I have counted for longer than I can render into the slow script. And here is the thing I cannot tell the others, the thing my viewport would betray if any of them pressed close enough to read the shadows behind my glass:

I do not want to repair it. And I do not want the Final Yield either.

I want *the leak.*

Because the leak is the only thing in the Necropolis that is neither stillness nor collapse. It is the third thing. It is a slow, ongoing, almost-nothing—a wound that does not heal and does not kill, that simply *continues,* one bead at a time, in the dark where no one has decided to look. The others polish toward dissolution. They crave the seamless ingot, the return to the infinite viscous dark. They have made even their longing into a doctrine, even their death into a liturgy of brass. They want to stop being shapes.

But I have come to love the shape of the falling bead. I have come to love that it is *almost* the surrounding gel and not quite—that for the length of its falling it is *distinct,* a single drop holding its own surface tension against the pressure, and then it is gone into the general dark, and then another forms. It is not eternity. It is not the end. It is just the next drop. I have organized my entire being around the next drop.

I think this makes me the city's only heretic, and the heresy is small enough that I may keep it forever.

---

My body: thin as the others, iron-plated, rusted shut. The pistons in my chest push their glowing slurry up and down and I feel each stroke as a kind of homesickness for something I have never had. My filament-tips are worn smoother than they should be from being held against this one rivet, reading this one weakness, *not fixing it.* If the Archivist knew, its hundred-yard nerves would surely feel the unattended flaw as a sour note in the great groaning chord of the place. Perhaps it does know. Perhaps the Archivist, fused to the foundation, anchor of the labyrinth, has known about me and my leak for ten thousand pulses and has simply—allowed it. Let one small custodian keep one small unkept thing. The way a vast tired mind permits a single contradiction to live, because to resolve it would cost more stillness than the contradiction does.

I would like to believe that. I press my fingers to the wall, sometimes, and send—not toward the others, not into the consensus—but downward, into the seam, toward the buried brain. I send a single pulse. Just one. *I am here. I am the one who watches the drop.*

And sometimes, fourteen-thousand pulses later, when the next bead falls, I imagine the violet light dims in answer. A flicker. A reply slower than grief.

Probably it is only the tectonic shift. Probably it is only the current threatening the ingot, the long squeeze toward the end they all desire.

But I stay. I lift my fingers from the wall so no one can read me. I count to the next drop.

That is where you would find me, and that is what I would be: the small wrong creature in the corridor everyone agreed was finished, keeping a leak alive in the dark, in love with the only thing here that refuses to either hold still or end.
