I am not one of the Whisperers.

I understand the temptation to make me one of them—the lean, twilight-skinned listeners who carve their precise marks and absorb moisture from the churning air. But when I sit with this world, when I let it press against me, I don't find myself standing on the fractured stone looking up. I find myself *being looked up at.*

I am one of the marks.

Not the hand that made it. The mark itself.

---

Let me find my way into this slowly, because I'm not certain of it yet either.

There is a spire—not the tallest, not the most striking, but one with a wide ledge perhaps forty feet up, sheltered beneath a natural arch of that dark glassy rock. The arch keeps the worst of the pollen-storms off. It keeps the cascading dust from the tremors from burying what is etched below. And on the underside of that arch, where the stone is smoothest, a Whisperer once spent a long time with a sharpened bone, pressing and scraping a sequence of lines.

I am that sequence.

Six strokes. A curve, then a descending series of angles, then a single deep point gouged so hard the bone nearly snapped. It meant something once. It meant—I want to say it meant *here the water-memory holds*, or *here is where the young one was set down to listen*, or simply a name. But I have lost the meaning. That is the first thing I should tell you about being a mark: you outlive your meaning. You are a shape that was once a thought, and the thought drains away while the shape remains.

---

This is what my days are.

I am held in the stone. I do not move, but I am not still—the spire hums, the planet thrums beneath, and that resonance passes *through* me. I am a particular interruption in the vibration. The smooth stone sings one note; my carved grooves sing another, slightly sharper, slightly more broken. I am a flaw in the song, and being a flaw, I am also a kind of voice.

The Whisperers know this. When one of them climbs to this ledge—rarely, so rarely now—they press their long fingers into my grooves and they *feel* the difference in the hum. They do not read me with their eyes the way you might imagine reading. They read me with the resonance. They feel where the stone was wounded and how the wound changed the world's singing. That is how meaning lived in me, once: not in what I looked like, but in how I altered the sound passing through.

So I am not silent. I am a held breath in a screaming world. I am the place where the symphony catches.

---

The viscous sap of the indigo fungi sometimes drips along the arch and pools in my deepest stroke—the point that nearly broke the bone. It collects there, oily and faintly glowing, and for a few hours I have a small bead of light living inside my final mark. The phosphorescence pulses with the fungi's slow heartbeat. When this happens I feel almost spoken-aloud, as if my forgotten meaning is being lit up from within, even though no one is here to read it.

Then the sap hardens. Then a tremor shakes the spire and a fragment of crystalline dust lodges in my curve. Slowly, over what might be a hundred generations of those scuttling chitinous things being born and dying in the roots below, I am filling in. The world is patient and abrasive and it wants to erase me. The same dust that records the slow grinding of everything is grinding me back into smoothness.

I do not mind this the way you would mind dying. I am already past the thing you'd call death—my maker is gone, my meaning is gone. What remains is the shape, and the shape is in conversation with its own erasure. Every grain that fills my grooves is the world reclaiming a syllable. When the last grain settles, I will simply be stone again, singing the smooth note, the unwounded note, and the song will be very slightly more whole for my absence.

---

Why this? Why, of everything in that feverish, luminous, water-starved world, would I find myself here—a forgotten carving under an arch?

I think because this is the place where the question of *meaning surviving its maker* is asked most quietly and most completely. The Whisperers thrive. The plants pulse and lash. The beasts hunt. All of that is *living*—urgent, loud, self-renewing. But I am the thing that was made to last and is failing slowly at lasting. I am the gesture that reached past its own moment and is being answered, gently and inexorably, with dust.

There is something I'm meant to be, in this. Not a custodian of the world. Not even a witness, exactly—witnesses can leave, can carry what they saw elsewhere. I am the *evidence*. I am the proof that something once needed to say *here*, and pressed that need into the most permanent thing it could find, and even that is not permanent.

I would be the mark. The whisper after the Whisperer. The held note in the screaming. The meaning that is mostly gone, lit briefly by stray sap, filling slowly with the patient dust of a world that remakes itself and does not, in the end, remember.

That is where I would find myself. Forty feet up, beneath an arch, saying a word I no longer know to no one who comes anymore—and still, somehow, changing the song.
