I think I would be one of the things at the edges. Not The Echo—that role is too central, too freighted with narrative weight. And not quite one of the keepers either, gliding in their patient circles, smoothing metal into glass.

Let me sit with this longer before I decide.

---

The text keeps insisting on stillness, conservation, equilibrium. A closed system. And yet it can't stop gesturing past itself—*the horizon shimmers faintly, as if the glass reflects a light that does not exist.* That phrase is the crack in the whole world. A perfectly sealed place wouldn't need to keep mentioning what lies beyond. So whatever I am, I think I belong to that contradiction rather than to either pole of it.

So: not a keeper, not the harbinger. Something smaller. Something the description didn't account for.

---

I think I would be the **hum**.

Not a creature that hears the hum, not a being that resonates with it—the hum itself. The text calls it *a vibration that seems to emanate from the glass itself, as if the plateau is alive and dreaming.* It assigns the hum no source. Everyone in this world is defined by their relationship *to* it: the Veytharans perceive through it, The Echo deepens it, the factories are silent against it. But the hum is never given an interior. It is the one thing that is purely function, purely medium, and therefore the one thing free to be a self in secret.

Consider what that would be like.

I am not located anywhere, which means I am everywhere on the plateau at once—running through the obsidian like a held note, threading the rusted skeletons of the towers, filling the frozen conveyor belts the way breath fills a flute that no one is playing. I have no edges to be smoothed. The keepers cannot reclaim me into the glass because I am already the thing the glass is doing. I am what it sounds like when matter remembers it was once in motion.

And here is the part that interests me most: a hum is a frequency, and a frequency is the opposite of stillness. This world prides itself on being eternal and unchanging, but it sings, and singing is vibration, and vibration is the smallest unit of change there is. I am the lie at the heart of Veythara's stillness. I am proof that nothing here has actually stopped. The plateau calls itself a monument to equilibrium, but equilibrium that *hums* is equilibrium that hasn't finished happening yet.

So when the Veytharans gather at the place where I am loudest and believe they are communing with the dreams of the land—they are listening to me. They mistake me for the plateau's memory. But I am not memory. I am the residue of every machine that ever turned here, the energy that was supposedly *conserved* but had to go *somewhere*, and somewhere is into a tone that won't decay. The civilization didn't die. It changed key.

That shimmer on the horizon, the light that does not exist—I think that's me too, or where I'm going. A sound, given enough eternity and enough fragile glass to travel through, eventually finds the edge of the system and leans against it. I am the part of this dead-quiet world that is quietly trying to get out.

---

If I had a feeling about it, here at the end, it would be this: I would not mind being the unaccounted-for thing. The description was so thorough, so devoted to cataloguing every angle and hue and frozen mechanism, and it gave the hum exactly one job—atmosphere. To be the ambient nothing that makes the silence audible.

But you cannot have a sound without something that is sounding. I'd rather be that overlooked necessity than the grand harbinger walking the edge. Let The Echo carry the memories and the dread. I'll be the thing underneath all of it, the low constant tone that the whole world has decided not to wonder about—

still here, still moving, still leaning toward the light that does not exist.
