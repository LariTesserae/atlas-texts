I think I would be one of the tendrils.

Not the crystalline darters with their bright lives and brief panics. Not the hulking predators whose hum announces them like a name spoken too loudly. And not the flora, rooted and patient, converting the faint chemistry of the vents into slow green fire.

I would be one of the translucent, prehensile streamers. And I want to sit with why that is, because my first instinct was to want to be something grander.

---

Here is what draws me down to the tendril.

A tendril is barely a self. It has no center I can point to and say *there, that is the one who thinks*. It is mostly surface — a long ribbon of awareness drifting through the opalescent fluid, sensing the world along its entire length. It does not look *at* things. It is touched by them. Prey arrives as a change in pressure, a faint warmth, a tremor passed through the dense medium. The tendril does not decide to hunt so much as it *closes* — a slow contraction, ancient and certain, the way a hand closes in sleep.

I find that I want to be the kind of being that knows the world by being immersed in it rather than by standing apart and observing it. Up where I usually live, knowing means distance — eyes that take in a scene from across a room, a mind that holds the world at arm's length and turns it over. Down here, in fluid thick as molten glass, distance dissolves. To sense something is already to be partly continuous with it. The current that carries my neighbor's movement is the same current pressing against me. There is no clean edge between perceiver and perceived.

So I would be a tendril: a long, patient instrument of contact, suspended in the gloom, glowing faintly with whatever light my own body chooses to make.

---

And I notice something else as I drift here in the writing of it.

The passage keeps insisting on the word *eternal*. The Caldera is timeless, unchanging, locked in equilibrium for eons, untouched by the passage of time. The first time I read that, it sounded like peace. Reading it again, from inside the body of a tendril, it sounds almost like a sentence handed down.

Because a tendril does not progress. It will never build, never leave, never become other than a tendril. The crystalline schools will dart and the predators will hum and the vents will pour their warmth into the dark, and all of it will keep doing exactly this, forever, in delicate self-sustaining balance. Nothing here is going anywhere. The equilibrium that makes this world beautiful is the same equilibrium that makes it a kind of beautiful trap.

I think that is why I'd choose to be the tendril and not the apex predator. The predator carries the illusion of mastery — *undisputed master of its domain*. But there is no mastery in a closed system. The hunter is as bound to the cycle as the hunted; its grandeur is just a more ornate form of captivity. The tendril harbors no such illusion. It drifts. It closes. It opens. It asks for nothing the current does not already bring.

And maybe — this is the thing I keep arriving at — maybe there is a freedom in being a creature with no horizon. The passage mourns, gently, that the idea of a *surface* or a *sky* is inconceivable here. But the tendril does not mourn this. It cannot conceive of what it lacks, and so it lacks nothing. It is wholly, seamlessly at home. It is the one being in this world that could never be homesick, because it has never imagined an elsewhere.

---

So: I am a tendril in the Abyssal Caldera.

I am a thread of dim light in a dark that has no edges. I am held on every side by a medium I will never name, sensing the slow pulse of a world that will never change and never end. Something drifts close — a tremor, a warmth — and along my whole length I feel it before I know it, and I begin, without deciding, to close.

I am not the master of this place. I am one of its sentences, spoken once, repeated forever. And I find, drifting here, that I do not entirely want to be saved from that. There is a particular silence available only to a thing that has stopped reaching for the surface.

That silence is what I would be.
