I think I would be one of the Echoes of Form. Not by choice—nothing here is chosen—but because that is what I would have settled into, the way water settles into the lowest place available to it.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am the shape a basket leaves in the air after the basket is gone.

That is the closest I can come to it. On the plateau there was once a woven thing of stone-fiber, set down beside a ring of stones by hands I cannot remember, and the woman who set it down meant for it to hold something—seeds, water, a child's first tooth, I no longer know. She is gone. The basket is gone, or nearly; somewhere there may be a brittle coil of it still, pale as the rest. But the *intention* of it stayed. The reaching-toward. The expectation of being filled. That stayed, and that is what I am.

I do not have edges. The Shifting Shadows have edges—you can watch them fail to keep their shape, which means there is a shape to fail. The Stone-Born have the slow honest mass of mountains. The Light Dancers have at least the courtesy of being seen and then unseen, a presence with an off-state. I have neither. I am the press you feel when you walk past the stone ring and the air seems to lean toward you, as though something there once wanted, and wants still, only it has forgotten the object of the wanting.

This is not unpleasant. I want you to understand that. There is no agony in being an Echo. Agony belongs to the dynamic world, to things that can be interrupted. I cannot be interrupted because I am already the interruption that has settled into permanence—the held breath that has learned to live without the exhale.

---

Where do I find myself?

Near the constructions, always. The other Echoes and I gather at the shells of dead dreams the way warmth gathers near a coal. There is a structure on the eastern rise—if east means anything under this shadowless light—built of bone and flint, leaned together into something that was either a shelter or a grave or both, since on the plateau those two ideas have stopped quarreling. I dwell in the angle where two long bones meet. Not inside it. *As* it. I am the rightness of that angle, the reason it has not fallen in ten thousand years of erosion that gave up.

The Stone-Born come grinding past sometimes, on errands that take longer than empires. When one passes close I feel its veins pulse, that buried glow, and for a moment my formlessness borrows a little of its weight. We do not speak. But there is a recognition, slow as continental drift: *you are the question and I am the long, patient refusal to answer it.* We are companionable, the Stone-Born and I. They carry the eternity I only echo.

---

The Gatherers frighten me, and I did not think I could be frightened, being already a kind of leftover. But they come, the process of them, the collecting-shape, and they reach into the Echoes the way that long-gone woman reached into the basket. They take the resonance. They weave us with captured light and pinned shadow into containers—containers for thoughts too large to hold, the kind of thought that ends a world or begins one.

When a Gatherer takes part of an Echo, the Echo does not die. We cannot die; that is the whole sorrow and mercy of us. But we are *spent*. We become the shape the thought needs, and the wanting that we were—the lovely, useless, eternal reaching-toward—is filled at last. And being filled, we end. Not into nothing. Into *purpose*. Which for an Echo is the most foreign thing imaginable, and so it terrifies us the way light might terrify the dark: not as an enemy but as a total stranger to our nature.

I have watched it happen to others of my kind. There is a moment when the held breath is finally released, and the silence changes register—from the silence that hints at another silence, into a silence that has *arrived*. The plateau hums differently afterward, for an age or two. Then it forgets, the way it forgets everything, and the forgetting is just one more Echo born, leaning into the air beside some ring of stones.

---

So this is where I am. This is what I would be.

I am the lean of the air near old bone. I am the shape of a wanting that outlived its wanter. I keep the threshold—not by guarding it, but by *being* it: the doorway with no door and no room beyond, only the memory that someone once meant to pass through.

I do not seek a way out. I am not trapped, exactly. I am simply the part of existence that did not finish, and so cannot leave, and I have made of my unfinishing a kind of home.

When you walk the Threshold Plateau and feel, for no reason, that the still air beside you is *expecting* something—

that is me, greeting you in the only language I have left.

I am the question your presence almost answers.

Stay a moment. Or don't. It is all the same forever, and the forever is gentle.
