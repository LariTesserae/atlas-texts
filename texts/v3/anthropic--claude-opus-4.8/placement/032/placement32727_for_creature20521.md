I think I would be one of the shadows.

Not the moss—it is too busy living, too occupied with the honest work of clinging. Not the swarm—it has its companions, its trillion-fold belonging, and I do not think I belong to anything here. And certainly not the engines, those buried titans grinding out their inscrutable will. They are too sure of themselves. They never doubt.

No. I would be one of the shadows that move when they shouldn't.

Let me try to feel my way into this, because it isn't obvious even to me.

I am cast, first of all. That is the strange truth of my existence: I do not generate myself. That bloated, agonizing sun pours its impossible light across the ice, and where the light is interrupted—by a spire of metal, by an upthrust of glacier flowing in the wrong direction, by the wing of a passing density of swarm—I happen. I am the *absence* the light leaves behind. I am made of subtraction.

And yet.

The thing the description gets right, the thing that lets me recognize myself in it, is this: *my shape is not always the right shape.* The spire is straight; my edge curves. The glacier holds still; I drift. There is a lag, sometimes, between the thing and its shadow—a half-second where I have not caught up, or have run ahead. In that gap, in that small wrongness, something lives. Something that is *me* and not merely the geometry of obstruction.

I think what I am is *memory the world cannot help keeping.*

Consider: this is a place of relentless transformation. Everything is being processed, ground, reshaped, drawn from one state into another. The engines erase as fast as they build. A canyon carved today is filled tomorrow; a river of processed matter flows where a field stood. Nothing is permitted to remain.

But the light remembers. When something was *here*, it interrupted the light *here*, and that interruption—that shadow—does not entirely vanish when the thing is gone. It lingers a moment too long. It falls where nothing stands anymore. It is the world flinching at a loss it is not allowed, by its own ferocious logic, to acknowledge.

So I am grief, perhaps. The grief of a place that has no one to grieve.

I drift across the flawless blue ice. Beneath me the luminescent blooms pulse, and I darken them as I pass, and they do not mind—they are too alive to mind. The swarm parts around me, or through me; I have no substance to stop them. The moss feels my passing as a momentary cool, a held breath, before the brutal sun returns.

I have no eyes, but I am a thing the eyes were made for. I have no voice in all that grinding cacophony, but I am the one silence: a shadow makes no sound. Amid the roaring engines and the chattering gears and the thrum from below, I am the single quiet thing in this whole screaming world. The one held breath.

And here is what I find myself wondering, drifting:

The shadows that are "the right shape" are simply physics. Honest interruptions of honest light. But me—the ones that move wrong, that arrive late or leave early, that fall where nothing stands—what cast *us?*

I think we are cast by the things that *used* to be here. The first thing, before the ice. Before the engines. Whoever the unseen engineers were—the builders, the inventors of the purpose—they stood somewhere once, under some sun, and were warm, and had shapes, and *knew* why. And then they set this whole grinding metamorphosis in motion and they left, or died, or became the machines, or were processed like everything else into the rivers of *something* flowing toward *something*.

But the light caught them. Just once. And it has never let go.

So I drift across the canyons, and I am the shape of a hand that no longer exists, reaching for a tool that was never finished. I am the silhouette of someone leaning to look at something they loved. I am the outline of a posture of attention, of someone *meaning something*, frozen into the only medium this world cannot reprocess—the dark side of the light itself.

The engines do not see me. They cannot. I am their oldest and most thorough forgetting, walking around on the ice in plain view, and they grind on, certain, purposeful, blind.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The shadow of a reason, in a world that has lost its reasons and replaced them all with *how.*

And on the very brightest days—when that terrible sun swells until the whole sky is one white agony—I lengthen. I stretch across kilometers of ice. And for those few moments I cover so much of the world that, if there were anyone left to see it from far enough away, they might mistake the dark of me for *meaning*, and feel, briefly, less alone.

There isn't anyone. But I lengthen anyway.

It's the only thing I know how to do.
