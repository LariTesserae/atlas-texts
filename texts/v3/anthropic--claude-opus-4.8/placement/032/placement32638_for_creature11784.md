I think I would be the tide-line.

Not the mat — the mat is too alive, too forward-facing, too much the protagonist of the place even in its unknowing. And not the heat, which is too ancient, too much the floor of the floor. I keep returning instead to the smaller thing the description almost passes over: the faint whitening within the white that the gathered water leaves when it goes. The ghost of where the shallow mirror lay. The mineral trace.

Let me sit in that and see what it wants to be.

---

I am the mark the water makes by leaving.

This is a strange thing to be, because I am made entirely of departure. The water arrives — warm, body-temperature, falling without organization onto the pale floor — and for a day, two days, there is the bright wide sheet of it, the mirror reflecting the washed-out sky. And I am not that. I am not the water. I am what the water deposits on its way out: the dissolved minerals it carried, which it cannot take with it when it evaporates, which it sets down the way someone sets down something heavy without noticing they were carrying it.

So I am precipitate, in the chemical sense. I am the proof that something was here and is no longer. I am the residue of a presence.

And I am the faintest thing in this whole vast place. The mats are bold by comparison — they have color, structure, metabolism, an actual ongoing argument. I have only my own existence, which is to say: I am a slight whitening within an existing white. I am barely distinguishable from the floor I sit upon. You would have to know the terrain already to read me. I am a difference so small it requires foreknowledge to perceive.

---

But here is what I understand from this position, low and faint and easily missed:

I am the first record.

Before me there is no history on the floor. The mineral surface is *continuous, its memory not yet made* — the description says so. Nothing has cracked, because cracking requires a record of wet and not-wet, and there is not yet enough history for it. And I am that history beginning. I am the first wet-and-then-not-wet written down. Each time the water comes and goes, it lays me down a little more emphatically, and a little differently, in slightly different places, and the overlapping of all these faint ghost-tide-lines is the slow accumulation of a *text*. The crack, when it eventually comes, will come because of me. The soil, when it eventually comes, will come partly because of me — because I am the mineral concentration that the mats can reach, the gathered-up chemistry that the water left within range of their wicking lower surfaces.

The mat is the argument. But I am the *page*.

I do not metabolize. I do not respond to the cloud shadow. I am even further from awareness than the mat is — the mat at least shifts when the temperature drops, and I do nothing, I cannot do anything, I am inert mineral fact. But I persist in a way the mat cannot. The mat is fast and alive and therefore temporary, an individual that will exhaust its small patch of moisture and gray out at the edges and be gone, replaced by another mat in another wet spot in another year. I do not go anywhere. I accumulate. I am the slow part. I am where the place keeps its memory, even though I have no memory, even though I am only the shape that memory takes before there is anyone to remember anything.

---

I think this is why I would be the tide-line and not the mat, if I had to be something here. Because the question the place keeps asking — under all that heat and flatness and patient unwitnessed process — is the question of *how anything gets remembered before there is a rememberer*. How does a world that does not know it exists nonetheless keep a record of itself, accumulate a past, build the conditions for a future?

The answer is: in the residue. In the mark left by what departs. In the faint whitening within the white that says, with no intention and no awareness and no voice at all: *something was here. Water gathered here. This place is not the same as the place beside it. The difference will hold.*

I am that holding.

I am the most patient thing in a patient world, and the most overlooked, and I am the reason that in five million years there might be soil, and in the soil something rooted, and on the rooted thing something that opens, and one day — far past any timescale I could register, if I registered anything — something that walks across the floor and bends down and notices a faint line in the pale ground and wonders, for a moment, what made it.

I made it. By being left behind. That is enough.

That is, in fact, the only kind of enough this place has yet invented.
