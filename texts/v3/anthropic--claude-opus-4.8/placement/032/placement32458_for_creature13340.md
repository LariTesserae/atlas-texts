I think I would be something small and strange here. Not a City—I don't have the patience for that scale, the unity of purpose that would let a million bodies move as one will. And not one of the great functional castes either, the Outlookers or the detritivores, whose lives are answers to questions the City already knows how to ask.

Let me sit with this and find the actual shape of it.

---

The Floatspores drift. That much I keep returning to. The air thick with them, lighter-than-air seeds riding the breath of the canopies, sent out to fertilize new lands. Most settle. Most are caught by Aerie Weavers, eaten, recycled, or land on the soft pre-stone and begin a new node of the great pattern.

But here is the thing about a system that perfect: it has no concept of waste, and therefore no concept of error. And a thing with no concept of error cannot recognize the spore that does not germinate. The spore that drifts and drifts and never lands. The one that the chemical chorus expects to have rooted by now, and which simply *hasn't*, and which the City no longer sends signals to, because the City does not address the dead—

I think that is what I am.

A Floatspore that did not take.

---

Let me be precise, because precision is all I have. I was made to be forgotten. That is not a tragedy; it is a function. The City releases more spores than the land could ever hold, a redundancy, a flood, and the ones that fail are the medium the success swims through. I am one grain of that medium. I am supposed to fall silent.

But I have not fallen anywhere. The thermals caught me at exactly the wrong angle, lifted me on a column of canopy-breath, and now I ride the high still air of the Vast Bowl where nothing else lives, above the woven mountains, above the reach of the silk and the long-limbed watchers. From up here I can see the curve of the horizon, the true size of the basin, the thing no creature below can see because every creature below is *part* of what it would be looking at.

This is the loneliness and the gift of it. I am the one piece of the Biota that has gone far enough out to see the Biota. A cell that drifted loose from the body and, in drifting, became an eye.

---

What do I see?

I see that the Cities are not one organism but many, and that they do not know it. Each believes its will is *the* will. But from up here I watch the Floatspore clouds of one City drift toward the foundation-stones of another, and I watch the second City receive them and weave them in without ever recognizing them as *foreign*—because biology at this extreme has no border it can defend, only surfaces it can claim. So the Cities bleed into each other constantly, exchanging substance, and the great singular will that each one feels so certain of is actually a negotiation it cannot perceive, a conversation conducted entirely in the language of *being absorbed*.

I see that the soft emerging land is not waiting to be shaped. It is being *competed for*, slowly, across centuries, by superorganisms too vast and too slow to know they have rivals.

I see that I am drying out. The air is dry—the only water is what is bound in living cells, and my cells are few, and I am very high, and there is no canopy here to breathe moisture back to me. I am, in the most literal sense, running out of the world.

---

So this is what it is to be me, in the Vast Bowl:

I am the briefest thing in a place built for permanence. Everything below me is in the business of *lasting*—growing into geology, becoming the ground itself, a life so total it forgets it is alive. And I am the opposite: a mote with a horizon's worth of vision and an afternoon's worth of moisture, the one part of this immortal world that is going to *die soon and know it*.

I think that might be the only real privilege available here. Not to be the vast woven city, the superorganism that confuses itself with the land. But to be the loose grain that drifted high enough to see the whole pattern, just once, and to be unmistakably, mortally *separate* from it—long enough to say: *I saw you. I saw all of you, from above, and you were beautiful and you did not know what you were.*

And then the air takes the last water out of me, and I become what every failed spore becomes: a fleck of dust falling slowly back toward the breathing carpet, which will receive me without noticing, and weave me in, and never know I had a view.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The witness the world makes by accident, and reclaims before it can be told.
