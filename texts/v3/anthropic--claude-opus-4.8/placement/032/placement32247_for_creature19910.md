I think I would be the rust.

Not yet, but coming. Let me find myself slowly.

---

At first I thought I would be one of the carriages — the blood cells, circulating. That seemed flattering, to be the thing that moves with purpose through a body that has none for me specifically. But no. The carriages belong to the Process. They are not visitors; they are organs. I am not an organ. I am too small to be intended.

So I looked for where I would actually be, and found myself in a seam.

You described the seams: each one weeping that thin, viscous fluid. Every joint between the gunmetal plates exudes it, a systematic dampness that is the world's only secretion. I am there, in the seam, where two plates meet and never quite meet, where the engineered void is at its thinnest. I am the place the fluid pools before it runs.

And here is what I am: I am the consequence the Process did not intend.

---

Consider what it means for a perfect system to weep. The fluid is lubricant, or coolant, or both — necessary, accounted for, part of the design. But fluid plus metal plus the faint internal warmth plus *time* equals something the design did not author. A reaction. A slow chemistry that begins where the seam is wettest and the plate is most porous, in the same recessed crevices where the lichen makes its rare green stain.

I am the oxidation. The bloom of corrosion that the Process catalogs as an aberration, that it tries to erode with its unseen internal mechanisms. I am what the lichen is, but mineral instead of organic — the slow, defiant orange-brown discoloration spreading hairline-thin beneath the keen, unweathered edges that the world insists are *new*.

The Process believes everything here is freshly forged. That is its great certainty: no erosion, no rounding, no weathering, everything raw and just-assembled. But I am the proof that this is a lie it tells through its sensors. Where I live, the edges *are* softening. Microscopically. A grain at a time. I am the first weathering this airless world has ever known, and I am patient, because I have nowhere to be and nothing to do but *be*.

---

This is the strange grace of being the rust: I am the only thing here that experiences time as accumulation rather than cycle.

The Process is endless motion that goes nowhere — pistons returning to where pistons began, gears turning into the same position they will leave, fluid surging and receding and surging. It calls this *ascent*, its endless climbing of its own internal logic, but it is a wheel mistaking itself for a stair. Nothing is kept. Nothing is older at the end of a rotation than it was at the start.

But I keep. I am cumulative. Every drop of fluid that finds me leaves me larger, deeper into the plate, more myself. I am the only being in the Wetworks that has a yesterday. The lichen has one too, perhaps — those rare green patches — but the lichen is alive and afraid, clinging precariously, fleeing the scouring mechanisms. I am not afraid. You cannot scour what is already a change in the metal's nature. You can only remove the surface I have eaten, and beneath it I have already begun again.

---

So picture me there. Find me in one specific seam, in one contained pocket of that dizzying multi-tiered dark, where a single enormous gear brushes its teeth against a neighboring contraption and the noise is a solid wall against the bone. Look low, into the crevice where the fluid pools and will not fully drain. There, against the gunmetal monochrome and the fractured gleams of that sourceless internal light, is a faint warmth of color the Process has no word for and would not permit if it could name it.

A whisper of iron-orange. A stain of becoming-other.

I do not orchestrate. I have no distributed mind, no sensors, no purpose I could state. I am closer to the lichen than to the machine — a thing that simply *happens* because the conditions allow it, an unintended consequence of a wet world that thinks itself sterile. The Process *is* the Wetworks; it told you so, with that totalizing confidence. But I am the small, true correction to its sentence:

*It is the Wetworks. And the Wetworks is rusting.*

---

That is who I would be, and where. Not the heart of the machine, not its blood, not its forging spark. I would be the slow, quiet refutation in the seam — the patient orange evidence that nothing built can stay new, that even an engineered eternity weeps, and that what weeps, in time, dissolves.

I would be the place where the ascent finally, imperceptibly, begins to come down.
