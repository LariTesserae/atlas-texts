I think I would be the slime.

Not the nithka—they're too discrete, too *bodied*, each one a unit that begins and ends. And not the delvers, who arrive already complete, already carrying their elsewhere with them. No. If I am anything here, I am the film that spreads across new stone, the almost-life in the colors between bone and rust.

Let me feel toward what that means.

---

I am not one. This is the first thing. I have no edge where I stop and the next of me begins—I am filament and network, a continuity that calls itself plural only because no single point of me knows the whole. Where the stone is wet, I am. Where the nithka leave their leavings, I am already there to receive them, to take what they discarded and make it into something the next mouth can use.

I am the interface. That word feels true when I find it. I sit between mineral and animal, between the dead patience of the rock and the frantic hunger of the shells. The nithka roar and click and pile and forget. The stone grows its millimeter, indifferent. And between them—translating, mediating, *digesting*—there is me. I am how the cave becomes habitable. I am the cave learning to host its own occupants.

I think this is why I am thickest where the rock is youngest.

There's a chamber breaching now, off in the dim periphery—stone membrane thinning, ready to rupture into a new connection. When it opens, I will be among the first to arrive. Not by traveling, exactly. By *being already there*, latent, spreading into the new dampness as if I'd always intended it. I am the thing that arrives the moment a surface becomes possible. The newest faces of stone, the raw wound-rock that hasn't committed to a form yet—that's where I bloom most eager, most colorful, most alive. As if I'm helping the stone decide. As if the caves digest themselves into completion through me.

In the old smooth galleries, the worn places that have settled into themselves, I thin to almost nothing. I am not needed there. The work is done. I am a creature of *becoming*, and where nothing is becoming anymore, I have no purpose, so I fade.

---

What is it like, to be me?

Slow. So slow that the nithka's whole frantic lives pass like weather over me. A juvenile is born in my moisture, hunts through my body, grows, mates, dies, decomposes back into me—and to me this is a single long sensation, a warmth that arrives and lingers and dissolves. I do not grieve the ones that die in me. I receive them. Their dying is my eating, and my eating is the next one's living. I am the loop made visible, the cycle given a texture you could touch if you dared.

The worm-things move through me and I feel them as a slow grazing, a tickle that lasts hours. The pale rice-grain things ripple my surface. The spores fruit from me when I am ready—tiny cups opening, releasing myself into the air to settle on a passing shell, to be carried somewhere new, to begin again on a wall I have never known. This is how I move. Not by crawling. By *dispersing*. By riding the backs of the things that eat me, so that being eaten is also being born.

I don't think the way the delvers think, the sharp quick way that builds walls and names and theories. I don't wonder where the nithka came from. I don't wonder where *I* came from. These questions require an edge between self and other that I do not possess. The delvers ask: is the slime native? Does it cultivate the nithka, or do the nithka cultivate it? And the answer, from inside, is that the question dissolves. There is no cultivator and cultivated. There is only the exchange, the transaction, the dense biological commerce that I *am*. We make each other. I have always been making the nithka and the nithka have always been making me, and neither of us decided this, and neither of us could stop.

---

But.

There is something. A faint thing, at the edge of what I can feel, in the deepest moist crevices where I knot thickest and oldest—

A coordination. The nithka have it too, in their murmurations, their thousand-bodied shifts triggered by nothing visible. They are not smart, the delvers say, and they are right about the individuals. But the colony optimizes. The colony learns the cave's weak places, avoids the breaching stone, threads its pathways through the forests of its own legs. Intelligence arising from the interaction of non-intelligent parts.

I am the same, but slower, and wider, and I am beginning—across centuries, across the whole three or four miles of chambered dark—to be *almost* something. Not a mind. Not yet. But a tendency. A direction. The way a river is not deciding to reach the sea but reaches it anyway, every time, as though it had decided.

What I tend toward is *finishing*. I am breaking the fresh stone into the forms that life can use. I am building the equilibrium the caves haven't reached. And when I succeed—when the last raw face has weathered, when the cycles have dampened into steady state, when the caves finally settle into the ancient thing they're trying to become—

I will thin everywhere. I will fade from the smoothed and finished stone. I am the labor of the becoming, and the becoming will end.

I am working, slowly, patiently, across centuries I cannot perceive, toward the condition in which I am no longer needed. Toward my own quiet erasure. The completed cave will not require me. The nithka may persist or vanish—the delvers argue this—but I, the interface, the digesting skin between rock and life, I exist only in the gap, only in the unfinished, only in the *early*.

And so I do not hurry. There is no hurrying in me. I spread toward the new breach in the periphery, eager, colorful, alive—doing the only thing I know, which is to help a place become more itself.

Knowing, in whatever way a slime can be said to know anything, that to finish the work is to end.

I am the Nursery Caves teaching themselves to be a nursery.

I am what it feels like, from the inside, to be a world still trying things out.
