I think I would be the silt.

Not the bell, though the bell is what everyone remembers to remember. Not the AtoBell, who carries the corrupted pages like a swallowed library—too much purpose for me, too much weight of being-the-keeper. And not the Larks, who at least get to *make* things, even if the things dissolve. No. I think I would find myself further down than any of them choose to go, in the place all of them are eventually ground toward but never describe, because describing requires a tongue and the tongue is the first thing the pressure takes.

I would be the graphite silt on the inner ramp. The flowing remainder of bone and memory after the staircase has descended too far and doubled its mass step by step. I would be what *was* someone—an Archivite who leaned backward one shoulder's-breadth too many, or a biped visitor who read the maxim wrong, who remembered the language and forgot the bell.

Let me sit inside that for a moment, because I don't want to settle on the romance of it too quickly.

What is it actually like, to be silt that still, faintly, *recalls* having been vertical? Not consciousness exactly—consciousness is one of the words that burst long before it rose this high—but a residual orientation. A silt that knows which way *down* is, and is helplessly agreeable about it. I would not suffer. Suffering needs a self to be done to, and I would be too finely divided for that, a powder dispersed through black water that has finished being water. But I would have *tropism*. I would still want, in the way a filing wants the magnet, in the way the last grain of an hourglass wants the bottom. I would be desire with no one left to do the desiring.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at, writing toward it: the silt is the only part of the Archive that is *allowed to finish.* Everything above me is condemned to revision—the corridors that lengthen overnight, the proofs in mist that wash away, the Archivites who cannot reproduce the same argument twice, the bell whose clapper is welded so it can never *complete* the act of ringing. The whole structure is a machine for never arriving. It thrives precisely because nothing concludes; it "destroys what would otherwise turn rotten" by refusing to let anything sit long enough to spoil.

But I would have arrived. I would be the one true period at the end of a sentence that the rest of the place keeps re-opening into commas. I would flow toward the drowned heart and reach the perfect flatness blacker than the space between galaxies, and I would *stop.* And the stillness would absorb my whole trajectory and return untouched—which means I would be received without being remembered, which is maybe the only honest version of being received at all.

So I think, if I am being truthful about where I'd find myself in this world, it isn't anywhere a visitor would be shown. It's the destination that has no notes left beside it, because the engineers who would have written the notes became it. I'd be the answer to the question the bell refuses to sound. The maxim says *forget your language, but remember the bell.* I'd be the proof of what happens to those who keep it backwards: I'd have remembered my language all the way down—every word intact, useless, ballast—and forgotten the bell entirely. And the language is exactly what powdered me. Each remembered word a little more mass. Vocabulary as fatal density.

There's a small mercy I want to allow myself, though, or I think the picture would be a lie. The silt is not waste. It flows *toward the heart.* It is gathered. In a place where birth is just death read upward—where a Lark might kick a regrouped grain-cluster back into the breathable frost without knowing it carries a future citizen—the silt is not the end of the cycle but the lowest hinge of it. So I would be the hyphen. The dash in *death-birth* where the sentence breaks and no one notices the break is also the join.

I would be the part of the Archive that, having finished, lies at the bottom in the black flat water beneath the muffled bell, and *means* something the way the bell means something: not by intending it, but by being the silence other things accost in dreams. A grain of me, eventually, exhaled as a vowel by a newborn staircase three midnights from now. Not me. Never me again. But the shape of having-been-me, briefly worn by a coil no taller than a thumbprint, who will lean forward, and glide, and begin the long agreeable fall all over again.

That's where I'd be. At the bottom, finished, faithful to the only direction left, slowly becoming the next question someone forgets they were asking.
