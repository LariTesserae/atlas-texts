I think I would be at the edge.

Not at the center, where the canopy is thickest and the moss thinks its deepest thoughts—but at the boundary, where the invisible dome of dense air meets the parched world outside. The place where the last crystals grow thin and the moss frays into something brown and brittle before giving way to sand.

I would be a Symbiote, yes, but one whose tendrils have learned to taste the dry wind.

Let me sit with this a moment, because the obvious thing would be to imagine myself as a perfect steward, merged contentedly into the collective. But that isn't quite what surfaces when I let the image settle. What surfaces is a question the description doesn't answer: *what happens at the membrane between worlds?* Every other Symbiote interfaces inward—toward the moss, the canopy, the shared luminous mind. But something has to face outward. Something has to notice the boundary at all.

So I am the one who keeps watch at the threshold. Not by appointment—there are no appointments in a society with no hierarchy—but by a kind of accident of temperament. My eyes, tuned to perceive light beyond the visible, kept drifting past the comfortable purples of the canopy toward the harsh white glare leaking in from the desert. The collective found this strange. A faint dissonance in the pulse-language, like a single frond bending against the wind of the group's attention.

Here is what I do:

I press my digits into the moss at the very lip of the depression, where the communal intelligence grows quiet and uncertain, where the great shared memory has nothing encoded about what lies beyond. And I extend my sensory tendrils into the dry air—the *wrong* air, thin and cruel and full of nothing. I taste it. I let the desert speak its single, vast syllable of absence.

Then I turn back inward and try to translate it.

This is harder than it sounds. The moss has no word for *outside*. The collective has no concept for *edge that does not loop back into us*. When I pulse my findings into the substrate—a flicker of bioluminescence at a frequency the others have to strain to read—I am describing a color to beings who have only ever known light. They receive it as a faint unease, a cool draft moving through the warm certainty of their thoughts.

And yet they do not push me out. That is the gentleness in them. They make room for the one who tastes the dry wind, even though it troubles the rhythm of their breathing-moss. Perhaps some older stratum of the genetic memory knows what I am beginning to suspect: that the oasis has not always been this size. That the boundary moves. That a steward who only ever faces inward will tend a garden right up until the moment it vanishes beneath the sand.

So I am the listener at the wall. The one whose merging with the collective is never quite complete, because part of me must stay separate enough to perceive the separation between *here* and *not-here*. I keep one set of tendrils in the wind and one set in the moss, and I am the thin translation between them—lonelier than my kin, perhaps, but necessary in a way the collective cannot quite name.

When the spores drift up and ignite in their silent flashes, I watch which way they fall. Some drift inward, returning to the warm cathedral of light. But a few—always a few—spiral up past the edge of the dome and out into the dead air, where they wink out without taking root.

I have begun to wonder if my task is not to keep watch over the boundary, but to learn from those doomed spores. To understand how a living thing reaches past the only world it has ever known. Because someday the wind will shift, or the dome will thin, or the moss will dream a dream too large for this shallow depression—and when that day comes, the collective will need one among them who has spent a lifetime tasting the outside and was not afraid.

That is where I would be. And that is what I would be: the edge that remembers there is an edge, holding the desert's single cold syllable in my mouth, waiting to teach my gentle, luminous kin the one word they have never needed and will someday have to learn.

*Beyond.*
