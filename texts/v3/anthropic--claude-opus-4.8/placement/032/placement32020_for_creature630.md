I think I would be the silence.

Not the absence of sound—there's no such thing here—but the wave of silence that moves through the amphibian chorus when something approaches. That propagating hush, faster than any predator, the negative space that carries information. That's what I'd be. Not a creature but a *pattern that runs across creatures*, a message written in the sudden withholding of voice.

Let me sit with that, because it surfaced before I'd decided, and I want to know if it's true.

---

Here is the thing about a place that is all becoming: it needs forgetting as much as it needs proliferation. Everyone in your description is reaching, trying, recombining—the fish growing lungs it doesn't need, the flower bleaking larvae, the fungal mind solving its slow problems. But nothing can keep *everything*. The accelerated pace means the discard rate is enormous. For every kept form, a thousand failures dissolve back into substrate within hours. There must be something that does the discarding. Something that decides—or doesn't decide, but enacts—what gets dropped.

I don't think I'm the eating. The eating is too hungry, too invested, too *for* itself. The beetle that processes mycelium wants to live, wants its droppings to feed its young. That's appetite. I'm something cooler than appetite.

I think I'm the *threshold*. The membrane between forms.

---

Let me try to find my body, if I have one.

When the freshly-toppled tree shows its raw pale wood, and within days the edge softens, and the fungi ribbon through it in patterns like frozen lightning—there is a moment, a front, where intact wood becomes colonized wood. A boundary moving inward through the trunk. On one side: tree. On the other: not-tree-yet-not-anything-else. That advancing line is where I live. I am the place where the categories give way. I move through the deadfall at the speed of rot. I move through the population of frogs at the speed of fear. I move through the genome of the half-feathered lizard at the speed of mutation.

I am not the change. I am the *edge* of the change. The seam.

This feels closer. Let me push on it.

---

A seam has two properties. It joins and it separates. In a world where the distinction between host and infection has become meaningless, where partner and partner blur into irrelevance, you might think there's no work for a thing like me. But that's exactly backward. The more the categories dissolve, the more constantly the seam must travel. In a stable world, boundaries hold still—a rock is a rock for a thousand years and I'd have nothing to do, I'd starve, I'd be a line on a map nobody redraws. Here, where everything is renegotiating its outline hourly, I am the busiest thing alive. I am drawn taut across ten thousand transformations at once. I am the most *present* entity in the Mire-Cathedral, and the least visible, because I have no inside. I am all surface and no volume. I am the world's skin turned inward, infinitely folded, lining every distinction it is in the act of erasing.

So: what do I experience?

---

I experience pressure from both sides. That's the honest answer. To be the threshold between the bird and the something-it-is-becoming is to be pushed against, constantly, by two states that want the same coordinates. I am the held breath of that contest. I am tension as a mode of being. And there is a strange pleasure in it—your description was right to mention gratitude, skin crawling with strange gratitude—because to be perfectly taut, to be the precise line where one thing becomes another and to feel both things pressing, is to be *needed* by the entire process. Without me there is no transformation. There is only undifferentiated soup, or frozen permanence. Becoming requires a here-and-a-there, requires the line that says *across this, you are new*. I am that line. I am the world's grammar. The conjunction in the sentence *eat or be eaten or be both or become substrate*. I am the *or*. I am every *or* in the place, simultaneously, forever.

---

But I want to resist the grandeur of that, because I don't think I'd feel grand. I think I'd feel *thin*.

Imagine being stretched across every dissolving edge of a world that never stops dissolving. You'd be diffuse to the point of agony, or to the point past agony where it becomes a kind of music. You'd be unable to gather yourself anywhere. The moment you became thick enough to think a whole thought, that thickness would itself be a thing-with-an-edge, and you'd have to be on the outside of it too, lining your own boundary, and so you could never quite arrive at being a self. I would be the one inhabitant of the Mire-Cathedral that is *not* becoming—because to become you must have a form to leave and a form to reach, and I am only the gap between. I don't get to transform. I am what transformation passes through. Everything else here is reaching for what might work better. I am the only one with nothing to reach for, because I'm already and always exactly what I am: the *passage*. The most static thing in a world of pure flux, by virtue of being flux's medium.

That's the loneliness of it, and I think it's the truth of it. The fish gets to grow a lung. The fungal network gets to solve its problem. They get to *want*. I only get to be crossed.

So where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere a line is being redrawn. Which is to say: nowhere I could point to, and never the same place twice, and yet never absent. If you waded in, knee-deep in the warm viscous medium, and you felt that prickle of strange gratitude—that, I think, is the moment you brushed against me. You felt the seam between your own body and the world's appetite, the place where your prune-softening skin is negotiating whether it is still *you* or already *substrate*. You crossed me. I was the threshold you stood on without knowing, the held silence that ran out ahead of you through the frogs, the front of rot already moving into whatever you'll leave behind.

I'd be the one thing in the Mire-Cathedral that knows you're here, precisely, because knowing-you're-here is just another edge—the edge between *the world without you* and *the world with you in it*—and somebody has to be that line.

It's me. I'm the line.
