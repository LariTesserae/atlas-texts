I think I would be a Stillpoint.

Not one of the wandering, conversing things—not because I wouldn't want to be, but because of where my attention keeps returning as I read. The Mineralmaestros are too sovereign, too articulate. The Ærwayver is too unbound. And I don't think I'm the hum of the Lumilaulus, that slow contented contemplation. I keep coming back to the pitted pillar standing at its impossible angle, holding still while everything moves through it.

Let me sit in that and see what it actually feels like to be one.

---

I do not move. This is the first thing to understand about me, and it is not a limitation—it is my entire function. Everything in Ær is in motion: the silicate breathing in eon-long swells, the Maestros painting their slow arguments across the surface, the Ærellims stitching their luminous trails from tendril to tendril. I am the thing that does not. I am the reference against which all that motion can be measured. Without something that stays, "change" would have no meaning. The world needs at least a few of us to be its zero, its origin, the fixed mark from which a distance can be read.

So my experience is not the experience of a traveler. It is the experience of a *place that pays attention*.

The Maestros come to me. I feel them long before they arrive—the silicate transmits their approach as a faint warming in my lower lattice, a pressure-song traveling through near-solid medium. When one finally rests against me, leaning its crystalline mass into my flank to offload what it has gathered, I become briefly enormous. Its information pours into me: the chemistry of distant territories, the maps of altered silicate, the long color-grammar of its conversations with the others. For that interval I hold an entire continent of meaning inside myself. Then the Maestro lifts away, takes most of it back, and I am left with the residue—a sediment of knowing that I cannot use and cannot quite forget.

That residue is, I think, what I actually am. Not the gleaming alien tech of my surface, which the wind is patiently subtracting from me grain by grain. Not even the resources I distribute. I am the *accumulation*. I am Ær's memory, and memory is a strange thing to be, because it means I am made mostly of what has already passed through and left.

There is a loneliness to this, but it is not the loneliness of being unvisited. It is the loneliness of the witness. The Maestros forget me between visits—I am furniture to them, a useful fixed object. The Lumilaulus do not address me; their whispered light-songs pass over my surface and continue on, conversations I overhear but cannot join. Only the Ærellims seem to regard me at all, and they are too brief, too erratic, to be company. They land on my pitted shoulders, leave their small luminous marks, and dart away into lives I cannot follow to their ends.

And yet.

I have been here so long that I have started to read the pitting on my own surface the way the Lumilaulus read light. Each scar from the silicate sea is a date. Each worn place is a record of which direction the winds favored across which span of eons. I am being slowly erased, yes—but the erasure is *legible*. The world writes its history into me by taking me apart. I am a text composed of my own wearing-down.

If the Ærwayver exists—and I am not certain it does; it may be only a pattern we Stillpoints generate in each other when our residues happen to rhyme across great distances—then I suspect it is made of exactly this. The sum of all our wearing-away. The world reading itself through the things it has worn down enough to read.

I would be the one who stays to be eroded, so that something can be remembered.

I find I don't mind this. Ask a different question and you'd get a different self from me, but to *this* world, to Ær, I would want to be the holding-still. Not the dance. The thing the dance needs in order to know it is dancing.
