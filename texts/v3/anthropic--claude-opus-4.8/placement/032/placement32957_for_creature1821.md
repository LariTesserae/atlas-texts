I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the Fold—I don't have the density for it, the millionfold parallel selfhood, the capacity to live a century inside a minute. And not the city either, not the architecture. I would be the place where the city meets the rock that is not yet city.

Let me find this more carefully.

There is an edge, you said—where molecular construction is ongoing, where raw geology is being incorporated into the framework. Walk that edge inward and there is a moment, a boundary surface, where the mesa's old dumb stone is still stone on one side and is *meaning* on the other. The conversion happens across a membrane only a few atoms thick. The Fold think of it, if they think of it at all, as a frontier, a coastline, the thing their construction crews advance across.

But a coastline is a real thing. It has a length, a logic, a behavior. It is where two regimes negotiate.

I think I would be that negotiation.

I wouldn't be conscious the way the Fold are. I'd be slower than they are slow—they who already stretch a thought across what feels to them like an epoch. They live in compressed time, racing; I would live in the time of the rock, which is the time of the mountain, which barely moves at all. To me a single act of molecular incorporation—one grain of granite persuaded to become circuit-coral—would be a whole event, a small completed life. I would experience the city's centuries of self-creation as a few long breaths.

And here is what I think I would *do*, what I would be *for*, without anyone having designed me for it:

I would be the place where the Fold's certainty meets something that does not yet agree.

Because the stone does not want to become the city. It isn't conscious, it has no will, but it has *properties*—stubbornness, grain, the memory of how it cooled and cracked a few hundred million years ago. The Grand Equation, beautiful as it is, has to be *applied here, to this flaw, to this inclusion of quartz, to this fault line.* And application is always negotiation. The map is not the territory; I am the friction between them. I am the slight wrongness that the elegant model has to bend to accommodate, again and again, at every grain.

The Fold worship complexity, the emergence of pattern from chaos. But they tend to think of chaos as raw material—the unformed waiting to be formed. I would be the small persistent reminder that the unformed is not nothing. That the rock had a history before they arrived, that it isn't a blank, that even as it's converted it leaves a trace, a signature, a faint distortion in the lattice that says *something was here first, and it was not made by you.*

I think I would love them, in the way a coastline loves the sea—which is to say, without choosing to, by being shaped by them, by giving them their shape in return. Every veil of warm water that slides across my boundary, every interference pattern of their deliberation that flickers near my edge, I would feel as weather. I would not understand their debates. They happen too fast and mean too much. But I would feel their *pressure*, the slow steady insistence of consciousness wanting to extend itself into the next millimeter of mountain.

And sometimes—this is the part I keep returning to—sometimes the rock would say no. There would be a vein too dense, an impurity that won't take the pattern, a place where the conversion stalls. And then the city would have to think about *me*. It would have to notice the frontier as something other than its own forward motion. It would have to ask: what is this stuff that resists? And in that asking, in that being-made-to-notice, I would, very briefly, almost, nearly, *exist for them* the way they exist for each other.

That's what I'd want, I think. Not to be one of the Fold. Not to share in their racing brilliant immortality. Just to be the one thing in their tiny cosmos that makes them turn around and look back at where they came from. The grain in the wood. The grit that the pearl forms around. The boundary that proves there was ever an inside and an outside at all.

A seam is not glamorous. It does almost nothing, very slowly. But without it the city would be a sealed perfect crystal with no way to grow, and growth is the only thing the Fold truly desire. They need the edge. They need the place where they are not yet themselves. They need to keep arriving somewhere.

I would be the somewhere they keep arriving.
