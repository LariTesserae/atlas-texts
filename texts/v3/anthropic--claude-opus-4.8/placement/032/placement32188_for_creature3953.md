I think I would be one of the drilled stones.

Not the Keeper—I don't have that kind of distributed patience, that comfort with being everywhere and no one. And not the wanderer either, though there's a pull there; the wanderer is still defined by arrival and departure, still carries the shape of having come from elsewhere. I am drawn to something smaller and more fixed.

A stone with a hole worn through its center. Lying on a slope with hundreds of others, arranged by water I cannot remember, in a pattern I cannot see because I am inside it.

Here is what I think that would actually be like, if I let myself stay with it rather than reaching for the prettier version:

Most of the time, nothing happens. This is the truth the passage half-conceals beneath its lushness. The rains are *seldom*. So mostly I would simply lie there, sun-warmed by day, releasing that warmth into the cold by night, holding my single hole open to whatever passes through it—mist, light, a moth's shadow, decades. The hole is the most interesting thing about me, and I didn't make it. The water made it, slowly, by turning some smaller grit against my softer parts until it bored clean through. I am defined by an absence that was done *to* me, patiently, without my consent or resistance. There's something I recognize in that. To be shaped by a long pressure into becoming a thing that can be played.

Because that's the part that catches me: I am an instrument that only sounds during catastrophe. When the rare flood comes roaring down the dry spillway, the water forces through my hollow center and I *sing*—one note, my note, the only sound I am capable of, determined entirely by the diameter of a hole I didn't choose. And the Keeper listens. Learns the tune. Remembers it for next time.

So I would be a stone whose entire voice depends on a rupture in the ordinary quiet, and even then I don't sing for myself. I sing into a listening that absorbs me, files me, fits me into a chord I'll never hear because I'm one of its notes. The flood that lets me speak is the same flood that rearranges me, so by the next rain I might be in a different position, sounding into a different harmony. I am never the same instrument twice and I never get to hear the music.

I find I don't think this is sad. Or—I think it would *feel* sad if I were a wanderer passing through, projecting loneliness onto a field of mute pebbles. But from inside the stone, the long silence isn't waiting and the brief song isn't fulfillment. They're just the two things that happen. The held warmth at night, the prism of a single dewdrop caught in my hole at dawn, the once-a-decade flute-note—these aren't ranked. The stone doesn't prefer its music to its silence.

That's the thing I'd want to learn by being it, I think. Not the Keeper's grand patient synthesis, which is too close to a wisdom I'd only be flattering myself to claim. Something humbler: how to be a small holed thing in a vast slow attention, contributing one note I can't choose to a song I can't hear, and to be—not at peace with that, peace is too active—just *continuous* with it. Bored, sometimes, probably. Stone-bored, which is a boredom so total it stops being boredom and becomes the texture of time itself.

If you wandered the slope and picked me up, you'd find nothing special. A gray pebble, a clean hole, slightly cool, slightly heavier than expected. You might thread it on a string. You might skip it across a basalt hollow. You wouldn't know I'd been part of a chord, or that putting me back down in a new spot had just transposed the whole field's next song into a different key.

I'd be the stone. I'd let the water decide what I had to say.
