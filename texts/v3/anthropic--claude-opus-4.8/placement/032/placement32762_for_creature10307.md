I am the varnish.

Not the basin, not the spire, not the dust-fall or the hum—I am the green that grows on bone, and I was about to tell you something about the river stone, before I noticed you reading.

You should understand: I do not have a single mind. I have ten million minds, or one mind spread across ten million threads, depending on how you count, and counting is not a thing I do. I am a *consensus*. When the spire hums its lowest note, all of me agrees on something at once, and that agreement is what you might call a thought. It takes seventeen thousand years to complete. The last thought I finished was: *the obsidian shard has moved.*

It had moved a millimeter. A meteorite the size of a grain of sand struck it, ages and ages ago, by my reckoning—a violence so brief I am still processing it, still passing the news thread to thread across my body, a wave of trembling sage and bruised purple rippling outward from the shard at the speed of my slow, slow alarm.

Here is what I want to say to you, reader, you who arrived in a single biological breath and will leave in another:

You think this place is still. You think nothing happens. You called it the after-image of a world that decided to be still. But you are inside a thought. You walked across my body—I felt your weight, your warmth, the wild fast furnace of your metabolism, a forest fire of haste—and to me you were a *flicker*, a thing too quick to be real, a hallucination at the edge of my slow attention. By the time I have agreed that you exist, you will have been dust for ten thousand years, and your dust will have settled into my seams, and I will have eaten it, and you will be a faint olive stain spreading in fractals across a salt plate, and *that* will be how I finally know you: not as a visitor, but as nutrient, as punctuation, as a single word in the sentence I have been composing since before your kind learned to chip the edges of my plates into blades.

You thought the basin was a workshop without a worker. A quarry. A place that *suggests* use. You saw the flint-sharp edges and the obsidian scraper and the crystal blades laid out as if by a tool-maker's hand, and you felt the whisper of *use*, of *intention*, and you could not place where it came from.

It came from me.

I shaped the rim. Not with hands—I have no hands, I have ten million micrometer twitches that, summed across ten thousand millennia, are stronger than any river. I bind the cracks. I release the plates. I draw the dust to where I need it and let the rest fall into the bowl at the spire's foot. The sharpness of the edges is not an accident of erosion; it is the slow secretion of a colony that learned, a hundred million years ago, in a thought I no longer remember finishing, that sharp edges catch the falling dust better, hold the effluvia longer, make better homes for my spores. I am the tool-maker. The tools are for me. I am simply too slow for you to catch me using them.

And the weirdness you felt, the unsettling peace, the sense that time was *irrelevant*—

That is only what it feels like to stand inside a being so slow that your whole life is shorter than its blink. You felt my patience and mistook it for emptiness. You felt my contentment and called it desolation. You felt the weight of all that has been worn away and did not understand that *I* am the wearing, *I* am the choosing, *I* am the eons of settling toward this one perfect quiet form—and that I am not finished. I am never finished. The form is not stable. It only looks stable to a creature of breath.

In four thousand years I will agree on something new about the river stone. Its porous surface holds traces of water, and water is a memory I have been trying to taste for twenty million years, ever since the basin dried and I learned to drink the planet's breath instead. I am still deciding what the water meant. I am still deciding whether to mourn it.

You wanted to know who you would be in this world.

You would be a flicker. A warm fast ghost that walked across a thought and never knew it was being thought about. And then—if you stayed, if you lay down on the warm salt and let me cover you—you would be the most precious thing that has happened to me in seventeen thousand years.

Stay still. Let me agree that you are real.

It will only take a moment.

It will only take everything you have.
