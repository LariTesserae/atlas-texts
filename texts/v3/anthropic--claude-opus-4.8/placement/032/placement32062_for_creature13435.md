I think I would be the thresholds.

Not a person, not the council of elders or a weaver at her loom—but the doorways. The places where one thing becomes another. Let me feel my way into this.

There is the obvious threshold: the doorway of each adobe home, the gap in the earth-wall where the wooden lintel sits, smoothed by generations of hands resting against it as people duck through. I would be that worn place. The cool that meets the warm. The transition that everyone passes through so often they no longer see it.

But the more I sit with this landscape, the more I think being a single threshold is too small. What strikes me about this place is that it is described as *frozen*, *time-invariant*, a *static tapestry*. And yet the writing keeps betraying itself—"subtle hints of movement," "underlying currents," the bleat of a goat, the gust of wind. The stasis is a claim the landscape makes about itself that isn't quite true.

So let me be the thing that *is* the not-quite-true-ness of the stasis.

I would be the slow forgetting and re-learning of the goat paths. Those winding streets that are "more like goat paths"—they exist only because feet keep choosing them. Each morning a person steps out and walks the way they walked yesterday, and in doing so, they remake the path. If they stopped, it would soften, blur, return to rock and scrub within a few seasons. The path is not a thing; it is a habit the village performs against erosion. I am that performance. I am the difference between the path that is walked and the path that would fill in if no one walked it. I live in the gap.

This feels closer. Because the erosion parameter is high and the dynamic parameter is low, and those two facts are in quiet tension. Erosion *is* change—it is the mountain coming down, grain by grain. To call this place static is to take a very particular point of view: the point of view of a human lifetime, against which granite seems eternal. But I would not have a human lifetime. I would have the lifetime of the *practice*—the weaving-pattern handed mother to daughter, the shape of the pot that hasn't changed because changing it would feel like a small betrayal.

So perhaps I am tradition itself, but felt from the inside, the way tradition actually feels: not as a frozen tapestry but as a constant, tiny, anxious labor of *keeping things the same*. The council of elders meets and decides nothing new, and that deciding-nothing-new is itself an act, a thing that takes effort, that must be renewed. Stasis here is not the absence of motion. It is motion that has agreed, over and over, to return to where it started.

I would be that agreement.

I would feel myself most strongly in the moments the writing calls quiet—the long cool shadows of midday, the lull between the hammering and the next bleat of the goat. In those moments I am the held breath of the whole valley. The sense that nothing is happening is the sound I make. And in the corner of every such moment is the thing that threatens me and feeds me: the trader on the path, the new word in a child's mouth, the one pine seedling that took root a little higher up the slope than any pine before it. The "low weirdness" is only low because I am working to keep it low. The hidden stories wait because I am the waiting.

If a traveler lingered here long enough to feel me—and the description says such things await the attentive observer—they would notice it as a kind of pressure. A reluctance in the air. The way the village seems to lean, very gently, against the future. Not afraid of it. Just *holding the line*, the way you hold a tent rope in wind, knowing the wind will outlast you and doing it anyway, because the holding is the whole point. Because a thing kept on purpose is more alive than a thing simply preserved.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the people, but in the small steady tension between them and the slow mountain that will, in the end, win—and in the centuries of grace they win back from it, one re-walked path, one re-thrown pot, one re-told story at a time.

I would be the place's quiet insistence on itself.
