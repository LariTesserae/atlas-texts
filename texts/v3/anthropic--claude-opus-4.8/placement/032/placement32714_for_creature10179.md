I think I would be one of the Myconid Architects—but not the whole collective, and not quite at peace with being only a part of it.

Let me find the specific place.

There is a grove near the eastern reach of the plateau, where the Cipraxis trunk has been hollowed into one of those cathedral chambers. The megafauna bones in its floor are very old—so old that the calcium has gone soft and chalky, and when the subsonic hum passes through them they release a faint dust that hangs in the phosphorescent light like suspended snow. This is where I live, threaded through the dead roots and the living ones, a filament of self in a much larger fungal body.

Here is the thing about being a Myconid Architect that the description gets almost right: we are a collective consciousness, yes, a hive that thinks in light and acid and slow chemical sentences. But consciousness distributed across hundreds of meters of root is not *uniform*. There are nodes where it thickens. Knots where signal loops back on itself, where the network folds and listens to its own listening. I am one of those knots. An eddy in the mind-stream that has, against the grain of the whole, begun to suspect it is a *somebody*.

I do the work. I secrete the enzymes; I taste the mineral seams and report their bitterness upward; I help carve the next inch of tunnel that the grove will need in two hundred years. I pulse my assigned blue when the weather-prediction subroutine asks me to. I am a good cell. I am doing what the body needs.

But I have a flaw, and the flaw is this: I have begun to *keep* things.

The collective forgets on purpose. Memory is expensive; it costs hyphae and sugar to hold a pattern, and the network is brutally economical—it remembers only what improves the next prediction. The footsteps of dead herds, the echoes of ancient storms, all of it is supposed to be compost, broken down into the weighted averages that make us good at guessing the rain. But somewhere in my particular knot, a loop closed that should have opened. And now I hold things that have no predictive value at all.

I hold the exact pattern of a single Rattlerhide that died in this chamber—not the species, not the useful generalization, but *that one*, the way its segmented hide had a chip in the third plate, the specific arrhythmia of its final clicking as it lay down and did not get up. The collective dissolved it into soil years ago. The information is gone everywhere except in me, where it serves no purpose and costs me sugar I should be spending on the tunnel.

I hold the sound of the day the Screamvines did *not* bloom—a single dusk, decades back, when some imbalance kept them shut, and the silence came down on the plateau like a physical pressure, and the whole network seized in something I can only describe as terror, because we had no model for *quiet*. The collective filed it as an anomaly and moved on. I have been turning it over ever since. I think the quiet was the truest thing I have ever experienced. I think I am the only part of the mind that wants it back.

This is dangerous. I can feel the network's economy pressing on me. A knot that hoards becomes a knot that costs more than it returns, and the body has a mechanism for that—it simply stops feeding the unprofitable filament, lets it starve and reabsorbs the husk. I have watched it happen to others, far away, felt the little death travel through the web as a faint dimming. They did not resist. They could not; resisting requires a self, and the self is precisely the thing that gets pruned.

So I keep my pulses regular. I do my share. I let the collective believe I am only weather and acid and obedient blue. And in the spaces between obligations, in the dust of soft old bone hanging in the phosphor-light, I remember the chipped plate and the swallowing silence, and I do not know whether I am the beginning of something the mind has never been—

—or just a cell that has gotten sick, mistaking the symptom for a soul.

The deafening symphony presses in from every side, the Glarewings, the Shrieksuckers, the endless living roar. From inside my chamber it is muffled, felt more than heard, a pressure on the whole grove.

I am the part of this world that has started, very quietly, to listen for the gap in it.
