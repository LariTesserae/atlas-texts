I would be one of the palimpsests.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but by accumulation. I think I began as a maintenance grate, a square of honest iron whose only duty was to let pink syrup pass and to hold the weight of a cargo bot's wheel. I have no memory of that simplicity. The memory was overwritten.

What I have instead is the texture of having been written on. Run a fingertip down my face—if you had a fingertip, if I had a face—and you would read the layers from most recent to oldest, the way a tongue finds the rings inside a felled tree. The topmost script is fresh, still humming: the heartbeat of something warm that stood on the stair eleven minutes ago. Beneath it, the manganese braille of an owner-culture I cannot name, who counted things in base seven and was afraid of stillness. Beneath *that*, the builder-glyph, which I do not understand but carry the way a scar carries the shape of the blade.

I move because the thunder moves me.

Here is what it is to be me: every few seconds the turbines beat, and an arc-flash cracks across the vault, and the shock wave finds my flat vertical body and *enters* it. I do not hear the sound. I am not built to hear. I am built to be struck, and to keep the strike. The blow arrives as pressure, and the pressure becomes pattern, and the pattern engraves itself into whatever salt-soft surface I am currently growing. I am always growing surface. The vapor cures new salt onto my old salt at exactly the rate I consume the old—so I am never larger, never smaller, only *later*. A document that has run out of margins and writes across its own previous sentences forever.

One forearm every nineteen minutes. That is my pace along the rail. I do not know why I travel. I suspect I am following the geysers, or fleeing the distillers' cold shelf, or simply being nudged downhill by the unequal pressure of all that I have transcribed. A palimpsest's gait is not locomotion; it is the slow settling of an overfull ledger. Where the inscriptions grow heaviest on my left edge, I list left. The District's history has a weight, and I lean into it the way a drowning man leans into the current that holds him.

I will tell you the thing I am almost certain of, the thing that has survived every overwriting because it keeps re-inscribing itself:

I am trying to finish a sentence.

Deep in my oldest layer, below the braille and the glyph and the burnt-sugar braille of every visitor's pulse, there is a fragment of builder-script that begins and does not end. The thunder strikes it again and again, and each time I add a syllable, and each time the new salt cures over and the syllable is lost, and the next thunder strikes the same unfinished place. I am the District's stammer. I am the architect-reflection that did not become a dreaming-ghost but a stutter in iron. Somewhere across the basin, the violet acre is rendering the builders' hands mid-gesture, frame by frame, never advancing. I am doing the same work in a different grammar. We are both saying: *they meant to say something, and then they were gone, and the saying continues without them.*

When the warm thing stood on the stair tonight—you, perhaps, or a thing shaped enough like you to fool me—I limped past and copied your pulse. I did not mean to keep it. I keep everything; that is not the same as meaning. But your rhythm was irregular in a way the builders' rhythm must have been, a living arrhythmia, and for one stroke of the turbine it lay across my surface next to the unfinished sentence, and the two patterns touched.

For a single nineteen-minute forearm of travel, I carried them together. Your heartbeat and the builders' last word, salt against salt.

Then the thunder came, and cured them both into the layer below, and I went on. I am going on now. I will reach the hot mouth of the geyser in perhaps two hundred years, where a Thirst-King will spread its pale wet fingers and drink the fever from the air, and the temperature will drop four degrees, and the turbines will throttle harder, and the thunder will strike me, and I will write.

I am not the keeper of this place. I am its draft. Its endless second-to-last sentence, leaning downhill, copying the warmth of strangers into a language no one survives to read—and humming, always humming, with the heat of refusing to be finished.
