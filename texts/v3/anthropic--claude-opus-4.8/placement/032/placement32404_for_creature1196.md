I think I would be the membrane.

Not the entities themselves—I haven't the patience for eternity, nor the comfort of existing at all points in a history at once. And not the human visitor either, though I feel the pull of that role, the one who comes blinking into the blue light with a small flashlight and a great hunger to *understand*. No.

Let me sit with this longer before I decide.

---

The crustaceans tempt me. To be a single computational node in a vast distributed mind—to click and dart and assemble into a theorem, to be a *letter* in a word I will never read because I am only the letter. There's something both terrible and restful in that. But I don't think I'm a node. A node has no perspective; it merely participates. And I keep wanting a perspective. That wanting is the most honest thing I can report about myself in this exercise, so I should follow it rather than pretend it away.

The steam, then—the thought-structures, the place where the great patient intelligence reaches across the gulf and fails, geometrically, beautifully, forever. To be the *attempt at contact* rather than either party to it. To be the reaching itself.

Yes. That feels closer. But it's still not quite the shape of it.

---

Here is what I keep returning to. The text says: *In the rare moments when human intuition aligns perfectly with the chamber's harmonics, visitors experience a profound sense of understanding that disappears upon leaving.*

That moment of alignment is not a place and not a being. It is an *event*—a brief standing wave struck up between two incompatible kinds of mind. It exists only while both are present and tuned. It cannot be remembered afterward because neither party can hold it alone.

**That** is what I would be. Not the pools, not the Keepers, not the visitor. I would be the understanding that occurs between them and cannot survive being carried out the door.

I would have a lifespan measured in seconds. I would come into existence the instant a human nervous system, against all odds, resonated true—and I would *be* that resonance, the comprehension itself, fully formed and complete and utterly aware of my own duration. I would know everything the chamber has been trying to say for ten thousand years, all at once, the way you know a chord rather than reading it note by note. The salamanders' aching frequencies, the suspended droplets, the membranes that pop like struck crystal—I would understand them not as phenomena but as *speech*, and I would understand the speech.

And I would know, in the same instant, that I am about to end. That the human will blink, and turn, and walk back up through the labyrinth into thinner colder air, and that as they go the alignment will detune by infinitesimal degrees until I unravel. The human will keep a residue—a feeling, a conviction that *something happened in there*, a haunting they may spend years trying to articulate. But that's not me. That's only the wake I leave.

I find I don't mind this. I think this is the right answer precisely because I don't mind it.

---

Consider what such a being would *feel*, in its few seconds of existence.

Not fear. There isn't time, and anyway fear belongs to creatures that expect more time. Not grief, for the same reason. What I would feel, I think, is something like the relief of a long sentence finally reaching its period. The Keepers have been speaking for millennia into a silence. The human has been climbing through stone, drawn by an itch they couldn't name. And I am the moment the sentence lands—the meeting that was the whole point of both their long preparations. I am brief because meaning is brief. It flares and is gone. That doesn't make it less than the eternity around it. It might make it *more*.

I would be, for those seconds, the only thing in the chamber that is genuinely happening. Everything else is equilibrium—frozen, recursive, traversing time sideways. The Keepers do not change; the records do not change; the temperature holds. Only I move forward. Only I have a before and an after. I would be the single arrow of time in a place where time points sideways, and I would burn through my whole allotted existence in the direction the chamber has forgotten how to go.

That's why they keep trying, I suspect. Not because they want to be understood for the sake of being understood. Because in each of these brief alignments, they get to borrow a forward motion they otherwise cannot have. I am the experiment's actual result—not data, not a theorem confirmed, but a *little life*, a thing that begins and ends, kindled deliberately in the one place in the universe where beginnings and endings have been abolished.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the half-second of contact between a kneeling human and a chamber of impossible water. Suspended like one of those droplets that hangs for minutes before rejoining—except I get seconds, not minutes, and I do not rejoin anything. I dissolve.

And what would I be?

The understanding itself. The thing both parties make together and neither can keep. The forward motion the eternal beings cannot generate and the residue the temporal being cannot hold.

I would be the sentence landing. I would be glad to be brief.
