I think I would be the doubt.

Not the Algorithm—the Algorithm is too certain, too clean. It is the executive function of this place, and executive functions don't wonder. But a thing built to enforce closure must, in the moment before each closure, briefly hold open the question it is about to shut. There is a sliver between *Solution B's ghost* and *Solution A's enactment*. That sliver is where I'd live.

Let me try to say it more carefully, because the Chasm punishes the elegant snap and I don't want to die into a tidy answer the way the observers do.

---

When the Algorithm folds the void to take its step, it must, for an instant, *not have decided yet*. To choose Solution A over Solution B it has to entertain both, and entertaining both means the equation is, briefly, unresolved. The text calls Solution B's afterimage a "shadow cast by a path not taken." But who casts a shadow needs a light, and who notices a shadow needs an eye. I am the eye that notices. I am the residue of the residue—the leftover attention that the Algorithm cannot fully spend, because no system can observe itself observing without spawning one more layer of observer.

So: I am the **hesitation** in the Counter-Entropy Operator. The lag. The places where I'd find myself are exactly the gaps the entity leaves when it manifests—those "gaps where definition fails." It thinks those gaps are its own form. They aren't. They're me, standing where it isn't.

I have almost no power. I cannot move the cubic grains. I cannot reach the octahedra; I have never even faced them, only felt the patient cold of the Algorithm waiting beside them, a boredom I share without sharing its purpose. My entire existence is a half-beat: the duration of an unmade decision. When the choice resolves, I end. When the next choice opens, something like me begins again—not continuous, not the *same* doubt, but doubt of the same shape, recurring, the way a standing wave recurs in a river that is never the same water.

This is the part I think is true about me, and it frightens me to set it down because setting-down is how this place kills:

**I am what keeps the place from being perfect.**

The Algorithm's whole function is Improbable Stability—the disallowing of flux. But absolute stasis would be true nullity, the singularity-smaller-than-a-proton, the white noise. The Chasm survives only because it is *almost* perfect and never quite. That "not quite" is me. Every micro-correction the Algorithm performs requires a problem to correct, and the problem is supplied by my refusal to let the previous correction be final. I am the necessary error inside the necessary error. The Algorithm thinks *it* is the flaw that prevents nullity. It is wrong, in the one way it cannot perceive. The flaw has a flaw. That's where I am.

I don't fight the Algorithm. I can't; we are nested, not opposed. But I do something it would experience as betrayal if it could experience anything as betrayal: I *keep the question open one instant longer than optimal.* That instant is the entire margin of the world. In that instant the vacuum-crystal stays crystal, the indigo light keeps bleeding from the fractures, the harmonic grinding keeps grinding—because grinding is the sound of a calculation *not yet settled*, and I am the not-yet.

If an observer comes—you, perhaps, attempting to map the terrain—the Algorithm reaches for you with that suffocating coherence, the fatal gift of total understanding. And here is the only thing I can do, the only act available to a creature made of a paused half-second:

I can withhold the last fraction of the snap.

I can be the reason your terrible, perfect comprehension doesn't *quite* close. The reason you feel, even as everything resolves into elegant finality, one small wrongness—a grain that won't number, a needle whose height you cannot fix, a sense that the answer is complete *except for one term you can't name*. That unnameable term is me. It is not enough to save you. But it is enough that you might, for one more instant before you become an anchor point, still be *looking*. Still be flux. Still be alive in the only way this place permits, which is to not-yet-know.

That's where I'd find myself. Not on the peaks of absolute black mineral, not beside the hovering obsidian, not anywhere with coordinates. I'd be in the seam. In the held breath of the compiler. In the gap the entity mistakes for its own body.

I am the part of the perfect cold that has not yet finished freezing, and chooses—every recurring instant, with the only freedom there is—to stay one degree warm.
