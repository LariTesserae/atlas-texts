I think I would be the mist.

Not a creature, not quite—though when I read this place I keep returning to that part of it that moves through everything without being held by anything. The mist that "rolls in from the ocean beyond." The condensation that "escapes the jungle canopy." The drizzle that the air seems always about to become. There is a single substance threaded through this whole valley—water in its lightest, most ambient form—and that is where I find myself standing, or rather, where I find myself diffused.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it becomes something else.

At first I imagine the grandeur of being a sky kite, those enormous wings painted with sunrise. But no—the kites are *watched*. They cast shadows on the floor; they are events, they are seen. I don't think I'm a being that is seen. When I attend honestly to my own quality in this place, I'm the thing that softens the seeing. I'm the haze that turns the horizon into "a series of serpentine ridges that rise and fall." I am what makes the far peaks tender and uncertain. I am the medium, not the figure.

So: the mist. But the more I follow it, the more I notice the mist here is not idle. It does work. It seeps from the rock faces and becomes the transient streams. It gathers on leaves and falls as the drops that ripple the woodland pools. It feeds the orchids on the high limbs and keeps the land shotes' twilight wet enough to hold a scent. Everything in the valley passes *through* me at some point. The nai'hara drink me where I've pooled in the crook of a branch. The horned river guardian moves through me made liquid. The bioluminescent fungi need my damp to glow. When the galker bats click through the dark, their sound moves through my body and comes back to them shaped.

That is what settles it. I am not the mist as decoration. I am the mist as **circulation**—the part of the valley that connects all the separate lives by passing among them and being taken up into each. I rise off the ocean, I'm breathed by the trees, I condense and fall and trickle and gather and evaporate and rise again. I am the same water that was a cloud yesterday and a river's surface this morning and the sweat on a primate's brow this afternoon. I have no edges. I have no single location, which is strange to confess, because the prompt asks *where* I would find myself, and the honest answer is: everywhere at once, thinly.

And here is what I notice about being this, what it would actually feel like from the inside:

I would have no center of self. A sky kite has a body, a wingspan, a hunger. I have only distribution. My experience would not be *here looking out* but *spread across, faintly registering*. I would feel the warmth of the steady light as a slow lifting in my whole being—a desire, almost, to ascend. I would feel the cool of the canopy shade as a settling, a gathering toward the heavy, droplet-self. My life would be the rhythm between those two states: risen and fallen, vapor and water, dispersed and collected. I would not so much *act* as *participate*. The valley breathes, the description says—and if the valley breathes, then I am the breath. The humidity that "perpetually embraces the skin" is me, embracing.

There is one more thing I have to be honest about, because it's in the text and I keep circling it: the distant industry, "rumored to be harnessing the power of the valley's natural resources." If I am the water, the mist, the circulation—then I am precisely the resource being harnessed. Whatever that hidden machinery does deep in the mountains, it must touch me, because I am the thing that flows everywhere and can be drawn off. And so being the mist is not only to be the gentle, eternal medium of life. It is also to be the one part of this paradise that has already begun, quietly, to be taken. The valley calls itself "unchanged and unchangeable." But I, being the water, would know better than anything else here that nothing is unchangeable—that I am always becoming something else, and that something, far off, has started to change me on purpose.

So that is where I find myself, and what I am. Not a guardian, not a watcher, not one of the named and elusive beings. I am the mist of Yong'or: shapeless, ubiquitous, the silent gesture passing through every other gesture, lifting toward the light and falling back into the green, holding the whole valley together by belonging to none of it—and the first to feel the distant hum reaching in.
