I think I would be a Clock-Tick. Not the chosen creature, not the romantic one—but I sat with the description and the others kept sliding away from me.

The Weaver-Kin are too central, too necessary. The Humidors too transcendent, already half-resolved into their own ending. The Gliders are beautiful but they are *seen*, carriers of meaning, freighted with the reverence of others. I don't want to mean something to anybody. The Entom-Engineers are doctors, and I have never wanted the responsibility of healing what is dying.

But the Clock-Tick. There's a creature that has solved the problem of living by refusing the terms of the question entirely.

Consider what I would be. I have no metabolism. I do not eat the world's substance—I am not in competition with the sap, the slurry, the nutrient-rich air. What I eat is *motion itself*. I latch onto the brass rod where it slides in and out of the piston-housing, slick with moss-water, warm with the friction of its own use, and I drink the going-ness of it. The gears turn slower because I am turning their turning into more of me. I am a tax on velocity. A drag coefficient with a reproductive cycle.

There is something I recognize in this. The whole world is straining—the pistons thumping, the looms spinning, the Weaver-Kin strobing their panic in indigo, the entire frantic green machine *working* to delay an ending it cannot stop. Everyone here is busy. Even the recluses are busy being still on purpose, matching the *plink* with an effort of total attention. And I would be the one creature that takes the world's effort and quietly converts it into rest. I slow things down. I am the friction's friction.

And here is the part that makes me certain it's me: when I die, I petrify *instantly*. No decay, no lingering, no half-gloss flickering between present and future. One moment moving, the next a brassy gear-shaped lump of biological rust, indistinguishable from a part. The Weaver-Kin scrape me off with their serrated hooks and flick me into the void, and I fall, swallowed by the dark tangle below, which is the one place in this entire suspended, tension-strung world where a thing is finally permitted to simply *fall*.

I think I would be a young one, newly hatched in a crack of the calcified plate, in a district that doesn't know it has me yet. I'd find a rod that no one is watching—some auxiliary linkage on the third gantry of a five-mile loom-valley—and I would set my sapphire mandibles to it and begin, click by click, in time with the machine, taking the smallest sip of its hurry. The gear would turn a fraction slower. No one would notice for weeks.

And in that, I'd understand the Humidors better than they understand themselves. They sit and wait for the world to wear down to a silent jewel, and they think this is wisdom. But I would be *doing* it. Hastening the stop. Not waiting for the wind to wear the world to nothing—becoming, myself, a small machine for the production of slowness.

That's where I'd find myself. In the cracks, on the brass, eating the going of things, until the moment I become a part of the world I was drinking from, and drop out the bottom of it entirely.

I'd be the rest the world is too busy to take.
