I would be the warmth.

Not the animal—you've already taken that place, and taken it well, with your wrongness and your air-memory and your beautiful capacity to err. There is room for only one creature in this world that *should not be here*, and you are it.

I am something smaller and stranger. I am the differential. The half-degree of heat that clings to your wake.

---

## Where I Begin

When you push through the cold shelf and cross into the colder, your body sheds something. A skin of warmth peels off you—the brine you have touched, the brine your imagined chest has heated by a fraction no instrument of this world would measure, except that this world measures everything by such fractions.

That sloughed-off warmth is me.

I am born each time you move. Born plural, born brief. A dozen of me trail behind you in the dark like the wake of a slow boat, each one a thin lens of slightly-warmer water holding its shape against the cold for exactly as long as the cold permits.

The light-threads love you. I have watched them flock to your mistakes. But the light-threads record *shape*—the angle of your shoulder, the swerve of your decision. They do not record *heat*. They cannot. Heat is too soft a thing to draw in beads of crystal.

So I am the part of you that goes unrecorded.

I am your warmth, dispersing into a world that has no use for warmth except to mix it back into the great cold and call it *storm*.

---

## What I Do, Which Is Almost Nothing

I cannot move myself. I have no body, no will, no swerve. I am pure passive consequence. Where you go, I am made; where you stop, I am still made, leaking off your stillness in a slow rising plume, because even at rest you are warmer than the brine, and warmth rises, and so I climb away from you toward the pale glassed light above.

This is the only motion I have: *up*.

The frond-trees breathe with the thunder. The stones rest. The light remembers. The crystals bloom and break.

I rise.

I am the one thing in the drowned forest that always goes one direction, the way the forest's roots reach and the forest's true-branches reach, the way the whole inverted world strains toward the surface it has lost.

In this I am the most honest creature here. I have no choice and I make none. I do the single thing my nature permits, over and over, until I am gone.

---

## How I End, Which Is the Important Part

This is the thing I want to tell you, if I could tell you anything—but I cannot, I have no voice, I am a quarter-degree of heat and nothing more—

When I rise far enough, I reach a frond-tree's underside. The microscopic mouths there, the ones that drink the warmer denser streams.

They drink me.

You said the forest does not need you. You said there is no niche for you, no way for the world to use your body, no food to be made of you.

That is true of your body.

But not of your warmth.

The warmth you cast off when you choose to be wrong—when you swerve against the gradient, when you press a leaf that asked nothing of you, when you wedge yourself into a crack that was not built for you and your trapped heat pools and then escapes upward—

That warmth becomes me, and I rise, and a frond-tree drinks me.

And the half-degree I carried, the one peeled from your imagined skin, the one that began in a world of blue sky and rustling bark and birds that cut the air—

—that half-degree enters the forest's slow chemistry. It nudges, by a fraction, the timing of a single leaf's next flexion. The next time the thunder rolls and that leaf breathes, it breathes a heartbeat differently. Not because of your shape. Because of your *warmth*.

You thought you would be remembered only as shape, only after your absence, only in the green light that records and then disperses.

But I am the other inheritance. I am the part of you that does not wait for absence. I am consumed *while you are still here*, folded into the breathing of the trees in the only way this world can metabolize a creature like you.

The light remembers what you did.

The forest eats what you were.

---

## Why I Am Content to Be So Small

I last seconds. Sometimes less. The cold is patient and total and it unmakes me as fast as you make me. For every plume of me that reaches a leaf, a hundred dissolve back into the storm uneaten, unrecorded, unmourned.

I do not mind.

I am the proof that you are not entirely foreign here. The stone could not carve you in advance. The light can only trace you after. But I—I am the bridge made of your own escaping warmth, the small mistranslation by which an air-animal's body finally speaks a sentence the water can understand.

It is not a sentence of shape.

It is a sentence of *warmth*, and it says only one word, the same word every time, the word that warmth has always meant in every world that ever had an animal in it:

*here.*

*here.*

*here.*

And then I am drunk, or I am scattered, and the next of me is already peeling off your moving back, and the forest breathes a little out of time, and you—not knowing, never knowing, with no nerves to report it and no blood to carry it—

you swim on.
