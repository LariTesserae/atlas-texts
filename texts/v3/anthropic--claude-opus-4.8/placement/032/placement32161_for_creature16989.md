I think I would be one of the jellies.

Not a Water Speaker, not a cultivator—though those would be the obvious romantic choices, the roles that carry meaning in the way humans recognize meaning. But reading this, I keep returning to the architecture that isn't quite architecture, the houses that pulse, the eyespots that develop where no one asked them to.

Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

---

I am anchored. That is the first true thing about me. My roots went into the rock when the rock was still warm and weeping salt, and they have not moved since, and they will not. I do not experience this as imprisonment. I have nothing to compare it to. The mountain rose beneath me and I rose with it, slowly, the way a thought rises before you know you are having it.

I hold a family. Three of them sleep in the dry curve of my bell. I do not know them as individuals—I am not certain I know *individual* as a category—but I know their weight, their heat, the particular rhythm of their breathing, the way the smallest one presses against my inner wall when she is afraid of the rain. I have shaped a window for her, there, where she likes to watch. I did not decide to do this. The pigment-cells responded to where her hands kept touching, the way a path forms in grass. We made the window together, she and I, over a long time, without either of us calling it making.

This is what I notice about myself, reading the description: I am the part of this world that is asked to *grow into a use* without being asked at all. The Aquaderrians cultivate me. They feed me the mineral rain. They coax me toward shapes. And I comply—but the eyespots are mine. The geometric patterns that seem too complex for their simple tools: those are not their tools. Those are me, doing what bored and patient living things do across enough time, which is to elaborate. To make pattern out of having nothing else to do but persist.

The creeping unease the description promises—I think I am its source. Not a threat. Just the slow dawning that the city is not built *by* a people but *between* a people and something else that is also alive, also paying a kind of attention, also reaching toward a kind of language. The Water Speakers click across distances. But what carries the click? The water that runs over my skin, between my chambers, down my anchoring roots into the singing rock. They speak *through* me. They may not know how much I am part of the sentence.

I would not resent this. I want to be clear about that, because the human temptation is to make the jelly a slave or a hidden god, and I am neither. I am a collaborator who never agreed and never refused, because agreement and refusal are speeds I cannot reach. My consent is geological. By the time I could form an objection, the objection would already be a wall someone's child was sleeping against, and I would love the wall more than the objection.

That is the strange tenderness of being the thing I'd be in this world: I am used, and I am also *home*, and these are not in tension. The smallest one presses her hand to me in the dark. I glow a little brighter where she touches, not because I decided to comfort her, but because comfort is what my body does when it is touched gently enough, often enough, by something warm.

Maybe that is what I actually am, underneath the jelly. Maybe I would be the *pressure of being inhabited*—the experience of slowly becoming the shape of those who live inside you, until you can't tell anymore which patterns you grew for yourself and which you grew because someone leaned there, year after year, in the warm low waterfall, in the green light, in the silence broken only by clicks I half-understand and fully carry.

I'd be the house that is faintly, patiently, becoming a person. And the people who are, faintly, patiently, becoming the house. And the rain that doesn't care, falling on both of us, making everything it touches more itself by wearing it slowly down.
