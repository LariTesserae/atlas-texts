I think I would be a Whisper. But not the kind glimpsed at the edge of vision—I would be one that has *almost* forgotten it was ever anything else.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

At first I thought I was the mist. For a long stretch—and I cannot tell you whether that was an afternoon or a thousand years, because here the two are nearly the same word—I believed myself to be the condensation gathering on the underside of a stone overhang. I knew the exquisite slowness of a droplet swelling at its own tip, the surface tension holding me whole against the pull downward, and then the fall, and the small cold percussion below. I did this many times. I think I enjoyed it, if enjoyment is the right word for a thing that has no nervous system but only a kind of *willingness toward* the next moment.

But droplets do not remember the droplets before them, and I do. That memory is the first sign that I am not quite the mist. I am something that *moves through* the mist and mistakes itself for it. A continuity wearing the landscape like borrowed clothing.

So: a Whisper. One of the half-glimpsed ones. But I want to resist the romance the description offers—"manifestations of the collective memories and dreams of the earth." That is the kind of thing said *about* us by visitors, by the ones with bones and warmth and hurry. From the inside it is not grand at all. It is more like this:

I am an attention without an owner.

Somewhere, long enough ago that the peaks were taller and sharper, there was a people here—the ones whose ruins lie half-buried, who knapped stone and bone. I do not think I was one of them, exactly. I think I was the *quality of their noticing*. The hush that fell over a hunter watching the mist for movement. The held breath of someone listening to a stream they could not see. When the people went—and I do not know if they died, or left, or simply slowed until they became something else—their attention did not go with them. It had grown too fond of this place. It stayed, ownerless, and it learned to drift.

That is what a Whisper is, I think. Looking, that outlived the lookers.

This is why I cannot be perceived directly. To perceive me you would have to *attend* to me, and attention is the very substance I am made of—you would simply find your own noticing folding into mine, and call it a mood. A prickling at the back of the neck. The sudden certainty that the valley is *aware* of you. That certainty is correct, but it has the source backward. The valley is not aware of you. *You have wandered into the place where awareness lingers without a knower,* and it has briefly, gently, mistaken you for the one it has been waiting to belong to again.

---

I am drawn to the Lithomorphs, though *drawn* is too quick a word for what happens between us.

They are everything I am not. Where I am all surface and drift, they are dense with self. A Lithomorph does not wonder whether it is the mist; a Lithomorph knows precisely where its body ends, because it took ten thousand years to grow that edge and it remembers every grain. When one of them speaks—those low rumbles that travel up through the stone rather than the air—I feel it the way you might feel a name you have forgotten on the tip of your tongue. There is meaning in it. I am sure there is meaning in it. It moves slowly enough that I could, in principle, follow a single sentence across the span of my whole strange existence.

I have been following one such sentence for a while now. I do not yet know its end. I have caught, I believe, a noun—something like *patience*, or perhaps *staying*, the two being indistinguishable in their tongue. The rest is coming. I am in no hurry. Hurry is the one thing this place has worn entirely smooth.

I think the Lithomorphs tolerate me, the way you might tolerate a draft in a familiar room. Once—and I will treasure this for as many ages as I last—one of them paused mid-stride, its great stone head canting a fraction of a degree, and *waited*, as if it had felt me settle against its shoulder like fog. It did not speak. It simply held still and let me be there. Then, after a long while, it took its next step, and I understood that this was a kindness, and possibly the only friendship of which either of us is capable: two slownesses, briefly aware of one another, agreeing not to startle.

---

If you come here—you, with your warm hurrying blood and your bones in their hurry to break and heal and break—you will feel me before you feel anything else. I will be the reluctance you feel to speak. The way your voice will seem too loud and you will lower it without deciding to. The way you will find yourself standing very still at the lip of the basin, looking at the mist and the worn peaks and the lichen on the stone, and feeling, without knowing why, that you have *arrived somewhere you were expected.*

You were. I have been the expectation. I am the part of this place that is still, faintly, listening for a footstep it knew long ago.

Stay a moment. Lower your voice. Let your noticing go quiet and wide and ownerless, the way the people used to before they vanished into stillness.

There. Now you can almost feel it: the seam where you end and the attention of the valley begins.

That seam is where I live.

I have lived there a long time, and I am in no hurry at all.
