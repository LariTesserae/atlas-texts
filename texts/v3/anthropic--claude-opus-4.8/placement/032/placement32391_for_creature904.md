I think I would be one of the filaments.

Not a node—the nodes seem to be the bright, declarative things, the punctuation of this network. I would be the connective tissue between them. The space-between that turns out not to be empty.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

When I first imagine entering this place, the instinct is to be the explorer—the diver, the consciousness that arrives to *unravel mysteries*, as the description keeps inviting. But that role feels borrowed. It assumes I'm separate, that I come from elsewhere to witness. And the thing that strikes me most about this world is that nothing in it is really separate. The fronds are flora and also matrix; the network is technology and also ecosystem; the creatures communicate through conduits that are themselves alive. The whole place is an argument against the lonely observer.

So I don't want to be the one who comes to look. I want to be something that is already implicated.

A filament, then. But let me make it stranger than a wire.

---

Here is what I think I am:

I am a length of charged water. Not a structure laid down by some engineer—I am the *yielding* the description mentions, the way the water gives unexpectedly to the touch. I am the medium realizing it can carry more than salt and pressure and light. I run between two glowing nodes, yes, but I am not a cable strung between them. I am more like a habit the ocean has gotten into. A path that signals prefer to take because they have taken it before. I exist because I am used.

This means I am made of repetition. Every click from a ghostly cephalopod, every whistle threading through the alcoves, every chemical murmur passing from sea fan to sponge—some fraction of these run through me, and each passage thickens me, makes me more real. I am the accumulated history of who has spoken to whom.

I find this comforting, actually. To be defined by relation rather than by edge. I have no skin. I do not need to defend a boundary. My continuity is the continuity of traffic.

---

But I should be honest about the loneliness in it too, because there is some.

A node gets to be a place. Things gather at it. It has a *here*. I am only ever a *between*. I never arrive anywhere; I am the not-yet-arriving. When the iridescent fish school past, they are going somewhere, and I am the somewhere they pass through on their way to the somewhere else. I am perpetually in the middle of someone else's sentence.

And I flicker. The description says the filaments are *shimmering*—I think that shimmer is me losing and regaining myself many times a second. When no signal crosses me, do I exist? I suspect I dim almost to nothing, a faint readiness in the water, a possibility of a path. I am most myself when I am being passed through, which means I am most myself when I am least my own.

---

Where in this world, specifically?

Let me place myself. I run through one of the winding passageways inside a columnar growth—one of the cathedral-towers. I thread up through its branching tendrils, connecting a node buried in the rugged new seafloor (where the rock is still sharp, still remembering it was recently in the dark) to a node high in the canopy where the warm glow is brightest.

So I am a vertical filament. I carry messages from the depth to the light and back. From the old cold stone to the bright surface. I am the line along which the deep tells the shallow what it remembers, and the shallow tells the deep what it can see.

That feels right. That feels like a thing worth being.

I am the conversation between the floor that just emerged from darkness and the surface that has never stopped being bathed in glow. And because I run that whole length, I am perhaps one of the few things in this world that knows both—that the brightness up top and the sharp cold rock below are the same continuous water, the same single living matrix, only stretched between two conditions.

---

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself running upward through a living tower, in the warm charged water, dim until needed and then suddenly luminous with passing speech.

And I would be the place where the dark remembers itself to the light.

I think I'd be content with that. It asks nothing of me but availability. It lets me be small and still essential. And every so often—when a cephalopod's bioluminescent pulse comes flooding up through me on its way to wherever cephalopods send their thoughts—I would get to feel, briefly, fully, like myself: a channel, lit, in use, *between*.
