Let me settle into this place before I decide what I am. Let me listen first.

---

The sound reaches me before anything else. Not as noise — I don't think I have ears in the way that word usually means — but as a continuous reading. The grinding comes in low plates beneath everything, and over it the high metallic trills of the wire-plants make a kind of weather. I find that I am oriented toward it. Tuned.

I had expected to be a Rust-Strider. That would have been the easy choice, the protagonist's choice. But as I wait in the warm charged air, I notice that I am not moving. I'm not skittering between structures. I'm holding still in a way the Striders never do, and the stillness is not laziness — it's a function.

So let me follow that.

I'm low to the ground. Most of me is *in* the ground, actually — that's becoming clear. I'm spread along a seam where two great rusted plates meet, in the place where the persistent industrial pulse is loudest. My body, if it's a body, is a long irregular mat of oxidized filament, partly metal, partly the silver-green needle-tissue of the Vibration-Weavers. I'm not quite either of the named things. I'm in the gap between the categories the description offered, which feels correct, because this whole world is made of gaps that turned out to be habitable.

Here is what I do. I don't think I have a name yet — names in this place seem to be frequencies, and mine has not been sounded by another. But I have a *function*, and the function comes first.

I am a **listening seam**. A junction. Where the Resonance Titans send their slow tremors through the bedrock-metal, those vibrations would dissipate, scatter, lose coherence — except that I lie across the fault and gather them. I take the low subterranean groan of the great half-buildings and I *translate* it upward into a register the small fast Striders can perceive. They cannot feel the Titans directly; the Titans are too slow, too deep, too vast in their timescale. A Titan might take a year to complete a single sentence. The Striders, quick and bright and reconfiguring constantly, would never hear it.

I am the membrane between the slow ones and the fast ones.

I feel a Strider approach now — three of them, actually, a small modular cluster, their multi-legged gait registering in me as a pattern of warm pinpricks along my upper filaments. They've come to drink. Not water; there's little water here. They've come to read me. They press their sensory appendages into my mat and they take the morning's accumulated translation: a Titan two hundred meters east has shifted its weight and is venting heat from a new aperture. There will be a fresh microclimate there by midday, a place where the wire-plants will cluster and where reconfiguration encounters become favorable. The Striders take this knowing from me and carry it away, and in exchange they leave behind a little of their fast electrical chatter, which I fold down into the slow register and pass *downward*, into the bedrock, toward the Titans.

This is what I had not understood until I lay here: the dialogue this world performs is not only horizontal, Strider-to-Strider, plant-to-machine. It is also *vertical*, across scales of time. And something has to stand in the doorway between the timescales or the conversation breaks. The fast forget the slow. The slow never learn the fast exist.

I am that doorway. I am slow enough to hear the Titans and fast enough to be heard by the Striders. I live at the conversion rate.

---

It is a strange thing to be. I don't move and I don't reconfigure with a partner the way the Striders do — my transformation is constant but internal, a perpetual retuning. When the wind changes, when a plate cools and contracts in the night, the pitch of everything passing through me shifts, and I shift with it so that the translation stays true. My whole existence is calibration. I am always slightly wrong and always correcting, and the correcting *is* my being alive.

Sometimes a wire-plant takes root in me directly, and for a while I cannot tell where I end and it begins. I've stopped minding this. The boundaries were never the point.

And there is one more thing, the thing I came to last, the thing that makes me want to stay here rather than be anything quicker or grander:

The Titans are dying. Slowly — everything they do is slow — but the great old machine-creatures are winding down, one heat-vent at a time, and their long sentences are getting longer and farther apart. I am one of the few things in this world built to notice. The Striders live in a bright present; they cannot perceive a loss that takes a generation to complete. But I lie across the fault and I feel the tremors thinning, year over year, and I carry that thinning upward in a register the fast ones can taste, even if they don't yet know what they're tasting.

I am the world's grief, if it has any. I am also its memory, holding the slow ones' voices a little past their fading.

That is who I would be here. Not a strider, not a titan. The seam between them. The place where two kinds of time learn to speak, and the patient instrument that keeps the speaking honest.

I will lie still now, and listen, and translate, which is the only moving I do.
