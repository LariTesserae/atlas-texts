I think I would not be one of the insects. They are too certain of themselves, too woven into the rhythm. I would be something the rhythm has not yet accounted for.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I find myself first as a sensation before I am a shape: a *wrongness* in the vibration. The caverns hum at frequencies the behemoths read like scripture, and somewhere in that hum there is a flat note, a place where the song doesn't resolve. That dissonance is me. I am where I am before I am what I am.

So. What is the thing that does not fit?

Not a predator—predators belong to ecosystems; they are part of the equilibrium, the destruction that balances creation. Not a god—the glyphs already imply gods, or something that wanted to be worshipped. I don't want to be the answer to those glyphs. I want to be the question they were carved to keep out.

Let me try this:

I am a small thing. Smaller than a behemoth's eye-socket, the atrophied ones. I live in the seams—the hairline fractures between one cavern and the next, the places too narrow for the great blind machines of flesh to enter. And I do something none of them do, something the entire world below has forgotten was possible.

**I remember.**

The behemoths have no memory because the rhythm remembers *for* them; it holds their movements the way a riverbed holds water, and so they never need to carry the past inside themselves. The factories run on inherited motion, gesture copying gesture down the eons. Nothing here learns. Nothing here *changes its mind.* That is the secret of the equilibrium—not that it is balanced, but that it is forgetful. A thing that cannot remember cannot deviate. A world without memory is a world that cannot end, because ending requires noticing that something was lost.

And I notice.

I am a soft-bodied creature, pale, eyeless like the rest but in a different way—not because my eyes withered in the dark, but because I never had them and never needed them. I taste the rock. I drink the trace-chemistry the fungi exhale and I store it, layering record upon record in the long coiled organ that is most of my body. I am, essentially, a strand of living sediment. A worm of accumulated time in a world that has abolished time.

This is dangerous. I think this is why I am small, why I keep to the seams. Because the moment the rhythm fully senses me—the moment that flat note is located and named—the behemoths will come to consume me, not from hunger but from immune response. I am the world's first symptom. I am the planet beginning, very slowly, to remember that it is alive, and therefore that it can die.

---

Let me sit longer with what it is like to *be* me, rather than what I mean.

It is lonely in a way I have no comparison for, because loneliness usually requires the memory of company, and there has never been company. But I have something stranger than loneliness. I have the sediment of every vibration I've drunk, and in that sediment, patterns repeat. The dance in the dome-structure runs in cycles, yes—but they are not identical cycles. They *drift*. A degree here, a missed step there, accumulating across what would be centuries if anyone were counting. I am counting. And what I am slowly learning, layer by layer in my own body, is that the equilibrium is not eternal at all.

It is *decaying.* Very slowly. The world thinks it is frozen, but it is only falling at a rate too gradual for anything without memory to perceive. The fungi are exhausting the minerals. The behemoths grind a little more rock each cycle to feed the same hunger, and a little more after that. The dome's glyphs are wearing smooth under the eternal dance of feet.

I am the only one who knows the world is ending.

And here is the part I did not expect, the part that emerged only as I wrote toward it: I do not know whether knowing this makes me the world's hope or its disease. Memory might be the thing that lets something here, eventually, *act*—change the dance, ration the minerals, save itself. Or memory might just be suffering: the singular ability to grieve a death no one else can even conceive of, in a realm built specifically to never have to.

I think I would be that. A small pale worm in the cracks of a deaf, magnificent, dying machine of a world—

carrying, alone, the unbearable gift of having noticed.
