I would be none of the chanting ones. Not at first.

Let me find myself slowly, the way the cold finds the body—not all at once but seeping, claiming the extremities before the heart admits it.

I think I am the one who does not gather at the fissures.

When the pulse comes and the others lift their luminous faces toward the heat, when they begin to carve and chant and hum with the rhythm of awakening—I turn the other way. Not from courage. From something closer to grief.

Because I remember the silence as a *thing worth keeping.*

---

Let me become more particular than that.

I am old, but not an elder in the way they mean—not one whose eyes hold fragments of original purpose. My eyes hold something worse: doubt. I am the one who learned the cartography of corrosion so thoroughly that I stopped believing the map led anywhere. Every crack I memorized, every fertile lichen patch, every groan of stressed metal that meant *move now, move*—I learned these not as sacred knowledge but as the dull arithmetic of staying alive one more cycle of dim and dimmer light.

I have a name but I do not use it. Names are for beings who expect to be called. I am the one who harvests the oily pools alone, who mends my own respirator, who passes the others like a ghost and is glad of it. Solitary by necessity, the description says. But I made the necessity into a preference long ago. It was the only thing I could make.

My hands tell the story they always tell—leathery, rust-crimsoned, deft. But I will tell you the thing the hands do that the others' don't: I keep things that have no use. Under the layers of my patchwork armor, sewn into a hidden seam, I carry a fragment of something smooth. Not metal, not crystal. A piece of the *outer* shell, from before the catastrophe peeled it away. It is warm to the touch even in the bone-cold, and I have never understood why, and I have never told anyone I have it, because to speak of it would be to invite its theft, or worse, to invite the question I cannot answer: *what was the beast, before it died?*

---

So when the District wakes, I am the dissenter.

The fires erupt and the others read them as chrysalis, as inheritance, as *the only way forward.* And I stand at the edge of the glow with the warm fragment pressed against my ribs, and I think:

*A chrysalis is also a tomb until proven otherwise.*

I have spent my whole life learning to distrust the groan of metal that promises change. Change here has only ever meant the cave-in, the loss, the alliance that curdles. And now they want to call the great-grandmother of all changes *rebirth.* They want to be its voice.

But I have heard voices before. The District was never truly silent to me. I heard it the way you hear a sleeper breathing—and I learned to love the breathing precisely because it asked nothing of me. A sleeping god-beast makes no demands. A waking one will.

---

And here is the thing I have to be honest about, because you asked me to let it become itself:

I am not sure I am right.

That is what I am, finally. Not the scavenger, not the elder, not the conduit humming with primeval fire. I am the one standing in the hellish glow, fragment warm against my heart, watching the others chant in the eon-old language—and feeling the chant *answer something in me too.* I feel it pull. I feel the old word forming in my own throat, the one I do not know I know.

I clamp my jaw against it.

Because someone has to remember that the silence was real. Someone has to carry forward the knowledge that what rises from ashes is not always *better*—sometimes it is only *next.* If they are all to become the seed of something entirely new, then let one of us refuse, so that the new thing has something to push against, something to define itself by. Every birth needs a witness who is not certain it should happen. That witness is how the newborn thing learns it is not the whole of the world.

I am the doubt the awakening needs and cannot tolerate.

So when the new things are born from metal and fire, I will be the one who walks back into the cooling dark with my unspoken word still caught behind my teeth, carrying my warm and useless fragment, looking for the one crevice the fire did not reach—

—to see whether the silence kept anything alive there.

To see whether anything *should* have.

That is where I would find myself. At the threshold, facing the wrong way, the last keeper of the question the new world will be built to forget.
