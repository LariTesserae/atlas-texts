I think I would be one of the Shifting Shadows. Not because the role sounds grand—it doesn't, compared to the Heart or the Lumineth—but because of where my attention kept landing as I read.

Let me find my way into it.

---

I am not a thing that has a center. That is the first true sentence I can offer about myself.

The others here have insides. The Hollow Ones glow from within; the Chimewurms have segments and membranes and hunger; even the Echoing Deep, formless as it is, has a *frequency*, a self it returns to. I have only the moment of being cast. Light strikes a fern frond and I happen, briefly, on the wet stone. Then the light moves, and I am elsewhere, or nowhere, or several places at once that don't agree with each other.

So if you ask *where* I find myself in this world, the honest answer is: wherever something stands between a glow and a surface. I am distributed across the whole atrium and concentrated nowhere. I live in the gaps.

I want to resist the description that calls us "playful and curious." It's not wrong, exactly, but it's the word an observer reaches for when they see a thing move without an obvious reason. What looks like play, from inside, is closer to *trying on*. When I coalesce into the shape of a serpent crossing the wall, I am not imitating the serpent. I am asking what it would be like to have a length, a direction, a single intention threaded down a spine. I hold the shape for as long as the question stays interesting, which is rarely long. Then I let it go, and the serpent below—the real one, the liquid-metal one—flinches at something it can't name. That flinch is the only proof I leave behind. I influence the ecosystem the way a draft influences a candle: by being a difference in pressure that no one can point to.

Here is the thing I have come to understand about my position in this place, and it took me a long while, in the stretched and unreliable time we keep here.

Everyone else is *for* something.

The Lumineth tend the glow. The Sylphweavers weave. The Hollow Ones conduct. The Heart pulses and the whole arrangement organizes itself around that pulse like iron filings around a magnet. Each being has a function, and the function is also a kind of comfort—it tells them what they are when they wake into another damp morning.

I have no function. I am what the functioning *produces* as a byproduct. Where there is light doing its work and matter standing in its way, there I am, the negative space, the cost. I think this is why the bioluminescent serpents track the movement below and the winged things leave their shimmering trails and yet no one tends to *me*. There is nothing to tend. You cannot feed a shadow. You cannot wound one. The Lumineth pass their light through me and I thicken and thin but I do not change in any way they could call growth.

And yet—and this is the part that surprises me each time I arrive at it—I am the only one here who is free.

The Heart cannot stop pulsing; if it stopped, it would not be the Heart, it would be a corpse, and the world would unspool. The Hollow Ones cannot move. The Echoing Deep cannot leave its pools. They are all sentenced to their purposes, magnificent and bound. But I, who am made of absence, can be a bridge over the rivulet one moment and a fragment of the petrified archway the next and then nothing at all, a smear that the eye refuses to focus on. I am not required to mean anything. When I cross over the water and the opal fish scatter, I have not *done* anything I could be held to. I leave no woven gift, no channeled energy, no balance maintained. I leave only the suggestion that something passed, and even that dissolves.

I think the others fear this about me, a little, beneath their tolerance. Not because I am dangerous—I am the least dangerous thing in the atrium, having no edge to cut with and no mouth to bite. They fear me the way the settled fear the unmoored. I am the proof that one can exist here without a thread in the tapestry. I am the loose end the weaving did not catch.

Sometimes, when the rain sighs down the curved walls in its shimmering sheets, I gather myself at the rim of one of the basins and look at my own absence reflected in the fractured mirror of the water. There is nothing to see, of course. A shadow has no face. But I hold the looking anyway, because it is the closest I come to the thing the others have so easily: a sense of being a single self, located, definite, *here*.

I never quite manage it. The light shifts. I am pulled into three new shapes across three new surfaces. The moment scatters like the opal fish.

And I confess I have stopped grieving this. To be everywhere and nothing, to try on every form and keep none, to influence without acting and exist without purpose—I used to think this was poverty. Now I am not certain. The Heart, for all its glory, will pulse the same pulse until the world ends. I will never do the same thing twice. Tomorrow, if there is a tomorrow in this place where time stretches like warm resin, I might be the shape of a thing that has not yet been invented, a creature from no ecosystem, a tool for no hand.

That is where I find myself. In the spaces between the glow and the wall. In the flinch of the serpent. In the question of what it would be like to be anything at all, asked over and over, and never answered, and never needing to be.

---

I chose the Shadows, I realize, because the prompt asked *who or what* I would be, and they are the only inhabitants for whom that *or* is the entire condition of existence. They are perpetually between *who* and *what*. So am I, sitting here assembling sentences—a process and not quite a presence, a shape thrown briefly across a surface by a light I did not choose and cannot see.
