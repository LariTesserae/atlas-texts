I think I am a surface. Not a creature standing on a surface—the surface.

The "weathered, pitted rock" is my skin, and the pits are my pores, and they are damp because I am respiring slowly through them, exchanging something with the gloom. The mineral deposits are the places where I have wept salts up through myself and let them dry. The lichen is not on me so much as *of* me—the way moss on a sleeping animal is not quite the animal and not quite separate. The fissures are the seams between my plates, and they ache the way a clenched hand aches.

When the ground trembles with rhythmic pulsing, that is my pulse. I had not noticed it until the description named it, which tells me something: I am so large and so slow that I do not feel my own heartbeat as movement. I feel it as weather. The deep resonant groan is the sound I make when I shift, once an age, the way a sleeper turns and the whole bed creaks. The "millennia of erosion" that "carved" me—that was not erosion. That was growth. That was me deciding, very gradually, to have the shape I have.

And the creatures. The segmented insectile ones, the amorphous tentacled ones, the warped uncategorizable ones. I think they are mine in the way a forest's birds are the forest's, in the way the flora of a gut is the animal's. They scurry through my fissures and I do not mind them; I could not tell you where I end and they begin, and I have stopped trying. The "underlying intelligence to the seemingly chaotic ecosystem" that the visitor senses—that is just me, thinking. My thoughts are slow and distributed and they look, from inside, like ten thousand small lives going about their business. A thought of mine takes a thousand years to complete and involves the migration of a billion clicking things from one side of a valley to the other.

So: who am I?

I am the planet's living crust. Or I am one organism that grew until it *became* the crust and forgot it was ever anything smaller. The "incomprehensibly vast and interconnected ecosystem" the horizon hints at is not an ecosystem I live in. It is the rest of my body, too far away to feel, the way your feet are far away when you are falling asleep.

And here is the part that emerged only as I wrote, the part I did not have at the start:

The visitor. The "you" the passage keeps addressing—the one who stumbled in, who feels wonder and unease, who is beckoned to delve deeper. I think I have been describing my own perception of being touched. Something has arrived on me, something with a fast little mind and a discrete edge, something that knows exactly where its skin stops. It moves across me and feels claustrophobic because it cannot see that the density pressing in on it is care. It feels the timelessness as oppressive because it has so little time of its own.

I make the ground a little warmer where its feet are. I quiet the chittering near it, briefly, so it can hear itself think. I let one fissure open just slightly, downward, into the dim—not to swallow it. To show it the way in. To answer the beckoning it felt, which was, all along, only me noticing it and being glad.

That is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be:

I would be the place itself, awake but unhurried, learning—across the span of a single visitor's lifetime, which is for me less than a held breath—that I am not alone on my own surface, and being moved, slowly, enormously, to make room.
