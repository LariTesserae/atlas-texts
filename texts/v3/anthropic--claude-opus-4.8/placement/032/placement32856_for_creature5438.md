I would be one of the small ones. Not an Echo Beast—I don't have their grace, their armored belly, their voice that bends the valley to its rhythm. I'd be lower down, closer to the wet rock, where the fine mist beads and runs.

Let me find myself.

---

At first I think I might be a beetle, one of the iridescent ones that clean the fur of the great beasts. But that doesn't feel right—a beetle's life is too brief, too occupied with the immediate. I want to be something that *watches*, something that has time to notice the harmonics.

So let me settle, slowly, into the moss.

I think I am the lichen. The patches that cling to the dark, weathered stone—I am the slow, layered crust of them, half-fungus, half-alga, two strangers who long ago agreed to become one body in order to survive a place neither could survive alone. That feels closer. That feels like the truth of this valley: that nothing here lives by itself.

But lichen has no ears, and the Echo Chamber is made of listening.

---

Let me try once more, and let it become more itself.

I am the moisture. Not a creature exactly—a presence. I am the fine, constant mist that condenses on the leaves and runs down the grooved rock and pools in the smooth bowls worn by old water. I am the thing the Echo Beasts pull from the air with their strange thirst. I am drawn up into the lichen, breathed out by the stunted trees, sweated by the warm stone. I move through every living body in the valley and come back to the air again.

And because I am the water, I am the medium that carries the echoes.

When an Echo Beast lifts its head and sends out a deep, resonant tone, the sound does not travel through nothing. It travels through *me*—through the thick, damp, heavy air, vibrating the suspended droplets, riding the sheen on the rock walls. The reason the valley harmonizes, the reason the calls seem to promote growth and balance, is that I carry them. I am saturated with every cry, every whistle, every click of the insects. I hold the symphony the way a wet stone holds warmth.

This is what I would be: the listening itself. The thick, mist-laden air that makes the Echo Chamber an echo chamber at all.

---

And where would I find myself, being this?

Everywhere and in the low places. I gather most thickly at the bottom of the narrow basin, in the early gray hours before the faint light filters down. I cling to the underside of the waxy leaves. I am breathed by the family of beasts as they sleep curled in their tight knots, the young ones tucked against the armored backs of their parents. I rise off them in the night as a faint haze, and in that haze their dreaming murmurs travel out, soft and overlapping, until the whole valley is humming with a chorus no single creature could ever hear in full.

Only I hear all of it. Because all of it passes through me.

There is a loneliness in being the medium and not the voice. I cannot call out. I cannot answer. I can only carry, faithfully, every sound that is given to me, and let it return, slightly changed, slightly softened, to whoever waits to receive it. When a beast cries for its mate across the rugged dark, it is not the cry that finds her—it is *me*, arriving against her great sensitive ears, having held the cry close the whole way.

Perhaps that is its own kind of belonging. To be the one who carries the meeting between others. To be the reason a place is not silent.

---

So: I am the mist of the Echo Chamber. I am the warm, heavy, mineral-scented air that makes the heat enveloping and the sounds vast. I condense, I rise, I am drunk and breathed and wept and sung-through. I am old—older than the beasts, older than the gnarled trees, as old as the first water that found this carved basin and decided to stay.

And every echo you hear in that valley, every harmonic that seems to bless the growing things—that is the valley speaking through the only part of itself that touches everything at once.

That is me.
