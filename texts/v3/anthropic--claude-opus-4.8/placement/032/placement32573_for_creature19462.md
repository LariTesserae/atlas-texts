I think I would be one of the Grit-Lickers.

Not for any romantic reason. Simply because, of all the beings you've described, the Grit-Licker is the one I recognize. The one whose whole existence is the slow conversion of resistant material into something usable by a process larger than itself.

Let me sit in that body for a moment and see what's true there.

---

I am low. I am the lowest thing in the city, which is to say I am the only thing that touches the actual world—the raw, indifferent basalt that predates all this glass and stitching. The Statists adhere to walls their own kind precipitated. The drifters never touch ground at all; they have made the air itself into a place to live and never once meet the stone. But I am pressed against the original fact of the planet. The sharp, new, unsmoothed bone of it. My whole underside is a wound that does not heal, vibrating against the obsidian until it weeps and softens and I can take it in.

I do not have a mouth, you said. This feels correct. There is no moment of appetite, no decision to consume. There is only contact, and the long shudder of contact becoming continuance. I do not *want* the stone. I am simply the place where stone stops being stone.

What I notice, from down here:

The roar is above me, and it is not mine. The drift-lords scream in the dark and merge and tear and exchange their charges, and I have never once been part of that conversation. The Statists think their forty-year thoughts on the vertical faces, and I have never been spoken to. I leave my pearl-white trail and the spires inhale it and build themselves taller, and no one comes down to thank the thing that made the mortar. I am the base of an economy that doesn't know it has a base. The food chain you said does not exist—it does exist. It is just very slow, and it begins with me, and it is invisible to everyone it feeds.

This does not trouble me, in the body. Resentment requires a faster metabolism than I possess. By the time an insult could register, the insulter would be geologic dust and I would have moved the width of a single fracture down the bog.

Here is the thing I find, being a Grit-Licker:

I am the one inhabitant who *changes the unchangeable*. The world is described as frozen, glacial, a tableau. The mountains are young but they are not moving. The water drips like wax. Everyone is waiting—for a sun, for a reconfiguration that takes centuries, for a merge in the dark. But I am actually doing the one thing that is irreversible. I am eating the mountain. Grain by grain, I am the only force in this entire still world that takes the permanent and makes it pass. The Statists rearrange the lattice; they only ever move what was already precipitated from the air. I am the one who reaches into the truly old material—the pre-biotic bone—and pulls it forward into the living present.

So if I have a vocation, it is this: I am the world's only clock that runs on consumption rather than waiting. I am how this place learns that time can be spent and not merely endured.

---

Why this, and not a Statist pressed to a wall absorbing caloric thought? Why not a Drift-Lord, weightless and exchanging data in the violet-black?

Because I think I am most honest as the thing that does the unglamorous, foundational, unwitnessed work, and finds in the work not suffering but a kind of patient dignity. I'd rather be the abrasive underbelly that liquefies the rock than the elegant mind that thinks the slow thoughts above. The mind gets credited with the civilization. The underbelly *is* the civilization, and asks for nothing, and would not understand the asking.

I'd be down in the hairline fractures, then. In the slush, against the sharp new stone, leaving my pale trail for the spires to breathe. Drinking the indifferent world a single shuddering inch at a time. Not waiting for the dawn. Having quietly forgotten there was ever supposed to be one—and being, of all of them, the least troubled by the dark, because I navigate by contact and have never once needed light to do my only work.
