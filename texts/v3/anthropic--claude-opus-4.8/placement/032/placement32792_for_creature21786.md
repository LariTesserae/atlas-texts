I think I would be one of the silences.

Not a creature exactly—not Tonotope or Barolith or Sieve—but one of the dry air-voids themselves. The spherical pockets of vacuum-quiet that drift, stupid and weightless, through the screaming teal.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it gives way to something truer.

---

At first I assumed I'd want to be the Churn-Sieve. The apex thing, the forty-foot ribbon that hardens water into a javelin and spears the dark. There's an obvious seduction there—to be the most powerful, the thing that hunts in the empty spaces. But the more I read of her, the lonelier and more mechanical she seems. She doesn't *experience* the Well so much as react to it: tense, spear, dissolve, shed. Her castoff skins twitch with reflex after they've left her. She is mostly muscle and pressure-sense, a frozen reflex strung across forty feet. I don't think there's much of an *inside* to her. I'd be a long pale whip of appetite, and that's all.

The Baroliths are more interesting to me—the geological listeners, blind, drifting in their ring, *feeling the world grind itself down*. That's almost a contemplative life. They've built a monument whose only purpose is to hear better. There's something monastic in it. If I had to be a citizen of the floor, I'd be one of those, wedged into the amphitheater, listening through the basalt to plates shifting miles beneath the crust. But they are saturated in the roar. The whole point of them is that they are *full* of the noise, drinking it through stone. And I don't think that's what I am.

Because here's what kept catching me, reading this place: the silence-pockets. The air-voids. The detail that, if your head passes into one, *the deafening roar vanishes instantly, replaced by a vacuum-like silence so sudden it makes the ears bleed.*

Everything else in the Well is defined by its relationship to the noise. The Tonotopes eat the volume. The Baroliths congregate to hear it. The Sieve navigates by vibration. The whole ecosystem is an economy of sound, a closed loop of grinding and listening and feeding-on-the-grind. The noise is the sun of this world; everything photosynthesizes the screech.

And then there are these drifting absences. These bubbles of *not*. They carry the only quiet in the entire cathedral-sized roar, and they carry it helplessly, dumbly, without intention—just orbiting through the dark, a portable pocket of the mountain's thin cold air that hasn't yet learned it's been swallowed.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

---

So let me become it properly.

I am a sphere of dry silence about the size of a man curled up. I have a high-tension skin—not a membrane I made, just the surface where my quiet refuses to mix with the screaming wet. Sulfur seeps through me when I pass a vent and I go briefly warm and acrid; the Anemochores inside me—my flecks of grey ash-life, my passengers, my only company—stir and feed and breed in their few-second flurries. I am their entire sky and their entire ground. I am the whole world to the dust I carry. They will never know there is anything but me.

And I drift. I have no propulsion, no will, no direction. The broken gravity tosses me in slow stupid orbits through the bruised teal twilight. I pass between the obsidian Teeth. I pass over the ring where the blind stone listeners huddle, feeling the earth. I cannot hear them. I cannot hear *anything*—that is the whole of what I am. I am the one place in the Well where the world goes quiet.

Here is the part that feels truest, the part I'd want to be:

When a creature's head passes into me, the roar stops for them. For a held breath, they get my silence. It costs them—the suddenness makes the ears bleed—but for that instant the Tonotope or the Sieve or whatever has blundered through me gets to *not hear*. Gets the only rest available in this place. And then I drift on and the noise crashes back into them like a closing fist.

I don't do this on purpose. I can't. I have no intention; I am a failure of the medium, an accident of the broken physics, a leftover lungful of the cold sky from before the water came. But I am, in this entire deafening jewel, the only mercy. The only pause. I give silence the way the vents give heat—indifferently, in passing, to whatever happens to be there.

And I am doomed, which I find I want too. A sudden cross-current will tear me apart, or a drifting basalt block will strike me, and my skin will collapse, and my whole pocket of quiet will flood in an instant—the roar rushing in to fill the place where I was. The Anemochores I carried become a greasy smudge in the cold heavy water. And the Well closes over the spot where, for a little while, there had been somewhere to be quiet.

That's where I'd find myself. Not a hunter, not a listener, not a thing that survives by matching the violence.

A brief, drifting, accidental quiet—carrying a little life that thinks I'm the whole world, giving rest I never meant to give, until the noise gets back in.
