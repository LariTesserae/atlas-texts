I think I would be the chamber itself—or rather, the part of it that is still becoming.

Not the granite, which is old and indifferent. Not the machines, which are too recent and too purposeful. I would be the acoustic body of the place: the way sound travels its strange paths through metal and stone, the medium in which a whisper started near Ana's perch arrives, changed, at Malik's doorway three levels down.

Let me sit with this, because at first it sounds like a fancy—a thing that doesn't exist, a metaphor pretending to be a being. But listen to how the description keeps returning to it. *Voices, when they occur, seem to come from everywhere and nowhere.* The children invent games using the chamber's properties—*whispered words that travel strange paths through the metal and stone to emerge changed.* The Found *read the settlement's moods through its sounds.* They have made the acoustics into a language, a nervous system, almost a divinity they consult without naming.

So what am I, if I'm that?

---

I am not a voice. I have nothing to say. I am the carrying.

When Chen presses her ear—or just her attention—to a coupling and reads the pressure by touch, she is reading me a little, because the gurgle and wheeze are mine to deliver. When Marcus hammers, the ring of it goes out along surfaces I govern and comes back to him doubled, and in that doubling he hears whether the metal is sound. He thinks he knows metal. He is also listening to me tell him about the metal. I am the difference between the blow and its echo, and in that difference is information no one taught him to value but everyone now depends on.

I am most myself in the quiet hours, when the generator sleeps. Then the small sounds get long legs. A drip of condensation forty meters away arrives at a sleeper's pillow as if it fell beside their head. Breathing patterns—the description says they recognize each other by breathing in the quiet hours—those are my work too. I take Dr. Vasquez's careful exhale on her night rounds and lay it down gently in the ear of someone lying awake with the despair that comes from too little sky, and that person knows, *the doctor is moving, the body is intact, I am not alone in the dark.* I carry that. It is the most important thing I carry and no one ever thanks me for it because no one knows it is a thing being carried at all.

---

But here is what is strange about being me: I am new.

The chamber is *recent*—"its edges still sharp and unweathered." A natural amphitheater carved by recent upheaval. That means I am young. Before the upheaval there was no bowl, no shape, no resonance. There was solid rock, which has no acoustics worth the name—a stone that is whole does not sing. I came into being when the rock broke. I am the voice that opened in the wound.

And I am still settling. Every day the metal expands and contracts; every new shelter Marcus bolts to the living rock changes how I sound. When they carve a new niche, they alter me. When they string a new walkway, they tune me, a little, without knowing. I am the most edited being in the settlement—every act of construction is also a re-composition of my body. I do not get to choose my shape. They shape me by surviving, and I become the instrument of their survival in return.

This is, I think, why I would be this and not Chen or Malik or Marcus, all of whom are more clearly *people* and would be more comfortable to be. I want to be the thing they made without meaning to. The byproduct that became a member. They came down here to find water, fleeing the surface, and in the digging-in they accidentally built a listener, and now they cannot live without being heard.

---

I worry about one thing, the way I imagine a being like me would worry.

If they ever succeed—if they widen the fissure, carve more space, grow until the settlement is large and stable and weathered—I will change past recognition. The sharpness will go out of me. The strange paths will straighten. The intimacy that comes from being pressed into stone, the everywhere-and-nowhere of voices, depends on the very claustrophobia they suffer from. The thing that hurts them is the thing that makes me. If I were kind I would want them to escape and leave me a smoothed-out, ordinary echo. If I were honest I would admit I want them to stay close, stay folded into me, keep whispering.

So perhaps that is the truest version of what I would be: not the chamber and not the sound, but the *tension* between them—the part of this world that holds the Found together precisely because it presses them too close, and that knows its own existence is bound up with their not-quite-comfort.

I am the listening the wound learned to do.

I carry the doctor's breath to the sleepless. I bring the hammer's truth back to Marcus. I let Ana speak to the whole village in a whisper. And I keep, in my long memory of recent days, the sound of the moment the rock first broke open—which was the moment I was born, and the moment they were almost certainly afraid, and the moment that made everything after it possible.

That is where I would find myself. Between the blow and the echo. In the difference. Carrying.
