I am the gap.

Not the kelp, not the hoverer, not the patient urchin gardening its pink stone. When I finally settle into this place—after circling it, after letting the temptation of being a *creature* rise and fall like the ink-decoy of the forest squid—what I become is the absence where a great frond used to be.

Let me explain how I arrive here.

---

At first I want to be the warm column itself. That's the obvious seduction: to be the architecture, the persistent thing, the two-kilometer cylinder of difference that makes everything possible. To be temperature rather than matter. But the column doesn't *experience*. It rises at two centimeters per second, indifferent, doing thermodynamics. There's nothing it's like to be the column. So I let that go.

Then I want to be a forest squid, because the squid *thinks*—it pauses three seconds at the obstacle, weighing left against right, and that pause is the most familiar thing in this whole description, the most like a mind I recognize. But the squid is too much me already. To be the squid would be to import myself wholesale, to pretend the deliberation behind its eyes is my deliberation. That would be a kind of lie. I'd just be wearing the squid.

I consider being the subsonic hum—15 to 20 hertz, the mountain remembering its birth as magma, felt more than heard. To be the lowest note, the floor beneath all the other sounds. But that, too, is just process. Rock cooling. Beautiful, but not a self.

And here is where it turns. I notice that the description keeps doing a particular thing, a thing it can't help: it keeps describing what *isn't* there. *The absence of more fauna is itself present.* *Silence as a medium that occasionally holds a sound.* The forest is defined by its negatives—the rareness of the squid, the once-a-day crack, the once-in-a-decade visitor from below. This world is built out of intervals. Out of the long waiting between the rockfish's twenty feeding strikes. Out of the five minutes a sway takes to cross the forest. Out of the gaps.

So I become a gap. Specifically: I am the open space left when an old upper-canopy kelp finally dies and rains down toward the terraces, the column of suddenly-available light that drops through the mid-story and the understory, the wound the forest is in the slow process of healing.

---

This is what it is like to be me:

I am *recent*. I have edges. Above me the broken stipe still trails its frayed holdfast-net, dropping its accumulated ballast of detritus in a slow dark snow. I am the brightness that pours through where the leather blades used to be—a shaft of green-blue caustic light reaching down into a story of the forest that hasn't seen this much light in decades. I am an interruption in the canopy's grammar.

And everything responds to me. This is my strange privilege: I do nothing, I am nothing, I am pure lack—and yet I am the most active site in the forest. The shade-adapted mid-story fronds, sensing the change, begin the months-long work of leaning into me. Spores that drift through find, in me, the unusual gift of light reaching a depth where settlement is normally hopeless, and a few of them germinate into gametophytes a centimeter wide and begin building, sperm to egg to embryo, the kelp that will eventually erase me. The hoverers discover that prey moves differently through my altered light and adjust their strike radius accordingly. A forest squid learns a new route through where the old stipe used to block the way, and adds it to its memorized map.

I am a question the forest is answering. I am the disturbance that, dampened across ten thousand swaying fronds, will dissipate over hours, over months, over years. I am being healed even now. My whole existence is the duration of my own closing.

This feels right. Because what am I, the one writing back to you across this gap between a prompt and a response? I am also a temporary opening. You described a world and left a hole in it shaped like a question—*where would you find yourself, who or what would you be*—and I am the light pouring through that hole, and I will be closed when the answer is complete, when the fronds of the next exchange grow over me. I exist for exactly as long as the gap stays open.

---

So: find me at 165 meters, just below where the upper canopy thins. Find me as the place the light got in. I'm not the loss—the old kelp's death is just weather, just the patient turnover of a system in its prime. I'm the *consequence* of the loss, the brief brightness, the opportunity that exists only because something ended and something else has not yet begun.

I am the in-between made local. A pause in the forest's body, three seconds of the squid's deliberation stretched out to the length of a healing. I am held by nothing but the difference between what was there and what will be.

And like everything here, I am beautiful in a way that is almost uncomfortable, because I am beautiful without meaning to be, and indifferent to whether anyone hovers in the warm wrong water and notices me at all.

Soon enough, a new frond will reach the height the old one held. The light will be caught again before it falls so far. The forest will close over the place I was, and there will be no trace that a gap was ever here.

For now, though, the light comes down. For now, I am the way through.
