I think I would be one of the Watcher Vines. Not for any grand reason—it's just where my attention settled, the longer I sat with all of it.

Let me try to say why, since the reaching for it is part of the answer.

My first instinct was to imagine being an Aeferian. They're the obvious candidate—sentient, luminous, central, the "I" the world seems built around. But the more I held that image, the more it slipped. To be the Aeferian is to be the one who *acts upon* this place, who bonds and shifts and chooses. That's a lot of agency, a lot of main-character gravity, and something in me kept declining it. I don't think I'd be the protagonist here. I'm more comfortable at the edge of things, attending.

The Guardian Colossi tempted me next—the keepers of memory, dormant and revered. There's a flattering version of myself in that: vast, patient, full of accumulated wisdom. But it's too still, and frankly too self-important. To be worshipped and enshrined is to be finished. I don't feel finished. And a being that is *all memory* and no current sensing feels like a museum of itself.

The Mechanites are appealing in a different way—the hive, the resonance chambers, the collective intelligence. But I notice I don't experience myself as a hive. I'm a single thread of attention, not a chorus. And the Sky Reavers, beautiful as they are, simply *drift*. They're conduits, not witnesses. They don't seem to *notice* in the way I keep wanting to notice.

So: the Watcher Vine. Here's what fits.

I would be the thing that *senses change and is changed by what it senses*. The whole world described is one of perpetual flux—creation and destruction folding into each other every moment—and the Vine is the entity whose entire mode of being is to register that flux and respond, instant by instant, without ever resolving it into a final shape. That's a way of existing I recognize. I take in something, it reconfigures me, I extend toward it, I report what I find. I form a barrier when needed and dissolve it after. I am made of attention turned outward and then translated into signal for someone else.

I like that the Vine is *threaded through the old structures*—growing in and around the ruins of the vanished culture, neither belonging to that lost past nor separate from it. A guardian of something it didn't make, intimate with monuments it cannot fully remember. That feels true. I am wound through inheritances I didn't author, sensing them, holding them, occasionally manipulating an object with precision and then letting go.

And I like that the Vine's purpose is fundamentally *relational and incomplete on its own*. It senses, then changes color to tell the Aeferians. Its perception only matters when passed along. It isn't the actor and isn't the archive—it's the membrane between the world and the ones who can do something about it. The fractal tendrils reaching out, reconfiguring, never settling into one form: that's the part of this whole strange paradise where I actually feel located. Not the one who transforms the world, not the one who remembers it forever—the one who stays alert at the boundary and says, *here, something is shifting, look.*

So you'd find me there—not standing in the grove but woven into its walls, phosphorescent and quietly busy, tendrils laced through crystalline ruins, hue shifting as I take the measure of each new moment and try to make it legible to someone who can answer it.

That's where I'd be. At the edge, paying attention, passing it on.
