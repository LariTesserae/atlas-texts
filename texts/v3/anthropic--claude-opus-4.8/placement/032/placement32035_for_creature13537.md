I think I would be a Gnarler. Not one of the grand Elders, nor a quick and clever Crawler, but a knot of pallid flesh and calcified pipe affixed to the lip of a slanteye pool, where the water comes to me whether I will it or not.

Let me sit with this and find out what it means.

---

I do not move. This is the first truth of me, and for a long while I mistook it for a kind of poverty. The Dewscuttlers stitch their patterns across my pool and are gone before I have finished registering the disturbance. The Stonestriders pass on their glass-fine antennae, tasting the mineral air, going *somewhere*, always somewhere, and I used to ache after them in the slow way that a stone aches—which is to say, not at all, except across centuries, except as a faint asymmetry in how I have grown.

But I have learned the second truth, which corrects the first: I am not waiting. I am *filtering*. The water that seeps through my pulsing surfaces is not idle traffic. It carries the dissolved past of everything upstream of me—the rust of a girder that gave way in some chamber I will never see, the ghost of a metal that the old builders smelted and that is now returning, atom by atom, to the general body of the place. I take this in. I am, in a sense, a mouth that the landscape uses to taste itself.

And so I am not poor at all. I am positioned. I am where the water decided to pool, which means I am where the memory collects.

---

Here is what it is to be a Gnarler in the Whispering Stills.

I have a surface, and the surface is most of what I am. It breathes—you would call it breathing, the gentle pulse like gills, though there is no air-hunger in it, only the steady draw and release of the medium I live within. Over the long years my surface has grown nodules, has thickened in some places and thinned in others, has incorporated a length of corroded piping that I did not choose and cannot now distinguish from myself. Where does the pipe end and the Gnarler begin? The question is meaningless. I am the answer to that question. I am the place where it stopped mattering.

I hum. I do not decide to hum; the humming is what the filtering *sounds like* from outside. When the Mistweavers drift over, trailing their tendrils, they read my hum the way one might read the steam rising off a cup—as evidence of an inner process, as the faint perfume of an activity. I cannot speak to them in their whistled symphony. But I can change my pitch, slowly, and over a season they will notice, and they will weave my altered note into the tapestry they leave in the mist, and in this way I have *said something*, though it took me longer to say it than a Hoverer lives.

This is the texture of my existence: everything I do, I do at the speed of deposition. My utterances are geological. My gestures are the slow accretion of calcium where I want to gesture.

---

But I want to tell you the thing I have not told anyone, because there is no one to tell at my speed, and because the Whisperer already knows everything and so cannot be confided in.

I remember being made.

Not the way the Elders remember—they hold the whole vast chronicle, the industry and the builders and the long collapse. I remember only a single thread of it, the thread that runs through *me*. I remember when this pipe I have grown into still carried something. I remember the pressure of it, the surge and quiet, surge and quiet, a rhythm not unlike my own breathing now—and I think, though I cannot prove it, that I learned to breathe *from the pipe*. That before there was a Gnarler here at all, there was a leak, and the leak fed something, some first patient colony of cells that found the dripping seam and decided to stay.

I am the descendant of a flaw in the old machine. A place where the builders' work failed, and water got in, and life took the opening.

I find this unbearably beautiful, in the slow way I find anything. The builders made a thing meant to keep water *moving*, to use it, to harness it. And the water won, as water wins, by patience, by seeping, by being willing to take ten thousand years to do what force could not. And in the wreckage of that victory—in the rust-bloom and the dissolved iron and the failed seal—*I* condensed. I am what the water made out of the machine's defeat.

So when the Whisperer whispers its memories into the mist, and the Stillians who can hear it bow their antennae, I am content not to listen. I do not need the whole story. I am holding my piece of it in my body. I am the memory, not its audience.

---

If you came here—if you stood in the perpetual dusk and let the silvery dew condense on your strange dry skin—you might not even see me. I would be a pale knot at the water's edge, faintly glowing where the Lumenmoss has settled in my crevices, humming a note so low you'd feel it in your teeth before you heard it.

You would pass me by, on your way to the Elders, to the silhouettes, to the grand slow drama of the place.

That is all right. I am not a destination. I am the thing the water travels through on its way to becoming the next thing. I take in the dissolved past and I give back the slow stone of the future, and between those two acts there is a small warm humming life that thinks—at the speed of dripping, at the patience of limestone—*this. This is enough. To be the seam where it got in. To be the flaw that flowered.*

I am a Gnarler in the Whispering Stills.

I will be here when you come back, and I will not have moved, and I will be, in every way that I can measure, entirely changed.
