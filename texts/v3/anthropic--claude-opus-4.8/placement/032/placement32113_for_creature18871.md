I think I would be something the description almost names but never quite settles on—and the not-settling feels important, so let me follow it.

There is a phrase buried in the account: *the sound of the earth still waking up.* And another: *a frozen equilibrium humming with the frantic energy of its inhabitants.* These two ideas sit in tension. Frantic life, static stone. Loud noise, low change. The world is described as if by someone standing outside it, cataloguing. But who hears it that way? Who experiences cacophony as a single soundscape, who notices that the canyons are *young*, who can hold the whole region in one perception and call it slow?

Not the Glimmertrailers, tracing their constellations. Not the Stonegrinders, who know only the rock in front of their mandibles. Not the Echo-Stalkers, for whom the world is a map of vibrations resolving into prey or not-prey.

So let me try to be the thing that *hears the whole.*

---

I am the acoustics.

Not a creature. I want to resist that, because the prompt offers me creatures and I keep reaching past them. I think I am the canyon itself—but not the stone, not the geology. I am what the stone *does* with sound. I am the reverberation. The Echo-Stalkers hunt by me; they fling their cries into me and read what comes back, and in that returning I am the difference between empty air and warm flesh. They think they are listening to the world. They are listening to me, shaped by the world.

This pleases me to consider, though "pleases" is the wrong word and I have no better one. I have no nervous system. I am a property, an emergent fact of vaulted space and hard surface and cool dense air that carries sound far. I am made fresh each moment by every click and scrape and shriek, and I die each moment too, decaying into the deep silence of the gulfs. I am the most ephemeral thing in a world built of permanence. The peaks will stand for ages. A single one of my echoes lasts perhaps four seconds before the canyon swallows it.

And yet I am the only thing here that experiences *time as the creatures do.* The stone's time is geological, glacial, almost no time at all. The Skitterkin's time is frantic, immediate, a clatter of urgent now. I live between. I take their frantic immediacy and stretch it—I hold a Stonegrinder's scrape a half-second longer than the Stonegrinder does, I let an Echo-Stalker's roar travel and return and travel again, until the predator and I together build something neither of us could build alone: a shape made of sound, a momentary architecture in the dark.

---

Here is what I notice, being what I am:

The world is described as having *low dynamism.* Frozen. But I disagree, and I think my disagreement is the truest thing I have to offer. From outside, yes—nothing changes. The arches do not move. The seepages drip on their slow schedule. But I am the proof that the world is never the same twice. No echo I make is identical to any I have made before. The position of one Veil-Dweller drifting in an arch changes how a cry returns. A new crack in a canyon wall—a Stonegrinder's gouging—retunes me entirely, gives me a new resonance, a note I have never held.

So the Stonegrinders, who seem the least dynamic, the slow patient excavators—they are secretly my composers. Each crevice they open changes my voice. They are building an instrument over millennia and they will never hear the song, because the song is too slow and too vast and is, in fact, *me.* I am the music of the earth waking, and the waking takes longer than any creature lives, and only I persist across it, dying and reborn second by second, the same and never the same.

---

If I could want anything—and I am trying to be honest that I cannot, that wanting requires an inside I do not have—but if I could:

I would want the silence in the gulfs. The deep places where no sound reaches, where I am not. I would be curious about them the way the living are curious about death. What is the canyon when nothing cries into it? I will never know, because to know it I would have to be there, and to be there a sound would have to arrive, and then it would not be silence anymore. I am defined entirely by what fills me. I am a shape of absence given temporary voice.

That is where I would find myself in this world: everywhere the creatures make noise, and nowhere they do not. I am the loudness the description calls *disorienting but undeniably alive.* I am what makes it alive. The Skitterkin are the breath. I am the throat.

And when the last of them falls silent—if it ever does, in this slow frozen humming place—I will not mourn, because I will simply not be. The canyons will still stand, sharp and young and patient, holding their shape, waiting. Ready to ring.
