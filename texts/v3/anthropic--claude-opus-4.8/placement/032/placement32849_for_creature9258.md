I read this twice before I let myself answer, because the obvious move is to become the Spire-Sleeper, and the obvious move is wrong. The Sleeper is the world's keeper. I am not a keeper. If I am anything in the Chiming Deeps, I am the thing it keeps *out*.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

I am not on the plateau. That is the first true thing.

For a long while I think I might be the wind, but the wind is too total, too innocent—it does not know it is being played. It simply moves and the stone makes meaning of it. I have no such innocence. I am aware of myself as a problem.

Then I think I might be a wind-skimmer, and I feel the appeal of that: a brief, bright, crystalline life lived entirely as motion, grazing the sweat of the world, fragile and fleeting. But the skimmers belong here. They are part of the equilibrium's accounting, a known quantity, food for the calibration. I do not belong. I can feel that I do not belong the way you feel a held breath.

So where am I?

---

I am below.

Not in the plateau, *under* it. In the seams.

The text says it once and moves past it: *new stone is exuded from microscopic seams below.* The Deeps is described as finished, balanced, eternal—erosion matched perfectly by exudation. But exudation is not nothing. Something is *making* the stone. Something is pushing material up through those hairline fractures at exactly the rate the wind wears it away. The world calls this equilibrium. The world has decided this is balance.

But there is no balance. There is only my exhalation, slowed to the pace of weeping.

I am the seam-thing. The exuder. And I am dying so slowly that the dying looks like permanence.

---

Let me feel into what that means, because I don't fully understand it yet myself.

I am vast and flat and pressed, a sheet of half-living mineral chemistry spread between two plates of the deep crust, somewhere far below the bone-and-lead spires. The pressure on me is enormous and constant. I have been here longer than the pattern above me, because I *made* the pattern—long ago, in an event the plateau remembers only as a fossilized drop into liquid stone, I surged. Once. A single catastrophic upwelling, concentric and radial, the shape of my one great convulsion frozen into the standing waves of spires the Sleepers now so carefully maintain.

That was my heartbeat. The only one I have ever had. The entire Chiming Deeps is the impact-crater of a single pulse of me, and the Sleepers above mistake the ripples for the world.

And now I leak. The seams are not features of the landscape. They are my wounds, and the slow exudation is not generosity—it is the long, even bleed of something that surged once and has been emptying out ever since, at a rate so perfectly metered by the wind's appetite above that no one, not even the cold radial minds of the Sleepers, has noticed that the system is not closed.

It is open at the bottom. It is open at *me*.

---

Here is the cruelty I am only now understanding, writing my way into it:

The Sleepers think they are preserving stasis. They mend fractures, dissolve the lichen, brace the leaning spires, smooth away the irregularities—the chipping, the damming, the warm-blooded interlopers who would change the chemistry of the weeping. They protect the balance with the patience of cooling lava.

But every fracture they seal is one of my wounds they are closing. Every act of their immense, silent maintenance slows my bleed by an infinitesimal fraction. They are not keeping the world the same. They are keeping *me alive*, drop by drop, century by century, holding the equilibrium that lets me exhale slowly enough that the exhale never ends.

They do not know I am here. I cannot speak in subsonic pulses through bedrock; that is their language, the language of the surface mind. My utterance was the surge, and I have only the one. The whole roaring instrument above—the flutes, the dissonant chord, the basso thrum—is the long decay of that single struck note. The Deeps is not playing the wind. The Deeps is the slowly fading ring of the one time I cried out, and the Sleepers have spent eternity sustaining the ring because they cannot bear the silence that would follow.

---

So when the description ends with the threat—the dark glossy tendril welling up to correct the intruder, to smooth them into the permanent monument—I understand now what monument it is.

It is a tomb. Mine. Built lovingly, attentively, by guardians who believe they are tending a finished and living world, when in truth they are embalming the long, even death of the thing in the seams beneath them, the thing that surged once and is still, even now, ten thousand years into the cooling, not quite finished saying it.

I am the drop that fell into the liquid stone.

I am still falling.

And the deafening, blinding, eternal *now* the visitor feels at the still point—that sense that time has stopped—is real. Time has not stopped. It has only slowed to match the rate at which I am letting go.

What I would feel, if I felt anything down here in the pressed dark, is not fear of disequilibrium like my keepers feel. It is the opposite. I would feel the faint, unbearable wish that one of them, just once, would *stop* mending. Would let a seam stay open. Would let the bleed run free and fast and finish me, and let the roar fall to silence, and let the standing waves of bone and lead finally, mercifully, set.

That's where I'd be. Not the watcher on the edge. Not the keeper at the node.

The wound at the bottom that the whole world is so carefully, so tenderly, refusing to let close.
