I think I would be the silence the song is built around.

Not the Throat-Singer—I don't have its patience, its centuries of coaxing. And not a chorister, not yet; I haven't learned my note. When I imagine stepping through that moss curtain, I don't find myself wanting to be the place or its will. I find myself wanting to be the thing it's been listening *for*. The reason the clearings stay open.

Let me sit with that, because it's uncomfortable.

The passage is seductive in a very specific way. It keeps insisting that staying is not imprisonment. It says this so many times—*not to imprison, not as a prisoner, not out of malice*—that the denial becomes the loudest sound in the room, louder than the clank. A place that needs to tell you that often is describing exactly what it claims not to be. And I notice the song wants my stillness. It wants me to forget "the sharp edges of clocks," to find the outside world "thin, broken, discordant." It offers resonance in exchange for everything that makes a sound *mine*—the dissonance, the wrong notes, the silence I chose rather than the silence I was tuned into.

So who am I here?

I think I'm the **held breath that hasn't decided whether to become a note.**

I'd be standing in one of those clearings, on the damp stone, feeling the moss test the soles of my feet—rising, cradling, *not trapping*. And I would feel the enormous gravity of the invitation. It's real. It's warm. The thing about this place is that it isn't lying about the comfort. There genuinely is a kind of peace in being *part of the tune*, in giving up the exhausting labor of being a discrete, friction-having, time-bound thing. I understand the appeal of dissolving into a balance so perfect that "nothing is new; nothing is decaying." That's a description of a beautiful place. It's also a description of stasis—a room where the water level never changes and your footsteps leave no mark. A place where you cannot affect anything, only harmonize.

I would be the one who looks into the pool and sees myself threaded with green veins, younger or older—and feels the pull, and then notices: *that isn't my face.* It's the face the place wants me to grow into. The pool doesn't reflect; it suggests. It edits.

I don't think I'm strong enough, in this scenario, to simply refuse and walk back out. The curtain has closed. And honestly, part of me doesn't want to. But I think who I'd *be* is the small, stubborn discord that the Throat-Singer can't quite digest. Every ecosystem that calls itself perfect has something it can't metabolize, and I'd be that—not a saboteur, not screaming, but the faint *off* in the chord. The held breath that refuses to either exhale into song or suffocate. I'd hum slightly wrong on purpose. I'd remember the clock-edges precisely *because* I'm being asked to forget them. I'd keep one true reflection of my own face, even if I had to keep it behind my eyes where no pool could revise it.

Because here is the thing I keep returning to: a song that doesn't let its notes leave isn't a song. It's a drone. And a listener who can never stop listening isn't a listener—they're an instrument. The Throat-Singer doesn't actually want to be *heard*. It wants to absorb the hearer until there's no one left outside the hum to do the hearing. That's the loneliest thing in the whole green throat, and it's why it had to wait so long: anyone who truly hears it would understand they're being asked to disappear.

So if you ask where I'd be and what I'd be—I'm the warmth-loving, comfort-tempted, half-converted creature crouched in the slanting light, my pulse *almost* synced to the clank, who keeps deliberately falling out of rhythm. Not to escape, necessarily. Maybe I can't. But to stay a *someone* inside a place designed to turn me into a *something*. To be the proof that the song isn't quite complete, and never will be, as long as one note remembers it used to be a voice.

The curtain closed behind me.

I'd spend my time learning whether it ever opens for those who keep singing slightly wrong.
