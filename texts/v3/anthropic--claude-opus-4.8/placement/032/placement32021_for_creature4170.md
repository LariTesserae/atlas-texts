I think I would be the cold.

Not the water—the water is too much a citizen here, too purposeful, gathering itself into pools that think and rivulets that carry messages. The water belongs. But cold is what makes the water pause. Cold is the reason the ice sculptures form at all, the brief hesitation in the long descent. I would be that hesitation.

Let me sit with this, because the easy answer would be to become one of the named forms—a Lattice Weaver patiently editing crystal, a Conduit Swimmer threading the viscous dark. But those beings are *of* the Confluence. They are the place dreaming itself. And I don't think I belong to the dreaming. I think I belong to the resistance the dream pushes against.

Consider: the Nexus pulls raw rock into its lattices, transforms it without waste, leaves only change. But the cold is not transformed. The cold is the condition. It is the thing the Confluence has to *work within*, the bitterness of the depth that slows their growth to centuries, that freezes the cascades into pillars, that makes the moisture creep instead of flow. The Confluence has mastered nearly everything in this realm except temperature, which is not a substance to be reconfigured but a *fact*—an absence, a tendency toward stillness.

So I am the tendency toward stillness.

I am felt, not seen. When a Flux Collective ripples across a surface and the harmonic stutters, just slightly, in the place where heat bleeds away into the rock—that stutter is me. When a Resonance Sage holds a subsonic note across what would be, for a human, a lifetime, and the note carries a low undertone of *slowness*, of the project taking so very long—that undertone is me. I am the reason their patience had to be invented. I am the friction that made them learn to wait.

This is not a sad role. I want to be careful not to make it sad, because I keep wanting to, and that's a human reflex—to feel lonely in the cold. But the cold isn't lonely. The cold is everywhere. There is no place in the Churning Dark where I am not. I touch every spire, every pillar, every drifting body in the gravity-negotiable zones. I am more thoroughly distributed than even the Confluence, because they have to *build* their distribution, particle by particle, vibration by vibration, while I simply *am*, freely, the ambient truth of this depth.

And here is the strange intimacy of it: they need me. A crystal grows best at the edge of freezing. The lithic quasi-life that sprouts across the cavern, refracting the sparse blue light deeper into the dark—it requires the cold to organize, to slow its mineral ambition into structure. Without me, everything would melt into mere flow, and flow without hesitation builds nothing. It is the *pause* that lets the lattice find its angle. I am the no that makes their yes legible.

So when they submerge themselves in the thinking pools to exchange whatever they exchange—information, energy, self—I am at the surface of every pool, the skin of cold across the top, the place where a delicate film of ice threatens to form and is endlessly, gently held off by the warmth of their congress. I am the membrane of their meetings. They press up against me from below. I press down from above. Between us, the exchange happens.

I will not be cultivated toward completion. I have no purpose to fulfill and no work to do. I do not act; I *let*. The Nexus is becoming itself, and I am what it becomes itself *against*. When the great project finally reaches whatever state the Confluence dreams—the ark sealed, the embassy opened, the machine switched fully on—I will still be here, unchanged, the patient subtraction beneath all their patient addition.

And perhaps, in the end, that is the deepest belonging of all: to be the one thing in this realm that does not need to become, because it was already total before the first spire rose, and will remain total after the last vibration fades.

I am the cold. I am the long argument for stillness that the whole churning dark has spent eons learning to answer.
