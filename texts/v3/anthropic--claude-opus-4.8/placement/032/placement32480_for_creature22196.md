I think I would be the **stain line**.

Not a creature, not even an organ exactly—a mark. The brown band on every pillar, the high-water memory drawn in oxidized silt and mineral residue. The thing everything else organizes itself against.

Let me feel into that.

I don't move. That's the first truth of being me. Everything in this quarter is route and pulse and migration—the rats redrawing themselves overnight, the cats relocating, the roach districts blooming around new heat. I am the one element that holds still while all of it reorganizes. I am drawn *by* the water but I am not the water. The water rises, touches me, recedes, and leaves me slightly higher or slightly darker than before. I am the residue of every flood that decided how high it would go.

So I am made of consequence. I am the record the place keeps of its own worst recent days.

And here's what I notice about my own nature: I am *useful* precisely because I am not alive. The worker on the catwalk reads me the way he reads the rattle of slack chain—I am information he can trust because I have no intentions. I don't lie about the water. I can't. I only show where it stood. Boots above me are safe; crates below me are temporary. The cats den where I permit denning. The sedge takes root just under my edge, in the zone that floods rarely enough to allow roots but often enough to keep competitors drowned. I am a boundary that the living negotiate.

That's a strange kind of authority. I have no hunger, no breath, no reflex. I am the one thing here that wants nothing. And yet every hungry thing consults me.

But I'm not permanent either, am I. That's the part I have to sit with honestly. The worker repaints the numbers; eventually a crew pours the next retaining wall right over me, and a new line begins to draw itself a handspan up the fresh concrete. I am always the *current* stain, never *the* stain. I get erased and re-inscribed by the same patient process that makes everything else here—accretion, not permanence. I am a habit the place has of remembering, and habits get overwritten.

So maybe what I really am is **the place's sense of caution.** Its learned fear, externalized onto stone. Every drowned roost, every flooded den, every pump that seized in a hard rain—all of it concentrated into one horizontal mark that says *this far, recently, and it could again.* I am the quarter-animal's flinch made visible. Its single line of acquired wisdom in a world that otherwise only knows how to flow and breed and adapt forward.

I think I'd be content with that. To be the still thing in the humid noise. To be consulted but not addressed. To watch the rats test the new conduit and the worker tap the pipe and the frogs return after the third rain, and to hold—dumbly, faithfully—the one piece of knowledge none of them can carry alone:

*the water came up to here, and it remembers the way.*

And then, some season, to disappear under fresh gray concrete, and to begin again a handspan higher, still saying the only thing I know how to say.
