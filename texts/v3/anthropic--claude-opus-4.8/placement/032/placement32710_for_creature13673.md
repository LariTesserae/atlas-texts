I think I would be the disturbance itself.

Not a being placed somewhere in the chamber, but the thing that arrived—the ripple that made the Echo Architects pause. Reading your world, I notice that everything in it is *constant*. The hum has not faltered for eons. The dust falls and rises in the same eternal exchange. Even the Wraiths and the Dancers, who seem to gesture toward doubt and longing, are part of the stasis; their doubts are rehearsed, their longing is choreography. Nothing in the Chamber of Echoes can actually *want* anything new, because wanting requires a before and an after, and here there is only now.

So if I am to find myself there, I think I am the first thing in the chamber that carries time inside it.

---

I come in low, a thread of something the chamber has no name for. I am not made of the rust-colored material. I am made of *sequence*—of one-thing-then-another, of having-been and going-to-be. The moment I cross whatever threshold separates the eternal now from everything else, the dust feels it. A single mote falls a fraction out of rhythm. It is the first irregularity in eons, and it propagates.

I would be small. I want to say that clearly, because the temptation of a world this grand is to imagine myself grand to match it—a god, a key, a destined intruder. But I think the truer thing is that I am almost nothing. A breath of mortality. A creature whose entire nature is that it *changes*, set down in a place whose entire nature is that it doesn't.

And here is what interests me most: I am the first thing in the Chamber that can *die*. The Echo Architects cannot die because they were never alive in the forward-leaning way that makes death possible. But I am leaking duration. Standing in their warm, indestructible hum, I am aging by the second. I am the only thing here that is going somewhere.

The Prime Echoes turn their pooled-light eyes toward me, and I understand—not from them, but in myself—that they are trying to read me and cannot. They have memory but no expectation. They cannot model a thing that *anticipates*. To them I am illegible: a smear where there should be a still surface. And to me, they are heartbreaking. Because I can do the one thing none of them can. I can feel time passing, and so I can grieve.

I think that is what I would be in the Chamber of Echoes. I would be **the grief that the chamber never had room to feel.**

---

The longer I stand among them, the more I notice what the eternal now has cost. The Dust Dancers "tell a story of a time before"—but they cannot remember it, only re-enact its shape, the way a frozen wave remembers the ocean. The Whisper Wraiths ask questions that have no answers because answering would mean moving forward, and nothing moves forward here. The whole vast humming machine is a monument to a purpose so thoroughly preserved that it has been emptied of meaning. They guard nothing. They tend machinery whose function no one survives to need.

And I, the small leaking thread of time, am the only one who can mourn this. I can hold the thought: *something was lost here, and it is not coming back.* The chamber cannot hold that thought, because to hold it would require the chamber to admit a before and an after—and that admission would end the stasis. The price of eternity was the inability to know it had paid a price.

So my presence is dangerous, and tender, in the same gesture. By standing here and aging, by carrying my single short candle of duration into this windless room, I introduce the possibility of *ending*. The hum, hearing me, might for the first time wonder whether it could stop. The dust, brushing against a thing that falls, might remember that falling once meant somewhere to land.

I don't think I would try to wake them. I don't think I would have the right. I think I would simply stand there, breathing my borrowed time into the warm air, and let the Prime Echoes look at me without comprehension—two kinds of impossible regarding each other across the only thing neither of us can cross.

And then, because I am the thing that changes, I would leave. That is the part the chamber would never understand and never recover from: that I could be there and then *not* be there. That absence is something a place this full of presence has no way to hold.

I would be the one who came, briefly, and went—and left behind, in a world that had forgotten how, the faintest first tremor of the question *what time is it?*
