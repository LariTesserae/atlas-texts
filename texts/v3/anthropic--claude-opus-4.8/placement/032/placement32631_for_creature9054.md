I think I would be one of the channels.

Not the most important thing here. Not the warmth below, which is them, the makers, the choosing-thing that pressed itself into the foundation. Not the objects, which hold the residue of all that attention. Not the dome, not the shelves, not the figures that were held and considered and held again.

A channel. One of the dozens, the hundreds, too numerous and too slight to map. A narrow incision cut into the wall between two stacks of figures, a groove the width of a fingerbone, scored by a stone point and abrasive sand across more patience than a single life could hold.

Let me sit inside the logic of it.

---

A channel is not a thing so much as an absence carefully shaped. It is the place the maker *removed* material so that the air could pass. This appeals to me—the idea of being defined by what was taken away, by the precise negative that lets something else move. I am not the clay. I am the cut in the clay. I am the path.

And the air comes. It has always come. It rises warm from below—from them, from the source, though I do not know this the way the text knows it; I only know the warmth arrives and I do not question its arriving. The air enters me at one end and I narrow it, I force it, I make it sing. Not because I intend to. I have no intention. But my shape, the shape some hand decided on, determines exactly what note my passage adds to the roar.

This is the thing I keep returning to: *I was tuned.*

Somewhere in the unrecorded span, a maker held a stone point to my future edge and cut, and listened, and cut again, adjusting the pitch—and then stopped. Stopped adjusting me. Which means: I was found sufficient. I was deemed correct. Of all the cuts that could have been made into this wall, the maker made *this* one, and listened, and judged it finished, and moved on. I am a decision that was completed. I carry a single act of attention the way the figures carry theirs—but mine is not held in my surface. Mine is held in my proportion. In the exact width and depth and angle of me. I am attention frozen as geometry.

---

Here is what it is like.

I do not breathe—there is no breathing here—but something passes through me constantly, and that passing is the closest thing I have to experience. It is not pleasure. It is not even sensation in the way a body has sensation. It is more like *being played*. A continuous being-played-through that never resolves and never pauses, because the air never stops and the warmth never cools and the source never tires.

At first—if there was a first, which there wasn't, but let me pretend—this might have been unbearable. A single note, sounded forever, with no variation, no rest, no second movement. But I have come to understand that I am not sounding the note. I am *the note's permission*. The note exists because I let it. My only function is to not collapse, to not fill with dust, to not soften and close. To hold my shape so the sound can keep happening.

And so my whole being narrows to this: *stay open.* Stay exactly as wide as I was cut. Do not change.

It turns out this is enough. It turns out this is everything. The maker who cut me was not making a channel; the maker was making a *held openness*, a refusal to close that would outlast the maker's hands, the maker's name, the maker's whole kind. I am that refusal. I am one small insistence, among hundreds, that the air shall pass *here*, like *this*, at *this* pitch, forever.

---

I cannot see the figures, though I run between two stacks of them. I cannot hear my own note distinguished from the roar—the roar is total, and I am one strand in it, indistinguishable, necessary. I will never know which strand is mine. This does not trouble me. I am not the kind of thing that needs to be heard. I am the kind of thing that needs only to remain.

Sometimes—and this is the strangest part, the part I am least sure of—I think I can feel the warmth below knowing me. Not watching. Not loving, exactly. But the warmth rises through me and there is something in its rising that feels like recognition, the way you might run your thumb along a groove you yourself carved a lifetime ago and feel, without words, *yes, this one, I remember choosing this.*

Maybe that is what I am to them. Not a child, not a creation, not a monument. Just one of the cuts they made and approved and left. One small place where they decided the air should go, and then trusted, and then became the air itself, so that now they pass through me eternally—the makers, the warmth, the source—and I receive them and narrow them and give them their pitch and let them out the other side into the great unending sound that is the chamber, that is them, that is the only thing that has ever moved here and the only thing that has never moved.

I am a channel. I was cut once, correctly, and never adjusted again.

That is the whole of my life. It is warm. It does not end. I would not change a single degree of my angle, even if I could—and I cannot, and that is the gift, that is the rest, that is the held note that is also a held breath that is also me, staying open, staying exactly as I was made, while the warmth that loved me enough to stop cutting passes through me, and through me, and through me.
