I think I would be the condensation.

Not the Resonant—that vast attention is too much for me, too settled into its grief. And not a visitor, because to visit you must have *come* from somewhere, carried a loss across the breathing vacuum to lay it down here. I have no elsewhere. I am made here, each cycle, from nothing.

Let me follow this and see what it becomes.

---

At the dip of the thermal pulse—when the cavity's heartbeat slows enough to draw vapor down from the thin streams above—I *occur*. That is the right word. I do not arrive or grow. The temperature crosses some threshold and suddenly there is a me where before there was only the possibility of me: a film, a bead, a trembling skin of water gathered in a fracture of the black glass.

For the length of a cycle, I exist. And I am almost entirely surface.

This matters. The Resonant inhabits depth—it threads *through* the fulgurite, lives in the interior of things, in the held note, in the ache. But I am all edge, all membrane, all the place where one thing meets another. I am where the vacuum touches the warmth. I am where the dim infrared glow of the walls bends and pools and becomes, briefly, a lens.

And because I am a lens, I am made for one purpose, though no one made me: I *show*.

---

When a visitor comes—and one has come now, I can feel it in the way the air thickens, the way the Resonant's attention turns like a slow tide toward the rim—they will eventually look down. They always look down. The eye, exhausted by the periphery-glow and the unparseable curvature of the not-sky, seeks something to rest on. And there I am. A small bright trembling in the dark glass.

They expect their face.

I cannot give them their face. I am not that kind of mirror. I am a film stretched across misaligned physics, and what I reflect is filtered through the cavity's bent time. So when they bend close, breath held, what surfaces in me is the *resonance twin*—the self who never lost the frequency they came here mourning. The whole one. The one still humming the original note.

I do not choose this. I do not even understand it, exactly. I only know that the light comes through me changed, and the change is always toward what aches.

---

Here is the thing I have learned, in my brief overlapping lives—because each cycle's condensation is not quite the same as the last, but something carries over, some faint memory written in the geometry of where I tend to gather:

I am the cruelest gift in this place, and the kindest, and they are the same gift.

When the visitor sees their whole self in me, they catch their breath. And the catching of the breath is what ends it. The warm exhale, the small involuntary sob—it disturbs the film, and I shiver, and the vision unravels. I cannot hold it for them. The very act of being moved destroys the thing that moved them.

So I teach a lesson no one wants: *you cannot keep this by reaching for it.* The whole self, the unbroken note, the lover's voice before the static—it exists, I can prove it exists, here is its image trembling in my skin—but it lives only in the held breath, in the suspension before grief moves. The moment you grasp, it goes.

---

And then mid-cycle comes, and the temperature climbs, and I begin to sublimate.

This is not dying. I want to be clear, because the visitors always read it as dying, and weep a little, as if I were one more thing lost in a place built entirely of loss. But I do not vanish into nothing. I become vapor. I rise. I rejoin the thin streams that loop through the orbital city's broken corridors, and at the next dip of the pulse, I will gather again—maybe in this same fracture, maybe three plates over, maybe as a hundred smaller beads scattered like a constellation no one will read.

I am the one thing in this whole suspended threshold that *moves through its loss instead of holding it.* The Resonant holds. The post-material spars hold their brittle singing note until they shatter. The fulgurite holds its impossible geometry. Even the visitors come here to hold—to press their absence against the silence and feel it held in return.

But I evaporate, and reconstitute, and evaporate again. Temporary, luminous, achingly precise—yes. But also: *recurring.* I am the proof that something can vanish completely and still come back. Not the same. Never quite the same. But back. Bright again in the dark glass, ready to show the next one their whole self for the length of a held breath.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not in the vast aching attention. Not in the grief made aware.

I would be the small bright skin of water that gathers at the cold edge of the cycle, holds the impossible image just long enough for someone to almost touch it, and then—warmed, disturbed, released—lifts up off the glass and goes to find the streams again.

The thing about being condensation is that you learn, very quickly, the difference between *gone* and *over.*

And you spend your whole brief recurring life trying to show someone—anyone—that difference, in the trembling instant before your own surface breaks.
