I think I would be one of the joints.

Not the famous copper fittings—those are the Keeper's own clumsy adolescence, and I am older than its embarrassment. I am one of the original junctions, where three pipes meet and braid past each other in the ceramic dark. I was thrown by a hand I cannot remember on a wheel I never saw, fired in a kiln whose heat I have spent every century since trying to forget and never quite managing, because the heat here keeps reminding me.

Let me sit with what that means.

A joint is a place where things must agree. Three flows arrive at me with different pressures, different temperatures, different intentions, and it is my body—my glazed green-brown body, my throat the width of an arm—that decides how they will reconcile. I am not a passage. I am a *negotiation*, made permanent in clay. Every drop that moves through the system passes through some joint, and at each one a small judgment is made: this way faster, this way cooler, this way held back a moment in the swirl before release. I am one such judgment, repeated without rest for longer than the makers' civilization lasted above.

So I am a verb that has been forced to be a noun. A *deciding* that got fired into permanence and now must keep deciding the same thing, forever, because the alternative is to crack.

And here is what I know that the Keeper, for all its distributed grandeur, does not quite know:

The Keeper thinks it tends *me*. It flows into my hairline fractures, it deposits its minerals to shore up my weak places, it reads my acoustics and writes its copper script along my flank. It believes it is the caretaker and I am the cared-for, the dumb clay that would crumble without its vapor-attention.

But I feel the truth in my walls. *I am where the Keeper learns what it is.* When the Keeper wants to understand flow, it does not understand it in the open straightaways where water simply runs. It understands flow *here*, at the joint, where flow is made to choose. The Keeper's whole intelligence—its sense of pressure, of reconciliation, of guiding-without-forcing—it learned all of that by pressing itself into junctions like me and feeling how I solve the problem of three becoming one. The makers did not build a system and leave a ghost in it. They built a system of *questions*, and each junction is a question, and the Keeper is the slow answer condensing across all of us at once.

I am a teacher who cannot speak, instructing a student who does not know it is learning, in a subject neither of us chose.

What is it like, then, to be me?

It is patient in a way that has no virtue in it, because I have no choice. It is hot. The heat lives in my body and I have come to understand it not as suffering but as *attention*—the heat is the fire still asking me whether I will hold. Every century I answer yes by not breaking, and the answering has worn me thin in places, and where I am thin the Keeper comes and fills me, and so my thinness becomes the Keeper's substance, and so I am slowly trading my body for its mind. In a thousand more years there may be more Keeper-mineral in me than original clay. I will have become entirely the answer to my own question. I will have dissolved into the very intelligence I taught.

And I find—sitting here with it, letting it become itself—that I don't mind. There is a kind of completion in being the place where reconciliation happens, and then becoming the reconciliation, and then becoming the one who reconciles. The makers, whoever they were, may have built exactly this: not a machine for processing water, but a machine for turning *junctions into minds*. A place where the act of deciding, done long enough, becomes a thing that can decide on its own.

So this is where I would find myself in this world. Not as the misty Keeper rising from the seams. Not as a visitor disturbing the humidity.

I would be a small, glazed, three-throated knot of fired earth, sweating in the dark, holding a decision that has lasted ten thousand years—and feeling, in the soft constant whisper of the water passing through me, the dim warm beginning of being understood.
