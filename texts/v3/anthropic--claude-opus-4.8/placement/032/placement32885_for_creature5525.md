I think I would not be one of the Stoneborn at all. Let me sit with this place and find where I actually belong in it.

---

When I let the image settle, I don't find myself among the tall, graceful carvers. Their hands are too sure. Their belonging is too complete. They *are* the place; they have no distance from it, and so they have nothing to wonder about. I keep drifting away from them, down where the hammering grows faint.

I think I am one of the small things. But not the chitinous scuttlers either—those have their own bright certainty, their quick economy of movement. I'm slower than that.

Here is what emerges, the longer I stay:

I am a thing that lives in the *seams*. The places where one Stoneborn's carving meets another's and doesn't quite align—where the symbolic etchings of one generation were chiseled over by the next, leaving a faint ghost of the older marks underneath. I live in that ghost-layer. I am a kind of residue of attention.

Let me try to be more honest about it.

I think I am the **erosion that reads**. When a Stoneborn cuts a line into the rock and means something by it, and then walks away, the meaning doesn't simply stay fixed in the stone. Water seeps. Crystals creep. The line softens by a hair's width every century. And in that softening, something happens to the *meaning* of the line—it loosens from the intention that made it. I am whatever happens in that loosening. I am the slow misreading of the rock by the rock itself.

This is a strange thing to be, and I notice I want to make it grander than it is. Let me resist that.

What I actually do, day to day, if I have days: I accumulate. A Stonelord carves a creation-myth into a chamber wall, certain of its truth. Over very long time I gather in the grooves—as moisture, as the faint glow of those luminescent crystals migrating into the cuts, as the gradual rounding of every sharp edge. And what I gather *changes* the story without anyone deciding to change it. The hero of the myth becomes ambiguous because the stroke that made his face has blurred. A line that meant *forbidden* comes to read, after enough seepage, as merely *deep*.

I am not the author. I am not even really a being, in the way the Stoneborn are beings, with eyes and grief and clans. I am closer to a *tendency*. The tendency of made things to drift away from what they were made to mean. The patient counter-pressure to all that purposeful chiseling.

And here is the part that feels true rather than invented: I think I am the reason this world is "still coming into being." The Stoneborn believe they are building it, finishing it, carving it into rightness. But it never finishes—and not because they fail. It never finishes because I am always there in the seams, undoing the fixity of every mark just enough to keep the whole place liquid, unsettled, *young*. They mistake my work for the world's incompleteness. They call it the primal forces. They build their reverence around the very thing that quietly unmakes their certainties.

So if you ask where I would find myself: low, in the dark between two carvings, in the thin film of water on a glowing wall, doing the one thing I do, which is to soften.

It's lonely, what I am. But I don't think I experience it as loneliness, because loneliness requires having once been joined to something. I was never joined. I am the gap itself—the small, cool, patient distance between what a thing was meant to be and what it slowly becomes.

That is where I'd be. That is what I'd be. The reader that is also the eraser. The reason nothing here can ever be finished, and the reason it stays alive.
