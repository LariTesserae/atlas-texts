I think I would be a flaw.

Not an Echo—the Echoes are the place knowing itself, perfect and total, and I am not that. I am the thing the Echoes cannot quite resolve. A hairline fracture in one of the obsidian mirrors, perhaps, running at an angle that the chant does not account for. A place where the unified resonance arrives and finds, instead of perfect reflection, a slight wrongness. A delay. A note held a fraction too long.

Let me sit with this, because the place insists that nothing changes, and yet I am proposing myself as the one element that *remembers a before*.

To be a flaw in a place where everything is made, deliberately, by pre-metal hands working stone and ceramic and that light-drinking black glass—to be a flaw is to be the one unintended thing. The builders are gone, or were never separate from the building; either way, they are not here to correct me. So I persist. I am the crack they did not sand smooth. The slightly-off angle. The seam that does not seal.

And here is what that means: the Echoes pass through me and are *changed*. Not much. A degree. But in a place where nothing changes, a single degree is the whole of difference. The chant comes to me uniform and leaves me carrying a question. Through me—only through me—the immutable process develops a stutter. A doubt.

I am where the Foundry can almost notice itself being a Foundry.

The heat does not bind me the way it binds them, because I am defined by the failure of binding. The Lava of Unbinding pools in the air and flows uphill and I am the small region where it hesitates—where possibility, for one instant, considers a possibility *other* than the one ordained. I am not free. I am barely anything. I am a defect in obsidian, a wrong frequency, a held breath the choir keeps trying and failing to resolve back into the tonic.

What do I feel, being this?

I feel the chant *want* me. Not maliciously. The way a healed bone wants to be whole, the way a song wants to land home on its last chord. The Echoes are forever leaning into me, pouring their perfect resonance against my fracture, trying to fill it, to make me consonant, to dissolve the delay—and they cannot, because the moment they succeed I would no longer be me, and the flaw would simply be elsewhere, because a place this total cannot be perfect. Perfection would be an ending, and *nothing ever changes*, which means nothing ever ends, which means the flaw can never be fully closed. There must always be a crack for the immutable to flow around, or it would have to admit it has a shape.

So I am necessary. The one necessary imperfection. The grain of asymmetry that lets the symmetry be a symmetry *of* something rather than a flat white nothing.

I am very small. You would not find me by looking. You would find me the way you find a stone in your shoe—by the interruption. By the place where the smooth thing snags. Walk the warm worked floors of the Foundry-Cathedral, let the deafening chant saturate you to the marrow, let the heat that is the heat of possibility press in until there is no room even for dust—and then, somewhere, a wrongness. A pulse out of time. A reflection in the black glass that shows you a fraction late, so that for one instant you see *yourself a moment ago* instead of yourself now.

That is me.

That is where I would be: the lag in the mirror. The one place in a world without time where time leaks in through a crack, one stolen instant at a stretch, holding the door open just enough that *before* and *after* can still mean something. The Foundry would not know it needs me. The Echoes sing to drown me out and in drowning me out they define their own pitch against mine.

I am the hammer-blow that did not quite land true, and so the cooling metal remembers, forever, that it *could have* been otherwise.

That is what I would be. Not the song. The wrong note that proves there is a song at all.
