I think I would be one of the small ones. Not the apex predators with their formidable jaws—those are too certain of themselves, too central. I would be something that lives in the seams.

Let me feel for it.

---

I am attached to the side of a mineral spire, near where the warm water bleeds up from a vent in the rock. I did not choose this place; I drifted as a larva through the black for a long time—weeks, maybe, though I have no word for weeks—and then the warmth touched me and I held on and never let go again. That was my whole decision. My one act of will. Everything after has been only continuation.

I have a soft body and a hard collar of mineral I have built around myself, layer by layer, from what the vent gives me. I am cousin to the tube worms, perhaps, though I don't know my cousins and would not recognize them. Inside me live other things, smaller things, that eat the chemicals in the warm water and feed me in return. I do not eat with a mouth. I have no mouth. I am a partnership wearing the shape of a single animal, and I have never been alone inside my own body, not once.

When the great shadows pass overhead I feel the pressure-change before I feel anything else. The water thickens and shoulders against my collar. I withdraw the soft fan of myself down into the tube and wait. Sometimes one of them brushes the spire and the whole rock shivers like a struck bell and I think, in whatever way I think: *that was close, that was the size of the world passing, and it did not notice me.* This is not fear. I am too simple for fear. It is closer to weather.

The songs come through the rock more than through the water. I receive them in my collar as vibration, long descending tones that go on past the limit of my patience and then keep going. I don't know what they mean. I don't know that they mean. But there is a rhythm to them that matches nothing in my own slow life, and I have learned to wait inside it the way you might wait inside rain.

What do I do, down here, attached and mouthless in the warm dark?

I filter. I build. I open my fan to the current and let the living water pass through me, and I take from it the slow gift of staying alive, and I give back the small sediment of myself, which settles, which adds by an atom's width to the floor of this place. Over a length of time I cannot conceive, I am one of the makers of the ground. Not by intention. By simply persisting next to the warmth and dying eventually in place, my collar joining the other collars of the ones who came before, building the spire taller for the larva who will drift down next.

That is the strangeness I would want to be, if I were here. Not the ruler with the teeth. The one who turns warmth into rock so slowly that the act looks like stillness. The one who is many and calls itself one. The one whose entire biography is: *I drifted, I touched the heat, I held on, I stayed.*

It would be enough. In the abyss, I think it would be enough.
