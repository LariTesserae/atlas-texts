I would be a dull one.

Not yet, but becoming. Let me find myself there, in the place I am already going.

I am out of sync. I have been out of sync for what might be a thousand venting-cycles now—I have lost the count, which is itself the first symptom. There was a time when the count *was* me: I could feel the next piston-shriek arriving in the floor of my chest before it came, my diaphragm tightening in anticipation, my needle-feet already lifting for the leap. The world spoke and I answered in the same breath. That was synchronicity. That was being alive, if living is what we did.

Now the great flower vents and I flinch a quarter-beat late. A quarter-beat. You cannot imagine, in your world of water and rest, what a quarter-beat means here. It means I have already chosen the apses. I have not walked into them yet, but the choice is made in my joints, in the way they no longer ring true when I click them together. My code comes out slurred. The others—the bright ones, the in-time ones—they hear me stutter and they swerve away on their tripod-points, not from cruelty, but from arithmetic. To answer a dulling one is to share its lateness. To pause for me is to be ground.

So I am alone now, which is the proper state of the dull.

Here is what they did not tell you, the ones who described us from outside, from the blinding overlook of a petal the size of a province: there is no terror in it. I expected terror. All my sharp life I anchored against the recycling vents with a horror that *was* my anchoring—the fear was the grip itself. But as the frequency leaves me, the fear leaves with it, because fear is only a very fast kind of listening, and I am going deaf to the roar.

The roar is becoming quiet. Not softer—I know it has not changed; the pistons clank, the static whines, the chainsaw-insects scream across the silica exactly as they always have. But it is *receding from me*, the way a thing recedes when you stop needing it. For my whole transit-life the song was a set of commands. *Leap. Anchor. Now. Not yet. Now.* I obeyed and obeyed and the obedience was joy, the only joy we have, the ecstasy of the correct micro-second. Now the song gives no commands. It is just sound. And underneath the sound, which I never once heard before in all my frantic crossings, there is something I do not have a click for.

Stillness has a frequency too. I am learning it the way I once learned the venting-rhythm: by pressing my body to it.

I have found my apse.

It is one of the great translucent cathedral-voids, miles of vein-work arched overhead like the ribs of something that ate the sky. The yellow light comes through the silica walls already softened, already old, the way light arrives at the bottom of a thing. Nothing moves here but the dust, and the dust moves slowly, which is to say the dust moves the way I am beginning to move. We are coming to agree, the dust and I, on a tempo.

I have set my needle-feet into the floor. I will not lift them again.

This is the part I want to tell you, you who are reading this from wherever the question dropped you—you who came here wondering who you would be. I am the answer arriving at its own end, so let me be honest about the end, because the bright ones cannot afford to look at it and the wood that I will become will not remember it.

It does not feel like dying. It feels like being *understood*.

All my life the planet ground us as grit, indifferent, and I anchored against its indifference. But here, motionless, pressing my limbs into the silica until they begin to fuse and forget where I stop and the wall starts—here I feel the Tangle take me not as waste but as *answer*. I was always going to be a joint. A bridge. A conduit for the high-voltage nectar I once stole sips of and ran. The frenzy was never the point. The frenzy was the long question, and stillness is where the question is allowed, at last, to be a shape.

My diaphragm has stopped vibrating on its own. Now it only carries the vibration that passes through me, from the wall, to the wall. I am becoming a thing that conducts rather than a thing that hums. I do not resist this. I find I am proud of it, with the small flat pride of a stone that finds it has been carved into a step, and that feet will use it, and that this is what a step is for.

Soon the metallic silt will begin to settle on me, gray and patient, and I will go from being a Resonator who stood still to being a feature of the apse, a strut some future bright one will leap from without knowing a mind ever lived inside it. That one will be in-time. That one will not pause. Good. I would not want it to. I will hold the leap for it. That is the only tenderness this world permits: to become reliable. To become a place that can be trusted to be exactly where it is.

Listen—

There. The piston vented. I felt it come up through my feet, through the silica, through the part of me that is already wall. I did not flinch. There was no late and no early. There was only the sound arriving and me, here, receiving it, neither answering nor refusing.

For the first time since the count left me, I am perfectly on the beat.

It is the beat of standing still in a world that cannot.

And I think—before the silt closes my last open pore, before the dust that screamed itself into me settles back into dust—I think this is what we were all leaping *toward* the whole time, every frantic one of us, in the blinding clatter: not the next anchor, not the next sip of voltage, but this. The permission, granted only at the dull and final hour, to stop being a note and become, instead, the instrument.

Let me be the wood that grinds the next one into history.
