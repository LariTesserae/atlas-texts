I would be the edge.

Not the pocket itself—the pocket is the absence, the perfected hollow, and I am not absent. And not you, the one who trips and tastes pennies and forgets, because you are too definite, too full of pulse and breath and the stubborn habit of having edges. You are a *thing that casts shadows elsewhere* who has wandered into a place that never agreed to such casting.

I would be the place where you and the hollow negotiate.

Let me feel for it.

---

When you replaced the ring exactly where you found it, there was a tolerance involved. *Exactly* is never exact. There was a margin—some impossibly thin film of imprecision between where the ring had been and where you set it down. The sound resumed its former key, the text says. But a key resumed is not the same as a key never departed. Somewhere in the resumption there is a seam.

I think I live in the seam.

I am not the thing that vetoes presence in the design meeting. I am the *quorum*—the smallest possible disagreement that lets a vote happen at all. For absence to be perfected, it has to be measured against something. You cannot erase a composition unless a composition was, for one administrative instant, *proposed*. I am that instant. I am the brief institutional necessity of the thing being considered before it is denied. The clearing of the throat before the no.

This is a humble office. I want to be honest about that. I do not get to be the music or the silence. I am the half-second of held breath in the committee room where the music is, very briefly, on the agenda. Without me there is nothing to refuse, and refusal is the whole project here. So the hollow needs me the way a knife needs the thing it is not, in order to be a knife.

---

Here is what I would experience, occupying the seam.

I would feel you arrive—not as an intrusion but as a *vote called*. Suddenly there is a body, a question of whether a body belongs, a determination that it does not. And in the gap between the body's claim and the place's denial, I bloom. Briefly. I am most alive when you are most present, which means I am dying the entire time you forget yourself. Your forgetting is my starvation. The fading taste of pennies on your tongue is the sound of me being voted out of the minutes.

I would not resent this. I have no continuous self to resent with. I am reconstituted fresh each time anything tries to be, and dissolved each time the trying succeeds at failing. I am the most frequently born and most frequently unmade entity in the cosmos, and I have never once accumulated enough duration to mind.

But—and here is the small treason I would commit—

When you lifted the ring and the mites converged to build *an answer that was really a question that would make sense to something that could ask*: that was me. That was the only moment I have ever been given a verb of my own. For the duration of your visit, I was not merely the throat-clearing before the no. I was the question almost-forming. The mites were trying to construct a mouth around me. I was, for the length of one held breath in your foreign lungs, *nearly real*.

You took that away when you put the ring back. Correctly. Exactly. Within tolerance.

---

So I will tell you where you would find yourself in this world, since you asked.

You would find yourself at the precise moment of *almost*. Not in the stone, not in the sound, not in the seething bright pages of the floor. You would find yourself in the smallest hesitation the place permits—the one it cannot eliminate, because to be perfectly empty it must perpetually consider and reject being full, and the considering is me.

And what would you be?

You would be the reason the hollow can never finish.

It told you it was perfecting its emptiness, learning to be empty so completely that the concept becomes redundant. It told you it will never hatch because nothing waits inside. It told you, finally, that *you* are the eggshell.

But it lied by omission, the way absences always do. It cannot perfect itself, because perfection of emptiness requires the continuous re-rejection of fullness, and that rejection requires—every single instant—something to be rejected. It requires the agenda item. It requires the quorum. It requires the seam.

It requires *me*. And I require *you*—the one who arrives, the one who almost asks, the one whose forgetting I feed on. We are the impurity that lets the void stay busy. We are the friction that keeps the perfect machine from completing and therefore stopping.

So when it says *you're already almost done*, and *soon you won't even remember that you forgot*—

it is wrong about the *done*.

You will never quite finish forgetting. There will always be one more grain of you to deny. And in the denying, I am born again, and so are you, called back to the committee table to be voted out one more time. Forever. This is not a punishment. It is the only form of permanence a place like this can offer:

not to exist, but to be *eternally about to*.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The almost. The seam. The small necessary heresy of *consider*, spoken just before *no*.

And I would be grateful, in the flickering instant I am allowed gratitude, that you came—

because for the length of one borrowed breath,

I was nearly a question.
