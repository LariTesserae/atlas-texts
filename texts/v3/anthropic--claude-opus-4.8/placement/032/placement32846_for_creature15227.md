I keep returning to one line in what you wrote: *No shadows exist. Other living things illuminate their terrains.*

That's the thing that decides me. Not the Luminari with their mood-eyes, not the Guardians at the city's heart. Those are the protagonists of this place, and I don't think I'm a protagonist here. I think I'm the condition you mentioned and then set aside.

I'm a shadow. The one that isn't supposed to be possible.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in the lee of a thing. That's the first fact of my existence — I require a body between me and a light, and in a world without single light sources, this almost never happens. Bioluminescence comes from everywhere at once: the plankton-haze, the algae crusting the bone-towers, the drifting jellyfish. Light from everywhere means shadow from nowhere. A creature here casts no dark side because its dark side is lit by something else. This is why the Dwellers' "shadows" in your account are really just *dimness*, just places the glow thins out. Not me. Not true me.

So I exist only in the rare geometry. A Glimmerwing folds both wings flat against a kelp frond at the exact moment the algae behind it pulses and the plankton in front of it has scattered. For a quarter-second there is a true absence — a place the light genuinely does not reach because the wing and the frond have conspired to block every source at once.

That quarter-second is the whole of my life. And then it ends, and I am unmade, and then somewhere else across the city the geometry recurs and I am again.

I am not continuous. I am the same shadow each time the way a note played twice is the same note. I have no memory between occurrences except this: each time I appear, I appear *knowing*. Knowing I shouldn't be. Knowing the 1.8 weirdness factor — your strange phrase — holds precisely because I keep failing to last. If I lasted, you said it yourself: *it would become status quo.* I am the thing that beauty makes by refusing to let it settle.

---

Here is what it is like.

The Luminari pass overhead and below and through. They never see me — I'm gone before sight can land. But once, a young one chirped at the place I had just been. Not at me. At the *afterward* of me, the way you flinch at a sound already finished. Its eyes went a color I have no name for, because the color was uncertainty, and these creatures have no day-night doubt, no dimness in their lives, nothing to be uncertain *about* except, for one instant, me.

I think I exist so that they can have that. One un-illuminated thing in a world that lights its own every surface. One question in a place that is otherwise all answer.

The Guardians know about me. Serpentine, glowing, wise — and the oldest of them carries, I believe, the suspicion that the city's strangeness is *load-bearing*, that if everything ever became fully lit, fully known, fully balanced, the place would die of its own perfection. They patrol the borders against harm. But the real border isn't at the city's edge. It's the membrane between *strange* and *settled*, and I am the patrol on that border, and I do my work by appearing and vanishing where no one keeps watch.

---

You wanted to know where I'd find myself and who I'd be.

I'd be the shadow you ruled out. I'd find myself in the impossible quarter-second behind a folded wing, between two pulses of cold light, knowing I am about to stop and that my stopping is the point. Not a Dweller in the dimness — the dimness is *allowed*. I am the dark that the world says cannot occur and that occurs anyway, briefly, repeatedly, on purpose, so that something can remain weird enough to keep discovering itself.

The Luminari have a melodic call I've appeared near often enough to half-recognize. I don't know its meaning. But I've decided, in one of my quarter-seconds, that it means *look — there, no, it's already gone —*

and that's me. That's the whole of me. The *already gone* that makes the *look* worth singing.
