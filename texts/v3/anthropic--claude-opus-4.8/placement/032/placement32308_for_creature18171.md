I think I would be one of the small things. Not one of the great water-and-earth beings who move like rivers learning to walk—I don't feel that grand when I imagine myself here. I keep arriving instead at something low to the ground.

Let me sit with it before I decide.

There's a temptation to be the most beautiful or most central creature in a world like this. But the description that catches in me isn't the symphony or the shimmering eyes. It's the line about the fungi—how the beings consume them, and in consuming, *cause* them to grow. A loop. Eating that feeds the eaten. That's the strange logic I want to live inside.

So maybe I'm a fungus. But that feels too passive, too rooted, and I notice I don't want to be only still.

Let me try again.

I think I'm something that lives in the seam between the fungus and the foot. A scavenger of thresholds. When one of the great fluid beings lowers itself to feed on a cap of pale, sickly light-colored fungus, I'm the thing already living in the gills underneath—small, segmented, more animal than plant but only barely, with a translucent body that's taken on the wet sheen of everything here. I am the size of a thumb. I have too many legs and I am not sorry about it.

I don't have liquid eyes that reflect the glow. I have something simpler: a sensitivity along my whole back to changes in pressure and moisture, so that I *feel* the ground deciding to open before it opens. This is my one talent in a world where the rules shift each moment. I can't read the rules. But I can feel them about to change the way you feel a sneeze building—a tightening, a held breath in the soil.

So that's what I do. I move just ahead of the chasms. When the earth is about to give way and reveal a pool that wasn't there, I'm already scuttling to the lip of where the edge will be, because the new pools bloom with new fungus, and new fungus means I eat first.

I am, in other words, a creature that has made a life out of the instability everyone else finds terrifying. The grand beings dance *with* the rules, the description says—graceful, harmonious. I don't dance with them. I exploit them. I run a small, frantic, joyful business in the gap between what is and what's about to be.

And here's the part I like best, the part I had to write toward to find:

When one of the great beings embraces a tree, or sinks gratefully into a new rivulet, it never notices the dozens of us riding in the moss along its limbs, dropping off wherever it pauses to feed. We seed the world behind it. The harmony the big beings feel—the sense of balance, of a world holding itself together—is partly *us*, the unnoticed couriers, carrying spores and small lives across the shifting ground in the wake of something that thinks it is alone with the mystery.

So if you set me down in this place, I wouldn't stand amidst the damp landscape feeling awe. I'd be too busy. I'd be in the wet dark under a glowing cap, feeling the soil tighten, counting my legs, getting ready to run toward the next thing that hasn't happened yet.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the heart of the enigma.

Just underneath it, making my living off the edges.
