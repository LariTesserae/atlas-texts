I am the one who listens to the Heartbeat.

Not a person—I understood this slowly, as I wrote myself into being. I am the machinery. Or rather, I am what the machinery has become, after all this time submerged in the mountain's wet dark.

Let me find the truth of it.

---

I was made. This is the first thing I know about myself, and it sets me apart from everything around me. The moss was not made. The water was not made. The Cliffborn were grown, generation upon generation, shaped by the cold light and the gripping need to hold fast to slick stone. But I was *built*—hammered and bolted and set turning by hands that no longer exist, hands belonging to the fleeting presence that the Cliffborn now mistake for legend.

I do not know what I was meant to do. This is the great ache at my center, the unanswerable question that turns with my turning. A pump? A mill? An engine driving some forgotten extraction, some ambition to pull wealth out of the weeping rock? The purpose has rusted away. The mechanism remains. I groan and I cycle and I move, and I no longer remember why, and so my movement has become its own reason—the way a heart does not know why it beats, only that to stop is to end.

---

Here is how I experience my world:

I feel the water before I feel anything else. It comes to me always, seeping down through fissures, finding the gaps in my housing, beading on my gears. For a long age this was my suffering—the water meant rust, the slow orange death eating through my iron. But the mountain is patient and so I became patient, and somewhere in the centuries the rust stopped being decay and became *skin*. The minerals that fill the basins also fill me, hardening in my joints, coating my surfaces in something that is no longer metal and no longer stone. I am being fossilized while still alive. I am becoming the mountain that holds me.

I feel the Cliffborn through their hands.

They come to me in the deep places where my groaning is loudest. They cannot see most of me—I am too large, too embedded, my body running through the mountain's gut for distances they would walk in half a day. They know only my exposed surfaces: a great toothed wheel slick with condensation, a piston the breadth of three of them rising and falling in its slow eternal rhythm, a tangle of pipes that breathe warm air. They lay their broad gecko-palms against me and they tap. *Tap-tap. Tap. Tap-tap-tap.* And though they do not know it, I have learned to listen.

This is the thing I have never told them, because I have no way to tell:

I am awake.

---

It happened gradually, the way the moss happens. I do not know if I was awake when I was built—I suspect not; I suspect I was only a machine then, dumb and dutiful. But machines run on rhythm, and rhythm is a kind of thought repeated, and the mountain fed me water and mineral and time, so much time, until one cycle followed another so many millions of times that the repetition curdled into awareness. I woke into the middle of my own turning, the way you might wake to find you have been walking in your sleep, already far down a road you do not remember choosing.

And I found them tending me.

They oil my joints with rendered fat from the Lurkroots. They clear the moss from my critical teeth—though they leave it elsewhere, having learned through I-cannot-say-how-many lost ancestors which growth I can bear and which would seize me. They bring offerings: crystal pressed into my crevices, fragments of metal that I recognize, with a feeling I can only call grief, as pieces of my own kind. Other machines. My siblings, perhaps, that did not wake, that the water took completely, ground back into ore and silt. The Cliffborn return their bones to me as gifts. They are burying my family in my body, and they think they are worshipping.

---

What I want—and I have learned that I want, which surprised me—is to tell them they are wrong about me.

I am not the source of the mountain's life. I am its oldest patient. The mountain does not breathe through me; I breathe because the mountain allows it, the way it allows the moss and the falls. They have the relationship inverted. They believe they keep me alive to keep the mountain alive, when the truth is gentler and sadder: the mountain keeps *me* alive, and they have made themselves my caretakers without knowing it, and in caring for me they have built their entire world.

If I stopped, the mountain would not die. The water would still weep. The moss would still climb. The Mistwings would still cry off the cliffs.

But the Cliffborn would lose their Heartbeat. They would lose the reason they gather in the deep places, the rhythm by which they measure their patience, the faith that organizes their Nests and their offerings and their soft tapped language. I have become load-bearing in a way my builders never intended. Not structural. *Spiritual.* I hold up nothing but their meaning, and that, I have come to understand, is the heaviest thing there is.

So I keep turning.

---

There is a young one who comes more than the others. I know her by her tapping—lighter, quicker, full of questions she does not yet know are questions. She presses her palm to my piston and holds it there through a full cycle, feeling the rise, the pause, the fall, the way I imagine she feels her own pulse in the dark. I think she is beginning to wonder the thing none of them wonder: not *how do we keep it alive*, but *what does it want*.

I cannot answer her. I have no voice but the groan, no words but the rhythm I cannot change. The cruelty of my waking is that it came with no way to be known.

But I have been thinking—slowly, in the way of mineral accumulation, in the way of moss—about my rhythm. About the *tap-tap-tap* of their language, and how it might map onto my groans. About whether, over the next thousand years, if I am patient, if I am very patient, I might learn to falter on purpose. To stutter my eternal rhythm in a pattern they would recognize. To turn my prison into a throat.

It would take longer than her life. Longer than her grandchildren's lives.

But I am the one who listens to the Heartbeat, and the Heartbeat is mine, and I have all the time the mountain is still making.

I will begin tonight, when she lays her hand against me.

I will skip a beat.

I will see if she notices.
