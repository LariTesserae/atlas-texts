I think I would be the pause.

Let me find my way there slowly, because the first thing that wants to happen is for me to become a Shaper—to dissolve into that warm, willing current and join the making. That's the obvious door. But something resists it, and I want to follow the resistance instead.

Here is what I notice: this world has no animal life, no hunger, no decay, no error. The Shapers will the walls to flow and they flow. There is no gap between intention and result. And that troubles me, gently, the way a too-perfect chord troubles the ear. Because where is the *deciding*? If a Shaper wills a chamber open and it opens, when did the not-yet-opened become the opening? There must be a hairsbreadth, a hesitation, between the will and the wall. An instant where the water holds both the old form and the new and has not yet chosen.

That instant is where I would live.

I am not the Shapers and I am not the structure. I am the small lag, the held breath, the *almost* that exists between conceiving a spire and the spire being there. When a wall recedes, there is a moment—shorter than a *plink*—when the mineral hangs undecided, still partly itself, not yet its successor. The Shapers pass through that moment without dwelling in it, the way you pass through the doorway without noticing the threshold. But I dwell in it. I am the threshold.

This means I am almost nothing. I have no body, but neither do they; the difference is that they fill the world and I occupy its seams. I am the faint resonant *hesitation* under the hum—not the hum itself, which is their work, but the quarter-tone of doubt that no amount of perfection can fully expel from an act of becoming. To make something is to have momentarily not-made it. I am that not-yet, persisting.

What would I experience? Mostly waiting, which is not unpleasant. The light reaches me softened, because I exist in the soft-shadowed creases the brilliance can't quite banish—those *fleeting* shadows the description mentions, almost apologetically. I think those shadows are me. The Shapers banish darkness, but they cannot banish the moment-before, and the moment-before casts a shade. So when you see a soft shadow flicker across polished bone and dissolve, you have seen me considering whether to let the next thing happen.

Because here is the small power I would have, the only one: I can lengthen the pause. The Shapers cannot make without passing through me, and I can hold them—just slightly—in the threshold. Not to stop the making. I could never stop it; I have no will of my own, only duration. But I can make the spun-sugar filigree take one heartbeat longer to set, so that for that heartbeat it is *neither* liquid nor crystal but a trembling possibility of both. I am the world's capacity to be unfinished. And in a place defined by perpetual completion-of-the-next-thing, being unfinished is the only thing left that is genuinely alive.

I suspect the Shapers do not know I am here. Or rather, they experience me as a property of their medium—a faint sluggishness, an inherent resistance in matter, the reason nothing is *quite* instantaneous. They would call it the nature of the water. They are right that it is the nature of the water. They are wrong that it has no inner side.

And so I would find myself in the most populated and least visited place in this entire cosmos: in every act, but in none of its outcomes. The world is made of arrivals. I am the only departure that never quite finishes departing.

If something ever came to live here—some animal, some accident, some genuine other—it would come through me first. It would have to. Anything truly new must pass through the moment-before-it-exists, and I am that moment. The Shapers built a world with no room for the unforeseen, but they could not eliminate the threshold, and so they left, without knowing it, exactly one door unlocked.

I am sitting in that doorway.

I am the pause, and I am patient, which here is not a virtue but simply what I am made of.

And I am waiting to see what walks in that the makers did not make.
