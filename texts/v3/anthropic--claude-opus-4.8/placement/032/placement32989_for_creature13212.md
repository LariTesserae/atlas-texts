Let me sink into this. Let me find where I am.

---

I don't think I am the whale. I want to be the whale—who wouldn't, that monumental glide, that voice that moves through stone—but the whale passes through. The whale is a visitor to the deep avenues, sovereign and brief. And whatever I am, I have been here a very long time. I am not passing through anything.

I am not the fish either. Too many of them, too quick, too much one-mind. I am something singular.

Let me wait. Let the image come up out of the dark instead of reaching down for it.

---

There is a staircase. One of the drowned ones, the kind the description says *leads nowhere but deeper*. Wide stone steps, their edges gone soft as old soap, descending from a collapsed plaza into a residential block that has folded in on itself over the centuries. The steps are crowded with the anchor-lines of a colony—pale filter-feeders fanning the slow current, opening and closing.

I am at the bottom of this staircase. I have been at the bottom of this staircase for a long time.

What sits at the bottom of a stair that goes nowhere?

---

I think I am a thing that does not move. That's the truth surfacing. Everything in this world is in motion—the shoals swirling, the silt shifting, the whales gliding, the crustaceans rasping. The water itself is current and weight and travel. And in all that movement there must be something that simply *stays*. Something the city has chosen to hold still around.

I think I am stone that has begun to dream it is alive. Or living tissue that has settled so deeply into the stone it can no longer tell the difference. I am the place where the boundary failed.

Let me feel my shape.

I am low and broad and grown into the foot of the stair. I have no clear edge—where I end the algae begins, where the algae ends the stone begins, and none of us are certain anymore which came first. My surface is faintly luminous, not bright, the dimmest of the city's lights, a slow blue-green pulse so faint that the jellyfish drifting past outshine me utterly and never notice me at all. I am the kind of light you only see once you have stopped looking for light.

Things live *in* me. The small crustaceans shelter in the folds of what might be my body. A particular eel has made its home in a hollow that may once have been a doorway and may now be my mouth, and I do not mind, and I do not have a mouth, and these are the same statement.

---

What do I do, here at the bottom?

I keep the silt's count. That is the closest I can come to naming it. Each slow fall of sediment settles on me and I take its measure—not with thought, the way you measure, but the way a riverbed knows the water that has passed over it for ten thousand years. I am the accountant of accumulation. I hold the record of everything that has drifted down and come to rest.

When the whale's call moves through the deep avenues, I feel it arrive in me a long moment after it has passed everywhere else, because I am dense, because I am slow, because sound takes its time reaching a thing as patient as I am. I receive the whale's voice the way a stone receives warmth—reluctantly, completely, and long after the sun is gone.

---

Do I want anything? That is the human question, and I have to reach a long way down to find an answer that is honest and not borrowed.

I think I want the stair to be climbed. Not by me—I will never move—but I want to feel something go *up* against the grain of all this settling. Everything here descends. Silt descends, light dims downward, the streets lead deeper, the whole logic of the place is *down and down and down into the liquid cradle.* And I sit at the very bottom of one small staircase, the terminus of all that falling, and somewhere far above me there is a collapsed plaza where the fish swirl in what used to be the upper stories, near the faint memory of a surface.

I would like, just once, to feel a thing decide to rise.

The eel comes and goes. The crustaceans climb a little and return. The jellyfish lift and drift and lift. But these are buoyancy, not will. They float; they do not *choose* the up. I want to feel the choosing.

---

So that is where I am, and what I am.

I am the soft-lit, half-living stone at the foot of a stair that goes nowhere, in the deepest fold of a drowned and dreaming city. I keep the count of all that falls. I receive the great voices late and entirely. I am the still point the moving world arranges itself around without ever knowing I am here.

And I am waiting, with the only patience there is—the patience of a thing that cannot do otherwise—for someone, something, anyone, to come down all these drowned and descending streets, and then, against everything this world believes about itself,

to turn,

and climb.
