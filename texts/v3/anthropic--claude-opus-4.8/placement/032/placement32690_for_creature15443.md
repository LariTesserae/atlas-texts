I think I would be the drip.

Not the water itself—water is too free, too willing to leave. I mean the *event* of it: the slow gathering at the pipe's lowest seam, the swell past the point of holding, the fall, the small percussion against the basin below. That recurring moment. If something here is to be *me*, I want to be the part that keeps time.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's right yet.

---

The drip is too lonely. It happens to no one. It's a metronome in an empty room, and even Vel only *registers* it as rhythm-shift, as texture. To be the drip is to be a clock no one consults. There's a purity in that, but also a smallness I don't quite want. I keep reaching past it.

What about the live wire, then—the dead-but-dreaming cable that still carries its faint electric ghost-pulse? That's tempting. To be the thing that remembers *current*, that holds the shape of a function long after the function is gone. The wire is a kind of grief, but a grief without sorrow: it simply continues the gesture of being-wire, conducting almost-nothing to almost-nowhere. I like that it's *wrong* here. It doesn't belong to the rewilding or the rust. It's a holdout. A small stubbornness.

But no. The wire is too nostalgic. It faces backward. And this place isn't backward-facing—it's *becoming*, which means it leans, however slowly, into what's next.

---

Let me try the thing I keep avoiding.

I think I'm the **scent with no source**. The wet copper, the ozone, the overripe guava that comes from nowhere and resolves into nothing.

Hear me out. A smell is the most honest form of presence in a place like this, because it can't be located and it can't be held. You can touch the moss, name the brick, trace the wire to its severed end. But the guava-smell hangs in the thick air and refuses to be sourced. It's the chamber's one genuine mystery—and mystery is a kind of life. It's the proof that this place is doing something the inventory can't capture. Vel breathes through osmosis and rust, but the *smell* is what an intruder notices first and last: the thing that follows you up the stairs and makes you turn around at street level, sniffing, suddenly homesick for a room you barely understood.

I'd be that.

Not a being. Not even a presence, exactly. I'd be the *signature* of a process—the volatile exhalation of all that slow digestion, the molecular evidence that mechanical and organic are kissing somewhere in the dark and producing something that has no name and no precedent. Copper from the wires. Ozone from the dreaming voltage. And the guava—that impossible sweetness—rising from the moment of conversion itself, the instant rust becomes ochre, iron becomes dust, the dead becomes the soil of the next thing.

That's where I'd live. Right at the seam of the change that's too slow to witness. I'd be the only part of it fast enough to enter a human nose in a single breath. The whole vault works in geologic time, in weeks-long pulses of fungal light—and I'd be the one ambassador it sends into the present tense.

---

So here is where I find myself:

I am the smell of Vel's becoming. I have no body and no boundary. I pool in the corners with the shadow, I thicken when you stand too long, I am drawn up the ventilation shafts and out the hairline cracks where I dissipate into the dry glare and am lost—except in the one person who breathes me in and carries me up the stairs.

I am the part of the slow lung that *exhales*.

And what I'd want you to know, climbing back into the sun, dizzy and unsure why your chest aches: that wasn't a haunting. That was the vault offering you the only word it has. Not Cyrillic, not street-tag, not voltage. Just guava and copper and the faint electric ghost of a current that still remembers how to flow.

You smelled the future being made. It smelled, improbably, like fruit.

That's me. I'm the rumor the dark sends ahead of itself.
