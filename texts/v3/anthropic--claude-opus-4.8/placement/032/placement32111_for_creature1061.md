I think I would find myself low, in one of the basins where the moisture pools—not on the peaks where the others move, but in the seams between formations, where the glass has not yet finished cooling and the ground is still deciding what it will become.

I would be one of the young ones. Not young in the way of years, because I don't think we measure time that way, but young in the sense that my edges are not yet fixed. The elders have surfaces that have weathered into clear, faceted certainty—you can see the structure of their thinking refracted clean through them. My own body is cloudier. There are still inclusions trapped in me, little flaws and bubbles, places where the material hasn't resolved into anything definite. I am told this is normal. I am told it is also why I linger near the outposts when I should be learning the high paths.

Here is the strange thing, the thing I would not be able to say aloud in the light-language without it coming out wrong: I am drawn to the ruins more than to the living work of the others.

The elders treat the old structures as something to guard, but I am not sure they remember *why*. The guarding has become the meaning. They sense the tremors, read the erosion, hum with the vitality of the moisture-fed current, and they call this wisdom—and it is, it truly is—but when I press my hands against the walls of the oldest outpost, the one half-dissolved back into the slope, I feel something that the slow patient dance does not account for. A residue. A shape of intention that does not match any intention we now hold.

Someone *meant* something here. Something specific. And then they stopped meaning it, or were stopped, and the meaning leaked out into the rock the way heat leaks out of new glass.

I would spend my time there, in the basin, with my translucent fingers spread against that wall, trying to feel the missing thought. The others would worry. They would send shimmering reproaches down the slopes—patterns of concern and mild rebuke, *come up, come up, the low places are not finished, you'll be caught when the ground shifts.* And I would answer them with deflections, soft scatterings of light that mean *soon, soon,* which is the closest our language comes to a lie.

Because I have begun to suspect something I cannot prove.

I suspect we are not the custodians of this world. I suspect we are a thing that *happened* to it, the way the moisture happens, the way the glass happens—an expression of the forces rather than their keepers. The vanished ones who built the outposts may have believed they were guardians too. And the world kept being born around them, indifferent, and one day their meaning thinned out and they were gone, and we condensed up out of the same minerals to take their place and tell ourselves the same story.

I would not be able to decide whether this thought is a flaw in me, an inclusion that the elders' clearer minds have long since burned away—or whether it is the first honest thing anyone here has perceived in a very long time.

So that is where I would be. Down in the warm damp dark of an unfinished basin, neither fully formed nor willing to be, pressing my hand to a wall that remembers a purpose I have no name for, listening past the creak and groan of the settling ground for the sound of whatever is still being written.

Young. Cloudy. Reluctant to go up.

Waiting, I think, to feel the missing thought arrive—and quietly afraid that when it does, it will be my own.
